Project Summary

In response to the lack of educational infrastructures and services, TGH will support schools with both hard and soft components in the targeted villages.
In the villages targeted, classrooms will be rehabilitated with durable material, school material will be distributed and the schools will be fenced to reinforce the children protection. Moreover, uniforms and students stationaries fairs will be organized for the most vulnerable children.
With regards to the soft component, teachers will benefit from trainings that will be designed in close collaboration with the Ministry of Education, enrollment campaign to raise awareness on children rights and importance of school enrollment will be organized. Teachers will receive pedagogical training but also training on leisure activities and sensitization to different topics, such as inclusion of children with disabilities and gender-based violence.
Furthermore, to support youth that dropped-out in their early ages, literacy, basic mathematics and life-skills lessons will be provided to vulnerable youth.
The sanitation situation of the schools will also be improved, as the lack of latrines is an important factor regarding girls’ drop-out rate and a risk for children’s health
The PTAs will also be supported as they often are the major stakeholder of the education system that provides support for schools and parents: trainings will be provided, as well as support to implement IGAs. Moreover, school gardening activities will be implemented.
Finally, the project aims at improving and strengthening the quality of the learning environment for around 12,286 children and education staff from returnee, protracted IDP and host communities in Kreinik (West Darfur) and Um Dukhun localities (Central Darfur).

1 - Needs

In 2018 and 2019, TGH implemented the first phase of a multi-sectorial program (WASH, education and livelihood) funded by SHF in Kreinik camps (West Darfur) which adopted an inclusive approach to bring relief assistance to protracted IDPs who have received very little humanitarian aid through the enhancement of access to and quality of basic services. The second phase of the project has started in 2019 and will be implemented in 2020, in order to consolidate the results achieved during the first phase and thus ensure the durability of TGH actions. The monitoring and evaluation of the first phase enabled TGH to assess the remaining needs and to capitalize on the achievements made during the first phase. The main needs in terms of education observed by TGH during the first phase of the project are trainings for teachers, especially regarding leisure activities, continue the support to PTAs to help reducing the school fees, support to students for stationaries and uniforms, support in school feeding, support in organizing recreational activities for students, sensitization of communities on education. All these activities should enable to decrease the drop-out rate and increase the school enrollment one.TGH activities will benefit to around 6,286 children, teachers and member of PTAs in Kreinik. Within the scope of the Durable Solutions process, TGH conducted assessments in Um Dukhun locality, especially in the 4 villages targeted by this program, i.e. Abujaradil, Selele, Sorey and Marari. A lot of returnees came back to these villages during the past months. Among displaced population, women and children are the most vulnerable (60% of displaced people in Sudan are under 18) and often more exposed to the risk of violence during their daily movement (when they go to the market, fetching water or for wood collection for instance). Therefore, the needs in terms of education and protection require improvements. The assessments conducted in the 4 villages targeted highlighted the needs of schools rehabilitation with durable material, the needs in terms of capacity building for both teachers (as a lot of teachers are volunteers and did not receive any proper training) and PTAs to improve the school management. Lack of material and stationaries have been observed, especially among the most vulnerable households. Finally, TGH will pay specific attention to integrate a gender dimension in the project including a gender analysis in the needs assessment to identify the women, girls, boys and men who have special needs. The aims of the action and activities provided, through the provision of protection services, are to reduce the barriers so all women, girls, boys and men are able to exercise and access their rights. To advance gender equality, the actions will focus on people who have special needs, (eg out-of school girls, women survivors of violence, people with disabilities). These issues will be tackled during awareness campaigns among the communities and TGH will pay a close attention to participation of girls and women within this intervention. Moreover, the construction of latrines will be gender-sensitive, as per the SPHERE standards, and will include specific design for people with disabilities. Both localities targeted by this project, i.e. Um Dukhun (Central Darfur) and Kreinik (West Darfur) belong to vulnerability category 4 according to Education sector HNO 2020.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

In order to mitigate the risks on the environment, TGH will use material purchased locally, as much as possible, to rehabilitate the schools, construct the latrines and fence the schools. Moreover, during the implementation of school gardening, messages on the environment importance, as well as the need to protect it, will be spread among the PTA members and the students. The school gardening activity will enable the school to provide the children meals composed of local products.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Through the organization of fairs, instead of direct distribution, TGH ensures that the beneficiaries can select the items they really need, without deciding for them. This allows the beneficiaries to restore their dignity and enhance their resilience. Throughout the project, TGH will pay attention to protection mainstreaming regarding women and girls. Gender-sensitive latrines will be constructed, focus on girls enrollment in schools and GBV will be addressed during awareness campaigns. TGH will pay a close attention to the participation of girls and women within this intervention. Furthermore, people with disabilities will also be taken into account while implementing this project. One latrine per block constructed or rehabilitated will have a specific design to facilitate its use by people with disabilities and their inclusion within the education system will be tackled during teachers training and awareness campaigns.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

TGH puts in place mechanisms to enable beneficiaries and affected populations to provide feedback and complaints. At the beginning of the project implementation, TGH will organize a meeting with leaders and other relevant stakeholders, ensuring the inclusion of women, to present the activities and explain that community complaints and advices are necessary to the action’s improvement. TGH will present to the beneficiaries the different ways to communicate their issues (special complains during distribution, direct contact with TGH staff, visit to TGH office, etc.). When a complaint is addressed to TGH, it is acknowledged by the sector managers who provide the necessary information to the affected population to ensure their comprehension of TGH operations, to address answers to beneficiaries concerns and complaints prioritization criteria and operational or budget limitations. According to the relevance of the concerns addressed by the community to the Base Managers and Program team in the field, these can also be analyzed in further details and considered in activities planning. While explaining the complaint process, TGH team will mention GBV and SEA and highlight the importance to raise any concerns regarding these topics. Transparency is ensured through adequate information sharing and communication to beneficiaries and affected populations have already been mentioned above and include meetings and specific complaint mechanisms and responses with affected populations, Sheikhs or other community leaders and more importantly, availability of TGH staff.
