Project Summary

MAG has been active in Humanitarian Mine Action in Northeast Syria since 2016. As one of the foremost actors in mine action and counting over 250 staff members, MAG is one of the most experienced actors in land clearance and release, both manual and mechanical. While significant clearance efforts have been conducted to enable early recovery and reconstruction, contamination remains high in many communities, including in and around key infrastructure such as schools, hospitals and power stations. Contamination also affects agricultural lands, which is crucial in a region of Syria known as the “breadbasket of the country”.
The aim of the intervention is then to ensure improved access to safe land and infrastructure for conflict affected communities and humanitarian actors, in a view of reducing the threat of EO and supporting economic and social recovery for vulnerable communities within Northeast Syria.
MAG also focuses on Community Liaison activities to ensure the full participation of the population. Through Community Liaison Teams, MAG intends to engage with communities and gather their feedback on the impact of land clearance and release. Also, the feedback collected through Non-Technical Surveys are relevant for prioritizing the clearance activities and needs of the population also in terms of Housing, Landing and Property rights.
Further, MAG invests a lot in Explosive Ordnance Risk Education Sessions to raise awareness within the communities about the risk of EO and enhance their protection and safety. Collaborating with schools and local authorities, MAG identifies individuals who can be selected as Community Focal Points and be trained to further train at risks communities, especially within IDPs camps and informal settlements of individuals willing to return to their own houses.
MAG is currently active in Ar-Raqqa and Al-Hasakeh Governorates and, depending on the funding situation, is willing to expand its operations to Deir Ez-Zor area.
In terms of land release, the target for 2023 is to release 1,900,000 m2 of lands, remove more than 300 Explosive Ordnance items. This will be beneficial for 19,361 direct individuals. Moreover, the Community Liaison teams will deliver 7,515 Explosive Ordnances Risk Education (EORE) to reach out to 75,146 direct beneficiaries (men, women, boys and girls) and will train 500 Community Focal Points to deliver EORE safety messages and to report on contamination. It is expected that 80 contamination surveys will be conducted as well as 20 Non-Technical Surveys for Susbected and Confirmed Hazardous Areas (SHAs and CHAs).

3 - Needs

The land contamination in Northeast Syria is still very extensive. According to the last Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 1 in 3 communities is estimated to be potentially contaminated by Explosive Ordnance. The highest rate is recorded in areas where hostilities were most intense, such as Raqqa, Hasakeh and Deir Ez-Zor in the Northeast Syria. Indeed, ISIS retreat left many improvised landmines, booby-traps and IEDs. The situation worsened as a result of the recent hostilities, such as the Turkish invasion in 2019 and the prison break in Hasakeh in 2022. As of May 2022, IMMAP recorded over 27 million square meters of land as contaminated with explosive ordnance (EO), but the full scale is likely much higher as the full extent of contamination remains unknown. The casualty figures in the region provide an indication of the widespread nature of contamination - between September 2021 and April 2022, there were 1,162 deaths and 860 injuries from EO in NES alone. Though anyone living in a contaminated area is a potential victim, data show that the most affected by EO are men and boys. This is strictly related to the activity conducted during the incident. In fact, men and boys are more exposed to farming, herding, collecting scrap metal from the ground, moving between towns. Usually, women and girls become indirect victims as they lose men family members and the breadwinners. Finally, the HNO shows that 76% of surveyed people did not attend any RE sessions in the past 3 months

4 - Implementation of activities

MAG will conduct the below activities: 1. Community Liaison activities, which include the provision of Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) sessions to affected communities, including returnees, IDPs and local communities. Community Focal Points (CFPs) training, communities’ mapping (Contamination Surveys) and Non-Technical Survey (NTS) to identify Suspected and Confirmed Hazardous Areas (SHAs and CHAs). 2. Clearance activities, which include the Technical Survey (TS), mechanical operations and manual clearance through the approaches: Improvised minefield clearance, Battle Area Clearance, House/Urban search and clearance and EOD spot tasks. MAG utilizes a range of mechanical assets specifically procured for enabling safe access to damaged infrastructure, route clearance or assisting with agricultural land clearance. Mechanical assets assist the efficiency and safety of manual clearance and work alongside the Multi-Tasks teams, EOD teams or Mine Action teams, increasing efficiency of clearance operations up to 50%. During the project period, MAG’s senior operations staff, with oversight from the TOM, will carry out the below QA/QC activities:• Routine and random QA/QC checks of operational sites (MAT);• Pre- and post-clearance checks of operational sites (MAT);• Routine and random QA/QC check of all EORE and NTS activities (CL);• QA/QC checks of all operational reports. Data is collected by using the Survey 123.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

MAG developed a department of Safeguarding, aiming at preventing any Sexual Exploitation, Harassment and Abuse. In addition to that, the Safeguarding Policy includes measures to investigate in case of any SEA case. Both in country and at HQ level, the Safeguarding department has measures in place to address potential cases. New staff is always thoroughly trained in local language as the material exists in both Arabic and English. Refresher training sessions are provided to all the staff members to make sure to achieve the goal of the training. MAG will apply 5 standards to PSEA: Safe People: MAG’s HR policies and processes are set out. Ensure staff with responsibilities for PSEA are appointed and skilled to undertake their roles. Staff is trained on the PSEA every year. Safe programs: risk assessments are conducted and in place to prevent harm and abuse to people and communities where we work. The national context is understood and reporting mechanisms are clear.Safe communications: use of information and images in MAG’s activities feature appropriate images and stories of communities and children and ensure they are not exposed to harm and abuse. Safe Response: MAG treats any allegations related to PSEA extremely seriously. We commit to respectfully listening and supporting individuals who want to raise a concern or make a complaint. We ensure that genuinely held concerns will be thoroughly investigated. Safe Governance: MAG’s governance is based on our values.
