Project Summary

The purpose of the project is to improve food consumption, food security, dietary diversity and coping strategies and enhance emergency food production of vulnerable populations in the targeted counties. This will be achieved through provision of Cash-Based Transfers (CBT) to address food needs of vulnerable communities during the lean season and provision of timely and appropriate complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs and fishing kits coupled with training in improved agronomic and fishing practices.
In line with the South Sudan FSL cluster HRP objectives and priorities, NPA will support vulnerable populations in IPC 4 and 5 locations as well as some IPC 3 locations. Specifically, NPA will target Canal/Pigi, Fangak, Pochalla and Bor South Counties of Jonglei State, Rumbek East, Rumbek Centre, Cueibet, Yirol East, Yirol West and Awerial Counties of Lakes State, Terekeka County of Central Equatoria State and Kapoeta North County of Eastern Equatoria State.
NPA seeks to support 86,200 HHs (517,200 individuals) through provision of complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs and fishing kits across the target counties. Some of the kits will be provided by FAO (core pipeline) while the rest will be procured and distributed directly by NPA to enhance food production. NPA will build the capacity of the HHs supported with the kits through training on improved agronomic and fishing practices, marketing and group dynamics. A Training of Trainers (ToT) approach will be utilized whereby lead farmers and fisher folk will be trained by NPA. They will then train/share the knowledge and skills with other farmers and fisher folk in their communities. The kits provided through this project and the knowledge gained from the trainings is expected to improve the agricultural and fishing practices of the target HHs thus enhancing and sustaining food production. CBT interventions will target 1,000 HHs (6,000 individuals ) in Rumbek East, Rumbek Centre and Cuiebet Counties in Lakes State to improve their access to food, diversify and improve their food consumption and dietary diversity scores and promote the adoption of positive coping strategies. The planned interventions are aligned to the FSL cluster objectives 1 and 2 and will provide emergency assistance and livelihoods support to save lives and avert total collapse of livelihoods in the target counties.
NPA will integrate development-oriented and conflict-sensitivity activities in this project to enhance the resilience of vulnerable HHs in line with the Triple Nexus. Therefore, 1,200 vulnerable HHs will be supported with small-scale cash grants to establish IGAs. The target HHs will be mobilized and sensitized to transition to and establish VSLA’s to ease access to financial services and encourage the spirit of saving among the VSLA group members. The IGAs will contribute to increasing and diversifying income and building the resilience of the target HHs at the same time improving functionality of local markets and boosting the local value chains.
NPA will employ a community-based beneficiary selection approach which will entail establishing Project Management Committees (PMCs) to lead the selection of eligible beneficiaries based on the beneficiary selection criteria. Vulnerable HHs with severe food insecurity including female, child, disabled/chronically ill headed HHs; persons living with or at risk of HIV/AIDS and the elderly will be targeted in this project.
NPA will ensure meaningful engagement of the target communities throughout the project life-cycle through holding regular meetings with the PMCs and community members and Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) to get their feedback on the project. NPA will ensure that the feedback is incorporated in the implementation of this project and design of future projects. Further, NPA will use a community-based complaints and feedback mechanism to provide a platform for beneficiaries to voice any issues or concerns.

1 - Needs

The people of South Sudan continue to bear the brunt of the impacts of years of conflict, violence, economic deterioration, climate-related extremities and limited development investment. Increasing intercommunal violence and attacks, the threat of the peace process unravelling, and dire humanitarian consequences of repeated cycles of flooding and drought across the country has placed renewed urgency on improving security and meeting basic protection needs for South Sudan’s civilians. This situation demonstrates the interconnectedness of the peace, development and humanitarian challenges and the need for an equally integrated response. The key drivers of food insecurity in South Sudan are the combined effects of high food prices, protracted insecurity caused by internal and external conflicts, and devastating floods that followed a prolonged dry spell earlier in the 2022 farming season which affected farming activities. The persistent macroeconomic challenges continue to impact on poor households’ purchasing power, forcing them to rely on emergency coping strategies to mitigate the food gaps experienced. Households are yet to recover from the multiple shocks that left them without assets and any viable livelihood options, thus compromising their resilience to future shocks. The target locations are amongst the counties of highest concern in terms of food insecurity in South Sudan. Populations in Fangak and Canal/Pigi counties remain in famine/catastrophe (IPC phase 5) with households suffering from an extreme lack of food and basic needs, leading to extremely critical acute malnutrition levels, and in the worst cases, starvation, destitution and death. The fluid and unpredictable security situation in Fangak and Canal/Pigi counties coupled with perennial flooding has exacerbated an already appalling humanitarian situation. Following the fighting between SPLM IO and Agwelek forces from August to September 2022, the humanitarian situation has deteriorated further with needs becoming greater than ever. Flooding remains a potential hazard in Rumbek East, Awerial, Cuiebet, Yirol East and Yirol West Counties in Lakes State. The floods experienced in 2022 and in previous years have displaced many people and destroyed infrastructure and livelihoods. The counties have limited livelihoods opportunities and only one planting season with most of the people relying on crops (sorghum, maize and groundnuts), livestock (mainly cattle and goats) and fishery value chain enterprises as means of livelihoods. Most of the produce harvested this year has been destroyed by the floods. While the flood waters are expected to recede in December 2022, the food security situation outlook for 2023 is bleak due to the massive destruction of the harvested produce and field crops. Pochalla county experienced the worst floods in 2022 as a result of overflowing of river Akobo and Oboth. Large swathes of crops (maize and cassava) are inundated with water and the community runs the risk of significant crop failure and lack of food reserves for 2023. Food insecurity has been a critical challenge in Kapoeta North for an extended period of time due to perennial droughts followed by heavy flooding. The county is currently in IPC 4 (emergency). Women and girls continue to be more vulnerable to food insecurity than men, yet women bear the responsibility for managing food, water and other provisions in the household. Cultural and social roles dictate that they should care for their families, feed them, and even sacrifice their own portions when and where necessary. Men generally eat first and receive the largest share, with children and/or dependents eating next. Women eat last and least. Women and girls are responsible for gathering water lilies and wild food for consumption when food is scarce. 2 - Project activities

Activity 1 - Project launch and community mobilization: NPA will conduct various meetings with project staff, local authorities, key stakeholders and community representatives to discuss the project objectives, strategies implementation plan, distribution plans etc.Activity 2 - Formation and training of Project Management Committees (PMCs): NPA will facilitate the formation of Boma-level PMCs. Each PMC will comprise of 10 members (5 women, 5 men) who will be trained and empowered to be a voice to and of the targeted beneficiaries as well as participate in beneficiary selection and monitoring of the project implementation.Activity 3 - Beneficiary selection and sensitization on the project activities: Under the guidance of NPA, the PMCs will identify and register the beneficiaries according to NPA’s vulnerability criteria for targeting food-insecure HHs with a focus on women/child/chronically ill-headed HHs, People with disabilities, widows, elderly, pregnant/lactating women, orphaned etc. This will ensure the prioritization of assistance towards those who need it most. The selected beneficiaries will be sensitized on the project implementation modalities, entitlements, duration of assistance etc. At least 55% of the target HHs will be female-headed.Activity 4 - Provision of unconditional Cash-Based Transfers (CBT) to 1,000 severely or highly vulnerable HHs to meet their immediate food needs during the lean season: The cash transfer values will be based on the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) as established by the South Sudan JMMI at the time of the transfer. Cash distributions will be done by a Financial Service Provider (FSP) who will disburse cash according to the schedule provided by NPA and will provide reports to NPA upon completion of the activities.Activity 5 - Distribution of complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs and fishing kits: NPA will distribute Crop kits (Maize, Sorghum and Cowpea), vegetable kits and tools (Amaranth, Collard, Okra, Tomato, Onion, Watermelon, Pumpkin and Maloda) and Fishing kits (hooks, monofilament and spools of twine) to the targeted HHs. Some of the kits will be provided by FAO (core pipeline) while the rest will be procured and distributed directly by NPA to enhance food production. Activity 6 - Formation and training of farmers and fisher folk groups: NPA will encourage and facilitate the formation of farmer and fisher folk groups to encourage team work and peer to peer exchange. ToT training on improved agronomic practices, fish preservation, marketing and group dynamics will be provided to 9,373 model farmers and fisher folk. The trained farmers and fisher folk will then sensitize and build the capacity of the other farmers and fisher folk in their communities. Activity 7 - Monitoring and reporting: NPA will conduct routine monitoring activities to gather data on the progress of the project and submit the required reports. NPA will also conduct PDM after every distribution of cash, vegetable and crop livelihood inputs and fishing kits to ascertain the appropriateness of the response. Outcome monitoring will be done periodically to assess the changes in the target communities as a result of the project. Feedback from the beneficiaries, lessons learnt and best practices will be used to inform subsequent project activities as well as design of future projects.Activity 8 - Provision of cash grants to 60 Income Generating Activity (IGA). The groups will be trained on business plan development and supported with cash grants for investment in business enterprises. The amount of cash grants to be disbursed will be informed by the nature and type of businesses based on their business plans.Activity 9 - Markets, traders and price monitoring will be conducted at the end of each month following each cash distribution cycle to establish changes in market accessibility, availability of commodities, traders restocking capacities, trends in market prices as a result of the CBT

3 - Project indicators

At NPA, Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) is a critical part of the project cycle management and is conducted in a systematic manner. NPA has a strong in-country MEAL unit led by a Monitoring and Evaluation Coordinator supported by M&E officers across NPA’s field offices. MEAL activities are strongly linked to project design and implementation. MEAL will be a continuous and collaborative process conducted by NPA staff, communities and other relevant stakeholders. The purpose of the MEAL activities will be to track progress against output and outcome indicators to inform NPA managerial and strategic decision-making to ensure maximum sustainable impact. MEAL activities will seek to establish how and why desired changes have happened and overall, how the project worked. NPA will develop and utilize a M&E Framework to ensure that project implementation progress, deliverables and indicators are monitored accurately and in a timely manner for reporting, learning and informing effective and efficient adjustments or recommendations to the project design. A mix of qualitative and quantitative monitoring techniques will be employed in this project to ensure that accurate data is collected and triangulated. Given the fluidity of the situation and the varied activities being implemented, monitoring will be conducted on a daily, weekly, monthly and quarterly basis by M&E staff, programme staff, Project Management Committees (PMCs), relevant stakeholders depending on the specific activity, output and indicator. The data will then be submitted to the M&E unit for entry, verification, analysis and management. Periodic meetings involving the relevant stakeholders will be conducted to review the findings of the monitoring activities. Monitoring activities including Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) using both household surveys and Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) will ensure that perspectives of women and men are captured, analyzed and acted upon throughout the project implementation. The following indicators will be monitored in this project:• Number of HHs (female, child & male headed) receiving agricultural inputs/ vegetable & fishing kits• Number of beneficiaries receiving cash transfers • Value of cash/ voucher received by beneficiaries (and proportion in relation to food basket)• Quantity and variety of crop inputs distributed• Quantity and variety of vegetable inputs distributed• Quantity and variety of fishing kits distributed• % women receiving livelihood kits (crop/ veg/ fish)• Quantity & variety of crops produced (outcome)• No less than 50% women committee members, in roles mirroring that of men• Beneficiary satisfaction with information, participation and CFM• Number of Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) undertaken• % of the target population with acceptable Food Consumption Score (FCS) and HDDS at the end of implementation• Number of IGA groups supported• Number of individuals participating in IGA groups

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

NPA has been implementing Cash-Based Transfers (CBT) interventions in the proposed target counties following market assessments to establish the suitability of a cash-based response. Throughout this project, NPA will continue assessing the functionality of markets through markets, traders and price monitoring. NPA will also rely on secondary information from the Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JMMI) on the functionality of the markets.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash transfers are recognized as an effective and efficient way of responding to humanitarian needs while also adding value by boosting local economies through liquidity injections to the wider communities and markets, promoting further commerce and improved functionality of local vendors while reducing logistical costs and promoting ownership, dignity and resilience within target communities. Cash-based interventions offer wider choice and flexibility to HHs in meeting dietary needs and accessing alternative markets for desired commodities.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

NPA will use a community-based complaints and feedback mechanism to provide a platform for beneficiaries voice any issues of concerns and feedback on the project activities. NPA will work with the PMCs to gather feedback on the project from their communities and share with NPA. To ensure inclusivity, NPA will support the PMCs to reach out to individuals living with disabilities, the elderly, women and girls to get their feedback through household visits and FGDs. The feedback will then be shared with NPA through regular meetings with the PMC's. This target group will also be sampled during the PDMs to ensure that their voice is heard. During distribution of inputs and cash, NPA will set up help desks and encourage vulnerable community members to share any feedback they have on the project.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Through community mobilization and sensitization activities, NPA will endeavor to provide accurate, timely and accessible information to beneficiaries about every aspect of the project including the planned activities, beneficiary entitlements, timelines and how to share feedback or report complains to NPA. Community mobilization will take the form of regular community and bilateral meetings. NPA will also place banners and signposts with project details at strategic points in the target communities. NPA will actively involve beneficiaries in identifying their priorities and solutions to their challenges to ensure that the voices and concerns of all groups are considered during the project’s design, planning and implementation.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

During the community mobilization and sensitization meetings, NPA will provide beneficiaries with information on how to safely and confidentially report SGBV/SEA if and when it occurs. NPA will use a community-based complaints and feedback mechanism to provide a platform for beneficiaries to voice any issues of concerns and feedback on the project activities. All NPA staff have been trained on PSEA and Safeguarding and are required to attend refresher sessions at least twice a year. In line with NPA's Safeguarding Policy on PSEA, NPA will inform the target communities about NPA's staff responsibilities and the expected code of conduct. At the start of the project, NPA will develop a plan on how information about PSEA will be disseminated. In all it's communication activities, NPA will re-emphasize that all the beneficiary entitlements are provided freely and that they should immediately report if and when they’re asked to do or provide anything in exchange of the project’s entitlements. NPA will work closely with community leaders and stakeholders and will sensitize them on how to report any SEA cases that they come across.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

NPA will maintain a flexible and adaptive approach throughout the project implementation to ensure that the project remains relevant in the target communities. Project adaptations and adjustments will be informed by beneficiaries’ feedback and will be done in consultation with the target communities and other relevant stakeholders. Feedback from beneficiaries will be reviewed on a weekly basis and discussed during project meetings.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

While NPA has recently conducted a country-wide operational, security and programmatic risk analysis, NPA will conduct a further in-depth risk analysis to ascertain the operational, programmatic and security related risks alongside the corresponding mitigation measures before commencement of the project activities. NPA has identified the following protection related risks and analysis.; 1. Risks that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services (Likelihood is low and impact is high). To mitigate this risk, NPA will ensure that registration is conducted and assistance provided as close to the beneficiaries as possible. NPA will work with existing community structures and local authorities to ensure that appropriate locations are identified for project activities so that beneficiaries do not walk for long distances for registration or to receive assistance. PMCs will assist in crowd control during project activities. Activities will be implemented in line with the seasonal calendar to ensure accessibility to project locations. Inputs will be distributed and trainings conducted prior to the rainy season during which some locations are either cut off or inaccessible. NPA will closely monitor the security situation at all field locations and make necessary adjustments to the project 2. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities (likelihood is low, impact is high). As a mitigation measure, NPA will adopt participatory, vulnerability and rights-based approaches throughout the project life-cycle from the onset of the project, consultations, identification, verification and registration of target beneficiaries. Under the guidance of NPA, the PMCs will identify and register the beneficiaries according to NPA’s vulnerability criteria for targeting food-insecure women/child/chronically ill-headed HHs, People with disabilities, widows, elderly, pregnant/lactating women, orphaned, Persons living with or at risk of HIV/AIDS, Survivors of GBV etc. This will ensure the prioritization of assistance towards those who need it most. 3. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms (likelihood is low, impact is medium). To mitigate this risk, NPA will use a community-based complaints and feedback mechanism to provide a platform for beneficiaries to voice any issues or concerns. NPA will strengthen community communication to ensure beneficiary and non-beneficiary communities are well informed about the project, beneficiary criteria and how to provide feedback or complains to NPA. This will be done through regular meetings with the target communities and where possible use of IEC materials to disseminate information in the target communities about the CRM. 4. Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources (likelihood is low, impact is high). NPA's mitigation measure for this risk entails developing and sensitizing the local authorities, PMCs on the beneficiary selection criteria. The beneficiary selection process will be monitored by NPA staff to ensure objectivity and impartiality. PDMs will be conducted to ascertain the objectivity of the beneficiary selection process and appropriate corrective measures will be implemented in case of any issues identified.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

NPA recognizes the essential role of women in improving HH food and nutrition security, which in South Sudan has become even more accentuated as the number of women-headed HHs increases as a result of the protracted conflict. For this reason, this project will focus as much as possible on women to ensure families, and above all women and children, reap the benefits. The project will put in place mechanisms to ensure that women, men, boys and girls equally participate and benefit from all interventions. Community mobilization, sensitization and implementation will be done through a gender lens and sensitivity by using the NPA gender checklist to reflect that women and men have different needs and priorities and are affected differently. To enhance the protection of women and girls during project implementation for instance, cash and livelihoods kits distributions shall consider the safety of women and ensure that they are not inadvertently exposed to additional risk. This will be done by ensuring that distributions are conducted in safe areas and during day time so that they do not have to return home after dark. Women will be encouraged to discuss issues related to the project and associated concerns. Monitoring of the project will ensure that perspectives of women and men are captured systematically. Gender and age-disaggregated data on project coverage and impact will be collected, analyzed and routinely reported on, and project activities monitored for improvements as well as beneficiary satisfaction for both women and men. The project has been designed to ensure that women and other disadvantaged members of the community benefit and participate in an equitable manner. In designing the project NPA, ensured that women’s views were also considered. NPA’s Gender Advisor evaluated the practical and strategic needs of women to ensure that the project takes into consideration women’s needs. The sensitization activities that will be on-going throughout the project will inform the communities on women’s rights and promote discussions on issues affecting women’s rights to participation, own income and ownership. The project will be implemented in ways that ensure that women’s protection needs are prioritized for example trainings involving women will be conducted in venues that are accessible and take into consideration the multiple roles and time-constraints that women face. In addition, safety audits and protection risk assessments will be conducted at the cash and livelihood inputs distribution locations. Furthermore, the project will ensure that women are well represented in all the committees and project activities. Gender equality and equity awareness campaigns will be mainstreamed during project implementation of all the planned project activities, for example by including sensitization campaigns on women’s rights and GBV throughout the project implementation

The cash transfers will improve the purchasing power of the target HHs thus enabling them to meet their food needs during the response period.
