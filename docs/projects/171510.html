Project Summary

Relief International’s (RI) proposed 2021 WASH intervention will deliver life-saving emergency WASH services to vulnerable communities across Gedo, Hiraan, Banadir, Galguduud, Mudug, and Lower Shabelle regions of Somalia.
Communities across Somalia continue to struggle with lack of access to safe water, adequate sanitation facilities and poor hygiene conditions. This has been further exacerbated in 2020 by the recurring drought and floods as well as the impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic. These combined factors continue to lead to competition for scarce water resources for human and animal consumption which in turn results in conflict and displacement.
In light of this context and to mitigate the poor water, sanitation and hygiene conditions in the target communities, RI will provide critical water services, restore access to sustainable water sources, improve access to faecal management services and sanitation infrastructure, including the construction and rehabilitation of latrines, and promote improved hygiene practices through mass media campaigns and distribution of water purification and hygiene kits to affected communities.
RI’s project will address the immediate needs of affected households with the provision of emergency water trucking and hygiene activities while laying the foundation for sustainable WASH solutions with the rehabilitation and construction of latrines and water points. Additionally, RI will establish simplified Fecal Sludge Management (FSM) systems to restore and maintain sanitation systems. RI will also reinforce improved sanitation and hygiene practices at household level by increasing access to water and sanitation services, and hygiene promotion.
To implement the intervention effectively, RI will collaborate with the WASH cluster and other partners and solicit feedback and input from the community on the activities, locations and their needs.
RI will ensure that community members can access WASH services and facilities in a safe and dignified manner and that protection concerns are considered in all WASH activities. For example, RI will remove any barriers inhibiting access and safety, particularly to women and children, ethnic minorities, displaced people, and people with disabilities. Further, RI will ensure that locations and access routes are safe and do not expose beneficiaries to any risks; that distributions are conducted during the day with gender-balanced teams and without overcrowding. WASH infrastructure will be made safe for children and women. RI recognizes that vulnerable groups within the community such as People With Disability(PWD), the elderly, female and child headed households, people from minority clans and IDPs do have additional barriers that they face in regard to access to safe and dignified WASH services and facilities. Therefore RI will be intention in consulting with these groups, identifying the barriers they face and addressing these barriers within the delivery of the project including targeting them for feedback such as post distribution monitoring, project review meetings among others.
Poor sanitation in RI’s target locations is a leading cause of acute diarrheal diseases (ADD) and malnutrition. RI’s WASH service delivery is integrated with health and nutrition interventions and will contribute to improving the overall health and nutrition outcomes of the affected communities. RI’s previous experience in the regions has shown a correlation between districts accounting for over 50% of the total incidence of ADD and poor nutrition outcomes, including Banadir (Dharkeynley and Heliwa), and Hiraan (Beletweyne), and areas where there is poor access to WASH services.
From January - September 2020, RI screened 37, 577 children aged 6 – 59 months and referred 11, 909 (32 %) for acute malnutrition. Over the same period, over 4336 PLW were screened at RI’s facilities, and over 3852 children under 5 suffered from acute watery diarrhea (AWD).

Project Activities

Relief International (RI) will implement the following activities in alignment with the WASH Cluster strategic objectives: Community planning and consultations including consultations with specific vulnerable groups such as People With Disabilities(PWD), IDP, the elderly, girls and women to ensure their safe and dignified access to WASH services and facilities is guaranteedTruck water to communities without safe access to clean water while ensuring accessibility to marginalized groups including PWD, elderly, minority clan among others; Promote improved hygiene practices through mass media campaigns, community dialogues, and household visits including use of various media that ensure inclusivity for diverse needs of PWD and the less educated and the elderly; Construct and rehabilitate strategic water points including making them disability and child friendly for use; Distribute hygiene, water purification, and menstrual management kits; Construct and rehabilitate latrines including ensuring PWD, children and teenage girls have safe and dignified access; Establish simple faecal management systems; Establish/support water management committees (WMCs) with representation from diverse groups represented in the community such as PWDs, minority clan, IDP, host community, ; Train water management committees (WMCs) on the maintenance of WASH infrastructure, including cost recovery, water utilization and access for diverse groups in the community including PWD, elderly, children among others; Identify and train community hygiene promoters, including members of diverse groups in the community; Develop emergency preparedness plans with WMCs to build resilience.RI acknowledges that PWD, the elderly, girls and women face extra barriers when it comes to access to WASH services and facilities. RI will ensure the participation of the community throughout the project in the following ways: Community members will be consulted through community meetings (involving community and religious leaders, women and youth groups) in the selection of project locations such as distribution points, water points and location of latrines. RI will particularly ensure women and children are consulted to ensure locations and access routes are appropriate and safe for them to access and facilities do not expose them to any protection risk. Throughout the project, community members will continue to be engaged through participatory monitoring activities (e.g. post-distribution monitoring and project review sessions) and will also be engaged through the Water Management Committees (WMCs). RI will encourage female participation in the WMCs. During distributions and monitoring activities, RI will seek to have gender-balanced staff to encourage female participants to access services and provide feedback. RI’s complaints and feedback mechanisms will be made accessible and available to all community members, including women and girls, ethnic minorities, people with disabilities and other vulnerable groups.

Mitigation Measures

1) Risk of violence: RI will mitigate protection risks for beneficiaries accessing WASH services and facilities by 1) distributing commodities in areas accessible to all groups and at times conducive to the most vulnerable beneficiaries, including IDPs, women and girls, ethnic minorities, and people with disabilities; 2) establishing water points in safe areas that all groups can access to avoid tensions; 3) ensuring water points and distributions are located in areas that do not require long travel times; and 4) Ensuring clan dynamics are considered when consulting communities and that voices from IDP and host communities are heard. 2) Risk of SEA: To mitigate this risk, RI will 1) Consult women, boys and girls at risk in the selection of locations; 2) Ensure latrines and other facilities are well lit, secure, gender-segregated and have lockable doors; 3) Distribute items with female staff present to create a sense of safety and confidence; 4) avoid long travel times for beneficiaries to access WASH services and facilities. 3. Risk of exclusion for marginalised groups: To mitigate this risk, RI will 1) actively seek out the input and feedback of marginalised groups, particularly women, children, ethnic minorities and people with disabilities in the selection of locations and throughout the project to receive their feedback/complaints; 2) Ensure that WASH facilities and infrastructure are accessible and safe for people with disabilities and in locations they can access; 3) Ensure that WASH facilities and infrastructure are safe for children and do not pose a protection concern (e.g. children cannot fall into latrines etc.)

The community will continue to use these techniques even after the project end. The rehabilitated and constructed weather resistant wells and latrines as well as water pipe connections will continue to benefit communities after end of the project.
