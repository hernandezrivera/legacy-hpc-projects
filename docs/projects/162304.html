1 - Needs

Gaps in Education in BAY States (Borno, Adamwa and Yube); As per JENA 2019 (Draft report)AccessSchool disruption: • Due to conflict, schools closure in Dikwa (40% of schools closed for an average of 36 weeks), Maiduguri (8% of schools closed for an average of 32 weeks), Gwoza (18% of schools closed for an average of 19 weeks), Konduga (16% of schools closed for an average of 19 weeks) and Magumeri LGAs (50% of schools closed for an average of 15 weeks).Attendance:• Students attendance is low in Borno as compared to Adamwa and Yobe States. • Attendance barriers indicates a need to focus on integrating education programming with livelihoods and protection, to enable families to be able to afford to send their children to school, as well as reduce the prevalence of children working and early marriage.Children with disability:• Schools across northeast Nigeria are not typically adapted for children with disabilities, with just 14% reporting having additional provisions, despite a majority (68%) hosting at least one child with special needs.Out of school children• Nigeria still has 10.5 million out-of-school children - the world’s highest number. Out of this 10.5 million, 60% are girls. • 60% of out of school children are in Northern States School Infrastructure In Borno – 11% need full recon., 28% need significant rehabilitation 37% minor rehabilitation, 19% minor rehabilitation. In Yobe, 58% of schools required significant rehabilitation or full rebuilding, compared to 47% in Adamawa Overcrowded classroomsthe number of students per functioning classroom is high: 129 in Borno, 128 in Yobe, and 63 in Adamawa. Within Borno, overcrowding was particularly extreme in Ngala (182), Jere (179), Maiduguri (155), Damboa (153) and Gwoza (140).WASH facilitiesWithin Borno, there are notably high student:latrine ratios in Jere (419), Gwoza (372), Maiduguri (290), Dikwa (278), Biu (288) and Bama LGAs (285). Schools typically did not have working handwashing facilities for latrines, nor water and soap available. This was particularly severe in Borno, where 87% of schools had no working hand washing facilities Teaching and Learning English was used or taught as a language in 97% of schools in Borno. Students were most commonly expected to transition to learning fully in English in grade 1 (26%), grade 4 (32%) or grade 6 (21%). This varied slightly between states, with the majority in Yobe transitioning in grade 4, and in Adamawa and Borno in either grade 1 or grade 6. Around half of schools (47%) reported that some teachers face difficulties communicating with children due to language issues. This most commonly affected around a quarter of teachers. A larger proportion of schools (62%) reported that children face challenges with learning due to language barriers, mostly commonly affecting around a quarter or a half of children.Teachers and volunteer teachersMore than 1 out of every 4 teachers in the affected area is a volunteer which speaks to the current lack of recruitment of qualified teachers (see below) and the potential impact on the quality of education provision Overall average student-to-teacher ratio is quite high when considering only registered teachers (1:55), especially in Borno (1:60). This drops significantly when the volunteer workforce is calculated as part of the ratio, with an overall average of 1:39. Most of schools’ report that 50-100% of their teacher workforce do not come to school regularly. Teachers training Safety of children Specific safety risks currently facing children in the area, the most-cited was that there are no risks. The other main responses risks were not directly conflict-related: “Crossing rivers/flooding” (22%) and “Heavy traffic/Crossing roads” (21%). Only 3% of headteachers mentioned ‘Conflict insecurity’ – although conflict-related insecurities, such as “Passing checkpoints” (12%) and “Mines/UXOs” (8%), did receive a slightly higher percentage

2 - Activities

Plan International has planned to: • Construct and/or rehabilitate 75 temporary learning spaces (TLSs)/classrooms in schools found in 24 particularly disadvantaged communities in Borno, Adamwa and Yube States. This will provide classrooms to children formerly studying under trees and in open fields with sever difficulties in the rainy season and to decongestant over-crowded classrooms in these areas. • Construct and/or improve WASH facilities in TLSs/ schools in the 24 target communities: the lack of latrines and WASH facilities has been identified as one of the major hindrances to quality and access to education as well as a significant challenge for the protection of girls in schools as detailed above. Plan International will therefore construct 75 blocks (one male and one female) sex-segregated toilets and conduct hygiene promotion campaigns in target communities in BAY states. This is a particularly timely intervention during the rainy season, where severe outbreaks of cholera are a common occurrence, forcing schools to be closed. • Provide in-service mentorship training to teachers and peer-to-peer education for students in the targeted schools: the quality of education is extremely poor in the targeted areas in 24 communities in three states. To strengthen the capacity of teachers to provide quality, learner-centred education, positive discipline, protection and psycho-social support to the traumatized children that come to their school, Plan International will have facilitated in-service mentorship training for 75 teachers (60% male and 40% female due to shortage of female teachers in the area) in the targeted schools. . Plan will work with students, teachers and SBMS to set up 75 children clubs with a membership of 20 children per club(10 boys and 10 girls) in each of the 24 targeted communities that will be supported by this project to facilitate peer-to-peer learning and to enhance the voice of students in the teaching and learning process. • Conduct gender-sensitive back-to-school campaigns: in addition to improving facilities and the quality of education through the activities detailed above, . Plan International will conduct back to school campaigns to increase school enrollment in the LGA, particularly of girls. A significant number of children are still out of school due to insecurity, poverty and lack of community awareness and prioritization of education and this project will improve access to education for children by targeting out of school children and encouraging them to come back to school.
