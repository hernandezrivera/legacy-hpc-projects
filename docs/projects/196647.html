Project Summary

Building on experience of project interventions in Syria since 2013,Oxfam continues to work with local authorities in responding to the vulnerable populations’ WASH needs in districts/subdistricts of Aleppo, Hama, Idlib,Rural Damascus,Dar’a &Deir Ezzor, based on assessments, severity of needs and in response to further emerging needs in NES and NWS.The subdistricts that will be targeted by this project have all been heavily affected by conflict and require WASH support.OXFAM conducted integrated WASH&EFSVL assessments from January 2022 to date covering up to 50 communities through group discussions and key informant in three governorates.This includes consultations with LNGOs, INGOs, secondary data and the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022.The project will focus during the years 2022&2023 on life-saving WASH interventions and support the resilience of existing WASH systems (water supply &sewer networks, wastewater treatment, solid waste management)&WASH in institutions, such as schools and healthcare facilities, ensuring coordinated and cohesive response strongly aligned to the WASH Sector. It is gender mainstreamed, with a response strategy overarched by a community engagement approach suitable to access and security challenges; implementation engages trusted community structures carefully screened private contractors, community-based volunteers and identified local partners, and strong collaboration with other actors.
The total estimated population, as per UN-OCHA datasets in these sub-districts, is over 3M people.OXFAM targets the timely response to emerging rapid onset emergency needs in hot spots& potential flashpoints, new returns,new displacements,affected by AWD, economic issues, and the current returned areas with persisting WASH needs. The target locations proposed are according to severity of humanitarian needs and number of populations in need which will be adapted to the evolving ground context, access and people's movement. Although Hama considered under severity scale 1, majority of AlKousor neighborhood suffers from water shortage& still rely on water trucking.Accordingly,Oxfam considered WASH needs there. The project aims to support lifesaving WASH interventions that are resilience-focused, sustainable, durable &cost-effective WASH systems using community engagement approach and mainstreamingCholeramitigation measures and response throughout all regular WASH activities: The objectives of the project are;1):support to water,sanitation/sewage&solid waste management systems to ensure regular services for affected people in Syria; 2):deliver humanitarian WASH supplies and services,&improve hygienic behavior and practices of most vulnerable people; 3) WASH coordination structures facilitated and enhanced;4) improve WASH facilities and services in institutions to minimize sub-standard WASH conditions of the most vulnerable people in Syria.The type of interventions strongly aligns with: SO1,SO2,SO3&SO4 which captures the sector efforts to respond and assist local water establishments in restoring WASH services to basic functionality for communities, schools&community health facilities.Oxfam will target all age groups for water and sanitation interventions. Hygiene interventions will target careers women,adolescent girls&school children. Accurate disaggregation of population data will be collected from relevant ministries and actors to ensure coordinated and relevant data and appropriate interventions during WASH assessments and interventions. Oxfam has unique MOUs with several ministries gaining trust,strong relations,&approval of interventions. Bi-monthly meetings at the MoWR allow insight and agreement for selection of prioritized possible activities and discussion of operational challenges.This project’s close alignment with the HRP in terms of outcomes, activities&methodology reflects Oxfam’s close engagement with the Sector,particularly through strategic discussions&information sharing throughout 2019&2022.

3 - Needs

The Syria crisis is now in its twelfth year of protracted crisis. The UN estimates that 14.6 million people need humanitarian assistance across the country, 9 % increase from the previous year. Macroeconomic conditions continued to deteriorate in 2022 and appear to be worsening further. The combined effects of currency depreciation, soaring prices, reduced fiscal revenue and increasing domestic debt and widespread losses in livelihoods have plunged additional segments of the population into humanitarian need. Climatic and human-caused shocks affecting natural resources, particularly water, have intensified. Erratic rainfall in combination with historically low water levels in the Euphrates River have not just reduced access to water for drinking and domestic use for over five million people, but also triggered substantial harvest and income losses, decreased hydroelectricity generation, an increase in water-borne diseases, and additional protection risks.On 10 September, the Syrian Ministry of Health (MoH) declared an outbreak of cholera in the Aleppo Governorate. Cholera spread to 7 Governorates. and confirmed cholera cases/acute watery diarrhea (AWD) cases have been reported from 14 governorates. which is an indicator of severe shortages of water throughout Syria. Euphrates levels continuing to decrease, drought-like conditions , much of the already vulnerable population of Syria is reliant on unsafe water sources, which leads to the spread of dangerous diseases.

4 - Implementation of activities

OXFAM’s emergency response integrated with resilience and protection activities (response is informed by the multi-sector protection analysis that Oxfam conducts early to identify protection risks that can be reduced by WASH actions),are:1)repair or rehabilitation of water systems to functioning level which includes water pipeline, high and ground storage tanks, pumping station and boreholes, rehabilitate existing electricity and diesel-powered water pumping systems with solar system due to high maintenance cost and lack of fuel supply; provision of communal and household water tanks;2)support to water quality assurance, o&m of water supply systems;3)repair, rehabilitation and operational support to sanitation/sewage which includes sewage pipe repair and unblocking, septage tank desludging and solid waste management systems;4)Distribution of essential emergency age- and gender-sensitive hygiene materials and WASH NFI, hygiene promotion and messaging on COVID19&cholera through community-based behaviour change campaign;5)support humanitarian emergency WASH facilities and services,6)market-based assistance for WASH services;7)rehabilitation or construction of separate toilets for male and female,develop community level communal water delivery plan for equitable share;8)support to WASH facilities and services in schools, health centers and child friendly spaces in coordination with Education and Protection sectors,9)support and capacity building of LWE WASH technical staff.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Due to access restrictions& limited funds, Oxfam cannot always adopt a blanket distribution, and at the same time is unable to directly identify beneficiaries eligible to NFI in alignment with its vulnerability/selection criteria, which generates a risk of selection bias, inclusion/exclusion errors and community perceptions of unfair eligibility criteria or discriminatory practices.Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High MM: community-based targeting and selection; Risk-informed selection criteria; multi-layer verification of the list of selected beneficiaries; Programme data is disaggregated per gender, disability, and status i.e., displaced, returnee, host, so red flags are identified if any. Oxfam, in 2023, will also review its conflict-sensitive framework to ensure building on the connectors and not exacerbating dividers throughout the project cycle. Risk: limited options of sites for distributions & bureaucratic/admin impediments that further condense the timeframe for implementation, could lead to compromising on essential safe& inclusive requirements e.g., access ramps for PWD or elderly, proximate distance from ben. residences, limited number of beneficiaries approaching the site per day (crowded setting can increase the risk of GBV and tensions). Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High MM: operational CFM, planning more in advance with considering external barriers, consider CVA modalities to improve dignified outcomes.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Exposure to safeguarding risks by women and girls’ especially the vulnerable and marginalized groupsSexual harassment, exploitation and abuse perpetrated by OXFAM staff, partners, and representativesViolence against women (VAW) and GBV: Changes in usual home dynamics and those brought by challenging gender norms and redistribution of care work, may lead to tension Risk mitigation: (1-Work with existing referral pathways through provision of technical assistance on the prevention of and management/handling of GBV & SEA. 2- Continuously review referral pathways especially during COVID - in case a service is closed or operating differently.3- Link with other INGO’s for community awareness raising on SEA, & disseminate messages 4- Establish feedback/reporting mechanisms and safeguarding focal points whose members should come from Oxfam, project partners and community representatives on safeguarding issues, and disseminate information on the existence of such to project participants. 5-Ensure there are community-based complaints and feedback mechanisms that are safe, accessible and functional. 6-Disseminating the information about safeguarding to the project participants. 7-Conduct or include in inception meetings/project activities on gender sensitivity or awareness raising and sensitization workshops/ learning sessions with project participants, local leaders especially on prevention of and protection of women from GBV, VAWC, SEA, shared household management, childcare&UCDW


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: tension develop between communities that benefit from WASH infrastructure rehabilitation and NFIs, and communities not reached by these interventions.Risk probability: High.Mitigation measures:1)selection of communities compromised access to WASH infrastructures and NFIs and who are using the resources of the neighbors2)coordination with WASH actors, local authorities,community leadersRisk: Men (elder male relatives) may resent women participation in hygiene promotion and menstrual hygiene sessions for defying norms that women stay at home and taking time away from care work,leading to domestic violence. Probability: High. Mitigation:1)develop allies among male community members to help in consultations about activity, 2)ensure that sessions are separate with men and women and held in private venues. Risk: Increase risks of GBV if toilets are not separated or no locks on the doors and windows in schools and communities. Probability:High. Mitigation:1)Consult females on location,design of toilets;2)ensure locks on the door,windows Risk: MoH and humanitarian actors fail to control cholera outbreak, becoming an epidemic. Mitigation:1) Close coordination with WASH,Health actors to identify and address main Cholera hotspot areas. Establish Cholera preparedness,prevention measures in current areas served,contingency to respond elsewhere.2)Coordinate with municipality and LWE to establish O&M plan3)Monitor AWD/Cholera trends and caseload in country to respond accordingly
