Objectives

Objective 1: Support to the Government of Bangladesh Camp-in-Charge Officers (CiC) in managing the camps and adjacent host communities to ensure equitable and safe access of refugees to standardized assistance and protection, against standards, coordinated emergency preparedness efforts and community engagement and representation. Objective 2: Holistically upgrade the settlement areas and immediate surroundings and their immediate surroundings, through community-participatory, evidence-based and mid-term planning, construction and maintenance works to develop safe, dignified, resilient and environmentally sustainable living conditions. Objective 3 Support informed humanitarian decision-making and synergized identification of needs, access to services and gaps among the refugees

Needs

In order to build an adequate and harmonized response across all camps, this project aims to strengthen and improve a wide range of Camp Management components including site management structures, access to services, community participation, site improvements that also take care of addressing vulnerability to natural hazards and natural resource management. In 2018 Camp Management functions are in place based on a crucial partnership between RRRC, UNHCR and Camp Management Support (CMS) agencies. In 2018, UNHCR supported CiCs by providing CCCM trainings, field monitoring, community engagement and service monitoring. As a result all of the camps have a functional camp coordination system in place; a pool of CiCs certified CCCM trainers, who are already training new recruited RRRC staff to be deployed in camps as well as other camp actors. Further this year in coordination with RRRC and CMS partners two nomination-consultation-election process were facilitated by UNHCR and its partner in two camps. As a result, two camp committees have been formed in these camps with 50 % female representation. Both camp committees are trained, participated in camp coordination meetings with different Sector groups, raise refugees’ concerns among other functions. A Community representation guideline has been submitted to RRRC for review (pending final approval) a Sectoral process in which UNHCR was heavily involved in. For 2019, there is a need to continue to reinforce CiCs capacities to strengthen the coordination and management in all 33 camps in line with community representation system. Alongside UNHCR shall focus its work on the 17 camps and Transit Centre that UNHCR is responsible for under the UNHCR/IOM agreement. All strategies and activities will be coordinated with the Site Management and Site Development Sector, other relevant stakeholders including refugee communities. Camp Management interventions shall be designed i) Ensuring that every refugee has access to services; ii) service monitoring iii) protection mainstreaming; iv) care and maintenance (small camp improvements) ; v)community participation and representation; vi) capacity building; vii) emergency preparedness and response; viii) information management and camp coordination. In 2018 all emergency disaster response and risk reduction interventions by UNHCR aimed to complement the physical site improvements in highly congested camp settings and focussed on communal facilities in areas prone to disaster. Coordinated interventions shall increase resilience, reduce exposure, improve preparedness and create early warning systems for adverse events To reduce the loss of life UNHCR shall continue to provide support for relocations either within their own community or to new land. For other areas and for those who cannot relocate, site development works shall be carried out to address community wide issues - improved access, channel clearing and strengthening and larger scale community works. The target for site development and community improvements is all 99,577 households within UNHCR’s area of operation however with a focus on those most at risk. In 2018, environmental impact assessment was done by UN agencies. The study reveals that pertinent actions are required to improve the environmental condition that includes massive reforestation, abatement of air and water pollution engaging both refugee and host community. Workshop with Asian Elephant Experts suggests holistic intervention is necessary to save the Asian Elephant and other wildlife that are threatened by direct and indirect impact of the refugee influx. Finally Information management plays an important role to maintain and reinforce the coordination system established in camps and allow stakeholder to make informed decisions. In 2018 UNHCR and its partner designed and produced number of information products including camp profiling and assessments to support camp coordination and camp management.

Activities or outputs

In close coordination with the Sector UNHCR will work together with partners to support the authorities to maintain and enhance a robust system of civilian administration in refugee settlements. UNHCR will support CiCs and CMS partners to reinforce channels of community participation and establish a representation system based on legitimacy, accountability, participation and transparency.Activities shall enhance equitable access of refugees to relevant services and protection. UNHCR will support the sustainability of Camp Management structures with the aim of enabling the Government appointed CiCs to effectively govern the camps through logistical, capacity building and technical support. In addition implementing partners shall be trained and funded to assist the CICs in performing their roles and supporting with coordination at the camp level based on service monitoring and protection mainstreaming. UNHCR shall work with the CBP Community Outreach Members in order to mobilize refugees to participate in leadership and decision-making structures. The mitigation of risks and hazards of GBV will be considered in activities including in the maintenance/improvements of sites (such as in the placement of solar lights) and in engaging with community and consulting with the GBV Sub Sector Coordination.UNHCR will ensure information management activities for Camp Coordination and Camp Management in collaboration with relevant partners and government counterparts for evidence based planning, monitoring and reporting.UNHCR will work to improve access and living conditions in refugee sites and adjacent host communities taking a holistic and integrated approach. These activities will enable intensive maintenance work to maintain access and immediately address infrastructure failure due the rain wind, landslides and flooding.Small scale labour-based community works will be instigated across all areas of operation by partners and shall inject liquidity into the refugee community, enhancing resilience and dignity, as well as enabling more owner-driven household level improvements. UNHCR will directly implement site improvements, with a focus on larger scale works. UNHCR will focus on four primary areas of intervention: drainage, access, stabilisation and community engagement. These works coupled with other activities under this project will reduce vulnerability to natural hazards. Interventions with the Site Maintenance Engineering Project (SMEP) the joint WFP, IOM and UNHCR project will continue. Community-based, robust Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) plans that enhance resilience and reduce the effects of the monsoon season and cyclone shall. Community based environmental and biodiversity awareness will continue across the camp to address deforestation and elephant conflict that increased with the rapid influx of refugees and threatened national conservation efforts. UNHCR plans to work closely with the Forest Department to strengthen the supply chain of planting material required for soil stabilization and environmental restoration. To abate water pollution, UNHCR intends to rehabilitate an important stream that runs through the camp by using mainly biological options. To address community lighting needs and enhance security, UNHCR and operational partners will install several thousand solar street lights. The information management activities through a specialized information management partner includes but is not limited to needs assessments, camp profiling, services mapping and monitoring, facility mapping, contact directories, training to partners and relevant stakeholders, risk and vulnerability analysis, etc. In addition, UNHCR will collaborate with relevant sector and partners on harmonization of data, systems and process.
