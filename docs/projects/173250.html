Project Summary

The project aims to continue strengthening and improving a wide range of Camp Management components including; maintaining and improving site management facilities, ensuring continuous access to services, promoting community participation, enhancing site improvements and site planning that address vulnerability to natural hazards and natural resource management.

1 - Identified Needs

From the onset of the response, Camp Management functions were put in place in partnership with the RRRC, UNHCR and Camp Management partners. UNHCR has been focusing on consolidating these functions in camps to the extent possible to enhance refugee participation and representation at camp level as well as strengthening the RRRC capacity both in Cox’s Bazar and at the camp level. Although there has been significant progress in service delivery and assistance since 2018, the Covid-19 pandemic has adversely affected the overall response given the limited access to camps for service providers. With the Government’s lifting of restrictions to access camps, there will be need to continue reinforcing RRRC, CiCs and site management capacities to respond, strengthen the camp level coordination including ensuring that all sectors present at camp level have decision making powers, increase refugee community representation and continue to ensure strong Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) response. In 2020, DRR works were affected by restrictions to camp access. 2021 will require proper planning and to improve camps conditions, reduce the risk of landslide and flooding. Further, UNHCR will continue working in a coordinated manner to ensure interventions shall increase resilience, reduce exposure, improve preparedness and create early warning systems for adverse events. UNHCR previously co-contributed towards the storm surge risk analysis that shall continue to better map coastal inundation risk. UNHCR has invested in making communities aware of risks and to help mitigate disasters. Additionally, water safety trainings shall continue, placing the community at the center of the response. Site development works will continue to be implemented to address community-wide issues including; improved access, channel clearing and strengthening, slope stabilization and larger scale community works. Heavier infrastructure constructions such as roads, drains and buildings for security actors and service providers will remain a significant part of UNHCR’s activities. This will be done through shelter partners, contractors and the continued collaboration in the Site Maintenance and Engineering Project (SMEP). Rohingya refugees are accommodated in settlements located on a vast track of forest land which belongs to the Forest Department. Studies show that slope failure resulting from vegetation clearance is causing erosion and which leads to both soil and environmental degradation. As part of its comprehensive response, UNHCR will work on environmental restoration, disaster risk reduction (DRR) and wildlife conflict-management to maintain refugees’ protection space.The Energy and Environment Technical Working Group (EETWG), co-chaired by UNHCR, will advocate for the Government and relevant actors to bolster environment and conservation programmes beyond the pre-emergency conditions. Stream rehabilitation supporting public health and reforestation are core elements of environmental plans. An extensive conservation programme in the area of human elephant contact will also continue. UNHCR will also work on mainstreaming environment-sensitive approaches across sectors, building on the “Environmental Sensitive Camp Protocol” developed in 2019-2020. This approach includes internal sector discussion and partnership collaboration from the on-going EETWG and includes community consultations with refugees from the onset. Components covered include climate change, stream rehabilitation, water conservation, watershed management, DRR, biological land stabilization, humanitarian conservation platform activities (elephants, snake and mobilization of communities), LPG assessment, and environmental education and advocacy. All activities should be in compliance with environmental law and regulations of the Government.

2 - Activities or outputs

Facilitating camp coordination mechanisms with Camp in Charge (CiCs), Site Management and Support (SMS) and other stakeholders for smooth operations of the camp and enhance access of services to the refugees.Continuous implementation of the Capacity Sharing Initiative through multi-sector trainings for CiC staff to capacitate and strengthen RRRC in Site Management functions.Building on existing community structures, enhance community participation in the camps with age, gender and diversity lens, including promoting camp and block representation committees in liaison with RRRC, respective camp in charge, community-based protection, site management partners and relevant stakeholders.Enhance complain response and feedback mechanism (CRFM) in camps for refugees to concerns and complains through various channels with camp management agencies ensuring they address or refer the raised issues and close the feedback loop within a diligent time frame.Ensuring information management activities in camps including but are not limited to; needs assessments, camp profiling, services mapping and monitoring, facility mapping, contact directories, training to relevant stakeholders, risk and vulnerability analysis, monsoon incident reporting, response tracking, etc.Site improvements and camp care and maintenance works to improve access and address infrastructure damages.Implementation of labour based-based community works to the extent possible across all areas of operation to enhance resilience and dignitySite Development works including DRR activities; drainage repairs, access improvement through road and pathway repairs and constructions, slope stabilisation to reduce vulnerability to natural hazards and collaboration with joint UN projects like SMEP shall for large scale works.Emergency preparedness to enhance resilience and reduce the effects of the monsoon seasons and cyclone shall continue by capacitating and mobilizing SUVs and CPP volunteers in camps and host communities. Further; ensuring multi-hazard camp plans to mitigate extreme weather effects and other related emergencies that emerge in camps including fire-preparedness amongst others. Community based environmental and biodiversity awareness across the camps to address environmental impact caused by the large camp settlementStrengthen the supply chain of planting materials required for soil stabilization and environmental restoration in collaboration with forest department. Rehabilitation of water ways to abate water pollution. Conservation activities shall be furthered to include “wildlife human contact” with rescue and relief programmes.Installation of camp solar street lights.
