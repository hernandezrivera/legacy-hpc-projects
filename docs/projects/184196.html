Project Summary

In 2021, Somalia continued to experience increased political tensions and violence, attributable to the delayed electoral process and power struggles for future leadership. In southern and central Somalia, conflict and insecurity increased resulting in displacement and consequently leading to disruptions to access to essential humanitarian services. Competition for natural resources often generated conflicts among different communities. The global Hunger index for 2021 placed Somalia among the worst affected countries. The country’s international poverty rate is projected to remain at 71% and this trend is expected to continue in 2022 and 2023. Somalia’s economy is rebuilding from the “triple shock” of 2020: the COVID-19 pandemic, extreme flooding, and locust infestation.
The protracted humanitarian crisis in Somalia is now in its third decade. In 2022, 7.7 million people will be in severe need of humanitarian assistance, with women and children constituting 16% (1.2M) and 64% (4.9M) respectively. A total of 6.56 million people will require lifesaving essential health services. Conflict and insecurity remain key drivers of internal displacements resulting in 2.2 M IDPs. Banadir region leads with the highest number of IDPs. A total of 1.2 M People Living With Disabilities (PLWDs) will be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2022. Somalia is experiencing some of the worst health outcome indicators in the world: average life expectancy is estimated to be 51.5 years. It is among the countries with the highest rankings in infant and child mortality rates of 76.6 and 121.5 /1,000 live births respectively. The maternal mortality rate is estimated at 692 deaths /100,000 live births. The fertility rate is an average of 6.9 children per woman which is also among the highest in the world. Due to a lack of health services and a skilled health care workforce, most of the births (79%) are home-delivered without skilled assistance. Overall, 99% of Somali women have undergone female genital mutilation, with 64% of these having suffered the Pharaonic type. Only 11% of children aged 12-23 months are fully vaccinated. In urban areas around 19% of children have received all basic vaccinations, while among children in nomadic areas only less than 1% are fully vaccinated. Measles vaccination coverage remains low (23%) which led to outbreaks in 2021 - thus the need to increase vaccination coverage remains a priority for 2022. Uptake of other vaccines has not performed well; according to SHDS 2020, only 37% received BCG, 12% received 3rd pentavalent vaccination dose and 26% received 3rd dose of polio amidst the fight to eradicate polio from the surface of the earth.
In Somalia, the primary drivers to excess mortality remain malnutrition and disease outbreaks such as (COVID-19, AWD/cholera, measles, malaria) as well as violence, non-communicable disease including complications of pregnancy and chronic disease. These are compounded by displacement due to conflict and natural disasters and a lack of preventive and curative healthcare. Since the onset of COVID-19 in March 2020 to October 2021 the country has confirmed a total of 19,980 cases, with a case fatality rate of 5.6%. Vaccine coverage has been very low - below 2% by the end of September 2021 - with some of this due to vaccine hesitancy.
The absence of healthcare services continues to affect populations living in hard-to-reach districts, some of which are under the control of non-state armed groups. Medair and the local implementing partners support health facilities along the coastal villages of Badhaadhe district as well as in Afgooye to lessen the burden of healthcare needs in these districts. Healthcare needs in the mainland of Badhaadhe district remain unaddressed. The COVID-19 pandemic impacted women and girls with increased levels of conflict-related sexual violence while the availability of health services for SBGV survivors remains extremely limited.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Minority clans/Ethnic groups are not included in consultations and/ or are excluded from the humanitarian services. Medair will ensure that internally displaced persons from minority groups will systematically participate through representatives of marginalized IDP communities in consultation meetings with local leaders during assessments to understand their needs. Consultation will involve the host and the displaced persons. Nutrition facilities will be open seven days a week for 8 hours and will ensure equal and direct access for both the IDPs and the host communities. Contacts with the local leaders from the IDP marginalized communities will be established outside the camp if necessary. A hotline number will be provided for the community to provide feedback and share concerns. Nutrition staff and committees will include representatives from the minorities and ethnic groups. Exposure of women and girls to sexual evidence including sexual exploitation and abuse. Medair will provide training for staff on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse/ protection mainstreaming and ensure they sign a code of conduct including specific clauses on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse as a commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries, including the confidentiality of patients. Medair has nominated a safeguarding officer to ensure continued sensitization, awareness and follow-up on any reports of sexual exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries. Care Group volunteers and health/nutrition workers will identify passive cases in the community and ensure they are reported as well as continuing to deliver behaviour change communication messages on sexual violence and exploitation. Psychosocial support and first aid will be provided to the victims and staff trained on clear referral pathways in coordination with the gender-based violence actors in the villages. Medair recognizes women and children are among the most vulnerable groups during conflicts and will ensure they are aware of their rights for seeking treatment whilst seeking to address stigma. Risk of exposure to violence as a result of distance to and location from humanitarian assistance and services or indiscriminate attack from armed actors Medair carries out security assessment where information is gathered from the local communities and the international security watch groups; information is analysed for threats and risks, prior to project set-up and during implementation. Safe central locations and access routes will be identified for the beneficiaries far from the military installations, hotspots roads with landmines and IEDs or violence. Services will be provided as close as possible to the beneficiaries, which reduces the risks to beneficiaries who may have to travel to access services. The Care Group Model and iCCM further optimize protection by bringing services to the household level in a low-key pattern that can continue to function during periods of tension. Medair will support a taxi referral network to ensure that beneficiaries with complicated cases safely reach the appropriate level of care (within the constraints of the security context). Medair recognizes women and children are among the most vulnerable groups during conflicts. Through the implementation of this project, Medair will ensure services are accessible to both the host and the displaced populations. The Community networks will provide assistance to women in the community through dissemination of behavior change communication. Medair has incorporated a mental health and psychosocial support component to help the targeted populations deal with trauma following a crisis.
