Project Summary

This project addresses the urgent life-saving water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) needs of the most vulnerable peoples in Libya during the COVID-19 pandemic. The project targets detained or stranded migrants in detention centers (DCs), migrants in transit centers (TCs) and Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) that have integrated into urban areas including, but not limited to, Tripoli, Misrata, Zuwara, Alazzawaya, Sebha and Benghazi. Gender and protection will be mainstreamed throughout the project, besides that GBV risks will be mitigated through enhancement of participation of women and girls by for instance site selection, determining suitable protective location and design for latrines within DCs and TCs, being recipients and providing feedback on the content of the of hygiene -and dignity kits. IOM in doing so adheres to the do no harm principle. The objective and targeting approach are coherent with the Humanitarian Response Plan’s (HRP) strategic objectives of the WASH Sector for 2021.
In addition, this project will enhance the capacity of the local/national government partners and international/national non-government organizations (I/NGOs) with training sessions on emergency planning preparedness, WASH in emergency settings and infection prevention and control (IPC). IOM will adopt an integrated WASH approach connecting access to water, sanitation aspects more clearly with hygiene promotion and awareness sessions. Furthermore, awareness raising on hygiene promotion, risk communication and community engagement (RCCE) on COVID-19 preparedness and mitigation will be a key part of the project approach, to reduce health hazards such as the COVID-19 pandemic. Thereto, all distribution and training activities will follow national and international guidelines on COVID-19 prevention measures, such as the observation of physical distancing measures.

1 - Needs

Despite the prospects for a durable peace following the signing of the ceasefire in October 2020, the situation in Libya remains volatile, as a result of years of protracted conflict and political and economic instability. A lack of repair and maintenance throughout the years, as well as attacks and force shutdowns on water infrastructure has increased the pressure on the already degraded water networks. The summer months of 2020 were characterized by frequent power cuts, negatively impacting the water supply, particularly in very populated areas in western Libya. The weak water system negatively impacted the sanitation and wastewater management systems in Libya. Furthermore, the effects of the COVID-19 pandemic and the preventive measures and curfews have further impacted the precarious socio-economic situation, especially for the most vulnerable. In addition to the effect of COVID-19 on people’s ability to work, the prices of many essential items have increased, making it more difficult for people to afford to purchase essential hygiene equipment and follow hygiene practices. With the spreading of the COVID-19 virus in Libya, access to clean water, hygiene and sanitation services has become even more crucial, to reduce the risk of COVID-19 infection as well as other water borne diseases and illnesses. This is particularly important in collective areas such as shelters, schools, health facilities and detention centers, where increased awareness raising and communication of key hygiene messages to ensure improved hygiene behaviors should be ensured to all people.According to the Libya 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), in 2020, nearly 438,000 people are estimated to need access to safe water, hygiene and sanitation services. This includes 155,000 displaced people, 124,000 non-displaced Libyans and 113,000 migrants. This is an 80 per cent increase, compared to 2019, with the most significant increases among migrants, returnees and non-displaced Libyans.The cities where WASH needs are particularly high are Alkufra, Benghazi, Ejdabia, Misrata, Murzuq, Sebha and Tripoli. While the totality of the population has expressed emergency needs in relation to WASH, IDPs and migrants are particularly vulnerable. Displaced people are more likely to be living in sub-standard accommodation where access to WASH services is more difficult. About 39 per cent of all displaced people need WASH assistance. For displaced people, the highest number of those in need is in Tripoli. In total, 21 per cent of all migrants need WASH assistance, with the highest number of those in Tripoli. Migrants are in normal circumstances already living in poorer conditions, compared to Libyans. The situation is even more complicated for those in detention centers, due to the often-overcrowded living conditions, the closure of some DCs resulting in longer stay in TCs. A lack of maintenance, upkeep results often in un-sufficient access to drinking water and sanitation services based on international humanitarian standards. In 2020, on average 3,000 migrants were detained in government-held detention centers in Libya at any given time. Next to sanitation there is a great identified need to improve the access to enough drinking water and rehabilitation of water treatment facilities to ensure the quality of it via desalination units which are either missing or malfunctioning. In terms of wastewater management, often leakage has been observed due to old pipe-network which shows cracks or no connection with the drainage. In the dry heat of summer water, sanitation and hygiene conditions can pose a severe risk to public health. In addition, not enough personal protective equipment (PPE) is available to the most vulnerable groups, making the conditions precarious at best. Continued and high need for hygiene kits remain among in DCs and disembarkation points (DP), as well as for Libyan populations during internal displacement.

2 - Activities

Activities include: • Improving access to safe water through rehabilitation of targeted wells in remote communities. Water issues in DCs and TCs are addressed and solarized water systems will be piloted. IOM ensures maintenance and easy repairs can be done by beneficiaries and considers improvement of quality of water through for instance desalination. IOM will also provide in emergency situations, water through water trucking, for example, at disembarkation points.• Rehabilitation of WASH infrastructure in four schools serving 1,800 IDPs and migrants. This may include improving sanitation facilities, handwashing stations inclusive of soap or drinking water facilities. As part of the handover an operation and maintenance plan should be developed with a dedicated focal point to be held responsible. • Hygiene promotion/ provision of non-food items (NFI): there remains a need for this activity therefore in total 3,700 migrants will be targeted, reaching solemnly those most remote and underserved. • Solid waste management: IOM sees this as part of improving sanitation facilities in for instance DCs, TCs and other priority facilities. Identified repair work can consist of re-connecting, up-grading of pipe-network with the sewage system• COVID-19 preparedness and response activities include provision of PPE for migrants mainly but also reaches first line responders. IOM ensures that facilities we rehabilitate will be disinfected and cleaned on a regular basis. For the wider public we focus on disseminating context-appropriate COVID-19 messages in 3 languages.• Capacity building of WASH sector actors: IOM plans to undertake training of government officials. The specific content of the sessions depends on identified capacity gaps and on topics that have not yet been addressed by sector’s partners.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

4.3 - IOM tries to reduce the tension within communities during the intervention as the selection of the project is done in close coordination with communities and local authorities and when we handover the project we have a launching event with inhabitants of the area and concerned people. During the implementation phase regular meetings are held with local authorities to increase the understanding of progress and discuss challenges. 4.4 - IOM tries to integrate the COVID-19 risks in its programming from the assessment phase throughout the program cycle. In addition, IOM internally works closely with the Health unit within IOM to understand the impact of rehabilitation of WASH facilities in DCs. Engineers are also trained on COVID-19 preparedness aspects and follow WHO guidelines to ensure this risk is being mitigated. 4.7 - Various programs implemented by IOM will continue to advocate for the alternatives to detention and improving living conditions in urban areas where migrants live. IOM remains in proactive communication with local authorities, management of DCs and other key stakeholders on dire WASH conditions in DC’s and will conduct technical assessment to identify the most immediate needs. IOM tries to reduce the sufferings of the migrants in DCs and at DPs, while fully focusing on the protection needs of the vulnerable migrants, especially women and children who are often overlooked. 4.8 - IOM will assess the possibilities of making WASH Infrastructure more sustainable, by piloting solarized water system. IOM will use construction material, design and BOQs that include durable high-quality material. IOM will also pay special attention to ensure safe and dignified access of all sex and age groups and will consistently consider mitigation of protection risks, including of GBV. 4.10 - While directly implementing through female and male staff various activities to address migrant needs, IOM will mainstream protection as an integral part of its overall intervention and identify needs of women and children. IOM will also pay special attention to ensure safe and dignified access of all sex and age groups and will consistently consider mitigation of protection risks, including of GBV. 4.11 - IOM will look at gender aspects and verify the needs of persons with disabilities when it comes to the rehabilitation of schools to ensure an inclusive approach is being used. 4.20 - IOM will liaise with local authorities and other coordinating humanitarian actors to understand which project areas are prone to mines. IOM will include advocacy messages on the risk of mines when working on IDPs willing to return. 4.21 - For WASH infrastructure IOM will use a transparent tendering procedure based on a SOP with fixed period of advertisements and applications processing, to be able to award contracts based on quality standards as localized as possible. This will avoid disruption of local markets.
