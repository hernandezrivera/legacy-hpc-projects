Project Summary

The protracted crisis in Syria, further compounded by the aftermath of the February earthquakes, continues to negatively impact the education system. There has been a 4 per cent increase in the PiN, compared to last year. This emphasizes the urgent need for emergency education services to support up to 7.246 million school-aged children and teachers, including Children with Disabilities (CwD). The PiN comprises 6.9 million children 5 – 17 years (46 per cent girls, 54 per cent boys) and includes 230,000 education personnel, with (48 per cent female and 52 per cent male). The PiN consists of 10 per cent of CwD, highlighting the need for specialized education services.
Furthermore, there are more than 2.4549 million Out of School Children (OoSC) (46 per cent girls, 54 per cent boys) facing gendered protection risks such as child marriage and consequent early pregnancies. Additionally, access to accreditation, including exams, remains a need.
The education infrastructure was already insufficient before the earthquake. An estimated 3,700 schools need rehabilitation/repairs.
In this Proposal, GAN will conduct light rehabilitation on the infrastructure of 28 schools to establish a secure learning environment for accommodating 16,185 children (7670 boys & 8,515 girls), including 250 children with disabilities (CWDs). This will facilitate access to quality inclusive education and essential child protection (CP) services in five sub-districts of Aleppo and Idleb governorates. These selected sub-districts are classified with high severity levels, ranging from 3 to 5.
Through this project, GAN aims to provide comprehensive educational support to 16,185 children (7670 boys & 8,515 girls), including 250 children with disabilities, across 28 schools in Aleppo and Idleb governorates. This includes implementing the SIG curriculum during the second semester of the 2023-2024 academic year and continuing for two additional academic years (2024-2025, 2025-2026). Additionally, GAN will establish contracts with 515 formal teachers and educational staff, covering their incentives. Running costs for the schools, such as cleaning materials, stationery, and water tracking, will also be provided. Transportation assistance will be extended to 250 children with disabilities. Moreover, GAN will procure and distribute stationery kits, including school bags and stationery, to all 16,185 children enrolled in the 28 schools.
In addition to the previously mentioned activities, GAN will also print and distribute textbooks to all 16,185 enrolled children, conduct safety and evacuation training for teachers and students, provide professional development sessions for staff focusing on topics like Psychosocial First Aid (PFA) and integrating children with disabilities, procure and distribute teaching materials for all schools, organize 28 back-to-learning campaigns, and offer winterization support to all 28 schools from January 1, 2024, to March 31, 2024, and from October 1, 2025, to December 31, 2025.
As part of the project, GAN will hire 63 remedial teachers (3 per school) to conduct one cycle of remedial classes over three months, from October to December 2024 and October to December 2025. These classes will use the Adopted Syrian curriculum and target 3000 students (1500 boys and 1500 girls) enrolled in formal education who scored less than 50% in their final exams in English, Arabic, and Math for the academic years 2023-2024 and 2024-2025. These students faced irregular attendance due to interruptions and displacement caused by earthquakes in 28 schools.
Additionally, GAN will recruit 28 catch-up teachers (1 per school) to provide one cycle of catch-up classes over three months to 500 out-of-school children (250 boys and 250 girls) using the curriculum (B) during the summer months, from May to July 2024 and 2025. GAN will print and distribute 500 copies of the curriculum (B) to children participating in catch-up classes.
