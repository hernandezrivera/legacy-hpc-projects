Project Summary

As part of its response to the Beirut Explosion, WVL will aim to support affected communities' food security through the distribution of food parcels to households affected by the explosion. In kind food distributions will be seen as a temporary solution to meet the needs of the most vulnerable population in a context of fluctuating prices, however cash modality will be prioritized when markets stabilize and the gap between the official and unofficial exchange rates shrinks. WVL is also exploring a modality to continue supporting cash distributions in this fluctuating environment through the distribution of multipurpose cash assistance to vulnerable households as a top up to cover diverse needs other than food and rehabilitation of their shelter.
WVL will aim to reach 11,000 households (44,000 individuals) and 500 businesses in Achrafieh, Medawar (Gemmayze, Mar Mkhayel, karantina), Rmeil (including Geitawi), Bourj Hammoud, Sin El Fil, Mazraa, Basta, Khandaa al Ghamee and Bachoura. WVL will partner with local organizations when possible or conduct direct distributions as needed.

1 - Needs

The explosion caused significant damage to the Beirut Port Silos, which have total grain storage capacity of 120,000 tonnes. In addition to damaging the country’s national wheat silos, the blast also damaged ships and containers that were loading off food products to the country. Nonetheless, while food stocks are reported as relatively low, no food shortages are expected occur as stocks of food are forecasted to be enough to cover the needs for two months. The threshold for food security being three months of stock however, the country is classified as food insecure . The challenge will be at the household level where the ability to afford food may impact quantity and diversity of food consumption. Many people had already exhausted savings and been forced to decrease their food intake, especially of expensive items, before the blast; coping capacities are low . According to initial sector assessments most markets are functioning with some exceptions, such as the food market in the neighborhood of Karantina which was destroyed. Food prices have been rising steadily due to the economic crisis but no evidence of increasing prices after 4 August have emerged. There is still a risk that prices continue to raise amidst the ongoing economic and political crisis, supply chain disruptions and the loss of up to 85% of the country’s cereal reserves . The food security of the poorest households and of those who had just enough to provide for their families will be increasingly threatened. Half of the Lebanese surveyed in June by WFP were already worrying about a lack of food, while humanitarian programming started including food parcels in October 2019 to address underlying food security concerns. According to WVL's assessment as part of its response to the Beirut Explosion, 77% of surveyed individuals in affected areas noted that they were in need of basic assistance with food assistance highlighted as their main need (72.5%) in addition to cash assistance (58%). Affected communities also highlighted shelter repairs and rehabilitation as one of their priority needs. Before the port explosion, livelihoods in the capital were deteriorating. Rising unemployment and salary reductions put households under financial stress. With inflation rising sharply for months before the explosion, more and more residents exhausted their savings and thousands of people fell below the poverty line. The latest data before the blast showed that 45% of Lebanese were considered poor . 78% of the responders surveyed by the LRC after the blast reported having no savings and 37% reported no income . Businesses and shops were also destroyed or severely damaged by the explosion, including hotels, bars, cafes and restaurants in proximity of the port. Damages to these businesses put at risk the livelihoods of many business owners and workers should these businesses decide not to open again due to the owners’ inability to conduct needed rehabilitation to their shops.

2 - Activities

Delivery of food rations parcelsCash based assistance to affected familiesSupport to micro-businesses affected by the explosion In response to the current economic situation in Lebanon, characterized by continuous variations of local currency exchange rates and fluctuations of the purchase power, coupled with the temporary unavailability of certain products in local markets located nearby affected households, the project will aim at the distribution of the food parcels to vulnerable households affected by the explosion. WV food parcels are designed in alignment with the food security standards and guidance, covering most of the energy/caloric needs for a household of five members over 1 month. The diversity of the food parcel is designed to align with the culture of targeted beneficiaries and would allow them to cook the traditional meals. WV has already pre-identified vulnerable households in some of the districts affected by the blast, under recent programmes, based on strong selection criteria and scoring mechanism in alignment with the vulnerability criteria set by the food security sector. COVID-19 prevention will be maintained for all distribution including use of PPE material and social distancing. Specific concerns for infant feeding and breastfeeding for nursing mothers will be addressed by WV to tackle the misconceptions that might be present in mothers during the aftermath of this crisis. Some of the concerns might be based in experience, and some might be deeply held but mistaken beliefs. E.g. “Stress prevents mothers from producing milk”, “The mother thinks she is not producing enough milk to feed her baby.” Breastfeeding counselling for mothers in ordinary circumstances can prevent and overcome most difficulties. WV will ensure the dissemination of informational material to households with <3-year-old children to demystify such misconceptions and Health organisations with access to the affected areas who have community-based health teams will be invited to hold such community interventions in households with breastfeeding mothers. WV aims to distribute multipurpose cash assistance to vulnerable households as a top up to cover diverse needs other than food and rehabilitation of their shelter. This cash assistance will be in line with the guidance of the basic assistance working group (at 400,000 LBP per month) and covers urgent needs that allow survival with dignity and prevent negative coping mechanisms households are urged to apply due to reduction or loss of income and depletion of reserves. Cash transfer will be made through WVL’s identified cash service provider on monthly basis or a one off for targeted families. In kind food distributions will be seen as a temporary solution to meet the needs of the most vulnerable population in a context of fluctuating prices, however cash modality will be prioritized when markets stabilize and the gap between the official and unofficial exchange rates shrinks. WV will also aims to support microbusinesses in rehabilitation of their physical assets to be able to resume works and income. Microbusinesses are identified through direct survey and referral where their damaged assets are inspected by experts and estimated cost of their repair or replacement. Supporting struggling micro-businesses would allow for restoration of their livelihoods as well as save the livelihoods of their employees. WV will transfer cash to beneficiaries through identified service provider as per a signed MoU including phased payments and milestones. An advance payment is first disbursed to beneficiary microbusiness and followed by final payment when agreed rehab/renewal is concluded. WV cash assistance are designed to cover physical assets replacement or repair cost without putting more financial pressure on the household. Cash assistance will be coupled with quick embedded financial education session to ensure the good use and decision of benefiting business owners.
