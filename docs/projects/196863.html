Project Summary

The Integrated Response for Resilience Programme aims at providing a comprehensive response through Early Recovery support in 80 communities impacted by the crisis. Communities, particularly those most dramatically affected – and under additional stress because of IDPs returns and other displacements - need assistance in reinforcing their resilience to address these shocks as well as the consequences of the protracted crisis, and to address their needs on seriously depleted infrastructures and service capacities.
The essence of the Programme is about providing Syrian communities with – still limited - support to regain control over their basic needs (livelihoods, access to essential services) and reduce their dependency on humanitarian assistance. Prioritization of locations, is the outcome of a context analysis and needs assessment conducted on more than 5,000
Syrian communities, based on the following three criteria:
1) Hard to Reach Areas
2) Areas of major IDPs presence and IDPs returns
3) Highest scoring in the Severity Scale
The integrated response consists of the following components: strengthening of social services-related capacities (access and coverage), livelihoods creation and rural recovery, PWDs support, social cohesion/community safety, reducing gender inequality and youth marginality, women empowerment, tensions reduction and rehabilitation of basic infrastructure and public services.
The program is built in a modular manner both in terms of geographical and sectoral/thematic interventions and foresees the allotment of average 1 Million US$ per community. Hence, donors willing to support this approach will be able to pre-select certain location(s) and/or area(s) of intervention of their preference, within the 200 prioritized communities.
The programme relates directly to the sector objectives:
1. Living conditions
2. Livelihoods
3. Access to social services
4. Access to productive and market infrastructure
5.social protection
6. Strengthening of local public services delivery capacity
7. Reinforcing opportunities and capacities for civic engagement

3 - Needs

The protracted crisis in Syria has depleted capacities at all levels to address basic needs. In 2021, 13.4 million people are estimated to require humanitarian and protection assistance, including 6.08 million children. This represents a 25% increase compared to 2020. The scale, severity and complexity of humanitarian needs have also increased due to the economic downturn, the rising cost of commodities, the devaluation of local currency; this all compounded by the impact of COVID-19. As per the UN Inter-Agency 2020 Covid-19 Socio-Economic Impact Assessment (SEIA), before the pandemic, 41.9% of HHs could cover their expenses, while currently, only 9.7% can do so. Poverty, at 13% before the crisis, went up to just under 90% as of mid-August 2020. About two-thirds of HHs lost one or more income sources, and about half lost between 75% to 100% of their previous income. In the absence of livelihood opportunities, HH have been forced to resort to negative coping mechanisms, including child labor; early marriage; and participation in the violence sector. As a result of the ongoing crisis, the number of IDPs has increased, which led to additional pressure on social and basic services, leading to a potential rupture in the social fabric. An assessment of the state of disability at the national level presents the most alarming situation.3.7 million people – or 27% of the population (aged 12+)–have a disability. Nearly 75% are without access to medical, social, and economic support

4 - Implementation of activities

A mechanism for consultation and participation will be established as one of the rst programme actions. This will be preceded or accompanied by a con ict-sensitive analysis, to ensure a full understanding when designing programme priorities and implementation options, so that con ict threats are not exacerbated, and community cohesion is reinforced. There will be a strong underpinning of ‘Do No Harm’ and ‘Do Good’ considerations ensuring that priorities supported will be identi ed transparently and with participation of communities or through their representatives. Spaces of participation thus created/reinforced, will be linked to all activities throughout implementation. Options will be prioritized that meet the following objectives: 1. drawing as much as possible on local– and preferably community-based – implementation arrangements, as far as they are inclusive (community groups; faith-based organizations; parents associations; farmer groups; youth clubs), 2. drawing as much as possible on local labor (with priority of recruitment among the most vulnerable) to enhance the overall positive impact, in particular for livelihoods, on those prioritized participants. The participation of local informal and small businesses will be encouraged, to ensure the programme also provides a rapid (even if small) boost to local economic recovery. Collaboration will be pursued with UN Agencies and with NGOs engaged in the same geographic and/orthematic areas.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Assumptions and risks: Key stakeholders allow UNDP and partners to apply minimum targeting criteria outlined here, ensuring impartial and needs-driven service delivery. Should UNDP and partners be unable to apply procedures, activities may be delayed/ frozen until permissions are negotiated. Areas of operation remain safe. Should life-threatening risks (such as ERW contamination, outbreak of active conflict, huge risk in V/PIED incidents, heavy IAF affecting local area) occur, activities may be frozen to safeguard lives. Local communities participate to all phases of initiatives and vulnerable groups are positively included in community-based mechanisms. Contingency/mitigation measures: Red line is inability to apply minimum targeting and do-no-harm procedures to ensure a principled response. Should restrictions compromise this, UNDP would open negotiations with stakeholders; and as a last resort liaise to modify/cancel planned activities. Withdrawal from a target area due to safety concerns could trigger a shift of activities to other areas or freeze before reassessment and re-entry into an area after the situation stabilizes. If a major devaluation of local currency continues, consequences to operational capacity of partners and contractors would be reviewed by UNDP. The timeframe is adapted to ensure mobilization at community level and sensitization on participatory approach. Activities will start after acceptance by local authorities and civil society representive

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Resources and response capacities being limited, there will remain perception of biased targeting of beneficiaries/ response activities, thereby creating or exacerbating tension; Mitigation measure: 1.perform conflict analysis before planning response; 2. Ensure that an updated needs analysis informs response formulation, and that this is openly documented and made transparent 3. Ensure those in similar situations/ within geographical areas of response, area reached equally; 4. Obtain validation of prioritization and of formulated response from a representative community group/ through well-selected key informants and/or participation mechanisms 5. Communicate planned response to communities 6. Deliver on obligation of ‘Accountability to (Affected) Populations’, by keeping communities regularly informed of project implementation 7. Ensure process of receiving complaints and responses to complaints 8. Mitigate if planned response does unexpectedly create tension (e.g. addon of other activities or change activities, in consultation with concerned communities. Risk 2: Escalation of security situation; Mitigation measures: 1. Focus on responses benefiting entire community rather than individual beneficiaries (e.g. rehabilitation of community infrastructure, rather than e.g. individual housing support); 2. Ensure there is a process established that allows to consult communities on how to proceed with proposed response in a deteriorating security environment
