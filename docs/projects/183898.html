Project Summary

This project is aimed at providing life-saving humanitarian assistance to IDPs, refugees and vulnerable communities in White Nile and Kassala - reaching a total target of 22,000 beneficiaries across various activities. It intends to improve the nutritional status for 9,389 under-five children (8,435 girls and 972 boys) in White Nile (773 children in Guli, 1,071 children in Tandaliti and 4,406 children in Eddiwuim) and Kassala state (2,043 in River Atbara and 1,096 North Delta localities). The project will also target 2000 pregnant & lactating women (PLW) in 2 States, particularly those with children aged 0-6 months. It will support the provision of nutrition-specific services through existing structures, in conjunction with maternal and child health services and it will link households with malnourished children and PLW to FSL projects in the same targeted locality. There will be integration of early childhood care and development (ECCD) services among 5 nutrition centres. Furthermore, the project will integrate some of WASH activities into nutrition centres. The project team will also ensure coordination with other similar/ ongoing interventions. The project will deliver these activities through community services adopting the sector objectives. It takes a people-centred approach, with the most vulnerable affected people at its’ core. Vulnerable groups will be specifically targeted through this humanitarian response focused on access to nutrition services with due regard for protection, disability, equitable access, gender equality and environmental sensitivity. The project will be implemented over a period of one year.

1 - Needs

During the political transition period in Sudan, humanitarian needs continue to grow and food insecurity is on the rise and becoming a life-threatening concern as stated in the state of food security and nutrition report. The most affected are under-five children and pregnant and lactating women. The effects of malnutrition in children will mean children do not thrive and develop as they should. The national nutrition situation has been characterized by persistent high levels of household food insecurity and under-nutrition since the first S3M survey was conducted in 2013. Based on available data (S3M II 2018), 36.6 per cent of children under five are stunted. The national prevalence rate of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) is at 13.6% although several States are above the emergency threshold of 15% according to the WHO criteria. Over the years, there has been substantial attention to the provision of nutrition treatment services in conflict-affected states. Nevertheless, in Kassala State the needs continue to be high because the situation has been deteriorating because of lack of consistent services and problems in service accessibilities for community members especially during rainy season and tribal conflicts. The indicators for the State according to S3M figures are: Wasting/ SEVERE (MUAC) at 4.3 %; Underweight SEVERE at 17.9 %; Stunting/ stuntedness SEVERE is at 26.6 %. Based on S3M results, White Nile State is also characterized by high levels of malnutrition. According to inter-agency assessments carried out during heavy rains and floods in August 2020 and 2021 in White Nile localities, there are also needs for capacity strengthening of healthcare workers on IYCF and running health education sessions. Nutrition services are almost absent in most health facilities, and Plan International noted that MUAC screening has not been conducted recently. Stunting prevalence is high among children under five years of age, at 36.6%, wasting at 14.4%, and underweight at 29.8%. Three localities particularly record high levels of underweight children - Edwiem 34.7%, Alsalam 34.4% ,and Guli 34.5%. Based on these indicators and observations from on-going work, there are huge needs across the two States for:- treatment of acute malnutrition (SAM and MAM), - strengthening facility service provision,- increasing IYCF counselling/ educational campaigns,- strengthening capacities of nutrition staff,- supporting community management of acute malnutrition,- improving assessments (SMART Surveys) and monitoring- increasing the number of OTP/ TSFP sites to 16 (9 in Kassala and 7 in White Nile).

2 - Activities/Outputs

The project will work in White Nile (Tendalti, Guli, and Edwiem& Umrimta & Aljabaleen localities) and in Kassala States (River Atbara and North Delta localities) to provide nutrition-specific interventions through community & facility-based services in 16 OTP centers. Based on current capacity and other actors present in these areas, the project intends to increase Plan International’s coverage of OTP/TSFP sites to a total of 16 (9 in Kassala and 7 in White Nile), broken down as follows: - Total 14 in North Delta, currently supporting 5, & plan to add 1; - Total 15 in River Atbara, supporting none, plan to add 3;- Total 8 in Edwiem, supporting 2, to add 1;- Total 5 in Umrimta, supporting none, to add 1;- Total 8 in Aljabaleen, supporting none, to add 1;- Total 16 in Guli, supporting none, to add 1; and - Total 7 in Tendalti, supporting none, to add 1. The broad intervention areas will focus on:1. Improving the quality of nutrition services provided in the nutrition sites.2. Scaling up nutrition treatment and geographic coverage of CMAM services.3. Strengthening monitoring and supervision of nutrition projects/responses.4. Ensuring adequate availability of core supplies at targeted sites.5. Enhancing Accountability to Affected Population (AAP).6. Conducting SMART Surveys in 2 states of White Nile & Kassala.
