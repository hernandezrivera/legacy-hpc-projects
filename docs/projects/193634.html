Project Summary

The project aims at increasing the response to emergencies in schools to enhance equitable access to education for children in vulnerable areas in the Gaza Strip. The action solves one of the most severe problems that face education; school dropping out due to school-related expenses, children's lack of interest, and insufficient adaptive education for CwD.
The project provides comprehensive interventions that came under the Education Cluster SO2, which will be addressed by: producing an offline free Mobile Application of 100 digital and innovative self-learning materials and educative video games covering the three core subjects at the primary schools, enhancing the basic learning skills for 30,000 children aged (6-13) in the most underserved areas in the Gaza strip, increasing the teachers and parents integration in the school and learning activities by learning capacity-building sessions for 10,000 teachers, caregivers and parents, and supply schools with the IT education tools and materials to be used during edutainment classes to increase the children's interest in learning.
Over 50 schools were damaged during the recent escalations which form a dangerous environment for children and teachers at schools so we will rehabilitate 20 schools for a safe and healthy environment to benefit 15,000 students and teachers at affected schools. Systematically, we will work for inclusive learning for everyone by providing adaptive digital materials for self-learning and assistive devices for 5,000 CwD. Moreover, 800 remedial schools and courses for children at threat of dropping out will be conducted to contribute to creating an inclusive, equal learning system for all children. All the activities considered gender equality and gender needs. Thus the mentioned above will consider parity. However, the remedial school and courses for 15000 children will prioritize the school-age boys since the need assessment demonstrates that their numbers far exceeded girls. So, we will target 12000M and 3000F.

1 - Needs

The context in the occupied Palestinian territory (oPt) remains a protracted protection crisis, as so-called by the OCHA, characterized by 70 years of Israeli military occupation, human rights violations, internal Palestinian political divisions, and recurrent escalations of hostilities. In 2021, humanitarian crises have been exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic and the 11-day attack in May that affected the humanitarian and economic-social situation in the Gaza Strip, mainly the children's right to equitable access to education (OCHA need assessment 2021-2022). The OCHA Education Cluster reported that around 270 schools were damaged by explosive weapons during the May 2021 hostilities. In addition, 63 schools operated by UNRWA sustained damages while being used as emergency shelters during the crisis. At least 169,300 students from UNRWA and public schools and over 10,530 kindergarten students were enrolled in schools damaged; these numbers equate to approximately 28-18%, respectively, of all students attending schools and kindergartens in Gaza at the time of hostilities (OCHA Education Cluster in the OPT, GCPEA, Measuring the impact of attacks on education in Palestine, 2021-2022) More than half of the people living in the Gaza Strip live in poverty, meaning over 2 million (UNICEF State of Palestine Humanitarian Situation Report No. 2: 01 July to 30 September 2022). Poverty and the economic situation are considered the main reason for the school dropping out of children and adolescents (7-18). According to the need assessment issued by the OCHA, 7.1% (2.7%of school-aged girls, 5.3% of school-aged boys) of household children are not attending school. The essay light that 35% of children drop out of school because of the inability to afford school-related expenses. Counting in detail that the deteriorating living circumstances, the continuous power outages and electricity rationing, poor shelter living conditions, economic deprivation, psycho-social impacts from the escalations, overcrowding, limited hours of instruction due to schools' double or triple shifts, and overloaded and unpaid teachers – which all affect educational completion rates and increase dropout. The numbers indicate that 28.0% of children have a lack of interest in education (OCHA, Need Assessment 2022). The latter came as a result of the traditional education methods that do not keep up with the modern changes that the children observe currently, the economic situation, the education came as the least priority for the parents’ which shake the children's trust in learning under the severe socio-economic and political situation, (SYFS’ Education Programme Survey. 2022)Illness and children working came as the third and fourth orderly. Although only 9.4% of children left school for work, this indicator still shows the threat of a child labour phenomenon. 3.8% of the households due to child labour for the current school year (2021-2022), (5.3% (ages 5 - 17)), 67.7% of 5.3% of school-aged boys- dropped out due to child labour, and 13.7% of the 2.7% of school-aged girls (ages 5 - 17) dropped out of school for the child labour. On the other hand, children with disability form 7.6% of children who do not attend schools due to the lack of adaptive environment and assistive devices at schools for Children with Disabilities (OCHA Need Assessment, 2022.)Gaza Strip was not immune from the rest of the world during Covid-19. The crisis brought up the need for inclusive and innovative learning methodologies, involving teachers and parents in systematical learning tools to keep abreast with the learning process during emergencies. 81.2% of refugee households reported their need to catch up on learning due to school closures during covid-19, and 76.7% of non-refugee households ensured their need for such (OCHA Need Assessment Gaza Strip, 2022). OCHA report for 2022 for Education clustered demonstrates that: school attendance for primary and secondary education is slightly higher for school-aged girls than for school-aged boys 5.7% of school-aged children reportedly dropping out of school. 5.3% of all school-aged boys and 2.7% of all school-aged girls during the current school year (2021-2022). Moreover, the number of children who are not planning to enrol in School-aged children is 9.1% due to school expenses, a child not interested in school, Children with disabilities and children needing to support their families. Among these reasons, school costs hit the head of the list of -school-age girls dropping out, while child labour is the main reason for boys not deciding to enrol. CWD needs an inclusive and adaptive environment for learning in support from teachers, parents and caregivers to build a safe space for CWD at schools to get involved in the education process without facing violations that hinder their access to one of their central rights. CWD face several challenges lightened that 49.7% of them state that bullying prevents them from accessing education, and 36% see that the schools’ infrastructure is not adaptive meanwhile, 28.8 said that classrooms are not adapted to needs.

2 - Activities

The Activities are designed according to the SYFS’ evaluation of previous activities, which cut across with the strategic priorities of the OCHA 2022-2023 objective3: The damaged schools in the latest escalations, children with disabilities, and out-of-school children. The activities were defined and selected in coordination with the education cluster and partners to avoid data duplications between the planned actions. The action provides a comprehensive education response by promoting relevant cross-cutting issues, protectivity, and gender-responsive policies, based on a child-central-based approach, including the implementation, data disaggregation, interests, and the different circumstances under accountability and a confidential complaint system and feedback, which will preclude any discrimination, inequality, and harm. These actions are rendered more relevant and important in emergencies, where marginalized populations, particularly children with disabilities, women with disabilities, and children from poor socioeconomic backgrounds. SYFS has strong linkages with the UNRWA, and the MoEHE through an MoU (Memorandum of Understanding). Activities include community committees of case managers, school counsellors, social workers, and teachers who will guarantee communication with the other referral partners for further interventions related to psychological, health, GBV, Child Labour, or any violations) facilitated by the SYFS cooperation with the other OCHA Clusters (food, shelter, health, Protection). Moreover, a social counsellor and the MEL Officer will raise the affected people's awareness, including, parents, teachers, and children, about their rights to humanitarian assistance and accountability through complaints channels (hotline number, email address, and WhatsApp application.) In addition, all the employees within the project will sign the Code of Conduct and the PSEA Policy according to the PSEA network framework and indicators. Output1: 40.000 people 10.000 parents/caregivers and 30.000 children aged 6-13 (50%F, 50%M, 5% CwD) of vulnerable students and school staff, have support to access learning. A1.1: Provision of support to strengthen the learning modalities and quality education services. SYFS will use digitalized modern self-learning methodology to facilitate children's access to educative and innovative materials, which is not only Eco-friendly but also will build children learning capacities, especially in the core subjects, thus it will increase their interest in education and school by providing IT tools and gamifying the learning materials (turning the Curriculums into Games to create a chance for the students to be in a network and become heroes). The action will be implemented in three phases: A- Producing an offline Mobile App of innovative digital self-learning materials for 20,000 children, 10,000 parents, and teachers, and contains assistive means for CwD: transcription, voiceover, sign language, and Sound sensors. The Mobile App will include 100 training materials that cover the formal educative curriculums of the three core subjects (English, Arabic, and Math) to enhance the basic learning skills: Reading, Writing in both languages (Arabic and English), and mathematics, in addition to friendly-customer educative Videos and digital video-games to be easy for all the participants and catchy for children. The digitalized materials do not use high storage capacity as most of the targeted have low-quality phones. Also, it will include two interfaces; the first for parents/caregivers and teachers includes guidelines for teaching and the second for the students. B- Provide 20 schools (10.000 students 50%F, 50%M) with IT innovative tools: Internet devices, LCD screens, entertaining education tools, and brochures that have QR-Code for the Mobile App. Each school will install the Application on their devices and conduct two classes per week for the students on the digitalized self-learning training materials. C- 130 capacity-building sessions for 2000 teachers and parents including women with disability to get involved in school activities, training, edutainment, emergency education tools, digital helping tools, CwD inclusive education, gender-responsive learning, and playing-based education to promote the love of learning among students. Output2: 15.000 (50% f, 50% m, 5%CwD) students and schools staff have benefited from emergency school rehabilitation for 20 schools. A1.2: Provision of essential and basic emergency rehabilitation to schools to ensure that school environments are inclusive and safe for students and teachers. According to the MoEHE data of the most damaged schools in the recent attacks, the SYFS will define the most school in need to achieve rehabilitation. Output3: 5,000 CwD (50%f and 50%m) have benefited from the adapted education services and assistive devices Support for school-aged children with disabilities to access education services. A1.3- Provide 1700 assistive devices for 1700 CwD As a part of the inclusive education process, the community committee in the targeted schools. A2.3- Provide 50 adaptive digital training materials and 200 film screenings by the cinema bus (SYFS bus for film screenings) for 3,300 CwD on the CwD and bullying to provide an adaptive environment at schools for CWD to get integrated into a safe school and adaptive education. Output4: 15,000 (20%F, 80%M: 3000F,12000M) of out of school aged-children supported by education services aiming at reintegrating them into the education system. A1.4: Provide 1000 remedial after-school sessions in English, Arabic, and Math to 10,000 school children (6-13) dropout children, injured or ex-detainees, Children with Disability, and children at risk of dropout being led by the school teachers. A2.4: 600 support learning sessions by playing to support 5.000 children and reintegrate them into schools.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

The community is the main stakeholder in the project, as well as the activities, which came as a direct response to the need assessment and the feedback on the community needs, and recommendations. Within this project, SYFS developed three main communication and feedback channels to be enhanced through various modalities, including community committees of teachers, social counselors, and case managers whose role is to identify priorities, map existing resources within the community, prepare school-based contingency plans and coordinate activities in the school. Parent-teacher groups will also be involved in collecting feedback from community members to enhance accountability to affected populations. Student groups will also ensure the integral to the design and implementation of emergency education activities. The formed committees at schools will work in one network to transmit the feedback of the children and the parents. SYFS developed feedback and complaint mechanisms, by hotline, generic email, complain box, and a survey that will be conducted post and pre Action. Moreover, the community committees in the most marginalized areas will conduct orientation sessions for the beneficiaries on the complaint mechanisms and who to provide their feedback and complaints. The corrective actions will be taken and communicated with the beneficiaries based on the nature of the complaints and feedback. Feedback on misconduct will be examined and based on the results the necessary measures will be taken by SYFS policies. Feedback regarding the outcome of the implementation will be used to build on SYFS lessons learned for future interventions. SYFS throughout the intervention cycle will take into account the needs of the most vulnerable population as well as their rights.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The intervention is based on the human rights approach and the gender-disaggregated analysis of each gender and age group's needs and threats that could be faced. Thus it is taken into account the rights for dignity, safety, and life. According to OCHA need assessment 38.4% of households identifying specific safety and security concerns for children with a disability, and 15.7% of households identifying specific safety and security concerns for women, as a result, The actions avoid the harm of transportation and movement by providing cabinets for services and data collection for girls and women and PwD who are unable to access the services because of unsafe transportation during the conflicts or bombs. The intervention guarantees the right to access the information and people's right to accountability, in which an orientation session will be conducted to explain the services provision criteria and goals. Access to education will be addressed with caregivers and teachers with protection mainstreaming with the activities delivered to children and parents. The intervention avoids exposing people to further harm as a result of the action through a safe selection of the intervention locations, ensuring that the beneficiaries will not be exposed to additional harm. In the case of escalation at the security level (internal clashes or military strikes), the activities will be postponed until the situation becomes stable. The nature of activities implemented is educational and recreational with the provision of referral support for most prone children to dropping out, teachers, and caregivers who are very vulnerable. Target groups are consulted at the level of intervention design, implementation, and end where a midterm review will take place within the project intervention, and the final term review will be consolidated and shared with all project populations whereupon need corrective measures will be taken. Targeting parents specifically women will be reached in suitable spaces in their areas, where easy access to both genders and people with disabilities to activity locations. The information will be managed and handled in a sensitive matter with restrictive measures on sharing information with stakeholders. Psychosocial referral services will be according to the good practices and referrals across child protection partners and family centers. A direct connection with the PSEAH network will be provided for the women and girls as well as it will be provided under high sensitivity and confidentiality to protect the affected people from GBV and sexual harassment the complaints will be received with high responsibilities and the cases will be reported to the specialized network.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The intervention addresses groups who constantly face financial issues. Retention to education facilities is the prominent area of support within the intervention where the primary target group is children and children with disabilities. Conducive elements of support are to address women being educators in the school facilities or caregivers in their role in the family, the centre location of the dominant support is the school and surrounding community. Children who will integrate with the additional support of the project will be targeted in the remedial and recreational support alongside psychosocial support and referral. The psycho-social support and referral services within the intervention are designed to tackle the most in need while letting no child behind within the category of support. Assistive devices will be provided for CwD to guarantee inclusive learning. The self-learning materials will be provided with adaptive means to facilitate the learning process for all children with guidance for parents and caregivers outside the schools. Families and children will participate and identify the needs through the pre-assessment to include all persons and children with disabilities with equipped staff who can deal with different types of limitations/disabilities. The assessment and monitoring will be fully managed by the SYFS MEL online system to verify and crosscheck the information of beneficiaries and manage their distribution times. The project will develop the capacities and abilities to lead the activities during the crisis, including teachers and parents from all gender and age groups. For example, capacity building and orientation sessions will be conducted to target parents and persons with disabilities to enhance their participation in the implementation of the projects’ activities. The adaptive materials will be provided for the CwD to lead their self-learning process. Community- committees will be formed across the Gaza strip to reach all the beneficiaries and address their needs and lead the evaluation process under the supervision of the case manager. The complaint and feedback measures will be provided by teacher/parent groups, students and CwD. The action meets the needs and the gender response where the intervention is in line with the gender-disaggregated data that address the exceeded numbers of school-aged boys who dropped out of school for child labour.
