Project Summary

The overall estimated Sector PIN for 2023 – of food insecure and those at risk of food insecurity – is estimated at 13.9 million, with 12 million facing acute food insecurity and 1.9 million people at risk of sliding into food insecurity (HNO 2023). The acute food insecure figure includes 1.9 million people living in camps and deemed to be 100 percent food insecure. Livestock production is vital in Syria to sustain food availability, food diversity/nutrition and income generation among vulnerable women and men affected by the drought-like conditions and prevailing economic hardships. Livestocksector is facing many challenges that are forcing small-scale vulnerable households to adopt negative copying strategies which includes distress selling-off of their assets, subsequently losing a considerable source of income.
In this context, FAO has designed a project to assist 90,667 vulnerable crisis-affected food insecure farming households (equivalent to 544,000 people) in Syria through protecting and restoring their main production assets (livestock).
The objective of the proposed action is to protect the livestock assets of targeted vulnerable herders through the provision of vaccinations and treatment as well as supporting the provision of feed for the herds of the most vulnerable. The expected project outputs are:
Output 1: Livestock productivity for 80,000 vulnerable crisis-affected breeders is maintained and improved.
This output aims at improving the availability of animal feed for the purpose of asset protection for 25,000 vulnerable households (300 kg of feed per household, in line with sector recommendations) as well as boosting milk availability through enhanced livestock production. Output 1 also seeks to provide veterinary services (vaccines and/or treatment) for 55,000 herders throughout the country.
Output 2. Improving animal reproduction for about 10,667 beneficiaries through artificial insemination and capacity building, contributing to increased livestock production. This output aims at restoring livestock production through distribution of animals to small scale vulnerable households who lost their animals due to the drought-like conditions and prevailing economic crisis.
All outputs would include capacity building to enhance the technical skills of herders and technicians and to enable them to resume production in a viable, efficient and productive way and better cope with the challenging conditions.

3 - Needs

The estimated FSA Sector PIN for 2023 is 15 million. The consequences of the crisis and other factors led to record losses in agricultural production for the second consecutive season and consequently a sharp decline in livelihoods and food insecurity for most farming families. Weather fluctuations and drought-like conditions sharply decline the pastures that were not in the best, and access to it is fraught with security risks mostly. These conditions forced the livestock to rely more on fodder (which has limited availability and doubled prices, driven by several reasons), also there is a weakness of public veterinary services resulting from the prolonged crisis, as well as the high prices of veterinary treatment and medicines, which increased the burdens of breeders. Resulted of these conditions, the nutritional and health status of livestock declined and the costs of raising them increased. In contrast, there was a decrease in their prices, which burdened the breeders and pushed them to adopt very negative and destructive coping mechanisms for their livelihoods.The protection of the remaining livestock assets is essential for poor herders, particularly women headed households. The provision of feed will help to preserve surviving herds particularly during the lean season until crop residues/fodder crops become available for animal feed.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Risk: Beneficiaries being targeted as a result of being selected for project/risk of community tension arising from beneficiaries selection. Mitigation measures: Inclusion of different categories of affected population (IDPs and host communities; returnees and refugees; men, women, boys, girls and youth).2) Risk: Inclusion and exclusion errors. Mitigation measures: Establish a local beneficiary selection committee with equitable representation of vulnerable groups; putting in place clear and precise guidance on beneficiary selection criteria and process; and ensuring an effective communication for guidance in local language.3) Risk of diversion of assistance/assets. Mitigation measures: Establish community-based beneficiary selection and community-based monitoring. The project has taken into account protection elements, in line with the do not harm principles. To the maximum possible extent, all measures will be taken to limit the risk of beneficiaries being targeted. Where IDPs are selected, representatives from the host communities will be included as well, to limit possible frictions, and a special attention will give to returnees to help them in settlement and resuming production activities. An accountability system for beneficiaries’ participation and information and feedback/complaint mechanism also will put in place to the communities.
