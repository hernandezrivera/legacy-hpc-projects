Project Summary

The proposed project is designed to address and support WASH related humanitarian and protection needs of returned families in the tribal district of North Waziristan who remained displaced and have now returned to their place of origin, ensuring smooth transition to longer-term recovery and development to have increased access to improved services particularly safe and clean drinking water & sanitation facilities at communal and household level. PREPARED considers provision of safe drinking water, adequate sanitation & hygiene practice recognized fundamental to human well-being, particularly for returned families in NW. The proposed project would likely to reduce vulnerability of returned families having low or no access to clean, safe drinking water & safe sanitation facilities in areas of return. In view of primary need assessment conducted by PREPARED, project aims to reach 20,000 individuals to ensure improved access to sufficient drinking water, adequate sanitation facilities is physically accessible for men, women, children, elders & PWDs within close proximity of HHs, educational or health institutions. PREPARED would like to promote behaviors change, adopting positive sanitation practices through PHAST approach to increase understanding of key health risk related to WASH, adopting positive hygiene practices to prevent most common waterborne/ vector control diseases through latrine construction/ latrine usage, in NW tribal district. Improved access to safe and clean drinking water facilities will cater through; rehabilitation of 12 water supply schemes. In parallel, water safety plan will be prepared ensuring effective implementation of service delivery aspect. While, adequate sanitation facilities provision would be met through construction of 400 latrines with sanitation kits (focusing on PWDs, Women headed HHs, Elderly, widow-headed etc.) & on most vulnerable HHs. Project also proposes construction WASH facilities provision in three schools and two health facilities. Additionally, sensitization of target community especially women, girls & children within cultural constraints of FATA through conducting of 150 Hygiene sessions for male and female community members. Along with community, sessions will also be conducted in schools to orient the school going boys with best hygiene practices. The project also includes distribution of IEC material distribution along with hygiene kits distribution to 3000 HHs. Total 24 pre and post water quality tests and 12 O&M trainings will be conducted. & 1120 hygiene kits provision in areas of intervention. The project proposes to contribute to Gender Mainstreaming through producing an impact working on Gender Marker with equal and increased participation of women with men as decision makers. Acting as a way in improving & maintaining female dignity. To promote hygiene behaviors, PREPARED will adopt & implement 7-step PHAST approach at community level. Social organizers and hygiene promoters would ensure implementation of PHAST approach at village level with close coordination with community and stakeholders

Needs

According to FATA Transitional Plan, 2018-2020, access to safe water and sanitation (WaSH) is limited, with severe implications for health, nutrition and well-being in FATA. Limited electricity hampers the operation of existing water supply schemes, the rocky terrain makes hand-pumps generally ineffective and dispersed settlement patterns make it difficult to provide WaSH services in a cost-effective manner. As a result, poor sanitation, lack of water for drinking, low water quality and poor hygiene practices are widespread. 45% of the population in areas of return rely on unsafe drinking water. 36% of returnees have no toilets and practice open defecation. Two-thirds of the populations do not have water facilities close to their homes, thus increasing safety concerns and the burden of domestic work on women and girls who are primarily responsible for fetching water. This has a detrimental effect on household hygiene, child care, women’s and children’s health and girls’ school enrolment. A lack of WaSH facilities in schools also affects girls’ education, as do limited WASH services in health facilities. Water-borne diseases are prevalent, with diarrhea reported by 33% of households. PREPARED has conducted rapid need assessment (RNA) in the month of March 2018 (March 19-20, 2018) in the area of return in NW by deputing qualified and experienced staff from programme. Different methodologies and sector specific qualitative tools for data collection like Key Informant Interviews, meetings with political administration, Pak Army field formation unit, agency health department, PHED, focus group discussions and observatory walks in the area were applied in assessment for the purpose to collect accurate quantitative and qualitative data/ information regarding the WASH needs of the returned families. Focus group discussions (FGDs) were conducted mainly with men that were possible in current circumstances in NWA which was also recommended by political agent. Main findings of the assessment reflect that the current communal water sources (tube wells) are either damaged or nonfunctional due to lack of power supply. Unprotected open wells (exposed to contamination), bore holes (Hand pumps, tube wells) and springs are the main sources of water. Poor maintenance of the existing water sources, open defecation because of the non-availability of latrines and poor hygienic practices are the potential threats to the outbreak of water borne diseases. Mostly the prevailing diseases are caused by contaminated water, poor drainage and stagnant water in the surrounding of water sources. Water-borne diseases like diarrhea spread easily when community members do not observe good hygiene practices. Returned families need to be educated that every individual within a community has a crucial role in practicing good hygiene practices and hold a responsibility to their fellow community members to promote good health and a clean environment. Un-hygienic practices, un-safely disposal of feces and un-safe handling food and water contribute to poor community health and members of households and the community at large suffer from the spread of disease. Moreover, the community members as well as different stakeholders were found to have very limited capacity (Knowledge, Practice, Resources and Plan) to cope with future potential shocks. Based on the findings of RNA, interventions in WASH at this stage would decrease the potential of outbreak of different water borne diseases, improve access of the community to drinkable water, creation of hygienic environment, minimize open defecation and the overall result will be improved health conditions. Further, including DRR in both hard and soft components of the project will result in higher resilience with in the target communities. DRR shall be made as the cross cutting theme of different project components.
