Project Name

Start Date

End Date

Project Summary

GOAL’s primary objective for 2020 is to provide quality and essential healthcare services, scale up responsiveness to needs of targeted vulnerable populations in Warrap, Upper Nile, and Central Equatoria through a network of primary health care facilities by delivering the basic PHCC, PHCU and mobile health care packages. GOAL will target 159,649 beneficiaries with a focus on children under 5 years (target 30,333 children) and pregnant and lactating women (PLWs – target 12,772 PLWs).

1 - Needs

GOAL proposes to focus on provision of primary health and nutrition services for vulnerable groups, with a focus on pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) and children under 5, in existing sites in Upper Nile, Central Equatoria and Warrap. About 13,310 IDPs remain in Melut after the closing of the Protection of Civilians (PoC) site in December 2017, when many actors left the county; GOAL continues providing an integrated package of health and nutrition services, conducting 35,589 curative consultations (9,374 consults for children under 5 years; 26,215 consults for over 5 years) between January and September 2019, with malaria as highest morbidity (35% of cases). The most recent SMART survey indicates high morbidity levels, particularly in children under 5 years (42.8% fever, 30% cough, 19.8% diarrhea) coupled with the GAM prevalence of 21.7% (SAM 4.9%, MAM 16.8%) (WVI SMART Survey, March 2018). Given IPC projections predicting an increase in IPC Phase 3 (“Crisis”) in the first quarter of 2020, this situation will likely deteriorate further. Similarly, Kajo Keji remains grossly under supported with only 15% of needs met and 5 functional health facilities (HFs) for the 24,025 identified people in need; SSHF funds 9% of needs (supporting 3 of 32 HFs) and HPF 6% (2 HFs). Most facilities (>80%) are nonfunctional due to damage or destruction, attacks to HFs, poor infrastructure, lack of medical supplies and qualified workers (>80% are volunteers), and lack of humanitarian support, and the only available referral pathway for specialized medical care is in Uganda. As an entry point from Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC), Kajo keji poses a high health risk to the population in the wake of the Ebola Virus Disease (EVD) outbreak in DRC. Other public health concerns include high risk of HIV/TB transmission and vaccine preventable diseases like measles and polio (coverage <1% due to lack of cold chain equipment) put the area on high alert in the absence of EPI services. Service uptake remains low (ANC2 attendance <50% and skilled delivery 25%), with main causes of morbidity including malaria (ranging from 33% to 70% of total consultations), Acute watery diarrhea (5% to 26%), high risk of disease outbreaks (AWD, Cholera, Meningitis, Hepatitis E) due to poor access to WASH infrastructure, and limited access to safe water for drinking or household use. In Warrap (Twic, Gogrial East and Gogrial West), heavily influenced by environmental conditions, high levels of morbidity and limited access to services is a major concern. Despite HPF support to Gogrial West (20 HFs), Gogrial East (14 HFs) and Twic (25 of 57 HFs), during the rainy season, most facilities are inaccessible. EPI coverage is low, exposing communities to the risk of disease outbreak, such as the measles outbreak in Gogrial East in April 2019. Malnutrition underlies a large proportion of the unacceptably high levels of child morbidity and mortality in the county, with SAM 5.5% and GAM 24.1%. Uptake of reproductive health services remains low, specifically for deliveries by skilled birth attendants and family planning. Such factors contribute to a worsening health burden in these counties; GOAL seeks to address challenges through provision of integrated primary health and nutrition services through both static and mobile sites, to enhance equitable access to quality care. In 2019 GOAL will offer this package through a network of HFs in Melut (2), Twic (25), Gogrial East (14), Gogrial West (20), and Kajo Keji (3); integrated health and nutrition will be provided at GOAL health facilities, with TSFP or OTP implemented by GOAL or other partners. GOAL will enhance linkages to nutrition and food security programming through referral of SAM to outpatient therapeutic programme (OTP) managed by WVI (Melut, Gogrial West, Gogrial East) or ACF (Twic), while also strengthening referral of beneficiaries into existing resilience projects to better address root causes of poor health.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

GOAL globally has experience supporting MOH to strengthen routine health surveillance and monitoring systems and better understand the scope of disparities in health outcomes and access to better support scale up of services to target populations. GOAL will leverage this expertise to support development of digital data gathering solutions, data management and analysis systems, and digitization of information processes to allow for stronger data use by state and CHD (including Health Management Information Systems and Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response). GOAL will continue to support CHD with frequent visits to do joint monitoring, supportive supervision and mentorship; the project will work within the structures of MoH health systems, providing complementary support to HPF/WB funded health systems stabilization activities in each county.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is closely linked with the South Sudan Humanitarian Fund positioning in 2020 by contributing to an overall impact of saving lives, alleviation of suffering and maintain human dignity, via timely and coordinated assistance that based on the principals of humanitarian assistance. The targeted geographical areas are listed as priority areas under the health sector. Further, this project will be implemented under the framework of the overall South Sudan Health System Strengthening, which GOAL implements through partnership with Health Pooled Fund III in Gogrial East, Gogrial West, and Twic, while SUHA implements in Kajo keji, and World Bank administers a complementary project in Melut through World Vision.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Staff sign a code of conduct to protect junior staff, beneficiaries, and community members. GOAL will ensure the project is implemented in line with GOAL safeguarding policy; guided by Core Humanitarian Standard on Quality and Accountability (CHS) to improve quality and effectiveness of support, the policy facilitates accountability to communities. GOAL's approach to protection is based on analysis and management of risks to reduce vulnerabilities (risks faced by communities, specific risks of most vulnerable groups within communities, and risks the project may create). GOAL will ensure protection is mainstreamed into project activities to contribute to reduction of identified threats without posing additional threats to beneficiaries. Further, GOAL will ensure that project implementation takes account of environment, context and impact (intended or unintended) on target children and communities. GOAL has a functional Feedback, Complaints Response Mechanism (FCRM), providing a platform for communities to air views on support and services provided by GOAL and response to any unintended consequences for project participants. Reporting protocols will be communicated to stakeholders at all levels to encourage communication of sexual exploitation and abuse.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes- the project will be implemented closely with the County Health departments (CHD), building the capacity for the CHD to take over the programme once the action comes to an end

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

GOAL will work in partnership to strengthen the relationships with the local authorities withthe aim of promoting equitable participation of all beneficiaries while ensuring project activities are gender-sensitive to meet needs of women, men, boys and girls. GOAL will ensure stakeholders' participation in the project; involvement of local community through project planning and implementation, and through committees, and awareness raising sessions etc., to identify the most vulnerable to ensure equitable access to services. GOAL will ensure its Community Feedback Mechanisms is used effectively to engage better with communities. Most primary health facilities are accessible by road, and teams will be equipped with off-road vehicles that can withstand bad weather and rough terrain to ensure GOAL can reach those most in need who have no other way of receiving life-saving services. GOAL will preposition supplies in facilities that risk accessibility challenges during rainy season to prevent stockouts so communities can continue to access services.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

GOAL is aware of gender disparities in terms of access to quality health and nutrition services. Often, women and children are the most affected where there is a breakdown in the provision of quality healthcare services. In addition to limited access to high quality healthcare services, women are also subjected tio various forms of GBV with limited or no support at all. GOAL strategy on protection of beneficiaries is guided by various policy documents that include the child protection, safeguarding, prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse as well as application of the Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS). To ensure elimination of inequalities that targets women and other vulnerable individuals, GOAL will in practice work with local structures that limit or influence the opportunities faced by women and other marginalised groups, including elderly and disabled persons. Trainings will be designed to involve both men and women to to include discussions on how to provide support mechanisms for women and help address gender inequality as well as inform context-specific protection strategy which ensuresparticipation and increased safety of all. Further, GOAL will train midwives in the criminal management of rape (CRM) and support health facilities with stock requisite kits to help to respond to and manage cases of GBV presented at the heath facilities. GOAL will also ensure gender balance among the CHWs. GOAL implements a Complaint Response Mechanisms (CRM), a community feedback mechanisms that allows community members to raise complaints about GOAL's commitments to delivering its programme and accountability to beneficiaries. GOAL will extend this approach by creating awareness among beneficiaries and train selected community representatives on how to use this facility to communicate feedback on GOALs' programming in the targeted communities.
