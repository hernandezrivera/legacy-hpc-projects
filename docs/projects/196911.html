1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The proposed project will aim to ensure children have access to safe and inclusive learning spaces and are able to resume learning as quickly as possible. The project aims at promoting and strengthening access to education opportunities for 6,500 internally displaced boys and girls, including children with disabilities, in Meconta District, Nampula Province who are affected by the crisis and deprived from the right to education due to conflict.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

World Vision will prioritise improving access to safe and inclusive learning spaces in selected communities in Meconta District, Nampula Province. Activities are listed as follows: 1.1 Procure and install temporary learning spaces (TLS) to accommodate children affected by the conflict; 1.2 Organise campaigns targeted at parents and caregivers for children’s enrollment and retention in school; 1.3 Conduct community awareness and engagement activities, including back to school campaigns; 1.4 Procure and distribute learning materials (textbooks, notebooks, stationery); 1.5 Procure and distribute teaching kits (illustrative boards, markers, flip charts, glue tapes and colour cardboards).

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Gender diversity is considered during the design of projects, with a focus on ensuring that girls have equal access to humanitarian support and services by addressing the barriers. Gender will also be considered at all levels of the project including recruitment and orientation of staff on gender sensitivity. World Vision will deliberately encourage participation of women and girls in decision-making in the implementation of the project. 48% of young Mozambican women aged 20-24 years are married before the age of 18, while 14% are married before the age of 15. Child marriage is associated with a significantly higher likelihood of leaving school early. Girls are more likely than boys to be pulled out of school in crises and less likely to return. Therefore, there is a higher risk posed to girls who have stopped going to school because of the humanitarian crisis in Cabo Delgado and the related spillover into neighbouring provinces than there is to boys. Promotion and protection of rights are core principles for the implementation of all projects. In humanitarian crises, child safeguarding and protection tend to get disrupted and/or compromised leading to high likelihood of child protection violations and incidents. To mitigate this risk, where possible protection principles will be incorporated into all activities including ensuring meaningful, non-discriminatory access to project services, prioritising the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and ensuring meaningful participation.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Child marriage and child labour might increase as a negative coping mechanism of families during the period that access to schools is restricted because of displacement. These are factors that will be taken into consideration in the project design. The project will align with Sphere, Child Protection Minimum Standards (CPMS), Inter-agency Network for Education in Emergencies (INEE), Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) and other relevant standards to ensure that the right to receive humanitarian assistance is safeguarded impartially for all affected populations especially children. Communities will be sensitised about protection concerns as well as reporting and referral mechanisms.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

World Vision will be intentional in considering the inclusion of persons with disability in the project. A survey that will include questions that contribute to the identification of persons with disabilities and their needs will be conducted. The adoption of inclusive interventions will be prioritised, not only to ensure protection of people especially children with disability, but also to address barriers to their access to assistance. With appropriate training of staff and on the job support, the project will build on successes and lessons from the previous projects so that persons with disability can participate in and benefit from planned interventions. In cases where temporary infrastructure needs to be installed such as learning spaces or handwashing stations, the project team will be sensitised to promote inclusion.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

World Vision Mozambique is aware of environmental considerations and will act appropriately to implement activities that are not harmful to the environment. These efforts will include working together with other partners to ensure issues to do with the environmental protection are properly addressed and good practices promoted at school and community levels.
