Project Summary

The IRC Child Protection (CP) program will provide life-saving immediate support to the extremely vulnerable and conflict-affected children in South Sudan who are exposed to abuse, exploitation, neglect, and violence to support them heal, recover and feel safe again when they do experience harm. The IRC CP program will reach 40,000 (17,903G, 15,106 B, 4,894 W, 2,097 M) people through an integrated response in the priority locations (severity three and four) of Panyijar, Koch, Aweil East, Aweil South and Pariang Counties of Unity State, Northern Bahr el Ghazal State and Ruweng Administrative Area respectively. The proposed CP responses are linked with the strategic objectives one and two of the HRP, the cluster objective one and two and the three priority areas of the CP sub-cluster for the HRP period in 2021. The comprehensive case management service will be provided to children with protection concerns, family tracing and reunification (FTR) services, and alternative care arrangements for unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) and cash assistance to meet the basic need of children. The mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) service will be provided for children and caregivers with psychological distress using a combination of approaches such as safe healing and learning space (SHLS) and schools for social and emotional learning, families make the difference curriculum for parenting skills of caregivers, supporting adolescents and their families in emergencies curriculum (SAFE) for life skills of adolescents, and IRC’s brighter futures model for malnourishment and children with protection concerns. Finally, the community-based child protection (CBCP) structures will establish/strengthen the lower structure of the community, community outreach on CP awareness and COVID-19 prevention messages and capacity building of the frontline staff and community volunteers.

1 - Needs

Among 8.3 million people in need of humanitarian support in South Sudan, an estimated 2.7 million people including 2.3 million children are at risk of violence, exploitation, abuse, and neglect and in acute need of immediate child protection service in 2021 (HNO 2021). The IRC operational locations for CP program are classified as extremely and severely affected locations such as Panyijiar (Severity 4), and the remaining four counties of Koch, Aweil South, Aweil East and Pariang were classified as severity three. According to the HNO 2021, children take up the significant portion of people in need (PiN) in all the targeted counties of IRC such as 58% of 93,700 PiN in Panyijiar, 53% of 96,300 PiN in Koch, 55% of 103,900 PiN in Aweil South, 59% of 235,500 PiN in Aweil East and 56% of 89600 PiN in Pariang (HNO, 2021). As per the IRC’s CP COVID-19 monitoring dashboard (October 2020), physical abuse, emotional abuse, child labor, early marriage and psychological distress were identified as top child protection concerns. Furthermore, the humanitarian situation compounded with the worst nationwide flooding that affected an estimated 1,066,000 people with 126,000 people were in Unity State. Currently in Panyijar County, 4,966 people (3,070 children) have been displaced and settled in primary schools with limited humanitarian response (assessment of schools occupied by floods affected households, December 2020). Moreover, in the second IPC projection period of April to July 2021, an estimated 7.24 million people are likely to face crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. Among other counties, Aweil South County projected likely to be in catastrophe (IPC Phase 5), and Panyijar, Aweil East and Pariang are also likely to be in emergency (IPC Phase 4) and Koch likely to be in crisis (IPC phase 3) acute food insecurity. As a result of multiple adversities that affect children, they will continue to be exposed to numerous protection risks including psychosocial distress, family separation, violence, recruitment by armed groups, and exploitation because of flooding, food insecurity and revenge killing. IRC has been responding to child protection concerns in the targeted five locations focusing on case management, FTR, social-emotional learning (PSS) using various training curriculums for different targets of children, adolescents, and caregivers, and strengthening the capacity of community-based child protection (CBCP) structures. However, due to the enormous CP need in all the operational locations, IRC could not address all the needs in the locations. The IRC has been reunified about 89 UASC with their biological parents in the proposed five locations and currently, there are more than 1330 active FTR and case management cases that are waiting for both services (CPIMS Report, Feb 2021). Even though, there is a high need for CFS participation in five Counties where IRC is currently operating only seven CFSs established due to the constraint of funding. According to the IRC’s assessment in Koch (Dec 2020), high rate of child neglect, psychological distress and lack of better parenting skills identified as key protection issues. The recent community risk mapping (IRC, Jan 2021) conducted in Pariang County also registers that communities experience a high prevalence of child labor, neglect, child marriage and physical violence among other risks and violations. Moreover, IRC has established the community CP structures in all the operational locations but still there are capacity gaps that need a continuous effort to improve on and get the required service from the structure. The CP sub-cluster targets 40,000 children in the IRC targeted five counties during the current HRP 2021 that is committed to reach the targets through compressive and integrated program response. The CP response will focus on the most flood and conflict-affected vulnerable children including children from displaced communities, UASC, and CAAFAG.

2 - Project activities

The IRC proposes life-saving child protection interventions focusing on both protective and preventive modalities through static and mobile service targeting 40,000 (17,903G, 15,106 B, 4,894 W, 2,097 M) most vulnerable and affected populations including children in the host community, IDP, returnees with a disability. The proposed CP responses are linked with the strategic objectives 1 and 2 of the HRP, the cluster objective 1 and 2 and the three priority areas of CP-sub cluster for the HRP period in 2021. The CP intervention will focus on the three program priorities of the sub-cluster which include: comprehensive case management/FTR services, provision of mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services including through mobile teams and strengthening community based child protection (CBCP), capacity building and community outreaches. The IRC will use the revised SS Comprehensive Case Management SOP during COVID-19, PSS guide (UNICEF), SHLS guide, families make the different parenting skills curriculum, SAFE and IRC’s brighter futures model. All the interventions will be aligned with COVID-19 program adaptations of IRC and technical working groups under CP sub-cluster. Planned activities include (a) Comprehensive case management: The IRC will continue to provide case management services including FTR to the most vulnerable children aligned with the SS vulnerability and risk assessment criteria matrix and affected by the flooding, food insecurity and COVID-19. The customized national inter-agency case management forms will be used to manage individual cases, conduct child and family assessments, develop a case plan, provide direct and indirect services to meet needs identified in the assessments, do the case follow up as per the plan, and close cases when goals have been achieved. In addition, revise the existing service mapping and referral pathway in all the operational areas in collaboration with the CP working group. Economic and social reintegration for formally released CAAGAG will be prioritized. To ensure the continuity of life-saving CP service the Covid-19 program adaptation and various guides from the CPSC and technical groups will be applicable. (b) MHPSS: The IRC will also provide PSS/SEL service using different approaches of SHLS, PSS clubs in school, CP help-desk, SAFE and parenting skills training. The IRC will establish new SHLS/CFS and enroll children for PSS/SEL activities. As COVID-19 continues, door-to-door PSS service will be considered as alternative approaches where children are only targeted in their respective homes with PSS activities to avoid overcrowding in the centers. Teachers, PTAs and SHLS facilitators will be trained on UNICEF’s PSS manual and IRC’s SEL and SHLS management standards. (c) CBCP: Finally, the CBCPC will be capacitated to better protect from and respond to abuse, neglect, exploitation and violence of children. The IRC will support the establishment of children and youth groups to ensure their participation in the community level CP interventions, advocacy and their voice to be heard and shape the projects. The CBCPC members will be trained on CPiE, case identification, and referrals to the CP actors. CP helpdesks will be rehabilitated or established in schools, health, and nutrition facilities. In addition, continuous community awareness of CP and COVID-19 prevention messages will be conducted using different platforms. The CP response will be complementing the existing IRC’s nutrition, health, livelihood and GBV programs to ensure comprehensive care. The existing health and nutrition facilities serve as screening and awareness-raising spots and will establish CP help desks. IRC’s GBV centers will serve as a point of referral for rape survivor girls and the livelihood section will support the cash-based intervention. Moreover, the CP team will be part of the integrated mobile team in all locations where IRC is operating.

3 - Project indicators

The designing of proposed CP response is based on the local context and need of the children and their caregivers which is aligned with the CPSC priority areas. The primary objective of this project is to prevent children who are exposed to abuse, exploitation, neglect, and violence to supports them heal, recover and feel safe again when they do experience harm. The CPSC has eight output indicators under the three priority areas where IRC will contribute for all indicators and will measure using the same output indicators with additional IRC’s outcome indicators. The IRC CP program regularly will collect and analyze the CP data for all the indicators using various data collection tools including CPIMS/CPIMS+, SHLS participants' safety and well-being assessment tool, parenting skills training satisfaction assessment tool, SAFE Adolescent Girl and Boy Survey, pre/post training assessment tools, training/event attendance, SHLS/CFS participation attendance. Each projects will have indicator tracking template that can help to see the progress of the indicators on a monthly basis. Moreover, to ensure the quality of case management service the CP Officers/supervisors will review the case files regularly using the standard file checking tool and conduct a weekly meeting with caseworkers. Finally, the IRC monitoring and evaluation team will do a quality assessment as the project happens using various checklist keeping all the confidentiality principle and need to know basis. The CP managers will be responsible to oversee all projects under their responsibility and the CP Coordinator monitor the overall CP program portfolio of IRC South Sudan and provide support to the team. The followings are the indicators:• Number of, girls and boys including children with disabilities provided with focused and non-focused MHPSS and other related services• Number of women and men who receive psychosocial support, parenting messaging and participate in parenting sessions• Number of women, men, boys and girls including with disabilities with protection concerns supported through case management and referral services and CBA• Number of unaccompanied and Separated girls and boys identified, documented, and receiving family tracing and reunification services (UASC) • # of women, men, girls and boys including with disabilities reached through awareness-raising activities • Number of CP referral pathways established /updated in targeted locations

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

IRC is committed to clients and staff safe from harm. It has a safeguarding policy that encompasses the PSEA. The client safeguarding policy mainly focuses on the protection of child and adult clients from exploitation and abuse - physical, verbal, emotional, mental, economic or sexual – by those working for or associated with the IRC. The IRC has two safeguarding focal leads at the country program and two safeguarding champions in each field office who are leading the staff orientations, community awareness and receive any allegation from the staff and clients. All the reporting lines and means are displayed in all the field offices to be accessed for all clients and staff. In addition, the program team disseminates safeguarding/PSEA information to all beneficiaries coming for the IRC services during the community outreach program.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

As part of the IRC’s client responsiveness SOP, particular attention will be given to the vulnerable members of the clients while feedback is collected through both proactive and reactive channels. The outreach and awareness methods will ensure that children are aware of the mechanism, understand its purpose and understand the feedback submission process of focusing on children between the ages of six and 18. In-person awareness-raising sessions will also be conducted by CP staff using child friendly messages. The CP team will develop child friendly communication channels that can be used for children: child friendly suggestion box, child friendly helpdesk service and focus group discussions. For Adolescents, the team will use more typical channels such as a helpdesk. “Trusted Caregivers” of a child will also provide feedback to IRC though they will use the traditional adult communication channels for providing feedback. The feedback mechanism will be inclusive of children with disabilities. There are outreach and awareness methods that accommodate the hearing, seeing, and learning impaired through video, song, image-heavy posters, and leaflets, and in-person communication. The suggestion box will be accessible to physically impaired children and focus group discussions and helpdesks will elicit feedback from impaired children.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The IRC has developed a mechanism of client responsiveness standard operation procedure (SOP) to ensure that the target population is involved in the whole project cycle. The IRC’s objective under responsiveness is to “make our work more responsive to the aspirations of the clients and communities we serve, reflecting the value we place on local leadership and the transference of power and accountability from donors to clients”. The IRC conducts the community consultation to get the feedback of girls, boys, women, and men together with community and government leaders that informs key decisions during project design, start-up, implementation and close-out. The community sensitization and information-sharing sessions on the project will be conducted from the very beginning of the project design and will continue throughout the project cycle. Design Phase: IRC plans the client feedback channels that will be used during implementation to inform project adjustments, with specific consideration to children’s safety and accessibility in using feedback channels. Feedback from past projects or other organizations will be used to inform project design.Start-Up Phase: IRC plans the proactive (survey, a focus group discussion, an individual interview) and reactive (suggestion boxes, hotlines, email addresses, office walk-in) channels that will be used to collect feedback from the clients during project implementation. IRC will use best practice guidance to plan how they will record, manage and refer the feedback that we collect during implementation with particular attention for reporting and responding to allegations of abuse and exploitation, including SEA, and other sensitive complaints. IRC will inform its clients about the project and the feedback channels. Particular attention to be paid to how IRC/partners communicate this with children and their caregivers. Implementation Phase: The IRC proactively collects and acts on feedback on a quarterly basis. IRC will manage at least one reactive feedback channel through which clients can provide feedback/lodge complaints. Particular attention to be paid to the ability of children and their caregivers to use the reactive channel. IRC will explain to its clients within the timeframe agreed on how their feedback was taken into account. Close-Out Phase: IRC will interpret the sex and age disaggregated feedback data identifying its implications for close-out and future projects. Explanations will be provided to the clients how their feedback was considered within the time agreed.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

All feedback will be confidentially filed and registered into the feedback registry. The designated client feedback focal point and/or M&E focal person will review the registry on a quarterly basis in each field location in liaison with the M&E Coordinator to ensure that all the feedback is addressed. According to the IRC feedback handling cycle, the collected feedback data will be compiled, interpreted, then decisions and planning will be made on what actions to take in response to the feedback. This decision could be made in consultation with a group of clients (such as a stakeholder reference group – a small group of clients representing the interests of children and caregivers). Depending on the nature and content of the feedback, client feedback will then need to either be communicated and acted upon internally (by the IRC) or referred to another organization or institution. Clients feedback that is related to the IRC programs and operations should be communicated internally to different categories of staff and with a different level of urgency (critical, high, medium and low). Any decision will be followed by immediate action for instance programmatic complain like aid entitlement missing or delayed, the timing or location of IRC services or timelines of IRC staff and partners will be addressed with immediate effect. Then those decisions will be explained and discussed with clients through different means depending on whether the feedback received was individual or collective, sensitive or non-sensitive. Confidentiality and security of the clients are of high importance and have to be considered when communicating the IRC response. Clients' feedback that is not related to the IRC programs and/ or operations should be as far as possible, referred to another organization or institution which is provided this type of aid or services in this given location. If there are not such organizations the IRC should inform and explain to the client that we are not in a position to act or to respond to the request. Finally the monitoring and evaluation unit will continue reviewing and monitoring the progress and impact of the action upon clients.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

The IRC anticipates the risks that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during distribution as a result of the distance to humanitarian service location, beneficiaries’ exposure to sexual violence, beneficiaries may not be using complaint and feedback mechanisms and interference of external stakeholders like local government/administration in the impartial selection of beneficiaries. IRC will continue to work with the CP sub-cluster, humanitarian agencies, local government, and community structures to respond to child protection need by mainstreaming protection principles in every response. The do no harm, promoting non-discrimination, meaningful access, safety, dignity, and participation of children and caregivers will be given a high priority. To ensure the participation of all beneficiaries throughout the project cycle the program will set clear targets disaggregated by gender, age and vulnerability status including disability so that anyone will not be left out in the dignified CP program responses. IRC has an integrated mobile response to actively reach marginalized population in hard-to-reach areas to minimize risk associated with beneficiaries traveling long distance for service and increase the access of the service. This mobile response and prepositioning of program consumables in the field location would increase access for the beneficiaries especially in the areas where inaccessible because of rains and poor road conditions, active armed conflict, restrictions of movements because of Covid-19 prevention measures and bureaucratic impediments.
