1 - Needs

Sudan is host to one of the largest South Sudanese refugee populations in the region, with a total of 926,000 persons living in the country in November 2022, the majority of them are from South Sudan (67%). According to the Sudan HNO – 2023, the disaggregated refugees by sex and age we get 48% male, 52% Female, 58% Children, 37% Adults, and 5% elderly.According to the UN Refugee Agency (UNHCR), there are more than 73,000 Ethiopian refugees living in Sudan since November 2020.This new wave joins more than 130,000 Eritrean and Ethiopian refugees and asylum seekers living in protracted situations in camps and urban areas across Aj Jazirah, Gedaref, Kassala, Red Sea and Sennar states. Moreover, over 110,000 refugees of various nationalities (Ethiopian, Eritrean, Congolese, Somali, Yemeni and Syrian) are living in Khartoum together with over 190,000 South Sudanese refugees, all in dire need of assistance (Sudan - Country Refugee Response Plan (CRP) - (January - December 2021). In this context, it is expected that over 1.14 million refugees will be living in Sudan by the end of 2021. This situation disproportionately affects women and girls, who usually bear the brunt of adverse coping strategies. Refugees in Sudan need multi-sectoral interventions to address their specific vulnerabilities, bolster their self-reliance over the long term, and maintain and fulfil their rights as refugees. Investments in local infrastructure and strengthening of gender-sensitive education, health, nutrition and water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services are also needed to ensure that local service systems can absorb the increasing needs of refugees and host communities, and further strengthen social cohesion and peaceful coexistence so both communities can thrive (Sudan - Country Refugee Response Plan (CRP) - (January - December 2021). Apart from basic needs such as health and food, refugee children need protection in their families, communities and when interacting with humanitarian services. Children make up over 56% of the refugee population. As refugees struggle to meet their basic needs in an increasingly difficult economic environment, children are at heightened risk of harassment, exploitation, neglect and abuse. Unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) are particularly vulnerable in this regard, as they lack the support structure and protection of their parents. Additionally, refugee youth suffer from a lack of education opportunities or vocational skills training, making it difficult for them to find a livelihood when they become adults. In addition, there is a limited amount of recreational or social activities targeting adolescents and youth, having a serious negative their development and psychosocial wellbeing. These refugees are coming from States with already high health needs. Men and women play different roles in the assessment of health and nutrition, Education needs, water use in a community, etc., it is important to consult both men and women, seeing that they have different roles in society. For example, Men might have a better knowledge of the use of water for livestock and women often have better knowledge of the amount of water used on a household level. The combined information will give more accurate information on the total need for water for a community. Equally, assessing the need for wood women often have better information on cooking practices, whilst men might have better knowledge of wood used for construction.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Through providing timely, sustainable, and equitable access to safe multi-sectoral services, and through pairing multi-sectoral programming together like Health, Nutrition, WASH, General protection, child protection, GBV, and Education sector activities, aim to provide packages of integrated services target the refugees affected populations. Sphere standards and EiE as well as other humanitarian standards will be applied in the design and implementation of the proposed interventions to ensure that the target program participants receive the assistance that meets the set humanitarian standards. Sudan refugee response standards will also be followed to the extent possible, within the context of emergency response. 5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project is going to address the environmental issues by considering the local environmental context of the targeted area, and a continuous assessment for potential environmental impacts will be carried out throughout the project life. The project is also going to do mitigation measures by compensating for negative impacts (if any) and enhancing environmental benefits in the project by practising some measurements, the followings are part of it: • Locally available material is recommended provided that it is not impacting negatively on the environment. • Include environmental awareness sessions in projects, and support solid waste management schemes • Reduce packaging material and discourage the use of plastic bags to reduce the amount of waste generated. • Raise awareness of refugees on environmental protection and conservation • Reduce wood consumption by supplying iron pipes and using environmentally friendly material for construction and rehabilitation, and promoting alternatives such as Fuel Efficient Stoves, and fuel-efficient cooking techniques. Consider also alternative energy sources such as solar panels for lighting and pumps.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection will both be mainstreamed into sectoral activities and also implemented as a sector program. As mainstreaming issue, the project will ensure that each activity will not expose the most vulnerable groups including women and children to protection risks. Community consultation will be conducted throughout the project cycle. Community feedback and complaint mechanism will be set up. The feedback mechanism will ensure anonymity and confidentiality to protect the reporting person. In cases of GBV or child abuse cases, support including referral to other service providers will be provided and the survivor will be ensured protection to prevent exposure from further harm. To a possible extent, community discussions will be held separately between women and men to create a safe space for everybody to participate equally. Discussions for children will always be held separately from the ones for adults. The project will ensure the inclusion of the most vulnerable groups within the target communities in close coordination and collaboration with key stakeholders. Selection of villages will be done in consultation with line ministries, HAC and other relevant agencies such as UNFPA, and the department of welfare in the Ministry of social welfare to ensure the needs of people with special needs are taken care of. The safety of beneficiaries, other project stakeholders and WV staff is not negotiable and the project will ensure implementation activities will not put beneficiaries, WV staff or project stakeholders at security risks. Activities for children will be done during the day with the presence of caregivers or other responsible adults from the communities. While the project will prioritize female and child-headed families to participate in project activities or benefit from the assistance, it will also ensure such inclusion does not create another type of exclusion. The discussion will be held to inform communities on beneficiaries’ selection criteria. The discussion will involve different groups within the communities including women and children. To adhere to Humanitarian Accountability Standard, a feedback mechanism will be set up using suggestion boxes or other means preferred by the communities. Program staff will use their current safeguarding and accountability standards and policies. Orientation to new staff will include a Code of Conduct and safeguarding principles to prevent exploitation of the beneficiaries.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

WVS is part of the RCF, and there is a direct reporting and coordinated mechanism. At that level, regular updates are and will be provided to the team. The RCF brings together all other RCF partners. WVS also has its incident reporting mechanism from the field to the highest administrative level. WVS management will then reach out to the funding with any outcome of an investigation Accountability to beneficiaries will be two-fold; to direct beneficiary groups such as refugee groups and accountability to host community leadership. Accountability to direct beneficiaries will similarly be based on MoU with group members and regular joint review of progress. Accountability to host community leadership will be through MoUs that WVI represented by ‘Village Focal Points’ - will sign with community representatives. The MoUs spell out the roles of the organization as well as the role and contribution of the communities. In addition to being accountability tools; MoUs enable WVI to communicate decisions such as criteria for the selection of beneficiaries and project locations. MoU is a reference point for issues that arise during implementation. MoUs have enabled WVI to hold communities accountable for commitments they make at the onset of projects. Participation: This programme will be designed in consultation with refugee and host communities' leadership and government entities. In addition to regular consultation as part of ongoing interventions, refugee and host communities and government leaders were informed of this project and involved in selecting beneficiaries and prioritizing interventions. At the project onset, kickoff meetings at the village level will be facilitated by project staff to further publicize the programme and communicate opportunities for participation. Feedback mechanisms: The program will institute feedback mechanisms such as ‘suggestion boxes’; quarterly feedback forums and ‘hotlines’ where feasible. The ‘hotline’ approach has been piloted successfully in Darfur. The project staff will be tasked with mobilizing communities – particularly women – to participate and will be responsible for investigating factors that hamper participation. These factors will have come through normal feedback mechanisms and/or through discovered through interactions. The project staff will work towards ensuring that beneficiaries are aware of their rights and entitlements, ensuring beneficiaries have timely access to relevant and clear information and they are satisfied with the opportunities they have to influence the response. The program staff will consult with beneficiary communities’ on the design, implementation and monitoring of complaints-handling processes. Program staff Welcome and accept complaints, and communicate how the mechanism can be accessed and the scope of issues it can address. The complaints will be managed in a timely, fair and appropriate manner that prioritizes the safety of the complainant and those affected at all stages.
