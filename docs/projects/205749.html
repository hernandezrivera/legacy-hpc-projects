Project Summary

Peace Corps proposes an emergency education response focusing on Scaling up of emergency life-saving integrated quality Education for conflict and flood affected school going children(3-17 years) from IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities in Counties of Twic County of Warrap State, Yirol West County of Lake State and Pibor GPAA of Jonglei state. This project targets 6,005 beneficiaries of which 5777 will be school going children aged 3-17 years (2,833boys, 2,944 girls), and 228 adults (132 Male, 96 Female) including disabled and marginalised children from IDPs, Returnees and host vulnerable communities. This project will run for a period of 12 months covering the three terms and the targeted 12 schools in selected counties. The proposed education project will incorporate and complement life-saving components such as psycho-social support, disaster risk reduction mechanism, and availability of gender-sensitive WASH facilities in schools is essential for keeping children in the classroom and ensuring their safety and well-being, with clean and secure separate toilets for girls and boys and hand-washing facilities to both students and teachers. PCO will collaborate with other clusters like WASH, Protection, Nutrition, Health and stakeholders to ensure coherent, coordinated and integrated responses driven by the assessed education needs of affected populations. PCO will also synergize with health and nutrition cluster partners who manage the health facilities and OTPs centers in the counties of operation, by offering outreach services to supported schools.
The Key activities of this project will include Construction/rehabilitation of temporary learning spaces (TLSs), providing psycho-social support, providing learning materials, providing WASH facilities, provision of MHM to Adolescent girls to maintain their dignity, training PTA members and teachers on psycho-social support, provision of ECD services to 3-5 years children, training of teachers on EiE, basic pedagogy, training of community education committee members and Education officials in school management, monitoring and record keeping and training of youth and adolescents (14-17 years) in life-skills and basic vocational courses. PCO will also set-up a simple, safe and accessible CBCM for SEA and protection of school children to add efficiency to strengthen reporting mechanism by incorporating local solutions into the overall SEA reporting system. The organization will involve SMCs, PTAs and Community leaders in community sensitization efforts focusing on access to education services, school WASH and protection messages, and prevention of SEA with the aim to increase access to education for boys and girls as well as preventing and reducing protection risks for children on their way to and from school. Community members will also be involved in the design of TLS, identification of a safe location for construction of TLS.
The project will also mainstream gender, GBV, conflict sensitivity and protection with hopes of contributing towards protection of children from risks, GBV and other protection challenges that have a potential of disrupting their education, enhance their psycho-social development, improve hygiene and sanitation and alleviate strains on their academic performance. PCO is the education cluster Co-Lead in WBeG and also the CCCM Cluster Coordinator for Pibor GPAA. PCO is currently implementing education project in 44 schools in Wau and Jur River counties supported by WFP ending in December 2024, implementing integrated Education and Nutrition project in 5 schools in counties of Twic and Pibor GPAA under SSHF SA1 funded by UNOCHA which will end on 30th Nov.2023 and implementing MYRP Programme in 40 schools funded by ECW since 1st June 2023 to 30th June 2024 in Counties of Gogrial West,Tonj East and Tonj North of Warrap State, PCO is currently implementing EiE in 5 school funded by GPE in Counties Wau and Jur River of WBeG State.

1. Needs

According to Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO)-2024 estimated that more than two-thirds of south Sudan’s population, 8.5 million people, are in dire need of humanitarian assistance and protection in South Sudan. The HNO estimated that 2.64 million people, about 50 per cent of the 5.59 million people in need of emergency life-saving integrated quality Education services. The Education system and infrastructure in south Sudan are fragile with limited equitable access to quality education for boys, girls and youth aged 3-17 years who represent 44% of the population (According to HNO 2022 report). This includes an estimated 3.4 million (51% are girls) school-aged children in needs of education services estimated to be out of school representing one of the highest rates of out-of-school children in the world, have no access to pre-primary, primary and secondary education in 2022. South Sudan’s education system has been negatively affected by protracted conflict, economic crisis, inadequate access to quality learning spaces, teaching and learning materials, inadequate teachers training, limited sanitation facilities at school which causes displacement, low enrolment and high drop-out rates. The negative impact on children has also increased as the education system is under severe strain due to the government’s inability to pay teachers regularly or provide any learning materials to support them to perform their functions or incentive to keep them motivated. Similarly, the overall food security situation has further deteriorated. Many schools have been destroyed, children and teachers are too scared to attend because they fear for their life, while some parents are no longer able to pay for uniforms and books as they deal with unemployment and the effects of a chronically devalued currency. The continued influx of returnees from Sudan has continued to stretch the already dire resources. These factors have led to a decrease in enrolment rates of school age children and an increase in the drop-out rate of school age children at the primary school level and also exposed them to different form of gender –based violence including forced marriage and teenage pregnancies especially amongst girls in WBeG state. Specific objectives under this project include increased access to quality learning opportunities for boys, girls and youth aged (3-17-year) in safe and protective environments. The intervention logic is that given the situation of the (largely man-made) disaster in South Sudan, there is an urgent need to support schools with interventions for ensuring equitable access to quality education and preventing school dropouts. Increased access to quality learning opportunities helps increase enrolment of Out of school children and improve retention in Schools. The construction of learning spaces will be created with due consideration to environmental concerns and the materials used will be those locally available. This project is linked to the general strategy of saving lives and providing timely integrated multi- sectorial assistance and reinforcing protection, particularly connected to the 2024 education cluster strategy by ensuring that children and youth (3-17 years) have access to quality formal and non-formal education and that girls and boys already in school are retained, improved school attendance and by promoting emergency WASH in schools and mainstreaming child protection, psycho-social support, hygiene, and GBV. The available limited sanitation facilities are not gender disaggregated and do not take care of the needs of those with disabilities. The project will also contribute to lower the risk of abuse and exploitation of children including early marriage, child labour and forced recruitment into armed groups. PCO will collaborate with other clusters like WASH, Protection, Nutrition, Health and stakeholders to ensure coherent, coordinated and integrated responses driven by the assessed education needs of affected populations.

2. Project activities

The overall objective of this proposed plan is to focuses on Scaling up of emergency life-saving integrated quality Education for conflict and flood affected to 6005 school going children and youths children (3-17 years) and adults (51% girls) from IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities through implementation of the 3 Education cluster objectives. With the Key activities: Ensuring to Enroll a total of 5,777(2,833boys, 2,944 girls) of which an estimated (51% are girls) children in schools/learning centers, Establish/rehabilitate 12 temporary learning spaces 2 classrooms each, Provide 300 (180 girls) children aged 3-5 children with ECD kits, Establish/ rehabilitate 24 sensitive gender-segregated WASH Facilities fully equipped with hand washing facilities, Distribute sanitation and hygiene kits (MHM) to 280 adolescent girls, Train 500(280 girls) adolescent and youth on life skill and vocational courses, Distribute learning and recreational material to children and youth (6-17 years), Train 72 (26 female) teachers in EiE basic pedagogy and child centred methodology and updated on PSS, Train 500 (280girls) youths and adolescents (14-17 years) on life–skills and basic vocational. Train 132 (65 female) PTAs/SMC member and local education Officials on school management, psychosocial support (PSS) and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), Print and distribute appropriate Information, Education and Communication (IEC) materials) in all the 12 targeted schools with a population of 6005 learners, Teachers, PTAs and Education personnel. Promote enrolment and recruitment through “back to school” and “back to teaching” through use of radio talk show campaign, Establishment of School safety audits aimed at identifying and mitigating protection risks on the way to school and inside school and Train teachers on life-skills, psychological first aid and disaster risk reduction. PCO will also ensure that normal routine monitoring of all the activities implemented in the 12 proposed schools throughout the entire project life cycle by both PCO staff and other externally by stakeholders to ensure activities are in line with the project design document and are successfully achieved. Reporting will also be done as required by donor and by the cluster through use of 5Ws shared monthly.
