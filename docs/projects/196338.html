Project Summary

"The strategic focus of Save the Children is to promote quality, and durable solutions to essential WASH services, ensure safe and inclusive access to WASH making WASH infrastructures functional, and resilient, and develop sustainable community engagement. Save the Children proposes to continue the existing WASH services in the four camps (17, 20, 25, and 27) that will be safe, inclusive, and resilient through provisions of existing WASH infrastructure functional by adequate repair and maintenance. Water quality in households will be prioritized as the critical stage of the safe water chain, and provisions made for gender-specific safe WASH spaces as wee institutional needs. Save the Children will improve latrines and bathing cubicles through upgradation and maintenance, and make them functional, usable, and durable. Enhanced monitoring of the facilities will ensure safety, and privacy and addresses the specific needs of vulnerable community members. Continue hygiene promotion activities in the working areas to reduce risky hygiene practices and conditions for women, children, and men.
Environment-friendly fecal sludge and solid waste management will also be considered a priority issue to deal with 200 tons of fecal sludge and 112 tons of solid waste produced daily in all camps. Save the Children will manage solid waste and fecal sludge in assigned camps which is one of the commitments of SC to limiting negative environmental impacts through robust planning, risk mitigation, and monitoring that will prevent any health hazards and outbreaks. WASH program focuses on a cost-efficient participatory program delivery to enhance the sustainability of the program.
Nonetheless, the program will ensure conflict-sensitive, sustainable, and gender-segregated water and sanitation services through innovative solutions for the Rohingya camps and adjacent host communities. SC will give special attention to the person with disabilities by making inclusive facilities and will prioritize water supplies inside the latrines and bathing spaces using renewable low-cost energy sources and preposition emergency latrine kits. The operation of the response will be continuously reviewed to ensure that it is appropriate to the scale and scope of the response and that a sustainable structure is in place. Save the Children’s WASH program ensures readiness to adopt situation-based needs in camps for all types of participants.
Save the Children will ensure community engagement at all levels of interventions with the active participation of the user groups. Save the Children will work to increase community participation and non-budgetary activities which will strengthen community ownership. Examples include WASH committees comprised of community members (with appropriate gender representation) who receive instruction on the proper maintenance and utilization of all facilities, thereby capacitating them to take over their own resources. Different groups like children, adolescent girls, the WASH committee, and non-paid youth volunteers will engage in new roles for bringing positive changes in the community. Save the children will emphasize on the women, girls, older people and people with disabilities engagement in WASH committee representation to be opened the windows to recieve the feedback from them and address the challenges of these group of peoples. Save the Children strictly follows the construction of climate-resilient infrastructures and keep the contingency plan, and contingency stock updated which can be activated to respond immediately to any disasters. WASH will be a focus for SC; a critical service to be available and accessible to both Rohingya and host communities. "

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"Over the last four decades, Rohingya refugees have been fleeing in successive waves to Bangladesh with 925,380 refugees now residing in 34 camps in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazillas. The massive influx of Rohingya refugees, extreme weather events and the economic ramifications of the COVID-19 pandemic are some of the key factors affecting WASH in Cox’s Bazar. These events compounded existing structural vulnerabilities in Cox’s Bazar’s Teknaf and Ukhiya Upazillas, affecting both the host community and the refugee population. Rohingya refugees face a variety of problems when accessing water points, latrines, and bathing facilities. This shows that even if Sphere standards are met in terms of the number of WASH facilities, access to them is not guaranteed. For water points, access issues include waiting times, distance to water points, and water points not functioning. For latrines and bathing facilities, they include lack of lighting, lack of gender separation, and facilities not functioning. Access is a concern, particularly for people with disabilities. At present, the sector context of solid waste management is high priority work due to the excess generation of waste in camps. Solid waste is often dumped in narrow spaces between shelters and landfill sites. The provision of appropriate solid waste management activities through active community participation is highly necessary for the camps. Based on these needs, the following are the priorities for WASH services in the camps: hygiene promotion behavior change intervention, considering equitable access for vulnerable people (children, people with disabilities, PLW (Pregnant and Lactating Women), old aged), durable solutions like water supply systems/network using renewable energy, fecal sludge management unit, female-friendly sanitation spaces, child participation in planning and monitoring program, and provision for menstrual hygiene management. Women and adolescent girls continue to report feeling unsafe using WASH facilities that are not separated by gender and not well-light at night. To avoid being seen bathing and defecating, women reportedly wash and defecate inside their shelters, restrict food, and water intake and restrict movement during their menstrual period. 12% of refugees and 26% of HC reported not having enough water to meet domestic needs and on average 51 persons share one bathing unit due to lack of space. Only 66% of women and girls’ access MHM kits as per standards. Only 51% of refugees are disposing of waste in the designated area resulting in 27% of households reporting garbage near their shelters. Regarding outbreaks, cases of scabies and dengue have respectively been multiplied by three and by twelve in 2022 compared to 2021.Regarding host communities, according to the survey report of WASH Mapping and Participatory WASH Planning for the Host Communities at Cox’s Bazar District (UNICEF & DPHE, 2020), no advanced water sources exists in Ukhiya. Ukhiya is one of the high-risk areas in terms of drinking water quality. 23% of schools in Teknaf and 11% of schools in Ukhiya had basic hygiene facilities. Furthermore, 28% have limited access to sanitation at schools. In Ukhiya 64% have limited access to handwashing facilities at schools and in Teknaf 65% have limited access to handwashing facilities at schools. Lack of awareness about the benefits of handwashing with soap and public health implications could be improved along with habits of handwashing. The use of safe water, safe sanitation facilities, food hygiene, personal hygiene, and environmental hygiene could be improved for all students, teachers, and School Management Committees (SMC). "

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project proposes to continue the existing WASH services at SCI’s assigned areas [four camps of Teknaf and Ukhia Upazila’s without any overlapping with other actors]. But we will continue the discussion with other actors and Area Focal Agency to find out more efficient way of rationalization process. Save the Children will strengthen the coordination and productive relationships with the government authority, AFA, INGO, and NNGO actors in camps and the host communities. SC will continue to work closely with the Department of Public Health Engineering (DPHE), the WASH sector and the technical working groups to ensure adequate design and implementation of WASH infrastructures and software interventions are aimed at Rohingya populations. At the field level, coordination with site management, WASH focal, local authorities and the CIC will continue. Through the project, SC will assess regular basis to identify the non-functional WASH facilities and improve & repair these to ensure safe water, sanitation facilities hygiene practices. The project aims to ensure quality, regular, sufficient, equitable, and dignified access to WASH services for all women, men, girls, and boys, inclusive of all age groups and persons with disabilities, in the Rohingya refugee camps and targeted host communities and build their resilience through training and capacity development. Save the Children will enhance community engagement in all stages of project activities through the WASH committee, user groups, nonpaid youth volunteers, adolescent girls’ groups, and children groups which will grow community ownership of resources and subsequently make projects sustainable.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

WASH in communities often overlooks gender-related needs, resulting in poor basic services and increased risks for women, adolescent girls and children. SC believes it is important to address the needs of adolescent girls, women, children, persons with disabilities, and other vulnerable groups in preventing WASH, Dengue, and COVID-19-related infection in healthcare settings. To this end, safeguards will be integrated into all phases of the project to ensure that the needs of women, men, adolescent girls, children, older and other vulnerable groups are considered and addressed. The assets are available to all vulnerable groups and the Supervisory Committee is actively looking for these groups. SC will establish a useful feedback and complaints mechanism for all users and will ensure a strong linkage with safeguarding and protection staff to address any protection issues.

3.2 - Gender

Gender-segregated WASH facilities will be ensured. Awareness-raising activities will be carried out through context-sensitive and local environment approaches, for example, by ensuring that female staff and volunteers are available to work with beneficiaries. Feminine hygiene volunteers will be recruited for community messaging, especially for teenage women and girls. They demonstrate the hand-washing process and discuss the frequency of handwashing including other domains of hygiene SC will ensure the opportunity to provide feedback through established feedback mechanisms. and complaints. All actions are designed considering the specific vulnerabilities of children of different ages, abilities, and backgrounds, gender barriers to services, and the various effects of the crisis on boys and girls. The technical team will ensure that all actions are addressed equally to girls, boys, women, and men, considering the gender perspective and gender balance.

3.4 - Diversity

Save the Children will implement integrated WASH packages in the assigned Rohingya Camps that are interrelated areas of Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene. Being critical to human life, ensuring access to potable (clean) water for drinking, cooking and domestic purpose is an obvious priority. SC will ensure that water sources in the target locations are functional [such as deep tube wells, and water supply pipe networks], and conduct regular testing of the quality of water . SC will improve and repair toilets, bathing space with the MHM, and handwashing facilities including the distribution of soap immediately after receiving it from AFA. Environment-friendly fecal sludge and solid waste management consider a priority activity. Conduct cleaning campaigns including drain and waste bin cleaning on a regular basis. SC will provide training to WASH committees, Maji, Imam, user groups, non-paid youth volunteers, and adolescent girls.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Save the Children believes that accountability not only gives voice to children and communities but also creates an opportunity for community members to influence relevant decisions affecting them, and whether and how Save the Children work with them. In practice, accountability covers – (a) regular, timely and accessible information sharing, (b) participation – discussing together, deciding together and working together, and (c) proactively seeking feedback from children and communities and handling their complaints, (d) monitoring, evaluation and learning, and (e) building staff competencies for accountable programming. Generally, Feedback and Complaints (F&Cs) received by Save the Children are processed, analyzed and referred to relevant sectors, external agencies (within 24-hour for Child Safeguarding-related and other complaints that require an urgent response). Save the Children reports on F&Cs publishing a monthly Accountability Snapshot and quarterly Accountability report

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Save the Children has an integrated contingency plan for all sectors with the WASH sector to respond to disasters and regularly conduct training and simulation for the Emergency Response Team. Save the Children also has the preparedness actions fixed and agreed upon by relevant sectors/functions such as Finance, HR, Admin & logistics, IT, resource mobilization, safety & security, Media Comms, etc. We keep some emergency materials for ensuring WASH support in any emergency [e.g. construction materials, Water treatment plant, aqua tab, hygiene, kitchen kit, etc]. As part of emergency preparedness, we conducted training for youth volunteers in camps. In designing the response interventions, SC considers the risk and vulnerabilities at the local level and adopts do no harm principles, and considers environmental compliance.
