Project Summary

The prolonged crisis in Syria, coupled with economic instability and increasing food insecurity, has profoundly affected the nutritional well-being of vulnerable groups. The 2024 HNO reveals that 12.9 million people face food insecurity, with over 5.6 million children and PBWGs urgently requiring vital nutrition services. The dire situation has arisen due to a three-fold surge in the prevalence of acute malnutrition in children under 5 over the past five years (escalation from 1.7% in 2019 to 4.7%), as well as elevated prevalence of maternal malnutrition across Syria, leaving over 50% of governorates with an acute malnutrition prevalence exceeding WHO’s threshold of 5% . Consequently, 508,892 children are estimated to be in need of life-saving treatment services for AM (representing an additional 370,000 children suffering from wasting compared to 2019 137,000). Simultaneously, stunting rates have consistently risen across Syria, from 12.6% in 2019 to 16.1% in 2023 impacting 650,000 children under the age of 5. The 2023 SMART survey highlighted a concerning decline in the nutritional status across specific governorates, notably in Deir-Ez-zour, Aleppo, Idleb, Hama, Lattakia, and Rural Damascus. This heightened vulnerability prompts the WFP to prioritize individuals mostly at-risk, including those with disabilities, through a comprehensive approach that addresses the underlying causes of undernutrition.
WFP will prioritize delivering lifesaving curative and preventive nutrition services to the most vulnerable children and PBWGs in locations under severity 3 and above. WFP aims to target 70% of the cluster’s target for treatment of moderate acute malnutrition in children under 5, and 100% of the sector’s target for treatment of acute malnutrition in PBWGs (overall 80% of HNO figure). Accordingly,161,890 beneficiaries (children under 5 and PBWGs) will be targeted in HCT locations and 100,087 in NWS (A 62:38 proportional breakdown of the total target ). WFP will simultaneously continue delivering nutrition preventive services in coordination with the food assistance and CMAM platforms ,targeting 159,559 children under 2 and PBWGs in HCT and 75,441 children under 2 and PBWGs in NWS (A 68:32 proportional breakdown of the total target ).
WFP’s M&E are integral components of the programme cycle. Through meticulous monitoring by dedicated field staff and third-party services, WFP ensures optimal implementation of nutrition services, especially in hard-to-reach areas, using a wide range of data sets collected, including quantitative and qualitative data through on-site monitoring, Focus group discussions and post-distribution surveys.
Accordingly, regular reporting and analysis of field findings provide crucial insights, guiding strategic decisions aligned with the organization and sector’s overall objectives.
Sector Objective 1: Improved access and quality to lifesaving preventive nutrition specific and sensitive interventions for 185,000 children (girls and boys) under five years of age and 50,000 pregnant and lactating women.
1-Provision of nutrition specific preventive services to children (boys and girls) aged 6 - 59 months, Adolescent girls, Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLWs)WFP aims to target 185,000 children aged 6-23 months with preventive lipid-based nutrient supplements (LNS-MQ) through two platforms: children within families prioritized for WFP’s monthly food assistance and those in remote rural areas accessed through WFP’s CMAM program. Additionally, WFP aims to enhance dietary diversity for 50,000 vulnerable PBWGs through voucher provision conditional to food, implemented in coordination with UNFPA for access to critical antenatal and postnatal care.
Sector Objective 2: Improved access and quality of lifesaving curative nutrition services to 194,510 moderately malnourished girls and boys between the ages of 6 and 59 months and 67,467 pregnant and lactating women (PLW)
