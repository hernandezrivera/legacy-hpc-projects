Project Summary

Rohingya camp: UNICEF aims to reduce the risk of mortality and morbidity by improving the nutritional status of vulnerable groups (children, adolescents, and women) through the prevention and treatment of malnutrition. A total of 14,100 children (6,909 boys; 7,191 girls and 200 with disability) under-five years with severe wasting will access treatment services across 34 integrated nutrition facilities (INF) and two stabilization centers integrated into primary health centers in 25 camps. UNICEF will also procure and be responsible for nutrition supply chain management for entire 33 camps including UNHCR-supported 11 INFs in 8 camps. A total of 81,654 caregivers of children 0-23 months will receive counseling and messaging on Maternal, Infant and Young Child Feeding (MIYCF) care and practices. The MIYCF and care practices will be reinforced through behavior change communication, cooking, and responsive feeding demonstrations. Monthly growth monitoring and promotion sessions are also conducted in all the INFs. To further promote early stimulation and positive parenting, early childhood care and development activities are integrated into the INFs targeting children 0-3 years old. In addition, iron folic supplementation and nutrition education are provided to pregnant women and adolescents for the prevention of anemia. Similarly, vitamin A supplementation and deworming are provided to the targeted children. At community level, community nutrition volunteers will conduct home visits for active case identification of children with severe wasting. The CNV also conducts community mobilization activities and follow-up visits to support treatment adherence. UNICEF also supports community nutrition awareness sessions with mother-to-mother and adolescent support groups. Furthermore, homestead gardening is promoted to increase access to access to diversified and nutritious diets. Overall, UNICEF will provide a comprehensive nutrition training package (including cross-cutting issues) to all existing staff and volunteers who have not received the training before for effective program delivery.
Host Community: UNICEF will continue working with the Government and partners to improve nutrition at scale in the host community as a part of humanitarian-development-peace building nexus. To ensure quality nutrition services, Upazila nutrition supervisors and counselors from Ukhiya and Teknaf, will support nutrition services in Upazila health complexes and the union health and family welfare centers. Among others, frontline service providers (e.g. Community health care provider, FWA, HA etc) will be trained in growth monitoring and promotion, infant and young child feeding counseling, vitamin A supplementation and reporting. UNICEF will continue providing essential nutrition supplies for the prevention and treatment of malnutrition to under five children and women. Community engagement activities including support to mothers/caregivers to improve the quality of complementary foods through mother-to-mother support groups, cooking demonstrations and Mukhe bhaat ceremonies will be implemented. UNICEF will leverage on development support for nutrition programming in host communities. Furthermore, UNICEF will continue collaborating with the Department of Agricultural Extension , the Civil Surgeon Office, Department of Secondary Education, and local NGOs to build the capacities of households on homestead gardening and adolescent nutrition in schools. UNICEF will continue supporting the multisectoral District and Upazila Nutrition Coordination Committees for governance and management of nutrition programs.
Moreover, UNICEF will provide technical support and coordinate with the Sector through SAG and TWGs. UNICEF will adhere to sector standards and response-wide guidelines, including harmonized salary structures, and cross-cutting guidelines, and will follow sector protocols for both wasting management and preventive interventions.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Refugee Camp: Humanitarian needs in the refugee camps in Cox's Bazar are driven by multiple factors, including the protracted Rohingya refugee crisis and vulnerability to climate-induced natural hazards such as cyclones, floods, landslides, and thunderstorms. The situation may be aggravated by poor living conditions, conflicts, and psychosocial distress, and hence pushing children and women into a spiraling cycle of malnutrition. In addition, the population has continued to grow nearly seven years into the Rohingya refugee crisis, with an estimated 960,258 refugees currently residing in the Rohingya camps in Cox’s Bazar. A recent wave of approximately 50,000 new arrivals in Cox's Bazar District has also heightened the need for urgent support (ISCG population estimated as of September 2024). Nutrition will focus on the most vulnerable groups including children agead 0 to 59 months, pregnant and lactating women, adolescent girls (age 10 to 19 years) while including those with disabilities. According to SENS 2023, wasting among children 6-59 months has increased from 12.3 to 15.1 percent, and severe wasting from 0.7 to 2.1 percent in 2023 compared to 2022 and hence the highest since the 2017 influx and exceeded the WHO-UNICEF emergency thresholds. The 2024 severe wasting admissions have increased by 18 percent compared to 2023, putting approximately 14,100 children at risk of death. Stunting among children aged 6-59 months is also high (41.3 percent). Furthermore, more than one-third (38.1 percent) of children under five years suffer from anemia. The prevalence of anemia among women of reproductive age is at 24.1 percent. The determinants of malnutrition include poor infant and young child feeding (IYCF) and caring practices, poor diets, household food insecurity, susceptibility to diseases such as diarrhea, acute respiratory infections (ARI), fever, insufficient access to WASH, limited health care, and nutrition services, maternal age, early marriages, and suboptimal birth spacing (Source: SMART/SENS 2017-2023, NCA 2019). Nearly 70 percent of children are exclusively breastfed while only 26.6 percent of children 6-23 months are consuming 5 out of 8 recommended food groups indicating that more than two-thirds of children are living in food poverty and lack access to a nutritious and diverse diet (Source: SENS 2023). The reduction of General Food Assistance by WFP in 2023 ($12 to $8 per person) may have impacted the overall food security situation. Ten percent of Rohingya households are classified as having poor diets.(Source: REVA 7, 2023). UNICEF will continue supporting and scaling up on-going commitments and plans for the comprehensive package of nutrition services in the Rohingya camps to prevent and treat malnutrition. In 2025, the additional needs will include: (1) increased supplies for treatment of severe wasting; (2) additional operational cost for eight INFs that UNICEF took over from UNHCR in July 2024; (3) continued management of 20 INFs previously funded by the World Bank, which ended in June 2024; and (4) urgent reconstruction of weakened temporary bamboo structures INF to improve disaster resilience and alignment of service flow with sector recommendations. Host Community: Cox’s Bazar district and host community nutrition situation is also of concern where wasting prevalence is at 10.6 per cent and stunting is at 29.9 per cent. In both Teknaf and Ukhiya, wasting prevalence is over 10 per cent (Teknaf 12.8 and Ukhia 11.1). The quality of diets for children aged 6 to 23 months are also inadequate where 27.2 per cent of children consume at least 5 of the 8 recommended food groups. On the other hand, only 45 per cent of women of reproductive age in Cox’s Bazar district meet the minimum dietary diversity (5 groups and above) and 22.5 per cent of adolescent girls aged 10 to 19 years are severely malnourished (body mass index of less than 16). (Source: SMART survey 2023).

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

UNICEF will work with children, families, and communities to prevent and address a wide range of protection issues especially focusing on children through mainstreaming protection across all components. UNICEF will also integrate gender equity and GBV risk mitigation into the nutrition program to improve the well-being of women and girls. All staff will receive training on the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), gender-based violence (GBV), and child protection. UNICEF will work to strengthen protection systems to prevent and respond to abuse and exploitation in humanitarian situations, including by supporting families, equipping community-based groups to monitor child protection concerns, and strengthening the social service workforce and case management services. UNICEF will provide psychosocial services focusing on psychosocial assessment of mothers and caregivers of malnourished children and referral of any caregivers and mothers who need support to mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) service providers. Linkage and referral pathways between nutrition and protection in the camps will be established to ensure access to protection services.

2.3 - Age & Disability

UNICEF will ensure that sex, age, disability and other context-specific disaggregated data are collected and analyzed to monitor service accessibility and make changes to ensure everyone is included. UNICEF will also ensure humanitarian responses are based on a gender and diversity analysis and recognize and respond to the specific vulnerabilities due to diversity and gender norms and cultural practices. The beneficiaries will be screened to identify disabilities and provide care and referral if required to health and other relevant sectors. In addition, all nutrition facilities will be equipped with disability-friendly facilities. UNICEF and its partners will take measures to reduce barriers and stress on meaningful participation and empowerment towards decision-making processes and accessibility initiatives. UNICEF will ensure that Disability friendly Feedback Mechanism is established, and community engagement interventions are ongoing to ensure the participation of persons with disabilities in feedback provision, decision making and designing programmes.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

UNICEF will reinforce the complaints response mechanism at all INFs and communities in line with core humanitarian standards (CHS). The AAP sessions will be integrated in all the training curriculums, community sessions, and sensitization meetings to ensure knowledge of AAP principles. Each INF will maintain a designated helpdesk where beneficiaries can make a complaint, seek information, or share their views on topics of concern. Moreover, UNICEF will continue to train its implementing partners on Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) principles to ensure the availability of a functional, efficient and accessible complaints system. UNICEF will ensure that partners establish feedback and complaints mechanisms (Information and feedback centers, helpdesk, FGDs etc) in all the centers with a clear plan to address any issues that arise in an integrated manner. Additionally, female Rohingya volunteers will approach mothers/women for feedback, and a hotline number will be posted at INFs through which mother/women can provide feedback anonymously. Complaints and concerns related to service provision will be addressed promptly. Furthermore, UNICEF will ensure that its implementing partners demonstrate their capacity to analyze the feedback routinely and integrate AAP aspects into the design and implementation of their activities.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

UNICEF will ensure that the rights of all children are protected, nurtured and children are free from all forms of violence, abuse, neglect, maltreatment, and exploitation as set out in the Convention on the Rights of the Child. This will be ensured by enforcing Child Safeguarding /Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) measures which must be adhered to by all UNICEF and partner staff. There will be zero-tolerance for violation of this. UNICEF will ensure that all frontline workers will be trained on PSEA and child safe guarding. Any PSEA-related concerns found in the complaints boxes or brought to the help desks at the INFs, will immediately be referred to the PSEA network and/or protection sector for prompt action. To ensure that partners will follow their approved PSEA action plans, UNICEF will follow up the implementation of the action plan systematically and timely through regular field monitoring visits and required reporting. Influential community and religious leaders will receive gender and SEA sensitization messages related to health and nutrition through awareness sessions to challenge harmful sexual and gender norms that inhibit adolescent girls and women to access nutrition services and taking action.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes. Cox’s Bazar District is vulnerable to natural disasters, including cyclones, landslides and flooding during the monsoon season and fire during the dry season. To mitigate this, UNICEF and partners will employ disaster risk reduction measures as guided by a Multi-hazard Preparedness and Response Plan. This includes the pre-positioning of a buffer stock of supplies and medicines in the nutrition facilities for rapid deployment in an event of an emergency due to floods and cyclones. UNICEF will also be conducting assessments of nutrition facilities affected by climatic conditions and periodically rehabilitate them to mitigate the effects of climate change. Furthermore, UNICEF is also dedicated to mitigating the adverse environmental effects of certain aspects of its nutrition programs by raising awareness and providing training to nutrition staff and volunteers on climate change principles. Awareness creation on climate change and mitigation measures will be integrated into the nutrition education, community engagement, and cooking demonstration package. Emphasis will be placed on the importance of proper disposal of nutrition packaging (i.e RUTP empty sachets) to minimize pollution and prevent improper disposal within the camps. The nutrition project will work with other sectors such as WASH to support the use of compost for homestead nutrition gardens to reduce the use of chemical fertilizers. The project will support the climate-proofing of INFs.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes. The existing Emergency Preparedness Response Plan (EPRP) will be revised to include new arrivals and needs of people affected by floods, landslides, cyclones, diseases outbreaks, fire outbreaks and conflicts. These EPR measures include a Business Continuity Plan to sustain life-saving critical nutrition services, Standard Operating Procedures to provide clear guidance for immediate response, and contingency funds for rapid mobilization of resources, supplies, and staffing. UNICEF will assess all 34 INFs across the 25 camps and renovations and reconstructions will be carried it out based on need. UNICEF will ensure that the EPR measures are fully aligned with sector-specific guidelines as well as the policies and procedures set by the ISCG. These measures will comply with sector standards for risk assessment, contingency planning, and response coordination. This alignment will foster a unified, efficient, and effective humanitarian response, minimizing duplication and optimizing overall impact. UNICEF and partners will employ disaster risk reduction measures as guided by a Multi-hazard Preparedness and Response Plan. This includes the pre-positioning of a buffer stock of supplies and medicines in the nutrition facilities for rapid deployment in an event of an emergency due to floods and cyclones. UNICEF will also be conducting assessments of nutrition facilities affected by climatic conditions and periodically rehabilitate them to mitigate the effects of climate change. UNICEF will also work closely with the Rapid Nutrition Team to disseminate early warning messages to families with children under five, and pregnant and lactating women, particularly those enrolled in SAM/MAM programs.
