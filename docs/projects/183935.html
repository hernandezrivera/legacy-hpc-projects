Project Summary

The project will provide emergency agriculture and livestock-based livelihoods interventions and resilience building support for vulnerable farmers, agro- pastoral and pastoral IDPs, returnees, hosting and resident communities in Sudan.
The vulnerability the target populations stems from the protracted crisis, the ongoing economic hardships at the macro and micro-levels, long dry spells and foods. The situation was aggravated with the impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic; the lockdown measures to prevent the spread of COVID-19 significantly decreased commodity movement, market function and cross-border trade, and compromised livelihoods and daily labor opportunities. This situation has led to high rates of acute and chronic food insecurity and malnutrition. FAO prioritized in a participatory manner 81 localities in the 14 states based on the high degree of vulnerability to food insecurity and malnutrition (Phase 3 and above).
FAO project targets 2,000,000 people among the most vulnerable communities including 800,000 IDPs, 600,000 returnees and hosting communities and 600,000 vulnerable residents. The interventions benefit 620,000 women, 400,000 girls (less than 18 year of age), 580,000 men and 400,000 boys (less than 18 year of age). The selection of the proposed interventions based on the basic and specific needs of the beneficiaries taking into account the factors of age and gender. The project will target about 180,000 elderly people among the adult population including persons with disabilities.
The project interventions include as a top priority, Supporting food insecure people through life-saving and life sustaining food assistance through agricultural assistance including crop, livestock and veterinary inputs and extension services targeting 2,000,000 people. This will include as well, building the capacity of local communities, governmental institutions and sector partners to improve preparedness and response for new emergencies. The second priority is provide assistance for building self-reliance of affected households by protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income generating to cope with current and future shocks and stresses. Agricultural production will be improved through provision of climate-smart crop production, livestock and veterinary inputs and support to services. Similarly, small-scale farmers will be targeted for reducing food losses and strengthened post-harvest techniques through improved food and crop storage and processing facilities. On and off farm livelihood assistance will be diversified, managed, and coordinated to respond to food chain threats including animal and plant pests specially locust; In addition, communities will be involved in building, restoring and maintaining productive assets through multipurpose cash support. About 300,000 people will be targeted for multipurpose cash transfer support in collaboration with Cash Working Group (CWG). The response will consider prevailing market conditions, availability of financial services and agricultural and livestock inputs prices in the markets; the preferences of the affected population; cultural and gender dynamics; accessibility, safety and security of beneficiaries. Accountability to affected populations and environmental concerns will be mainstreamed into the proposed activities, also risk to GBV will be taken into consideration and application of the PSEA measures in will be applied in collaboration with sister UN agencies.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

This project has a medium impact on the environment since it focuses on agriculture,Livestock activities in 14 states out of the 18 states in Sudan. The project by its nature has some negative implications on the natural resources particularly soil due to agricultural and livestock activities. Soil erosion related to deforestation andinappropriate agricultural practices is a key challenge to sustainable natural resources use in the targeted areas. The project will promote practices that favour soil protection and regeneration and don’t harm the local ecosystems to favour their progressive recovery. It will promote the cultivation of multipurpose legume crops that enrich and protect the soils, and strongly contribute to improving the nutrition of humans and their livestock. It will ensure that wastes from livestock vaccination and treatment are disposed in a proper way. Awareness rising in better natural resource management will be part of training. The project will engage in a number of mitigation activities that include tree plantation, training of households in effective and durable caretaking of the planted seedlings, and on agro‐forestry practices and use of LPG and fuel‐efficient stoves for domestic uses. The project has an environmental impact in general and in Darfur, North Kordofan and Eastern Sudan in particular, are experiencing more droughts that are frequent, floods and storms causing damage to the environment and people's livelihoods.Poorly managed livestock has much more implications in GHG emissions and its intensity varies between regions causing climate change phenomena. The project will contribute effectively to the efforts that can build resilience, prepare for, and adapt to climate change through some mitigation measures that are explained below:# Increasing livestock productivity through supplementary feeding interventions will help in reducing the number of non-productive animals which bear full responsibility in increasing emissions;# Reducing the prevalence of diseases and parasites through the animal health component would eventually reduce emissions intensity as healthier animals are more productive and thus produce lower emissions per unit of output;# Adoption of climate smart agriculture techniques by targeted trained farmers will result in incorporation of manure into soils, which can reduce emissions while maintaining farm productivity;# Pasture rehabilitation and tree plantation activities will store more carbon into the soils; and,# Raising awareness of the target groups on rational utilization of the natural resources to ensure sustainable production.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The Organization’s overarching goal is to improve the food security and nutrition ofcommunities affected by conflicts, shocks and disasters, while enhancing families’ self-reliance and resilience to threats and crises in line with national priorities and FAO’s corporate Strategic objectives. The project will consider the protection of people depending on their needs and requirements, the project will provide suitable interventions that improve capacities of women, men, youth and people with disabilities to produce all or part of good quality food, to meet their own requirements and generate cash income for their families. The project will give equal opportunities to all categories of the population includingwomen, men, girls, boys, elders and people with disabilities to access the provided livelihood support, addressing the rights of all population categories to decent work to restore their food security and livelihood situation. The targeted communities have been involved in dialogue through meetings, interviews and group discussions throughout steps of this project pre-design and they will be directly involved during the implementation process including beneficiaries’ selection, registration and implementation of the activities, monitoring the implementation at the field and beneficiaries levels. As explained in the above paragraphs, FAO provides the necessary guidance for promoting the four pillars of decent work for the beneficiaries in the targeted rural areas. The project is considering protection of women and girls throughout the project cycle depending on the social environment in the mentioned areas. Sector Partners will provide the training and input distribution in suitable locations at their resident villages. Consult them and their leaders on the appropriate time for the training. Awareness on GBV and use of fuel-efficient stoves to avoid distant firewood collection in far unsafe places

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Accountability to affected population integrated into all aspects of the way that FAO works. Accountability incorporated into the definition of results in the Strategic Framework and Medium-term Plan, and how FAO plans, implements, monitors and reports on its programme of work. FAO accountability towards affected people starts from selection of types, varieties and quantities of livestock and agricultural inputs. The inputs selection defined in a participatory manner during the consultation phase. FAO encourages the local communities not only to participate in the implementation of the activities but also to ensure ownership of the project. As a result. FAO assists beneficiaries to form a local committee that represent all all people in the community, train them on group management, the roles and responsibilities of the local leaders and other related topics. This training facilitates the implementation of fair distribution of livelihoods support to its members. Either Beneficiaries” complaints about types, varieties and quantities of inputs or any other question received directly by FAO staff, or they can call the person in charge to respond or in case the reply is not convincing, he/she can raise the issue to the higher managerial level in FAO Sudan. Comments and recommendation usually collected during post distribution missions.FAO shows continues interest to hear the voices of the beneficiaries about its standard packages of inputs, which have been subject to change every year taking into account the views of the targeted people. FAO urged all its implementing partners to open hot lines with the beneficiaries to allow them to express their opinions about the intervention and give them the chance to bring out any grievances they may have about the interventions. The project will promote understanding between the different livelihood groups (pastoralists and farmers) through group meetings, discussions and other community platforms to reduce conflict and encourage mutual support. Special focus will be on enhancing the knowledge and skills of men, women, adolescent girls and other at risk-groups on natural resources management and leadership to ensure sustainable livelihood interventions; this will help in avoiding competition over natural resources. Some FAO staff are aware about the Secretary General’s recent Policy Brief on The Impact of COVID-19 on Women with concern that “as the COVID-19 pandemic deepens economic and social stress coupled with restricted movement and social isolation measures, gender-based violence increasing,” and economic impacts that felt most severely by women and girls. The economic devastation of the pandemic is leaving women and girls from affected communities, already in vulnerable situations, a greater risk of SEA. SEA complaint mechanisms have become even harder to access due to the rise in self-isolation measures, such as physical distancing policies, curfews and quarantines.
