Project Summary

Street Child will provide educational opportunity for 100,000 conflict affected children that are not able to access any form of learning during the school closure decided to contain the spread of COVID19. Based on specific government measures and location specific accessibility, Street Child will ensure continuation of learning of basic education and COVID19 preventive knowledge and behaviors through a set of different distance learning approaches that includes home paper based, use of radios, use of phones or through mobile teachers working in small groups. All education activities will include provision of psycho-social support for IDP, host community and returnee children in both host communities and camps.
Ongoing remote professional development will be provided to improve the quality of distance learning.
In addition, Street Child’s integrated approach to EiE and CP in all areas of operation will ensure that spread of COVID19 among vulnerable population is prevented through a continuous awareness campaign and that personnel involved with children are organised and trained to respond or prevent protection concerns with a system for safe reporting of incidents and are equipped with preventive and protective equipment.

1 - Needs

An estimated 1.8 million people are displaced in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe and around 40 percent (0.8 million) of that population is comprised of school aged children between three and seventeen years displaced across the three states. While the number of displaced persons and returnees increased compared to the previous year, the total number of children and education personnel in need decreased from 2.9 million for 2018 to 2.2 million for 2019 based on education sector estimates (EiEWG HNO, 2019). Recent data on the key education indices in the NE still indicate a dire situation in education with Borno recording the highest number of girls out of school in Nigeria at 71%. As of 25 April 2020, in Nigeria, confirmed cases have increased to 1,182 with 35 fatalities across the country. Cases have been registered in twenty-seven states and the Federal Capital Territory (FCT), with Lagos and the FCT reporting the highest number of cases. 30 positive cases with 3 deaths reported from Maiduguri while one positive case reported from Adamawa Satte while no case reported so far from Yobe state.The Global Humanitarian Response Plan for COVID-19 lists conflict-affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe as high risk, vulnerable populations, due to food insecurity and IDP presence. Currently the national school closures is affecting 46 million students of whom 4.2 million students in BAY States, the most vulnerable groups of children living under an existing protracted crisis. About 600,000 IDP children attending some form of learning in the camps and host communities will be affected by the stoppage of learning activitiesDepending on the duration of school’s closure, children may lose the learning skills, or the entire school year and find themselves into other tasks helping the family.School closure also represent a Child Protection risk factor considering also the Federal government has closed all non-essential services– including child services. It affects not only the gains made in education in the region over the past years but will negatively affect mental health. This will result in negative psychosocial and security ramifications on children, families and communities. The absence of physical attendance in schools’ leaves girls at a disproportionate level of risk of exploitation due to forced labor, sexual harassment and early childhood marriage.In addition, more often school is where children learn life-saving information as it should be for COVID19.This indicates a strong need for integrated education and protection serviced to support the most vulnerable and marginalized children.

2 - Activities

Prevention will be the focus during the whole response through targeted awareness raising so that children, teachers and the entire community should be informed on means of protecting themselves so as to reduce the spread of the virus. These messages will be provided in local languages (hausa and kanuri) and child friendly tailored. Several means of disseminations will be utilized according to their availability in each targeted location as SMS messages, radio programs and other audio and visual materials. The sensitization messages will not only focus on the spread of COVID-19 but also on the risks incurred by children in schools’ closure. MHPSS is an important element to address during this pandemic, as protection and MHPSS risks can increase when schools close. For this reason activities provided for distance learning will include SEL and PSS elements. A young girls-oriented response will be developed to ensure that tailored contents are available in the different approach; reading sessions in the family are profitable to both girls and boys and ill be provided with distribution of local language and age appropriate reading materials. Different materials shall be tailored capturing children with special needs.While waiting for the return to the normalcy, the response aims at preparing and organizing the secured, protective and inclusive return of children into the learning spaces. Depending on the duration of the school’s lockdown, we shall ensure that children resume schools on a strong go back to school campaign coupled with a family follow. SC will be responsible to disinfested/fumigated temporary learning spaces previously established with the provision of handwashing water point with soap. Street Child is committed to localisation and will work through local partners to implement project activities and provide tailored mentoring and capacity building to strengthen technical and organisational capacity. This will be intensified also to provide local organization a timely understanding and capacity around COVID19 prevention and response and learn more about different distance learning approaches (including how monitor progress and impact).

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

SC will utilise proven mechanisms for engaging stakeholders and beneficiaries throughout all stages of project planning, implementation and evaluation. A needs assessment will be conducted before implementation, and will include mainly household interviews respecting social distance or FGD if reduced number of people in grouping will be allowed. It will include different community groups - boys, girls, men and women.A dedicated complaints mechanism (CRM) officer will be identified for all targeted communities. This person will be responsible for collecting feedback and complaints, and liaising between community members, partners and Street Child to share this feedback.Street Child will provide a toll-free line which can be called at any time by community members to provide feedback or report urgent concerns (such as safeguarding concerns). All feedback received through these diverse mechanisms will be fed into review sessions and management decisions, allowing activities to be adapted to respond to feedback where appropriate in line with Street Child's commitment to accountability and evidence-based learning.During COVID19 Street Child will also introduce remote mechanisms to collect feedback from communities through continuous engagement through phone or door by door with appropriate Protective and Preventive Equipment of staff. Key will be the continuous assessment of effectiveness of COVID19 preventive messages, analyzing emerging needs and identify gaps to tailor and strengthen our COVID19 response
