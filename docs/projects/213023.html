Project Summary

In 2024, 16.7 million people (8.4 million female, 8.3 million male) are assessed to need humanitarian assistance across Syria, up from 15.3 million in 2023. Of the 16.7 million people in need, 5.5 million are displaced, including over 2 million who live in last resort sites. Protection sector is the sector with the most number of people in need with more than 15.3 million, out of those about 6 million people live in the two governorates where Amal GBV program for 2024 is implemented. For GBV a total of 131 sub district are 4-5 severity based on 2024 Syria HNO. Amal is planning to continue the provision of the GBV prevention, response and risk mitigation services in the two women and girls safe spaces in Idleb and Harim cities and expand to Suran in Azaz district to provide GBV services as well. GBV services provided in the three locations will be complemented by CP and livelihood services.
GBV activities will include: GBV case management, individual and group PSS, vocational and recreational activities, awareness raising and information session as well as referrals. Outreach teams will also provide services in the camps.
PRA: 1. Resistance or backlash from sections of the community and community leaders on GBV messaging 2. Backlash from family or community on women participating in recreational, vocational or empowerment 3. Women and adolescent girls with child-bearing responsibilities might have difficulty accessing services in the WGSS. All these risks come with medium possibility but with high impact. These risks will be mitigated through 1. Community engagement from onset of project, from assessment phase and throughout all project cycle. It includes meetings with male and female community members and community leaders 2. Ensure the support of male family members by engaging them in awareness-raising activities 3. Adaptation of GBV messaging to ensure sensitivity to the context and culture 4. Equip all WGSS with child-care services 5. Provide transportation support for those in need specially older persons and people with disability.
PSEA: SEA remains a risk in all humanitarian interventions but it's in the minimal possibility in GBV programs as all the staff are specialized service providers trained on PSEA, all staff and contractors are trained and have signed the CoC. Beneficiaries have access to confidential and easy to access complaint channels like boxes, whatsapp and email. Two main risks that we are facing in the implementation of GBV interventions in NW Syria. First one is the regulations from the local authorities which limits sometime the ability to tackle some key GBV related topics like sexual violence and early marriage for example. We are working on minimizing this challenge through a continues engagement with local authorities and also with the GBV AoR to benefit from the experiences and best practices. The second risk is the closure of services through limitation of funds. This affects the trust building we create with the community and prevent big number of the affected population from accessing services. We are working to minimize the consequences of this risk by keeping an emergency plan ready in case of any planned or emergency closure of services and keep engaging and advocating with donors about the importance of GBV services specially in the context of NW Syria.
