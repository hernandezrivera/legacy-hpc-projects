Project Summary

This project aims to promote and improve psychosocial support & well-being for children affected by the conflict through appropriate education services. The component will integrate gender issues and sensitization to gender-based violence, through teacher training, promotion of equal and safe education for girls and boys from IDP and host communities. To achieve this, NRC will ensure close collaboration with the decentralized services of basic education in Cameroon, adopting a conflict-sensitive approach while ensuring respect for Do No Harm principles. The project results will be achieved through activity packages aimed at strengthening the capacity of education actors at the community level and ensuring the protection and development (cognitive, physical, emotional and psychological wellbeing) of children and adolescents, boys and girls.
The activity package includes learning and teaching kits distribution, teacher training on psycho-social support and NRC better learning program, revamping school governance structures, set of recreational , breathing, relaxing and calming exercice activities with children and care givers. Additionally, awareness and sensitization activities will be carry on within targeted communities for protection of education from attacks and safe school declaration per on the South West and North West security context.
However, At the beginning of the project a baseline will be set up to have measurable and qualitative data to be analyzed at the end of the action. This aim is to have a better understanding of the impact of the project within the targeted community. In addition, NRC M&E staff will carry out outcome and output monitoring field activities according to the project period. They will also set up feedback and complain mechanism in targeted communities to ensure our accountability to donors and beneficiaries as well and to ensure compliance of our action with NRC program and operations policies.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Access to education opportunities: The OCHA situation report revealed that about 90% children have been out of school for nearly three years with 80% of schools closed or burnt. Children forced to flee to bush areas have been left without access to any form of education. More than 4,482 schools (80%) in NW/SW Cameroon have been forced to close. More than 201,000 school aged children have been directly affected by the war and having no access to education. The barriers to access to education and reasons for increasing numbers of out of school children include (a) the presence of armed groups and military personnel with security threat, (b) closed off or non-functional schools and learning spaces, (c) lack of teachers due to insecurity, and (d) inability to pay school fees. Need of Psychological support for affected children: Children in the North-West and South-West regions of Cameroon have been exposed to traumatic incidents such as witnessing conflict and violence, destruction of homes and villages and mass displacement. More than 90% of children are now out of school and are therefore completely exposed to a variety of protection risks including sexual exploitation and abuse, gender-based violence, harassment and recruitment by armed actors, arbitrary arrest, early marriage, pregnancy and child labour. Children require urgent support to manage their emotions, understand the normal reactions they are having to an abnormal situation, and improve their psychosocial well-being through play-based learning and positive social interactions with peers and adult role models. A sense of normalcy and structure needs to be established before children will be emotionally and socially ready to re-start their learning. In addition, it’s also important to strengthen linkages with GBV actors by increasing teacher’s capacity on safe and ethical referral.
