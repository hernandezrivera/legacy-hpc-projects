Project Summary

This HRP project aims to reduce the risk of Waterborne diseases among populations in Cholera hot spots as well as those affected by flooding, conflict, and food insecurity in South Sudan. South Sudan suffers from poor WASH indicators due to low investments in WASH infrastructures that exacerbate distances covered by communities to safe water points, inadequate capacity in water quality monitoring and basic water treatment among communities, high rates of underground/boreholes water contamination, heavy reliance on the surface or unimproved water sources, and inadequate availability of safe water storage facilities. The most affected areas are the Nile Basin states of Jonglei, Upper Nile, Unity, Warrap and Lakes, Eastern Equatoria and Northern Bahr el Ghazal states. Consequently, the country is prone to water-borne diseases especially cholera due to the weak public health systems and a myriad of threats that come with incessant flooding, poor waste disposal(human excreta/open deification) and management systems, overcrowding in IDP camps, and the existence of a high number of people on the move.
To address the above challenges, WHO will employ 2 major approaches (1) Build capacity of WASH partners South Sudan red cross volunteers in water quality testing, monitoring, and surveillance to improve early detection of risk through tailor-made training on sanitary survey and point of use water treatment at household level and basic water quality testing, produce and provide household basic water treatment and water safe handling handbook for use by partners.
Procure and disseminate backfield water candle filter to 31 nutrition stabilization centers, provide user training, maintenance, and Standard Operating Procedures (SoPs) to enhance safely handling of drinking water in targeted stabilization centers. In addition, WHO will monitor and conduct support supervision to render the requisite capacities on issues related to delivery of safe water.
Secondly, WHO intends to expand water quality surveillance and sanitary survey in cholera hots pots and other flood affected high risk areas including nutrition sentinel sites to ensure availability of timely data on water sources which are contaminated of bacteriological and health risk priority chemical.
The project will support partners and communities to expand routine water quality surveillance including nutrition stabilization centers through investments in basic water quality testing kits, reagents for comprehensive water quality test parameters of bacteriological, sanitary survey and health risk priority chemical including monitoring of aluminum concentration at surface water treatment plants, emergency water treatment kits and supply of water candle filters for stabilization centers.
The above interventions will contribute to the WASH Cluster sectoral objective SO1. of reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality in counties through WASH integrated programming with nutrition.

1 - Needs

The ongoing complex humanitarian crisis in South Sudan is a result of prolonged political conflict that started in 2013, and the subsequent occurrence of sub-national violence, flooding, acute food insecurity, and their associated public health consequences whilst the country is currently experiencing grade three protracted crisis. South Sudan reported at least eight emergencies in 2021; the complex humanitarian crisis, flooding, food insecurity, Hepatitis E virus, CVDP2, Yellow Fever, and COVID-19 pandemic outbreaks. The cumulative effect of these emergencies has left an estimated 8.3 million people in need of urgent humanitarian assistance including 314,000 refugees and asylum seekers. An estimated 2 million South Sudanese are also internally displaced while 2.3 million are refugees in neighboring countries (UN-OCHA November 2021). The above shocks are in addition to the already very poor performance of the country's economy. South Sudan remains among the poorest countries in the world with 85% of its working population surviving on non-wage agriculture and livestock; four out of five people subsist below the poverty line ($1.90) a day. The government's ability to provide basic services is low and inconsistent across the country due to years of under-investment, conflict, and disasters resulting in high dependency on humanitarian aid. The weak capacity of government to provide for its population is reflected in the general deficiency in service level availability across most sectors with significantly poor performance on key development indicators, for instance, only 39 percent of the country’s population have enough water to meet their household needs, meanwhile, 49 percent of households rely on surface or unimproved water as their main source. Communities also lack water storage facilities as only 14 percent of households have at least one jerrican or bucket to safely store water (FSNMS, 2021). Latrine coverage is estimated to be 15% among households, moreover, 43 counties have 0-10% of the population using latrines (HNO, 2020), with an overall 80% of the population without access to the latrine (FSNMS, 2021), increasing the risk of water contamination.Inadequate coverage of WASH services compounded by widespread open defecation exacerbate public health risks, especially during floods when latrine pits seep into waterways. Poor access to WASH services and high levels of food insecurity is linked to alarming rates of malnutrition in most parts of South Sudan. In 2021, National GAM prevalence for children 0-59 months was 11.3% while Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) was at 1.7% (FSNMS, 2021). Water-borne diseases was also reported, with 16 percent of households reporting children under 5 had been affected by diarrhea in the two weeks leading up to data collection (FSNMS, 2021). Finally, insecurity, access to water, and risk of protection for the most vulnerable are linked. For instance, in 2021 18 percent of households, both rurally and in urban settings, reported they felt unsafe while collecting water in the two weeks prior to data collection (FSNMS, 2021). Of those, 12 percent of women and girls were reportedly avoiding water points due to GBV concerns (FSNMS, 2021). The issue was particularly acute in counties like Fangak, Luakpiny/Nasir, Akobo, Tambura, Mayom, Balliet, and Ibba, where more than 40 percent of households reported feeling unsafe while collecting water (FSNMS 2021). Consequently, a combination of the above WASH, health, nutrition, and protection challenges will leave an estimated 6,159,886 million South Sudanese people’s living standard and their well-being impacted in 2022 (WSC, 2021).To address the above challenges, World Health Organization in partnership with the WASH cluster and the line ministries in South Sudan will tackle the public health risk of this chronic poor WASH situation through investments in capacity for water quality surveillance, early detection, and response.

2 - Project activities

• Training of trainers (TOT) on point of use water treatment and safe handling.• Conduct water quality testing and monitoring in cholera hotpots and expand on water quality testing sentinel sites. • Procure, disseminate basic and water quality monitoring kit ( Pool tester, turbidity tube) to WASH cluster partners.• procure and disseminate backfield water candle filters for 31 stabilization centers,provide user training, maintenance, and Standard Operating Procedures (SoPs) to enhance safely handling of drinking water in targeted stabilization centers. • Develop, print and disseminate household basic water treatment handling handbook. • Develop, print and disseminate SOP for water sampling for laboratory test. • Procure reagents for conducting comprehensive water quality testing parameters including aluminum test and prioritize health concern chemicals analysis. • Request PUR sachets and Aqua tap from the WASH cluster core-pipeline for household emergency water treatment.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback on WHOs WASH water quality interventions is anticipated to come from established formal and informal complaints and feedback mechanisms e.g community WASH committees, water sanitation and hygiene line ministries, field assessment and monitoring exercises for water quality surveillance, age, and gender structured Focused Group Discussions (FGDs), meetings with community leaders, WASH cluster implementing partners TWG meetings, WASH cluster coordination platform and meeting. The feedback obtained during the implementation of the project on water quality surveillance will be used to inform the design of water safety plan to suit the unique context and needs of the population. Feedback from joint monitoring missions will be used to design capacity-building initiatives for WASH committees in the communities and WASH cluster implementing partners and ensure the project is achieving its intended objectives.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

WHO will utilize community networks and structures to ensure feedback is taken from vulnerable members of the community. In this case, disability networks/organizations, women, and youth groups will be used to collect feedback from respective segments of the vulnerable communities. WHO will also use community leaders/representatives and caretakers in the case of children and severely impaired who have the knowledge of the needs and challenges vulnerable people face to elicit feedback against which interventions will be designed. Feedback from the most vulnerable members of the community will be used to support mainstreaming of vulnerable and at-risk groups’ needs into regular WASH programming and advocate for water quality surveillance inclusive programming that takes care of the needs. It will also be used to increase representation and participation of at-risk and vulnerable groups through community meetings, the direct participation of at-risk groups in the day-to-day implementation, and monitoring of project activities Where protection risk has been identified in the feedback from affected people, WHO will use the information to engage the protection stakeholders to increase protection assistance and safeguards for beneficiaries of WASH services.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

WHO has a zero-tolerance policy on PSEA and has established structures and systems for disseminating and handling all PSEA issues. At the national level, WHO has mainstreamed PSEA into all media briefs by the senior leadership for partners and communities to appreciate WHO’s role in safeguarding, as such, radio talk shows, public briefs are used as a platform to sensitize communities about WHO’s PSEA policies. At the community level, the state’s offices have PSEA policies posted in offices, vehicles, and public boards with clear steps for reporting. Staff, contractors, and volunteers do mandatory PSEA courses and participate in regular townhalls on PSEA and they have also signed PSEA policies as part of their contractual agreements. Through these channels, WHO informs its staff on the immediate and confidential mechanisms to report abuses including for WHO staff members and WHO collaborators or any other person who may have been a victim of PSEA or may have witnessed or otherwise been informed of a case of PSEA involving WHO, as such, all staff members at all levels organization-wide, collaborators and beneficiaries who have reason to believe a SEA situation may have occurred, by a WHO staff or collaborator, or another UN international civil servant, must report either by contacting the PSEA focal points, the ethicsoffice@who.int. or through the Integrity Hotline, where they will receive confidential advice.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will adopt context specific approaches, WHO will utilize existing community networks, that include functional water committees, men and women social groups, youth groups and community leaders to ensure feedback is taken from the vulnerable members and people with disabilities. Feedback from the most vulnerable members of the community will be articulated in reports and share with the WASH cluster and donor for decision making, to support mainstreaming of vulnerable and at-risk groups’ needs into regular WASH programming and advocate for inclusive programming that takes care of the needs. It will also be used to increase representation and participation of at-risk and vulnerable groups through community focus meetings, the direct participation of at-risk groups in the day-to-day implementation, and monitoring of project activities Where protection risk has been identified in the feedback from affected people, WHO will use the information to engage the protection stakeholders to increase protection assistance and safeguards for beneficiaries of WASH services.
