Objectives

• Ensure effective, sufficient and continuous provision of life saving water and sanitation services for targeted men, women, boys and girls • Ensure that all WASH assistance promotes the protection, safety and dignity of targeted people, and is used equitably to men, women, boys and girls• Ensure sanitary conditions for refugees and host community via labor intensive SWM and FWM • Promoting gender sensitive approaches in WASH infrastructure • Promote positive hygiene and sanitation practices amongst refugee and host community in order to reduce vector presence and health risks

Needs

The extreme population density of the Rohingya refugee camps and makeshift settlements makes appropriate WASH facilities and services a critcal factor in ensuring the health and wellbeing of the population. Currently, Acute Watery Diarrhoea (AWD )and other WASH-related disease outbreaks represent a real danger, and the health sector has already reported incidences of Hepatitis A and E. Assessment results indicate that a high degree of undernutrition among refugees may have more to do with WASH problems than with food shortages (Rohingya Emergency Vulnerability Assessment, Nov./Dec. 2017). A combined 53% of households continue to have challenges to accessing WASH facilities including distance, overcrowding, and location and overflowing due to high water table and construction challenges. According to latest NPM 119,423 people lack access to sufficient safe drinking water, 92,204 people have limited or no access to latrines. Lack of access to Solid Waste Management and Fecal Sludge Management is widely documented, the same counts for decommissioning/rehabilitation of latrines. About one third of the population dispose of solid waste in an indiscriminate manner and waste often ends up in the open drains resulting in blockages/flooding. The Rohingya community displays poor hygiene practices and lack access to basic hygiene items. The capacities of the WASH sector with regard to hygiene promotion were generally quite poor; the focus remains on the distribution of didactic messages, and involvement of the target group is limited. Significant language and communication problems continue to challenge hygiene promotion teams. Insufficient access to WASH services undermines the sense of security of women and children refugees and exposes them to protection risks. For example, women often report feeling vulnerable to gender-based violence when using the public latrines at night because the settlements lack lighting. In NPM round 10, it was determined that 59% of the inspected locations, including bathing and washing facilities, represent safety risks for children; 67% of the locations represent safety risks for women. In addition, women reduce their water intake to avoid having to use public latrines. However, this behaviour has grave consequences for their health, leading to skin diseases and rashes. Other negative coping mechanisms adopted by women in this context includes makeshift bathing areas within the living shelter - a risk factor that increases the grey and sometimes black water released into the open drains.

Activities or outputs

Improved access to safely-operated and dignity-preserving water and sanitation facilitiesand introduction of safe hygiene practices through:• WASH infrastructure set-up and upgrading, using CfW modality as far as possible• Operation and maintenance of WASH infrastructure, using CfW modality as far as possible• Set up of SWM infrastructure• Labor intensive FSM and SWM operation and maintenance, including treatment of organic and non-organic waste• Hygiene promotion and vector control • Provision of gender adaptations for WASH facilities (gender segregation, outfitting, household level bathing space)
