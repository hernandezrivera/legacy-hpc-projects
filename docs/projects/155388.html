Needs

With 3.6 million people facing food insecurity, FAO is ramping up efforts to provide emergency agriculture assistance to the most vulnerable farmers and herders through immediate and early action interventions. FAO Zimbabwe is adapting its response based on lessons learned and good practices from the previous El Nino occurrence – which brought the worst drought the country has seen in over 25 years. This is being done in tandem with the relevant government ministry responsible for agriculture and other key partners. The provision of essential agricultural input assistance is required to enable vulnerable and food insecure small holder farmers to rebuild their productive capacity. Without such assistance, targeted farmers would face difficulties in obtaining needed inputs to for agricultural production and would subsequently slide deeper into food insecurity with devastating consequences. The bulk and quality of grazing is poor especially in the southern provinces of the country since it received little moisture during the growing season. Farmers require urgent agricultural assistance in the form of stock feed, drought tolerant seeds and enhanced access to water. According to the Department of Veterinary Services, an estimated two million cattle need to be vaccinated annually against FMD. The spread of FMD is exacerbated by drought due to the coping mechanism used by farmers to save their cattle from the scourge of drought. Due to a critical shortage of vaccines caused by the deteriorating economic situation in the country, only cattle in the strategic vaccination barrier zone will be prioritised for vaccination in order to prevent further spread. The total cattle population in the barrier zones is estimated at just over 900 000 and the vaccine requirement is 2 845 938 doses. The spread of Anthrax has increased because of cattle grazing too close to the ground due to lack of grazing. Apart from causing high mortality in grazing cattle and other animals Anthrax can also be transmitted to humans through contact with infected animals or their products, or consumption of contaminated products; causing serious and sometimes fatal disease in humans a total of 1 722 500 cattle need to be vaccinated. Fall army worm and tuta absoluta are crop pests that have spread to all provinces in the country posing a huge threat to agricultural production that can severely lower harvest prospects. Availability of water for livestock and agricultural production is a major critical factor in all the drought affected districts and this has been exacerbated by the high number of dysfunctional boreholes. There is urgent need to rehabilitate existing boreholes to ensure access to clean and safe water for both humans and livestock.
