Project Summary

This project will aim at improve access to food item, to conflict affected people including IDPs, Returnees and Host Communities in Littoral and South West regions by enabling them to fulfill their immediate food needs and enhance access to livelihoods opportunities to ensure an efficient resilience programming. The target will be men, women, boys and girls.
From NRC gender analysis in NOSO in November 2019, it emerged that food security challenges are the major concern for the displaced households as they expressed that they did not have enough land to farm. They also expressed having the feeling of being a burden on some of their host families due to their inability to contribute to the household income. As a result, women (IDPs) indicated to have adopted coping strategies which were either to reduce food consumption frequency and to decrease the amount of the food consumed. IDP households indicated also that displacement meant a loss of resources for food and self-reliance. It primarily affects the access to a land to cultivate, the loss of livestock and of the family’s productive assets.
NRC will support them through CVA, distribution of food production inputs and awareness campaigns held on food hygiene and dietary diversity.
CVA will aim at support household to improve households ability to meet their needs, mainly food. Local markets are functional and accessible by both women and men, although those markets are not yet operating at the same level as before the crises. Cash approach will help ensure that households have unrestricted access to nutritious foods and additional income that would protect their remaining livelihoods and assets. Provision of Seeds and livestock will aim at support reestablishment of agricultural production.
NRC will target 5000 beneficiaries in South West Region and 4000 beneficiaries in Littoral Region.

Besoins / Needs

An estimated 462,000 people in the North West and South West regions fall within the Crisis and Emergency phases of food insecurity (Ministry of Agriculture, March 2019). This situation revealed a need for action to protect and revive livelihoods, reduce food consumption gaps, and reduce acute malnutrition. The Emergency food security assessment (EFSA) conducted by WFP in January 2019 revealed that proxy Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates for those 02 regions are estimated respectively 4.4% and 5.6%.There is not enough sign of deescalation of crisis. Displacement continues due to the ongoing conflict. People by displacement are more vulnerable because of services deterioration and resilience is eroded (OCHA, July 2019). IDPs have lost all theirs belongings and need emergency shelter, NFIs and food items. Preliminary findings of a food security needs’ assessment by NRC in May-August 2019 showed the same trend than previous studies. It emerged that displaced people in the two regions continue to suffer from the loss of productive assets and high prices of agricultural outputs, this in turn leads to low food production. Their food needs remain unmet.
