Project Summary

Action Against Hunger (ACF) Mental Health, Care Practices, Gender and Protection (MHCPGP) program implements an array of comprehensive, community-based mental health and psychosocial support services for the crisis affected Forcibly Displaced Myanmar Nationals (FDMNs - Rohingya refugees) and vulnerable host populations in order to build long-term resilience and holistic well-being with special attention to intersectionality of gender and ensuing protection needs of the population. Project activities are channelled through established in-patient and out-patient treatment facilities (e.g. 3 PHCs, 5 Upzila Health Complex, 12 OTP, 4 SC, 10 BFS and 2 SSWGs) in 20 camps and 5 Unions, as well through referral and follow-up coordination with partner agencies.
ACF engages into health system strengthening by building capacities of health care staff, ensuring adequate monitoring and supportive supervision by mhGAP trained Clinical Psychologists and provision of mental healthcare infrastructure (e.g. mental health corner in PHCs and Upzila health complexes). We strongly adhere to WHO proposed clinical interventions and IASC guidelines to provide specialized MHPSS supports (e.g. Thinking Healthy, Problem Management Plus, EMDR, Truma-focused CBT for Adolescents and Adults, etc.). Adolescents and women in reproductive age are provided safe and easy access to peer support groups, combined life-skills and sexual and reproductive health education, as well as trauma-informed counselling and psychosocial supports through clinical case management, appropriate referral and follow-ups. Extended family development approach includes parenting and caregiver health education, such that family and community-level psychosocial supports and networks are strengthened for the affected individuals.

Needs

Recent National Mental Health Survey (NIMH, Oct 2019) suggests prevalence of 16.8% mental disorders in adult population (18-99 years) and 13.6% in children/young adolescents (7-17 years) with average treatment gap for all diagnosed mental disorders as high as 93.4% - staggeringly high by all means. It indeed has implications for any health programme, especially non-communicable diseases prevention and treatment, as well as WHO’s push for Universal Health Coverage. Whereas from the 2017 refugee crisis, boys and girls represent 55% of all refugees; the majority of whom need MHPSS services, especially due to the emergence of non-communicable diseases/NCD risks from displacement, over-crowded settlements in camps, and growing caregiver stress. While significant progress has been made in delivering psychosocial support to large numbers of affected population, the need to scale up specialized mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services that addresses chronic trauma symptoms and viloence experienced by the majority is essentially required to improve resilience and well-being of affected populations. Community-based care arrangements through primary health centers need to be prioritized for the larger communities (including refugees and host) to seek timely, easily accessible, and quality mental health supports. Also host communities living in Cox’s Bazar District, especially those residing in close proximity to refugee camps; in particular the rural less-developed Unions of Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazillas have unaddressed basic needs and are also exposed to serious mental health and protection risks due to a combination of factors, including poverty, limited to no access to spcialized mental health services. The Rohingya influx has inevitably had a significant impact on competition for limited resources, access to key health services, and the environment in host communities as well as inter-community relations. Enhancing communication and community-based mental health initiatives for particularly vulnerable groups, as well as implementing community-owned need-based interventions are required to mitigate health burden with reduction in common mental illnesses, morbidities, and injuries. Supporting sustainable community-based initiatives will be required to mitigate exposure to further protection risks and enhance social cohesion.

with Health Sector and Camp-in-Charge (CIC)• Appointment of qualified, skilled, mhGAP trained Psychologists in the 2 Upzila Health Complexes and Cox's Bazar Sadar Hospital for the provision of MHPSS services• Capacity-building, continuous quality monitoring, and supportive supervision of healthcare staff (including General Practitioners, Nurses, Paramedics) through mhGAP trained qualified Psychologists• Awareness-raising on psychological distress, indigenous self-care strategies promoting resilience, bonding, and healthy living • Screening, identification, and referral for mhGAP prioritized common mental illnesses by community volunteers and lay counsellors • Parenting skills sessions for parents and caregivers promoting early childhood development, psychosocial care of children and adolescents, and remediation of developmental delays • Peer-to-peer support groups of adolescent girls, women in reproductive age on MHPSS-focused essential life-skills and sexual and reproductive health education • Individual psychosocial and psychological consultations for vulnerable population with mild to moderate psychological distress keeping up with evidence-based trauma-informed specialized psychological interventions (e.g. Thinking Healthy, Problem Management Plus, and EMDR) • Develop trauma-informed Integrated Group Therapy Protocol ToT for MHPSS WG partners, organize capacity-development trainings and establishing supervisory support mechanism• Establishing and regularizing MHPSS-centered women & child committees, self-help groups, men forums for improving care-seeking, referral, and local grievance redressal regarding access to key MHPSS and health services
