Project Summary

Save A Life International (SLI) will provide integrated life-saving Food Security and Livelihood assistance to 7,000 households among the most vulnerable internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees and vulnerable host communities in Nasir County, Upper Nile State. For returnees and internally displaced persons who have no access to land, assistance will be provided in form of fast growing vegetable kits to enable them establish kitchen and engage in fishing. For vulnerable host communities, returnees and IDPs with access to land and fishing waters, they will be provided with crop, vegetable and fishing kits.
By providing crop, vegetable and fishing kits and training on improved farming and sustainable fishing methods, beneficiaries will be expected to diversify their diet, increase their food production for home consumption and for sale. FSL project staffs will support and participate in FSL assessments initiated by the FSL cluster and Ministry of Agriculture and Fisheries. Also, the staffs will monitor crop disease and pest outbreaks and make referrals to FSL cluster and MAF in target locations. The project will mainstream protection in order to reduce risks and exposure to Gender Based Violence. Most importantly, the project will support the existing coping mechanisms of the local community and will build resilience towards other shocks that have direct effect (such as the inflation and flooding) through integrating the FSL response to NFIs, Wash, Nutrition,Education, Health responses in target locations.
SLI will ensure activities are tailored to the specific gender and age of target population as detailed in the needs analysis section. SLI will also strengthen the capacity of farmers and fisher forks through thematic workshops and training as per the needs identified in each micro-community. Farmers groups (Trainer of Trainers) will be trained on integrated farming, disease and pest control methods in their demonstration gardens and community outreach. Topics on nutrition, food wastage, harvest management and storage will also feature prominently during the training. Fisher Fork Groups will be trained on sustainable fishing methods, preservation techniques, and hygienic ways to handle and package fishing, value addition and fish marketing.

1. Needs

In the month of August, 2024, Nasir County experienced conflict between South Sudan Defence Forces and local youth leading to destruction of properties and displacement of civilians. According to recent Inter Agency Rapid Assessment findings, 8,700 persons were displaced in Koat, 6,500 in Kuetrengke and 6,500 in Dinkir Payams (IRNA, August 2024). In the second week of October, fighting escalated leading to death of 17 people, destruction of properties, injuries and more displacements. The conflict in Nasir is happening at a time floods have wrecked havoc on families, farms and livestock. As of 10th October 2024, the county has registered 14,000 people who are affected by floods. The conflicts and the floods are happening when the Nasir has been experiencing acute food insecurity. According to Integrated Phase Classification (IPC) report covering April and July 2024, the county experienced emergency food insecurity (IPC4) or worse. In the same report, the county has experienced critical acute malnutrition from January to July 2024. Drivers of food insecurity include disruption of farming and fishing activities which has led to reduced availability of food and fish, reduced ability to buy foods due to increased prices. In areas enjoying relative calm, there is reduced availability of agricultural seeds and tool due to disruption of markets and reduced funding gaps. Causes of malnutrition include household food shortages that is typical during lean season, time poverty, lack of access to productive assets, limited livestock ownership and poor diets. Over and above, the rainy season has resulted in increased cases of Malaria and acute diarrhea which often led to reduced productivity and depletion of savings and family assets for family members seeking treatment. In light of acute food needs and critical malnutrition situation in Nasir County, Save A Life International will carry out an integrated food security and livelihood intervention with the aim of boosting food production, increasing food availability to household and diversifying food diet. The project will target an estimated 7,000 households among the returnees, vulnerable host community and returnees. All the proposed activities will be coordinated with the FSL cluster, Nutrition and Health partners as well as local authorities to reduce overlap and duplication of activities.

2. Project activities

OUTPUT 1.1: 7,000 households comprising of 5000 Host Community,1000 Internally Displaced and 1000 Returnees Households provided with emergency FSL kits. ACTIVITY 1.1.1: Conduct community communication, consultation and sensitization meetings on the FSL project with local authorities, community leaders and members to provide project information and the role of the community in the implementation process. Advocate for increased and meaningful participation of women, persons with disability and marginalized communities in the project planning and implementation. ACTIVITY 1.1.2: Community Mobilization, Registration, verification and selection of 7,000 households’ beneficiaries with the participation of community members, leaders, and local authorities, and beneficiary’s representatives. SLI’s project staff working closely with project implementation committee, chiefs, local authorities, site managers, locally hired enumerators and existing partners to identify, register, verify, and select 7,000 most vulnerable households among internally displaced persons, returnees and host community who will benefit through distribution of emergency FSL kits. ACTIVITY 1.1.3: Prepositioning of vegetable, crop and fishing kits items from UNFAO core pipeline. SLI will collect FSL kits from the core-pipeline in Malakal, thereafter transport the items by water to Nasir for prepositioning. ACTIVITY 1.1.4: Conduct distribution of vegetable, crop and fishing kits to 7,000 Households. For the dry season, the livelihood support package is composed of at least five types of vegetable seed per household.ACTIVITY 1.1.5: Formation and training of 55 vegetable and crop groups (Trainer of Trainers), each composed of 25 individuals. Beneficiaries of vegetable and crop kits will receive training on how to establish gardens and raised beds to raise seedlings (due to rainy season), land and nursery beds management, planting methods (row planting and number of seedlings per row), seedling care management, thin/transplanting, mulching, soil and water conservation, pest and disease control (integrated pest management), harvesting and harvest preservation. ACTIVITY 1.1.6: Conduct joint field monitoring visits to farmers and farmers groups with project staffs, community members, local authorities and officials from MAF. Joint monitoring meant to obtain information on project progress, challenges faced and to seek continued participation and support from target communities. ACTIVITY 1.1.7: Conduct farming stakeholder’s forum to obtain information on challenges faced by farmers. ACTIVITY 1.1.8: Conduct PDMs on crop and vegetable kits distribution. A post distribution monitoring exercise will be conducted targeting 15% of all beneficiaries who have directly befitted through the distribution of vegetable, crop and fishing kits. Data collection methods employed in the PDM will include key informant interviews with local authorities, community leaders, chiefs and RRC officials to gauge their perception of the intervention and seek their views and recommendation. ACTIVITY 1.1.9: Conduct a joint Post Harvest Survey with farmers, Officials from MAF and local authorities to obtain data on post-harvest food harvest, losses and waste. Also, the survey will aim at documenting some of the success stories as a result of the project. ACTIVITY 1.1.10: Conduct environment education and awareness targeting farmers and fisher fork groups to mitigate effects of Global Warming. ACTIVITY 1.1.11: Formation and training of 33 Fisher Folks Groups each composed of 15 members. Training topics to include benefits of fish diet, fish preservation, sustainable fishing, net making, fish packaging, fish handling and marketing.
