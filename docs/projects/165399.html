Project Summary

HIHFAD seeks to continue integrating nutrition-related activities into the healthcare facilities it is currently operating through mainly continue the screening activities of Malnutrition besides the referral capacity to HIHFAD Children hospital for treatment, HIHFAD aim to do the screening for almost 112,416 (78624 children and 33792 PWL)
in more details :
• Facility-based and outreach messaging about infant and young child feeding IYCF practices.
• Nutritional assessment for pregnant and lactating women and children under 5 years of age (Application of mid-upper arm circumference MUAC and Z-scores among other assessment tools) and referral of undernourished cases to the appropriate facility as needed.
• one to one and one to Group nutrition counseling for pregnant and lactating women.
• IYCF counseling by trained staff
• Outpatient and inpatient management of moderate and severe acute malnutrition cases of children under 5 years of age.

3- Patient Exit Interviews: To verify the quality of service in the PHC, via patients, parents and companion feedbacks. (Monthly)

4 - Needs

Chronic malnutrition is evident in most sub-districts throughout Syria – notably in the Northwest of Syria (Aleppo, Idlib) and North East Syria (Deir Ez Zor, Al-Raqqa and Hasakeh). Chronic malnutrition is also on the rise with almost half a million children stunted (low height for age) in 2020 and face losing their future due to hindered learning and physical developments, as well as early death.Acute malnutrition remains relatively high among internally displaced children and mothers as well as population affected by conflict. Throughout Syria, about 240,000 children under the age of five years and over 50,000 pregnant and lactating mothers are in need for urgent lifesaving curative nutrition services4 . At least 36,000 boys and 35,000 girls are acutely malnourished and are at increased risk of health complications, including death.5 Furthermore, it is the youngest children that are the most vulnerable and affected, with higher numbers of acute malnutrition amongst infants under 6 months of age.Anemia remains a challenge in Syria where at least one out of every three mothers and children are anemic. Anemia among mothers and children is a multifaceted problem caused by limited access to health care, reduced quality meals (diverse diet) as well as negative coping mechanisms such as child marriage, which could lead to negative outcomes such as increased maternal mortality and morbidity, partiularly in conflict-affected, overburdened, and underserved areas. Currently over one million children and over three million mothers in Syria are in a need of multi-sectoral support to treat and prevent anemia.8 Without this support the cycle of chronic malnutrition in Syria will continue, over 90% of mothers will develop complications, and 5% of them could possibly die(HRP2020 )

5 - Implementation of activities

• Support facility and community based 1-1 counselling on WHO recommended Infant and Young Child Feeding practices: As per the guidelines lately communicated by the nutrition cluster, HIHFAD will ensure investing more effort in terms of delivering peer to peer IYCF counselling rather than the group approach. To promote the optimal preventive practice, the implementation of this activity will take place at both facility and community levels. It’s imperative to mention that HHFAD will stick to the guidelines communicated by the health cluster in terms of SOPs and educational materials that will be discussed with the beneficiaries. In the same context, HIHFAD will ensure that the staff that are planned to conduct the counselling sessions possess the capacities required prior to conducting such sessions. This activity will be implemented at all of the proposed facilities.• PLW being reached with IYCF education in MBAs, health facilities, child protection sessions like mother-toddler and positive parenting, as well as community outreach: In combination with the providing the caregivers with the counselling about IYCF, HIHFAD will ensure that the caregivers are also provided with standardized messaging about the best IYCF practices. To ensure coordinated approach, all of the distributed educational materials will always be those adopted by the nutrition cluster. This activity will be implemented at all of the proposed facilities.• Provide Micronutrient supplementation to women and children for prevention of micronutrient deficiencies: As a preventive way of approach, HIHFAD will provide eligible women with Iron and folate supplements, and children under 5 with multivitamin supplements. This activity will be implemented at all of the proposed facilities.• Support facility- and community-based screening for acute malnutrition for children and PLWs • Support treatment of acutely malnourished children and PLW

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Nutrition is a human right and underpins success in education, poverty alleviation, and the empowerment of women and girls. It is critical that nutrition programs in Syria assess, plan, and implement nutrition interventions with a protection lens and take a Do No Harm approach to ensure affected populations are protected from risks associated with the conflict in Syria as well as avoid unintended adverse effects of nutrition assistance. Therefore, the nutrition sector will carry out both sectoral and project level protection risk analyses to demonstrate thoughtful and safe mitigation measures in all planned nutrition interventions. These protection risks include:• Violence against health and nutrition facilities and health and nutrition providers• Gender Based Violence• Child Protection• Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)• Provision of and equitable access to nutrition services for vulnerable groups• Conflict Sensitivity• Protection of Humanitarian staff, volunteers and associated personnel• Nutrition Data protection• Exposive Hazard Considerations
