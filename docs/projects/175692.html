Project Summary

This project is a six month emergency main season food security and livelihood support intervention with promotion of appropriate small scale resilience activities amongst 9,415 high food insecure household in Gulumbi, Kimba ,Lujulo , Panyume and Wudabi Payams of Morobo County of Central Equatoria State ,the immediate objectives is to support 9,415 vulnerable households . The objectives are in line with HRP strategic objective 2(SO2) and in support of FSL cluster objective 2. To deliver the required results the key program strategy through distribution of vegetable kits, fishing kits and farming tools to boost household food production and by extension of food security, the distribution of FSL emergency kits will be complemented by training of farmers and fishermen groups, Farmers will be train on improved agronomics while fishermen receive training on sustainable fishing, hygienic handling and preservation of fish in order to reduce post-harvest loses. Post distribution monitoring, post fish harvest and post planting will be conducted
The project will be implemented during the lean season and benefit food insecure IDPs, Returnees and Vulnerable host communities classified Under IPC3 and IPC4 or worse food insecure.
Programmatically, to ensure Accountability to affected population, a committee would be formed to address issues that will address that will not affect the beneficiaries as a result of implementation of the project. The project will target mainly vulnerable returnees, IDP's and host population that missed the main cropping season. A total of 9415 Households will be supported from Morobo County.
The main driver to the disaster is high insecurity, Economic breakdown and fighting with other non-signatories to the RTGoNU) couple with COVID-19 that have resulted into issues such as rape, killing and obstruction of the livelihood activities. On the issues of rape, women and girls are the most affected the intervention will therefore to a larger extent address the needs of Women, girls as they are the main victims of the disaster. This category of people are at high protection risk. The intervention will therefore support these people.
The project will promote inter-cluster strategy with WFP Food for asset beneficiates and farmers affected by Fall Army worm.
RWDSS is a very strong and active partner of the Food security and livelihood cluster at National level and have participated in several assessment missions in Morobo County. But have implemented Food security and livelihood project in Yei County which a neighbouring county to Morobo .RWDSS has historical experience working with FAO ELRP activities and WFP in Greater Equatoria
They have been FAO/WFP partner since 2016 currently, they are FAO implementing partner in Yei River County, Lainya Mundri West, Mundri East, and Mvolo for El-RP main season support. RWDSS is also implementing Emergency food security project funded by FAO in Yei River County. This solid experience of RWDSS both in terms of the context and the active presence in greater Yei gives RWDSS the relevant opportunity to implement similar project
The grant obtained will be utilize to distribute fishing kits to 9415 HHs. Each of the 9415 HHs received twines, fishing hooks and monofilament. This would mean that a total of 9,415 twines, 9,415 fishing hooks and 9,415 monofilaments will be distributed to targeted households. Fishing kits will be distributed to households with access to fishing water and fishing knowledge. Also, each of the 9,415 targeted households will receive vegetable kits and digging tools. In total, 9,415 emergency vegetable kits comprising of okra, onion, tomato maker, and pumpkins will be distributed to households with each household also receiving one piece of malouda. In total, 9,415 vegetable kits and 9,415 pieces of Malouda will be distributed to targeted HHs.

1 - Needs

In Central Equatoria State, an estimated 686,000 people (45.5% of the State population) faced Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity during the October to November 2020 post-harvest period, of which 480,000 people were in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, and 206,000 people were in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. During this period, Morobo, Kajo Keji and Terekeka were classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, whereas Juba, Lainya and Yei counties were classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. Food insecurity was driven by insecurity mainly in Kajo Keji, Morobo, Yei and Lainya counties, movement restrictions related to the COVID-19 with resultant negative impacts on trade and markets, devaluation of the local currency resulting in high prices of food, and excessive rainfall that resulted in atypical flooding which reduced crop yields and displaced some populations. The impact of COVID-19 on the volume of traded commodities in the main market in Juba County was apparent and resulted in high prices. The COVID-19 pandemic also pushed large number of people out of jobs and many businesses were closed leaving a substantial proportion of the population with little or no access to food. Between December 2020 and March 2021, an estimated 421,000 people (27.9% of the population) are likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 355,000 people are likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, and 66,000 people are in likely to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. All the six counties in the State are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. During this period, even as majority of households are enjoying access to harvests, insecurity is likely to continue in Kajo Keji, Morobo, Yei and Lainya counties, thus affecting food security in these counties. COVID-19 control measures are also likely to continue affecting livelihoods, markets, and trade. This will affect household incomes, reduce their purchasing power, and limit their access to food. However, the proposed project intervention is the provision of vegetable kit and fishing kits in targeted areas identified by the current call for proposal as the prioritized Counties owing to the fact of the food deficit in these areas as indicated in the IPC report. The type of activity is a quick impact intervention that will have immediate effect in addressing the food needs of the community through their own effort. The fishing intervention proposed for instance will be done in areas where people depends on fishing as main source of survival. Vegetable support are quick and short maturing that can provide food in less than 6 weeks. Besides been quick maturing, it terms of seasonality these are the activities that are only appropriate at this time that could sustain ably address the food need. This therefore indicates that the support is in line with the Food security cluster priority needs similarly, the type of intervention falls within the Food security cluster objective two (2) which include provision of life saving livelihood support.

2 - Project activities

• Mobilization and sensitization of communities RWDSS will mobilize and sensitize, in close collaboration with County Agriculture Department (CAD) including local authorities-Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC) and community leaders, the community on the seed fair and direct distribution interventions, as well as on the criteria for identification and selection of beneficiaries at payam level . • Formation and Training of Four Accountability to affected Population (AAP) Committee. Previous Committees and similar FSL projects have taught us that ensuring communities and people affected by the distaste actively participate in responses is key to success and sustainability of the projects. Also, we believe that affected communities have the capacity to help themselves, plan their own action, manage and respond to needs or shocks facing them if right kind of support is provided to them. We therefore sought to empower affected communities during the intervention to build local capacity, accountability and ownership. • Beneficiary’s identification and registration: RWDSS will target households, for the direct distribution of kits, as defined by the Integrated Phase Classification (IPC phases 3, 4 and 5), The RWDSS will target (in the areas listed in the Distribution Plan the most vulnerable communities including the displaced and severely food insecure. • Collection of Kits from the nearest FAO storage hub and transport them to the distribution areas RWDSS will request inputs from FAO hub and transport them to the distribution area• Direct Distribution of Emergency Kits Distribution of Emergency Kits RWDSS will closely guidelines on Accountability to Affected Populations during the distribution; RWDSS will establish distribution points that are safe and most convenient for the recipients. And will inform the affected person about the language and manner so that they can understand about the quantity and type of inputs to be distributed. • Rapid Post-distribution Monitoring RWDSS will conduct rapid Post-distribution Monitoring (PDM) during the direct distribution. • Post-planting and Post-harvesting assessments, RWDSS will conduct crop and vegetable post planting and fish harvest assessments one month following seed/kit distributions. The sample size for crop and vegetable post planting and fish harvest assessments should be between 10-15% of targeted households. • Establishment of Farmers Field School RWDSS will establish new 10 FFS in the proposed area. The FFS will serve as the basis for the long-term sustainability of the food security and livelihoods intervention. Each FFS will include 30 members on average, selected by RWDSS in consultation with County Agriculture Department and village leaders based on motivation of participating farmers to learn and spread knowledge within their communities. But also based on vulnerability criteria, intended to ensure that vulnerable groups such as female-headed households, households with elderly or disabled individuals and recent returnees and IDPs are included.• Training vegetable growers in vegetable production and fisher folks in net making In handing out agricultural inputs through direct distribution, RWDSS will create awareness about improved techniques in the use of agricultural and fishing inputs distributed, RWDSS will train selected groups of farmers on basic post-harvest management techniques, along with improved cooking practices that promote nutrition within the household• Monitoring and Evaluation RWDSS Monitoring and Evaluation officer together with Project Implementation Committee (PIM) which will Comprise of Chiefs, Women Leaders, and representatives of IDPs, Returnees and Host community, youth leaders, Individuals with no community links, Individuals living with disability will be form to act as a link between project staffs and affected community as well as to ensure smooth implementation of the project. 7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project will encourages all beneficiaries and community members to report suspicions of sexual exploitation and/or abuse involving RWDSS Team Members, Partners, or Visitors. In order to facilitate reporting, RWDSS commits to establishing appropriate and accessible Community Accountability Reporting Mechanisms (CARM) and accountability to the affected population (AAP) where RWDSS and its partners work, especially for programs that present a significant risk of sexual exploitation or abuse of beneficiaries. RWDSS Team Members and Partners who receive allegations of sexual exploitation or abuse through CARM must follow their reporting obligations in accordance with the Ethics Complaint and Whistleblower Policy. Confidentially because of the nature and sensitivity of allegations involving sexual exploitation and abuse, RWDSS treats all reports and allegations with the greatest respect for confidentiality of all individuals involved. RWDSS will seek to maintain privacy at all times during the investigation process, consistent with our responsibility to maintain individual and team safety.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Previous Committees and similar FSL projects have taught us that ensuring communities and people affected by the distaste actively participate in responses is key to success and sustainability of the projects. Also, we believe that affected communities have the capacity to help themselves, plan their own action, manage and respond to needs or shocks facing them if right kind of support is provided to them. We therefore sought to empower affected communities during the intervention to build local capacity, accountability and ownership. RWDSS will make sure that women, girls, men and boys will be involved in decision making right from project inception, implementation and monitoring process. An inception meeting will conducted with chiefs, Payam Leaders, youth and women leaders to provide them with information regarding the project (targeting criteria, number of beneficiaries in each payam, Boma on the modality of intervention, role of the community in supporting the project, avenues for submitting complaints and receiving feedback as well as dates and locations for distribution). The Inception meeting will provide the affected population with the opportunity to express their priority needs and how well their needs can be addressed through the project. This culminate into formation of Project Implementation Committee and will Comprised of Chiefs, Women Leaders, and representatives of IDPs, Returnees and Host community, youth leaders, Individuals with no community links, Individuals living with disability will form to act as a link between project staffs and affected community as well as to ensure smooth implementation of the project. The PIM will receive basic training on activities planning, beneficiaries entitlements including basic human rights, Sexual and Gender Based Violence Prevention and reporting, how to receive and respond to beneficiaries complaints while observing their privacy and protection. The PIM also will participate in project activities such as identification, registration, verification and selection of beneficiaries, distribution, and post distribution monitoring, post planting and post-harvest assessments. In this approach, we believe we empowered affected communities, ensured accountability, transparency, build local capacity and generated local ownership.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Rural Women for Development South Sudan is committed to ensuring that all individuals we come into contact with through our work, whether Team Members, host community members, beneficiaries or others, are treated with respect and dignity. We expect Team Members, Visitors and Partners to put the well-being and best interests of its beneficiaries as the highest priority, to treat all beneficiaries and host community members with respect and dignity, and to never engage in conduct that could be perceived as abusive, exploitative or harassing. We expect management and all Team Members to fully foster a culture that supports this standard. We will not tolerate sexual exploitation, abuse, or harassment by our Team Members, Visitors or anyone else receiving RWDSS services. We are committed to the core principles regarding prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse laid out and the standards set out in the secretary general bulletin special measures for protection from sexual exploitation and sexual abuse(ST/SGB/2003 "Statement of Commitment on Eliminating Sexual Exploitation and Abuse by UN and non-UN Personnel This policy sets forth our expectations of personal and professional behavior related to sexual exploitation and/or abuse of beneficiaries and community members and the required procedures to ensure we uphold our commitment to preventing such conduct.
