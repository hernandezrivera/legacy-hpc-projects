Project Summary

Education Cameroon (EDUCAM) is a child/youth centred community development organisation that seeks to create environments where children, their families and communities can live and benefit from their own societies. EDUCAM works together with children and communities to promote quality education and psychosocial wellbeing to children and youths.
In the South West Region, 2,687 schools and 1,764 community learning centres were temporarily closed on the 18 of March 2020 in an attempt to contain the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic. This has greatly reversed progress in access to learning as a result of the COVID-19 outbreak. As a result, an estimated 463,000 school-aged children in the South West were forced to stay at home due to the dual emergencies, the COVID-19 outbreak and the ongoing socio-political conflict. The Government of Cameroon announced the reopening of schools for examination classes on the 1 June 2020 while children in other classes waited until October 2020 to resume school in a new academic year. As indicated in the OCHA Humanitarian needs overview, the most pressing needs for school-age children by the crisis include; access to a protective learning environment, quality and relevant education services provided by teachers and education personnel with the knowledge and skills to deal with large class sizes and children in distress, teaching and learning materials. Emergency measures are thus required to meet the educational need of children in this dual crisis. In alignment with the humanitarian response plan for 2020, psychosocial support and risk mitigation (including COVID-19 prevention) becomes vital in all interventions.
Most schools re-opening in the villages are private schools operating under difficult circumstances. They often have limited human resources and didactic materials to support learning. Regular lockdowns have caused children who attend these schools to have limited hours for learning.
The deteriorating situation in the concerned regions in terms of economy, social structure and psychological wellbeing for the affected population have serious consequences on children who have the opportunity to attend school. In terms of the economy, most parents have either lost their source of livelihood, or business is not as successful as before the crisis, making it difficult for them to fully support their children’s education. IDP parents are faced with serious challenge sustaining in their new environment, an issue having serious consequences on their school-going age children. Foster parents hosting IDP children of school-going age also have an additional burden to care properly for children, not theirs. Teenage mothers have issues balancing schooling and child-rearing.
As indicated above, Manyu Division is one of the most affected zones in the North West and South West Region of Cameroon. Education Cameroon carried out a need assessment for 3,000 students and pupils in three (3) subdivisions, from Manyu Division in the month of October and November 2020. The results show that at least about 50% are IDPs and are in need of serious help to support their learning.
The general objective is to provide learning support to children of school-going age in formal and non-formal educational establishments in Manyu Division, South West Region. The project targets the following:
1) 5000 people benefitting from COVID-19 response materials and knowledge
2) 20 Education Support Structures established
3) 600 students/pupils and teachers benefitting from didactic materials/teaching aids
4) 5000 people receiving sensitisation on child's rights to education
5) 2200 children and youths receiving psychosocial support
6) 1000 children benefitting from nutritional support in schools
7) 200 parents trained in providing learning support for children in difficulties

1 - Besoins / Needs

The crisis in the North West and South West Regions of Cameroon is a risk factor for children going to school. Some NSAG still uses schooling as a tool of resistance, thus, putting children who dare go to school at risk. The deteriorating situation in the concerned regions in terms of economy, social structure and psychological wellbeing for the affected population have serious consequences on children who have the opportunity to attend school. In terms of the economy, most parents have either lost their source of livelihood, or business is not as successful as before the crisis, making it difficult for them to fully support their children’s education. IDP parents are faced with serious challenge sustaining in their new environment, an issue having serious consequences on their school-going age children. Foster parents hosting IDP children of school-going age also have an additional burden to care properly for children, not theirs. Teenage mothers have issues balancing schooling and child-rearing.Within the framework of providing learning support to children and youths at risks in Manyu Division, South West Region of Cameroon, Education Cameroon carried out a need assessment in the month of October and November 2020 for both secondary and primary school children. The results show that most children going to school in Manyu have serious needs such as school needs, psychosocial needs, nutritional needs and health needs:-50% of students/Pupils assessed are IDPs (Separated and Unaccompanied children)-80% of students/pupils lack school needs such as textbooks, exercise books, and school bags-50% of students/pupils do not eat most of the time before going to school and need food support in schools-60% of students/pupils have lost a love one in the crisis-5% of students/pupils report being sexually assaulted either in their home, school or in the quarter.-50% of students/pupils need some form of psychosocial support-More than 60% lack personal protective equipment such as Face mask against COVID-19.
