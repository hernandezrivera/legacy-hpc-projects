Project Summary

SEMA through this project intends to provide both preventive (raising awareness, empowerment activities, Recreational activities, safety audit) and responsive (CM including CMR, PFA, and referrals to other specialized services) these services for (W, M, B, G).
SEMA will focus on enhancing integration between GBV and RH in both GBV and health delivering points, using 2 types of modalities (static, and mobile) for this purpose in all locations.
SEMA will support 2 established WGSS (Sharran, Atmeh camps), and will provide integrated GBV services in health facilities (Azaz, Al-Bab, Idlib, Atmeh, Kafirtakharim, and Ariha) as well as in mobile clinics at camps (Azaz, Atmeh, Ariha).
Key strategies will be taken into consideration during the implementation:
1- Provide specialized GBV services to survivors with supervision, all cases will be uploaded to GBVIMS+, Providing Individual case funds using the GBVSC SOP’s,
2- Provide activities empowering women and girls and GBV prevention activities including Awareness-raising toolkit, life skills activities, and vocational training, small grants will be provided for empowerment purposes, SEMA will use the guidance note (Sops of Cash Income Generating Activities -IGAs) developed by UNFPA during the implementation of this activity.
3- Providing and enhancing specialized adolescent girls programming, including life skills programs and AMAL initiative.
4- Support women and girls with disabilities to reach service delivery points (both static and outreach).
5- Train humanitarian actors (non-specialists) to reduce the risks of GBV and refer to specialized services.
6- Support women-led-organization inside Syria to expand their GBV technical knowledge and GBV programming
7- Conduct regular safety-audit in the formal camps.

4 - Needs

The year 2021 marked the end of the 10th year of the conflict in Syria (WoS GBV AoR2021 advocacy brief)-Women and girls reported higher levels of intimate partner violence in 2021, predominantly associated with men spending longer periods of time at home, either due to COVID-19 restrictions or unemployment. These forms of violence are eroding the resilience of women and girls and can cause severe physical and mental health consequences, including unwanted pregnancies, permanent disabilities, and increased levels of psychological distress, which can sometimes result in suicide. Social stigmatization continues to affect GBV survivors, including women and girls who survive detention, and marginalized women and girls more broadly, women and girls are facing physical injury and health complications due to GBV, including serious and life-long sexual and reproductive health issues. There are increased reports of early and forced marriage as well as sexual harassment and exploitation. Cases of femicide are becoming a growing concern in some areas. Lastly, technology-facilitated GBV, such as harassment and sexual exploitation committed online, has also been reported more frequently. According to Voices2021, women and girls still experienced violence from partners or husbands. They also experienced violence from family like brothers and fathers and strangers each day. In certain places like camps, women and girls were more likely to experience violence.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will be directly managed, in cooperation with the GBV Sub Cluster Coordinator, and directly through the SEMA field who has a great GBV experience over the few past years, Staff have been hired from the targeted communities in Syria which build better acceptance with targeted communities and increase the understanding for the essential needs of those communities.All activities will be conducted to achieve the following overall objective: Enhanced quality of GBV services and prevention of GBV.This objective will be achieved through the following activities:1: Provide specialized GBV services to survivors2: Train the GBV actors to respond to GBV through a survivor-centered and inclusive approach3: provide safe spaces for women and girls.4: Provide activities empowering women and girls and GBV prevention activities including Awareness-raising activities, life skills activities, and vocational training.5: Providing and enhancing specialized adolescent girls programming:6: Support women and girls with disabilities to reached service delivery points (both static and outreach).7: train humanitarian actors (non-specialists) to reduce risks of GBV and refer to specialized services. 8: Support women-led-organization inside Syria to expand their GBV technical knowledge and GBV programming9: provide Individual Protection Assistance (IPA) to respond to the immediate lifesaving protection needs10: Conduct regular safety audits in the formal camps.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SEMA ensures the privacy of the beneficiaries and vulnerable groups through adjusting and keeping the data of the beneficiaries secured, being responsible for the principle “Do-no-Harm” to the beneficiaries, and keeping their dignity. There is effective feedback and complaint mechanism and beneficiaries are made aware of how to access it. This mechanism includes developed practices and channels for reporting Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. The concept of PSEA is also considered in the awareness-raising topics offered for children and adults to raise their awareness on their rights and safety and the safety measure to report such misconduct. IEC materials, banners, and posters will be available in all facilities and distribution points. Staff will be oriented in the contracting stage on SEA issues and will sign the CoC. Refresher training for the staff will be conducted on a regular basis. All complaints will be taken into consideration and will be investigated following SEMA’s investigation policies, Disciplinary measures will be taken against any breach of CoC.
