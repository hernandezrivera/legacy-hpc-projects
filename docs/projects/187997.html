Project Summary

This project will greatly contribute to supporting the self-reliance of affected households by protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income-generating opportunities to save and sustain lives
This project aims to support the olive sector and vulnerable olive farmers in Idlib and Afrin districts to support workers group’s resilience through the provision of agricultural services and inputs in addition to the cash for work related to income generation activities through supporting farmers and households.
The project’s interventions will target IDPs and host residents regardless of their ethnic, cultural, or social background, focusing on female-headed households by increasing their knowledge on how to benefit and use the olive chain value by food production and marketing awareness sessions provided. Moreover, the elderly and persons with special needs to be targeted through the project activities with appropriate modalities.
Sadad well-trained and experienced technical team will focus on improving food security and supporting livelihood by providing two main components:
1-Support for best agriculture services to increase the productivity of 1000 hectares of olive trees (2000 HHs, most of them females headed by support 5 dunums per household) through providing the following:
• Autumn and spring land plowing;
• Harvest service;
• Two rounds of leaf-nutrition high K and high N;
• Pest controls (olive fly, olive moth, stem digger, and peacock eye disease);
• Technical pruning service;
• Providing the targeted 2000 olive farmers' HHs with 2 olive-oil extraction vouchers per household (each voucher will be used to extract olive oil from 1250 kg of olives;
• Conducting the olive oil extraction process in the selected and contracted mills;
• Conducting awareness-raising seminars for 700 farmers on integrated olive-cultivation management, pest control, olive trees' services calendar, and avoiding negative methods related to olive cultivation, food production, and marketing of products for local usage.
The project targets 2535 workers in the CFW system to support 50 a thousand olive trees by providing support for 1000 A farmer who has olive trees as follows:
- 1400 beneficiaries will be hired, divided into groups, and provided with cash for 25 working days to conduct olive fruit harvesting service;
- Vocational training on technical olive tree pruning, 450 male beneficiaries will receive 7days of training on technical olive tree pruning and then will be provided with cash for 36working days to perform technical pruning to the targeted hectares;
- 365 Males' beneficiaries will be provided with cash for 30 working days to conduct spraying service, two rounds, for the targeted 1000 hectares of the olive;
in addition to a three-day training workshop on integrated olive management, and an introduction to minimum standards in humanitarian action including how to deal with benefit caries according to humanitarian standards
• Mainstreaming General protection through conducting 36 awareness-raising sessions on; GBV related risks، child labor and child marriage, awareness on mine action issues
and this will include distributing awareness-raising brochures and leaflets.
As a result, the project will contribute mainly to enhancing food security by supporting live agriculture and income generation means and supporting the resilience of the vulnerable groups within the targeted communities.

4 - Needs

The agriculture sector, which was once a cornerstone of the Syrian economy, has also been severely impacted. High cost and limited availability of agriculture inputs, degradation of livelihoods and income-earning opportunities, direct and knock-on effects of COVID-19 on food security and the agriculture sector, the devastating wildfires, and other climate-induced shocks such as erratic rainfall, have all contributed to the worsening situation in Syria.According to needs assessment analysis conducted by Sadad’s field teams shows that:• There is a high cost of agriculture services like; pesticides, pruning, and milling leading to an increase in production costs. • The high cost of fuel for supplementary irrigation, led to the olive farmers depending on only rain to water the trees as a negative adaptation;• The spread of pests and the high costs of the control process (insect diseases, and fungi diseases);• Marketing difficulties and limited access to local markets.• The high costs of daily workers led to low farmer incomes and high services costs.Also, the needs assessment shows that 90% of those residents of the surveyed areas were depending on olive trees production or service provided to it as a source of income with a lack of provision of assistance from organizations and local authorities, especially in light of Covid-19 epidemic and the devaluation of the Syrian pound. Sadad makes sure to target the most vulnerable groups and high Districts (Afrin and Idleb),

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will be implemented through Sadad well trained technical teams in Idleb and Afrin Districts. The implementation process will be done with the collaboration of local councils and the agricultural offices in the targeted villages. After that, announcements of the project's job opportunities will be done, then signing the contracts -Dene and select the regions and villages appropriate for the project’s work according to criteria ( density of olive trees - population density - number of displaced) -MOUs will be signed between Sadad and 8 Olive presses-Register the beneficiaries and accept according to certain criteria.- Purchasing leaf nutrition and pests control materials (high K and high N)-Contracting with tractors and related plowing equipment-Providing some exercises for the project beneficiaries The operations of serving, caring for, and maintaining the olive tree.-Conducting deep need assessment to select beneficiaries according to specific criteria. -Contracting with 3850 workers to provide various olive services such as spraying pesticides, picking, loading and unloading, and various land services this depends on the season of services provided-During implementing the whole project's services, the Sadad protection team will conduct awareness-raising sessions about common protection issues and feedback from beneficiaries-Follow-up service operations, especially M&E

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Sadad during implementing the activities of the project may face these risks according to the project's activities. Protection risk 1: Community tension arising from the selection.Likelihood: high / Impact: High. Mitigation: Sadad will consider the most vulnerable female-headed household, males with PSNs HHs, considering the children situations and difficulties within these HHs (highlighted to HHs including males and children withdisabilities or chronic diseases and unaccompanied elderly.), to receive services duringthis project. Sadad has collaborated with local leaders (to make them aware of the wholeproject activities and interventions) in designing the project to minimize harm andsupport local capacities to build peace. Protection risk 2: Risk of harassment of womenand girls by the humanitarian workers. Likelihood: medium, Impact: high. Mitigation:establishing a confidential complaint mechanism and enhancing accountabilitymechanisms besides training the staff to deal with beneficiaries in addition to providingworkers with training on humanitarian standards and signing a code of conduct


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Sadad has a Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA policy that usually is attached to each employee’s job contract clarifying all the related procedures. Sadad is an active member of the PSEA Network and will collaborate with the PSEA network to support quality implementation in the project. Risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers. Mitigation: Sadad ensures to have in a place an accessible mechanism for reporting sexual exploitation and abuse in line with Sadad safeguarding policy. Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and who services are for. Provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA. Risk: Lack of understanding of employees about PSEA issues. Sadad understands that, in order for the PSEA/Child Safeguarding policy to be well understood and effectively implemented, it is essential that there are high levels of awareness regarding the policy and that staff and others are clear, con dent and competent in putting the policy into practice. Disseminate and promote the policy widely. Copies of the policy will be made available and accessible to all stakeholders, both in hard copy and electronically in order to ensure that the policy commitments, principles, and practices are publicized and that everyone is clear about Sadad's position in relation to PSEA and safeguarding children.

The security situation within the targeted villages is estimated to be safe to ensure the best implementation and provision of services as the villages related to rural Idlib and Afrin districts of them are considered to be safe after the spread of Turkishmonitoring points and located under Turkish government control areas (Olive Branchareas and Idlib). Risk 1: beneficiaries registration process. Likelihood: Low. Impact:Medium. Mitigation: • beneficiaries to be selected based on clear selection criteria;•conducting meetings with a community representative, interested parties to be invited through a public announcement to ensure fair distribution and selection of Bens;• field staff will be trained on the code of conduct, and PSEA procedures;• Verification process of selected beneficiaries;• Feedback mechanism to be activated in the field to respond to these issues.Risks 2: Community tension between host and IDPs. Likelihood: Medium.Impact: Low. Mitigation:• A community-based selection of beneficiaries of most vulnerable host and IDPs in close coordination with local authorities;• Clear selection criteria of beneficiaries;• Feedback mechanism to be activated in the field to respond to these issues.
