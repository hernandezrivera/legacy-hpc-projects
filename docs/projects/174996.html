Project Summary

Promote humanitarian access in a complex operating environment, where the security situation remains volatile; (ii) Strengthen humanitarian response coordination mechanisms and strategies as appropriate and adapted to the evolving context; (iii) establishing an efficient humanitarian coordination mechanism in Niamey, Diffa, Tillabery, Maradi and Tahoua (iv) optimizing the cooperation with the Government of Niger and development actors trough the operationalization of the Nexus humanitarian / development, capitalizing on the willingness of government of Niger to take the lead on humanitarian issues while considering how to further support them in terms of capacity building; (v) Advocate for principled humanitarian action and adequate funding for humanitarian activities in all sectors by working closely with donors towards more support and funding tools to national responders; (VI) Promote the development of a shared regional understanding of common humanitarian issues (Lac Chad Basin, Sahel Central and Liptako Gourma) amongst OCHA offices and contribute to Regional Office products; (VII) Increased accountability of the humanitarian system to meet humanitarian needs

1 - Besoins

Niger, a Sahel country, is a fragile island of stability in a region in turmoil and is struggling to respond to deteriorating humanitarian conditions. All its eight regions are grappling with humanitarian emergencies at varying scales. The armed conflicts that affect Nigeria since 2013 and Mali since 2012 have spread over Niger resulting in significant protection and humanitarian needs in the Diffa region as well as in the Maradi, Tillabéri and Tahoua regions. Killings, lootings and kidnappings have become more frequent, creating tensions and fears among the local communities with Non-State Armed Groups (NSAG) increasingly targeting civilian population and humanitarian organizations, causing new displacements and preventing the displaced population from returning to their community of origin. As a result, more than 90,000 new IDPs and refugees have been identified as of September 2020 to a total of 530,000 people displaced in the four regions, Diffa, Tahoua, Tillabéri and Maradi. The context of armed conflicts exacerbates the inter-group tensions and opportunistic criminality in these regions, making it very difficult to clearly understand the nature of violent attacks or other threats to either the local population or relief actors. Natural disasters, continue to affect vulnerable populations and their livelihoods across the country. These are the floods that generate large numbers of disaster victims (620 000 people), (Source, MAH/GC, Nov.2020), food and nutrition insecurity and epidemics.The health situation is characterized by the prevalence of diseases with epidemic potential such as measles, meningitis and the coronavirus pandemic. Humanitarian needs have increased due to the coronavirus pandemic. Also, the barrier measures to be observed have reduced access to populations in need who have become more vulnerable.Civilians and humanitarian workers are increasingly the target of attacks by non-state armed groups causing further displacement and preventing displaced populations from returning to their home communities. In such a context, OCHA will have to respond to the following needs: • Need to work towards the setup of a more strategic coordination framework that affirms the leadership of the Government over the implementation of the humanitarian response in the framework of the New Way of Working. This will entitle the design of new humanitarian mechanisms that take into consideration the need to advance the humanitarian / development nexus at the national and regional level • Need to adapt the information products to the needs of various actors including the government and development and humanitarian actors • The need to ensure access to humanitarian workers to provide adequate and timely assistance to those in need • Need to ensure coherent communication on the humanitarian context, needs and challenges • Need to support informed decisions making process by the leadership, namely HC and HCT. • Need to reinforce mechanisms for the mainstreaming of accountability and resilience throughout the program cycle and into cluster strategies • Need to reinforce coordination mechanisms to consider cross-cutting issues • Need to ensure that the Protection of civilians remains at the center of the humanitarian response • Need to ensure that PSEA is consider in all activities • Importance of operationalization of the PHDN (Peace-Humanitarian and Development Nexus).
