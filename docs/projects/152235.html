Project Summary

The proposed project supports both non-formal and formal spaces to ensure the most marginalized children are attending and learning. The project will support children in gaining relevant literacy, numeracy and social emotional skills. In the formal schools, we will establish a tutoring program to provide children at risk of drop-out with needed additional academic support. In the Non Formal Learning Centers (NFLCs), we will provide an accelerated learning program using the national basic literacy curriculum for NFE, and will support learner’s transition from non-formal to formal school

Needs

The IRC is currently implementing an education in emergency project DFID EiE in 10 local government areas of Borno and Yobe State to establish non-formal learning centers for IDP children affected by the insurgency, children with disabilities and those from the poorest households. IRC’s work is informed by several assessments conducted in the target area, these include: Community Education Conflict Assessment baseline survey followed by rolling assessments every six months to date in five states including Borno and Yobe; baseline and end-line for each cohort of ECR students across the states– assessing their literacy, numeracy (using ASER) and SEL (using SDQ); IRC and Creative joint team in-depth review of the ECR model in 2016; IRC’s education assessment in March 2015; a joint INGO education assessment conducted in Adamawa in December 2015; a rapid multi-sectoral assessment conducted in Mubi North, Mubi South, Maiha and Hong LGAs (Adamawa State) in June 2015; a rapid needs assessment in Maiduguri metropolitan area in October 2015; and Save The Children in March 2017 report: Turning Education Around: Responding to Crisis in Northeastern Nigeria and Humanitarian Response Plan 2017. Some of the key issues found are highlighted below; • Lack of access to learning in IDP sites and host communities. • Reduced economic livelihoods that result from displacement have also led to lower attendance in school and the preference to send boys to school, rather than girls, given the limited and shrinking resources of displaced families and strict gender roles and societal expectations• Overstretched learning spaces and teachers in IDP sites and host communities. School closures remain high, particularly in Borno where 943 remain closed as of June 2017• Small pool of teachers in the affected areas due to displacement, killings, fear and teacher turnover.• Learning infrastructure damaged, destroyed or occupied by IDPs due to conflict. • Classrooms no longer conducive to learning due to loss of classroom equipment, destruction by insurgents, looting and occupation by IDPs. • Unsafe learning environments. Girls surveyed in assessments have reported being harassed on their way to school • Children unable to focus on learning due to the debilitating effect exposure to various stressors such as displacement, violence, or loss of loved ones • Teachers have inadequate capacities to identify psycho social issues in children in the classroom and to refer them onward to mental health professionals• From baseline ASER assessments in ECR – this has resulted in alarmingly low literacy rates, and very limited numeracy skills• Poor attendance and continuous disruption of education due to displacement, economic insecurity among others• Fractured communities, where violence and victimization has eroded the social cohesion and levels of resilience.

Activities

The IRC will implement the proposed project activities in 10 in LGAs of Biu, Jere, Kaga, Konduga, MMC, Monguno, Gwozo ( in Borno) and Potiskum, Damaturu and Bade (in Yobe). The project intervention targets educationally marginalized and vulnerable children who are out of school, and children who are in school but struggling academically and are at risk of drop out. The project has five Outputs outlined below: Output 1 - Girls and boys (6-14) enroll in and attend safe, predictable and caring non-formal education program - aims to provide functional education opportunities for marginalized children through the establishment of NFLCs, overseen by CCs. This is a nine month accelerated learning program focusing on literacy, numeracy and SEL, that aims to transition students to upper primary. Central to Output 1 is working with communities to support enrolment and regular attendance of learners, providing children with material support to attend non-formal and formal schools, and helping learners transition from non-formal to formal schools. Output 2 - Tutors and Learning Facilitators deliver quality literacy, numeracy and SEL instruction - aims to ensure that all learners have relevant literacy, numeracy and SEL skills. Activities will focus on the development and/ or enhancement of relevant, quality curricula tailored to learners’ needs and level, for both the NFLCs and the tutoring program. Activities will also focus on the professional development and continuous support of both the learning facilitators and tutors via face-to-face training, continuous mentoring and learning circles.Output 3 - Parents of NFLC children are informed and supported to enroll their children into formal schools – aims to ensure that parents are aware of education opportunities available, and are supporting their children to enroll and encourage their learning. Activities will focus on information sessions with parents at the community level and dissemination of information on round opportunities available and processes to enroll, as well as the removal of financial barriers via bursaries for parents who can’t afford the indirect costs of education. Output 4 - Communities support the enrolment and retention of learners, particularly girls and IDPs - will focus on working closely with community structures, such as community coalitions and school management committees (SMCs), to target the most marginalized learners, to ensure the relevance and ownership of the project, and to ensure activities are sustained beyond its life cycle, where feasible. Output 5 - Government actively supporting NFE – aims to ensure that the government is fully informed and fully involved in every step of the program, and that key interventions are sustained by the government beyond the life cycle of the project. Activities will focus on the setting up of a steering group committee and technical working groups, involving SAME and SUBEB officials, National Commission for Mass Literacy (NMEC) and Nigerian Educational Research and Development Council (NERDC), and staff from colleges of education and universities in coaching and mentoring.
