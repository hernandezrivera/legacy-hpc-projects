Project Summary

Zimbabwe is experiencing a rapidly deteriorating humanitarian crisis with high inflation, increasing food prices, deindustrialization, increasing unemployment; this economic decline has been further impacted by recurrent droughts and cyclone Idai. This has resulted in Zimbabwe facing its worst food insecurity in decades with 3.5 million people (38% of the rural population) in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse and requiring urgent action to reduce food consumptions gaps and save livelihoods. Among these, an estimated 2.47 million people face Crisis conditions (IPC Phase 3) whilst 1.10 million are in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). Particularly vulnerable to malnutrition in this rapidly deteriorating environment are children under 5, and more particularly those living with disabilities and HIV.
Responding to this emergency OPHID will support the most vulnerable children under 5 years suffering from SAM and MAM and will deliver high impact interventions to save lives in two provinces and 10 districts of Zimbabwe. There is a substantial connection between, malnutrition, HIV and TB prevalence. HIV has been shown to be a significant cause of severe acute malnutrition with children who have HIV being treated for SAM facing a significantly higher risk of dying than uninfected children being treated for SAM. Lack of food has been cited as one of the reasons for poor ARV treatment adherence in the region. Poor nutrition may also reduce immunities and increase the risk for co-infections of TB and HIV together with malnutrition. Ensuring that the risk of children in therapeutic feeding programs to attain TB is assessed, and IPT prophylaxis is provided as per guidelines is critical to achieving high survival rates. Furthermore that children are tested and treated for HIV. HIV and TB both impact on the recovery prognosis of malnutrition treatment.
The project is leveraged on OPHID’s presence in all of the 10 districts under the PEPFAR/USAID funded Families and Communities for the Elimination of HIV (FACE-HIV) (we have a district office, transport and a team in each district). Furthermore, we have focused on the Provinces of Masvingo and Manicaland where OPHID also has provincial offices. The work builds on OPHID’s UNICEF Emergency Response to Cyclone Idai and the drought. The project is running in 6 of these 10 districts until 29th February 2020. The ten month Project will be implemented by the deployment of one Project Assistant in each of 10 districts with one Project Coordinator based at head office providing technical assistance and monitoring with the oversight of the Deputy Executive Director and the support of a well capacitated finance and operations team. OPHID will focus on the districts of Buhera, Chipinge, Makoni, Mutasa and Mutare in Manicaland. And Chivi, Chiredzi, Gutu, Masvingo and Zaka for a period of 12 months.
OPHID’s will scale up high impact interventions to all health facilities in each of the 10 district and focusses on identifying and treating children under 5 with SAM and MAM, testing them for HIV and screening for TB and treating appropriately. VHWs, EHTs, WNCs and HCCs are capacity built in IMAM and mentored to identify children with SAM and MAM and ensure referral for treatment and care. HCWs are capacity built and mentored in Outpatient Therapeutic Management of Acute malnutrition (OTP) and inpatient facility based management of Severe Acute Malnutrition to ensure that children with acute malnutrition are identified and appropriately treated for malnutrition, tested for HIV and screened for TB and referred to treatment and care with each case correctly documented and reported to recovery.
