Project Summary

Health systems strengthening is key in reduction of morbidity and mortality rates and especially for pregnant and lactating mothers and children. South Sudan suffers the effects of poor health systems that has led to poor performance of these key global indicators of improved health outcomes.
Women Agency for Resilience and Transformation (WART) aims to reduce the mortality and morbidity of people especially children and pregnant mothers in Rubkona and Ayod Counties that have been affected by conflict and flooding and other vulnerable people such as IPDs and host community by improving access to quality integrated health care and lifesaving services. WART plans to achieve this through Mobile clinics and Static Locations (PHCUs) and outreach activities that will offer primary health care as per the basic package for health and nutrition services.
WART will in the health facilities and mobile units offer outpatient consultations, immunization, comprehensive reproductive health service, laboratory test, response to disease outbreaks through disease surveillance intensification and timely response to epidemic-prone diseases alerts.
In addition, WART will conduct mobile clinic with mobile heath team of qualified health workers to provide mobile health services to people located in hard to reach areas and especially the displaced populations cut off from health services by the floods that have affected health service delivery in Rubkona and Ayod Counties.
With this project, WART will scale up out reach and mobile EPI services that will help in improving Immunization coverage in Rubkona and Ayod Counties since they are faced with poor immunization coverage. This will be done by offering mobile vaccination and vitamin A supplementation services to children under five years. Vaccinators will be identified, trained and equipped to conduct outreach immunization and defaulter tracing to ensure that all the children complete their immunization schedule and avoid increased under immunized and zero dose children.
WART will improve access to reproductive health services such as antenatal care, health facility delivery done by skilled birth attendants, Folic acid supplementation, family planning by emphasizing sensitization for women and girls of the importance of utilization of reproductive health services, as well as by engaging men and community leaders importance and availability of reproductive health services in the health facilities and the advantages of the utilization of these essential services.
WART will ensure quality of services by mitigating medical commodities and equipment shortage due to weak supply chain, interruption of medical supplies affecting the quality of preventive and curative services in all locations. In order to mitigate delays and also access challenges during the rain season, WART will preposition essential medical supplies and train health workers on rational use of drugs and proper drugs prescriptions. This will be done in collaboration with the cluster and the county health departments in the target locations.
WART will address the high stigmatization of mental illness at the community level which affects case detection and management. There will be SGBV services to the vulnerable and affected populations to ensure that the survivors of Sexual abuse and gender based violence have access to health services.
Nutrition screening and growth monitoring will also be done at the health facilities including referral to nutrition centers for management .
WART will target 27500 vulnerable and affected populations in Ayod and Rubkona Counties. This will include 8000 males and 9500 females, of which 10000 will be children under five years and 2750 people with special needs including People with disabilities and the elderly.
WART will offer mobile health services to the affected populations by having 4 PHCUs and 4 Mobile units in two counties to reach the most vulnerable and flood affected communities.

1. Needs

According to an IRNA conducted in Ayod County in September 2024, Health challenges are acute in Ayod county due to compounding issues linked to Flooding and poor health systems. Due to the floods, there is evidence of high prevalence of malaria, Pneumonia and diarrheal cases in the areas of IDP settlement as per health facility registers and respondents.Other key findings were poor referral systems as patients are carried on local stretchers due to flooding that exposes them to delayed service delivery and thus increased mortality rates. Inadequate antimalarial and other essential medicine stock supplies in the health facilities puts the populations in Ayod to risk of poor health service delivery in the health facilities. The health workers reported likelihood of stock out of essential drugs before the next quarterly supply due to increased population in the areas.Lack of elderly and disability inclusive health and WASH facilities in two health facilities/nutrition sites of Pagil PHCC of Pagil Payam and Paguong PHCC of Wau Payam are submerged in water hence reduction in health service delivery. They are also highly inaccessible especially for the elderly and persons with disabilities. Host/IDP communities in the flooded areas far away from the health facilities face challenges in accessing health services.Pit latrines in the assessed health facilities have since collapsed due rain and flooding. This poses the threats of contamination of household water sources thus increasing the risk of water borne diseases. There was evidence of snake bites in the assessed Payams. (AYOD IRNA Sep 2024).Rubkona County is facing similar health challenges due to the flooding and acute disease outbreaks. According to Cholera outbreak issue 5 Nov 2024, Rubkona county is among the 17 counties having an active outbreak. This can be attributed to poor sanitation facilities and contamination of water points.Rubkona County, located in South Sudan's Unity State, is facing critical health and nutrition challenges due to a convergence of conflict, climate-induced flooding, and a large influx of internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees. Here are the key aspects of the current situation:Health conditions are deteriorating, with a rising incidence of diseases like malaria, diarrhea, and hepatitis E, linked to poor water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) conditions. Approximately 41% of surveyed individuals reported illness in the two weeks prior to a July 2024 assessment.Medical supply shortages and limited access to healthcare facilities exacerbate morbidity. In some areas like Rotriak, funding gaps have led to the withdrawal of health partners, leaving critical gaps in health care service delivery. (Impact Initiative Sep 2024)The county is experiencing severe food insecurity, with many IDP households classified under IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) and pockets at Phase 5 (Catastrophe). Key drivers include flooding, high food prices, and limited access to arable land for farming (Relief Web Humanitarian Situation analysis Oct 2024).Sanitation is a major issue, with 38% of households practicing open defecation, and most latrines in IDP camps like Bentiu are either full or non-functional. These conditions increase the risk of waterborne diseases. This has led to an active cholera outbreak in the county.All these health needs contribute to the poor country health indicators with maternal and child health indicators remain very high in the country with maternal mortality rate of 789/100000 live births, infant mortality rates of 64/1000 live births and under five mortality rates of 96/1000 live births being one of the highest globally (UNICEF 2024). These therefore calls for integrated health service delivery to ensure that vulnerable and affected populations have access to integrated health care services in Rubkona and Ayod Counties.

2. Project activities

WART will carry out health activities in line with the health cluster objectives as follows:Reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic-prone diseases and health insecurities in Ayod and Rubkona Counties by:• Timely detection and response of health alerts through timely reporting to EWARS and IDSR to the county health departments• Immunization of the children under five against vaccine preventable infections• Vitamin A supplementation and deforming for children under five yearsImprove access to integrated health services in the target Counties by:• Management of communicable diseases like malaria, pneumonia and acute watery diarrhea with appropriate treatment protocols• Laboratory screening of malaria using RDTs• ANC services and skilled birth attendants deliveries in the health facilities• Postnatal care for both the mothers and the neonates.• Immunization services to children under five years• Folic acid supplementation of pregnant mothers• Health education and promotion with key prevention messages on early health seeking behavior, utilization of preventive health services like immunization and good hygiene and sanitation practices to prevent diarrhea diseases . • Training health workers on infection prevention and control and other key topics like IDSR and Case management of rape.• Referral of complicated cases to higher level health care facilities.• Mental health and psycho social support to displaced populations• Provision of SGBV services to the vulnerable and affected populations• Offer reproductive health services like family planning• Screening and management of malnutrition including referral to nutrition centers.• Trauma management and wound care• Community consultative meetings on the implementation of the project• Engagement with the county health department and the state authorities to avoid duplication of services.• Support supervision to the health facilities in collaboration with the county health departments.• Reporting on EWARS and 5W to the cluster.
