Project Summary

The crises in South Sudan has yet continued to pose a lot of suffering to the people of South Sudan albeit the implementation of the Revitalized Peace Agreement signed two years ago. The main drivers noted to have exacerbated the continued humanitarian needs in the Country in 2020 were: intensified conflict and sub-national violence, a second consecutive year of major flooding, and the impacts of COVID-19. The increasing needs were majorly found to be driven by rising food insecurity. 8.3 Million people are projected to be in need of humanitarian support in 2021. Out of the 8.3 Million people in need, those food insecure are the majority standing at 7.7 Million people across the country( The Humanitarian Needs Overview 2021). The purpose of this emergency livelihood support is to save lives, protect livelihoods, enhance food production and strengthen the resilience of vulnerable communities by building their coping capacity. As such, the intervention sets the foundation for enhancing the long-term adaptive capacity of communities in Lainya County and strengthening synergies among the activities Humanity Aid Service with its partners will pursue towards building resilience. For Lainya County, the Integrated Phase Classification 2021 projected 93.5 thousand people to be in need, by severity phase, gender, age and disability with 39.4, 33.1 and 21 thousands in severe, extreme and catastrophic levels respectively.
Within this context, Humanity Aid Service with support from the South Sudan Humanitarian Response Fund will distribute assorted agriculture livelihood kits( vegetable and crop seeds, tools and equipment) to a population of 55,469 to the affected population; those in crises IPC 3 and IPC 4 in Lainya County of Central Equatoria Sate as estimated by the South Sudan Food Security Cluster. The emergency livelihood support is guided with the food security cluster objectives:
i) to enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetables seeds, crop seeds and farm equipment distribution;
ii) to reduce dependency on food and agriculture inputs to support and strengthen households' ability to absorb shocks. The project will also integrate the COVID-19 operational guidelines developed by the food security cluster and most profoundly the Standard Operational Procedures(SOPs) developed by the Food and Agriculture Organization which guide the activities for the FSL interventions. The South Sudan Food Security Cluster estimates a total population of 55,469 for Lainya County to be in IPC phase 3 and above for the year 2021. Some proportion of the affected population will be supported with the same intervention by Humanity Aid Service through the Food and Agriculture Organization pipeline in which the organization has been an implementer from 2018-2020 and will continue for the 2021 Emergency Livelihood Response Program.
As an experienced NGO in the county implementing similar food security and livelihood project for the last three years, the entity posses the capacity in all levels to deliver the project with ease to meet its goals.

1 - Needs

The implementation of the Revitalized Peace Agreement signed two years ago has not reduced the humanitarian needs around the country. An estimated 8.3 Million people across South Sudan are projected to be in need across sectors. In 2020, the communities were hit hard by shocks attributed to intensified conflict and sub-national violence, a second consecutive year of major flooding, and the impacts of COVID-19. People’s physical and mental well being, living standards and coping mechanisms are expected to further deteriorate in 2021. Some 8.3 million people in South Sudan are estimatedto be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2021. These include 8,000,000 South Sudanese women, men, girls and boys and 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers. This is an 800,000 increase in absolute numbers from the 7.5 million people in need in 2020. According to the intersectoral severity of needs analysis, humanitarian needs are most concerning in Pibor County in Jonglei which was classified as the only county in catastrophic need. A total of 72 counties face extreme needs while five are in severe need. The increase in needs is largely driven by the rising food insecurity. According to the HNO 2021, Humanitarian needs in South Sudan are mainly driven by the impacts of years of conflict and exacerbated by the impact of climate change. Spikes in sub-national violence during 2020 resulted in more than 2,000 civilians killedbetween January and October and people displaced. COVID-19 has had a devastating and multi-faceted socioeconomic impact on people, including severe economic contractions, spikes in prices of basic commodities, loss oflivelihoods particularly in urban areas, increased protection risks, and disrupted access to basic services. The already serious humanitarian situation has been compounded by severe flooding, affecting approximately 1 million people each year in 2019 and 2020. The South Sudanese people also continue to be highly vulnerable to epidemic diseases, due to low immunization coverage, a weak health system and poor hygiene and sanitation. Although people across the country are impacted by the protracted humanitarian crisis, states such as Jonglei, Upper Nile, Unity, Lakes and Warrap continue to face multiple shocks, further deepening extreme levels of vulnerabilities. HNO 2021 also documented that, a marked escalation in sub-national violence has been taking place in recent years, especially in 2019 and 2020. The security situation at the sub-national level deterioratedin 2020, with complex conflict among ethnic groups operating along increasingly fractious, and shifting alliances and targeted military/armed campaigns being carried out from January to July, UNMISS documented more than 570 incidents of sub-national conflict, an increase of 300 percent compared to the same period in 2019 Sub-national violence between communities was most commonly reported type of violence during the same reporting period. Organized sub-national and localized violence accompaniedby cattle raiding and revenge attacks between ethnic groups, as well as between sections or clans of the same ethnic group, intensified, resulting in deaths, destruction and looting, especially in Jonglei. While such sub-nationaland localized violence have often reflected attempts to settle disputes over land and resources, they have also been shaped by and linked to political developments at the federal and state levels. The food security and livelihoods ranks the highest across the needs with an estimated population of 7.7 people in South Sudan with 59% of them in extreme levels of the IPC phase classification. 33% and 8% are projected to be in the severe, stressed phases respectively. The Food Security Cluster estimates a total population of 55,469 to remain in IPC phase 3+( IPC 2021) for the rest of the year 2021, hence a dire need of integrated assistance with agriculture inputs distribution as one of the crucial components to improve livelihoods.

2 - Project activities

1- Recruitment and training. Project assistants and volunteers will be recruited and trained to help enhance their capability to deliver the project out puts as may be described.2. Establishment of community mechanisms. Community representatives who will be selected, trained and tasked to represent the different social structures. Community Representative Committees and Accountability to affected populations committee(AAP) will be the main committees in which the Women, men, representatives of the vulnerable, PSEA representatives among others will be selected. The formed committees will be represented in all the project life.3- Safety Audit to establish activity sites/locations. Safety Audit will be conducted before the selection of activity locations (mobilization, registration, training sites and most importantly distribution points). It is intended to assess the possible risk factors regarding the protection of women and girls men and boys. Data will be collected using the GBV Safety Audit Tool or any other relevant tool that will be provided by the food security cluster. The recommendations from the safety audit will determine the selection and establishment of the activity sites and most importantly distribution points. 4-Mobilization and sensitization of communities. The mobilization and sensitization process will be carried out by the HAS project team with support from the established community mechanisms: chiefs, CRCs and AAPs. This will be through initial meetings with the local authorities at the county, local leaders and representatives of community vulnerable groups and further sensitization at the grass root where the chiefs of the areas play the greatest role in mobilization and in coordination with the Community Management Committees. The process is aimed for the community to well understand about the design of the project and the prerogative roles of each stakeholder. This stage also involves coordination with the partners for easy coordination during need i.e FSL, Health, GBV, Protection, WASH which are crucial for enhanced modalities.5- Identification and Registration of beneficiaries. A community based approach that is stigma sensitive will be used for targeting the beneficiaries. The preselection in the approach will include the chiefs and the elders as they are the information base at the community level. The beneficiaries will be pre-selected by the community leaders and elders and later registered after verifying their eligibility. The protection partners are crucial at this stage to help in identifying the survivors of GBV and other protection related issues. The verification of the beneficiaries for the kits to be received will be through FOAs' criteria and the main key determinants being: a piece of land land to utilize the inputs, consent to attend capacity building trainings, presence of fishing ground etc.6- Community Awareness. The beneficiaries will be made aware of the inputs to be distributed and are trained on their use. Protection related awareness and training will also be done to the community including COVID-19 messaging and distribution of PPE to participants during each activity.7- Procurement and distribution of inputs. The supplies will be procured and supplied timely with adherence to the requirement procurement processes. 8-Monitoring. This step involves the post distribution monitoring, post planting and catchment and finally post harvest. 9-Technical Training. Group members including local chiefs and the County Agriculture Department. Demonstration plots and kitchen gardens will be established at designated areas close to the groups.10- Reports. Progress, Interim and Final Reports will be the main reports. The financial report will be through the FTS system.11- Evaluation. The project will be evaluated on quarterly basis to gather the lessons learnt and challenges to inform decision making. The post project evaluation will be done to assess impact.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Protection against sexual exploitation and abuse is a very crucial concern in planning humanitarian response. This is well captured as the program is developed on the principles of theory of change which involves bottom-top alignment of needs to inform programming.Precisely, the established feedback mechanism that involves filled complaints, toll free numbers and meetings will be the mechanisms for informing the beneficiaries about PSEA but most profoundly, field monitoring through identification of PSEA Community Focal Persons at the area level to report issues related to the act will be the effective channel. On notice of such, the established linkages/pathways through protection partners and the government authorities at the grassroot will all together be notified to have harmonious approach.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The principle of exclusivity and representation will be the mechanism through which feedback from the vulnerable members is ensured. This will involve; representative grassroot recruitment for junior positions i.e assistants, having a gender and disability responsive beneficiary selection criteria and involvement of the representatives of the local authority in all the activities that will be undertaken.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The community engagement strategy will involve the participation of the local authority at the county level, the chiefs and elders, formed committees( Community Representative Committees and AAP). The committees will include representatives from the different social groups and the vulnerable. Prior activities, mobilization and sensitization will be conducted to downscale the project plan at the grassroot and coordination pathways are established through the committee members.Committee/community meetings, filled letters of complaints, hotline numbers, complain desk committee members at distribution points and radio talk shows will be the main channels of the feedback mechanisms to be used.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiaries' feedback is an important AAP component in the project cycle. The beneficiaries' feedback will help to ensure that the adopted project design is safe and provides for dignified access to the assistance in proportion to their needs, priorities and preferences.
