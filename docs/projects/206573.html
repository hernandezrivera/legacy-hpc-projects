Project Summary

This project will focus on returnees from the Sudan crisis, IDPs, and resident communities in priority 1 counties (Rubkona and Leer) and priority 2 counties (Mayendit, Koch, Guit, and Panyijiar). As of November 12, 2023, UNHCR and IOM recorded 373,094 people crossing the border from Sudan into South Sudan. In the month of November alone, 19,314 people arrived, with an average of 1,609 people arriving daily (Humanitarian Updates Note, November 15, 2023). This situation is expected to worsen with the continued escalation of Crisis in Sudan.
A recent assessment conducted by CH in October 2023 in Rotriak further revealed that among the thousands fleeing the conflict in Sudan are South Sudanese nationals returning through Panakuach, Unity state. Most of these returnees have settled in Rotriak because many villages in Unity are still flooded. Other returnees lacked the means for onward movement to other parts of the country, forcing them to settle in Rotriak with more than 90% currently seeking shelter beneath trees, with only a few having been integrated into IDP communities. Access to food is utterly scarce in Rotriak. Both internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees depend solely on the limited humanitarian aid. There is no available arable land for farming, and the residents cannot afford to buy food from the market. This situation has exacerbated concerns about hunger and malnutrion in the settlement as well as in other last destinations for the returnees i.e., Koch, Mayendit, Leer, Guit and Panyijiar.
In line with HRP and Food security and livelihood cluster objectives, this project will focus on CO2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock where returnees, IDPs and resident communities will be supported to grow food and harvest fish to meet their immediate to mid-term food needs and sell surpluses. The affected population will also be supported through livestock vaccination and treatment. Through this project, Coalition for Humanity will target 10,000 HH (60,000 Individuals) to improve food security and livelihood across 5 counties. Segregated by gender.
Men = 10,000

Coalition for Humanity will implement the following activities integrating strong protection mainstreaming and prioritizing people with special needs including women and children.
1. Community mobilization and sensitization of households
2. Identification, registration verification and distribution of quality livelihood kits to targeted households.
3. Formation and training of lead farmer groups in every county on identification and reporting of early warning signs of pests, diseases and floods.
4. Training of Agricultural extension workers, per county on pest and crop disease control e.g. detection, prevention & eradication.
5. Provision of farm level support of farmers in every county on improved production/modern farming methods.
6. Providing farmers with emergency livelihoods support such as crop kits, fishing kits and fast maturing vegetable seeds
7. Training community animal health workers (CAHW) on livestock diseases, prevention & treatment especially quarantine measures on zoonotic diseases e.g. RVF, Anthrax, Hemorrhagic septicemia (HS), black quarter among others that are common in these counties.
8. Mobilization and sensitization of communities and identification of Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs)
9. Collection and distribution of Livestock Kits to the CAHWs and regular replenishment.
10. Conducting monitoring, surveillance and reporting of diseases in the target areas.

1. Needs

Floods and droughts are a part of life in South Sudan due to natural climatic variability. Over generations, people have developed strategies to address these hazards. However, there are indications that global warming is contributing to change in South Sudan’s climate and that these will only increase in the future, taking the form of extreme rainfall and flooding, gradual increases in temperature and changes to seasonal precipitation patterns and averages. According to research completed by SIPRI, “both long-term climate change and short-term extreme climate events have indirect and interlinked implications for peace and security in South Sudan”. The targeted population is particularly vulnerable to these climatic events due to their high dependency on primary sector activities such as farming, pastoralism, and fishing. Widespread violence and displacement have resulted in the destruction of infrastructure and markets, loss of livelihoods, severe mental health problems, limited access to essential services, GBV, and general disrespect for rule of law in all targeted states. Furthermore, agricultural activities are frequently disrupted, leading to food insecurity. In 2023, the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) shows a record high number of 7.8 million people in IPC 3 in the lean season. Displacement of communities has disrupted traditional farming practices and other livelihoods, leaving many without sustainable livelihood opportunities. Irrigation and other infrastructure have been damaged by conflict and markets are disrupted. The lack of storage facilities and post-harvest processing infrastructure also affect the quality and marketability of agricultural produce. This situation is expected to worsen with the continued escalation of Crisis in Sudan. As of November 12, 2023, UNHCR and IOM recorded 373,094 people crossing the border from Sudan into South Sudan. In the month of November alone, 19,314 people arrived, with an average of 1,609 people arriving daily (Humanitarian Updates Note, November 15, 2023). A recent assessment conducted by CH in October 2023 in Rotriak further revealed that among the thousands fleeing the conflict in Sudan are South Sudanese nationals returning through Panakuach, Unity state. Most of these returnees have settled in Rotriak because many villages in Unity are still flooded. Other returnees lacked the means for onward movement to other parts of the country, forcing them to settle in Rotriak with more than 90% currently seeking shelter beneath trees, with only a few having been integrated into IDP communities. There is no available arable land for farming, and the residents cannot afford to buy food from the market. This situation has exacerbated concerns about hunger and malnutrion in the settlement as well as in other last destinations for the returnees i.e., Koch, Mayendit, Leer, Guit and Panyijiar.

2. Project activities

Coalition for Humanity will implement the following activities integrating strong protection mainstreaming and prioritizing people with special needs including women and children.1. Community mobilization and sensitization of households2. Identification, registration verification and distribution of quality livelihood kits to targeted households.3. Formation and training of lead farmer groups in every county on identification and reporting of early warning signs of pests, diseases and floods.4. Training of Agricultural extension workers, per county on pest and crop disease control e.g. detection, prevention & eradication.5. Provision of farm level support of farmers in every county on improved production/modern farming methods.6. Providing farmers with emergency livelihoods support such as crop kits, fishing kits and fast maturing vegetable seeds7. Training community animal health workers (CAHW) on livestock diseases, prevention & treatment especially quarantine measures on zoonotic diseases e.g. RVF, Anthrax, Hemorrhagic septicemia (HS), black quarter among others that are common in these counties.8. Mobilization and sensitization of communities and identification of Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs)9. Collection and distribution of Livestock Kits to the CAHWs 10. Promotion of Local Seed Production & Multiplication: In this Action, vegetable and food producers will be supported to produce quality local seeds for vulnerable families, using a climate resilient livelihoods approach. Producer groups for seed production and multiplication for Open pollinated varieties (OPV) will be identified by the local community and Ministry of Agriculture and County Agriculture Department, and plots for multiplication will be made available. These farmer producer groups will receive the necessary input for the production of local seeds. The Action will use a mix of traditional and modern, locally adapted varieties and breeds to maintain output, increase yields and ensure stability in the face of climate change. Training will be given on multiplication techniques and usage of seedbanks, resource management, financial literacy, and small business management. The Action will focus on women and youths in seed production and multiplication, strengthening and ensuring equal participation in agriculture cooperatives and value chains. 11. Strengthen and support producer groups to graduate to agriculture cooperatives, and value chains: In addition to the activities with regards to seed production and multiplication explained in activity 10 this Action will support the mentioned farmer producer groups with capacity building on value chains, specifically on processing, storage, and marketing. Furthermore, interested producer groups will be supported to formalize and graduate to cooperatives, to be able to have access to financial services, in ensuring linkages to (local) markets and with improved or scaled-up climate smart agriculture.
