Project Summary

South Sudan is still facing significant challenges which cuts across the different World Health Organization (WHO) health systems building blocks specifically, service delivery, essential medicines, health workforce and financing. The country public health indicators are still high amidst other health challenges. The maternal mortality rate is 1,223/ 100,000 live births (World Bank Data 2024), under 5 mortality rate is 98.8 deaths/1,000 live births (UNICEF Data 2024) and 22.7% of children under 5 years of age are nutritional affected higher than the average for the Africa region of 6.0% (2022 Global Nutrition Report). Over 5.5 Million people will be needing health services in the year 2025, which also includes addressing multiple diseases outbreaks, new burden from returnees and refugees in the country.
Jonglei that includes Greater Pibor Administrative Area (Pibor County) and Northern Bahr el Ghazal State (Aweil East and West Counties) has a substantial number of inter-sectoral needs, despite Aweil West being classified as Priority 3 (UNOCHA), Pibor County and Aweil East Counties are amongst counties projected in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) in the lean season and equally classified as priority one (1). Aweil East and West counties are facing new burden of refugees, returnees and IDP’s the effect of on-going floods and disease outbreak. Pibor County faces Persistent conditions due to insecurity and experiences of cyclic conflict worsening situation. All the three counties have high rate of acute malnutrition (AM) as the Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) Prevalence based on weight-for-height Z-score (WH-Z score above WHO threshold (≥15%).
In Pibor County poor vaccination coverage in the past years contributed to outbreaks of vaccine-preventable diseases (VPD), particularly measles. The Aweil Counties, due to multiple vulnerability including unknown immunity of returnees, many communities remain at risk of disease outbreaks, including waterborne diseases worsened with poor access to safe water, sanitation, and recurrent flooding. In Pibor and Aweil (East and West) counties 40% of primary health care facilities are not functional, moreover, women and girls face barriers such as cultural norms, discriminatory practices that prevent them from enjoying their full sexual and reproductive health rights.
In 2025, ForAfrika will prioritize aligning its services with Health Sector Transformation Project (HSTP) collaboration with other stakeholders to ensure complementarity and coordinated response. ForAfrika will Provide essential health care assistance to 41,428 in terms of the age Sex, age, and disability disaggregated (SADDD) – 19,528 boys and girls below the age of 5 years; 7,214 children and adolescent (between 6 -17 years of ages will be reached this includes 15% PWD. In line with the Strategic Objective 1, 2 and 3 and anchored to health cluster sectoral objectives 1,2 and 3. Health education, risk communication during outbreaks and 12 community health events for behavioral change will be supported targeting individuals including People with Disabilities (PWD) and elderly. Support Supplementary Immunization excises (SIA), that includes measles and other VPD Campaigns.
Surveillance reporting (IDSR) and essential services will be provided through four (4) static Primary health care units (PHCU) and 2 mobile units. Through engagement of qualified staff, clinical Consultation services to 20,000 beneficiaries this includes 21% of children ≤ 5 years will access curative services including IDP’s, returnees within the target location. Plan is to support (10%) Pregnant and Breastfeeding women(PBW) of the target population; qualified mid-wife will be engaged to aid both ANC and PNC care, support abrupt deliveries. Community feedback will shape the design, informing interventions including, GBV and mental health (MHPSS) as per local needs, Staff will be trained on basic psychosocial first aid and use of existing referral pathway.

1. Needs

The economic challenges have hampered the availability of adequate government investment to build a stable and sustainable health service delivery infrastructure. The capacity to address health needs is hampered by availability and quality of the health workforce, infrastructure, medicines and medical supplies. Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA) health system gaps is worsened by frequent population displacement and interruption of services due to sub-national violence. Moreover, substantial number of individuals in extreme remote locations remain without adequate services as only 8 villages are supported by Boma Health Initiative (BHI) and Six (6) facilities supported through Health Sector Transformation Project (HSTP). A total 13 facilities, 61% are functional leaving a gap of 41% facilities non-functional in GPAA. According to Pibor County, ForAfrika - SMART survey Report (2024), the prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) by Weight-for-height Z scores (WHZ<-2 and edema) is 25.4% and the prevalence of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) is 7.5% all above WHO emergency threshold of ≤ 15% and ≤ 2% respectively. The same report indicates upwards trends of Crude and Under-five (U5) mortality rates of 1.0 and 0.36 deaths per 10,000 persons per day (2024) compared to 0.77 and 0.20 deaths per 10,000 persons per day respectively. Vaccine coverage is low at 54%, with high defaulter rate in the County, there is high susceptibility to vaccine-preventable diseases. Newborn Care (BEmONC) is only offered in 60% of health facilities, with essential laboratory diagnostic capacity. The services are minimal in Gumuruk and Lekuongole Payam which are 45km away from the main county hospital indicating the dire need for access to life-saving interventions, including major obstetric Care. Only 28% of mothers are reported to have made at least 4 ANC visits during pregnancy and the proportion of births attended by a skilled health worker is at 21% (DHIS-2 Data 2024). There are no Health Science Institutions in GPAA, notable high level of illiteracy and lack of adequately skilled human resources for health contribute to 60% of the CHD staff being untrained, and thus there is a need to ensure that the health workforce is trained and can deliver quality services. In Northern Bahr el Ghazal, the influx continues with refugees and returnees arriving through six entry points located in the Northern-West part of South Sudan sharing border with Sudan. Unfortunately, this is happening at a time when the state is already grappling with a large number of internally displaced persons (IDP) most of whom have fled violence, food insecurity and floods. The state has limited capacity to provide support to IDPs/refugees due to resource constraints. Consequently, humanitarian assistance in the state has severely been affected due to extreme constraints on the available resources and services being provided mostly by aid agencies. All the two counties (Aweil East and Aweil West) have high rate of acute malnutrition (AM) as the GAM Prevalence based on WH-Z score is 19% Aweil East; Aweil West has an increase from 11.3% to 21.3% all far above WHO threshold (≥15%). The situation is made even more critical due to floods and multiple disease outbreaks including Cholera. Host communities and immigrant population lost income opportunities, and health services lack proper medicine and equipment and only 60% are supported by HSTP. There is need to address challenges that are noted in medical vaccines and supplies commodities, starting from the high cost of last-mile delivery, lack of appropriate storage infrastructures inefficiency, and ineffective management by the untrained CHD personnel. The low health facility density and inadequate basic amenities, equipment, and diagnostic capacity constraint service delivery and all these needs to be addressed across the three mentioned counties.

2. Project activities

To address the needs and the mentioned challenges, ForAfrika will use integrated and participatory approach in implementation arrangements needed to support in strengthening the health system and delivering the BPHNS contributing towards national strategic objectives. ForAfrika shall undertake extensive consultations with local stakeholders and beneficiaries, including women’s and children of the local communities, promotes accountability and local ownership in the counties. In improve equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and SGBV health services to 41,428 crisis affected populations (SO1). Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases (SO2). Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms (SO3). Implementation will be guided by hosting country policy within the W.H.O pillars of health system and guidelines. The proposed response will work in coordination with MOH, W.H.O, UNICEF, UNFPA and CHD for technical guidance, access to medical supplies and commodities including (IEHK), Surveillance reporting and coordination through the cluster. In addition to reporting into DHIS 2 as well as reporting 5W; to ensure case fatality are maintained within WHO threshold during outbreaks; ForAfrika, will support alerts being verified with 24hrs through real time disease surveillance, timely submission of data in EWARS/IDSR, communication and reporting for coordinated response. The plan is to ensure the project is integrated to the nutrition, WASH, FSL intervention to reduce morbidities. ForAfrika will provide curative services to 18,980 individuals through treatment of communicable diseases and non- communicable diseases: capacity building of the local available staff. Provide – MCH (antenatal care, skill birth and postal natal) to the target 2790 women and ensure staff are trained on basic EmONC, provision of focused Ante- Natal Care and emergency obstetric care to pregnant women, using skilled personnel. Facilitate, referral for complicated delivery to skilled health care worker, this aimed at improving maternal health and reducing <5 morbidity. There will be provision of routine vaccination, and involvement is supplementary immunization (SIA) including targeting of 4256 boys and girls between 6 months – 15 years through reactive measles vaccines coverage. Additional ForAfrika will ensure last mile delivery to improve vaccination coverage for PENTA3 in <One-year-old targeting 1620. Plan is to coordinate with developmental partners to ensure integration and referral of eligible cases for tuberculosis and HIV/AID’s care and treatment including sero- reactive pregnant mothers and sero-exposed infants.ForAfrika is cognizant of health cluster response priority and aligning services with South Sudan National health policy 2016-2026 and 2023-2027 health sector strategic plan to delivery complementary services; thus in Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MPHSS) the plan is to undertake awareness to 10,877 individual treat 20 SGBV in needing of CMR and referrer eligible cases according to the referral pathway; in addition to the training of frontline health staff on the basic MPHSS and SGBV. Also, the project will ensure referral of SAM cases with Medical Complication to County Hospital Stabilization Centers. To Enhance resilience, community health events and monthly meetings will be conducted through facilitation of CHD; At least three supported facilities will have community feedback mechanism additionally annual community consultative meetings will be conducted to identify needs, participation in response, and reduce impact on their lives including persons with disability.
