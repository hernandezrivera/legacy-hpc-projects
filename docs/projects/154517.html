Project Summary

This project will target the most vulnerable IDPs and returnees identified by the shelter cluster in Kirkuk governorate with NFI and shelter assistance throughout 2019. HRF will procure adequate stocks of NFI/winterization kits and shelter/SOKs to cover the needs of in and out of camp IDPs and returnees across Kirkuk governorate, provisionally based upon target beneficiary numbers provided by the Shelter/NFI cluster.
HRF will build on existing relationships with vulnerable groups in Kirkuk which have been developed through existing HRF programmes in the governorate, this is particularly the case with in and out of camp IDPs in the three target districts, Daquq, Kirkuk city and Hawiga.

Needs

The Shelter/NFI cluster in Iraq has identified 25,821 vulnerable individuals (approximately 4,304 HHs) in need of NFI assistance. The largest need is amongst IDPs in Daquq and Kirkuk city, and returnees in Hawiga and Kirkuk city. The cluster has identified 30,774 IDPs and returnees (approximately 5,129 HHs) who will require urgent shelter assistance in 2019. The greatest need is amongst IDP populations in Daquq and Kirkuk city, and amongst returnees in Hawiga. Using these targets provided by the cluster, HRF intends to conduct in-depth needs assessments to select beneficiaries, using the socio-economic vulnerability assessment developed by the CWG.

Protection Risk Analysis

1. Sexual exploitation and abuse.- Women and/or girls are requested to exchange allotted kits for sexual favours, or other acts. 2. Safety problems at distributions points.- Harassment of women.- Separation of children.- Recruitment by armed groups. 3. Safety problems enroute to and from distribution points.- Attacks on beneficiaries.- Physical barriers or natural hazards.- Theft of rations. 4. Safety problems following distributions.- Retaliation attacks on recipient communities by armed groups or neighbouring communities.- Diversion of assistance to armed groups. 5. NFI/shelter kits cause injury or harm to beneficiaries - Faulty items in kits cause injury- Kits or some of the items within them unsuitable for beneficiary, for example the less-able or children.

Mitigation Measures

1. Sexual exploitation and abuse- Segregate men and women at distribution points either through use of a physical barrier or offering separate distribution times.- Inform all distribution team members about appropriate conduct, including training on HRF’s safeguarding policy. - Use of female ‘guardians’ to oversee distribution and post-distribution of items.- HRF’s in-country focal points to monitor reporting channels and act on any issues that arise. 2. Safety problems at distributions points.- Ensure crowd control measures are in place.- Consider separate waiting and queuing areas for unaccompanied men and women.- Establish meeting points in case of separation. 3. Safety problems enroute to and from distribution points.- Consider moving distribution points to other locations.- Consider establishing additional distribution points to increase proximity to communities and reduce travel time.- Consult with communities on safest transfer modality and consider adjusting accordingly.- Encourage people to travel in groups. 4. Safety problems following distributions.- Create awareness on targeting criteria with neighbouring communities. - Consider need for inclusion of vulnerable groups in neighbouring communities.- Create awareness in targeted communities and households on targeting criteria. - Involve communities – recipients and non-recipients – on defining vulnerability and targeting criteria.- Consult with men and women on identification of most appropriate household entitlement holders. 5. NFI/shelter kits cause injury or harm to beneficiaries - Procure high quality items in line with HRF’s procurement policy and procedures.- Conduct detailed needs assessments to ensure appropriateness of intervention; regularly liaise with beneficiary population to ensure continued appropriateness and to allow for any problems to be raised.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Protection sensitivity is embedded within all HRF programming, and HRF strives to include beneficiaries in all stages of the project cycle. Within this project, HRF will engage with the target communities in Kirkuk prior to distributions starting, to assess needs and explain the purpose and scope of the project. Focus group discussions (FGDs) and beneficiary interviews will be conducted periodically throughout the duration of the programme, in line with the M&E schedule, to monitor beneficiary satisfaction and to provide an opportunity for suggestions and concerns regarding the programme to be raised. Information will regularly be disseminated to recipients, and accessible formats will be used for people with different communication requirements. Clear signs in the local language with large font, contrasting colours and symbols or drawings will also be used. Information will include: - distribution plan e.g. time, date, location, frequency.- quantity and type of kit to be distributed, and the reasons for any deviation from the plan, if applicable.- channels for recipients to obtain more information on the programme and the process for complaints. Recipients will be consulted to create a culturally appropriate feedback and complaints mechanism. The mechanism will track the following aspects of the project and help to ensure accountability to recipients: - stakeholders’ preferences on distribution methods- information provided to beneficiaries on distribution (e.g. timeliness, accuracy)- receipt of items: actual versus planned (timeliness, quantity, quality, appropriateness)- safeguarding: alongside HRF’s organisational safeguarding reporting mechanism, this feedback mechanism will allow another channel for beneficiaries and staff to raise safeguarding concerns. HRF's MEAL team will conduct post-distribution monitoring (PDM) activities to help ensure beneficiary accountability and satisfaction, and allow for adjustments to be made to the programme where necessary and feasible. PDM methods will include through individual phone calls, and a selection of face to face FGDs. A statistically representative number of beneficiary households will be interviewed. Sample size will be calculated based on 90% confidence level, with a margin of error +/-10%. The results of PDM activities will be communicated within the wider humanitarian community. This will be completed within 6 weeks of the distribution.
