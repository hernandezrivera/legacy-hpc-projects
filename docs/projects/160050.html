Project Summary

To ensure that children are at the center of every intervention and that they are protected from harm, exploitation, violence and abuse, child protection interventions will be mainstreamed into all sectors as well as direct responses to the child protection concerns by the child protection team. The project will benefit 933,282 beneficiaries across Somalia through various child protection interventions. These will include 30,112 girls, 28,152 boys; 15,636 men and 19,383 women from IDP camps, returnee and host communities. Key interventions will include strengthening community based child protection structures to raise awareness, prevent, respond and refer cases of violence against children, providing case management support and referral services to children with protection concerns including SGBV, providing FTR services to UASC and alternative care arrangements. In addition families under stress will receive support through our parenting without violence programme to prevent physical and humiliating punishment in the home. Trainings on child protection, SGBV and psychological first aid (PFA) will be delivered to different stakeholders and partners, including sector staff (Health, Education, Nutrition, WASH, Livelihoods) to ensure mainstreaming of CP and improved coordination of services. Efforts will be made to ensure equal participation of minority groups like excluded clans, women, girls and as well as people and children with disabilities.

Mitigation Measures

In order to mitigate the above identified risks Save the Children will undertake the following measures (most of which address more than one risk);1. Empowering girls and boys - Children play a key role in protecting themselves and each other, therefore Save the Children will empower girls and boys to protect themselves according to their specific and unique needs. This will be achieved through participatory methods to facilitate discuss protection issues and plan their own actions to improve their situation and influence adults around them.2. Community mobilization: Like families, communities are often the first line of protection for children, but also a source of harm, so we always engage and mobilize community actors in our child protection programs. Community mobilization is a capacity building process through which local individuals, groups or organizations identify needs, plan, carry out and evaluate activities on a participatory and sustained basis, so as to improve the protection of children. In community mobilization, we support women’s groups, establish community child protection focal points or groups or committees (CWCs), facilitate community dialogues and conversations around child protection issues, support child rights clubs, youth groups and facilitate mass media campaigns such as radio programs and messaging.3. Save the Children will develop a beneficiary selection criterion in close collaboration with local leaders and community representatives to ensure transparency and selection of beneficiaries will also be supervised by SC staff.4. Save the Children will apply effective data protection measures to ensure that beneficiary data is kept confidential and safe from unintended use5. Survivors of gender based violence will be facilitated to seek assistance in a confidential and dignified manner that does not expose them to further trauma6. All SC staff are trained in Child Safeguarding and Sexual harassment in addition to signing a legally binding code of conduct as part of their employment contract. In addition, all SC service providers are screened and sensitized on the same.
