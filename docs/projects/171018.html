Project Summary

Action Against Hunger (AAH) and the Rural Center for Sustainable Development (RCSD)’s proposed project in 2021 will target the most vulnerable families in Area C (including Bethlehem, Hebron, Jerusalem, Jericho, Ramallah and Tubas governorates) and in Hebron’s H2 area. These groups suffer from no, or limited, access to water and sanitation services due to Israeli restrictions on construction and development in the area. This makes them particularly vulnerable to the threat of forced displacement. Further, the COVID-19 outbreak and its associated restrictions, compounded by the growing Palestinian Authority (PA) budget deficit and suspension of salaries for government employees, have added additional challenges to these communities to meet hygiene standards and prevent the spread of the virus. The 54 target communities, the majority of which are farming and herding families dependent on livestock and agricultural activities to maintain their livelihoods, heavily rely on water availability, quality and affordability. The lack of water has forced families to adopt negative coping mechanisms such as reducing their consumption to an average of less than 35 l/d/c (Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 2021), depending on unsafe water resources or reducing their expenditure on basic needs such as health and education to afford the high cost of water.
In line with Strategic Objectives 1, 2 and 3, the project aims to:
1. Decrease target communities’ vulnerability and dependence on trucked water or unsafe sources of water by constructing and rehabilitating water networks, filling points and household cisterns.
2. Improve communities’ access to sanitation facilities through the construction and provision of household WASH facilities such as latrines and plastic tanks. Families often disregard these facilities and prioritize other basic needs such as food and water. This negative coping mechanism has left many households in poor health, hygiene, privacy and dignity conditions, which are contributing to household members’ vulnerability to face COVID-19.
3. Mitigate the risk of flooding during winter through the supply and installation of storm water systems for areas in serious needs. This will prevent target communities to experience further assets losses, displacement and disrupted access to livelihoods and services.
4. Increase hygiene awareness through trainings and workshops on subjects ranging from hygiene promotion to water saving techniques and cistern management, at both household and community levels.
All these activities will mitigate the effects of the Israeli occupation on water access and thus improve the target communities’ livelihood prospects. The project will reduce the exposure to forced displacement of an estimated 45,955 people (11,343 men, 10,967 women, 11,265 boys, 12,089 girls, and 291 PWD) and provide them with the means to show resilience in the face of these challenges, exacerbated by the pandemic.
The proposed project is guided by AAH Gender and Protection policy and AAH staff endeavor to take into account safety and dignity in the design and implementation of all humanitarian interventions. Accountability and participation will be ensured through a detailed Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) framework specifically developed to ensure that beneficiary selection is conducted in a transparent and accountable manner and through applying AAH’s thorough Feedback and Complaint Mechanism to guarantee the inclusion of communities’ suggestions into the project. AAH seeks to achieve equal and impartial access to vulnerable populations, aiming for all community members to be involved and consulted as much as possible including through community consultations and focus groups to further achieve beneficiaries’ empowerment and sustainability beyond the project’s lifetime.

1 - Needs

According to the 2021 HNO, nearly 159,000 people across the West Bank reside in areas that are either not connected to piped water services or are very poorly supplied. Area C communities, particularly Southern Hebron, East Jerusalem periphery and the Jordan Valley, are particularly affected by this issue. Approximately 300,000 Palestinians in Area C are directly affected by the Israeli restrictions and control over WASH-related infrastructure and water sources. Assessments completed by AAH in April 2019, in Area C, indicate that water scarcity is one of the main factors leading vulnerable families to poverty and food insecurity, as well as further deteriorating living conditions and hygiene practices. With the spread of the COVID-19 since the spring 2020, both communities and service providers have been put in a difficult position considering their limited means to obtain necessary water and hygiene material to practice and enforce preventive measures as shown in the two assessments led by AAH in May 2020. Almost all (99%) the community leaders interviewed in Area C reported their communities not to have the means to purchase hygiene items. Additionally, most Bedouin and herding communities in Jericho, Ramallah and the East Jerusalem periphery receive water through old water pipes laid over-ground. Irregular water supply, the small size of old water pipes, high water losses and the destruction of water pipes by settlers and the ICA (e.g. in the R317 communities of South Hebron) restrict access to safe, sufficient and affordable water. Data from AAH’s 2017 Area C Water Master Plan and Water Distribution Modelling (updated in April 2019), validated by the Palestinian Water Authority (PWA), highlighted that most of the targeted communities consume less than 50 l/c/d and use rain harvesting cisterns and trucked water as coping mechanisms for the water shortage. Thus, adequate water storage capacity is vital for vulnerable families to harvest rainwater in winter and store trucked water during dry months and AAH has finalized early 2020, a Rainwater Harvesting Master Plan to identify locations suitable for rainwater harvesting at community level in the South of the West Bank.AAH, through the West Bank Protection Consortium, uses a community-based approach to assess on a continuous basis than 70 communities in the West Bank. It established that many families in Area C lack latrines and do not have sufficient water storage capacity (< 5m3). Most of them must buy water using small tanks (3.7m3 tractors) instead of large tanks (10m3) with prices up to 30 NIS/m3 as the trucked water cost is affected by road conditions, tank capacity, distance and fill up time. High water prices and limited income force families to reduce their water consumption to as little as 30-50 l/c/d. Women, many of whom are widowed, disabled or divorced, must borrow water from relatives and neighbours, which is then stored in small containers or kitchen pots. According to AAH’s sex and age disaggregated data and gender analysis in August-September 2019, men and women have distinct tasks: men provide payments for water truck services, while women are responsible for the storage and use of water. AAH and educational directorates conduct regular assessments and coordination meetings on WASH facilities in schools in Hebron, Bethlehem, East Jerusalem, Tubas, etc. including as part of WASH in school Task Force lead by the MoE and AAH at the start of the COVID-19 outbreak in the Spring 2020. These meetings, field visits and interviews with women, men, students and teachers indicated that many schools are not connected to a regular water supply and/or have poor WASH facilities. Substandard sanitation facilities in schools increase children’s exposure to health risks such as the COVID-19, unhealthy social environment and the school dropout rate among girls, specifically because of privacy issues.

2 - Activities

The project will achieve its objectives through:(1) The rehabilitation and construction of filling points and simple water networks and water storage capacity for vulnerable communities including Bedouins and herders in Southern Hebron, Bethlehem, Ramallah, the Jordan Valley, Tubas and East Jerusalem periphery. AAH will rehabilitate and/or install 32 simple water networks, provide 107 polyethylene tanks (of 1.5m³ capacity), and rehabilitate 18 wheeled water tanks. (2) The construction and rehabilitation of household cisterns in Area C and seam zones. According to the Area C Water Master Plan endorsed by PWA, 21 household cisterns will be constructed in South Hebron for communities that cannot be connected to a piped water service. Based on a socio-economic baseline survey, female headed households and families with members with disabilities will be prioritized. The activity is cash based, and will be implemented directly by the households when feasible to contribute to dignified assistance, reduce the risks of material getting controlled by the Israeli and increase time and cost efficiency. (3) The construction/rehabilitation of WASH facilities (handwashing, latrines, sewer systems) for 5 schools in Area C. This will improve student’s access to adequate, safe and healthy WASH facilities, crucial in times of COVID-19 and reopening of the schools, and will also improve their general educational environment, thus upholding the children’s right to education. (4) The provision of access to sanitation and hygiene for communities in Area C: This activity will be composed of the following two components: 1) Rehabilitation of 170 vulnerable household sanitation facilities in 4 governorates (Hebron, Jericho, Ramallah, and Tubas) covering 10 communities in Area C. This activity is a cash-based one, and shall be implemented by the beneficiaries under the direct supervision of AAH & RCSD field staff. 2) The provision of 600 hygiene kits while conducting hygiene promotion awareness campaigns. AAH shall provide these kits to all 5 schools targeted in this project, as well as 16 of the most vulnerable communities, including the majority of the communities targeted under component 1 of this activity. (5) The construction/rehabilitation of stormwater network/rainwater drainage systems: Rehabilitation of storm water network/rainwater drainage systems in H2 area (1 community), suffering from a lack of efficient stormwater drainage systems. (6) The development of WASH services operational and management plans and analysis: Via the implementation of a hydraulic map and conducting technical capacity building training at As-Samu in Hebron governorate. This plan will serve in designing WASH interventions in the area, contributing to provide equal and impartial access to all. AAH will coordinate with the PWA, Ministry of Education (MoE), UNICEF and other organisations (GVC, CISP and PAH) to avoid gaps and overlap. Stakeholders and partners in the targeted locations (municipalities, village councils and RCSD), who have been involved in the design of the project, will support the collection of baseline and end line data to ensure gender needs and priorities are taken into account. Together with AAH, and based on their extensive experience in implementing proposed activities, RCSD will be responsible for monitoring project’s implementation, for the collection of feedback from project beneficiaries and handover. RCSD staff will continue to be trained on designing and monitoring WASH activities in sensitive areas, respecting the principle of Do No Harm, following AAH policies and including PSEA considerations.

3 - Indicators

By improving access to basic WASH services to sustain livelihoods and avoid/respond to displacement, support the WASH advocacy strategy and improve coordination platforms, the proposed interventions will contribute to improve the safety and dignity of women, men, girls and boys, and the overall protection of the targeted communities.Expected results include:1. Improved water quality for vulnerable families: construction and rehabilitation of water networks will decrease the dependency on trucked water and will deter families from sourcing unsafe water. Catchment area improvement, chlorination of cisterns, hygiene promotion campaigns on hygiene practices and cistern management will encourage behavior changes and contribute to a reduction in waterborne diseases.2. Increased availability of water supply. Men and women will participate in the planning, design and implementation of the activities. Families will reduce their expenditure on trucked water, consequently they will have additional disposable income for other basic needs. The family member responsible for fetching water will have more time to fulfil other household activities.3. Improved awareness, knowledge and hygiene practices: Hygiene promotion activities and awareness raising will be provided at household level, with a special focus on women for topics regarding domestic consumption management, as per the previously conducted gender analysis of household roles and responsibilities. The core hygiene promotion messages will be hand washing, efficient use of water, domestic hygiene, cleaning and maintenance of WASH facilities and water storage tanks, especially relevant in times of COVID-19.4. Strengthened capacity and resilience of vulnerable communities to respond to adverse environmental conditions resulting from natural disasters, as well as respond to the pandemic. The distribution of water, rehabilitation of sanitation facilities, and non-food items (plastic tanks) will improve and strengthen the capacity of vulnerable communities to withstand adverse environmental conditions.5. Improved information management, coordination and dissemination of information among the cluster members and other stakeholders: the use of GIS and statistical software in data collection will facilitate data comparison and analysis; ad hoc databases will be set up to collect and compare information collected during the project implementation; complemented by AAH’s data management system. Elaborated data will be shared with relevant stakeholders and made available through regular reporting. KAP surveys and general project monitoring and evaluation will be carried out throughout the entire project in coordination with other agencies operating in the area; AAH will contribute to exchange of information/lessons learned, fact sheets and stories among the cluster community and to be used for advocacy purposes. Expected indicators and targets for the project are: • Water expenditure decreased by 30% • Water consumption increased by 30% • Interruptions of water supply in the network decreased by 30% • Water loss in the network decreased by 30% • Number of small expensive water deliveries (with tractors) decreased by 60% • Water storage capacity for 128 families with be improved through the construction/rehabilitation of water cisterns and the provision of PE tanks • WASH facilities in 5 schools improved through construction/rehabilitation of handwashing, latrines, sewer system • Living conditions for 293 women, 305 men, 287 girls and 310 boys are improved through the rehabilitation of household sanitation facilities • 34 communities improved their access to water through the construction/ rehabilitation of water pipelines, as well as the rehabilitation of water wheeled tanks • 1 community improved mitigation and prevention of floods • 600 hygiene kits provided to 5 schools and 16 communities.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

AAH considers safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability as well as participation and empowerment of paramount importance, specifically for projects implemented at household and school levels. AAH has been working on its own sector specific gender and protection mainstreaming checklists. Up to date, AAH has made use of cluster specific Protection Mainstreaming Tip Sheets. Overall AAH’s aim is to take decisive humanitarian action with attention to risk mitigation thereby avoiding doing harm to the greatest extent possible. AAH in oPt prioritises this through context specific (risk) analyses as well as the protection coordinator position stirring questions and discussion. AAH and RCSD (and community representatives, legal organizations and local authorities) will conduct a thorough risk analysis for proposed activities in each targeted location to identify potential risks and propose mitigation measures, including: 1- Community legal status: to assess if there are military orders or other legal concerns, and to ensure that the proposed interventions will not trigger the community legal case in a harmful way. 2- Community location related risks: to assess the community surrounding risks, like the existence of settlements, military base, firing zones and bypass roads, and to ensure that the proposed interventions will be applicable and operational in such context, not exposing beneficiaries to further risks. 3- Relations and sensitivity inside and outside the community: to assess the different cultural and social relations inside the community and in its surrounding, to ensure that the proposed interventions will not increase the social sensitivity or tension between the community, households, and with the surrounding communities. 4- Type of activity: to assess the community capacities and needs to follow up on activities such as the communities’ ability to operate and maintain the water networks which can contribute to the sustainability of the delivered assistance. In addition to doing no harm, the participatory approach contributes to the safety and dignity of beneficiaries. In order to further inform its assessments and implementation, AAH continuously works on understanding gender roles and household dynamics. By doing so, AAH believes that the organisation mitigates the risks of doing harm. In 2019, AAH conducted a masculinity study in West Bank, focused on identities/roles of men. AAH has in place mechanisms to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse by all staff. All AAH staff have the obligation to adhere to the organisation code of conduct. AAH also has in place gender focal points at base and mission levels, as well as safe and confidential ways to report abuse. Moreover, in spring 2019, all staff received specific in person training on the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Even more, most of AAH staff have gone through protection cluster facilitated trainings with the aim to know how to detect and refer potential cases of GBV. The indicator “% of communities/ project beneficiaries reporting that humanitarian assistance is delivered in a safe and dignified manner” will be measured through an end line survey of the beneficiaries. AAH will ensure that its activities, especially connecting communities with the pipe water service, will not cause overcrowding of the limited water sources and will not cause competition between the communities regarding water supplies, through the coordination with PWA and water service providers.Moreover, and in cooperation with community representatives, AAH will ensure that the project fully covers the water and sanitation needs of communities and households (each household has the right to have 1 latrine, water connection and 1 water tank), avoiding more tensions between the community families and other surrounding communities.Safety of the students of targeted schools will be improved, through increased adequate access to water and sanitation facilities.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In 2019, AAH has conducted in its Middle East country offices a Protection Mainstreaming Internal Evaluation for which the consultant spent the month of March in oPt. Particular attention has been paid to the way AAH includes vulnerable groups in its work, for instance through participatory assessments. As one of AAH’s core values, AAH thrives to prevent discrimination and to ensure equal and impartial access though transparency and accountability to affected population. AAH aims for all community members to be involved and consulted as much as possible, including with households, community consultations and focus group discussions accessible to all. In order for their participation to be meaningful, AAH focuses on ‘qualitative inclusion meaning safe spaces for beneficiaries to participate. Also, AAH started engaging with Humanity & Inclusion as inclusion experts to sensitise and inform AAH’s programming. For instance, instead of asking directly if people have a disability, AAH is starting to use increasingly ‘The Washington Group Short Set of Questions’ to detect people with disabilities. AAH is reporting under the HRP disaggregated data by sex and age, though pending the reporting format AAH usually includes further disaggregation on vulnerability such as data on people with disabilities. In addition, AAH started in its assessments to consider ‘barriers’ of particularly vulnerable groups and ‘enabling factors’ for people with specific needs.AAH has a safe and accessible beneficiary feedback mechanism in place which allows beneficiaries to provide their inputs, complaints, and satisfaction regarding the activities implemented. It is designed to increase their protection and complies with sensitive data management guidelines to uphold the principle of ‘Do No Harm’. Beneficiary knowledge of this system will be promoted through several field assessments and monitoring visits in which staff explains how the mechanism works. In addition, a paragraph will be included in each agreement with beneficiaries explaining the steps to report any complaint through the AAH feedback and complaint mechanism which is accessible by phone or email according to AAH protocols. AAH will process and treat all information collected with ad-hoc committee composed of project staff to understand the situation, identify appropriate solutions and include technical adjustment or modifications if needed. A monthly meeting will be conducted at community level to update beneficiaries and community leaders relating to project development and challenges. All formally received complaints and feedback are registered by the MEAL unit, considered for further investigation and adequately addressed. Additionally, the AAH anti-corruption and Anti-Fraud Policy and PSEA guidelines are implemented. With regards to access in this specific project, AAH tries to achieve equal and impartial access through for MEAL measures. AAH’s MEAL unit uses results-based monitoring tools in accordance with AAH’s M&E guidelines. A detailed MEAL framework will be developed to ensure that beneficiary selection is conducted in a transparent and accountable manner and prioritizes vulnerable families and those who care for members with disabilities. For this project AAH will use its own criteria for WASH interventions to measure each household vulnerability regarding water and sanitation services. The response will therefore be designed cover each household vulnerability using the same standards, extracted from the WASH cluster minimum standards and HRP prioritization. M&E measures will be participatory, both developed and conducted collaboratively with key stakeholders, target groups and beneficiaries, and with emerging findings and results shared transparently throughout the project. The aim is to ensure equal participation of all, sustainability and follow-up beyond the project’s lifetime.
