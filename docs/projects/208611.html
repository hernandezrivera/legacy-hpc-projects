Project Summary

Contribution of the program to the WFP core mandate (saving lives, assets preservation, gender)
• The project will help conserve the environment particularly the soils based on the climate smart agriculture practices promoting increased organic matter in the soil that acts as a carbon sink.
• The planting of trees as part of agro-forestry acts as a carbon sink and the tree roots help bind the soil reducing soil erosion.
• Decreasing food waste through improved post-harvest handling, increases access to food, reducing malnutrition, and increases incomes to the households, promoting household resilience.

2 - Activities

Emphasis on the development of the targeted value chains will be switched from finding new markets to improving the efficiency of the value chain actors and creating jobs for the women and youths. 1. Production is only one of a number of value-adding links; import, supply, fiscal, transport and export policies and business support services must be aligned to support sector performance. 2. Lobby government to invest in upgrading storage facilities, warehouses and drying facilities3. Train women and youth in financial literacy and relationship management with market actors4. Develop and disseminate market information through radio, SMS services and mobile applications to inform the women and youth decision making5. Collaborate with private sector and financial services to enable women and youth to access affordable credit and insurance products6. Promote inclusive business strategies to address gender disparities to access to resources by women and girls and involving them in decision making as producers and processors of the sorghum and millet 7. Upgrading the performance of individual farmers and enterprises along the value chains may have little impact if the ‘bigger picture’ is not taken into account through an advocacy strategy that facilitates performance for the entire sector coordinated by the multi-stakeholder platforms addressing the following:• Issues of the production context and status, technology and product development, • Market access, • Agro-processing potential, • Organization and management, • Regulatory policy, • Finance, • Input supply, • Infrastructure, • Relationships of the different actors / stakeholders, • Potential partnerships, The women and youth to be targeted for receiving horticulture inputs during the dry season will be selected by the community based on agreed vulnerability criteria, access to land and water, motivated and willing to teach others especially youths in vegetable production and marketing, willing to attend training and dedicated to agriculture and have time to manage the crops, and previous experience could be an added advantage. Complementary interventions could include linking the horticulture producers to the beneficiaries of cash- based transfers under the WFP program in the same localities and training the women and youth on aspects of home-based horticulture processing and packaging as a value addition process to increase incomes.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

GENDER AND PROTECTION MAINSTREAMINGIn the target areas women and girls are most vulnerable as violence and cultural orientation expose them to child marriage, sexual abuse, illiteracy, emotional/physical violence, and exploitation while boys are most at risk of recruitment by AOGs. COOPI will consider issues of gender norms and practices and make sure that gender roles and responsibilities do not discriminate against people or act as a barrier to accessing services. COOPI will map out safe and unsafe areas and identify the underlying causes of gender discrimination in the communities. It will work in partnership with government institutions and the community to comprehend the impact and provide solutions to reduce gender discrimination and improve participation. COOPI will ensure that gender is considered on a needs basis in all interventions, and men and women are equally represented in community structures as well as facilitators working with children. COOPI will strengthen protection mainstreaming by promoting safety, meaningful access, dignity, and ensuring the well-being of conflict-affected populations, including people with disabilities, across all its activities. COOPI will mainstream safeguarding in all program areas and processes such as ensuring that implementation approaches do not further expose beneficiaries to harm such as family separation, worsening beneficiaries’ situations, crisis/conflicts in the community, procurement of inferior materials etc. Potential red-flag areas the COOPI pay particular attention to include; FDPs selection in an unsecured location, activities depriving children and women from going to school, exposing beneficiaries to more vulnerable situations, setting criteria that support family separation, engaging unskilled personnel, unguided data information sharing, bridging of data protection protocols, exposing GBV survivors to further harm, causing stigma and discrimination among community members. To prevent such scenarios, protection will be mainstreamed in all activities and outcome areas to ensure that all activities meet the needs of beneficiaries, and ensure services are rendered in a safe and dignified manner that does not expose beneficiaries to further harm. A help and feedback desk and complaint boxes will also be set up at each distribution site for the people that would like to remain anonymous

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

COMPLAIN AND FEEDBACKIn addition, COOPI will capitalize on the existing accountability units within the MEAL department to ensure that participation, feedback, and complaints mechanisms are integrated in all strategies, plans and program and provides a range of rapid and accessible channels of communication with affected populations. These channels will be used to inform the affected population and project participants on procedures and processes so they can make informed decisions and choices. The components of accountability to affected populations are intrinsically linked and built on each other in COOPI’s mission accountability systems and would be adequately considered throughout the implementation of the proposed project. In addition to the WFP’s toll-free line, COOPI will build on the findings from previous humanitarian accountability assessments conducted in the target locations to determine communities’ preference on the type of feedback mechanism to put in place, prior to the set-up/strengthening of the mechanisms for the project. These feedback/complaints whether negative or positive will be timely addressed in a confidential manner and will inform corrective measures in the project. This is done through dedicated Gender and Protection/CFM Assistants whose responsibility will be collecting and responding to complaints and feedback. COOPI would ensure that its staff adhere to the principles of “Do No Harm” and as such will periodically evaluate actions of the project (including staff and community members) to ensure that harm is not coming to any of the beneficiaries as a result of the project implementation. Where any harm may be identified, it will be documented for accountability and quickly addressed
