Project Summary

The aim of this project is to respond to emergency shelter needs of vulnerable households, mainly in Area C of the Jordan Valley, with provision of timely and adequate shelter and NFI solutions through a comprehensive integrated approach that supports building resilience, reinforcing protection, and supporting the steadfastness of the most poor and vulnerable households at risk of displacement or exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns.
The intervention will addresses immediate needs, as well as the underlying conditions that give rise to those needs through a multi- pronged approach that includes; (i) Establishment of community based protection committees and provision of tailored capacity building to empower affected people to cope with sudden and protracted shocks and stresses, increase accountability, information dissemination and transparency specifically to report protection concerns, disseminate DRP practices with the inclusion of training on SPHERE standards and monitoring of intervention. (ii-a) provision of durable climate protection shelter materials and technical rehabilitation support and oversight to the most vulnerable households whom are exposed to harsh weather or protection concerns or at risk of displacement. (ii-b) Prioritized Shelter rehabilitation with full technical support and oversight for Persons with disabilities and widowed, separated or abandoned female headed households through MA’ANs local technical team. (iii) Stockpiling and prepositioning of adequate shelter/NFIs stocks, and protective shelter material for HHs under risk of displacement, , and temporary displacement due to military training closures, and/ or at risk of exposure to natural or manmade hazards or settler violence and increase the capacity to respond to natural and manmade hazards in a timely, efficient and effective manner.
The intervention is built on MA’AN and ACTEDs Community Protection and emergency response approaches, & previous shelter rehabilitation experience in the area, placing emphasis on the themes of (1) Engagement at the community level with a focus on community ownership and responsiveness (2) Community Driven Resilience focusing on Protection mechanisms for vulnerable groups in communities. To ensure effectiveness of intervention, targeted communities and neighborhoods have been coordinated with the shelter cluster, other partner organizations and actors working in the area to avoid overlap of assistance and ensure best reach in emergencies. MA'AN has prior experience and good understanding about the current legal situation for the construction in Area C and employs strategies in employing a low profile including: Rehabilitating structures from the inside of shacks and tents, Carrying out work at night, Staggering assistance over a prolonged period of time, coordinating with households and key communities members to stay updated with IOF activity in the area. Moreover, As the communities are subject to ongoing threats, and harassment, Project Management will also supervise the promotion and public announcement of the project among the inhabitants of the targeted communities to ensure an update of priority households in the database at the project start. Women in these communities will play an integral part in the selection of beneficiaries as part of the established CBPC , other members include village council heads and representatives, Mokhtar ( village head), and community members. Project Management unit will ensure that CBPCs and selected households are well informed about their rights, obligations and potential risks involved regarding housing, land and property issues and provide their consent to all proposed project activities.

1 - Needs

The approximate 300k Palestinians living in the WBs ‘Area C’ are especially vulnerable as a result of Israeli occupation policies. The dire living conditions of these communities makes them at risk of forced displacement due to the lack of basic necessities for dignified humane living conditions. This is heightened by the new 1797 military order allowing for demolition of unlicenned structures within 96 hrs. The 2018-2020 HRP revealed that there are 13,000 demolition orders in Area C of the WB affecting 15,000 ppl , and over 10,000 people live in 63 communities in Area C of the WB at heightened risk of forcible transfer. The 2020 HNO has revealed a 122% increase in demolitions of residential shelters in 2019 with over 3450 shelters in need of rehabilitation from 8 area C communities alone. Based on coordination with the Substandard Shelter assessment in Area C by Shelter Cluster and partners and on in house data on communities, MA’AN and ACTED have pre identified in 2019 , 26 Area C communities (JV, Tubas, Ramallah) in need of shelter rehabilitation, presenting medium to high shelter vulnerability and in need of structures improvement, subject to settler harassment, and at risk of forced displacement. These 26 communities were selected after a rapid community sample assessment in Apr & Sep 2018 and updated in Aug and Sep 2019, and are considered highly vulnerable in shelter under OCHA’s Vulnerability Profile Project. Further in 2019, ACTED in coordination with the Tubas governorate has identified 114 HHs from 6 of these communities whom suffer from temporary shelter displacement due to military closures in firing zones, which occur approx. 6 times/yr and per community. The HHs are warned to evacuate max 2 days in advance by order. Sometimes they only have 12 hrs to evacuate. In such cases, most HHs do not have anywhere to go and sleep either in their pick-ups, after covering w/ PVC, or resort to sleeping in the bare. During the winter they go to relatives’ houses in surrounding communities. The impact of these conditions further disproportionately affect marginalized groups within these communities such as PWD, the elderly, and female headed. Dominant types of shelter are make shift shelters in the form of a shack, tent, and substandard brick or mud structures and were often overcrowded with insufficient space for gender privacy and safety. The structures suffer from water infiltration, moisture damaged wall plaster, improper ventilation, collapsing and damaged ceilings and roofs and make shift walls providing no insulation A significant # of the Bedouin communities houses have no cement flooring, this is coupled with poor utilities and inadequate sanitary units. Furthermore, a number of the HHs in these communities have experienced contained harassment(settler intimidation, demolition of livelihood & residential infrastructure, and demolition orders). The assessment further confirmed lessons from both partners previous experience in the sector concerning the impact of substandard shelter on vulnerable HH members. Particularly, the lack of appropriate WASH structures in homes pose a burden for managing daily household tasks for women. In HH with multiple wives, women suffer from lack of privacy comfort & lack of decent living spaces. Large HH size and small shelter also poses a challenge for the privacy and safety of boys and girls as in some cases over 9 children occupied only one room. Female headed HHs, the elderly and disabled have less capacity to respond to emergencies and ensuring protection of their children due to limited access to resources and restricted mobility and human resources to upgrade their shelters. These groups are in dire need of comprehensive support with shelter upgrade to ensuring adequate dignified climate protection, safety&Security.Timely access to health services in cases of emergencies is limited, distribution of protective assets including first aid kits,fire extinguishers are a priority.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Addressing the needs of full community clusters and involving community members as leaders throughout the project’s implementation, the proposed intervention seeks to minimize implementation risk and enforce protection standards for the “do no harm” policy. Community leaders will be involved throughout every phase of project implementation. The security situation is monitored closely on an ongoing basis by MAAN and ACTED internal security rules to ensure timely advice and relevant security precautions can be implemented. Further MA’ANs local coordinators and continoues presence in the communities along with the CBPC members will report regularly on any risks to safety and security during implementation to mitigate time and effectiveness impact. When needed, MA’AN has depended security and safety approaches to infrastructure work in Area C where. To ensure safety and security of HH’s and beneficiaries during implementation and in line with the “Do No Harm” principles, MA’AN ensures that infrastructure work in all its forms in Area C do not affect the arial view of shelter dimensions in the community to reduce chances of future demolition orders. Further, during provision of Materials, MA’AN ensures all material provided are hidden under existing structures to avoid confiscation. When needed, provision and rehabilitation work is done during night hours as a response to security and financial threats in the target Areas. Further, ACTED’s Security Advisor works closely with UNDSS and attends monthly security briefings and alerts in a timely manner of any security incidents or threats. As member in the WBPC, ACTED will serve to mobalize partnerships in identification of risks and their responses.- The communities targeted by the project are very risky locations (surrounded by settlements or under ICA threat of demolition), therefore MA’AN and ACTED will ensure that the assistance is provided in times where control is limited (at night, during week-ends) in coordinated times with the community committee and beneficiary to assess the security situation of the community. -MA’AN mobializes/employs local coordinators for its projects in Area C to mitigate access risk in locations where security incidents are likely to occur. And when possible in alignment with its procurement policy and guidelines procures from local sources. - MA’AN and ACTED have been working for many years in the Jordan Valley, including for shelter projects, and have a good relationship with the communities, so they will be made aware of any problem with ICA or IDF in the communities in real time.- ACTED is a focal point for demolition response and is aware ahead of time of any demolition incident or security incident in the West Bank, so will use this to ensure access to communities.Aligning with the “Do No Harm” Principle, the implementation team will ensure all planned rehabilitation work by the beneficiaries through Self-rehabilitation and project is completed with project closeout. In instances where HHs are unable to effectively conduct the shelter rehabilitation, MA’AN and ACTED technical teams will directly supervise the rehabilitation work for disadvantaged households (female headed households , persons with disabilities …etc).

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The criteria will focus on the socio economic conditions of the households as well as on the housing conditions and the gender make up of the household. As women are responsible for the household in Palestinian society, the number of females in the household will also be an important part of the application process. women headed households, seniors living alone, and PWDs are disproportionately affected by poor housing conditions, such as those in the selected villages thus these groups will be a prioritized during the selection of beneficiaries. Further, the implementation team will ensure all planned rehabilitation work by the beneficiaries through Self-rehabilitation and project is completed with project closeout. In instances where HHs are unable to effectively conduct the shelter rehabilitation, MA’AN and ACTED technical teams will directly implement the rehabilitation work for disadvantaged households (female headed households , persons with disabilities …etc).Moreover, protective assets will be distributed to HHs meetings this criteria based on the rapid technical assessment of the communities. Shelter rehabilitation/ upgrade and winterization will be conducted for these groups by the projects technical teams including provision of material, and conducting the rehabilitation work instead of provision of material and supervision. The shelter technical needs for selected households will be evaluated individually and customized to meet the needs of the groups in terms of access, safety, protection and privacy rather than a standardized implementation for all shelters. Moreover, the CBPC will ensure representation of these marginalized groups and their needs will be accounted for in the customized protection of DRP trainings in communities.
