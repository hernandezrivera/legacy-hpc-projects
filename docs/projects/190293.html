Project Summary

Over 85 percent of people in Ethiopia rely on agriculture and livestock for their livelihoods and are consequently heavily dependent on seasonal rainfalls, recurrent droughts are critical drivers of food insecurity and related protection risks for millions of people. The food security situation in Borena zones of Oromia Region continues to deteriorate following the poor performance of 2021 Genna rains , resulting in severe harvest losses, poor livestock performance, and livestock death, household famine, an alarmingly increasing rate of malnutrition, and limited feed and water availability, resulting in reduced milk and meat, low livestock prices, which have contributed to rising food insecurity and malnutrition rates among livestock dependent households . Furthermore, on October 5, 2021, OCHA flash update report revealed that, 71 percent of harvest loss has been reported in Borena Zone and resulting in over 30 percent of the total population need of emergency food assistance. Most of the water sources in the zone have all dried up. In the last three months, malnutrition cases in the zone are showing an upward trend and according to the MUAC readings, children under five years and pregnant and lactating women are at risk. This can be attributed to a lack of consumption of milk and an imbalanced diet. Household milk availability has significantly decreased. The household food security situation has been significantly compromised and pastoralists who have very limited purchasing power are relying on market purchases to get food.
ACF in coordination with the Borena Zone Pastoral Development Office (PDO) and Disaster Risk Management Office will respond to drought-affected areas. Based on The Livestock Emergency Guidelines and Standards (LEGS), ACF will closely work with pastoralists to target inputs for livestock protection based on existing local strategies.
Response Analysis
Livestock-based drought response in pastoralist areas aims to protect key livestock assets and support the rapid rebuilding of herds after drought. This project is responding to the needs of the affected population as identified by Assessment the type of response and the prioritization of this project is to support vulnerable households with Animal feed , Agricultural input,Animal Health Treatment and Multipurpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) before animal conditions further deteriorate and drought-affected Household food insecurity and malnutrition cases escalate
Animal feed and Animal Health Treatment: To ensure animals' health during the upcoming rainy season (Summery season) and minimize livestock deterioration and death, both Animal feed and Animal health treatment will be provided to 8000 drought-affected vulnerable households for 24,000 livestock on a case by case basis, while accessing the veterinary service through the woreda Livestock Office and woreda level Animal Health technicians. Emergency medication preventing diseases in livestock populations during emergencies and rainy season, which will cover infectious diseases such as anthrax, clostridial diseases, pasteurellosis, deworming tablets, Newcastle diseases, and other treatments as needed will be conducted. This activity will be handled in collaboration with Zonal and Woreda Animal Production and Veterinary Office. Animal Health Technicians and Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWS) will provide the treatment for the livestock and faclitate Animal feed disturbution .
MPCA: provides HHs the freedom to make immediate choices and prioritize family needs to survive and recover with dignity. It is a preferred modality by regional governments and affected people, particularly where delays in in-kind assistance and local markets are functional and accessible. 1. - Projects activities

Activity 1: Beneficiary Targeting and Awareness Raising Participatory community-based targeting of the beneficiaries will be used for the identification of project-targeted HHs .The “ general assembly modality” will be implemented, whereby the kebele community selects the targeting committee and the appeal committee, as well as validates/refine the HHs selection criteria communicated by ACF. The kebele and woreda authorities will validate the final beneficiary.Beneficiaries will be made aware of project objectives, targets and duration to ensure their full understanding of the project and their commitment to organizing community meetings and home visits for households with mobility problems (elderly, chronically sick, and disabled). Based on a set of eligibility criteria for targeting households including PLWs, households with SAM/MAM U-5 children, PLWs, women, and children headed households, a household with chronically ill and disabled members will be targeted. Activity 2: Procurement and Transportation of Animal feed,veterinary drug and water purifiers for beneficiaries Veterinary drug and Water purifiers of drinking water will be procured based on ACF procurement policy and procedures from a local market and transported to respective woredas, and animal health treatment for the selected animals will be implemented in Talitale , Dubulke , Miyo, and Dhas Woredas and the specific Kebeles will be selected based on the need and accessibility in consultation with local governmentActivity 3: Hygiene and Sanitation promotion for project Beneficiaries Hygiene and sanitation awareness-raising sessions will conduct and Drinking water purifiers will distribute to project target HHs to prevent the households from waterborne disease ( cholera outbreak ) in the upcoming rainy season Activity 4: Provide Veterinary services for drought-affected household’s livestock To ensure Animal health and to minimize their death, this activity will provide livestock treatment to 8000 drought-affected vulnerable households for24000 livestock through provision of vouchers on a case by case basis, while accessing the veterinary service through the woreda livestock office and woreda level animal health technicians. Activity 5: Distribution of MPCA ACF will provide cash for drought-affected households to cover their basic nutritional and other needs. A total of 8000 HHs will receive 2,000 ETB each month for a total of 6,000 ETB in three months. The transfer value is set at 50% of the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) for a HHs of five which is determined locally through market price assessment and based on the value of the MEB calculated in the two zones with due consideration of inflation, the common food baskets and other basic needs. Throughout the project period, ACF will continuously monitor the capacity of the markets to respond to any sudden, unanticipated changes in local conditions, if needed. ACF has been working with Awash bank over the last 2 years and successfully implemented cash assistance programs in hard-to-reach areas. Activity 6: Provide Livestock feed and Agricultural input for drought affect 8000 hhs The supplementary feeding aims to protect livestock loss for drought affected most at risk community. Provision of livestock feed can also have an impact on maintaining milk production for children , and provision of Agricultural input enhance house hold reslience for drought Activity 7: Post Distribution MonitoringACF will conduct post-distribution monitoring within two weeks of the distribution for each distribution to verify cash transfer to determine exactly how HHs benefited in terms of meeting their basic nutritional and other needs. The main purpose of the monitoring is to assess the effectiveness, accessibility, safety, and accountability of the distribution process. Besides, there will be an exit interview during distribution to monitor the transparency and accountability of the process
