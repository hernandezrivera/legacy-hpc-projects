Project Summary

Ongoing conflicts resulted in severe negative impacts on people in South Kordofan; casualties, and trauma among women. Child, women and young females have been at risk and live within very destructive environment; possible incidents of GBV, poor health services, and many other issues affecting their life.
There is a lack of protection services especially services to address Sexual & Domestic Violence and Family tracing & reunification, and referral system.
Newly IDPs are often overlooked during humanitarian interventions, as they are not mainstreamed into programming and face obstacles in accessing support that has not been tailored to them. There is an increase in different forms of GBV such as domestic violence, FGM and early marriage of young girls. Vulnerable displaced and refugees families are exposed to the effects of conflict and displacement, including family separation, destitution and lack of assistance and other services which led to leave vulnerable people without adequate support to address their basic needs, community safety nets are broken and social institutions are inefficient to provide services. (Compilation field prioritization results for sector Protection for 2016)
Through engaging men, women, girls and boys , older people, PSNs ensuring the full and equal respect for the rights of all individuals, regardless of age, gender, ethnic, social, religious or other background into need identification, project design, implementation and monitoring.
Interventions will be focused around priorities, as identified by IDPs and host communities in order to enhance both resilience and sustainability.
Through this project, AMVO will construct/rehabilitate 4 community meeting centres, CFS, women centers to empower IDPs and host communities and provide a safe public spaces and serve as a key resource to foster peaceful coexistence, social events, recreational activities, public information and education. Ensuring integrated approach with protection services GP, CP and GBV services. 4 quick impact projects will be implemented to serve host communities and IDPs benefitting 20000 beneficiary. Capacity of 8 CBPNs (2 Net/locality, 160 members) and 8 social workers will be strengthened to improve the identification of PSNs including survivors of SGBV to detect their basic needs, make their voice heard & ensure DMS to 2000 vulnerable people provided to cover their specific needs beside strengthen referral pathways to relevant service providers including health insurance, mobility equipment (crutches) in particular for elderly or people with handicap. 48 Network meetings, one per month per locality, will be organized by CBPNs to identify IDPs protection and GBV issues. As a result of meetings, 16 quarterly forum will be held with CBPNs & relevant decision makers’ representatives to respond to issues raised at the network meetings.
2000 women will be provided with PSS services and refer victims/survivors (Medical, Legal, Police) through well-defined referral system in addition to 2000 PHKs will be distributed for women and girls. Provide 1000 women & girls with recreational activities, skills trainings will be determined by marketing survey. AMVO will held capacity building training to 100 health personnel and service providers from Kadugli, Elabassiya, Abu Jubaiha and Eliri to support protection of IDPs such as definition of protection, guiding principles on internal displacement, GBV and PSS in Sudan. Also 30 stakeholders will be trained on GBV and effective referral mechanism at the locality level.
Furthermore, awareness raising campaigns will be held for 9600 of the CBPNs and community members on protection, GBV and PSS including the International Day for the Elimination of VAW with other partners join together to speak out against gender violence and to promote the rights and principles of the declaration

Needs

Large number of civilians in South Kordofan (SK) State are displaced and severely affected by ongoing fighting between the SAF and the SPLM N. Per the latest figures, the number of verified IDPs and resident people in need for SK is 206,894 people (109,337 children U18 Years, 63,484 adults from 18 to 60 years and 6,445 elderly over 60 years). 68,820 individual were displaced from SPLM-N controlled areas to Kadugli which hosts the largest number of IDPs in the State, 23,112 individual in Abu Jubaiha, 3,661 individual in Eliri and 20,062 individual in Abbassiya. This project will prioritize interventions in Kadugli, Abu Jubaiha, Eliri and Abbassiya locality.The influx of displaced persons and refugees puts communities under stress, and may trigger tension/ conflicts. Mechanisms and capacities contributing to conflict resolution and peaceful coexistence, as well as advocacy with authorities in displacement and return areas, need support.Breakdown in family and community structures leaves vulnerable people without adequate support to address their essential needs. Community safety nets are severely weakened, and social institutions are usually too stretched to provide adequate protection services to the affected population. GBV has been exacerbated by the conflict, as well as by inadequate living conditions and the lack of privacy, which IDPs and refugees face in camps and elsewhere. Lack of awareness among communities makes it challenging to ensure prevention of GBV and to ensure access of survivors to the relevant multi-sectoral services. Vulnerable women and girls in areas of conflict need support to prevent risks of GBV, in particular to reduce their exposure. The IDPs are mixed within the host community. The main consequences of this conflict include disruption of basic services, exposure of women, girls, men and boys to further insecurity and violence, disruption of economic and agricultural activities added additional burden placed on host families.Almanar has a well-established main office in Khartoum state and 4 sub- offices in South Kordofan state in Kadugli, Dilling, Abu Jubaiha and Abassiya with qualified and committed staff recruited in each office and good relation with the government and communities. Almanar has a successful experience in implementing protection programs in South Kordofan with UNHCR, EU, SHF, UNFPA and other donors through working with well - trained protection community based committees / social workers &LGAs: awareness raising sessions on Human rights, women’s rights, child rights, citizenship, peace building & conflict resolution, cross cutting issues: (GBV, HIV/AIDS, environment), legal aid, protection materials support, PSS to GBV survivors and recreational and basic vocational skills as well as provision of protection support to the prisoners. Also Almanar is working in building the capacity of government authorities, community leaders and CSOs in the geographical areas where the proposed intervention target

Impact on environment/natural resources

Environmental mainstreaming will be integrated through the entire project management cycle. Environmental mainstreaming will be integrated in the assessments to ensure that environmental harm is reduced or eliminated and environmental benefits are maximized. Key environmental concerns will be built into the project in order to reduce deforestation in Sudan. Almanar will attempt to switch away from fired bricks to alternative construction methods such as stabilized soil blocks (SSB) or concrete blocks (CSB) depending on the prerequisites of the region. Environmental mainstreaming will be included during monitoring and evaluation of the project outcomes of the response to ensure that the effects sought was indeed delivered, and to help identify possible negative environmental effects in time in order to adjust activities accordingly. It is a necessary part of ensuring that the principle of do no harm is fulfilled. Furthermore, to help identify best practices and lessons learnt that offer valuable insight for making improvements in future aid efforts. Almanar will work with local NGOs, CSOs and communities including working with the national and state governmental authorities, since Community consultation and participation is a key part of environmental management. Throughout the project cycle gender will be considered to access to, and management of natural resources to receive consultation from both men and women. The combined information will give more accurate information on the total need for the community and ensure equally assessing.
