Project Summary

The goal of the project is to contribute to the reduction in health related mortality and morbidity, and improve access to high quality Lifesaving emergency health interventions to floods affected people in need in both Duk and Twic East County with focus to women, children, people with disabilities,IDPs & returnees.
Both Duk and Twic East counties primary health care system has low coverage of antenatal care (ANC) first and fourth visits as well as deliveries by skilled birth attendants (SBA) are extremely lowest with ANC 1 at 20% and ANC 4 at 8% while skilled delivery is less than 6%, since few health units are permanently staffed with skilled birth attendants. The unmet need for family planning remains high with only 4% utilizing services (DHIS2 2020). Child Health indicators are alarming with low immunization coverage which is extremely very poor with Penta3 coverage of only 14% (DHIS2 2020), this situation was further complicated by looting of cold chain equipment and limited access to and difficulty in vaccine supply to remote areas; therefore, children under the age of 5 is at risk of measles outbreaks (DHIS2 2020).
The counties have high burden of preventable diseases caused mainly by malaria (80%), diarrhea (15%) and pneumonia (5%) in U 5 years which have a direct negative impact on the nutrition status of young children and calls for high sensitization campaigns for timely health seeking behavior, good hygiene practices and public health initiatives (DHIS2 2020).
The counties are in level IPC Phase 4 with GAM rate in Duk and Twic East at 26.61 % and 22.2% respectively (HNO 2021) which are above the WHO threshold for emergency intervention.
These statistics show the significant need to step up efforts on provision of essential emergency health care to ensure adequate availability and accessibility of health services to the communities in the two counties.
This project intent to reach 51,046 (M=25,513, F=25,533) people in Duk while
26,526(M=11,855. F=14,672) in Twic East (PIN 2021) with curative and preventive services. These targets are focusing on the vulnerable people among IDPs, returnees and host communities who have no access to health care interventions.
The package of services to be supported includes Child health services, Maternal and neonatal health services, Basic and comprehensive emergency obstetric and newborn care, Sexual and gender-based violence services, Distribution of essential medicines and supplies , Emergency preparedness and response, Disease surveillance and outbreak response and quality improvement and supervision.
The proposed project objectives will be:
1. To reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic-prone diseases and health insecurities in Duk and Twic East by the end of 2021
2. To improve access and scale-up response to integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable populations in Duk and Twic East by the end of 2021
3. To increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled and mental health disorders in Duk and Twic East by the end of 2021
The proposed activities will be:
• Emergency preparedness and contingency planning for early disease outbreaks detection and response.
• Provision of normal deliveries attended by skilled birth attendants
• Provision of PENTA3 vaccination to under one year old
• Supporting Primary healthcare centers to provide Basic Emergency Obstetric Neonatal Care (BEmONC) and Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric Neonatal Care (CeMONC)
• Provision of essential lifesaving basic health services through outpatient and inpatient consultations
• Provision of MHPSS and CMR services in health facilities
• Provision of rehabilitation services for the disabled
• Provision of emergency Measles vaccinations to children 6 months to 15 years
• Provision of WASH and Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) in health facilities for COVID-19 prevention and response to the communities
• Provision of feedback mechanism

1 - Needs

The high poverty and long history of conflict has left an already very basic health care system extremely weakened, with severe shortages of health workers and vandalized health facilities leaving vulnerable communities with limited access to health care services. The people in the two counties have limited access to optimal health services due to ongoing conflict, flooding and an underdeveloped health care system. These targeted counties affected by floods are inhabited by an extremely vulnerable and traumatized community majority being women, girls, boys, and elderly people, persons with disabilities who are physically and psychologically exposed to multitude of stressors. The population is likely to continue to be highly vulnerable to epidemic disease outbreaks due to low immunization coverage, a weak health system and poor hygiene and sanitation. The already limited functionality of health services is likely to be further strained by COVID-19, including the macroeconomic adverse impacts, deepening vulnerabilities. Both Duk and Twic East counties primary health care system has low coverage of antenatal care (ANC) first and fourth visits as well as deliveries by skilled birth attendants (SBA) are extremely lowest with ANC 1 at 20% and ANC 4 at 8% while skilled delivery is less than 6%, since few health units are permanently staffed with skilled birth attendants. The unmet need for family planning remains high with only 4% utilizing services. Child Health indicators are alarming with low immunization coverage which is extremely very poor with Penta3 coverage of only 14% (DHIS2 2020), this situation was further complicated by looting of cold chain equipment and limited access to and difficulty in vaccine supply to remote areas; therefore, children under the age of 5 is at risk of measles outbreaks (DHIS2 2020). The counties have high burden of preventable diseases caused mainly by malaria (80%), diarrhea (15%) and pneumonia (5%) in U 5 years which have a direct negative impact on the nutrition status of young children and need sensitization campaigns for timely health seeking behavior, good hygiene practices and public health initiatives (DHIS2 2020). The counties are in level IPC Phase 4 with GAM rate in Duk and Twic East at 26.61 % and 22.2% respectively (HNO 2021) which are above the WHO threshold for emergency intervention. The ongoing flood situation will further aggravate the health situation, the spread of existing outbreaks and the likelihood of new outbreaks in 2021. This highlights the critical need to strengthen disease surveillance and response system to prevent, detect and respond to future outbreaks. The absence of basic health care including vaccination services has resulted in low immunization coverage throughout the country, increasing the risk of vaccine-preventable disease outbreaks. Planned mass vaccination campaigns were disrupted by the COVID-19 containment measures in place, which worsened the vaccination coverage and related health risks. To avoid excess mortality and morbidity and improve the health and wellbeing of people affected by conflict, national disasters and a weak socio-economic system, the provision of essential lifesaving basic health services are critical. JDF will support health facilities including community-based healthcare systems to serve vulnerable people including women and girls, aged population and persons with a disability must be supported and strengthened; Immunization services need to be strengthened to increase coverage and prevent disease outbreaks; Disease outbreaks need to be detected early, responded to in a timely manner and adequate prevention measures put in place; Referral pathways for mental health and psychosocial support, sexual and gender-based violence survivors and for maternal and child health emergencies also need to be strengthened; COVID-19 prevention and response activities must continue till COVID-19 is no longer threat to communities.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The protection analysis emerging from a community- and rights-based approach that is underpinned by a comprehensive system of accountability will inform and shape the overall strategy, enable the design of appropriate preventive, responsive and environment-building interventions by protection actors. The project will ensure an equitable and meaningful access by girls, boys, women and men of all ages and diverse backgrounds to protection and assistance programmes. The project will employ participatory assessments at key stages, incorporating systematic engagement to identify the diverse perspectives, risks and capacities of groups within crisis-affected communities, including men, women, girls and boys, and groups such as older persons, persons with disabilities, persons belonging to minority groups, and persons of diverse sexual orientation or gender identity complemented by Do no Harm and conflict analysis. Protection sensitization sessions shall be conducted in partnership with other protection actors in the locations particularly during community outreach. During such platforms beneficiaries shall be made aware of their rights such as right to be served with dignity, right to aid, right to information, right to give feedback, right to participation during any engagements. Existing community notice boards shall be used where they exist to stick useful protection information so that beneficiaries are empowered. Where protection committees exist such structures shall be used to strengthen the protection capacities at individual and community level. Also GBV is rooted deeply in harmful social norms and anchored in patriarchal traditions, such as strict gender roles and identities, patriarchal authority, women’s low social status and power imbalance reflected by their limited access to decision-making and reproductive rights, poverty, inequality in the area of employment or education, and discriminatory practices (Protection Cluster, 2018). With overall reduced access to health service by population, clinical management of rape services including even the most basic GBV services are also limited, adhoc and severely compromised by the lack of the right and quantities of skill sets able to deliver an effective response. JDF is in compliance with Code of conduct on PSEA that Prohibit sexual exploitation and sexual abuse that constitute acts of serious misconduct and are therefore grounds for disciplinary measures, including summary dismissal; JDF staffs are obliged to create and maintain an environment that prevents sexual exploitation and sexual abuse. The project will significantly scale-up access to services for GBV victims in the two counties. Health facility staff will be trained as to be able to administer Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) and improve access to and provision of confidential and sensitive health services to survivors of all forms of GBV. Identification, counselling and proper referral for victims of gender based violence, including rape victims, will be strengthened both at facility and community level through training of Community stakeholders and non medical staff working in health facilities on Psycho social First aid (PFA), GBV principles and referral pathways. GBV risk mitigation efforts will be conducted by JDF and will include the training of the staffs on protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Under this project, JDF will ensure that all their employees and personnel comply with PSEA. JDF will further ensure that none of its employees and personnel exposes any intended beneficiary, including women and children, to any form of discrimination, abuse or exploitation and that each of the its employees and personnel complies with the provisions of other JDF policies relating to protection of children as advised by JDF.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Community consultation and participation are crucial aspects of JDF programme design processes and are critical in terms of ensuring success and promoting future sustainability. This approach fosters community ownership and enables JDF to focus on the sustainability and appropriateness of interventions. JDF is accountable to the women, men, boys and girls whose lives it aims to improve, and places this responsibility at the core of its humanitarian policy. As a matter of human rights and meaningful programming, JDF defines Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) as “an active commitment by humanitarian actors and organizations to use power responsibly by taking account of, giving account to and being held to account by the people they seek to assist”. In line with JDF’s commitment to social accountability, which includes a complaints and feedback mechanism, JDF will engage the community from the initiation of the project in order for the community to own it. Stakeholders meetings will be held in the location identified to enable community members to communicate issues and concerns associated with the health care services they are being provided, to provide feedback and monitor implementation of the response. The mechanism will have multiple access points (community consultations, suggestion box, Health facilities, telephone and email) and information about its availability will be posted at all health facilities. The community will be routinely consulted and provided with information regarding the project implementation. This will include engaging existing South Sudan’s Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC), State and County Health Department representatives, community elders, women, youth groups, religious leaders and representatives of beneficiaries. Aside from through JDF field and Juba-based offices, this will be the medium through which communities will be encouraged to express their concerns, views and provide regular feedback to JDF in a regular structured modality. Other reasonable modalities for feedback that is useful to the communities/beneficiaries will also be considered and complaints will be taken seriously by the project’s management to ensure concerns are addressed. The project will ensure an equitable and meaningful access by girls, boys, women and men of all ages and diverse backgrounds to protection and assistance programmes. The project will employ participatory assessments at key stages, incorporating systematic engagement to identify the diverse perspectives, risks and capacities of groups within crisis-affected communities, including men, women, girls and boys, and groups such as older persons, persons with disabilities, persons belonging to minority groups, and persons of diverse sexual orientation or gender identity complemented by Do no Harm and conflict analysis. The project will ensure that information dissemination includes, as minimum, accessible and timely information on cluster strategies, targeting criteria, geographical focus, services provided and entitlements, programmatic changes, and community feedback and complaints mechanisms; The project will ensure that visible and accessible feedback and complaints mechanisms are in place, tailored to the operational context and the preferences of communities, which can appropriately handle complaints like violations of the law (such as sexual exploitation and abuse),institutional policies / codes of conduct; and quality and appropriateness of humanitarian programmesThese feedback will form part of the project performance reporting to JDF and will help guide the fine-tuning of the project to enhance positive beneficiary experience.
