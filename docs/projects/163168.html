Project Summary

The Project aims to provide immediate unconditional 12-month in kind food assistance to the most vulnerable IDPs and local populations affected by crisis in the North-West and South-West. The number of people hiding in bushes is hard to identify or access without signiﬁcant risk. In the absence of a formal platform for beneﬁciary registration and selection, WFP is implementing a beneﬁciary selection tool to enhance the household targeting of food assistance activities in the South West and North West regions. The process involves coordination with other partners and communities to identify and select beneﬁciary households based on the following vulnerability criteria: Household demographic status; Household dependency ratio; Displacement status of households; Accommodation status of the household; Access to income.
Local support committees established at community level prepare lists of beneﬁciaries respecting the vulnerability criteria. The beneﬁciaries interviewed, and information veriﬁed WFP plans to meet the immediate food and nutritional needs of the population affected by the crisis through unconditional food assistance and enhance the capacity of our partners.
In line with the results of the Multisector Initial Rapid Assessment (MIRA), Emergency Food and Security Assessment, and the Cadre Harmonise. WFP will provide food support to 250,000 targeted people affected by the crisis in the South West and North West regions. To save lives and protect the food security and nutrition status these vulnerable people, WFP will provide immediate humanitarian food assistance to the targeted 250,000 IDPs and local vulnerable host populations (180,000 in the NW and 80,000 in the SW) in their various locations in urban, semi urban and bushes. The modality of assistance will be in-kind general food distributions. Special attention will be paid to beneﬁciaries located in rural areas and diﬃcult to access settlements. The food basket and the daily ration are rations of 350 g rice, 100 g pulse, 35 g vegetable oil, 50 g super cereal and 5 g salt per person. to implement the Project, WFP will work though experienced Cooperating Partners that have a good mastery and knowledge of the culture and driving forces of the areas. Organizations World Food Programme Implementing Partners Reach Out; Caritas-Kumba, LUKMEF, COMINSUD, CBCHS, CARITAS-Bamenda, SHUMAS and other NGOs.
During Project implementation, regular monitoring activities will be conducted during food distribution and monthly data collected against performance indicators for reporting through WFP COMET system. During post distribution monitoring, attention will be paid on the role played by men, women, girls and boys including decision-making on food consumption and food security in the household. Nutrition, Gender and protection issues are taken into consideration at all the stages of the project elaboration, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. Gender and protection risk analysis will be carried out and the most vulnerable will be targeted, including female-headed households, pregnant and lactating women, persons with disabilities.
In line with WFP accountability affected populations, targeted vulnerable IDPs and local populations will be closely involved in various stages of including the targeting process, food distribution. They will be regularly informed on the project activities and plans. Through the complaints and feedback mechanisms they will freely express their views and concerns on the project implementation. In line with WFP accountability to affected populations, beneficiaries’ complaints are treated respectfully, and their views are considered for projects adjustment.
WFP together with partners will conduct assessments to identify an update the list of the most vulnerable people among IDPs and the local populations to be considered in the projects based on vulnerability criteria. Besoins / Needs

Since November 2017, the socio-political crisis in these regions has progressively translated into insecurity and armed violence. The escalation of tension and upsurge in hostilities between non-state armed groups (NSAG) and government defense and security forces have triggered humanitarian needs across the two regions, marked by signiﬁcant internal displacement. Assessments carried out by IOM reported that more than 542,158 people have been displaced in the North West and South West regions as of October 2019. The crisis continues to have a negative impact on livelihoods, trade, markets and access to land for the affected population. Farmers, small-scale ﬁshermen and their families ﬁnd themselves in an extremely vulnerable position due to the disruption of their activities (Cadre Harmonise, Dec 2019). IDPs have had to resort to negative food-related coping strategies, these include; reducing the number of meals (to one meal a day) and /or the quantity of food consumed by adults in order to feed the children. According to a recent Cadre Harmonise (October 2019) and Emergency Food Security Assessment conducted by WFP in October 2019, more than half of the households have inadequate food consumption in these two regions. Worrying trends include: • Up to 1.5 million people are food insecure of which 900,000 in North-West and 600,000 in South-West region (EFSA, January 2019); • Up to 57.5% in NW and 53.7% of households in SW have insuﬃcient food consumption (Cadre Harmonise, EFSA, October 2019); • Over 51.0% in NW and 70.2% in SW households apply potentially irreversible “crisis” or “emergency” coping strategies to cope with food insecurity and other basic needs (Cadre Harmonise, EFSA, October 2019); • Global acute malnutrition in SW and NW deteriorated to 7.4% and 5.1% respectively (Cadre Harmonise, EFSA, October 2019) compared to last results of January (EFSA 2019). The most vulnerable people in these regions highly need emergency humanitarian food assistance to save lives.The NWSW crisis that led to the displacement of thousands of people has create food insecurity as it is unsafe for people income generating activities such as farming and open shops or move for business. The food insecurity risks have impacted on those in desperate need for food thus leading to negative coping mechanisms. Assessments by humanitarian actors revealed that the high rate of displacement is due to to insecurity; armed clashes between NSAG and Government military forces. Also, there is high risks of enrolment in armed groups as a source of livelihood; increased kidnappings, killing, mutilations arbitrary arrest and disappearances. As far gender and protection is concerned, the assessments indicated that sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV), has been on the rise among other protection risks. Rape is being used as a weapon of war; younger girls are forced to engage in survival sex, (including with armed groups) leading to unwanted pregnancies and sexual and reproductive health problems, which need to be treated but are left unattended owing to ﬁnancial constraints. There are families separated and relatives killed, schools and school children are threatened, and civilians living in a „climate of terror‟ (HRP 2019). Freedom of movement is greatly restricted and becomes riskier by the day, and people who have ﬂed to the forests or bushes live precariously. Persons living with disabilities are subjected to targeted abusive treatment, torture and killings by the conﬂicting parties in addition to the disproportionate impact of instability, sudden movements and violence places on them (Protection Cluster Strategy, August 2019). There is a distinct overlap between groups of people who are most vulnerable to food insecurity and those who face the greatest protection risks, and these include households headed by a child, by a single mother, by an elderly person, by a person with a disability (WFP EFSA).
