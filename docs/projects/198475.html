Project Summary

The Conflict in Northeast Nigeria stemming from the insurgency of non-state armed groups (NSAG) continues apace. Violent attacks by NSAG have led to the displacement of millions of people, destroying agricultural activities, other livelihoods and disrupting essential services. The flooding seen in 2022 has further aggravated the plight of the affected population in Northeast states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) with over 513,000 individuals affected. Floods often lead to an outbreak of waterborne diseases. As of 30 September 2022, 7,700, cases of Cholera were recorded, including 324 deaths reported across these three states. The current context has left several displaced individuals in acute need of lifesaving assistance and livelihood opportunities. Although the need for essential life-saving assistance is still widespread, IOM recognizes the urgent need for early recovery interventions, including restoring livelihoods and access to essential services, including access to justice, to strengthen the self-reliance of affected populations, especially girls, youth, women, and the elderly, against future vulnerabilities and shocks. These elements are also crucial for creating conditions for durable solutions for IDPs and restoring social cohesion. Without those pre-conditions, an untimely return of displaced populations may lead to secondary displacement, intensify IDPs’ vulnerability, and exacerbate tension and social polarization between IDPs, host communities and returnees. IOM considers insufficient capacity in areas of return to be a direct risk of the overall progressive resolution of displacement in the Northeast. This project seeks to contribute to the recovery and resilience building of conflict and flood affected populations in Northeast Nigeria through restoring infrastructure, promote early recovery, including through economic and livelihoods restoration, and facilitating a robust resilience building process. Livelihood interventions under this project will include Multipurpose Cash Assistance (MPCA), and Cash for Work (CFW). Cash-based interventions are a more dignified way of assisting affected populations, as they empower people to determine their own needs and the best way of meeting them.

1 - Needs

An estimated 8.4 million people in the BAY states (of whom 2.2 million are internally displaced) are in acute need of humanitarian and protection assistance. Of the 61 local government areas (LGAs) that the Joint Intersectoral Analysis Framework (JIAF) classified using the Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) and other data, 22 are classified as 'extreme' in terms of severity of needs; another 20 have 'severe' needs. Ten LGAs are at 'stressed' level, while the remaining one has 'minimal severity' of needs. Qualitative consultations with affected people in Northeast Nigeria indicate that the top-priority needs are food (93%), livelihoods and income generation. Therefore, the critical needs for crisis-affected groups remain livelihood support and job creation, access to credit and vocational training, support to productive assets and linkages to public and private sector for value chain addition and sustainability. Revitalization of the economy through restoration of markets and critical infrastructures, such as hospitals, schools, roads, and waste management systems are also key in achieving a voluntary, sustainable, and dignified return. There is an urgent need for early recovery interventions, including restoring livelihoods and access to essential services, including access to justice, to strengthen the self-reliance of affected populations. These elements are also crucial for creating conditions for durable solutions for IDPs and restoring social cohesion. Specific measures to restore livelihoods include short-term employment creation, providing market-based skills, including vocational training, and micro and small enterprise recovery, targeting the recapitalization of small enterprises for youth and women, derived from community consultation.

2 - Activities

This project seeks to contribute to the recovery and resilience building of conflict and flood affected populations in Northeast Nigeria, and to set a preparedness pace for reoccurring flooding/other natural disaster through a series of community support interventions. As people continue to return to their places of habitual residence from before the insurgency, there is an urgent and significant need to restore infrastructure, promote early recovery, including through economic and livelihoods restoration, and facilitate a robust resilience building process. Livelihood interventions under this project will include Multipurpose Cash Assistance (MPCA), and Cash for Work (CFW). Cash-based interventions are a more dignified way of assisting affected populations, as they empower people to determine their own needs and the best way of meeting them. They can also promote peaceful co-existence between IDP, returnee and host communities, as well-designed and run cash-based interventions have a multiplier effect, jointly engaging all members of the community and directly benefitting the local economy. In addition, vocational skills training and skills enhancement activities, apprenticeship, and public Private Partnership will be encouraged to ensure sustainability of the intervention. Women, youth, and girls will be engaged and trained in market driven skills as per the outcome of a thorough labor market assessment to be conducted as part of the project to create human capital as necessary for the early recovery phase. Each targeted beneficiary will also receive business start-up capital to establish small scale business. CFW will support activities such as comprehensive upgrade and equipping of key public infrastructure and will contribute directly to recovery in conflict and flood affected areas. In addition, this project will work towards enhanced coordination and complementarity between humanitarian, stabilization, and development partners, through a people-centered approach. To supplement economic recovery interventions, the project will additionally support restoration of social cohesion and local governance mechanisms in the areas of intervention. To this end, the project aims to provide technical and logistical support to community peace committees to address social cohesion concerns. Furthermore, to ensure protection is guaranteed in recovery efforts, the project will build the capacity of law enforcement bodies, including traditional/religious leaders, to prevent and respond to gender-based violence (GBV) in compliance with human rights standards and through a victim-centered approach. IOM will align its response plan to the 4 strategic objectives of the Early Recovery andLivelihoods Sector as follows: • Support livelihood stabilization; • Promote inclusiveness and peaceful co-existence among idps in host communities, returnees, and host communities;• Support restoration of local governance administration and services; • Improve access to infrastructure and basic social services through rehabilitation and reconstruction of public service facilities.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

IOM will identify groups at risk of unsafe livelihoods practices (eg. child labour, survival/transactional sex) and prioritize the provision of alternatives for these groups. IOM will also ensure women and girls have equal and fair access to all opportunities as men e.g. micro-credit, cash transfers, agricultural inputs and skills training and that they receive the same benefits for their input and work. In coordination with protection actors, IOM will enhance the capacity of community food security and livelihood workers to monitor, report and refer protection concerns (such as abuse and exploitation). IOM will consult women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, chronically ill, older persons, pregnant and lactating women and marginalized persons to collect accurate information about their specific food and nutritional requirements and preferences for food distribution points and livelihood services.
