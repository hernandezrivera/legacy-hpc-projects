Besoins / Needs

The results of the latest nutrition survey (with the SMART method) conducted in November 2019 by UNICEF and the government of Cameroon showed a GAM rate of 5.2% with 1.4% of SAM. For chronic malnutrition, Far North had the highest prevalence with 38.2%. Situation related to the main underlying causes of malnutrition remains fragile with limited access to potable water especially for refugees, IDPs and their host communities, 56% in Far North with Internally Displaced Person having less than 15l / person / day. Regarding health situation, low immunization coverage, weak access to essential health care services (ratio between the population and the number of doctors remains far below the norm) and essential drugs increased risks of epidemics.The prevalence of food insecurity is 11% in the Far North region (CFSVA February 2018, including 1% in severe food insecurity. The proportion of households whose food consumption is poor remained relatively high in the North (9%). In this context, indicator related to infant and young child feeding practices are among the poorest in the country, especially for the Far North region with exclusive breastfeeding rate of 30.8%, a timely introduction rate for complementary foods of 33.3% and a low dietary diversity (dietary diversity score of 19.7% and minimum acceptable dietary intake of 32%). Indicators related to early initiation of breastfeeding and diet diversity follow the same trends and highlight needs to reinforce knowledge and practices around young child feeding. A minimum of 107,000 people will suffer from global acute malnutrition (GAM), including 17,000 pregnant and lactating women and 90,000 girls and boys under the age of 5. It is estimated that approximately 41,000 girls and boys under the age of 5 will suffer from severe acute malnutrition, or one in two severe acute malnourished children in Cameroon.
