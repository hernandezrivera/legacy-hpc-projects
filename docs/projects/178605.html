Project Summary

In 2020 ZOA implemented projects in multiple sectors including FSA, reaching a total of 35,081 beneficiaries. In 2020 ZOA was able to secure $4,439,160 in funding, of which $1,331,000 was SHF funding. In 2021 ZOA has to date secured $3,888,000 with around $1,8 million secured for food security interventions.
Currently, ZOA is implementing FSA interventions in Al-Hasakeh, Rural Damascus and Lattakia with interventions aimed at increasing food security that include agricultural support to farmers, food vouchers, food baskets and multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA).
ZOA is proposing an expansion of these interventions to reach communities most in need of food security in underserved conflict affected areas in Rural Damascus, Al-Hassakeh and Lattakia. Based on community based needs assessments and sector guidance, ZOA will target the most insecure households with an emphasis on those in areas with high severity of needs. Activities are aimed at improving the food security status of assessed food insecure households through life sustaining food assistance. According to needs, market assessments, sector guidance and beneficiary preference, appropriate modalities that include vouchers, food baskets, cash assistance, grants and other support to farmers will be used. Assistance will be prioritised to the most vulnerable groups that include female-headed households, persons with disabilities and the elderly.
ZOA prioritises communities with high severity of needs, and selects vulnerability and selection criteria in line with sector standards. ZOA’s vulnerability criteria also incorporates protection concerns including PWDs. Issues or concerns highlighted during assessments feed into project and implementation planning to ensure a mainstreaming approach and impactful support to households. Project committees will be set up to oversee the project implementation and take ownership of the project activities after project end. Voucher and cash values will be based on Cash Working Group recommendations. At least 5,320 households are targeted and the cost per beneficiary is estimated to be $65.
The proposed interventions are in line with the HNO as the 2021 Needs and Response Summary indicates that “Syrians prioritise access to food as by far their number one need” (p. 2), as well as the sector objectives. Also, all the targeted sub-districts fall under the severity score 3-4 based on the HNO 2021 severity master sheet.
All targeted sub-districts have 3-4 severity scale according to the HNO severity mastersheet. The locations of Lattakia has been chosen in response to the wild-fires. Tal Tamer has been selected to close the gap of food insecure households in a hard to reach area. As for al Nashabiyeh sub-district the support will go mostly to returnees and host communities in order to boost their livelihood.
ZOA has taken into consideration the Food Cluster recommendation and is aware of the HRP 2021 related documents "Recommendations for Response Packages for Specific Objectives 1, 2 and 3." We will also take into consideration the Cash Working Group recommendations. Therefore all Multi Purpose Cash assistance will be 200,000 SYP and the in kind food assistance will be selected according to one of the clusters basket samples. It is unfortunate that we don not have the capacity to do these distributions over 8 months. It is planned to do both distributions for 3 months. ZOA will inform its plans through the FSA 4Ws so that duplication of activities are avoided. As for the agricultural input the cap per farmer will be $400 for the whole agricultural season. We are targeting framers who have 3-5 donum farms, in order to support their livelihood.

4 - Needs

The crisis in Syria has entered its tenth year and the humanitarian needs continue to be extraordinarily high. Conflict, displacement, returns & the destruction of agricultural infrastructure have severely affected Syrians livelihoods & food security (FAO June 2020 report). There has been an increase in the number of people in need largely due to households depleting their savings & assets and struggling to find livelihood opportunities to meet their food needs as the crisis remains protracted. Massive depletion of livelihoods infrastructure, high levels of unemployment & poverty coupled with high food prices, sanctions and inflation have all contributed to food insecurity, as households struggle to meet their daily food needs. The bread basket has decreased significantly due to reduced access to food products in the north & due to the fires in the west. Therefore, food production in the nearer region is crucial to halt the food insecurity. The new Caesar sanctions imposed in Syria from July 2020 have added a further layer of complexity, affecting the import of food and impacting food security. Moreover, the instability in Lebanon has had a cascading effect on food markets in Syria. COVID-19 has compounded the already precarious socio-economic environment, worsening existing humanitarian needs and resulting in raising the MEB in December 2020 by 236% compared to December 2019 (HNO 2021 Needs and Response Summary). With the loss of job opportunities due to the impacts of COVID-19, including those involved in agriculture production, it is likely that food insecurity, malnutrition and adoption of undesirable coping mechanisms will increase, exacerbating existing substantial humanitarian needs across the country.

5 - Implementation of activities

ZOA will work closely with the Ministry of Agriculture, relevant local authorities, FSA sector & local communities to ensure that assistance reaches the most vulnerable. The activities and steps undertaken to achieve results are: 1. Conducting needs assessment to ascertain the most vulnerable households. 2. Consultations and coordination with affected communities. Consultations with beneficiaries including decisions on the contents of food baskets, agricultural tools and materials, distribution planning and assistance in the identification of people at risk. 3. Training and capacity building of project staff. Training will include topics on accountability to beneficiaries, protection and gender mainstreaming, safety and security and COVID-19 awareness. 4. Identification and selection of beneficiaries. 5. Provision of agricultural grants to farmers (for seeds, fertilizers, irrigation kits, tools, livestock, livestock vaccinations and poultry farming etc.) to support food production and sustainable livelihoods. 6. Distribution of food vouchers 7. Cash disbursements for food assistance 8. Distribution of food baskets 9. Provision of agricultural tools and olive trees 10. Post distribution monitoring (PDM). ZOA’s M&E is the responsibility of the country based Manager of Program Quality, supervising MEAL officers in-country. ZOA will monitor its interventions based on the logical framework- indicators and activities with sex, age and disability disaggregated. Field level monitoring will be key in quality assurance, tracking progress, improved accountability, and demonstrating impact.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

R1: Tension in community, for perceived inequality, discrimination, favoritism, including risk for project staff. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigation Measures: 1. Establish adequate selection criteria mechanisms based on vulnerabilities and tools made available by the FSA sector, such as WFP’s beneficiary targeting tool; 2. Consult with actors on the ground through operational coordination mechanisms to establish targeting criteria that are as harmonized as possible throughout protection service providers; 3. Consult with actors on the ground through operational coordination mechanisms to ensure that selected beneficiaries to which ZOA/partners just provides three months of assistance can be covered through other actors for the remaining months; 4 Consistent M&E and feedback mechanisms that contribute to project development/ changes. R2: Selection criteria influenced leading to project not accessing most vulnerable. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigating measures: 1. Clear complaint and feedback mechanisms; 2. Ongoing engagement with community leaders and clear community communication; 3. Humanitarian standards, accountability and PSEA training for staff. R3: Physical safety of beneficiaries and risk of exploitation/abuse Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium. Mitigating measures: 1. Liaison with communities and local stakeholders to develop detailed implementation plans; 2. Project coordination, sharing and seeking lessons learned with other actors; 3. Amending project plans based on identified protection vulnerabilities, such as disabilities, age and gender; 4. Regular staff training; 5. Raising awareness for all beneficiaries on complaint mechanisms.
