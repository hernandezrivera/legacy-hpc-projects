Project Summary

SAMS in this project is part of FAST Consortium. Through this project, SAMS in collaboration with consortium members are prioritizing the reduction of morbidity and mortality through improving the access to Nutrition Services in 20 health facilities in Aleppo, Idleb and Hama.
As part of its health intervention under FAST, SAMS will support nutrition services (CMAM and IYCF practices), for the IYCF component SAMS will contextually appropriate social and behavior change communication to prevent and manage malnutrition of infants, while via CMAM ractices SAMS will strengthen the capacity of the health staff to integrate these nutrition services in the facilities. These includes screening and treatment of acute malnutrition, multiple, micronutrients supplementation, The support will be through capacity building of health staff and provision of nutrition supplies for facilities in Hama, ldleb and Aleppo . The targeted facilities will also be part of the nutrition surveillance system.
For the past year, SAMS have been applying CMAM and IYCF-E in the supported facilities. In coordination with the Nutrition Cluster and nutrition actors, SAMS conducted a training
onboth CMAM and IYCF-E for medical staff in the HFs (Pediatricians, midwives and nurses) and CHWs.
Under FAST, the outpatient departments of the supported HFs and CHWs will provide the nutrition activities which include CMAM and IYCF components such as MUAC screening to children under 5 and PLW in all health facilities, reach the families with IEC materials, Provide preventive and curative nutritional supplies to all health facilities, IYCF counseling and group sessions.
Where possible, a dedicated space will be made available in the supported HFs for promoting IYCF practices, especially lactating exercises. Private space to conduct IYCF activities, particularly for teaching mothers how to breastfeed or manage re-lactating issues, is
provided in some health facilities depending on availability of space. In smaller health facilities which do not have space availability, IYCF activities are conducted in reproductive health clinics or labor rooms.
FAST partners will continue to coordinate with other health actors through the Health Cluster, Nutrition Cluster and the NGO forum where routine data is gathered and shared through the 4Ws and HERAMS. FAST partners are also coordinating with other humanitarian actors at the field level to assess the needs, strengthen referral pathways, and respond to emergencies, among other ways. Moreover, SAMS and the FAST consortium will closely coordinate to share information about needs, ensure an effective and coordinated response to new emergencies, harmonize the salary scales and staffing structures across HFs.
SAMS through this project will target 60,000 beneficiaries disaggregated by gender and age as following: 39,600 Female; 20,400 Male; 42,000 Children under five and 18,000 young adults.

Needs

Close to eight years into crisis the overall scale and complexity of humanitarian needs of people in Syria remain staggering in terms of magnitude and severity. The number of people in need is slightly higher for 2019, with 4.7 million women and children requiring support compared to 4.6 in the previous year, those disaggregated gender and age as the following: 3,197,036 Female; 1,537,854 Male; 3,138,477 Children under five and 1,596,413 Adults (HNO 2019). Across Syria, a total of 152 out of 270 sub-districts are categorized with severe nutrition problems, which are considered acute and require urgent humanitarian assistance. 96 sub-districts were ranked with major problems and 5 sub-districts were categorized as moderate, and likewise require humanitarian assistance. In addition to SMART surveys, assessments, and nutrition screening, the information sources are also supported by outcome monitoring data from ongoing nutrition programs and joint analysis with the food security sector. SMART surveys and nutrition surveillance in sub-districts throughout Syria in 2018 indicates that in areas with newly displaced populations such as in Idleb, acute malnutrition of children 6-59 months of age had rapidly increased in a space of 6 months from January 1.5% to June 4%. Moreover, Pregnant and lactating women (PLW) remain particularly vulnerable due to their specific nutritional needs and health risks. Reporting data on nutrition screening in 2018 confirmed that moderate acute malnutrition rates of PLWs increased by 134 percent. That is, 10,064 pregnant and lactating women were malnourished in July 2018 compared to 4,297 in July 2017. Lack of access to health services and negative coping mechanisms such as meal reduction are some of the causes linked to deteriorating maternal nutrition. Findings from an IYCF assessment shows exclusive breastfeeding as low as 3 percent in some communities. Barriers to appropriate feeding of young children include breastfeeding difficulties, illness of the mother, affordability, and lack of support from family. Coverage of IYCF services are largely awareness raising messages rather than the needed specialized services such as one-to-one counselling to better support pregnant women and caregivers on maternal nutrition and to safely feed their young children during the critical window of 0-24 months of age. Capacity of health and nutrition practitioners in specialized IYCF skills needs to be strengthened on a national scale. High acute and chronic malnutrition rates might be related to gender-inequitable access to nutritious foods, quality health care and WASH services. Some women and adolescent girls, especially those who may have physical limitations and/or are socially isolated, such as divorced women and widows, may not be able to access basic services including nutrition. Access may be further limited by safety concerns and/or custom and traditions that restrict women from leaving the home in order to access nutrition support, especially if services such as Mother Baby Areas (MBA), Women and Girl Safe Spaces (WGSS), and Child Friendly Spaces (CFS) are located far from their homes.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Sexual Exploitation and abuse by Facilities staff toward beneficiaries. Possibility: high. Effect: high. Mitigation measures: 1. Set PSEA Policy and effectively implement the policy in Hiring the staff and oriented them on the policy, training them, and make sure every staff sign the policy, 2. Effective complaint mechanism in place, 3. Awareness raising for beneficiaries on PSEA, 4. Clear complaints channels for beneficiaries and distribute brochures and leaflets in all places in the facilities (waiting rooms, entrance, Etc.).Risk: Sexual and Gender Based Violence in the facilities between beneficiaries. Possibility: high. Effect: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Put cameras in the entrance and every floor of the facilities and corridors, 2. Use or establish separated toilets for men and women, 3. Establish spacious waiting rooms to host huge number of patient. 4. Put keys for the empty rooms if any. Risk: Facilities are inaccessible for the disabled patient and elderly persons. Possibility: moderate. Effect: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Build ramp for disabled people at the facility entrance, 2. Make sure of the availability of logistics supplies needed for elderly persons and people with disabilities. Risk: impure or spoiled medications and supplies that could be harmful to beneficiaries. Possibility: moderate. Effect: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Procure medication with long time validity (taking into consideration the transportation time for these medication and deliver it to the facilities then to beneficiaries to be used), 2. Procure the medications from reliable sources, 3. ensure cold-chain transportation for drugs requiring low temperatures, 4. Regular monitoring of the storage conditions of the medications and the validity date.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Control by unaccepted groups/entities or GoS.Mitigation measures: SAMS will be re-running all anti-terrorism/background checks on all staff, volunteers, partners, hawala, and other vendors associated with the current award. Additional Money Transfer Verifications will take place as well where SAMS has added further anti-fraud verifications conducted by SAMS staff in Syria and Turkey before monthly disbursal of salaries to recipients. Separately, SAMS MEAL staff are continuously running their own separate spot checks, analyzing a sample of the payroll, conducting staff interviews, and verifying the salary amount received, in addition to their regular visits to the facilities, where they observe staff presence and conduct inventory checks.Risk: Attack by GoS and its allies / others.Mitigation measures: Selecting fortified locations; Security assessment (site & building); Evacuation SOP; Sourcing alternative locations, in case of need for relocation.Risk: Potential high turnover of staff receiving salariesMitigation measure: Potential high turnover of staff receiving salaries; Repeating the trainings regularly to ensure that all new staff delivering it; Adopt appropriate salary-scale to retain the staff as possible.Risk: Abduction (kidnapping)Mitigation measure: Hostile Environment and Safety Management Training; Access and movement SOPs; Local networks to monitor security situation and trends; Communication plan and local acceptance; Security Briefing; Counter Abduction; Abduction Management training for staff and partners.Risk: TheftMitigation Measures: Safety and security upgrades for SAMS facilities, including CCTV cameras and guards; Photo verification of received shipments, signed waybills, and notification if any missing items noted in HF count within 72 hours; Review of stock/inventory records during monitoring visits; Hostile Environment and Safety Management Training; Access and movement SOPs; Local networks to monitor security situation and trends; Communication plan and local acceptance; Quarterly physical inventory of warehouses; review of monthly consumption reports; Security Briefing for staff and partners.
