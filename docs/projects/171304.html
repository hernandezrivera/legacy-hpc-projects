Project Summary

This project will ensure the most vulnerable Palestinian children (boys and girls aged 12-17 years old) exposed to conflict-related violence are reached with protection, impact mitigation and prevention services in the West Bank. It will also foster increased respect for IHL and IHRL and accountability for violations against children through documenting and monitoring violations to inform advocacy and increase accountability.
This project is in line and contributes to the Protection Cluster objective that aim to ensure vulnerable Palestinians have access to protection services that mitigate the effects of occupation and conflict-related violence.
The main objective of this child protection project is for children in the West Bank to be safe, protected and secure from violence, exploitation and grave violations. The project will:
• support initiatives to engage vulnerable and at-risk children positively in structured recreational, educational, awareness-raising, life skills and resilience building activities to improve their resilience and coping mechanisms.
• support protective services and awareness-raising for at-risk children and families to inform children and families about their rights and where to seek assistance in case of violence, arrest and/or detention.
• support vulnerable children previously arrested and detained by Israeli Forces with integration and rehabilitation services especially structured counselling and MHPSS support.
• support capacities of families, community members and leaders to identify, prevent and respond to violence against children through families and community groups discussions, counselling and awareness raising sessions, and trainings.
• support incident monitoring, data analysis and reporting on grave violations trends related to child protection concerns in Israel/Occupied Palestinian Territory (the WB) to provide evidence and inform advocacy. Data collection and monitoring will be enhanced through documentation of testimonies of children in military detention.

1 - Needs

Owing to the COVID-19 pandemic, the resulting movement restrictions, school closures, job losses and socio-economic pressures have exacerbated vulnerabilities in the West Bank with increased reported incidences of negative coping mechanisms including domestic violence, child labour and child marriages. The impact of COVID-19 on the needs of children is confirmed by the 2020 Needs Identification and Analysis Framework (NIAF) which highlighted an increase by 40 percent in Gaza and 26 percent in the West Bank respectively, of services provided to children experiencing violence and abuse at home. According to NIAF, also the provision of services to children, mainly boys, engaged in child labour heightened by 52 percent in Gaza, and the significant decrease in reported cases of early marriage of girls suggests meaningful underreporting. In the oPt, a total of 12,934 Palestinian children were affected by all types of conflict-related grave violations between July 2019 and June 2020 (2021 HNO). While the conflict-related violence has an impact on both boys and girls, boys continue to be at a higher risk of being injured or killed. Since the declaration of a state of emergency related to COVID-19, the oPt has continued to witness violence during the COVID-19 period. Whereas the number of incidents resulting in killing and injuries of children has decreased since 2019, some 1,005 Palestinian injuries (among them 710 men, 10 women, 120 boys and 6 girls including 1 Israeli boy) and 13 killed (3 boys, 10 men) were reported between 5 March and 31 August 2020 in comparison to 1,105 injuries and 12 killed (3 boys, 9 men) from 1 January till early March 2020 prior to COVID-19 (OCHA Database). Many injured or previously injured children remain in need of continuing case management support, in some cases requiring previously closed cases to be re-opened. An urgent need has been identified for the scale-up of monitoring and documentation, to ensure data collection, verification and reporting and timely analysis to inform the humanitarian response. This was clear in light of the limited capacities of protection partners to conduct monitoring activities to the extent required in the context of the current violence and COVID-19 crisis. In the occupied West Bank, a non-permissive environment intensifies, driven by demolitions, forced evictions, discriminatory planning, access restrictions, settlement expansion and settler violence, generating a risk of forcible transfer for many Palestinians in Area C, East Jerusalem and the Israeli-controlled part of Hebron city, H2 Since the beginning of 2020, 4 Palestinian boys were killed by live ammunition and 342 children were injured during conflict-related incidents in the West Bank. From January 2020 to date 2,733 people including 1,401 children were affected by demolitions and displacement with 705 people displaced and 531 donor-funded structured demolished (OCHA database). Of particular concern are the vulnerable communities especially the Bedouin communities living in Area C and East Jerusalem in proximity and around the so-called settlement blocs in light of the threat of annexation in the West Bank and the Israeli settlement expansion plans in those areas. Between July 2019 and June 2020, an average of 181 Palestinian children (boys aged 14 to 17) were held in Israeli detention facilities for alleged security related offences on a monthly basis (Source: Israeli Prison Service). Detained children remain at high risk of violence, ill-treatment and due process violations during their arrest, transfer, interrogation and detention, affecting their psychosocial well-being even after release (NIAF 2020). Owing to prevalent gender and social norms, adolescent boys are the most affected, and when released, they are at risk of school dropout and/or mental health disorders.

2 - Activities

This intervention aims for children (boys and girls aged 12-17 years old) in oPt to be safe and protected from violence, exploitation and grave violations. The project’s main goal is to reduce vulnerabilities of children and families in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, and improve their resilience. It also aims to document Children affected by Armed Conflict (CAAC) incidents within the oPt/Israel as a 'Situation of Concern'. This will be achieved through: 1) Protecting children at-risk of conflict with Israeli Forces through coordinated community level prevention programmes and protective services in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem; 2) Responding to the needs of directly affected children and their families through provision of impact mitigation services and psychosocial support and 3) Documenting and reporting on CAAC incidents within the oPt/Israel as a "situation of concern". To address the increasing needs resulting from COVID-19, the intervention will prioritize children at risk of domestic violence, children with disabilities, children exposed to conflict related violence and children living under the poverty line. Partners will ensure provision of both face-to-face and remote services, especially for those unable to afford or access internet. In order to prevent increasing child protection concerns, the intervention will scale up awareness-raising activities on child protection, the impact of stigma, social exclusion and bullying caused by COVID-19. UNICEF and partners will consult children to review existing feedback mechanisms including accessibility, and then process their feedback, and complaints in line with UNICEF Operational Guidance on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP). Acknowledging the different needs of boys and girls and thus the need to design interventions accordingly, gender will be mainstreamed throughout the programme cycle; during implementation and monitoring. The project will seek to achieve the following outputs and activities: Output 1: Support vulnerable children and their families to access prevention and protection services to improve their resilience and coping mechanisms in the worst affected areas in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem. (Target: 1,050 children and 1,400 adults); Activity 1.1 Engage vulnerable and at-risk children positively in structured recreational, educational, awareness-raising, life skills and resilience building activities to improve their resilience and coping mechanisms.Activity 1.2 Raise public awareness and commitment of community members on children’s right to protection, the impact of stigma, social exclusion and bullying caused by COVID-19 through community outreach materials, social media campaigns and competitions.Activity 1.3 Provide children previously arrested and detained by Israeli Forces and their families with integration and rehabilitation services especially structured counselling and mental health and psychosocial support.Activity 1.4 Strengthen capacities of families, community members and leaders to identify, prevent and respond to violence against children through families and community groups discussions, counselling and awareness raising sessions, and trainings. Output 2: Support incident monitoring, data analysis, documentation, monitoring and reporting on grave violations trends related to child protection concerns in Israel/State of Palestine (WB including East Jerusalem) to provide evidence and inform advocacy. (Target: 24 children and 48 adults); Activity 2.1 Ensure partners have capacities to monitor and document of violations against children (including collection of testimonies of children), verification and legal redress of cases of children arrested, detained, indicted and sentenced.Activity 2.2 Collect testimonies of children's experiences in military detention.Activity 2.3 Support in house internal capacity for documenting, reporting and advocacy.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

This project aims to engage with diverse community members on child protection concerns both in relation to COVID-19 protection concerns and to the VAC agenda. This engagement will include community groups discussions, awareness raising sessions, social media campaigns etc. with the appropriate measures including personal protective equipment (PPE) put in place to mitigate the spread of COVID-19 and respect current restrictions. In all of these activities, UNICEF and its partners will ensure a diverse participation of community members including girls and boy, women and men, adolescents, the elderly and people with disabilities. Participation of children and their parents/caregivers in these activities will be voluntary and based upon their informed consent/assent. Global guidance such as the Child Protection Minimum Standards (CPMS) and UNICEF’s internal policies on informed consent/assent, confidentiality, data protection and information sharing will be applied in accordance with the best interest of the child principle. UNICEF and partners will determine whether informed consent/assent is legally required or expected of a parent/caregiver. They will also determine from whom informed consent/assent is legally required in cases of child-headed households or children in institutions. They will ensure that consent is given in a form and language that reflects the capacity of the child. Data confidentiality and data protection will be ensured through all phases of collection, transfer, storage and communication. These procedures will guide UNICEF and its implementing partners interaction with children and their parents/caregivers as well as between UNICEF and the implementing partners. UNICEF will also ensure that its own staff adhere to its child safeguarding policy and that the selected implementing partners have such a framework put in place. Children with disabilities (CWDs) will be included in the various phases of the project in line with the Do No Harm principle. Also, children will be engaged in ways that are appropriate to their level of capacities taking into account power imbalances that may increase risk of vulnerability for CWDs. Children will be listened to as a first step to enhance their participation in the decision-making processes, the design and development of activities, and the implementation of the evaluation of impact of the intervention. CWDs will have sufficient spaces to contribute to forums/discussions the same way other children are able to take part in. They will also have opportunities to challenge some decisions that are made by adults in the communities where they live and which impact their access to quality child protection services. Gaps in formal services provision will be addressed by partnering with local organizations of people with disabilities or community-based programmes.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project will be informed by the United Nations Response Strategies that highlight the needs of children in oPt: the findings and recommendations of the East Jerusalem Action Plan, the Evaluation of EJ Action Plan and the consultations conducted by UNICEF with key stakeholders. In addition, UNICEF and its implementing partners will involve children, adolescents and caregivers by conducting virtual focus group discussions, and community outreach activities. Children will be consulted to review existing feedback mechanisms, to express their views and provide suggestions on how to make those mechanisms more accessible. Upon collection and receipt of complaints, responses and feedbacks, UNICEF and partners will process the feedback, responses and complaints in line with UNICEF Operational Guidance on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) developed in the oPt.
