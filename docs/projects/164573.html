Project Summary

This planned programme provides streamlined lifesaving, humanitarian multi-sectoral assistance and strengthening the basic infrastructure support in IDP sites, as well as disseminates operational information on movements of IDPs and returns on a timely basis.
The programme is targeting North Syria camps which have been extending the capacities due to the limited spaces available vs. huge figures of displaced people. Through coordination with the other clusters, QRC CCCM teams suggest providing multi sectorial lifesaving aids through sheltering IDPs who need tents, NFIs, food, hygiene, and sanitation services. Winterization of tents (internal and external insulation) in the existing IDP settlements before the next winter is a very high priority in the absence of alternative shelter solutions and scarcity of heating fuel. The programme also considers directly delivering aid through improving the camps infrastructure (roads gravelling) in order to ease access to main facilities within camps. This suggested proposal proposes implementing livelihood activities within camps to support those residing in camps.
The programme will seek feedback from both male and female beneficiaries and fully interact with both genders to ensure equitable access and equal opportunities. Recruiting and training equal numbers of men and women for registration team. The programme ensures that the service is equally accessible and responsive to women, girls, boys and men.

4 - Needs

As of 29 January 2020 UNOCHA, situation report about the recent developments in Northwest Syria, since 1 December 2019, nearly 390,000 people have fled from their homes, mainly from southern Idleb and western Aleppo governorates, mostly escaping hostilities by moving to urban centers and IDP camps in northwest Idleb. Tens of thousands of others have reportedly moved to areas such as Afrin, Azaz and Al-Bab in northern Aleppo governorate. Most of the people displaced in northwest Syria since 15 January have been displaced multiple times before, with each new displacement introducing new risks and intensifying existing vulnerabilities. Winter conditions are compounding the vulnerability of displaced people. Many who fled are in urgent need of humanitarian support, particularly shelter, food, health, non-food and winterization assistance. As per CCCM snapshot on 02/02/2020, there are more than 586,000 displacements since the first of December, of them about 116,000 live in camps. According to QRC field reports and updates NW Syria camps are overloaded with those IDPs who suffer from the lack of services and infrastructure deteriorated situations. This is common due to huge figures within those camps and the IDPs are suffering from accessing the camps facilities accordingly.

5 - Implementation of activities

The programme is involving lifesaving activities to enhance the lives and protect those IDPs based on needs and prioritizing the most vulnerable groups as the following: 1.1 Assessing the camps in Idleb and Aleppo, 1.2 Graveling of internal and some access pathways and roads in camps including appropriate rain drainage system. 1.3 Helping the women and children who are continuously using the improved roads. 1.4 Monitoring, evaluation and reporting. 1.5 Providing the unified operational data on IDPs movements that is done with the CCCM cluster on monthly basis. 1.6 Staff capacity improving aboutthe CCCM tracking reports template. 1.7 Providing the IDPs tracking report monthly and shared with the cluster. 1.8 Establishment Camp Management system 1.9 Forming in camp committees 1.10 CCCM training, 1.11 Contingency planning (fire fighting system, evacuation plan, etc) 1.12 Coordination and monitoring services providedThe project will seek feedback from both male and female beneficiaries and fully interact with both genders to ensure equitable access and equal opportunities. Recruiting and training equal numbers of men and women for registration team. The project ensure that the service is equally accessible and responsive to women, girls, boys and men.

QRC is committed towards mainstreaming protection principles into all of our projects and do so through the use of several mechanisms.Protection Risk1: Risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment) of women and girls. Likelihood: High Mitigation: 1. Establish a confidential complaint mechanism (Publishing its phone number and email) 2. Train staff on code of conduct 3. Incorporate strong consideration for protecting the rights of the beneficiaries and maintaining their dignity, 6. Committing towards mainstreaming protection, capacity building of all staff on protection and mainstreaming. Monitoring: Monitoring staff, M&R report. Protection Risk 2: Beneficiaries might be exposed to risks if attending badly planned, organized and conducted implementation in connection with poor safety and security planning and/or lack of adequate consideration for vulnerable groups. Likelihood: Medium, Mitigation: 1. Carry out thorough safety assessments on the areas where the sites are planned, 2. Ensure that the programme services are completed in time for beneficiaries to reach their shelter/ tents during daylight, 3.Consider specific risks to women and seek community opinion on how to best avoid and mitigate risk, 4.Communicate intensively with national and local authorities, stakeholders and community leaders. Monitoring: as Risk 2, plus the following: 1. Monitor the time of daylight and assess the distance of travel for beneficiaries, 2. ON-SITE implementation monitoring, 3. PDM, 4.
