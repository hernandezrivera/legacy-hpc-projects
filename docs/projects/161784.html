Project Summary

Since the beginning of the humanitarian crisis in North-East Nigeria, affected populations have been suffering from a continued deterioration of living conditions, a disruption of pre-existing protective mechanisms and a lack of access to essential services. They have accumulated protracted stress and experienced extreme violence, and loss of family members, social connections, and property. Displaced populations face grave protection risks and abuse including different forms of gender-based violence (GBV), exploitation, family separation, restrictions on freedom of movement and insecurity. These vulnerabilities are exacerbated by pre-existing and emergency-induced problems faced by the affected population. This is particularly true in terms of the most vulnerable sub-groups of the population, including the elderly and chronically ill, persons with disabilities, female and child-headed households, unaccompanied and/or separated children, adolescent boys and girls, pregnant and/or lactating women and people returning from captivity.
Under this project, activities will focus on Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) and stand-alone protection interventions through MHPSS teams and resource centres/safe spaces, capacity building for humanitarian actors and GBV risk mitigation. IOM’s MHPSS Mobile Teams and safe spaces will implement GBV prevention and response services in camp and camp-like settings.

1 - Needs

Ten years into the humanitarian crisis in north-east Nigeria, conflict-affected populations’ living conditions continue to deteriorate in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe states. The recent intensification of attacks by non-state armed groups (NSAGs), most notably Boko Haram, has triggered new waves of mass displacement, increasing the total number of internally displaced persons (IDPs) to nearly 2 million, of whom 80 percent are women and children . Despite that cases GBV and other forms of abuse often remain unreported due to fear of retaliation or stigmatization, a recent study found that six in ten women and girls in north-east Nigeria have experienced at least one of the above harmful acts. This includes women and girls who have been abducted by NSAGs who often endure rape and forced marriage during captivity and face stigmatization and possible rejection from the community upon their release. The conflict affected population is facing additional stressors created by the displacement itself, such as family separation, difficulties and sometimes denial of access to services, limited opportunities to livelihood, breakdown of family and community support mechanisms, potential stigmatization, secondary and repeated displacement. These factors can contribute to increasing risks of exposure to GBV and TIP. Within the IDP camps there are serious protection and GBV risks exposure also includes exposure to sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), forced early marriage, recruitment and use of children and child labour. Boys and men are at risk of forced recruitments by armed actors and at risk of arbitrary detention and exposure to different forms of violence, yet the life-saving services are not readily available in some areas. Increased access to empowerment and livelihood will mitigate the occurrence of survival sex.Gender dimensions of the crisis are not effectively captured to understand the differentiated needs of the returned abductees; depending on their roles in captivity. The types of GBV that are experienced by the survivors are classified as separate harmful acts. The emphasis is on the harmful act only rather than the perpetrator or the context in which it was perpetrated leaving out response gaps to these experiences. More needs to be done to ensure a human rights/survivor based, and gender age sensitive approach, an approach that responds to the tailored needs of women, girls, men and boys as different groups with specific needs. The provision of adequate support and specialized and comprehensive service is essential. Challenges of the capacity to respond also relate to the lack of specialized staff to deal with the experiences of survivors and women/girls in captivity who can offer healing messages and this result in some of the survivors living with complex medical and psychological conditions as they fear disclosure and maltreatment.

2 - Activities

1. Deploy MHPSS teams who will provide community-based MHPSS by supporting GBV survivors (including new arrivals) through individual and group counseling, facilitation of the establishment of peer-to peer support groups, psychological First Aid (PFA) and by facilitating referral to specialized services, such as psychiatric care, access to justice, safety and security mechanisms.2. Develop responses to address the immediate needs of GBV survivors/women and girls formerly associated with armed groups & forces/victims of trafficking and make appropriate referrals to access immediate emergency response.3. Provision of PFA and material support for at-risk individuals and survivors 4. Establishment, maintenance and running resource centers 5. Provision of re/integration assistance through vocational training and livelihoods activities integrated with MHPSS. 6. Build the capacity of humanitarian actors on how to make safe referrals for GBV, TiP, SEA survivors including sensitization on the services available using existing referral pathways.7. Conduct roll out trainings for the Nigerian Police Force and Nigerian Civil Defence Corps on GBV, TiP prevention, response and community engagement using the already existing training manuals.8. Provide training to leaders of community- based women’s committees. 9. Carry out GBV/Counter Trafficking/SEA sensitization activities targeting vulnerable populations of host and IDP communities, with increased focus on engaging men and boys. 10. Through the safe spaces IOM will engage adolescent girls in structured discussion around self-confidence, problem solving, information on available services, dangers of early marriage, importance of girl child education and share information that aim to progressively contribute to positive changes in the community.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

During the design of the project and throughout the planning stages, IOM will promote active participation and feedback from the targeted population in assessing their needs and specific vulnerabilities, including those of women, youth, elderly, and persons with disabilities, among others. Focus group discussions with IDP representatives, Persons With Specific Needs such as disabled, child headed households, pregnant and lactating women as well as other vulnerable groups will ensure the community is fully involved in the decision making process. IOM`s feedback (and complaints) mechanism is managed by the IOM Camp Coordination and Camp Management Unit.
