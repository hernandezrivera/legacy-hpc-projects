Project Summary

"Plan International's project in Cox’s Bazar seamlessly aligns with the Rationalization Principles, particularly emphasizing Ref. #3 - Adopt an integrated approach, and Ref. #4 - Ensure predictability and continuity. Through an integrated approach, the project comprehensively tackles multifaceted challenges faced by impoverished households in the host community and Rohingya camps.
To ensure predictability and continuity, Plan International has established sustainable frameworks. Temporary training centers and partnerships with existing facilities contribute to the project's flexibility and adaptability. Aligning with the Bangladesh Technical Education Board's curriculum enhances the standardization and longevity of the training, ensuring that acquired skills remain relevant in the evolving job market. Connection with MFIs provide ongoing support for entrepreneurship, fostering economic sustainability. PIB's working structure in the host community facilitates vocational and technical training and networking with job providers to connect trained-up youth for suitable employment.
This livelihood and skill development project will be implemented at ukhia and teknaf upazila in host community and camp 21,22,24,25 and 27 for Rohingya community. PIB will provide non-technical training on different non-traditional and market driven trades selected and endorsed by the LSDS sectors for both host and Rohingya community youths through gender transformative approaches. The total beneficiaries will be 65 for AAL and 120 for non-formal technical training in the camp and 200 for technical training and strat up support in the host community.PIB will emphasise on most vulnerable households like female headed, old aged, widow, separated women, pregnant and lacktating mother, persons with disability, ethnicity and other excluded community while selecting participants. In support of LSDs interventions, PIB will initiative accelerate adult learning opportunities for the illiterate or semi-literate selected participants selected for LSDs interventions. This accelerate adult learning opportunities will specially focus on functional literacy and numeracy skills in Burmese language.
Special attention will be given in Persons with Disability selection and the Washington Group questionnaire will be utilized. Identified persons with disabilities will be engaged in awareness program, trade based training ensuring meaningfull participation that is training center facilities, special accomodation, input supports and other tailored interventions deriving through separate budget required by them. After completion of the training they will be engaged in suitable trades both in self and wage employement. The project will initially identify suitable beneficiaries requiring business start-up support for small-scale enterprises and job creation. PIB will continue business startup support to strengthen their assigned businesses as entrepreneurs and enhance their job performance.
Plan International Bangladesh has the tools for the protection of the target people from any harmful situation such as risks assessment before organizing any event as well as mitigation the identified risks. Plan International always take immediate attention for prevention, mitigation and responding any child protection issues. PIB will Mobilize youth to fight harmful practices such as child marriage and Identify the barrier of GBV and find out the way forward for execution accordingly.
By embracing these Rationalization Principles, Plan International's project not only addresses immediate skill development needs but also establishes a foundation for long-term, predictable, and integrated solutions . This empowers individuals in both host communities and Rohingya camps, fostering sustainable development and resilience."

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

"The job market assessment for Ukhiya and Teknaf, referencing the Bangladesh Bureau of Educational Information and Statistics (BANBEIS), highlights a significant dropout of adolescent boys and girls. The absence of a Technical Training Center (TTC) in Teknaf and the emphasis on technical skills education in the 8th Five Year Plan (8 FYP) create a gap for youth employability. Many youths in Teknaf are inclined to go abroad due to this dearth of skills.The lack of a standard BMET facility in Teknaf results in youth joining low-paid informal work due to insufficient technical skills. Recognizing the demand for skilled workers locally, nationally, and abroad, the project focuses on providing market-relevant skills to girls, boys, and young women for dignified work. The Joint Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (JMSNA) reveals that only 42% of households in the host community can access employment, and 29% can work on electricity, solar lamps, and battery maintenance.The J-MSNA highlights that 29% of host community households and 53% of refugee camp households lack nearby income and livelihood opportunities due to limited income-generating options in the area. Limited data on Rohingya youth engagement in education exposes them to exploitation, protection risks, and negative coping mechanisms. The Camps have made strides in recruiting female teachers, but a majority are Bangladeshi, posing challenges in delivering education services.According to the J-MSNA 2023, the most commonly reported income and cash inflow sources for households over the last 30 days prior to data collection were humanitarian assistance (92%), income from casual labour (48%), and loans or support from family/friends (not including remittances, 48%). The reported median household income was 3,000 BDT per month (28 USD) while from other cash inflow sources they reported 6,400 BDT monthly (59 USD), of which the majority came from humanitarian assistance (e-voucher) (4,300 BDT or 40 USD). The median reported income for households with a female head of household was 0 BDT while male headed households reported an income of 3,500 BDT (32 USD). Overall, households reported monthly median expenses of 7,480 BDT (69 USD), which meant that their frequent expenses surpassed the humanitarian assistance receives, households needing to find alternative sources of income or cash inflows.Lack of inclusion has caused gender diverse populations, such as Hijras, to be left behind without addressing their needs. There is a gap in understanding theRohingya patriarchal cultural context among humanitarian actors. Thus, the advancement of economic empowerment of women eventually met with considerablebacklash in the community from men.To address these gaps, there's a need for NTVQF-level 1 training for the host community and non-formal training for Rohingya individuals, focusing on essential soft skills. The utilization of the Washington Group questionnaire is imperative for participant selection. This comprehensive approach seeks to bridge skill gaps, create employment opportunities, and empower both host communities and Rohingya individuals.

2.3 - Age & Disability

The project targets people from mostly 18-24 years age group and will follow the inclusion & diversity principles to include marginalized and excluded people in collaboration with the Department of Social Services, Camp in Charges, and Site Management sector. It is expected that 8% of the targeted beneficiaries will be persons with disability. Besides, women-headed households and households with more persons with disability will get priority. During beneficiary selection, project staff will explore persons with disability through Washington Group Questionaries, HH visits and verification by PWD-specialization organization.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

AAP: Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) is an active commitment by Plan International Bangladesh. Plan International inform people in the camps and the host community about their rights and entitlements, and participation in decisions that affect them. The courtyard session is a platform in the project where the target people express their opinion which has been included in the design of the project. Income opportunities strengthen the youth, especially girls and young women and creates space for control over resources. Awareness raising on disaster preparedness is part of the project. The project enhance the access to services from the government offices for better resilience and self-relience. In addition, PIB is maintaining a Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) through suggesion box, Field visit,Focus group discussions, Community meetings, Child Safeguarding reporting structure, Via SMS, Feedback Survey Question, Feedback Form, Via Phone Call etc. This all are the way of making a complaint or raising any protection and PSEA issues and PIB has a strong policy guideline on safegurding and PSEA. PIB will maintain confidential Reporting: Safe, confidential channels for reporting sensitive issues, especially GBV and timely Response: Protocols for prompt response to feedback and protection issues. PIB is following a pathway to reffer any protection issues to the case management team of PIB and other relevant service providers and following up of the case. project paricipants aged 18-24 will be oriented or trained on PSEA, safegurding and maintaining of CFRM which will increase the access of project participants in the whole CFRM process.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Considering the heightened climate risk, the project will emphasize on off-farm livelihood activities and trade based skill development which will be Climate adaptive. The project will also include some on-farm livelihood opportunity and enterprises where climate smart agriculture technologies will introduce and a contextualized Climate Risk Insurance (CRI) package for on-farm enterprise beneficiaries as a tool to protect them from sudden climatic shocks. As a new concept, CRI will be initiated with learnings captured regularly to suggest improvements for future programming. To strengthen disaster readiness in the local communities, the project will emphasize strengthening early warning systems and forecast-based early action. All these efforts are expected to strengthen disaster readiness and climate change resilience at the community level.
