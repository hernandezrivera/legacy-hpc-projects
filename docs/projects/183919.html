Project Summary

In response to the need for accurate information on displacement and the identification of beneficiaries for the delivery of humanitarian aid, the International Organization for Migration (IOM) began its Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) program in 2004 to support the Government of Sudan (GoS) and its humanitarian partners in establishing an accurate data collection system and providing data on displacement in areas most affected by conflict and natural disasters. IOM has been working in close collaboration with the Humanitarian Aid Commission (HAC) and partners on the field to establish a unified and systematic method to collect individual data on internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees in seven states. Since 2004, DTM Sudan has provided rapid emergency registrations with a priority of meeting immediate information needs for guiding direct assistance to vulnerable people. In 2010, DTM expanded to include biometric registrations and data verifications – implemented in response to government or humanitarian partner requests.
Registration activities were used to collect data according to selected characteristics of displaced individuals and their family members. The information gathered was to build a list of all individuals present in one location at a point in time (updated beneficiary lists) and provide details of their characteristics including demographics information, vulnerabilities, and identify their needs for food, shelter, livelihoods, education, etc. Population groups covered in the past were IDPs and returning IDPs, but the exercises can
be expanded, and the methodology adapted to include other target groups such as Sudanese returnees from abroad and third-country nationals.
Registration data is captured by DTM field teams through direct individual interviews in the target locations, usually with the heads of households. All enumerators are trained on conducting interviews and engaging with female headed households. Additionally, throughout all activities, DTM team secures gender-responsive data collection by establishing gender mixed field teams, collecting sex-disaggregated data, and capturing other forms of social difference (wealth status, age, ethnicity, etc.). Registration data is shared with partners by means of data sharing agreements and used for beneficiary selection, vulnerability targeting and programming.

1 - Needs

Currently, DTM in Sudan provides rapid emergency registrations, biometric registrations and data verifications to meet the immediate information needs for beneficiary selection, vulnerability targeting and programming. Registration has been conducted in over 1,500 locations across Sudan starting in 2004, and it is activated upon request by the Humanitarian Aid Commission (HAC), one of IOM’s humanitarian partners, or in response to IOM’s internal programmatic needs. Data sharing agreements need to be signed prior to registration with all entities requesting individual and non-anonymized records for assistance purposes. DTM will continue to register affected populations based upon requests and its use for direct assistance.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Main Output: individual / nominal list of affected populations is disseminated to specific humanitarian agencies and sectors to inform direct response. Activities 1. Registration and veriﬁcation of IDPs and returnees. 2. Update the existing database to include all the collected information and train staff in data entry, database management and how to generate information. 3. Trainings and refreshers on standard DTM tools and protection practices for IOM enumerators. 4. Undertake information campaigns and sensitization meetings on the aim and modalities of DTM prior to data collection exercises. 5. Data andinformation sharing: datasets will be shared based on valid data sharing agreements and quarterly reports will be shared with the Government of Sudan, humanitarian actors and relevant stakeholders/partners considering sensitivity of information and sharing as appropriate in the public domain or bilaterally.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Registration activities are conducted to collect data to deliver humanitarian and other short and medium-term assistance to IDPs, returnees and affected populations. In cases of protracted displacement, registration can inform long-term assistance and response planning. IOM has been conducting registration activities in Sudan since 2004 and has a well-established methodology that ensures the protection of IDPs and returnees during registration activities. Activities are conducted with a suitable security presence where necessary, in open areas and in daylight. Sensitization campaigns prepare the data collection, and informed consent is sought prior to the start of the interviews.DTM has been active in Sudan for a long time, and it is aware that humanitarian partners from different sectors rely heavily on the information it provides on vulnerability. However, to ensure the protection of beneficiaries and target populations, data is only available upon request, de-anonymized and in line with the data protection principles, based on vetted data-sharing agreements.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

IOM has recently conducted PSEA training for all IOM staff, including those who will be carrying out the activities under this project. The DTM teams support communities and their representatives by holding discussion sessions so that beneficiaries could ask questions or raise issues of concern with the team directly or through the complaint desks set up during every registration/ verification exercise, as well as an email complaint mechanism. DTM teams utilize community feedback to enhance data collection tools by adding relevant indicators to refine registration and verification activities. During the registration and verification exercises priority will be given to most vulnerable people (such as the disabled, elderly, pregnant and / or lactating women, female headed households) to ensure that they are registered and verified without delay. Additionally, throughout all activities, DTM team secures gender-responsive data collection, by establishing gender mixed field teams, collecting sex-disaggregated data, and capturing other forms of social difference (wealth status, age, ethnicity, etc.). IOM will also provide water, shade, and sanitation facilities to ensure that the beneficiaries are comfortable while the exercises are ongoing. To mitigate the transmission of COVID-19, DTM teams will use Personal Protection Equipment (PPE), implement crowd control measures, and maintain physical distancing measures based on the national guidelines during registration / verification activities. Data so far has only been used for the delivery of humanitarian assistance. However, the data can also be used for longer term planning and as a baseline for in-depth studies that monitor the progressive resolution of displacement situations.
