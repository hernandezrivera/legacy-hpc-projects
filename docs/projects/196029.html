Project Summary

The proposed action seeks to increase the access and quality of education and PSS services in target areas by implementing a combination of formal (non-accredited) and non-formal education activities that complement each other where possible. PIN takes an integrated and holistic approach to address the Education in Emergencies (EiE) and Child Protection needs. PIN will employ a dual approach to supporting education through establishing temporary learning centres (TLCs) for provision of PSS and non-formal education using the Self-Learning Programme (SLP) curriculum in areas with acute concentrations of OOSC – including in IDP camps and informal settlements; and assistance to non-accredited schools where education has been disrupted through providing construction and repair works, school furniture and equipment, teaching and learning materials, support for running costs, PSS sessions will be provided to all enrolled students and teachers as its been integrated to schools regular timetables. Additionally, provision of after-school catch-up and remedial classes to support pathways into education and reduce drop-out. Specific consideration will be given to vulnerable households with OOSC or children at risk of education disruption, who will be provided with psychosocial support and pathways for (re)integration into education as well as with opportunities for caregivers to support their HHs through Cash for Work (CfW) modalities. PIN takes an evidence-based approach to programming based on rigorous feedback loops to promote accountability and enhance impact. All activities will be implemented in line with relevant Education cluster tools, materials, and approaches, in addition to core INEE and Sphere standards, with a focus upon promoting safe learning spaces and essential WASH facilities and maximizing accessibility for those at risk of education disruption, including girls and children living with disabilities. Teaching and student kits will be provided to facilitate quality learning opportunities and minimize education barriers for children from low-income HH. Additionally, PIN will address the recent Cholera outbreak by supporting schools and the surrounding communities with preparedness through child-friendly and caregiver awareness sessions, training for teachers, school management and cleaners, IEC materials and water testing. Geographical targeting will occur through further in-depth needs assessments in areas pre-identified in cooperation with the Education Cluster. These will determine schools and communities most in need of educational and child protection support. The process will ensure the participation of relevant stakeholders to consider local priorities while also ensuring that transparency is at the forefront and that PIN is accountable to the populations in its areas of intervention. Assessments will be conducted by PIN staff and engineers in collaboration with local authorities, where relevant. PIN will target communities where education services have been disrupted due to conflict, where there is no, or limited, access to education. Experience highlights that significant gaps also exist in such areas to meet teachers’ and students’ needs for safe and quality learning. There will be a strong focus on community outreach and engagement throughout the entire cycle, including caregivers. All activities have the final aim of reintegrating children into education through formal and non-formal accelerated learning programmes and the creation of child protection safety nets. The locations were identified through needs assessment targeting the vulnerable communities with lack of education services, please check the needs assessment section. PIN is providing a holistic approach by providing access to Education the learning enters and providing numeracy and literacy condensed courses.

3 - Needs

Based on HNO 2023, the education system in Syria remains overstretched. There were a total of 25 attacks on educational facilities in 2022. 6.9M Syrians are in need of educational services with 97% being children. HNO assessments reveal that 12% of children are out of school. This assessment confirms the need for comprehensive educational support, especially the establishment of learning spaces and provision of learning materials, and training and incentives for qualified teachers. As in the previous years, the number of children attending school is the lowest for the IDP population in sites/camps. So far, PIN has addressed these needs by beginning the process of setting up and repairing learning centers in communities were high numbers of children are out of school, conducting teacher trainings and promoting PSS activities. PIN is also preparing to conduct parenting sessions to promote awareness among caregivers. Additionally, in order to respond to the Cholera outbreak PIN has began supporting schools and the surrounding communities with preparedness through child-friendly and caregiver awareness sessions, training for teachers, school management and cleaners, IEC materials and water testing. PIN will continue this activity into 2023.

4 - Implementation of activities

Schools and Temporary Learning Centres (TLCs) will be supported and established, providing non-formal and formal education with integrated PSS activities. Trained school teachers and facilitators provide teaching for classes with support from PIN and identify cases with protection concerns and physical disabilities to be referred to external service provider. Activities foreseen include setting up non-formal learning centers, construction work and light rehabilitation for schools and classrooms, monthly operational costs, and school supplies including teaching and learning materials for formal and non-formal settings. PIN will provide quality physical and online classes when needed, distributing essential items for quality learning and ensure safe access to integrated PSS activities. PIN will also run mobile PSS activities to reach children who can't access TLCs. In 2023, PIN will start implementing community PSS intervention for adolescent and will also invest more in empowering students’ caregivers and promoting awareness through conducting parenting sessions. Learning outcomes will be monitored by ASER tests. Learning outcomes will be measured by PIN's monitoring and evaluation team by ASER tests. The ‘Annual Status of Education Report' (ASER) tools are internationally recognized as a reliable tool to measure reading fluency and comprehension as well as arithmetic and basic numeracy. PIN coordinates with other clusters to make sure assistance is not duplicated.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

PIN’s protection mainstreaming strategy carries out regular protection risk assessments. These protection risk assessments feed into the development of protection risk and mitigation matrices. The following risks (R) and respective mitigation measures (MM) were identified:1/R: Specific vulnerable groups are excluded from accessing education;1/MM: accessibility for males and females and children with disabilities; Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) mechanism, conducting consultations and FGDs to understand barriers related to access and safety (such as distance), organizing community and awareness-raising events, referral of children with mobility and non-mobility related disabilities to specialized actors; Conflict Sensitive teacher training (disability sensitive classroom for smooth integration of children with disabilities with their peers and reduction of the negative social effects of their injuries, such as stigma and bullying. 2/R: Additional threats (child labor, early marriage, poverty and economic situation) 2/MM: targeting communities with different types of assistance; to minimize education barriers; PIN aims to integrate Cash for work activity to eliminate some financial barriers. PIN’s referral system community awareness sessions. 3/R: Behavior of parents and education personnel threatening children’s wellbeing. 3/MM: signature of Code of Conduct; Child-friendly CFRM workshops are available in schools; training to all staff CP policy.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

PSEA considerations are integral part of PIN protection mainstreaming strategy. In all programming, PIN takes seriously all complaints and has in place rigorous Community Feedback and Response Mechanism, which is accessible to all stakeholders including beneficiaries. All feedback and complaints are being carefully considered, collected and responded to. Besides other concerns, any possible sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment (SEAH) in line with its Investigation guidelines for Syria and Case Management-Survivor Centre Approach are thoroughly investigated. Violations of PIN's Code of Conduct and other key policies, including Child Protection, Safeguarding Policy are not tolerated. All staff is trained in protection mainstreaming. PIN's Code of Conduct is signed by all PIN staff, local authorities, daily workers, and other stakeholders involved in PIN projects. Protection risks and mitigations matrix is developed for all the activities implemented by PIN in order to ensure that PIN has well-thought identification of possible gaps and standardize the precautionary and preventive measures required.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

(A1/R1): Security situation in target areas remains conducive to implementation;(MM1): Suspend/relocate activities to areas where access is upheld; and/or switch to remote management, potentially through pre-identified local partners. (A2/R2) PIN's staff, offices and warehouses in Syria are subjected to targeting/abductions/raiding by armed actors. (MM3): rigorous security monitoring and management mechanism. If specified risks occur, PIN temporarily suspends activities; conduct internal/external investigations; advocate for dialogue with actors, inform counter-terrorism agencies/authorities/partners of incidents; and seek to relocate the intervention;(A3/R3): LCs/others continue to collaborate in line with principles. (MM3): Temporarily suspend activities; engage in dialogue to re-sensitize on principles of engagement. If such action fails, PIN relocates resources to other areas. (A4/R4): Armed actors present in areas of operation facilitates safe humanitarian access; (MM4): Suspend operations, advocacy with local authorities and armed actors (jointly with UNOCHA, NGO forums, Working Groups) to restore free and unhindered access. If such efforts were unsuccessful, relocate activities to areas facing acute needs; (A5/R5): Local markets remain functional; (MM5): If border crossings to Syria were closed/restricted or PIN was prevented from transferring goods, advocate with UNOCHA/local authorities to facilitate access; purchase locally if possible; shift to Cash program.
