Project Summary

This project is designed to respond to the urgent need of the targeted group in relation to the poor sanitation in Al-Karama School as well as putting on track the Wadi Al Arroub Wastewater treatment plant. This is necessary to avoid the collapse of the treatment process and therefore will cause catastrophic results to both human and environment of citizen and areas downstream. This project will result in reducing the vulnerability of these people who are at environmental and health risk resulting from the flooded of the untreated wastewater through providing adequate safe sanitation and hygiene services and facilities to 7,232 citizens (1269 young girls, 2463 women, 844 young boys, and 2656 men).
More specifically, the project will seek to rehabilitate the WASH basic services of the Al-Karama School and reconstruct the pipeline of the school to the main line of the sewerage system and Wadi Al Arub Wastewater treatment plant (WWTP). In addition to rehabilitation of the treatment lines of the Wadi Al Arub WWTP that is currently running with its half capacity. So that, the 60 % of the citizens are exposed to polluted environment which adversely affects their health and their livelihood.
The agricultural lands at the targeted area cover approximately 40 % from the total land. These lands are planted with different kinds of trees by the farmers which represent one of the main source of income for the targeted population. The untreated wastewater that will flood in all these lands at Wadi Al Arub will put the agricultural lands at risk through soil and crops contamination, and will have a negative health impact on the farmers, people and particularly the children. This is a result from direct exposure to pathogens in the untreated wastewater as well as indirect through the food borne outbreaks from the contaminated products.
Therefore, the project will solve the problem of the collapse of the overloaded waste water treatment plant which in turn will prevent the agricultural lands from being polluted by the untreated wastewater. This in addition to reduce the health adverse risk from the contaminated food products.
Children and women population especially benefit from the improved sanitation and hygiene services with respect to their specific needs which are vital for their health, development and social well-being. Improving access to sanitation is a critical step towards reducing the impact of the disease. It also helps create physical environment that enhance safety, dignity and self-esteem. Therefore, the project will also contribute to protect the health of Girls students at Al Karama School through access to improved sanitation and hygiene practices in the targeted area. The project activities will pays special attention to hygiene education through giving the Girls students important roles in disseminating hygiene messages between themselves and the other school mates at the targeted area.
This project will be implemented by the Applied Research Institute, Jerusalem (ARIJ), and in coordination with the Sair municipality, and Ministry of Local government (MoLG) and Palestinian Water Authority (PWA) to reap the benefits of their input and experience in the field. This project is considered an urgent humanitarian project and part of ARIJ Response Plan, and in line with WASH Cluster HNO 2020 and the PWA plans.

1 - Needs

Inadequate sanitation and hygiene conditions can be a significant carrier of diseases especially among young children. According to the most of the international policy environment reflects the issue of providing adequate levels of WASH in schools is direct relevance to the UN SDGs of achieving especially those on education and gender. While significant progress has been achieved in the West Bank, large percentages of schools especially in Hebron Governorate (71%) remain without access to adequate and equitable sanitation and hygiene. Several assessments of the WASH needs in this particular area especially the school were conducted in year 2017 and 2018. In addition, ARIJ conducted a rapid assessment during year 2019 to update the information revealed in previous assessment. ARIJ team utilized several methods including field survey, technical assessment, focus groups meetings with relevant stakeholders. In addition, several meetings and semi-structured were conducted with the citizens. This participatory approach method was used to analyse the current challenges, needs and concerns. The need assessments results show that there is an urgent, humanitarian need in the targeted area which is suffering from the absence of adequate sanitation. This situation is not only damaging the environment but also it comes into direct and indirect contact with the people living in the surrounding area with consequent damage to health. Al Karama Secondary School located close to WWTP with 232 girls students (ages 5 - 15) is witnessing a deterioration of the basic WASH facilities including the connection to the sewerage main line, drinking water points, toilets and hand washing facilities. Bad infrastructure of WASH basic services is resulting unsafe place where diseases are transmitted. Moreover, the targeted school is suffering from the flooded wastewater as a result of the deterioration of the pipeline that connect the school with the sewerage main line. Also, the school has never received hygiene education. Also, there is also an urgent need for hygiene education and a need for the rehabilitation of WASH basic services, which have become dangerously poor and old. Having an incomplete infrastructure of Wadi Al Arubb WWTP with only half design capacity operating, is currently resulting in overloaded system and thus incapable of guaranteeing safe effluent whether for environmental discharge or water reuse. The persisting situation with no enough treatment infrastructure to properly handle the current sewage flow, this in addition to the surged loads due to population growth, is resulting in the collapse of the current treatment process. The current faced challenges urges a fast intervention to put a definitive stop to both the resulted health hazards and environment deterioration. Solving the overloaded problem will not only result in solving the health and environmental problem, but also will result in the increase of the agriculture activity specially in the absence of available water resources. Although the existing process is treating 1200 m3 of the generated wastewater from Al-Arrub Camp serving 12,000 citizens, however this is more than the capability of the existing process. Thus, this leads to health and environmental disaster in the area. Women will be given particular attention to ensure more equitable benefit of project intervention between men and women. Women especially girls at that age (10-15) are especially vulnerable to adverse impacts from unimproved sanitation and as girls experience their healthy transitions to womanhood. Inadequate availability of safe hygiene is exposing women and children to high risks of diseases and illnesses. In addition to the impacts of inadequate WASH on child morbidity and mortality rates, education also suffers when sick children miss school and women economic opportunities are routinely lost to the impacts of child illness and the time-consuming process of caring for a sick child.

2 - Activities

Activity 1.1: Conduct an introductory workshop to the related stakeholders to launch the project, its activities and results, quality standards as well as update the work plan based the workshop results. Activity 2.1: Create and promote the feedback/complaint response mechanism (CRM) to provide the targeted communities to raise their feedback and complaints:The feedback/complaint response mechanism (CRM) will be activated and promoted to provide the targeted communities with the opportunity to give feedback and to make complaints about the delivery of the project activities through direct contact with project team and the implementing organization. The team in cooperation with the related stakeholders will evaluate any complaints or feedback received from the community and take the appropriate action needed. Activity 3.1.1: Update the previous conducted assessment in-depth through technical evaluation of the WASH services to guarantee that this project physical intervention is properly respond to the needs of the targeted area especially women and girls. Activity 3.1.2: Prepare all the needed documents related to the implementation of the works on the ground. This process will include the tendering process, awarding, signing the contract; amongst others. Activity 4.1.1: Solve Al Karama School sewerage problem and connect it to the WWTP, enhance school water and sanitation infrastructure: The school not only has facilities that are inadequate in both quality and quantity for its girls student, but also they are experiencing a shortage in water supply. When water is supplied, the school store water in the storage tanks and in cistern is used by the girls for drinking and hygiene. However, the cistern was detected to be polluted and the storage capacity is not enough to provide the water during water cuts. Therefore, rehabilitation of the cistern and some measurements to prevent its pollution and increase access to safe and clean water through rehabilitating water cistern and increasing water storage capacity by installing 4 tanks with a capacity 1.5 cubic meter/tank and rehabilitate the drinking water point. Moreover, since the existing hygiene facility is unimproved, this results in unhealthy sanitation conditions putting at risk girl’s health. Thus, the project will rehabilitate 10 toilet facilities at Al Karama Secondary to improve the access to safe hygiene in addition to reconstruct the school to the main pipeline and to the WWTP. Activity 4.1.2: Installation of electromechanical equipment of the second line of the treatment plant to prevent the collapse of the WWTP and thus preserve the contamination of the agricultural land from pollution. Also, a test for the treated waste water effluent will be conducted. This activity will illuminate the health and environmental disaster which has a negative impact in the lives of the targeted population. Activity 5.1: In line with the project activity and to provide the targeted population with proper training at both levels; the school, treatment plan operators, and the community with deal with the proper management of the plant, hygiene facilities. At the school level, a training will be provided on hygiene practices to the school girls and help them to disseminate hygiene messages through innovative approach such as drama sketches, water audit form to be provided by the project team. At the community level, the project will set up hygiene leaders through the establishment of one community-based hygiene hub in the targeted area (10 of which 5 are women, and 5 are male). These leaders will receive an intensive training course to be responsible for spreading this knowledge to other citizens. At the treatment plant level, building the capacity on how properly operate and maintain the new treatment components to guarantee appropriate treatment and long lasting infrastructure in both quantity and quality of raw wastewater.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

In order to fulfill the needs and priorities of the targeted citizens, ARIJ will ensure having an environment helpful to respect for human rights and to avoid exposing the targeted beneficiaries to any threats and risks especially to gender-based violence. Therefore, protection measures and risk mitigation will be provided during the project implementation to ensure dignity, safety, accountability to them. The project team will establish a Complain Response Mechanism (CRM) and will conduct Community based meetings with the participation of the representatives of Sair Municipality, Palestinian Water Authority, Ministry of Local Government and School Administration to enlighten them with protections issues even beyond the end of the project. The project will also inform the beneficiaries about their rights to raise their feedback, complaints, and suggestions and encourage them to report any issues or concern. ARIJ will ensure that their feedback is well-received and reflected on the ground. ARIJ involved the targeted citizens in the project design and it was designed based on their needs raised during the meetings and assessments. Furthermore, ARIJ with the related stakeholders particularly the PWA will inform the targeted beneficiaries of the risks that may arise in addition to the technical protection measures integrated in the project activities execution. Do no harm approach will support that all beneficiaries (including the elderly, women, children, and people with disabilities) are targeted in having equitable and impartial access to all project activities and interventions without any discrimination and without to being exposed to any hazards or threats. ARIJ has developed Monitoring, Evaluation and Accountability system and assigned a professional team under the lead of professional person who has more than 15 years of experience in project monitoring and evaluation in order to improve the quality of its program and its fulfillment of the agreement commitments. Most importantly, the system focuses on the project accountability commitment to the affected persons through the development of various measures that ensures the principle of "Do No Harm" to the affected people. The M&E staff from ARIJ are responsible for ensuring that the project is going in line with its indicators, quality standards, outputs and outcomes on monthly and quarterly basis. ARIJ M&E staff will produce a progress report on a quarterly basis comparing the activities, quality, quantity, and showing the progress of the project according to the planned activities. Any deviation will be raised and solved immediately. Sex and Age Disaggregated Data’ (SADD) will be reported within the produced progress report. Moreover and in order to measure the project progress and outcome, the project staff will update the conducted baseline assessment and will conduct an endline survey on the project outcome indicators. Random sampling interviews with the beneficiaries and field visits will be conducted to measure the overall project impact and their overall satisfaction on the implementing of the project activities. A final report will be produced to present the final outcomes and shared with the WASH cluster.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

ARIJ adopts an approach to maximize the beneficiaries of the vulnerable groups as well as they can to maximize the benefits of this project based on equal access to assistance and services. ARIJ uses community participatory approach in the design and implementation of the activities to target the direct and indirect beneficiaries who will benefit from the project interventions. Even the adequate sanitation fall more heavily on women and children than on adult men, it is also detriment the standards of living for all well-being. Therefore, throughout the project activities outcomes, the direct beneficiaries are 7232 individual; among them 1269 young girls, 2463 women, 844 young boys, and 2656 men, will experience improved compliance between the adequate sanitation and the standards of living for all. The project interventions are tailored to the specific needs of each gender group for example girls at age (10 -15) are especially vulnerable to adverse impacts from unimproved sanitation and as girls experience their healthy transitions to womanhood. Therefore, the project will illuminate the issue of gender based violence through providing the adequate WASH services. For instance, the school who will benefit from the WASH activities will receive access to assistance and services based on their needs since they are suffering from unhealthy sanitation conditions. Therefore, the provision of clean, safe sanitation and hygiene will not only have a positive impact on the entire school, but also it will affect the whole environment and the indirect beneficiaries (i. e parents especially mothers, households etc). The same apply for the farmers where the project intervention will guarantee safe access to improved hygiene and sanitation services through the definitive stop to both the resulted health hazards and environment deterioration. Solving the problem of the overloaded WWTP will not only enhance the access to safe environment and food production for the citizens living near the treatment plant, but also the benefit will be exceeded to the community at large.
