Project Summary

The Big Smile Foundation through the project is strengthening women and girls' resilience and safety to be self-reliant.
The project goal will be achieved through a series of activities targeted at affected populations in crisis-affected communities of Jere and Ngala LGAs this includes IDPs, Returnees, and Vulnerable women, men, boys, and girls living within implementing communities. The project will leverage on TBSF long-standing presence and experience in providing implementing community-based driven initiatives in GBV, WASH, and Nutrition to deliver quality GBV responses that meet the needs of vulnerable people living in host communities.
The proposed project aims to meet existing gaps between IDPs and returnees in the Host communities, who still lack adequate protection and access to basic services, and are at risk of violence, abduction, rape, gender-based violence, forced and child marriage, and other violations of their rights. The proposed project is expected to reach 20000 direct beneficiaries and 5000 indirect beneficiaries. The GBV project will focus on actions that will increase and strengthen the resilience of women and girls while increasing safety through a community-based approach.
Through this project, formal and informal structures and institutions which include women groups, youth groups, SBMC, CPC, CBOs, and more will be strengthened to facilitate the uptake and strengthened community-based GBV response and prevention within implementing communities through adequate referral of survivals to services, lay counseling, Psychological first aid and more.
The project is logically based on: if communities are strengthened with knowledge and capacity of GBV prevention and response then community members will mobilize the facilitation of immediate and adequate community based GBV prevention and response within their communities, this will enable and ensure community members hold each other accountable on GBV. For instance, community and religious leaders, as well as bodies with existing responsibilities of providing alternative dispute resolution, including meditations and negotiation within the communities will have their capacity and systems strengthened to ensure that women, girls, men, and boys have access to these services.
TBSF will conduct activities to improve the resilience and coping mechanisms of women, girls, men, and boys affected by GBV and foster social cohesion will target people through community-based psychosocial support, provided in identified existing safe spaces that will be supported by the project.
Women and girls will benefit from structured life skills and positive parenting sessions using the adolescent life skill curriculum and positive parenting manual.
TBSF will support women and girls with standard dignity kits Vulnerabilities criteria for support will include pregnant women, child mothers, adolescent girls, GBV survivors, and lactating mothers.
TBSF will provide vulnerable women and GBV survivors with safe social economic livelihood support/income-generating opportunities to improve and strengthen their capacity to be self-reliant.
TBSF will increase local capacity by strengthening community-based mechanisms, through the training of community leaders, religious leaders, community-based organizations, security personnel, and government representatives on the GBV core concept and referral pathway and will work with communities to identify GBV risks in the targeted communities and develop action plans to mitigate these risks.
The project will also increase awareness of men, women, boys, and girls within implementing communities on the impact of GBV and sexual reproductive health rights (SRHR) through community outreach activities that will be delivered by trained community members. Men and boys will also be engaged at the community level through peer discussions to raise awareness and reduce stigma towards women and girls during menstruation.

1 - Needs

The conflict stemming from the insurgency of non-state armed groups (NSAGs) in northeast Nigeria continues as intensely as ever. The attacks and insecurity have displaced millions of people, devastated livelihoods, cut off essential services, including GBV, and health, and caused a crisis of protection. According to the 2022 Humanitarian response plan, a total of 8.4 million people including 2.2 million IDPs, 1.5 million returnees, and about a million from inaccessible locations require humanitarian assistance in Northeast Nigeria of which about 1.4 million individuals are in need of GBV response.Of the 1.5 million returnees in need, 97% reside in LGAs that are classified as having extreme or severe intersectoral humanitarian needs. A significant number of returnee households have returned to insecure or untenable conditions. Protection remains a great concern for many, especially for girls and women living in camp settings, many of whom are at higher risk of gender-based violence. Overcrowding in many of the camps severely affects living conditions: around 430,000 IDPs – more than half of IDPs living in camps – reside in highly congested conditions.The environment particularly in Borno State, remains extremely volatile, where all the major supply routes have become dangerous for civilians, including aid workers. More than 400,000 IDPs live in highly congested camps, making it nearly impossible for humanitarian partners to improve services. Families have been separated, with women and girls becoming heads of households in a context where men and boys have been conscripted or killed by Boko Haram (BH) and detained by the state security forces. Many women and girls have had to adopt negative coping mechanisms – transactional or survival sex, child marriage, to gain financial resources to support themselves and their families. Women and children make up 81 percent of the overall crisis population in Northeast Nigeria and 87 percent of returnees.The conflict has strengthened already existing gender-based barriers and vulnerabilities faced by women and girls which has created limited access to education, literacy, and vocational skills training services for young women and girls. Also, sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) of IDP women and girls are increasingly becoming an issue of grave concern in humanitarian programming in Nigeria. An estimated 38% of rape cases are associated with possible sexual exploitation and slavery. Through the project, The Big Smile will improve and strengthen family, community, and individual capacity to enhance Gender-Based Violence Prevention and Response in Jere and Ngala LGAs Borno State.

2 - Activities

This project will strengthen the resilience of women and girls by empowering women and girls for enhanced GBV prevention and response across four conflict-affected local government areas: Jere and Ngala LGAs inBorno states. It will build on ongoing protection projects in areas where TBSF has a long-established presence, and we will deliver a holistic set of activities that draw on our collective experience and strength in Women and girls’ empowerment programs.Leveraging on our long experience in humanitarian interventions in BAY states, TBSF will provide a range of feedback mechanisms to ensure that all our work is accountable to the local population. The project is logically based on: if communities are strengthened with knowledge and capacity of GBV prevention and response then community members will mobilize the facilitation of immediate and adequate community-based GBV prevention and response within their communities, this will enable and ensure community members hold each other accountable on GBV. For instance, community and religious leaders, as well as bodies with existing responsibilities of providing alternative dispute resolution, including meditations and negotiation within the communities will have their capacity and systems strengthened to ensure that women, girls, men, and boys have access to these services. Similarly, Additionally, activities to improve the resilience and coping mechanisms of women, girls, men, and boys affected by GBV and foster social cohesion will target people through community-based psychosocial support, provided in identified existing safe spaces that will be supported by the project. To achieve the project goal, TBSF will implement the following activities: 1. Provide women, adolescent girls, and other at-risk groups with safe avenues for generating income;2. Enhance their knowledge and skills base for microenterprises, financial management, natural resource management and leadership;3. Create and raise awareness about GBV, gender norms and power imbalances in the family and community in a sensitive way; and4. Improve the management of natural resources thereby supporting sustainable livelihoods5. Engage communities to ensure that communication materials are locally relevant, translated, acceptable and appropriate, such as pictorials for communities with low literacy. 6. Conduct livelihood mapping and risk analysis which can inform you on the gaps, needs and barriers for women and girls accessing livelihood opportunities within implementing communities7. support women and girls directly benefitting from TBSF activities and referred from other humanitarian actors with quality and standard dignity kits. The vulnerability criteria for selection and support will include pregnant women, child mothers, adolescent girls, GBV survivors, lactating mothers, and persons with disability. TBSF will preposition dignity kits as part of her contingency strategy for implementing communities in Ngala and Jere LGA of Borno state. 8. Train community leaders, religious leaders, community-based organizations, and security personnel on the GBV core concept and referral pathway. The training will be community-based. 7. Support the community with materials to conduct GBV awareness and sensitization within their communities to mitigate issues relating to GBV. 8. Identify and refer women and girls for multi-sectoral services which include Medical, Justice, TBSF will also mainstream crosscutting issues through training on disability, PSEA, climate change, and child safeguarding, developing responsive monitoring activities that encourage active participation from our stakeholders and participants, and creating shared CRM systems that hold us accountable to the local population. We will work with relevant government line ministries like the Ministry of Women affairs and social development in Borno and include social workers in all training and field activities.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

TBSF has a strong protection mainstreaming policy that guides all our activity and engagement. TBSF will ensure adherence to the policy and the use of the organization activity checklist to ensure in particular protection, gender and disability are mainstream in all activities. TBSF will ensure that all its staff and other stakeholders involved in the delivery of this project undergo adequate training on the four key pillars of protection mainstreaming to ensure that these elements are adequately integrated thought out the project implementation. This will include.1. Prioritizing safety & dignity, and avoiding causing harm: TBSF will ensure to prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of the planned activities which can increase the affected population's vulnerability to both physical and psycho-social risks2. Meaningful Access: Planned activities will be implemented in a way that is accessible to the target beneficiaries and in proportion to need and without any barriers (e.g., discrimination). We will ensure to pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services. 2. Accountability: We will ensure to set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints through a robust complaint and feedback mechanism.– Participation and empowerment: We will support communities through the development of self-protection, capacities and assist targeted beneficiaries to claim their rights, including basic human rights, rights to shelter, food, water and sanitation, health, and education and specialized GBV services including access to justice.All TBSF staff are responsibility and will ensure the highest level of protection and gender is well mainstreamed in the project implementation
