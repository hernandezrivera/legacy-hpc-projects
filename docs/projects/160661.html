Project Summary

This projects aims to reach 25,000 beneficiaries (1.2% of the Health Cluster target) comprising of 12,750 female and 12,250 male in 9 Counties (Juba, Magwi, Torit, Ikwoto, Budi, Lafon, Bor South, Panyikang and Baliet) in 2020. Health Link intents to achieve 10% of the cluster target in six counties (Juba, Magwi, Torit, Budi, Bor South and Panyikang) and 20% of the cluster target in 3 counties (Ikwoto, Lafon and Baliet). Health Link will implement specific activities under the 3 Health Cluster Objectives as;
1. Reducing excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic prone diseases
Under this objective, Health Link will under take key activities including but not limited to;
- Develop county based contingency plans for emergency preparedness and response to disease outbreak
- Conduct disease surveillance in 9 Counties. Health Link will support Weekly Reporting through IDSR for all the supported health facilities and EWARS for IDP Sites.
- Establish 1 emergency team that will ensure Preparedness and Response to any outbreaks within the Country. Potential diseases of importance will include Malaria, Measles, Cholera, Meningitis and Hepatitis E among others.
- Capacity building for health care workers at health facilities in integrated emergency preparedness and response.
- With looming threats of EVD, Health Link with SMoH will enhance surveillance in 3 Counties of Juba, Magwi and Ikwoto considering potential entry points of Nimule, Pogee, Lobone, Tseretenya, Lotukei, and Bira.
- Integration of IPC/WASH at all health care facilities will be central to the implementation of this project. IPC/WASH will be integrated at 3 PHCCs, 7 PHCUs and 2 Hospitals.
-Conduct surveillance of attacks on health care in the selected counties and report any vandalization of health care infrastructure, theft of equipment, essential medicines and supplies.
-Participate in 3 RRMs along with the HC Partners
2. Improve access and scale-up response to integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable populations.
Health Link intends to increase access to integrated quality essential health care services through a wide range of activities including;
- Support 12 health care facilities to provided integrated and quality static health care services, 2 Hospitals (Al-Sabbah Children Hospital Stabilization Center, Magwi CeMONC center), 3 PHCCs and 7 PHCUs across the selected Counties.
- Conduct curative consultations at 12 health facilities to reach estimated 25,000 beneficiaries
- HLSS will set up 1 Mobile Clinic that will be manage by 7 Mobile Teams– 1 in Bor South. Each team will conduct integrated mobile outreach at least once every week.
- Support the management of SAM with medical complications at Al-Sabbah Stabilization Center and in Bor PoC, estimated to reach 150 SAM/MC cases.
- Training of health care workers in diagnosis and case management of malaria and safe motherhood. Health Link intends to train 40 health care workers in total, 25 clinicians and nurses in malaria and 15 midwives in safe motherhood to ensure skilled deliveries.
- Provision of foods for in-patient feeding and care takers at 12 health care facilities (2 Hospitals, 3 PHCCs and 7 PHCUs).
- Provide BeMONC and CeMONC services at Magwi County Hospital.
- Establish and provide CMR services at 5 health facilities, 2 Hospitals and 3 PHCCs
- Provide rehabilitation services to disabled and Psycho-social support services to those with mental illness at 12 supported health care facilities.
3. Increase access to services to survivors of SGBV, disabled and mental health disorders.
Under this objective, Health Link intents to carry out specific activities that will include;
- Training of HCWs in CMR and MHPSS. HLSS intends to train 25 HCWs in CMR, 3 persons from each CMR unit and 24 in MHPSS, 2 persons from supported health facility.
- Provide CMR services at 5 health facilities, 2 Hospital and 3 PHCCs
- Provided MHPSS services at 7 health facilities to reach 768 beneficiaries.

1 - Needs

Estimated 56% of the population live at least 5km away from the nearest health facility and have much difficulties accessing essential health care services (SSHHS 2008, NHSSP 2017-2022). This is associated with poor health indicators, for example high mortality rate of under 5 children at 90.7/1000 and maternal mortality rate of 789/100,000 (South Sudan Mortality Statistics, 2016) among others. Displaced populations suffer more constraints in accessing lifesaving and essential health care, with children under 5 most vulnerable to vaccine preventable diseases as well as women of reproductive age facing high health risks of unskilled care during pregnancy and child births. Immunization coverage over 2 years (2017 & 2018) for all vaccine preventable diseases have persistently remained below 50%. This has created a big gap of 42% of children under 1 year at risk of measles (WHO EPI Report Sept 2018). Recurrent outbreaks, and or upsurge of epidemic prone diseases including acute watery diarrhoea/cholera, measles, malaria, meningitis and hepatitis E compounded by food insecurity presents increased susceptibility to morbidity and mortality especially to pregnant mothers and children under 5 years of age. Over stretching the health care system is the current looming threats of importation of viral heamorrhagic fever (Ebola Viral Disease) in the Country. With the ongoing revitalized peace process the anticipated returnees would even pose greater risk to the importation of EVD. This necessitates for early and adequate preparedness and response to potential outbreaks. Cross cutting issues like people with disabilities, mental disorders, HIV/AIDS, TB and non-communicable diseases including lifesaving trauma care are being cut-off from life-saving services. This has been related to contextual disruption of services and unpredictable health care funding in conflict affected locations with increasing IDP burden. Under this response plan, HLSS intends to fully integrate health care activities with nutrition, IPC/WASH, Protection and Food Security and Livelihood. Protection areas of focus will mainly include the clinical management of rape for survivors of SGBV as well as MHPSS services.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

This project targets vulnerable populations in IDPs, host communities and returnees. Health Link South Sudan recognizes that beneficiaries have rights to access the basic essential health care, shelter, rehabilitation especially among the disabled, SGBV and MHPSS services to mention but a few. Under this project, HLSS will ensure equal access for men, women, boys and girls to services. Special consideration will be put in place to ensure adequate space for provision of CMR services to survivors of SGBV, the disabled, Psychosocial Support services with high level of privacy and confidentiality. Beneficiaries especially survivors of SGBV will be supported to seek legal assistance through the county led legal system in placed.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

This project is designed to ensure access to essential health care services to IDPs, host communities and returnees given the ongoing process of the implementation of the revitalized peace agreement in the country. Modality for services delivery will be directed to displacements sites and hard to reach population through mobile clinics. Women, men, boys and girls will be provided services without discrimination on the basis of gender. The project intends to ensure that the services provided doesn't pose threats to the beneficiaries but rather address protection concerns across the population group targeted through carrying out joint activities and integrating some of the services within the population targeted. Further more, the project will engage the local authorities in the proposed project locations for their full engagement during the project implementation which in one way or the other shall minimize some protection concerns that would arise due to exclusion of minorities from service delivery.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

In line with the National Health Sector Strategic Plan (NHSSP 2017-2022), this project has been designed with full participation of the State Ministries of Health in the different states, County Health Departments, local authorities and the beneficiaries to ensure ownership and a smooth transition from the emergency context to development at an appropriate times. Coordination meetings to discuss on the modality for transition at County and State levels will be ensured while addressing the most urgent needs; for example the prevention of EVD through surveillance to mitigate the impact of outbreak in high risk locations. The community will be consulted and provided with information regarding the project implementation. This will include engaging existing CHD, Boma Health Workers, Community elders, Women, Youth groups, religious leaders and representatives of beneficiaries during the project implementation.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project has been structured to meet the Health Cluster Objectives that were derived from NHSSP. This will ensure appropriate allocation of the limited resources to meet the most critical needs of the vulnerable populations in the project locations. Short term performance indicators to achieve the cluster objectives have been developed with attention to full project integration of health, WASH, Nutrition, Protection, and MHPSS among others. A wide range of activities to be implemented to achieve/measure performance have been selected.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

This project was designed to significantly address access constraints to protection concerns of the most vulnerable section of the population especially women and children. Although the political environment remains fragile, the implementation of the revitalized peace agreement is beginning to take shapes and there is currently relative calmness. This is anticipated to further improve access to remotes areas, reduce risk of new displacement and subsequent stable system that will ensure protection of humanitarian workers and beneficiaries. The implementation of this projects will ensure equal access to men, women, boys and girls to services. Special consideration will be put in place to ensure adequate space for provision of CMR services to survivors of SGBV, the disabled, Psychosocial Support services with high level of privacy and confidentiality.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Health Link South Sudan clearly understands the need to ensure sustainability of this project in the selected counties. Health Link South Sudan will work closely with the County Health Department, State Ministry of Health, the current World Bank/UNICEF partners in Jonglei and Upper Nile States and HPF3 Partners in the other states to ensure a well coordinated effort in the Counties. Focus will be to build the capacity of the County Health Department personnel and the State authorities in health care supervision, monitoring of program activities, collection, compilation and reporting of health related information. Involvement of the County Health Department will ensure a smooth transition of this project from health partners to County Health Department and SMOH. Through monthly and Biweekly coordination meetings as well as other channel, communication will be shared about the progress of this project implementation and closeout plans at least 2 months before end of the project period. This will ensure the SMOH and the CHD takes the responsibility of providing the most critical health care services in selected counties without significant interruptions at the end of the project period.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Health Link South Sudan clearly understands the risk involved in delivery of humanitarian assistance and services to IDPs, hot communities and returnees vulnerable communities. In order to avert the eminent risk identified, HLSS will under take the follow approaches;- Established and operationalize 5 CMR centers to support the treatment of survivors of SGBV, 2 at Hospitals and 3 at PHCCs.- Coordinate activities with other partners and UNDSS to gazette areas suspected to have explosives from access by humanitarian actors and beneficiaries.- Work with the local authorities to ensure service delivery points are well placed out of disputed areas.- Carry out violent prevention through effective communication and community/beneficiary mobilization for services.- Ensure compliance with established tax policy and report to Health Cluster/UNOCHA any intention to Levi tax by authorities in the field outside of the existing policy.- Establish standardized criteria for registration of beneficiaries in IDP or Returnees settings and set up restrictions to regulate registration.- Conduct continuous awareness creation to beneficiaries to indicate availability and service delivery points to survivors of SGBV and support legal assistance services whenever required.- Ensure appropriate data management and reporting systems are in placed. Confidential matters will only be reported to specific individuals or institutions having responsibility to handle such matters.- Establish a feedback desk/information desk with free access to beneficiaries to be able to provide feedback or any concerns.- Ensure a mobile clinic services made available to reach out to locations which hard to reach.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Health Link South Sudan has developed a culture of engaging the community from the initiation of the project in order for the community to own the project. The community will be consulted and provided with information regarding the project implementation. This will include engaging existing CHD, Community elders, Women, Girls, Youth groups, religious leaders, people with disability and representatives of beneficiaries. HLSS will engage communities using appropriate language that the community members understand best to express their concerns, views and feedback. These feedback will form part of the project performance reporting to the health cluster and will help guide the fine tuning of the project to enhance positive beneficiary experience. Health Link provides feedback to beneficiaries through weekly and monthly meetings, share weekly and monthly reports with the authorities at community level whereas dissatisfaction by the beneficiaries are channeled through the County Health Department to the SMoH for appropriate corrective measures.
