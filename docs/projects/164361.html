Project Summary

The project implements Economic Recover and Market Systems (ERMS) activities which are designed to support rapid employment opportunities for vulnerable populations who are currently forced to resort to negative coping strategies. The project foresee the use of the Cash for Work modality. Cash-for-Work will provide temporary employment opportunities, with opportunities for participants to learn new skills while also helping to restore vital public services, including initiatives to support local markets. Project will target Aleppo, Ar-Raqqa, Hassaka and Deir-ez-Zor Governorates.

4 - Needs

The prolonged effects of the crisis, in its eighth year, continue to affect the ability of the population to access livelihood opportunities as well as their capacity to respond to further shocks and develop better resilience mechanisms. The HNO reports that 8.7 million people in Syria are in need of early recovery and livelihood assistance. According to an ESCWA report almost 83%, and rising, of the Syrian population is currently living under the poverty line. Almost half of the potential workforce (individuals between 18 and 65 years old) is unemployed with many forced to rely on negative coping mechanisms, such as child labor. In urban settings the lack of sufficient livelihood and employment opportunities is critically affecting returnees, IDPS and conflict-affected communities. Recent assessments in Raqqa city reported a low coping strategy index with an average of 38.6 for the assessed households, evidence of growing vulnerability and weakened resilience of assessed families to new shocks. Taking Ar Raqqa city as a case study for the disruptive impact of conflict on livelihoods, recent surveys by REACH (March, 2019) show that there are still large unmet needs and that food insecurity is on the increase with basic food items being unaffordable to a growing number of people. Only a little under half of the population of Ar Raqqa is estimated to have any form of employment or income generating opportunity. Of this, the vast majority is irregular casual labor. The impact of this growing unaffordability is pronounced. According to REACH’s assessment, only 29% of the core food items are currently perceived to be affordable by households in Ar Raqqa . High food prices, the absence of employment opportunities and the exhaustion of household assets and reserves have forced many to adopt negative coping strategies. The most alarming of these negative coping strategies is an increased reliance by households on child labor.

5 - Implementation of activities

Under the CfW scheme this Sectoral response will provide small rehabilitation works through extended services and small public infrastructure repairs that the entire community can benefit from. To guarantee the maximum efficiency of the work and a good cost-benefit ratio, all proposed works will be discussed with local authorities and other livelihood/CASH actors. Whenever possible, the activities will try to directly support existing plans of works that cannot currently be implemented due to lack of labor force. The NGO will support the labor force through CfW while providing workers with equipment and also partially contribute with materials (materials are normally provided by Municipalities and Departments).For more technical works, the presence of a technical expert is also foreseen for guidance and organization of the work. The skilled supervisors will work along with the workers providing technical guidance (1 skilled supervisor each 15/20 workers); in this way the NGO will guarantee the quality of the delivered works and, at the same time, will foster the development of the technical skills of the cash for work workers to enhance their potential access to the labor market. Concerning the cleaning of the schools, the NGO will solely support activities devoted to debris and rubble removal in non-functioning sections of the educational facilities. Schools will be identified in coordination with the local Education Committee. Workers and supervisors will be employed to clear classrooms, corridors, back and front yards currently blocked or obstructed by debris. Workers and supervisors will not perform any cleaning work in already operative sections of the schools and the work shifts will be organized not to interfere with any of the schools’ activities. This activity will ease the process of rehabilitation of such infrastructure for the Education Committee or will directly allow the non-functioning section of the school to host activities again.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The creation of new temporary employment opportunities through cash for work will enhance the self-reliance and wellbeing of beneficiaries and their immediate family. Access to income reduces the need to resort to negative coping mechanisms such as child labor, early marriage and asset depletion. As such, a concerted effort will be made to ensure that women have equal access to these temporary employment opportunities. Due to conservative genders norms, the selection of the cash for work activities will need to take into consideration those activities that women can participate in without social recrimination. The working times for cash for workers are gender appropriate, in particular ensuring the working hours are a suitable time and length (e.g. in winter months when the days are shorter, this means work will need to finish at least several hours before dark to allow beneficiaries to safely return home). Whenever possible People with Disability (PwD) will be included in the activities taking in consideration their specific needs as well as the type of works to be conducted. In case the implemented works would not be suitable for a PwD to participate in, Concern will consider the opportunity to involve another member of the household in order to guarantee indirect assistance to the individual or to refer the case to the Protection programme for further follow up and specific assistance. To ensure child safeguarding, the NGO ensures that those who are working with the affected population adhere to NGO Code of Conduct and Associated Safeguarding Policies which clearly outline the professional standards of behavior demanded of all staff and partner staff. Additionally the NGO has in place a complaints response and feedback mechanism (CRM) which has a number of options provided for raising confidential complaints including suggestion boxes at every office, a hotline that allows both calls and Whats App and an email address which are all manned by a CRM focal person.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

3) There is a broader risk of more widespread resumption of conflict between the SDF, GOS, TAF and FSA, especially in border districts such as Manbij, Kobane and Tabqa. In 2019, peaking in October, the Turkish Military incursion resulted in the establishment of a “Buffer zone” from Talabied to Ras Al Ain. This situation resulted in the temporary relocation of international staff to Dohuk and suspension of some project sites. Further escalation of the conflict, or deterioration in the security situation, across NES as a result of any of the following: IS resurgence particularly in the wake of reduction in support from coalition or redirection of military efforts to other conflict areas; tension between authorities and communities or inter-community conflict; movement of GoS troops into the area. In these instances, partial or complete access to the project areas would be lost. Additionally the NGO will take the due mitigation measures to minimize the risks related to the specific modality of support such as aid diversion, fraud, bribery and exploitation. All the beneficiaries will be acquainted with the use of the CRM mechanism enabling them report possible complaints. Cash distribution are normally performed together by finance and programme team and would be monitored by the M&E team as well as the protection team reporting possible protection related issues. Team supervisors are in charge to transmit on a daily basis attendance sheets and short report of the daily works; un announced visits are conducted on a weekly base by the programme team on the work sites minimizing the risks of fraud (ghost workers).
