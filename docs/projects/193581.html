Project Summary

The proposed project is to contribute to the live saving and protection services for IDPs and vulnerable resident focusing on women and girls by strengthening community rights as well being supporting protection structures and services at community and local government levels in two states, North Darfur and South Kordofan. The project will take place in six localities two in North Darfur ( Elfashir rural and kutum) South Kordofan in four locality (Kadugli, AbuJebeiha, Rashad and Elreif Alshargi). The project target 60,000 people and 6,000 children (60% female) through strengthen community-based protection mechanisms, and 3000 targeting for GBV as well as the coordination and management of community protection and GBV issues across two states.
The project has been designed based on NPO previous experience which have taken place as part of our project. It will engage closely with the Ministry of Social Welfare and support the establishment of community-based protection mechanisms through Community-Based Protection Committees (CBPCs) and strengthen women role in the community to lead and decide to promote the protection.
The project has two specific objectives aiming to contribute towards strengthening community base protection, and the other on strengthening the formal state response to community protection. The linkages between these two objectives are critical to ensuring good coordination and engagement.
NPO has previous experience with technical staff on protection involve in each localities offices able to deal with children, GBV and people with specific needs . also NPO has office in target localities will facilitate the implementation , reporting and monitoring.

1 - Needs

According to OCHA report , identified 12.8 million people, 7.4 million women in need of humanitarian are in need for protection and Gender Based Violence assistance due to major political developments, insecurity, violence, conflict, civil unrest, weak rule of law and lack of basic social services of Civilians, particularly IDPs and returnees and host community. Protection sector prioritize IDPs, returnees and vulnerable challenged by conflict, political instability, economic degradation and climate change. The Sector plans to reach 2,559,412 in 76 localities, targeting 1,650,651 IDPs 83% of the persons in need reside in Darfur the Sector plans to target 2,066,115 of them. The Humanitarian Needs indicate that IDPs and returnees are in need of legal assistance, civil documentation and property rights, especially for women need support for community projects and community-based structures to identify and resolve protection issues to respond the needs in 2023 according to Protection Sector plans objectives:• Objective 1: Address immediate and acute protection risks and needs of vulnerable IDPs, vulnerable crisis-affected and host communities by providing equitable and timely lifesaving services; • Objective 2: Strengthen the protection environment through robust protection monitoring and analysis, identification of protection risks, human rights violations, gaps in available service provision and advocacy; • Objective 3: Strengthen community-based protection mechanisms to address protection needs, enhance preparedness and early warning capacities and prevent or mitigate protection risks.key issues to address within the activities of the Protection Sector. The focus of the Protection Sector Strategy is on the Protection of Civilians, targeting IDPs, Returnees, and affected members of the host communities.Violations against civilians, including arbitrary arrest, torture, beatings and looting by armed actors, continues to hamper freedom of movement for IDPs, returnees and members of the host communities. The continued presence of armed actors forces many IDPs, especially women and girls, to limit their movement outside of camps, where security is more stable and predictable. IDP returnees face challenges with respect to accessing land, firewood collection points, water. Certain locations are difficult to reach due to insecurity associated, notably due to the widespread proliferation of arms and constant threats of violenceCivil documentation, including identity documents, are often lost, or destroyed during displacement. Many IDPs and returnees face difficulties in accessing public services.
