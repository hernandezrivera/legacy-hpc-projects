Project Summary

This project intends to strengthen the capacity of boys, girls and parents to prevent abuse against children in their communities. The project will target beneficiaries from Blue Nile i.e. Kurmuk & Baw and South Kordofan i.e. Dilling and Abujubaiha (level 5 severity ranking of protection needs, HNO 2022).
The proposed CN is aligned with the following HRP 2022 Protection Sector Objectives:
• Quality, specialized child protection services are available and accessible to the most vulnerable and at-risk girls and boys.
• Improved protective environments for girls and boys through interpectoral collaboration and targeted advocacy with communities and duty bearers.
• Improved capacity and resilience of children, families and communities through coordinated and contextually appropriate approaches.
Key interventions will include:
1) Strengthening child protection services to support the most vulnerable and at risk people in need in BN and SK states through a static children’s center, that will provide specialized services i.e. case management for children with trauma that will provide specialized services; structured and sustained PSS activities for children, adolescents including recreational activities and counselling (individual and group); as well as vocational and life skills courses for adolescent girls (including sewing/tailoring, handicrafts, knitting, IT, literacy, English courses, etc.).
2) HA will mobilize communities and conduct community-based protection interventions by strengthening the existing and establishment of three child protection patrol and network committees across various neighborhoods of both states. These committees will be linked with the available CP, awareness raising and referral services.
3) To reach the most vulnerable children in the wider Kurmuk, Baw, Al Leri, Talawdi localities district and increase their access to services, specifically for those unable to travel to capital state in BN and SK, HA will strengthen its delivery through a mobile approach. 4 Mobile Teams will undertake outreach services in the neighboring communities to provide awareness raising, case identification and referral services. They will also utilize a mobile Ideas Box to deliver PSS, educational and recreational activities to children and adolescents. The mobile approach would include also case management for women and children, awareness-raising sessions on CP response.
4) Through both static and mobile centres HA’s legal protection team will continue providing legal assistance, representation and legal counselling to vulnerable children (girls and boys), their families/guardians survivors to ensure self-reliance, supporting access to birth registration or/and any missing civil documentation that may hinder children from being able to exercise their rights.
5) Capacity building, coaching and mentoring of the government and non-governmental organizations (NGOs) on child rights and supporting conflict-affected children in BN and SK with legal documentation and/or birth registrations etc.

1 - Needs

According to Unicef Sudan Child Protection (https://www.unicef.org/sudan/child-protection) sixty four per cent of children aged 1-14 years experienced various forms of violent discipline. And children, particularly boys, are actively participating in armed conflict. The ratio children in detention reached 238/100,000 of the whole Sudan’s child population. More than 32,407 children across all 18 states (19,224 boys, 13,183 girls) were in contact with the law as victims, witnesses and alleged offenders. While diversion outside the judicial system increased, children in contact with the law still face lengthy judicial procedures and can be placed in pre-trial detention even for petty offences. Sudan is also a country of origin, transit and destination for children, with more than 2.2 million internally displaced people and refugees from neighboring countries, and war-affected countries. Children are on the move more and more in Sudan due to conflict, economic pressures, trafficking and migration. Children on the move are frequently unaccompanied or separated from their families and are exposed to violence, abuse and exploitation. There are various types of harmful traditional practices in Sudan that pose risks to children's physical and psychological health and wellbeing. Female Genital Mutilation/Cutting (FGM/C) prevalence in Sudan is 87 per cent among women aged 15-49 years old and 31 per cent among girls aged 0-14. And marriage before 18 years old occurred among 38 per cent of women aged 20 to 49, while 12 per cent were married before age 15. Additionally, one quarter of children in Sudan are engaged in child labor, with higher rates for boys, poorer, rural children and unaccompanied and separated children. There are wide disparities between States (49.4 per cent for East Darfur and 11.2 per cent for River Nile). Child labour is linked to poverty, insufficient social protection and services and school non-attendance.

2 - Activities/Outputs

1- 900 children (640 girls and 260 boys) receive structured psycho-social support, as well as participate in life skills, non-formal education and vocational courses. 2- Community-based CP structures established and strengthened benefiting 3,000 individuals (500 women, 500 men, 1000 girls, and 1000) boys. 3- 1,420 children and their families are provided with child protection legal assistance or counselling. 4- 100 government and NGO staff (50 women and 50 men) are provided with structured capacity building initiatives, mentored and coached to enhance child protection in their sector in BN and SK.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Due to limited assistance in targeted areas, populations are forced to engage in risky behaviors, including walking long distances to access services, exposing them to theft, assault or forced recruitment perpetrated by armed soldiers. Lack of access to services disproportionately affects persons with disabilities, female headed households, PLW and child headed households. Strengthening community protection mechanisms and improving access to services through information dissemination on available services, identification of the most vulnerable and referrals will be prioritized. Protection issues will be mainstreamed and considered during planning and implementation of the protection intervention to ensure that beneficiaries are protected and that services are easily accessible.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The proposed project will continue working with the host communities, IDPs, community leaders, volunteers and existing community structures to implement and adapt the interventions. The beneficiaries will be a key partner in providing essential information, such as identifying areas with many children targeted, providing feedback on the program and encouraging buy­in from potentially resistant local entities. HA-UK will encourage the participation of women in the decision­making process through engaging with women leaders and men. Caretakers and key figures will be regularly informed about the progress of the project and requested to participate in discussions and contribute to providing feedback for the improvement of the project. Direct involvement of the targeted beneficiaries will be integral in ensuring that the interventions are appropriate and reach the most vulnerable. Community members will be consulted through sensitization sessions and focus group discussions at the start of the planned activities. HA-UK will work with community leaders and other key stakeholders to ensure appropriate Bunin for the interventions and to transfer knowledge to ensure sustainability and continuity of services.
