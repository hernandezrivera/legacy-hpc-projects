Project Summary

The waste management sector itself is in a precarious position in the ‘east-line’ villages of Deir ez-Zor. Waste management initiatives funded as stand alone projects are withdrawing, and the area consequently faces a complete collapse of service provision. This brings additional risks to the legitimacy of the municipal bodies that are responsible for the delivery of such services and creates significant public health and environmental threats on the region. Critically, standing waste in urban environments heightens the spread of water-borne and vector-borne diseases, causes groundwater contamination, and damages agricultural land that is essential for the local economy.
In response, CAID is proposing a programme of support to empower 11 municipalities in Deir-ez-Zor to deliver waste management services. The programme of support will focus on:
• Training and capacity building of municipal waste teams
• Removal of accumulation of waste-piles, to provide clean streets and public areas
• Distribution of community bins and commencement of scheduled waste collection services
• Dumpsite rehabilitation and safe disposal of waste
• Piloting of waste segregation and recycling programmes
• Cost recovery and sustainability investigations including waste-to-energy (WtoE)
• Accompanying awareness and public health campaigning
These services will be provided in collaboration with target communities and responsible municipal bodies.
Labour will be mobilised through a targeted cash-for-work (CfW) programme that will recruit from the most vulnerable households in each community to deliver waste management services to those respective
communities. The programme will be implemented in an inclusive, gender and conflict sensitive approach that will ensure that all livelihood opportunities offered are extended among marginalised and disadvantaged groups, which will augment the project’s impact.
CAID have successfully delivered two waste management programmes in Deir ez-Zor. As part of the first pilot programme CAID undertook a comprehensive investigation of the waste management environment throughout Deir Ez-Zor. The second phase of programming instituted the collection of standing waste deposits across nine communities, established regular waste collection services and upgraded dumpsites to receive the collected waste. This project proposes to consolidate and capitalise on the investments made through these projects by investigating and trialling initiatives to achieve a self-reliant and sustainable waste management system, with the ultimate objective of achieving a full handover of services to the communities served.
To achieve this there will be five primary activities:
1. Optimise waste collection and disposal operations
2. Reduce the amount of waste being collected and disposed
3. Cost recovery opportunities
4. Identify or establish suitable entities for continued operations
5. Handover of solid waste collection and disposal operations in all nine towns and villages
Through the formation of inclusive municipal steering committees to guide, consult and feedback on the project, we will ensure that a diverse range of voices are heard and streamlined into each activity. The project will consider climate change throughout all delivery and, where possible, attempt to offset harmful
greenhouse gases (GHG) in the design of the waste management services and facilities. For example, the
introduction of recycling and composting initiatives will showcase options that reduce the generation of GHG, provide an alternative to chemical fertilisers, and generate livelihood opportunities for local communities.

4 - Needs

Public Health: Solid waste being left to decompose in open areas provides the perfect conditions for the spread of various water-borne and vector-borne diseases spread from mosquitoes, ticks, sandflies and other insects, spreading diseases such as dengue, malaria and leishmaniasis. Waste left in open areas has a higher likelihood of contamination of sources of drinking water, leading to risk of water-borne diseases such as cholera and typhoid fever. According to the World Health Organisation (WHO), in 2018, 35% of all Syrians had no access to safe drinking water, putting them at risk of waterborne diseases as a result of waste-contaminated sources of drinking water. In Deir ez-Zor, 761,000 people are in need of health assistance and acute water, sanitation and hygiene (WaSH) assistance, among the highest rates in Syria.Contamination of the natural environment: Groundwater supply can be impacted by leachate fromuncontrolled decomposing of dumped waste having a detrimental impact on valuable agricultural land. With an estimated 6.5 million Syrians food insecure, Deir ez-Zor is also among the worst effected regions with 213,000 people in need of food security and livelihoods assistance, with 34,000 at risk of food insecurity. Deir ez-Zor’s largely rural population rely more heavily on agriculture as a necessary productive asset than other, more densely populated areas whose access to alternative sources of food is more readily available.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

CAID and the implementing partner Khotoat have delivered CfW waste management services for 2 years in the same areas and foster excellent relationships with the residents and local authorities in the communities. We will continue to follow the Do No Harm approach by consulting with steering committees which are made up of local council representatives. to ensure the project does not distort local labour market, the daily wage rates will be in line with the recommended amount by the CWG. The project will follow the CfW Minimum Recommended Actions Guidance developed by the CWG and ERL. It will foster social cohesion through working in their areas of residence, training, capacity building of managing local dumpsites and WM services and enhance community resilience via the health campaign. The project will coordinate with actors to ensure that cfw streams are harmonised if there is geographic cross-over with other CVA projects in DZ. The project will promote community buy-in by prioritising cash for work staff from vulnerable households so that it is clear among residents the purpose of the project. A vulenerability assessment for CfW will be implemented to ensure that the most vulnerable are selected and trained for delivering WM services. The vulnerability assessment will be developed under the recommendation of the CWG and PWG so as to meet both livelihoods and protection standards. Approximately 65% of the project will be cash assistance.
