Project Summary

BINAA aims to provide children protection service through establishing two community centers in Ma'arrat Tamasrin and Jarablus,targeted IDPs and host population in line with GBV minimum standards to reduced child protection concerns including violence, abuse, neglect and exploitation through improving the access of at-risk girls and boys to response and preventive services through provision of full child protection specialized services including provision of focused and non-focused psychosocial activities,-awareness-raising, life skill building including youth and adolescents - provide case management services for child survivals and at-risk cases,- and child-friendly spaces for girls and boys,-Empowering and integrating of PWDs with the community through specialized activities including psychological rehabilitation, and provision of PSS activities, life skills, non-formal education, and awareness-raising sessions about their rights within the community, Provide transportation. referral to specialized services, -Providing emergency cases with a one-off cash assistant to enable immediate response when it is needed. In the same way, this project aims to enhance local capacity to provide child protection services through building the capacity of child protection teams, service providers including school personnel, and the establishment of local child protection committees/ networks and building their capacity on Child protection. The project aims to reach about 1800 beneficiaries through structured psychosocial services PSS, 280 children through case management, 20 women and men through capacity building,200 through awareness-raising.

4 - Needs

Across Syria an estimated 11.06 million people (50% women and 43% children) are in need of various forms of humanitarian assistance including GBV protection where GBV continues to disproportionally, with women and girls facing particularly high levels of vulnerability. 5.7 million children continue to face pervasive violence and a range of protection risks. Grave child rights violations remain a significant concern, including in areas where hostilities have declined, with children at risk of killing and injury, recruitment and use in hostilities, torture, detention, abduction, sexual violence, attacks on schools and hospitals and denial of humanitarian access by parties to the conflict.Specifically, the humanitarian situation in north-west Syria has experienced continuous deterioration during 2019. A total of 834,965 movements were tracked in Idleb between January and October. Between April and September more than 400,000 people have fled their homes in search of safety and to access basic services, while in December only, almost 300,000 people were displaced - mostly in southern parts of Idleb - to reach safer areas in the north. Of those, 80 percent are estimated to be women and children. (Voices of Syria 2020, HNO 2020)

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

risk-1: through provide response services,there is a risk of discrimination or perceptions of discrimination if a service is provided to specific groups or individuals at the exclusion of others.Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High.Mitigating measures: BINAA Staff will ensure a comprehensive service mapping is undertaken and communication with communities can be proactive and informed.risk-2: Lack of quality services and referral expose survivors to harm, enhancing risk of stigmatization reappraisal and further trauma.Likelihood:Medium. Impact: High.Mitigating measures:Case management with survivors to be conducted by trained personnel only.GBV risk-3:Exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers when accessing assistance and services.Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High.Mitigating measures:integrating PSEA in BINAA policies and making sure that all personnel read and signed code of conduct, and that all contracts with partners/suppliers/contractors include clauses on PSEA.BINAA will ensure that complaint mechanisms for reporting sexual exploitation and abuse are accessible (Complaints boxes, WhatsApp, email) and that focal points for receiving complaints understand how to discharge their duties. We will take appropriate action to protect.CP Risk-1: risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls, and boys (and their caregivers) through the provision of specialised child protection services if service providers are not adequately trained/ services are of low quality.Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium.Mitigating measures:BINAA will ensure training packages are in line child protection minimum standards and consider remote managed context.CP Risk-2: risk to physical safety of child protection service providers if perceived to be interfering in family-based concerns. Likelihood:high.Impact:High.Mitigating measures: BINAA will continue involvement and coordination with relevant autohorities.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: - Locations where the project activities will be conducted are secure and accessible. - Materials/labor are/is available. Risks: 1. Losing Access to targeted location Interference from armed groups or the local councils outside the specified context. BINAA will mitigate the risk of access limitation through: • Regular monitoring for INSO security and incidents reports. • If it’s during the implementation, it may be necessary to develop several different ‘exit scenarios’ according to GBV SC ethical closure of GBV activities as mitigating measure for GBV programming.will ensure continuous updating of the GBV exite Plan as needed.Assumptions: • Targeted communities are accessible. • Project activities are accepted in the targeted communities. Risks: • Targeted communities are not accessible (risk level – medium)• Project activities are not accepted in the community due to context and culture. Risk mitigation: • Targeted communities were selected among communities that are far from frontlines or areas that are likely to have bad security situation. • Continuous coordination with active actors on the ground and AWG and security update groups and protection cluster and sub clusters team to avoid overlapping.• Implementing security and safety measures and risk assessments on timely manner during project period. • Updating GBV exit strategy and plan as needed on regular basis. • Community inclusion and consultation in all project phases to achieve community acceptance and required knowledge and awareness of the project.
