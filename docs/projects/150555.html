Needs

The ongoing Israeli blockade, the residual impacts of the 2014 hostilities, and internal political divisions continue to have a devastating impact on socio economic conditions for Palestine refugees, severely restricting the movement of people and goods and crippling the once vibrant local economy. The socio economic context continues to be characterized by widespread unemployment and stagnating low incomes, continuing to trap the majority of the population, 70 per cent of whom are Palestine refugees, into poverty. Unemployment reached 56 per cent for refugees in the second quarter of 2018, compared to 49.3 per cent for non refugees. The moderate boosts to the economy generated by the post conflict reconstruction activities have proved to be transitory and have not been enough to balance the salary cuts imposed on Palestinian Authority’s employees and the cuts in energy supply, as well as the protracted effects of the land and see blockade which entered its twelveth year in 2018. GDP per capita in Q2 2018– equal to approximately $2.8 per person per day and about less than half of the West Bank – has also been on a decreasing trend since Q3 2016. The economy has been extremely volatile, characterized by alternating periods of relative growth and periods of recession. Growth was not the result of improved productive capacity but rather linked to the rebuilding of destroyed infrastructure, which heavily depends on exogenous factors such as import of raw materials and donor aid inflows. These trends have had a particularly severe impact on vulnerable groups, including women and young people. Unemployment is particularly high amongst refugee women (79.3 per cent). In addition to lack of job opportunities, women face social barriers to employment in Gaza, particularly when this involves travel to a workplace away from home. Young people between the ages of 15 and 24 are also particularly affected. 87.4 per cent of young refugee women were unemployed in the second quarter of 2017. Despite being highly educated, young people in Gaza face the prospect of unemployment, remaining dependent on already strained families and isolated from the outside world, rather than creating an independent future for themselves. This lack of opportunity further strains coping mechanisms, fostering hopelessness already prevalent as a result of the isolation of the blockade and trauma of recurrent hostilities. Consistently high rates of unemployment and deteriorating purchasing power eroded many household’s capital and coping mechanisms, rendering the majority of the population of Gaza dependent on humanitarian assistance to meet basic needs and trapped almost half (40 per cent) of the population in food insecurity. As a result, food insecurity in Gaza remains persistently high; not because all basic commodities are generally unavailable on the local market, but because many families cannot afford them. UNRWA projects that cover one million refugees in Gaza will require food assistance in 2018, an estimated 98,000 of whom are chronic poor, and will be assisted through the Agency’s core human development programming. The increase in emergency needs is dramatic a 12 fold increase on the 80,000 refugees who required assistance at the start of 2000. UNRWA identifies refugees in need of assistance through a Poverty Assessment Survey, conducted by UNRWA social workers during home visits to each household. Social Workers revisit all households classified as poor on a two year basis to identify any changes in needs or status, as well as the homes of all new applicants. Any family can obtain updated information on its poverty status at the nearest Area Relief and Social Services Office. Families who are not satisfied with their eligibility classification can appeal if (i) the family is reclassified from abject poor to absolute or non poor, or from absolute poor to non poor and (ii) they were found non poor after a new application or re application.

Activities

Through its Job Creation Programme (JCP), UNRWA will support refugees coping with protracted threats and shocks, and mitigate food insecurity through offering short term employment opportunities to approximately 11,082 poor Palestine refugees. These will be equal to 1,133,808 work man days, and will inject $ 13.51 million into households, generating 3,937 full time job equivalents (FTEs). The short term job opportunities will include 7,920 unskilled positions, 3,016 skilled positions and 146 professional positions. Priority will be given to applicants from households who have been assessed as living below the poverty line of less than US$3.87 per person per day. Targeting criteria will also include skills and qualifications, location, age and gender. UNRWA aims to provide 40 per cent of skilled CfW opportunities to women and about 40 per cent of all CfW opportunities to youth (18 29 years old). In order to ensure benefits are spread widely, contracts for unskilled positions are offered for three months and up to four months, and contracts for skilled positions are capped at six months and up to nine months . Only one member of a given household is eligible for cash for work contract. This intervention will inject US$ 13.51 million in wages into the local economy, indirectly benefitting approximately 314,000 people and supporting the recovery of economic activity in Gaza. The salaries provided will remain below average wage level so as not to distort the local market, and will range from US$ 10 to US$ 19 per day. (Although salaries are here indicated in US$, beneficiaries will be contracted and paid in local currency to avoid the impact of fluctuations in the exchange rate). In cooperation with cluster partners, UNRWA will provide cash for work opportunities to key services through Community Based Organisations, Youth Activity Centres, Women Activity Centres and Community Rehabilitation Centres which aimed in making sure all vulnerable groups are included. As a part of the agency wide Gender Mainstreaming strategy, a minimum of 40 per cent of direct hired beneficiaries put as a target for providing skilled jobs while offering employment to unskilled women in Gaza remains one of the main challenges of the JCP due to the limited unskilled employment opportunities available for female labourers, even though JCP is continuously working to explore ways of alleviate this particular gap and seeks to enhance employment opportunities targeting unskilled women through prioritizing mapping and conducting needs assessment and project proposals since 2015, the problem of unskilled female employment is purposely addressed by the JCP through the creation of culturally acceptable jobs for women in the current economic and social context in Gaza. Like; in the Agriculture sector, where the standard target of 60 per cent of this sector’s beneficiaries to be women is set in any proposal with this component and increased to one hundred per cent in year 2018 . Additionally, JCP succeeded to create new meaningful jobs would help to absorb more vulnerable unskilled women where JCP increased the number of school attendants to expand the base for absorbing unskilled women and more interestingly, also created new jobs in new working sectors for women in packing centres as labourers and absorbed more females to work as guards in UNRWA health centres and cleaners. It is worth mentioning that JCP is a gender sensitive program integrates gender mainstreaming in its planning, operations and monitoring to ensure gender equality and development of women for competing in labour market. Moreover, Women working under JCP contract modality are treated in accordance with the principles of UNRWA policy on the subject of (1) Maternity (unpaid). (2) Recruitment of pregnant. (3) Pre-Natal. (4) Resuming Work- Post Natal. (5) Breast feeding working hours.
