Project Summary

In alignment with the Humanitarian Response Plan 2021 and the United Nations Strategy on Mine Action 2019-2023, UNMAS will field eight multi-tasking teams (MTT) with embedded Community Liaison Officers (CLOs) to provide the following services: explosive ordnance disposal (EOD) tasks, survey and clearance of confirmed hazardous areas (calculated in square meters (sqm)), as well as conduct explosive ordnance risk education (EORE). UNMAS intends to deliver EORE sessions to 96,000 beneficiaries disaggregated as follows 24,000 boys, 24,000 girls, 26,000 women, and 22,000 men. During the EORE sessions, further information will be gathered by the CLOs on suspicious hazards, which will then be shared with the clearance teams for surveying, marking and clearance. EORE alerts communities on how to identify, avoid and report explosive hazards they may encounter to reduce their threat of injury or death. Additionally, the clearance teams will survey and clear 5,000,000 sqm of land consisting of cluster strikes and minefields. The eight clearance teams will be highly mobile in order to respond to urgent and prioritized tasks throughout South Sudan. The teams will provide survey and clearance of explosive hazards as well as provide risk education to women, men, boys, and girls to mitigate the threat of injury or death from explosive hazards.

1 - Needs

As of January 2021, there were approximately 350 hazardous areas recorded in the Information Management System for Mine Action (IMSMA) database, which are either suspected or confirmed to be contaminated by landmines or explosive remnants of war (ERW). However, the total extent of contamination across South Sudan is unknown, particularly in areas with ongoing conflict as illustrated in the Greater Equatoria region. Therefore, there is a need to survey and locate unreported or suspected hazardous areas as well as to conduct clearance and removal of explosive ordnance (EO). The legacy of a protracted and multi-dimensional conflict has resulted in civilians living in close proximity to landmines and ERW. Specifically, there are five counties: Canal/Pigi, Juba, Magwi, Terekeka, and Torit, with the highest record of confirmed hazardous areas, and an additional 26 counties which are contaminated with two or more hazardous areas throughout South Sudan. These explosive hazards threaten the lives of civilians, particularly those fleeing active armed clashes. It further inhibits the return of displaced persons, the resumption of livelihoods, and the delivery of emergency and life-saving assistance. During the removal and clearance of explosive hazards, concurrent provision of EORE will ensure that communities and individuals are able to develop strategies and coping mechanisms to reduce the threat of landmines and ERW. The teams will be highly mobile and able to rapidly deploy to situations which require immediate or emergency response in priority locations to enhance protection needs, support the returns of internally displaced persons (IDPs) or refugees, as well as support humanitarian agencies delivering aid to vulnerable populations.

2 - Project activities

At the technical level, UNMAS ensures that all mine action activities are conducted in accordance with the International Mine Action Standards (IMAS), the International Ammunition Technical Guidelines and Standards (IATG), the Strategy of the United Nations on Mine Action 2019-2023, and the National Technical Standards and Guidelines (NTSG). Survey, clearance and EORE personnel are trained and accredited by UNMAS annually. UNMAS will ensure the effective prioritization of tasked locations (in coordination with key stakeholders, including relevant cluster partners in addition to Protection, FSL, WASH, amongst others). Technical and non-technical surveys to locate the presence or absence of explosive hazards will also commence. Confirmed hazardous areas (CHA) will be recorded, marked and cleared. The surrounding communities will be informed and made aware of the status of the hazardous area. Once the hazardous area has been cleared, the land will be handed over to the community in liaison with the local authorities and the National Mine Action Authority (NMAA). The IMSMA database will record clearance progress and update maps accordingly on a monthly basis and the updated information will be shared with all the relevant stakeholders. CLOs will provide EORE tailored to the needs of the audience (IDPs, returnees, host communities, children, men and women to receive targeted messaging and teaching methods).

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

All stakeholders must adhere to work proactively in preventing and protecting affected persons from any form of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). The UNMAS project will ensure beneficiaries have both male and female CLOs available for reporting purposes in order to create a safe space for information sharing, as well as ensure that the beneficiaries know how to report a case anonymously if needed when raising abuse, discrimination, or other inappropriate behaviour.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The CLOs are specifically trained to speak to diverse audiences and do not only consist of both males and females, but are always from various regions and tribes within South Sudan to uphold the community’s cultural relevance and norms. This ensures continuity amongst the beneficiaries and guarantees that the vulnerable are able to express their views/opinions.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The CLOs are mixed gender in order to ensure they are able to provide a safe space for women and girls to speak freely and share information. Additionally, they seek out all various community groups, to share and gather information, the latter being key to enhance the mine action clearance process in addressing previously unknown explosive hazards.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The UNMAS 2021 HRP Project will ensure that the beneficiaries’ feedback is incorporated into the project by conducting non-technical surveys and EORE. It is at this stage where beneficiaries are able to express their concerns or fears of explosive ordnance as well report previously unknown hazards in and around their communities. UNMAS utilizes the information to conduct survey and clearance operations as well spends informs the community members on specific actions to mitigate their risk to explosive hazards.
