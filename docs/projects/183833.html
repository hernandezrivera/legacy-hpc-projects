Project Summary

Kassala Community WASH will target rural communities in the Telkook and Hamesh Koreib localities to rehabilitate four water sources with solar pumps, new pipelines as necessary, and upgraded water tanks to ensure water resources to the largest number of people possible, including to those in hard to reach villages and to the womens sides of communities such as Hamesh Koreib and Hashaieyb where women are dependant upon the men to provide water resources. Pump locations will be determined by the results of the Samaritan's Purse WASH assessment that took place in September and October of 2021. Water User Committees and Pump Technicians will be trained at each pump location to ensure proper care for pumps and pipelines following rehabilitation. Committees will be selected with representation of the women, men, young, elderly, and most vulnerable in mind to ensure equal access to all parties in the area.

1 - Needs

The Hamesh Koreib and TelKook Localities in Eastern Sudan are hard to reach, having experienced fighting in the past decade and with rough environmental conditions that make living there challenging. With all these difficulties, access to safe drinking water is also a significant concern in most parts of the localities, but the challenge is worse for rural villages. The area receives very little rainfall annually, and the population has poor living standards. There is also very little development to WASH infrastructure. A recent assessment done by Samaritan's Purse indicates only a few operational water points in the two localities. The majority require rehabilitation and upgrade. In TK, for example, the WASH system installed runs on a generator. Because of the fuel crisis in the country, making diesel fuel difficult to obtain in these areas, a high percentage of the population forced to rely on unprotected water sources and inadequate quantities of water, i.e., less than 15L/P/Day. Women and girls have to travel more than the recommended 500 meters, and hence spend more than half an hour walking in the scorching sun, only to have to wait in the long queues to collect the precious commodity. In HK, the situation is very similar, as the area is mountainous, with restricted access for men and women. With almost 11 potential water points visited by SP in TK, less than half are working, and the remaining require rehabilitation and repairs for the systems to be operational again. Most of the water points use diesel generators as a source of power to run the pumps, and the fuel crisis in the country instills more burden to the already stretched community who are trying to make their ends meet. The system installed provides the best platform for an upgrade, and replacing the generators with Solar panels will be more sustainable than a fuel based system. During the needs assessment, the Samaritan's Purse team noted that the community has a positive attitude towards this initiative and is ready to provide security and support for the program. There is another existing WASH infrastructure like the distribution lines, which only requires troubleshooting. There are also willing community members to be trained to manage the water points and receive basic training for repairs and rehabilitation.

2 - Activities/Outputs

This project will involve different stakeholders in a dignified way from it’s onset. During the project launch, the community representatives, the community themselves, leaders, government representatives, and other relevant stakeholders will be informed on their roles and duties, complaint and feedback mechanisms, and the project's activities and duration. This community involvement will create an open and conducive environment that will ensure coordination and unity throughout the running of the project. The community will also be encouraged to accept and own the project, ensuring project objectives are met and creating sustainability. The HK and TK communities are unique because they hold Islamic solid tradition and practices. This project is designed to reflect the different needs of men and women independently to account for these practices and cultural traditions. General water points will be rehabilitated, however, the project will utilize Focus Group Discussions with respective beneficiary types to identify the ideal locations to rehabilitate distribution points and systems. The water points that the project will rehabilitate will be identified with the government WASH technical officer and the local community members. A consensus will be reached about which water points to rehabilitate and work will begin accordingly. Approximately four water points will be rehabilitated and outfitted with solar powered pumps, eliminating the need for fuel that has grown increasingly more difficult to source. Pipelines and water tanks will also be installed or replaced as appropriate. To ensure the project's sustainability and increase community buy-in, water user committees (WUC) will be formed and trained. Each committee will be made of 10-14 individuals at each water point. Members will be selected from willing participants within the communities who have been selected and verified by community leaders. Gender representation will be guided as culturally appropriate and based upon location of tanks and pumps. In communities within HK, with gender segregation, WUC’s will be composed of members located in the area of the points and tanks. Efforts will be made to ensure that water is distributed to women and water points repaired within the women’s side of town as possible. To coordinate the mens and womens WUCs, at least one set of delegated male and female relatives that are culturally able to talk over the phone will be chosen. These committees will be responsible for the overall running of the water points and conduct maintenance when needed. With each WUC maintain its own water point, the longevity of the points will be extended, ensuring water for their communities in both HK and TK beyond the life of this project. The Kassala Community WASH project is meant to complement the work being done under USAID-BHA Kassala Integrated Response Project (KIRP). While KIRP will rehabilitate the water points specifically at the health centers, this project will take that initiative beyond health centers and into the communities themselves, assisting in the decrease of water borne illnesses at health centers and at home.
