Project Summary

The proposed project is designed to integrate nutrition services in the 11 BRAC health facilities located in 10 different camps which provide essential primary healthcare services to the Rohingya refugees of relevant camps. The objective of the project is early detection of malnutrition among children 0-59 months (Female 81% and Male 19%) and provide proper counseling on nutritional practices and sick child care with the view to keeping nutritional status satisfactory during the medical treatment and onwards. Alongside, children of 0-36 month age group will be provided with age specific Early Year’s Stimulation services for optimum physical and mental development and improve nutritional status. Furthermore the project will focus on improvement of Nutritional status of pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) by providing necessary nutrition related counseling and diet management for PLW with malnutrition and other medical conditions (diabetics, hypertension, Anaemia, CKD etc.) while they will be visiting BRAC health Facilities for ante-natal and post-natal care. At the same time, adolescent girls will be targeted for counseling on nutritional practices and recommended diet intake. Under the project, Vitamin-A Capsule, IFA and deworming tablets will be provided to children 6-59 months, adolescent and pregnant and lactating women as if they are missed in other ongoing programs. With all these, the project will significantly, contribute to improve nutritional status of elderly people (> 60years) and patients with chronic disease, like Tuberculosis, Hypertension, Diabetics, Cancer, Hepatitis, CKD, HIV, etc.

1 - Identified Needs

Though the level of both acute and chronic malnutrition in Rohingya refugee camps have been improved significantly since the influx in 2017, still these remain unacceptably high. According to a recent SMART survey which was conducted in the time period September & October 2019 by ACF in collaboration with Nutrition Sector in Cox’s Bazar, the prevalence of Combined GAM (cGAM) found as 12.2%, while the Combined SAM (cSAM) was 0.9% among children 6-59 months of age in Makeshift settlements. GAM per WHZ among children 6-59 months was observed as 10.9% which is in the public health threshold category of “HIGH” according to the new 2018 WHO/UNICEF classification for Wasting. And, the prevalence of global chronic malnutrition or Stunting per HAZ among children 6-59 months was found 32.6% in the same survey, which is also considered as “Very High” based on the new 2018 malnutrition threshold classification for Stunting. Moreover, the prevalence of underweight per WAZ among children 6-59 months was found 28.9% in the same community. Besides, the overall prevalence of Anaemia (Hb<11.0 g/dL) among children 6-59 months was found 37.4% which is quite close at the WHO cut-off of 40% for significant public health concern. In a comparison of prevalence of Anaemia among children 5-59 months, it is observed that it is higher (60.0%) in the age group of 6-23 months, which exceeds the WHO emergency threshold (High) of 40% in Makeshift camps. Hence, strong attention requires to address the very high rates of Anaemia among the younger children (<2 years) in Makeshift camps. Alongside, the rate of Anaemia in Adolescent girls (13-19 years) was found as 7.5%. Furthermore, the prevalence of Women’s Low MUAC (below 210mm) in the Makeshift Settlements among all women 15-49 years was 4.5% and in pregnant or lactating (PLW) group it was 4.8% which has also an impact on nutritional status of the children.An “Infant and Young Child Feeding in Emergencies” (IYCF-E) monitoring exercise conducted in March/April 2019 indicated a lack of desired behavioral practices related to breastfeeding and other nutritional issues, which aggravates the malnutrition situation. The results showed that 50.18% of children under 6 months were not exclusively breastfed and given pre-lacteals foods and complementary feeding (after 6 months) is relatively low at 61% for the range from 6 - 8 months. The Minimum Acceptable Diet (MAD) is only available to 26.51% and Minimum Dietary Diversity (MDD) to 46.18% of the households.The World Health Organization (WHO) estimates that 50% of cases of child under-nutrition are due to repeated diarrhoea and intestinal infections caused by poor sanitation and hygiene practices. Hand washing with soap is a critical determinant for achieving and maintaining good nutrition. This healthy behavior plays an important part in preventing micronutrient deficiencies, stunting, wasting, and deaths. Though the exact number of patient with non-communicable and chronic diseases in the camp is not known, they need special support to guide them regarding lifestyle modification and diet management to maintain a good nutritional status and physical wellbeing. To address the above critical situation of malnutrition in the Camp, BRAC has planned to deliver promotional nutrition services through existing 11 BRAC Health Facilities in 10 camp. The nutrition services will include, nutritional assessment of children under 5 years, pregnant and Lactating Women, IYCF and other nutrition related counseling, early years stimulation services for 0-36 month children, diet management and life style modification for pregnant, lactating women, elderly people and patient with chronic diseases, distribution of IFA, Vitamin-A, deworming tablets.
