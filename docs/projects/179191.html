Project Summary

(Planned – SO2 )
First: Provide 800 livestock breeders with Veterinary Assistance ( Veterinary vaccines, veterinary consultations, distribution of veterinary supplies, vitamins, and salt blocks) in the North West of Syria.Three main diseases spreading widely among livestock in North West of Syria, including the following:
1. Foot and Mouth Disease
2. Sheep-pox and cowpox
3. Enterotoxemia
- MSO seeks to implement a comprehensive project to vaccinate sheep, goats, and cows(Three types of vaccines against the aforementioned three epidemic diseases in three rounds ) - They will conduct onsite consultations to raise veterinary awareness among livestock breeders in the region as well as provide technical consultations on causes, treatment, and prevention of livestock diseases Sheep, goats, and cows.
Second: - In Tell Abiad will the distribution of agricultural livelihood assistance ( seasonal agricultural kit) through cultivating small homesteads this intervention will benefit 700 households.
- Distribution of sheep & fodder for 700 households.
- The project will incorporate support through the project life agricultural extension support.
This will yield higher household-level income and ultimately improve food security. The integration of these activities, in a phased and comprehensive approach, is essential for ensuring a sustainable impact on the productive capacity and long-term resilience to shocks for communities in northeast Syria.

4 - Needs

The crisis has also led to an increase in serious bacterial and viral diseases affecting livestock, especially sheep, cows, and goats as their owners face difficulties and financial burdens dealing with these diseases. After nine years of crisis, the people of Syria, including those residing in camps, continue to face great challenges, many struggle to feed and educate their children. Food security remains a serious challenge due to continued localized hostilities, new and protracted displacements, increased numbers of recent returnees and the sustained erosion of communities’ resilience after almost nine years of conflict. Around 6.5 million people in Syria are estimated to be food insecure and in need of food and livelihood support. An additional 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity and need livelihoods support to strengthen their resilience (FAO/WFP Crop and Food Security Assessment 2019) Lack of employment opportunities and increased fuel and commodity prices limited households’ purchasing power, causing more families to adopt negative coping strategies such as consuming less preferred and less expensive food, cutting the number of daily meals or withdrawing children from school in order to work. The situation in Tell Abiad is characterized by progressive stabilization following the change of control in northeast Syria, The main trigger of food insecurity is uncertainty, depleted livelihood opportunities, poor access to food, decreased crop production, and livestock decline. Areas with a higher concentration of IDPs have higher rates of food insecurity, reaching up to 54% of the population.
