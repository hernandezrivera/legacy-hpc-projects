Project Summary

Through the proposed program, RI will provide comprehensive quality curative primary health care at static facility level in both static and mobile facilities, Integrated Community Case Management (ICCM) of childhood illness, RRMs, and capacity building of health staff. Additionally, RI will Prevent, detect and respond to epidemic prone diseases through strengthened the disease surveillance, a review and update of the preparedness and response plan, strengthening EPI services, and integrate WASH response for improved sanitation, hygiene, and access to potable water.

Needs

Timely access to regular health services in Upper Nile State (UNS) remains challenging, though improved by the intervention of Relief International in 2018. Thousands of displaced populations face the most complex challenges in accessing health care services due to insecurity, remoteness, unequal geographical coverage of the health system, and natural factors including floods during the rainy season. Children under 5 remain the most vulnerable to preventable diseases. Health service provision remains low, although enhanced through interventions including the Rapid Results Health Fund, the level of measles coverage is low in UNS (7.2% in Maiwut and 52.7% in Maban). The observation is the same for Vitamin A supplementation: 5.7% in Maiwut and 57.7% in Maban. Besides, the level of malnutrition is still high as per the last SMART survey conducted in the targeted counties: the GAM rate is 17.2 in Nasir, 10% in Maiwut, 14.1% in Ulang, 15.8% in Longechuk and 10.2% in Maban. The prevalence of morbidity among children under 5 in the community at large remains high due to inaccessibility, low capacity at the healthcare facilities without support from INGOs, and frequent population movement as a result of conflict. Additionally, the under 5 mortality rate is remains high (i.e of 3.24 death /10,000 population/day in Maiwut) and requires a continuation of humanitarian services in Upper Nile. Moreover, women of reproductive age face serious health risks as they have inadequate access to skilled personnel during pregnancy and childbirth. Mental health cases and non-communicable diseases are somehow neglected, and those affected have no or limited access to services. To respond to these needs, RI will employ a combined approach including static and mobile clinics to provide comprehensive, integrated essential life saving services to hard to reach populations. This will be complemented by community based services through community health workers (CHWs) to ensure continuity of basic health services during displacement or closure of health facilities as a result of inaccessibility. Moreover, rapid response missions (RRMs) will be conducted to response to emergencies such as outbreaks or population displacement or return. An integrated approach will be utilized as exiting RI nutrition, WASH, and FSL programming will be linked to healthcare services.

Activities

- Improve access and scale-up responsiveness to essential health care needs: 1. Comprehensive quality curative primary health care at the static facility level (PHCC and PHCU). This is package is comprised of OPD for children under 5 and adults. Screening and management of non-communicable diseases and mental health and psychosocial support will be provided for patients. Maternal and child Healthcare will be provided through: antenatal care, deliveries, post natal care and timely clinical management of rape cases. 2. Integrated community case management (ICCM) of childhood illnesses will be provided; to improve access to health services, CHWs will receive training on ICCM guidelines and will be given equipment, drugs, and supplies to treat malaria, diarrhea and pneumonia in hard-to-reach communities. CHWs will also take the opportunity to screen children age 6-59 months for malnutrition by checking MUAC and oedema and will refer any malnourished case to the nearest nutrition site. This ICCM activity in addition to prepositioning of medicines will be of high importance during the rainy season where flooding will prevent some access the health facilities 3. Mobile clinics to provide the minimum curative and preventive package of lifesaving health services to hard-to-reach communities, IDPs, and influxes of returnees. 4. RRMs will be provided to assess displacement of populations, including returnees from Ethiopia, who may come following hopes of renewed peace. Any suspicion of disease outbreak will be investigated for prompt action through RRMs. The emergency response plan for malaria, measles, meningitis and hepatitis E in the five targeted counties will be updated. 5. Capacity building of health staff and CHWs to address various primary health care topics including but not limited to management of common illness, management of non-communicable diseases, mhGAP, surveillance, and/or will be conducted. - Prevent, detect and respond to epidemic prone diseases:1. RI will strengthen disease surveillance through completeness and timely reporting of IDSR and EWARs in identified health facilities2. Review and update the preparedness and response plan for malaria/measles/ cholera/meningitis and hepatitis E outbreak in the five counties.3. Strengthen EPI services at the facility level and conduct EPI outreach activities coupled with deworming and vitamin A supplementation to improve measles and Vit A coverage among children under 5.4. Integrate WASH response in all health facilities to ensure availability of clean water at the facility level, work with RI WASH programming to construct gender-segregated latrines in health care facilities for improved sanitation, as well as raise awareness on proper hygiene (including the provision of handwashing stations at all healthcare facilities, both static and mobile).

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Messages will be provided in both verbal form as well as through visual aids. Mass messaging for all groups will be provided in a generic format to blanket-reach beneficiaries. To reach the most vulnerable including women, girls, the elderly, and disabled, specific messaging including house-to-house visits and messaging by female staff and CHWs will be provided.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Separate discussion, consultations will be held through meetings and FGDs with specific age groups including adolescent girls and young mothers (below 18) for specific topics or issue related to their age (EPI, GMP, MIYCN, prevention of malnutrition). The same type of discussion will be conducted with men to actively engage them in the program for better acceptance of services including but not limited to family planning, delivery in facility, male condoms distribution, and EPI. Discussions with the elderly will also be organized to understand specific needs and identify how best they can be addressed.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

At field level all Relief International new staff attend mandatory training on sexual exploitation and abuse; refresher sessions will be conducted for other staff on a bi-annual basis. The community and beneficiaries will also be oriented on RI PSEA policies. In collaboration with the community, a feedback and complain mechanism will be established for cases of SEA. This platform will serve as a bridge between RI and the beneficiaries and will facilitate sensitization for prevention of SEA. Moreover, an RI focal person for SEA has been identified in each site to collect at the field level any case and treat it as per RI policy through thorough reporting including to the Country Director and headquarters.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Local leaders - informal and informal have been consulted on a regular basis throughout 2018 for feedback on 2019 programming through formal and informal meetings. Consultation with beneficiaries also occurred through meetings with patients at the time of consultation as well as through feedback meetings with CHWs and the CHD. Beneficiaries of RI's 2018 programming are consulted on a regular basis for feedback on programming and quality of service to improve and enhance programming in 2019. Additionally, coordination meetings with national and international NGOs take place in Juba and in the field to review ongoing programming and plan for2019.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Feedback and complaint mechanisms are established through multiplemechanisms including: - compliant boxes at health facilities (mobile and static) sites - anonymous and non-anonymous reporting mechanisms to a reporting focal point in each of RI'sfield offices and health care facilities. - Supervisory and beneficiary feedback meetings conducted by senior monitoring staff.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Monitoring and evaluation includes patient follow-up, site/household visits, as well as regular supervision by field and Juba staff. The community participates in monitoring through regualr feedback received at the facilitiy and through CHWs. the communtiy, together with local authorities and the CHD assit RI in resuloving disputes and any potential problems which may arrise. Additionally, the community participates in monitoring through established complaints mechanisms outside of the facility, wherein they can immediately report to RI and local authorities any violations. Moreover, the community is consulted through one-on-one interviews and group discussions on improvements in programming for 2019.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Protection risks include the following: - HIV/TB patients not accessing services due to stigmatization.- Mental health and psychosocial patients not accessing services due to stigmatization.- Insecurity for women in most of the counties where the facilities are at 2 or 3 walking days.- Communal differences at the payam level can reduce access to services- Some communities may not access the services during the rainy season due to flooding. RI is addressing these specific stigmatization and protection needs through targeted messaging, communal meetings to resolve disputes, consultations with local authorities and partner NGOs, as well as through consultations with the CHWs.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

At the start of the project and during implementation, consultation at the community level will be conducted to identify the root causes of stigmatization of mental health patients as well as TB and HIV patients. Feedback, conceptions of the community will be analysed and a context-based strategy will be designed to address identified barriers to access the services. Regular awareness campaigns on HIV, TB, and mental health will be conducted.For the communal differences that may arise and which prevent some communities to attend the health services as well as inaccessibility due to rains and/or conflict, mobile clinics will be utilized and outreach activities will be applied whenever possible. Consultation will also be conducted between the program team, local authorities, beneficiaries and the community at large to explore other options to serve better people in need.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Inequalities are addressed mainly through the prioritization of women in beneficiary selection as well as in decision-making processes. Women are prioritized for hiring, and are consulted on all decision-making procedures to ensure their voice is heard and considered. The threat of coercion andcommunity tension is mitigated through involvement in programming by local leaders and beneficiaries, to ensure transparent and accountable mechanisms.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Serving beneficiaries without harming their dignity is part of RI fundamental values and as such all staff will be oriented on how to preserve the dignity of beneficiaries in each stage of treatment, from their reception to their release. This orientation will provide the knowledge and skill to the staff to ensure that all verbal and non-verbal communication with the beneficiaries will ensure respect to beneficiaries, respect privacy of beneficiaries, confidentiality and right to listen from and to the beneficiaries. This dignity will be measured during a midyear FGD to gain feedback of beneficiaries on their feelings and their impression on the relationship with the health staff.
