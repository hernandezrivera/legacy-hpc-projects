Project Summary

WHO in its 2018 country cooperative strategy stated that South Sudan has some of the worst health outcome indicators globally: maternal mortality ratio stands at 789 per 100,000 live births, whereas neonatal and under-five mortality rates are 39.3 and 99.2, per 1000 live births respectively (2014). The immunization coverage in South Sudan is also low and outbreak such as measles are common. Access to clean drinking water is very limited and open defection is a common practice. Household and community hygiene and sanitation levels are extremely poor throughout the country. All these are drivers that increase cases of acute malnutrition in children under five and PLWs.
The Greater Equatorial region has been severely affected by insecurity and the economic crisis, which have disrupted agriculture-based livelihoods –and for a traditionally surplus-producing area, this has been hard-hitting. Farmers were not able to safely access their fields to plant, fishers unable to venture out to fishing grounds and traders limited by hyperinflation, lack of hard currency, insecurity and access. More than 1 million people from Greater Equatorial have fled to Uganda, citing insecurity and hunger as the main reasons for fleeing. Displacement has left many farmers without access to the tools and inputs needed to restart their livelihood activities when they return to their lands or settle with host communities.
Greater Bahr el-Ghazal, a region traditionally dependent on markets, has been heavily impacted by disrupted trade routes and diminished purchasing power of the local currency. As a result of these factors, as well as inter-communal conflict, massive displacement and climate extremes that have affected production, the region faces record levels of food insecurity, with the largest number of food insecure people. In the Greater Upper Nile, recurrent fighting has caused massive displacement, impacting livelihoods and disrupting humanitarian access. The conflict has also affected livestock migratory routes, resulting in an increase of livestock disease incidents and compounded by weakened capacity to respond. This situation has also affected the nutrition situation of family, household, especially children and women.
The overall situation of acute malnutrition has slightly improved this year compared to the same period last year with no counties reporting extreme critical levels (GAM above 30%) of acute of malnutrition in 2018. But the level of acute malnutrition is still high and this can be attributed to severe food insecurity, poor access to health and nutrition services, high morbidity, extremely poor diets and poor sanitation and hygiene. Levels of acute malnutrition are expected to improve marginally between October and December 2018 due to the seasonal availability of local production, increased availability of fish and milk, and relatively better access to markets and key services. However, levels of acute malnutrition are expected to deteriorate in the first quarter of 2019 with an early onset of the lean season as most households are expected to deplete food stocks from their own production. In 2018, World Vision South Sudan conducted a SMART survey. The findings showed a Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate in Gogrial East County of 18.0% (14.0-22.8 95% CI), while severe and acute malnutrition (SAM) prevalence was found to be 2.8% (1.7- 4.7 95% CI). In Tonj North, the GAM rate for the County was 11.6% (9.0 – 14.9 95% C.I.), and the severe acute malnutrition (SAM) rate was 3.1% (1.8 – 5.3 95% C.I.).). In this assessment, no cases of edema were observed. The findings indicate a serious level (GAM 10.0-14.9%) phase of malnutrition according to WHO classification. Although there is a decrease in GAM and SAM rates compared to July 2017 results {14.7% (95%CI: 12.5-17.3), July 2017}, the deterioration is not statistically significant, and the levels indicate a sustained serious nutrition situation compared to July 2017. There was no SMART data for certain counties in 2018 due lack of funding. It is important to note that in 2017 the GAM rate in Upper Nile, Western Equatorial and Juba were respectively as follows: Juba (14.6%), 5.1% (Ezo, Nagero, Nzara, Yambio, Tambura), 21.8% ( Fashoda, Manyo, Panykang, Melut and Baliet), 22% ( Gogrial East), 22.8% ( Gorgrial West), 14.7% (Tonj North), and 35.9% ( Twic county). Western Equatorial has experienced armed conflict this year, since May which has led to increase internal displacement. In 2019, WVSS intends to continue providing emergency nutrition interventions in Upper Nile, Warrap, Western Equatorial, and Central Equatorial states. WVSS will be targeting 1,938 SAM children and 4,776 MAM children.

Needs

Under the 2019 Humanitarian response plan, World Vision South Sudan will prioritize its nutrition response on the provision of life-saving treatment services targeting children under five and pregnant and lactating women because they are the most vulnerable groups to acute malnutrition due to their increased biological and physiological needs. Prevention activities will be incorporated in the response to reduce incidence of acute malnutrition and promote good health. In line with the nutrition cluster HRP 2019 strategic paper, WVSS intends to implement an integrated approach where emergency nutrition intervention will be integrated into other sectors such as health, WASH, and food security and livelihood. WVSS will provide the emergency community based management of acute malnutrition (community mobilization, Outpatient therapeutic programme for treatment of severe acute malnutrition without medical complication, Inpatient treatment of severe acute malnutrition with medical complications, Targeted and Blanket supplementary feeding programme ) and Maternal infant and child nutrition practices in the community and at the nutrition facilities in the former Warrap, Central Equatorial, Western Equatoria, and Upper Nile states. Most of the nutrition services will be provided through static site mostly in secure and accessible areas. Outreach services will also be provided in sites with expansive catchment areas. In 2019, World Vision South Sudan will establish a Rapid Response mechanism (RRM) which will be used as the mode of response in insecure and inaccessible locations. As per the Ministry of Health of South Sudan and the nutrition cluster guidelines, WVSS will ensure that most of the nutrition sites are integrated in health facility as this will strengthen and ensure the continuum of care for the targeted beneficiaries.

Activities

In line with the nutrition cluster strategy for the 2019 HRP, World Vision South Sudan will provide lifesaving emergency nutrition services focusing on the following activities:  Treatment of severe acute malnutrition among children 6-59 months  Treatment of moderate acute malnutrition among children 6-59m months Training of health and nutrition care providers on management of acute malnutrition Provision of BSFP for under-fives and PLWs in prioritized counties Provision of quality MIYCN services in nutrition sites and community level Conduct nutrition SMART surveys in prioritized counties Carrying out testing and treatment of malaria among children with SAM aged 0/6-59 months Provide WASH NFIs kits to children with SAM in nutrition sites

WVSS being a child-focused organization has a child protection policy. This policy is especially important to the nutrition department, as the staff in this department are in regular contact with children, and other vulnerable groups in the community. It is mandatory for all WVSS staff to adhere to the child protection policy, or otherwise face disciplinary action, which includes termination and prosecution. Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Safety Audits are completed for each project site and/or service point, with recommendations used to informed the continued use of the location and any adaptions that are required to improve the safety of the site / service point (or reaching this location). Safety Audits are completed prior to establishment of the project site or service point (ex. WGSS) and repeated quarterly or ad hoc, for example in the instance of population displacement or significant event that may be perceived to change local dynamics. Safety Audits are complemented by Safety Perception mapping, which captures the safety issues or unsafe areas perceived by beneficiaries, which may be different from observation; this is completed quarterly or ad hoc as necessary and used comparatively to inform project implementation and any adaptations as necessary.
