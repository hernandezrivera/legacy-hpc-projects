1 - Needs

This disaster has had serious consequences for families on several levels, affecting their shelter, health, hygiene, protection, livelihoods and food security. The well-being of children is also affected by the distress and post-traumatic stress caused by the experience of the explosion. On the other hand, displacement puts them at risk of violence, abuse and exploitation. Some are even at greater risk of being separated from their parents. Child Protection concerns - Families' safety nets have been affected. Psychosocial well-being: The explosion itself, in addition to the experiences and images of destruction and violence, will have short-, medium- and long-term effects on the well-being of individuals, especially children, who may suffer from post-traumatic stress excessive behaviour, anxiety, among other symptoms. In addition, movement/relocation can negatively impact the well-being of children.- Child protection: A large number of children have been separated from their families. Children in areas where child labour is rife have been affected (e.g. Nabaa, Bourj Hammoud), and there is a need to mitigate the risks of neglect, abuse and violence to children as well as to promote and respond to the risks they face in temporary shelters set up by humanitarian actors.

2 - Activities

Psychological First Aid (PFA) and Psychosocial Support (PSS) will be provided to affected girls and boys on a group level. The aim of this support is to decrease the psychosocial distress, improve girls' and boys' well-being and their emotional resilience, and refer medium to high risk cases to case management and other service providers to address urgent needs. The sessions will be conducted in community centers or established tents in affected communities and it will be open to all children/adolescents as long as they need to attend, focusing on affected girls and boys. The session will be around 1,5 hours and will include 10 children maximum due to COVID-19.Activities include culturally appropriate activities with PSS objectives such as drama, crafts, traditionally storytelling, sports and session with key CP-GBV messages and facilitated by trained animators (2 animators per group) and one team leader will be overseeing the whole implementation and supporting in referrals, distribution and reporting.The project will provide PSS kits to affected girls and boys. The distribution of kits aims to support children and their families cope with the distress and harsh conditions due to the blast and enhancing adolescents’ well-being through fun and recreational games. The kit will include age-appropriate items, such as a board games, mandala colouring book, colouring pencils, stress ball, jumping rope, white board, etc.While providing PFA and PSS and also during distribution, animators might identify children and/or families with immediate and urgent needs. With the support of animators in collecting the needed information, the team leader will make referrals to other, external service providers specialized in health, education, house repairing and rehabilitation, food security etc. All referrals and the overall coordination of services provided to the child or his family are managed by the team leader. To facilitate effective referrals, the team leader will depend on the service mapping to know about the available services in the area and will conduct bi-weekly follow-ups to make sure the child and his family are receiving the appropriate services from the receiving agency.Caregivers will be targeted through awareness raising messages through different channels (WhatsApp, social media, posters, etc) on how to provide care and protection to their children after the blast.This awareness raising includes:a) Information on how to psychologically support their children after the blast, explain what happened and letting them express their feelings.b) Information on how to recognize signs and symptoms of distress and the importance of reporting to the team leader or animators.c) Dissemination of referral pathways and hotline numbers. Plan Lebanon will target a total of 500 children (0-17), 350 children through In-Kind cash support and 150 children through In-Hand cash support. Plan Lebanon will prioritize vulnerable adolescent girls and boys and their families who have been affected by the Beirut Port Explosion. The Cash for Protection (CfP) will be utilized for urgent provision of cash and in-kind assistance for children and their families affected by Beirut Explosion who are at high-risk or have increased vulnerability due to the explosion. This includes children who have been hospitalized, Unaccompanied or separated children, children with a damaged house, displaced children, children with disabilities, headed household with disability, children survivors of or at risk of exploitation/abuse, and child headed households.
