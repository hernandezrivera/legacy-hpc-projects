Project Summary

This Project seek to improve access and utilization of health services to people in need in Jonglei and lake states with focus on post conflict movement, flood displacement and food insecure vulnerable population in Bor South, Duk, Twic east and Awerial, living in IPC4 counties. Targeting 40,241 beneficiaries segregated as 19,718 Male, 20,523 Females, 11,670 Boys and 10,865 Girls. Our focus will be Increasing access to quality integrated lifesaving health interventions, prevent, detect and respond to epidemic prone diseases and Integrate Wash/Nutrition/food security and protection and strengthen the capacities of health services to reduce morbidity/mortality of common disease among the vulnerable groups. The project aim to improve the health status of the IDPs and host communities through provision of primary health care services in form of PHCCs, PHCUs and Mobile units to reaching everyone and supporting the most vulnerable group.
The health services package include:Disease surveillance OPD consultation, IDP services, Immunization, against preventable diseases, maternal & Newborn services (ANC, reproductive health,) distribution of Vit A, De-worming, Clinical management of Rape (CMR) trauma, GBV, Disability and Referral services. Health promotion, education and awareness raising about epidemic prone diseases (COVID-19, Cholera and measles). Screening, detection and management of medical complication in malnourish children under 5 years. Our Humanitarian response is to support 4 PHCCs, 4 PHCUs and 6 Mobiles units in the 4 targeted IPC4 counties of Bor South, Duk, Twic East and Awerial Counties.

1 - Needs

Two Year after the Signing of revitalized peace agreement, its implementation has not reduced the humanitarian needs of the South Sudanese people. Lack of durable peace and limited investment in basic services are holding people back from stability and sustainable development. In 2020 communities were hit hard by the triple shock of intensified conflict and sub-national violence, a second consecutive year of major flooding, and the impacts of COVID-19. Some 1.6 million remained internally displaced and another 2.2 million as refugees in the region. Around 1.4 million South Sudanese children under age 5 and another 483,000 pregnant women are expected to be acutely malnourished in 2021(HNO2021). Vast inequities in geographical spread and coverage of health services due to inadequate health infrastructure, human resources, funding and essential commodities for health implementation have resulted in poor health outcome that will impact the well being south Sudanese . An estimated 2.4 million people will be targeted by the Health clusters in 2021 as people in need of health services with 50 % female, 52% children and 15 % with disability (2021 HRP_Health Cluster respond plan). Only 7.5 per cent of the 1,763 reporting health facilities provide basic health services. According to South Sudan integrated Diseases Surveillance and Response system, Malaria remains the top cause of morbidity (64 per cent) and Mortality (45 per cent) (HNO).Low Immunization coverage throughout the Country, with Measles outbreaks been recorded in Eight counties, Including Bor South 2020. Planned mass vaccination campaigns were disrupted by the COVID-19 containment measures put in place. Improved access to essential health services will contribute to the overall reduction of the maternal mortality rates (789 deaths per 100,000 live births) and under-five mortality rates (90.7 deaths per 1,000 live births),in South Sudan.. The Sudan Medical Care will involve the communities and existing development health programs along with the County Health Department in the planning and organization of emergency health services . (2021-HRP_Health Cluster respond plan ) The Integrated Food security phase classification Analysis released on 18th December 2020, classify Awerial, Bor South, Duk,and Twic East has Emergency counties (IPC4).The Severe food Insecure counties are those with chronic vulnerabilities attributed to combination of shocks like conflicts, flooding, and macro-economic crisis. Other factors are an earlier depletion of food stock, limited access to livestock and increasing market prices. These areas have limited access to optimal health services. Our target population is 53,654(26,521M 27,133F) for the project, with Jonglei counties representing 71 % of the Population and 29% in Lakes state; the vulnerable are Mothers, Children and PwDs that will require more focus and specific intervention,health services, GBV/CMR & protection.Segregated by counties: Bor South 14,532 (7,411M 7,121 F), Duk 22,191(10,874M 11,317F) and Twic East 1508 (679 M 8,29F),while Awerial County population is 15,423 (7557M7866 F) all the four counties are IPC4. The prevailing health system challenges will require the adoption of a holistic integrated response to achieve the intended service delivery outcomes, by training Health care workers, improve infrastructure development, manage and eliminate malnutrition as well as strengthen surveillance activities to prevent and reduce the spread of COVID 19, Measles, Cholera, EVD among other highly contagious diseases. Some counties are cholera hotspot (Duk) for cholera outbreak and will require constant surveillance and increase level of service deliveries, like wise Awerial, Bor South and Twic East with sizeable IDPs and vulnerable host communities with need of health, nutrition & sanitation facilities. This require our robust response in form of integrated services to support 4 PHCCs, 4 PHCUs and 6Mobiles units reaching everyone.
