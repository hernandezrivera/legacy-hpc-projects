Project Summary

Afghan refugees in Pakistan constitute one of the world’s biggest refugees’ population with more than 2.3 million persons. Pakistan is currently hosting more than 1.39 million registered afghan refugees, whereas an estimated 1 million refugees are still unregistered. 880,000 refugees had been issued Afghan citizenship cards (ACC) in 2017 under the National Action Plan to register undocumented persons living in Pakistan. After 2016 and a high repatriation spell, there has been a decline in refugees’ repatriation. During 2018, 14,017 registered and 30,692 unregistered afghan refugee families were repatriated to Afghanistan . Given the situation in Afghanistan, the return of Afghan refugees remains a distant possibility as majority of them do not intend to return to their country of origin in the near future. After an agreement between UNHCR and the Pakistan government, 0.88 million registered ACC card holders were given the right to stay in Pakistan.
The life in refugee camps incorporates many psychological issues beside other detrimental effects. They are living with an unknown future, without any support and guidance and are heading towards an unknown direction. Moreover, the market disruptions and lockdowns as a result of the COVID-19 situation have significantly affected the livelihood of afghan refugees in the country who are mostly unskilled labor and rely on daily wage employment. Over 60 percent of their expenditures are related to food and related items. These family suffer from food insecurity as they have no resources to purchase food for household consumption amid market disruptions and lockdowns.
According to the door to door assessment conducted by Secours Islamique France (SIF) from 2017 to 2020, carried out on 5,744 afghan refugees’ households a majority of the individuals in the Afghan refugees’ camps are engaged in daily wages labors work, as this is the major source of their livelihoods. A majority of the Afghan refugees’ households were involved in daily wage labors followed by casual labor including construction labor, shop keeping, agri-farm crop sale, livestock, tailoring, loading/unloading of commodities and goods as well as small businesses.
SIF has a track record of working with Afghan refugees and host communities in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP) since 2014, implementing Food Security and Livelihood related projects to improve living condition of the host and Afghan refugees’ communities through improving their technical employable skills, financial support for establishment of small scale enterprise development and linking them with market along with other long term integrated development as well as emergency response projects.
Through this proposed project, SIF will further ensure to improve the living conditions of the targeted communities in the selected project locations by providing them relevant vocational trainings and financial support to establish their livelihood means for sustainable development of the host as well as refugees communities. Host communities have also been immensely affected by the situation, loss of employment and market disruptions have resulted in increased food insecurity among host communities as well. Considering the vulnerability context and needs of the refugees and its adjoining host communities,6 locations were selected for the proposed project. As SIF is having an extensive working experience in the same localities since the last 4-5 years and a strong context knowledge, geographical understanding, and more importantly technical experience and expertise in livelihood sector, the desired objective of the project thus can be achieved while engaging communities and other relevant stakeholders very easily. In addition, SIF has been conducting a similar livelihood project and has built strong relationships with the local communities and would be able through this much needed intervention to strengthen existing communities structures.

1- Project Activities

The proposed project aims to address the food security and livelihoods needs of refugee’s and host communities families which are in dire need of support through food security, skill development and micro enterprise development interventions. Under the proposed project, SIF aims to provide immediate support for food security, livelihoods, agriculture and livestock services through the following activities:1: Vocational skills and enterprise development trainings: Employable skills trainings will be imparted to 700 semi-skilled Afghan refugees youth in camps as well as some beneficiaries in host communities in locally applicable/employable trades. Besides technical trainings, the trainees will also be equipped with business management skills like marketing, book keeping, customer dealing and other management skills. The purpose of the programmed is to provide income and employment opportunities for the targeted families. It will provide alternate source of income for 700 youth of the targeted area that will result in local economic development. Interested individuals will be identified in the targeted area. For this purpose, a MoU will be signed with a training institute registered with Skills Development Council and Technical Training Board (TTB). The main activities carried out will be:• Tailoring trainings• Plumbing trainings• Electrician trainings• Mobile repairing trainings • Refrigeration and air-condition mechanic • UPS & solar panel installation mechanic 2: Distribution of poultry: Poultry will be distributed amongst 500 families to support their livelihood at households’ level. Each family will receive a set of 5 hens and 1 rooster. This will benefit targeted households in the following ways:• Source of income: The households can sell the eggs and generate income to support their livelihoods. They can also reproduce more poultry to expand their income over time.• Mainstreaming gender: Looking after poultry and gathering eggs will be the responsibility of the woman in the family. This will involve women in the economic cycle as they can sell eggs, generate income and these beneficiaries will be linked with concerned shop keepers. SIF field staff will ensure that women are getting actual benefit from these interventions. This will be further ensured through sensitizing men and women while engaging them in community level meetings.• Food security: Some households might consume some eggs obtained from the poultry provided as this will also contribute to assisting these households with nutritious food assistance and will also contribute to combating healthcare issues through improved health status.3: Distribution of goats: The most vulnerable families, having some potential for management and animals rearing skills will be provided goats for earning purposes. 500 families will be provided a set of 2 goats. This activity will not only supplement in developing livelihoods but will also help in securing food security for the beneficiaries through milk production.4: Agriculture/kitchen gardening trainings and provision of seeds and tools: 400 individuals (female) will be trained in agriculture/kitchen gardening trainings and also provided with quality seeds and tools for kitchen gardening/agriculture as well as farming purpose. A group of 400 female headed households will be selected for kitchen gardening/agriculture activities. Most of the vegetables like Tomatoes, Green Chillies, Brinjal, Cucumber, Okra, Bottle Gourd, Sponge Gourd, Bitter Gourd, Pumpkin, Arum, Mint, Turmeric, Ginger, etc. can be produced. Varieties of vegetable will be selected based on needs identified by the targeted groups.
