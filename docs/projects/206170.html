Project Summary


In 2023, approximately 3 million people in South Sudan will struggle to meet their basic needs in SNFI due to the continued destruction of houses and loss of essential goods from the ongoing floods and conflict. The increment is attributed to the current economic crisis in the country, new displacements and ongoing floods and conflict, mainly in Jonglei, Lakes, and part of Western Bahr el Ghazel States. Of the total population in need of SNFI assistance, 58 per cent are IDPs, 33 per cent are returnees and 12 per cent are vulnerable host community members according to the 2023 HNO.
TITI Foundation Shelter and NFI response will provide time-critical and life-saving assistance to populations facing greatest needs due to displacement through i) improved access to safe, appropriate emergency shelter and life-saving non-food items to newly displaced and the most vulnerable populations in secondary displacement and ii) improved living conditions of highly vulnerable IDPs in protracted displacement, refugees, returnees and host communities unable to meet their S/NFI needs. The response will be targeted,
based on assessed needs and the context. The response will support populations in Lakes state, Jonglei and Northern Bahrel ghazal with repair and replacement materials, construction of shelters in case of influx, as the static SNFI partner.
TITI Foundation aims to focus 15% of its interventions on cash in locations where security is more stable with functioning and accessible markets. This will give beneficiaries more flexibility in meeting their needs and also contribute to market recovery. TITI will consult with beneficiaries prior to distributions to ensure that shelter and NFI materials are as socially and culturally accepted. The total targeted beneficiaries will be 30,556 individuals with various S/NFI needs.
More distress and havoc have affected the communities with Flooding causing more harm hence Internally displacing people in Lakes state and northern Bahrel ghazal .
The effects of communal conflicts have been very drastic in the areas of Jonglei and Lake states with sporadic attacks that are a result of revenge killing and cattle rustling. Gender-based violence cuts across all three states hence the need for TITI Foundation to ensure adherence to the Do No Harm principle and to reduce community tensions. The most vulnerable host community or returnee populations will also be selected as vulnerable and not exclusive IDPs. Beneficiaries will be selected based on strict criteria identified with the participation of the communities to ensure needs are properly identified. The most vulnerable will be prioritized including PLW, households with the largest number of under 5 children, child-headed households, disabled, elderly, widows, orphans, and vulnerable female-headed households. TITI Foundation will conduct a rapid distribution Monitoring and PDM after two to four weeks of distribution to ensure satisfaction on the needs of the community beneficiaries are of high quality and respond to their intended purpose.

1. Needs

In 2023, approximately 3 million people in South Sudan will struggle to meet their basic needs in SNFI due to the continued destruction of houses and loss of essential goods from the ongoing floods and conflict. The increment is attributed to the current economic crisis in the country, new displacements in Tonga and ongoing floods and conflict, mainly in Jonglei, Lakes, Upper Nile, Unity and part of Western Bahr el Ghazel States. Without political stability and peace, people may continue to be affected by violence in 2023 with subsequent displacement. Localized outbreaks of sub-national violence will likely continue to drivedisplacement and destruction of shelters and household items. Another year of sustained flooding will cause further destruction of shelters and depletion of household goods and may further drive temporaryand permanent displacement. The lack of flood-resilient construction and continued depletion of natural resources for temporary and transitional shelter continues to drive vulnerability, placing households in a continuous pattern of emergency need without the opportunity for recovery. areas with functional markets lacking essential shelter materials and NFIs. This presents particular challenges to displaced people who have faced multiple displacements as their resources likely have been depleted and they face competition for items with local communities. In addition to the destruction of shelters and NFIs, three consecutive years of flooding have led to an increase in longer-term displacement and relocation of communities from areas that have been completely inundated and are prone to flooding in the rainy season. This will exacerbate existing issues related to the security of tenure among communities and may drive non-displaced people or those who do not rely on humanitarian shelter and NFI support to seek managed sites to access resources. This decreases the resilience and coping mechanisms of affected people and residents of formal sites continue to remain extremely dependent on humanitarian support to meet all basic needs. South Sudan continues to receive returnees from neighboring countries. The pace of returns held steady in 2021 before increasing sharply in October 2022 with more than 60,000 returns registered in that month alone and over 300000 after the outbreak of war in Sudan in 2023. Returnees usually arrive with minimal resources after walking for days or weeks and need shelter and NFI support. The multiple displacements and compounding factors created a situation where affected populations do not have access to the market, nor the means to obtain basic household NFI items, hence the call for external support through this project.In 23 counties, more than half of the affected people require emergency shelter support, particularly in Jonglei, Lakes, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Unity and Upper Nile states, areas with the highest concentration of displaced people. TITI Foundation will target 30,556 individuals newly displaced people, IDPs, host communities and returnees unable to meet their SNFI needs. The intervention will be applying the WHO COVID-19 guidelines and measures to mitigate the spread of the virus to the vulnerable HHs hence avoiding causing more harm to the population. The measures will highly consider social distancing, hand washing with soap, clean water and the use of face masks. TITI Foundation interventions will be based on multisector needs assessments preceding any particular response. This will allow TITI Foundation to respond to the specific needs of the displaced communities, being fully accountable through the identification andassessment of their needs according to the cluster strategy. TITI Foundation Emergency Preparedness and Support have extensive experience in conducting Emergency NFI response activities in Jonglei, lakes and Northern Bahrel ghazal States.

2. Project activities

The project is in line with the S/NFI Cluster objectives to Improve access to safe, appropriate emergency shelter and life-saving NFIs to newly displaced or vulnerable populations in secondary displacement and the living conditions of highly vulnerable IDPs in protracted displacement, returnees and host communities unable to meet their S/NFI needs to rebuild lives through coordinated, integrated and targeted S/NFIs solutions. TITI Foundation will conduct a targeted assessment based on the need to support the mostvulnerable IDPs, host communities and returnees to improve their living conditions and ensure privacy and dignified conditions. 1. TITI Foundation will use its two response modalities to respond to emergency shelter and NFI needs for the vulnerable population across Lakes, Jonglei and Northern Bahrel ghazal in correlation with the protection team: a) TITI will focus on CVA projects where there will be voucher distribution of unconditional but restricted commodity vouchers for locally available shelter, and NFI materials. CVA interventions will be based on functional and accessible markets through conducting rapid market needs assessments, needs analysis among the beneficiaries and provision of conditional or unconditional cash support; Titi CVA project staff will enhance sustainability through the mobilization of Locally available materials for shelter, repair and rehabilitation as per the prescribed standards and; Formation of a Community Lead distribution committee comprising both men and women for transparency. this will be combined with the In-kind (reconstruction and rehabilitation) needs analysis in areas that are hard to reach. 2. Conducting beneficiaries' identification, verification and registration based on the most critical needs through the TITI Foundation emergency response team in collaboration with the host community leaders, local authorities and beneficiary’s leaders. Based on the communicated criteria to the Leaders, the TITI static team will disaggregate the population by gender, age and vulnerability. 3. Cluster approach will be utilized by the static team during the distribution of the Emergency Shelter, NFIs items from the Cluster Pipeline. 5. Quality post-distribution monitoring: Post distribution monitoring (PDM) s for up to 50% of our distribution will be conducted, depending on a location accessible and security within a month after our NFIs distributions. PDMs assess beneficiary satisfaction, quality and quantity of the NFIs items distributed. Feedback on the PDM reports will be shared with the beneficiary community, partners and NFI clusters. 6. CFM/Communication and Community Engagement; in caseof the emergence of complaints or feedback that can be channeled through both static response team and CVA for shelter (Reconstruction & rehabilitation) project. Effective messaging around the associated potential impacts of responses on protection, environment and usage of the distributed materials, and translation of all IEC materials to local languages will be a priority.Project indicators# of people reached with in-kind standard emergency shelter # of people reached with in-kind standard lifesaving NFIs # of people reached with flood kits # of people reached with essential emergency shelter upgrades # of people reached with essential NFI replacements # of people reached with cash and voucher for essential emergency shelter upgrades # of people reached with cash and voucher for essential NFI replacements # of Partners trained on CVA programming % of beneficiaries who have access to safe, childand gender-sensitive complaints, feedback and reporting channels in humanitarian response sites for reporting sensitive issues and concerns in aid delivery and misconduct by aid workers disaggregated by age, sex and diversity % of national and local organizations and community members involved in decision-making mechanisms.
