Project Summary

This project is designed in line with the Humanitarian Response Plan 2020 published by OCHA in March 2020 and WFP Country Strategic Plan (2018-2020).
In 2020, the HRP mentionned that humanitarian community will pay particular attention to the protection of people most at risk, including:
1. Internally displaced people, especially women and girls, and those in areas that are hard to access like the Menchum Valley;
2. Children and adolescents, especially girls and young men, affected by the crisis in the North West and South West;
3. Residents of villages affected by the violence in the North West and South West, including affected host populations and people living with disabilities;
4. People returned to their villages of origin in the Far-North, North West and South West, especially women-headed households.
Based on the HRP 2020 mentionned before, 839,229 vulnerable people, over half of them within the North West and South West regions, are targeted to receive unconditional food support through food and cash transfers in 2020. 120,750 children between 6 and 23 months are targeted with supplementary feeding and severely malnourished children will be provided with integrated care. 464,210 women are targeted to be assisted during deliveries by a skilled health personnel. 37,818 vulnerable people in the Far North and the North West and South West regions are targeted with timely, appropriate life-sustaining emergency shelter support. In January 2019, WFP published an Emergency Food Security Assessment - EFSA (North West, South West). The key findings show that across regions, a total of approximately 1.5 million people are food insecure, thereof 906,461 (60%) in North West and 600,952 (40%) in South West. Results show that food insecurity in both regions is more prevalent among IDP than non-IDP populations as measured by the Food Security Index (FSI). EFSA findings also reveal a rapid deterioration of the nutrition situation with SAM prevalence increasing by more than three times in South West and by more than five times in North West (from 0.4% and 0.5% in 201412 to 1.5% and 2.8% in 2019). An estimated 16,335 SAM and 20,175 MAM children are in need for immediate treatment. Based on the EFSA findings and expected incidence of both SAM and MAM, an estimated 76,580 children will be affected by acute malnutrition if no appropriate response is provided on time. In this framework, WFP establish that intervention packages should comprise case management of moderately malnourished children and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) while ensuring continuum prevention-treatment- prevention among the most affected population to prevent further deterioration of nutritional situation putting the children in high risks of morbidity and death.
Based on the needs highlight before, Caritas – Bamenda’s intervention will be designed to meet the emergency food needs of the vulnerable population through distributions of general food assistance with a fortified basket, whilst addressing the two underlying factors contributing to food insecurity and poor nutritional status identified in the problem analysis: scarcity/high cost of food and the collapse of livelihood opportunities. By adopting a holistic approach, Caritas would support IDPs and host communities who are food insecure to become more self-sufficient. Until further notice, Caritas Bamenda will implement mitigations measures and adapt its activities to avoid the risk of COVID-19 contamination.
Concerning accountability to the affected persons, Caritas Bamenda will placed suggestion boxes in all distribution sites, create a hotline, create committees at the level of communities to monitor implementation.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The proposed project aims to enhance food security of vulnerable conflict-affected households in the North West region and aligns with the 2021 HRP Food security cluster objectives. The overall objective of the project is to provide Emergency General Food Assistance to the Population of Menchum valley Subdivision and Bafut who are not yet receiving any food assistance( 7000males and 8000females)Strategic Objective 1: Ensure continued and regular access to adequate and nutritious general food assistance to vulnerable IDPs and host communities Strategic Objective 2: Improve the nutrition and the health status of vulnerable IDPs and host communities and prevent all forms of malnutrition of children aged 6–23 months and pregnant and lactating women (PLW)Daily rations will be distributed on monthly basis from January to December 2021 and CARITAS BAMENDA’ and specific arrengements as decribed in this proposal will be implemented to avoid COVID-19 contamination during this project.The Food Distribution Point’ areas and characteristics, number of days of distribution and number of beneficiaries are mentionned in the budget. The implementation will be flexible. Procurement and Food Delivery :Caritas is responsible for procurement and delivery of life saving food assistance. A food basket will be defined at the beginning of the project. Storage arrangement (location, contract duration)Warehouse unloading will be conducted by Caritas. Two warehouses with a capacity of 720 MT (located in Bamenda and Wum) have been identified for rental by Caritas-Bamenda to store food items from January 2021 to december 2021. When feasible, Carritas will establishe two storage sites in FDP to enable quick distributions.Distribution arrangement (beneficiary communication, FDP set up, beneficiary database): Distribution announcement: Caritas will will conduct transportantions and distributions on the same day. Caritas Bamenda will be responsible for forewarning local leaders and population in target villages of upcoming distribution 48 hours and 24 hours in advance to confirm the day of intervention and security conditions at the FDP. Local leaders will take a lead in mobilizing local communities about the time and location of distributions. Arrangements in FDP:During distributions, Caritas staff will cross-check beneficiary ID cards or other indentification gadgets based on the context, against approved beneficiary lists, after which beneficiaries will sign their name to confirm their presence and will then be directed to receive their food assistance. Caritas Bamenda will establish multiple distribution sites to avoid creating crowds. Distributions will mainly be done in selected distribution points however, house to house distribution will be also used to reach beneficiaries with specific needs. Volunteers who have influence, andare respected in the community by independent of armed groups will be recruited by Caritas to support the implementation. Distributions will take place during daylight hours to enable beneficiaries and teams to travel home safely. Caritas Bamenda will ensure when feaasible that women staff are present at FDPs.Supervision: Caritas Bamenda will put in place an appropriate mechanism to ensure the supervision of all activities under this project. Each distribution site will be led by a team leader supported by 6 community volunteers and 2 on-site volunteers representing the beneficiaries, responsible for Beneficiaries List management, crowd control, feedback and complaints desk, Psychosocial First Aid (PFA) and distribution of food rations. A total of 23 team leaders and team assistants as well as 230 community volunteers will be identified and trained before and during the overall project.Access arrangements if applicable.
