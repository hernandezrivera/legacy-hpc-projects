Project Summary

The food security and the nutrition situation remains fluid and fragile across the north-east. An estimated 1,600,367, 870,162 and 515,005 people in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa states respectively were in a crisis and emergency food insecurity situation throughout the months of June to August 2018 (March 2018 Cadre Haromonisé). Malnutrition is worse among certain population groups, for example IDPs who are arriving in ‘reception sites’ from inaccessible areas.
In 2019 the nutrition response aims at strengthening access to quality integrated nutrition services for the most vulnerable malnourished children and women in North-East Nigeria though strengthening of existing government health system, deployment of mobile nutrition outreach teams in hard-to-reach areas and ensuring coordinated and convergence approach. It will facilitate access to life-saving nutrition services through early detection and referral of malnourished children to nutrition service centres for appropriate nutrition services as well as strengthen government system and empower communities for better resilience.
In alignment with the sector response, UNICEF interventions will consider acute, moderate and chronic needs to reach 1,187,924 beneficiaries made up of 729,713 children and 458,211 women. An amount of $27,225,083 is required for the response.
Objective
• Enhance access to quality nutrition services for prevention and treatment of malnutrition among children, pregnant and lactating women.
• Strengthen capacity of nutrition service providers and community volunteers for timely identification, referral and follow up of malnourished cases and provision of appropriate nutrition services.
• Enhance routine monitoring of the nutrition situation through regular assessment and analysis of data.
• Strengthen coordination within the nutrition partners and other sectors to address the gaps and avoid duplication.

Needs

The ongoing conflict in North East Nigeria has led to increased population displacement, loss of livelihoods, food insecurity, malnutrition, inadequate health services and poor sanitation. The food security and nutrition situation remains fluid and fragile across the North-East, particularly given the high levels of aid dependency, compounded by the limited access to land and other livelihood opportunities. Preliminary results of the fifth round of the Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance showed spikes in GAM rates during the lean season (May to August) in North-East states, implying low resilience of the households. State level analysis also revealed that there are differences in contexts between states. Domain level analysis further show that contexts and severity differ even within the same state as evidenced by GAM rates which ranged from 8.8 per cent (East Borno), 14.9 per cent (Central Borno) both in Borno State; 10.9 per cent (South Yobe) to 14.1 per cent (Central Yobe) in Yobe State and 6.9 per cent (South Adamawa) to 10.1 Per cent (North Adamawa) in Adamawa. Sub-optimal Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices are still prevalent in locations within the North-East State. Findings from an assessment in February 2018 showed that less than six per cent of the children aged 6 to 23 months in the surveyed locations were receiving the minimum acceptable diet. The Cadre Haromonisé analysis completed in March 2018 estimated that 1,600,367; 870,162 and 515,005 people in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa state respectively were in a crisis and emergency food insecurity situation throughout the months of June to August 2018. The analysis recommended that in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe States there is need to sustain the ongoing humanitarian assistance and complement it with recovery and resilience interventions to promote self-reliance and prevent further deterioration of the food and nutrition security situation during the lean season.Nutrition surveys conducted by UNICEF and its partners show high rates of malnutrition in certain areas, and among specific population groups. A survey conducted by Save the Children International in Kaga, Jere, Konduga and Magumeri LGAs in August 2018 showed GAM above the emergency threshold, at 15.7 per cent. Among the most vulnerable are children arriving in ‘reception centers’ from inaccessible situation. A recent Standardized Monitoring and Assessment of Relief and Transitions (SMART) nutrition survey conducted by UNICEF in Bama found a global acute malnutrition rates of 37.5 per cent (as compared to a GAM rate of 7.8 per cent in the rest of the IDP camp) .

Activities

In 2019, UNICEF will respond under three main intervention areas. This is based on evolving local context, security situation and in alignment with the Nutrition Sector response. • Address acute humanitarian needs: IDPs and returnees make up a significant proportion of the population in several LGAs in Borno state - Bama, Gwoza, Dikwa, Kala-Balge, Ngala, Mobbar and Kukawa - where the nutrition situation remains critical. In these areas, UNICEF will prioritize life-saving nutrition interventions integrated with preventive components including detection and treatment of severe acute malnutrition (SAM), micro-nutrient supplementation and promotion of optimal IYCF practices, delivered through government health facilities as well as mobile/out-reach teams. UNICEF will aim to strengthen the nexus between the emergency nutrition response and development programmes in these areas to ensure life-saving service delivery. • Address moderate humanitarian needs: in several LGAs of South Borno and South Yobe, where the security situation is relatively more stable and people have resumed their normal social and agricultural activities, UNICEF will prioritize support to government and communities for transition to recovery and development through strengthening government health system for comprehensive nutrition service provision, community sensitization and empowerment for production and use of nutritious foods and advocacy for nutrition sensitive interventions. Considering the volatile and dynamic security situation in neighboring LGAs, preparedness plan for the provision of life-saving nutrition services will also be maintained to allow the flexibility to adapt UNICEF’s response as needed. • Addressing chronic needs: malnutrition was widespread in north-east Nigeria even before the conflict. In most LGAs in Adamawa state and some LGAs in Yobe state fall within this category, where people suffer from chronic nutritional needs that are have been indirectly exacerbated by the conflict, longer-term interventions are required to address the situation. Emphasis will be placed on system strengthening, including integration of nutrition services in existing government health system, advocacy for state governments’ allocation and release of funds for nutrition, community sensitization for consumption of locally available nutritious foods and leverage partnership to ensure availability of nutritious foods.
