Project Summary

SORDES integrated project package will target communities selected districts of Lower Juba and Gedo Region regions with the highest level of displacements, critical Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) above with lower than the national average access to water and sanitation. Due to the heavy rains, rescrticted access, water and food scarcity, loss of assets, displacement and disease outbreaks continue to be reported in these regions. SORDES proposes to provide an integrated humanitarian support to 15,000 affected beneficiaries.
Through:
• Improved access to emergency health services for 15000 individuals through fixed facility and mobile clinics.
promote hygiene and sanitation for 3000 HH
• Improved access to water and sanitation will be achieved through both water trucking and rehab/ construction of water sources as well as latrines. This activity will reach 10,000 beneficiaries Centrality of protection principles are embedded in this proposal. All the activities are complemented by the delivering of health and wash components. Men, women, boys and girls actively participate in the programme design and in the implementation through a gender sensitive approach. Disaggregate data will be recorded and analysed at every stage of the action.

Needs

Most parts of Somalia have received above average to average rainfall during the first months of the 2018 Gu season (February – May). The increase has resulted to the rising water levels of Rivers Shabelle and Juba, thereby leading to floods in Central and Southern Regions, most parts, being affected as well. Nearly 720,000 people have been directly affected with 220,000 people displaced. The floods have led to damage of crops, shelters and critical service infrastructure, including water, health, nutrition and education facilities. Additionally, there is an increasing need for livelihood and nutrition services due to the critical levels of acute. FSNAU forecasts improved food security as a result of above average Gu harvest coupled with sustained humanitarian assistance. Further, the above average Gu rainfalls have led to regeneration of pasture and water availability in most parts. This has consequently improved livestock body conditions; with medium goat and sheep births in most areas as well as few camel births in central and northern areas. Despite milk availability increasing, it remains below average due to low livestock herds. Nevertheless, FEWSNET/FSNAU projections of June – September 2018 indicate persisting acute food security crisis (IPC phases 3) in some regions of Somalia in absence of humanitarian assistance . Acess to clean water has been a key challenge that links to health concernce with AWD and other waterborne disease being one of the contributing factor. Lower Juba has for a long time been hit by conflict and poor access hence hindering any humanitarian aid in the region. Outbreaks are localy managed hence resulting in high morbidity and mortality rates particularly for children under the ages of 5 and PLWs, plus women of child bearing age. Poor access to water and WASH interventions especially during floods or drought has been a key concern rendering the communities prone to infections. The current Rainy season has caused alot of dispalcement

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

There is need for a better understanding of the risk/threat of exclusion and identification of those in need of assistance and protection, either due to poorly defined vulnerability and targeting criteria, manipulation of humanitarian processes, lack of understanding of distribution biases inherent in the humanitarian architecture, and/or deliberate denial of assistance including, in forms of economic or physical blockages.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

This project is designed to meet the distinct needs of women, girls, boys and men to generate positive and sustainable outcomes. All staff will be recruited competitively by ensuring that all processes are gender sensitive and ethical consideration. All male and female community groups will also have equal access to the integrated emergency life-saving services and will be reflected in reports to show that both genders are well represented. SORDES will also conduct gender segregated community consultation meetings to enable women and girls are well represented and take their role and to address their specific concerns, prioritize their needs and appropriately inform the development of the proposed activities. Planned training activities for staff and community volunteers will also benefit both male and female staff and will contribute to maximize gender equality through equal access to training opportunities and participation of service delivery.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The IDPs in these displacement locations frequently live in undignified and hazardous circumstances, where they face multiple protection risks/threats such as, unlawful evictions, overcrowded and unsanitary environments with limited access to basic services, exposure to explosive hazards, increased risk of Gender Based Violence (GBV), negative coping mechanisms such as child marriage and child labor, and tension with the host community. Children and young people (mainly male) in displaced situation, particularly those from Al-Shabaab controlled areas face risks of arbitrary arrest once out of those controlled areas, and therefore live in fear and sometimes isolation. Lastly, IDPs continue to struggle to end their displacement and pursue voluntary, informed, safe, and dignified durable solutions based on their individual and household needs. By utilizing the reach, capacity, and weight of the whole humanitarian system, the safety, dignity, and well-being of these displacement affected populations (including the heavily burdened host communities) can be strengthened.
