Project Summary

IMPACT RELIEF & DEVELOPMENT (IRD-SS) South Sudan is a national non-profit, humanitarian and development organization, established in 2017 - operating in South Sudan. IRD-SS’s mission is to advance community prosperity and resilience and support grass-root communities and vulnerable groups in the society to get equal access to basic needs. Its implement multi-sectorial programs that include WASH. And, is staging quick impact and sustainable response to address the humanitarian needs of internal displaced persons, returnees and host communities to realize stability and access to basic needs and assistance.
Under the HRP 2020, IRD-SS intend to support and provide integrated water, sanitation & hygiene services for severely affected returnees, displaced persons, and host communities in Raga county – Western Bhar el Ghazal states. The project targets a population of 14, 400 vulnerable affected with WASH risk-related severity factors, indicators and challenges as shown by the WASH cluster severity analysis matrix. With the realization of longer-term solutions needs to elevate suffering of the target vulnerable groups in the location selected, this project aims to contribute to the WASH cluster strategic objectives 3 ‘’ Sustainable WASH services will address the vital needs, public health and protection issues through an AAP community approach to target the acute vulnerabilities based on inter/cross-sectoral evaluations and analyses 3 & 4’’. This aims to support at-risk communities with basic water, sanitation and hygiene services to enhance recovery and resilience from shocks. To effectively achieved the program objectives, IRD-SS is committed to, engage, participate and work closely with most vulnerable targeted groups to ensure increased access to improved safe water sources by provision and installation of new water sources and rehabilitation of the presence water sources. This project aimed to provide the target beneficiaries with integrated and sustained water, sanitation and hygiene services and gender-based violence supports and ensure access to improved water sources, access to WASH NFIs, and GBV support are increased.

1 - Needs

South Sudan continues to be affected by crisis and the threat of conflicts uncertainties. This situations, coupled with long standing environment of poverty, lack of infrastructure and food insecurity which caused mass displacement which occurred in an atmosphere where WASH coverage is also still very low at 50% coverage with basic water supply, 10% coverage with basic sanitation and with 61% of the population still practicing open defecation according to the UNICEF-WHO 2017 Joint Monitoring Programme. The situation is further compounded by the lack of sustainable operational and maintenance systems for existing WASH facilities and infrastructure as a result of governance challenges.In nutshell, due to the first outbreak of the South Sudan conflict in 2013, the majority of the sustainable WASH development projects designed for implementation in rural and urban settings were suspended and the WASH investments shifted focus to delivering critical water, sanitation and hygiene services for the life-saving interventions. This protracted conflict has continued to affect the already poor rate of access to basic facilities, including safe water sources that increased children's vulnerability to water borne diseases - WASH-risk factors. According to August 2019 IPC, WASH severity is a significant problem because of the chronic nature of waterborne diseases attributed to lack of safe water sources, low use of latrines, poor personal hygiene and living environments, and limited access to soap for hand washing. The low access to health services is identified and reported significantly poor and this leads to high incidences of WASH risk-related causes and factors that not only affect the health of the population, but also negatively affects the coping mechanism of the crisis affected people. The WASH needs in Raga county is critical and high that require significant intervention and response to reduce WASH risk-related factors and problem associated with lack of safe water sources accessibility. As indicated in the severity matrix of WASH prevalence, Raja County 1% households (HHs) of the population catchment have access to improved water sources with average coverage of WASH percentage stand at 22%. The categorization indicate Raja county severely affected by lack of improved safe water sources. In the context of reduce conflict violent, anticipation of the peace stability, return of refugees and displaced population, this project constitutes focus on delivering critical lifesaving water, sanitation and hygiene services in Raga counties to address the severity of the water, sanitation and hygiene challenges and gaps in interior of the most inaccessible locations. In accordance with HRP 2020, IRD-SS seek to solicit resources to address needs of this WASH severity in Raja County through provision of accessible, improved and sustainable WASH services which are essential for all humanitarian, recovery and development interventions remain critical and highest severity in most part of South Sudan mapped. The challenges and gaps that exist are lack of rehabilitation & upgrading, construction of boreholes/hand-pumps to improve access to water sources and critical water supply infrastructure, management support for operation and maintenance to attain improved hygiene and sanitation services which aims to increased significant impact on improving the health and well-being of children and women, vulnerable groups including malnourished children. The objective of this project is to provide access to clean drinking water to crisis affected communities (returnees, host, etc.) in Raga counties in safe & effective manner particularly for women & children. The project also aims to build capacity of counties teams, raise awareness of water usage & hygiene to enhance sustainability of the facilities & to promote better health outcomes that aimed to contribute towards SDG 3 “Good Health & Well Being” & SDG 6 “Ensuring Clean Water & Sanitation for all’’.

2 - Activities

• Conduct timely, and coordinated WASH needs assessments in the WASH severely and conflict affected areas in Raja corridors in a holistic, transparent and participatory manner with reference to South Sudan minimum standards for WASH cluster.• Establishment of hand-washing facilities constructed in identified public institutions such as nutrition & health centres, schools, etc. and solid waste management campaigns; provision of tools. • Provision target populations with improved access to safe water sources through borehole rehabilitation and repairs, borehole drilling, and water trucking.• Water testing at water points to assure that FRC to assure that FRC level is sufficient Water testing at household level (particularly if HHWT products such as aqua tabs are given) to assure that FRC level is sufficient.• Train hygiene promoters on key messages, including in support of WASH NFI provision, sanitation infrastructure provision.• 20 training sessions on household water management, sanitation management, water treatment and storage, handwashing and appropriate waste disposal at the community levels.• 60 Hygiene promoters trained in line with WASH Cluster hygiene promotion standards and recommendations on hygiene promotion messages (i.e. importance of handwashing with ash or soap at critical times, proper disposal of human excreta).• Conduct 20 sessions of awareness campaigns: house to house hygiene promotion, community hygiene promotion activities, IEC activities, and focused group discussions.• Provision of WASH NFIs, post distribution monitoring and reporting.• Strengthening capacity for information dissemination about GBV & nutrition services and prevention measures; Raising awareness about the negative health impacts of GBV, e.g., pregnancy-related complications, low birth weight and alcohol/drug use; and conducting outreach activities that provide information regarding referral services - finalize referral pathway mechanism agreed by the service providers (clusters).• Create peer support groups (e.g. women's groups, youth groups, men's groups) for prevention and support. The main objectives of these activities is to improved access to water sources and integrated sanitation and hygiene services through increased water supply and sanitation interventions aimed to reduce morbidity and mortality associated with diseases and hazards resulting from deficient health conditions as a consequence of conflict crisis or structural weakness of the water supply services in Raja corridors.

Under IRD programme policy working document, the beneficiaries’ rights are recognized as the legal obligation of the organization to respect and enforced them in project interventions stages. IRD adhere to the humanitarian principles that guide its projects and support to the crisis affected people and communities. 6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

This proposed project aimed to support most vulnerable displaced persons, and returnees as the priority of the planned activities specially in hard to reach locations of Raja counties to be provided with WASH integrated services. However, the project team will ensure the needs of displaced persons and returnees will first be considered in all phases of the project. The decisions and concerns of this group will be voice out to promote integration of the returnees and displaced people into the communities. The project will target and access hard-to-reach populated internally displaced and returnee’s locations with most significant challenges and gaps identified to be supported in this intervention. Under the resilience and recovery interventions, IRD advocate for the immediate support of the displaced and returnees concerns to ensure basic services are provided. To recover from the shocks impacted by the crisis, the displacement-affected people lack basic needs such as water and health support which are very critical for the recovery and resilience of the displaced persons and returnees. This project work to improved access to water sources and hygiene &sanitation services for displaced persons in Raja corridors.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project is directly link to the government and community systems where both involves in the design and planning of the project implementation and evaluations. the community will be engaged to make decision on where the new water sources will be installed and constructed with guide of the local authorities. The organization will make sure the participatory approach will be adopted as a mechanism to strengthen government and community systems links and connections. This will enhance more effective approaches on mechanism to tackle and address immediate needs of the target vulnerable crisis affected communities. This will promote understanding and information sharing that enhance ownership and sustainability of the services provided. The engagement of the community systems focuses on the needs of different groups of the community such as women & girls, youth, men, elderly, and the chiefs to decide to make and shape decisions that directly affects the project positively through feedback mechanism and community engagement processes on the implementation of the project. Additionally, women groups are given the high chance of contributing during the process of monitoring and evaluation of the project activities because they have more knowledge and information which is clear regarding the challenges that are facing WASH services and even knows what is required in order to improve access to water sources. Women and girl’s groups are mostly the target beneficiaries to this project because they are the persons who collect drinking water and usage of the water in the domestic services unlike other groups of the beneficiaries. Therefore, they are critical to the implementation of the projects and decision making processes. The local authority’s mechanism of supervision and involvement is equally link to the community system of ownership of the services delivered by this project will be strengthened.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

This project aligned to overall objectives of the HRP 2020 that enshrined principles of Saving Lives: Save lives by providing timely and integrated multi assistance to reduce acute humanitarian needs among the most vulnerable people, Centrality of Protection: Reinforce protection and promote access to basic services for the displaced and other vulnerable people, and Resilience: Support at risk communities to sustain their capacity to cope with significant threats. This project aligned its activities with protection framework, Internally Displaced person’s conventions, and National framework on return, resettlement and reintegration that will incorporate protection WASH service delivery and ensure that the project activities target the most vulnerable, enhance safety, dignity, and promote and protect human rights of the beneficiaries without contributing to or perpetuating discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect and exploitation. It will ensure to prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of the intervention, which can increase people's vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. The proposed project will ensure ‘’do no harm’’ principle to the beneficiaries during the project implementation.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

IRD-SS will be guided by it internal policies of protection environment safety policy in organization field site for the implementation of the project and adopted the Core Humanitarian Standard and incorporated provisions that protect staff, beneficiaries, members from the beneficiaries’ community, and humanitarian workers from any of protection risks. The project team will conduct protection needs assessment in several locations targeted by the project, both those intended to be served by static and mobile activities. Primary and secondary data collected during the assessments informed a protection risk analysis that particularly considered the needs of women, recently displaced persons, older persons, persons with disabilities.All project staffs, contractors, and persons engaged directly in the project implementation shall sign up to the code of conduct that restricts behaviors to avoid harm. IRD-SS has reporting protocol that is communicated to stakeholders at all interfaces to encourage communication of related instances of risk from the community’s site. At field level, project team is a recipient for cases where instances do not directly target staffs but entities the project engages to contribute to project objectives. Cases involving the provisions where Payam oversight committees communicate directly with national office through senior management staff that travel to field on quarterly basis to interact with the committees In the proposed project locations, possible tensions of access to limited resources between IDPs and host communities have been factored in the project engagements with key stakeholders whom IRD-SS is already currently engaging through other projects in the county. Past experience shows that when prior engaged, local authorities are willing to protect returnees/IDP populations to access resources.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

IRD will ensure proper delivery of basic skills and information to the beneficiaries through trainings and awareness creations to add on needs identified addressed. This project will promote local authority involvement and community participation in the process of implementation and evaluation where they will fully get engaged to provide necessary information needed and suggestions to the project. The trained WASH committees and local authority under the physical infrastructure department staffs will continue to provide support to the communities when the project ended. As the local agency, IRD will look for more funds through other sources to support water, sanitation, and hygiene programmes in the project locations as continuity and sustainability mechanism. The hardware part of the project will be handed to the governments and the community to provide support to the rehabilitation and sustainability to the project. The stakeholders and development actors will also be engage to support the crisis affected communities with more integrated services which will contribute to the sustainability of the services provided in this project.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

To mitigate the risks selected above, IRD will ensure women and girls are involving in the consultations, discussion and decision of the provision of water points in the communities selected. This will promote the rights of girls and women who are GBV risks vulnerable in the process of collection of drinking water for the domestic usage. The marginalized/minority groups shall include in the process of consultation to bring out their views to the project implementation to avoid exclusion of the services provided. The project team will work closely with service providers (partners) in the project areas to share information and create awareness of the available services through referral pathways mechanisms. The project team will put in place strategic plan to ensure services provided should eliminate factors that expose women and girls’ sexual violence such as distance, security risks, etc. The decision will be made to access constraints link to conflict security issues that determine the location of the allotment of the water sources in the communities and vulnerable groups. The project team will take in account the contribution of external stakeholders such as the local government to direct the services to the areas of their interest. The organization will ensure the decision to install water sources such as boreholes will base on the needs identified in assessed areas of the project.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

To ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members, project team will put in place tools that collect information as feedback from the most vulnerable groups of the community such as elderly, disable, children, etc. through direct discussion, accessibility to service points, observations, and key informants to the monitoring and evaluation of the project. This will be incorporated into monitoring and evaluation process of key indicators and objectives of the project to promote integral accessibility of the services provided to the most vulnerable community members. Within the internal monitoring and reporting mechanism, the basic tools developed for the project reporting will display aspects to check on consistence and balance between planned and executed activities that capture the needs of the most vulnerable groups in the communities targeted. The project officer will be expected to draw a field monitoring matrix including quality assurance for checking on progress as per the set strategy verses expected outcome. The project team will engage in monitoring, evaluation and accountability for learning (M.E.A.L) personnel to inform the management and operation team on status of the project from time to time, this help the management in decision making to address the challenges and matters arising from the course of implementing this project. IRD-SS project team will conduct need assessment in project targeted areas to identify the most critical needs of the crisis affected communities in Raja corridors mainly for WASH integrated services that aimed to improved access to water sources. The project team will engage with targeted beneficiaries to learn how they are assessing their needs. IRD will engage beneficiaries to learn how they are assessing their needs and develop and adopt a philosophy of engaging the community from the initiation of the project in order for the community to own it. Stakeholder workshops will be organized in the location identified for implementation of the response and the community will be consulted and provided with information regarding the project implementation. This will include engaging existing CWD, CHD, Community elders, Women, Youth groups, religious leaders and representatives of beneficiaries.This will be the medium through which communities will be encouraged to express their concerns, views and provide regular feedback to the implementing partner in a regular structured modality. Other reasonable modalities for feedback that is useful to the communities/beneficiaries will also be considered. These feedbacks will form part of the project performance reporting to the WASH cluster and will help guide the fine tuning of the project to enhance positive beneficiary experience. IRD-SS has an established complaint response mechanism (CRM) with two components a) beneficiary complaint/feedback collection mechanism and b) complaint handling and response mechanism. IRD-SS has also its own accountability systems, founded on Humanitarian Accountability Partnership Standards on accountability and quality management, and Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) recommended principles and values. For this project, IRD-SS will conduct community sensitization and information sharing sessions on project activities with girls, boys, women and men, government representative, etc.
