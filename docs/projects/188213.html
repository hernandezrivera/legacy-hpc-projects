The roles and activities of community health workers (CHWs) are tailored to meet the unique needs of the communities they serve. A CHW's role depends on factors such their education, training, lived experience, and experience working with specific populations. CHWs may perform the following roles:
• Create connections between vulnerable populations and healthcare providers
• Help patients navigate healthcare and social service systems
• Manage care and care transitions for vulnerable populations
• Educate healthcare providers and stakeholders about community health needs
• Provide culturally appropriate health education on topics related to chronic disease prevention, physical activity, and nutrition
• Advocate for underserved individuals or communities to receive services and resources to address health needs
• Collect data and relay information to stakeholders to inform programs and policies
To ensure system strengthening the following programmatic areas will be in the focus of the implementation of this project:
- Ensuring Access to Essential Primary Health Care Services

- Referral services
- Capacity building
Strengthening the capacity to efficiently implement the COVID-19 Preparedness and Response Plan 2022 in NWS
Risk communication is key to improving familiarity with and adherence to preventive measures, in particularly during health emergencies. Failure to communicate the right message effectively can result in loss of trust, damage to the economy and loss of lives. For risk communication to be effective, risk messages have to be shared with the public in an openly and timely manner, so as to reduce the knowledge gap and to convince the public to adjust their behavior during a crisis. In addition to disseminating recommendations that are easy for the public to understand and comply with, trust in the source of the message is important for an effective risk communication.
This project aims to Increase the community awareness of the COVID 19 symptoms, means of transmission, and prevention measures including hygiene promotion (HP) messaging in northwest Syria.
This project will continue the same scope of services for the first project by increasing two aspects:
- Using the virtual information and engagement platforms in the local communities involving trusted community influencers and using different social media platforms to ensure widespread among communities with providing opportunities for answering questions and providing up-to-date recommendation about COVID-19 response including text, voice, and video.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

AFAQ coordinated with Nutrition cluster and humanitarian actors to determine the gaps and selected the in-need communities for intervening. AFAQ will assess the potential risks of women, girls, boys and men and people with disabilities to ensurethat nutrition services are conducted with no harm. AFAQ is integrating protection mechanisms to understand the needs of community for targeting them through: Delivering services impartially to ensure reaching to all in-need BNFs without discrimination, with prioritizing the safety and dignity of BNFs and avoid harm. Identification of suitable BFSs, times and dates, considering needs of female and children. And secure BFSs like safe locations and movements. Hiring the majority of staff from females. Collecting and monitoring of data disaggregated by sex, age, and disability. Monitoring and overseeing potential barriers to accessing the health services. Conducting exit interviews to adapt the counseling and preventative health services to respond the need.AFAQ will develop a service map that identifies all Protection service providers within the operational areas.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

AFAQ has different channels for information dissemination including, but not limited to, WhatsApp groups, IEC materials, fixed posters and banners - PSEA messaging and awareness-raising will be done with beneficiaries through one or more of the following (track and report):SMS/WhatsApp messaging• All project staff trained on GBV prevention and response in emergencies and PSEA, in order to ensure full adherence with the Do No Harm principle with strict dealing with Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) and GBV issues. AFAQ is working to improve the speed, accuracy and comprehensiveness in reporting serious allegations of sexual exploitation and abuse; the pace and quality of investigations; and follow-up and accountability. Urgent action upon receipt of a report of sexual exploitation and abuse creates the best conditions for investigations. If an allegation of misconduct by AFAQ personnel have been reported and the allegation are substantiated, the perpetrators will be held accountable and face sanctions that correspond to the seriousness of the offense. This can range from a reprimand to dismissal. If the acts may amount to criminal conduct, AFAQ can refer the matter for criminal prosecution by the staff member’s state of nationality.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

•Assumption: increase confirmed cases of COVID19•Risk: COVID19 pandemic spread•Likelihood: high •Impact: high•Mitigation: 1- Applying IPC protocols inside the facility and referring any suspected cases according to the case definition 2- Disseminating a message to the community through outreach workers to avoid visiting the health facility unless there is an urgent need3- All other needed measures will be applied according to the cluster recommendation •Assumption: All needed medical supplies are available on a timely basis •Risk: shortage of medical supplies •Likelihood: medium•Impact: High•Mitigation: 1- Contingency stock will be in place for shipments to avoid any impact of shortage of medical supplies Assumption: People have access to health facility, despite restrictions of movementRisk: Access to areas of intervention are restricted due to the high level of conflict, change of controlling entity Likelihood: LowImpact: HighMitigation: 1- In the event of major macro-level conflict-related threats, AFAQ will take appropriate action that is commensurate with the scale of the threat. This may include:A: Following AFAQ safety and security guidelines and established safety protocols. B: Shifting resources and operations to more secure locations
