Project Summary

Needs :
• According to Syrian HNO 2024 “After 12 years of hostilities and persistent economic crisis, people in Syria saw their situation worsen when a series of earthquakes hit northern Syria and Türkiye on 6 and 20 February 2023. The earthquakes uprooted hundreds of thousands of families, resulted in almost 5,900 deaths and more than 12,800 people injured in Syria, and severely damaged infrastructures already weakened by the conflict.”
• At the end of 2023, during the implementation of Saed's project to provide heating fuel within several camps in the Jindires sub-district, observations made by the project team, along with feedback obtained through feedback mechanisms and MAEL activities, revealed severe suffering among residents of 12 newly established camps in response to the February 6 earthquake. This suffering was primarily due to poor infrastructure in these camps and the urgent need for improvements in shelter and WASH sectors. Based on this, the Saed team conducted an initial needs assessment for these camps through discussion sessions with diverse age and gender groups representing all camp residents. Here is a summary of the findings:
- All roads in 10 camps are unpaved, with only two camps having one main gravel road, making it difficult for residents, especially the elderly, disabled, and children, to access services, particularly during the winter season.
- There are no water drainage system , and all camps suffer from flooding and water accumulation during the winter season. Some low-lying dwellings are prone to flooding, resulting in negative health and psychological consequences for residents, particularly vulnerable groups, due to prolonged exposure to stagnant water.
- Approximately 64 % of the residents of these camps live in partially constructed dwellings that require rehabilitation. Some of these dwellings need complete rehabilitation and finishing to become dignified shelters.
- The population of these camps is 16032 IDPs(3367 men,3527 women, 4649 boys, 4489 girls), distributed among 3,083 households, including 425 households headed by women and 99 households with disabled members. Additionally, there are 227 individuals with disabilities, 408 lactating and pregnant women, and 826 elderly individuals.
Intervention:
This proposal aims to ensure safe and dignified living conditions for the most vulnerable individuals in 12 camps in the Jindires sub-district, Afrin region, totaling 16032 IDPs(3367 men,3527 women, 4649 boys, 4489 girls).
The objectives of this proposal will be achieved within a timeframe spanning between 2024 and 2025 as follows:
1. Improvements to infrastructure in 12 camps will be made by establishing closed and open drainage systems for rainwater, rehabilitating roads and pathways within the camps. Saed conducted topographical, hydrological, and geotechnical studies for these camps and designed comprehensive interventions to drain all rainwater in the area passing through the targeted camps. This activity will be implemented through contracts with specialized contractors.
Activities will be completed within a maximum timeframe of 6 months before November 2024. The total beneficiaries of this component are 3,083 HHs,16032 IDPs(3367 men,3527 women, 4649 boys, 4489 girls)
the # of beneficiary HHs :3038
The total cost of these activities is $570355 at a rate of $185 per HH.( actual cost )
2. Installing and building 220 decent shelters/temporary transitional shelters for 220 of the most vulnerable families who suffer severely from poor housing in the targeted camps.
this activity will be implemented through contracts with specialized contractors.
# Of housing : 220 , # of beneciaries = 1232 IDPs
The cost of \each housing unit is 2900 USD, with a total cost of $63800USD for this activity.
All activities within the proposed project will be according to the Shelter cluster objectives and will be designed according to the SPHERE standards.
