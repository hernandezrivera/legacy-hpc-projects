Project Summary

Sudan is transition following deteriorating economic and political environment which saw the ousting of former regime and the establishment of a transitional government. In particular, the depreciation in Sudanese Pound and the hiking of prices of food and essential items, etc. have inevitably affected the lives and living standards of thousands of people including refugees who are currently struggling to make ends meet in their respective host communities across Sudan including South Kordofan State (SKS), West Kordofan State (SKS), White Nile State (WNS) and Kassala State.
In September 2019, UNHCR population Dash Board estimates 859,286 South Sudanese refugees currently hosted in Sudan, out of which 364,269 are in SKS, WKS and WNS, while Kassala State hosts 124,125 Eritrean refugees. Hosing refugees in communities has occurred along with pressing needs thereby creating enormous pressure and competition in accessing available basic services and economic opportunities with triggers for conflicts if unabated. To make ends meet, refugees and vulnerable community members have resorted to ravaging forest resources such as the collection and sale of firewood, wild fruits, use of burnt bricks for shelter construction, etc. The situation has triggered the need for humanitarian assistance especially with fading absorption capacity of host communities. Unless something was done to abate the situation, it will reach a point where refugees and communities would no longer cope with the situation. Proposed intervention will focus on reducing the vulnerability of refugees (South Sudanese and Eritrean refugees) alongside other at risk groups affected by shocks, while seeking to support a gradual transition from humanitarian aid to self-reliance. Specific objectives are:
• To improve refugees’ access to basic needs (food, clothes, medicine, shelter, etc.) through lifesaving emergency agriculture, temporal jobs and other sustainable livelihood activities.
• To promote community resilience and enhance refugees’ access to lifesaving services through the provision of basic services and socioeconomic assets depleted as a result of overuse.
• To promote coexistence and social cohesion between refugees and different groups.
• To support reforestation and renewable energy use in household meal preparation for refugees and host communities.
Intervention will support lifesaving through emergency agriculture and temporary job creation for refugees and host communities. The project will restore social services and socioeconomic assets depleted as a result of overuse due the presence of refugees in communities. The project will deliver social cohesion activities that will reduce friction and restore trust and confidence among refugees and other groups.
Lessons from previous interventions informed that targeting individual refugees without the communities has limited impact and has potential to create conflict between refugees and host communities. Proposed intervention will be inclusive and community-based, ensuring direct support to refuges while enhancing the capacities of communities to sustain recovery and stability. 75% of assistant will go to refugees and 25% to host communities. This is a multi-year intervention that seeks for $22,189,000 to support 35,000 individuals (5,834 households) in four states.
Intervention will strengthen ongoing support by UNHCR and UNDP in targeted locations to maximize impact while paving way for humanitarian-development-peace nexus. Intervention will be area-based and multi-sectoral in approach.
Environmental safeguards will be incorporated in all aspects of the project activity to reduce vulnerability to climate change-induced risks. Intervention will promote improved natural resources management, efficient energy utilization and environmental protection through training on NRM and community environmental action plan (CEAP) development and implementation. The use of improved solar stove will be promoted.

1 - Needs

SKS, WKS, WNS and Kassala State are among those States hosting larger number of refugees. The large presence of these displaced populations in the affected states has outmatched the available basic services and economic opportunities thereby triggering a clear need for humanitarian assistance. The situation has also led to growing competition and tension between displaced populations and host communities over the available basic services, land resources, livelihoods and economic opportunities, thereby creating increasing pressure in host communities with the propensity to precipitate social instability except something was done to remedy the situation. In a joint analysis by UNDP and UNHCR in 2018/2019, it was estimated that over 50,000 people are in need of short terms assistance including food, medicine, emergency jobs, seeds and tools, etc. The needs assessment also shows the clear lack of economic opportunities, high youth unemployment, diminished absorption capacity of host communities and high dependence on humanitarian aid. Despite no alarm over poor harvests in the area in the past one year, food insecurity remains an issues among displaced population due to rise in food price. This situation is compounded by the ongoing economic crisis including high rate of inflation, increase in prices of food and essential items, – all of which have negatively affected refugees thus presenting a huge risk and severe consequences to the livelihoods of the already-vulnerable populations who are net buyers of food and essential items. The situation requires urgent attention. The risk of no action will exacerbate the humanitarian situation which will force refugees and vulnerable households to resort to negative coping strategies. The proposed intervention will critically analyze the vulnerability of refugees and other vulnerable groups and design interventions tailored to the specific needs of each group with focus on lifesaving. Protection, gender, women empowerment and environmental safeguards in all states of project activity.

2 - Activities / Outputs

Project activity will include support to lifesaving through emergency agriculture and temporary jobs creation – distribution of seeds, tools and fertilizers; agricultural trainings; extension services. Farming activities (such as vegetables production, backyard gardening, small ruminant production) with nutritional outcomes will also be considered. Any surplus food will be sold by refugees to meet other needs like medicines, clothes, shelter, school fees of children. Project will support vocational skills in various trades including the provision of start-up kits and start-up capital; support to small businesses including training in business entrepreneurial skills, provision of start-up capital to enable beneficiaries establish and operate own businesses. Reforestation and renewable energy use such as solar stove in preparing household meals will be supported. The intervention will build the capacity of local communities for improved preparedness in handling new emergencies and to cope with future shocks and stresses through tailored trainings. Activity will restore/deliver social services and socioeconomic assets depleted/destroyed as a result of overuse due to the presence of large number of refugees. Project will expand existing water points into a more reliable water distribution yards to cater for additional consumption attributed to the presence of refugees. Rehabilitation of community schools and health centres will also be given due consideration. Improved co-existence between refugees and other groups in the communities – Activities will include the training of refuges alongside community members on how to identify and address issues affecting refugee welfare. It will also include organizing sporting and other joint social activities between refugees and host communities aimed at increasing community acceptance of refugees. Furthermore, project will contribute to gender empowerment including livelihood support to women alongside men, sensitization of gender equity, SGBV and women reproductive health will also be carried out. Gender and age dis-aggregated date will be analyzed to inform programme decision.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

During needs assessment carried out by UNDP in the proposed areas in 2019, it was discovered that ravaging forest and other natural resources was among the coping mechanisms of displaced populations and vulnerable members of host communities. Actions such as firewood collection, use of burnt bricks for housing, collection of wild fruits, uncontrolled burning of farm sites, improper disposal of refuse, to name but a few ware among the environmental hazards practiced in the targeted communities. Hence the proposed intervention will incorporate environmental safeguards in all aspects of the proposed interventions to reduce vulnerability to climate change-induced risks. Interventions will seek to promote improved natural resources management, efficient energy utilization and environmental protection through training on NRM and community environmental action plan (CEAP) development and implementation. Proper refuse disposal will be promoted among beneficiaries. The use of improved fuel efficient stove will be promoted especially among women who are frequent users of natural and forest resources. Soil and water conservation techniques such as minimum tillage, controlled burning of farm sites, mulching, the use of organic manure for backyard gardening, etc. will be encouraged.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Capacity building trainings of CMCs, beneficiaries and key community members will include topics with focus on human rights, refugee rights, gender equity, SGBV, rights of people living with disabilities among others. UNDP has adopted the strategic plan (2018-2021) which aligns with the 2030 agenda and SDGs. The principle of equity and or “leaving no one behind”, targeting the most vulnerable and most in need with all its interventions. Targeting criteria will be used throughout to ensure the most vulnerable are targeted. Protection specific activities have been incorporated in planned activities. The project will ensure vulnerable people have access to project assistance including sustainable livelihoods in addition to ensuring the use of a well-defined selection/identification criteria of beneficiaries for project assistance. The project will also ensure to share information on protection issues that the project will come across with humanitarian and protection partners working in the state. The project will also ensure stronger coordination with humanitarian actors, especially UNHCR to avoid missed opportunities for integrating protection information in refugee profiling for project assistance. The project will seek for cooperation with NGOs, civil society and community-based organizations on any protection issues that will be countered during project implementation.
