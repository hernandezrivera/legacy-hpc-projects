Project Summary

The project aims at providing life-saving WASH services to most vulnerable (minorities, women and children, elders, people living with a disability) 9000 persons (4320 women, 2880 men, 720 boys and 1080 girls) in Banadir District, Somalia by constructing latrines in IDPs and host communities including disabled friendly latrines, rehabilitation of community water points such as berkads and shallow wells, well disinfection and chlorination, provision of water containers and transport, capacity building for water management committees hygiene awareness campaign and distribution of hygiene kits in IDP camp and host communities.
About 42 per cent of the Somali population (6.61 million people) require support to access emergency life - saving WASH services in 2022 of which 4.2 million will need safe drinking water, 3.2 million sanitation services, and 5.2 million hygiene supplies. Severe drought condition, seasonal flood, diseases outbreak and conflict related displacement are contributing to the increase in the number of people in need.
Banadir district is in severity 3 and has an estimated 692,643 people in dire need of wash services of which 543, 917 are non IDPS and 148,726 are IDPS. A combination of poor access to safe drinking water, lack of adequate sanitation facilities especially in both host and IDP settlements and poor hygienic practices combine to threaten the survival and development of Somali population. Limited access to WASH facilities and services poses a huge burden on minorities, women and children, elders and people living with a disability. Women and girls are exposed to protection risks including gender-based violence (GBV) when collecting water from too distant water sources or using unimproved overcrowded sanitation facilities which lack safety and protection features. The WASH safety index shows that protection issues when accessing or using WASH services are a concern for 2 per cent of boys or girls at household level. It also highlights that only 38 per cent of households reported access to latrines with walls and locks on doors, while only 17per cent reported having access to latrines with internal sources of light. Lack of locks and less solid structures do not allow privacy and increase risks of GBV, especially for women and girls.
Among other people in need are 965,754 (15 per cent) of persons with disabilities and 254,465 (4 per cent) of elderly persons who face the risk of exclusion from assistance due to the barriers they face when accessing WASH services. According to WASH Cluster disability inclusion assessment 40 per cent of females and 75 per cent of males with disability never collected water for their household due to distance and in-accessibility of water points and rely on neighbors and children to collect water for them. The lack of consultation and engagement with persons with disabilities in WASH designs, limits the opportunity for an inclusive response and contributes further to the marginalization of this vulnerable group. Therefore, in regards to persons with disabilities the wash services will be designed in a manner which will make it easier for them to use. Meetings with people living with disabilities will be conducted throughout the project phases whereby they will be consulted and engaged in regards to the designs and location of the wash services.
As a result, the project will aim at improving sanitation conditions by construction of 30 latrines , rehabilitation of 20 community water points such as berkads and shallow wells, well disinfection and chlorination, provision of water containers and transport, hygiene awareness campaign for 5000 IDPs and host communities, capacity building for water management committees and distribution of hygiene kits to the most vulnerable conflict and drought displaced as well as vulnerable host communities/men, women, boys and girls including the elderly and people living with disabilities.

1. - Projects activities

The project will implement the following activities :- -Extension/rehabilitation of existing water supply systems -Activities related to WASH/Safety and Protection: latrines with solid walls, locks on doors, light if justified, gender separated if justified, reasonable walking distance to water points and toilets, involvement of population in design/location of WASH facilities -Hygiene promotion campaign -Distribution of hygiene kits Constructing 20 latrines in IDPs and host communities. To preserve the safety and dignity of users, the latrines will be build separate for males and females and marked clearly in pictorial form. For privacy, secondary enclosures will be provided around facilities and the doors will also be lockable. Establishment of 10 community water points in the IDP camps. Considerations will be in place for children, pregnant women, and persons with disabilities. A Raised platform, ramps and handrails on both sides of the latrines will be constructed to ensure easy access of the services for children, pregnant women and people living with disabilities. Hygiene promotion campaigns will be conducted by hygiene promoters. SSWC will ensure the promotion services are respectful and inclusive of religious and cultural practice and there is appropriate number of female hygiene promoters. The hygiene promoters will work in the selected IDP camps and host community, they will conduct door to door visits in the camp to disseminate information to the residents of the camp. SSWC will use 20 hygiene promoters in sensitization campaigns who will target 5000 individuals drawn from the targeted camps. SSWC will train hygiene promoters on how to pass key hygiene messages and how to use the tools for gathering information (Hygiene Promotion Tracking tool/Check list). The 20 hygiene promoters will be trained for two days. In the campaign SSWC will also use IEC materials to disseminate hygiene messages and provide timely information to the public. The project will provide hygiene kits for use by the vulnerable persons in IDP camps. the items in the kits will improve the hygiene situation of both at household levels and the camps at large. The hygiene kits contain sanitary pads, water containers, cleaning detergents, mats, chlorine and others.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

The risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services may occur. SSWC will ensure that the water sources are located in visible, central locations and not more than 500 meters from settlement. Lights will be installed near the communal facilities. Accompaniment arrangements to reduce threats to the safety and dignity of women and girls e.g. fetching water in groups will be introduced to the beneficiaries during the project implementation period. SSWC will also identify central locations for distribution points to avoid beneficiaries from traversing long distances or pass through conflict zones to access distribution points. Distributions will take place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall. During delivering assistance, exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance is possible and SSWC will prioritize safety & dignity and avoid causing harm. Save Somali Women and Children will ensure the location of and access routes to distribution points and services are safe with enough space for orderly distribution. The distribution centers will also be centrally located for easy access.The risk of minority groups not being included in consultations or humanitarian services may occur. Save Somali Women and Children will consult marginalized persons to collect accurate information about their specific WASH requirements and preferences for location and design of WASH facilities. SSWC will also ensure that a certain percentage of the selected vulnerable population is from minority groups.
