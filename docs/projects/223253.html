Project Summary

This project aims to address the high prevalence of malnutrition among under-five children in Michika and Madagali Local Government Areas (LGAs) in Adamawa State and Maiduguri Metropolitan Council (MMC) in Borno State. Using the Nutrition Sector needs analysis outlined in the 2024 Humanitarian Response Plan, the project focuses on the treatment of Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) and Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). It adopts an integrated approach, combining therapeutic and supplementary feeding with community-based management strategies. The intervention will directly benefit 20,000 children under five and indirectly impact 40,000 caregivers and community members by improving access to life-saving nutrition services.

1 - Needs

According to the 2024 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) data:Adamawa State: Michika and Madagali LGAs are among the most affected by malnutrition, with GAM (Global Acute Malnutrition) rates significant and SAM rates at 3.5%. Factors include conflict-induced displacement, food insecurity, and limited access to healthcare services.Borno State: Maiduguri Metropolitan Council has a GAM rate of 15.8%, with SAM rates at 4.2%. The area is heavily impacted by ongoing conflict, disruption of livelihoods, and poor access to clean water and sanitation.Contributing Factors:Food insecurity: Prolonged displacement has disrupted livelihoods and access to nutritious food.Healthcare access: Limited healthcare infrastructure and trained personnel.Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH): Poor WASH conditions exacerbate malnutrition by increasing susceptibility to diarrheal diseases.Behavioral practices: Suboptimal Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices.Implications:Failure to address MAM and SAM will lead to increased child mortality, reduced cognitive and physical development, and a weakened community resilience against future shocks.

2 - Activities

Objective 1: To treat 1200 under-five children with SAM and 5,000 with MAM in targeted locations.• Activity 1.1: Provide supplementary food for MAM cases.• Activity 1.2: Train 50 healthcare workers and 100 community nutrition volunteers on the Integrated Management of Acute Malnutrition (IMAM) protocol.• Activity 1.3: Conduct bi-weekly follow-ups for enrolled children and monitor their progress.Objective 2: To prevent acute malnutrition through community engagement and resilience-building.• Activity 2.1: Conduct nutrition education sessions targeting 10,000 caregivers on IYCF practices.• Activity 2.2: Promote community-based growth monitoring and promotion (GMP) in collaboration with local women’s groups.• Activity 2.3: Integrate malnutrition screening with immunization campaigns and antenatal care visits.Objective 3: To address underlying causes of malnutrition through a multi-sectoral approach.• Activity 3.1: Improve access to safe water by rehabilitating 10 water points in Michika, Madagali, and MMC.• Activity 3.2: Distribute 5,000 hygiene kits to households with malnourished children to prevent waterborne diseases.• Activity 3.3: Partner with food security actors to distribute fortified food to households with high-risk children.
