Project Summary

The WASH condition in Sudan is deteriorating from time to time. Access to WASH services becomes more challenging for communities. Several WASH infrastructures remain in disrepair due to lack of spare parts and lack of maintenance budget and chronic socio-economic and political problems. East Darfur, South Darfur and West Kordofan states are amongst the highest affected states with more than 50% localities having WASH severity 4 & 5. In these states, lack of WASH services is exacerbating the health and nutrition problems. Reports showed that severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and WASH-related diseases are still very high among IDPs, returnees, and other vulnerable populations. The condition needs humanitarian intervention and working on long-term sustainability of WASH facilities.
Following the recent conflict in Sudan, The humanitarian situation in Sudan is dire, with 24.7 million people in need of assistance, including 18.11 million who face life-threatening health and mental issues. The main sectors of need are health, nutrition, WASH, protection, and FSL, which are driven by multiple factors, such as conflict, insecurity, social tensions, climate shocks, poor governance, and disease outbreaks. The recent conflict has worsened the situation for all families, but especially for the vulnerable groups, such as new and protracted IDPs, refugees, and returnees. These groups live in overcrowded conditions and face overcrowded conditions and face repeated emergencies and displacement.
This project aims to contribute to lifesaving of targeted communities in Khartoum, Aljazeera, East Darfur, South Darfur and West Kordofan through provision of timely WASH services to cope up current emergencies and respond to future emergencies.
Alight will provide inclusive WASH services that respond to the immediate needs of affected people and implement activities that contribute to the long-term sustainability of services. The following are main activities;
• Repair, Maintain and Rehabilitation of existing nonfunctioning water supply systems
• Upgrade (solarize) and extend/expand water supply systems.
• Installation of Emergency sanitation services for emergency affected communities
• Establish emergency water supply systems through treatment of surface water or through distribution of water treatment chemicals
• Distribution of essential WASH NFIs
• Support operation and maintenance of the water supply systems, and sanitation facilities,
• Conduct water treatment and ensuring water qualities
• Increasing awareness and behavioral change on hygiene, sanitation & environmental practices,
• Promoting community participation and improving sense of ownership.
• Support communities and local governments in improving the management of O&M of water supply systems and
The specific target locations (sub-localities and villages) will be selected through joint need assessments and identification of hotspot areas for emergencies. To maximize the impact; the project prioritizes service delivery for IDPs, returnees, and vulnerable populations affected by emergencies (including WASH-related epidemics and water resource-related conflicts) and facing severe acute malnutrition (SAM) & high food insecurity. This project contributes to overall national and state level humanitarian assistance initiatives by implementing the humanitarian WASH response activities; responding to their immediate need of emergency affected people and building resilience to future emergencies; i.e. by implementing community & local government empowerment activities. The project aimed to reach 492,000 most in-needed people in targeted areas. The project facilitates community participation especially women, girls, young people, and marginalized groups and engage throughout the different stage of project assessment and implementation.

1 - Needs

Following the recent conflict in Sudan, the humanitarian situation in Sudan changed into dire, with 24.7 million people in need of assistance, including 18.11 million who face life-threatening health and mental issues. WASH is among the main sectors critically needed, which are driven by multiple factors, such as conflict, insecurity, social tensions, climate shocks, poor governance, and disease outbreaks. The conflict affected all families, but especially vulnerable ones, such as new and protracted IDPs, refugees, and returnees, survival is though. These groups have been living daily life without any saving and exposed to shocks, they are in overcrowded conditions and face repeated emergencies and displacement. comparatively they have minimal coping capacity and resilience. The conflict has destroyed several public facilities such as health care and water supply facilities in urban areas, leaving them without personnel or necessary supplies to provide basic human services. Furthermore, the conflict has resulted in attacks on civilian infrastructure and looting of homes and humanitarian offices, leading to concerns over an increase in criminal activity and a breakdown of rule of law, putting vulnerable groups at risk of displacement, violence, and human rights violations. Regarding WASH, prior to the conflict in Sudan, inadequate water, hygiene, and sanitation practices posed significant risks to communities, with water and sanitation-related diseases being a leading cause of death among children under five. According to the 2022 UNICEF report, 17.3 million people in Sudan lacked access to basic level drinking water, putting them at risk of disease, while more than 24 million lacked access to proper sanitation facilities and over 10.5 million engaged in open defecation. This created a significant hazard to communities and had a profound impact on food security, rising malnutrition, disease outbreaks, conflict, and ultimately, child morbidity and mortality. The lack of WASH services had a devastating impact on the health and well-being of the Sudanese people, increasing the risk of waterborne diseases like cholera and diarrhea and leading to malnutrition and stunting in children.The conflict in Sudan has further exacerbated the WASH crisis, resulting in the displacement of millions of people and damage to water and sanitation infrastructure. This has made it even more challenging for people to access safe water and sanitation facilities. The conflict has resulted in damage to WASH facilities in healthcare and nutrition centers, leaving them without maintenance and unable to meet the specific needs of vulnerable groups. To mitigate the adverse effects of this situation, multiple means of access to safe water, including water trucking, point of use water treatment chemical distributions, rehabilitation of non-functional water supply and sanitation facilities need to be carried out. Targeted hygiene promotion should be provided to households, and the importance of good hygiene and health, as well as simple but critical actions that help to prevent diarrheal diseases and malnutrition, should be emphasized. Additionally, environmental health efforts, such as safe solid waste collection and disposal and drainage management, will need to be conducted. Affected and vulnerable communities will also receive emergency WASH NFIs. A comprehensive assessment of the area will be conducted by Alight to ensure efficient and effective delivery of services. These actions are crucial to ensuring the health and well-being of crisis-affected populations in emergency situations and play a vital role in humanitarian response efforts.

2 - Activities/Outputs

ALIGHT will continue providing lifesaving WASH services for conflict affected and vulnerable people, new IDPs and IDPs in protracted camps and rural host communities of target locations. By this project, existing strategic water facilities will be rehabilitated, expanded and hybridized with Solar energy installation. Alight Planned to rehabilitate 78 nonfunctional water facilities (47 hand pumps, 16 water yards, 15 protected wells), manage the O&M of 20 existing high-service water facilities, and operate water trucking in critically affected and drought prone areas when needed. Specific location will be led by assessments and need identifications, however current conflict affected people, high population density and lack of water sources at reasonable distance will be key factors for targeting. Also, Alight planned to build the capacity of 116 communities by forming water committees. The committees will get training and technical support. Where there is lack of improved water source and available surface waters, Alight will distribute POU water treatment chemicals for about 10000HH to treat their drinking water. Regarding sanitation, Alight planned to construct 1000 shared HH latrines and 50 communal toilets (4 stances each), 10 institutional latrines (4 stance each) and rehabilitate 500 shared HH latrines. Regarding hygiene promotion and practice support, 240 cadres will be trained on essential hygiene and sanitation promotion and covid-19 RCCE activities and participate in awareness raising and hygiene campaigns in communal set ups (include Nutrition and Health facilities, schools, or other communal setups ), 32 sets of solid waste collection tools will be provided (including cleaning tools, PPE, disinfection and operational support for environmental cleaning at health/nutriton care facilities ). The project also support SAM affected families, in this regard, Alight will provide standard WASH NFIs for 5000 HH whose children is affected by SAM. In an event of new IDPs, 5000HHs will get Dignity kits and 6000HHs will get essential hygiene kits (soaps, jerrycans, Buckets, ibriq). At communal places such as public toilets, health & Nutrition institutions, schools 240 hand washing facilities will be installed. In this project it is planned that up to 20% of the budget will be Cash Based Intervention (CBI) for WASH services (Such as Water trucking services, NFIs, etc). Also, the project will implement COVID19 IPC awareness raising through community health workers (CHWs) in camp and rural areas. We will print and distribute MOH developed IEC materials and intensify specific awareness raising campaigns in public places and by house-to-house visits. In total, by this project WASH intervention an estimated 492,000 people who are living in Khartoum, Al Jazeera, West Kordofan, East Darfur and South Darfur states will get improved WASH services and conditions.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

ALIGHT will use environmental friendly approaches such as use of solar energy instead of fossil fuels to power its health facilities, water supply systems, and conventional and locally available materials for construction and maintenance activities. ALIGHT uses local culturally appropriate materials for latrine construction such as grass, bamboo sticks and these are easily renewable and has less environmental impact. Also, Alight includes sensitization activities on environmental degradation and climate change impact. Communities will get awareness through trained community volunteers and encouraged to plant indigenous trees. At start of rainy season to reduce the impact of malaria, Alight will conduct dedicated malaria prevention campaigns and environmental interventions such as drainage improvement and collection and safe disposal of solid wastes and filling potholes and draining pools. These activities will be conducted by community participation.Regarding Boreholes drilling and rehabilitation, Alight will follow the GoS environmental assessment before intervention and apply its recommendations. Infrastructure locations will be carefully selected in order to avoid water sources and environmental contamination. Our environmental health activities that include solid waste collection, transport and disposal and de-sludging and disposal of latrines will be carried out in consultation with the states WES and according to the regulation of the country. Alight will use conventional WASH Chemicals, such as disinfectant chlorine and water quality reagents, PUR, DPD1, 3, and Bentonite cement for borehole drilling and alcohols and detergents for personal and facilities hygiene. These chemicals are already in the approved list of the country regulation, except that Alight do not use any products that have negative effect to the nature and environment.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The WASH response activities are designed based on safe programming criteria. For example, latrine construction designs will take into consideration protection needs of different segments of users based on Gender, Age and Diversity (GAD) as it affects the Safety, Access and Dignity (SAD) of users. Designs consider the diverse needs and ensure privacy, dignity and security of women, girls and children, and give easy access for pregnant women, the elderly and people with disability. WASH facilities will be constructed at safe places with the consultation of the community. Latrines will be near to homes so that they can be accessed safely at all times of day and night. Institutional latrines will be gender segregated and ensure privacy and dignity, will have features such as lockable doors and solar lights where possible. The sitting of latrines will take into consideration water sources to avoid possible contamination. To ensure safe access, water point's site will be selected in consultation with community. As much water points as possible will be placed at public places (open space) and be close to the living places which will reduce distance as well as prevent incidence of GBV. Furthermore, we construct conventional emergency shelters with lockable doors and ventilation and equipped with light during night that provide good protection in use for people with special need (PSN) in IDPs camp and for others with extreme vulnerability.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

In regard to AAP; Alight will employ the AGD policy that is implemented through community based, participatory approaches that are already well established in the IDPs camp and host community areas. The Project will ensure that participation, feedback and complaints mechanisms are integrated in all strategies and through the project cycle. This is to ensure the project decisions and actions are responsive to the expressed priorities, needs, capacities and views of all persons of concern irrespective of Age, Gender, Diversity and Disability backgrounds. This is aimed at ensuring meaningful participation, enhanced communication and transparency, improved feedback and response. ALIGHT will ensure a balanced boys, girls, women and men in all project activities with relative 60% female and 40% male. Data for beneficiaries will be disaggregated by gender, age groups and location, and project activities will be tailored for different groups in line with developmental stages, needs and vulnerabilities. The Project will ensure that all persons of concern have equal and non- discriminatory access to protection, assistance and solutions, this will build on their resilience and recovery. Activities like awareness raising, dialogues, will be conducted in different WASH trainings and in the communities, specific needs and vulnerabilities identification and culturally sensitive issues discussed separately. Alight will maintain effective feedback systems (including comments, suggestions and complaints box) using a variety of communication channels that are accessible to all persons of concern and that are appropriate for both sensitive and non- sensitive feedback e.g. use of complaints boxes, toll free hot lines. In regard to PSEA; All Alight staffs have committed to and signed PSEA policy, Child Protection policy, Code of Conduct as well as the engaged community based networks Alight is a member of National and State levels PSEA task force and regularly coordinates with other partners in relevant PSEA forums. Under the Project, we will conduct Safeguarding and SEA risk assessments to mitigate any potential or identified risks of SEA. Alight has contributed to Inter Agency Action Plan for the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and will ensure Zero Tolerance - No Excuse to SEA in all its operations and project. Jointly with the PSEA task force, we will develop relevant SEA IEC materials and distribute them in communities, raise awareness on SEA, strengthen community-based complaints mechanisms, enhance reporting and ensure appropriate measures are undertaken for investigations.
