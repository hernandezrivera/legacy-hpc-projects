Project Summary

The protracted conflict in Northeast Nigeria is the root cause of one of the most severe humanitarian crises today affecting more than 8.7 million people, 3.9 million of which are in Borno State. IDPs, returnees, and host communities in Borno have lost their capacity to meet their most basic needs.
Borno State has been particularly affected by the ongoing conflict in Northeast Nigeria, hosting 81% of the 1.9 million internally displaced persons (IDPs) in the country. The protracted conflict has cut people off from their primary livelihoods, constrained access to markets and limited opportunities for women, youth and other vulnerable groups. UNOCHA’s February 2021 Situation Report indicates that up to 5.1 million people are at risk of being critically food insecure during the lean season of 2021 (June-August), a figure similar to 2016-2017 when Borno state was on the brink of famine.
It is estimated that 1.5 million people in the area are food insecure (IPC phase 3-4), 2.88 million are unable to meet their essential WASH needs, and 1.33 million need urgent Protection/GBV assistance. The project seeks to to provide life-saving CVA to social protection for households impacted by conflict and displacement in Northeast Nigeria, enabling them have access to effective, coordinated and inclusive assistance.
With the new forced returns of IDPs out of camps to their host communities, numerous vulnerable households have stayed back in host communities in the metropolis without a stable means of livelihood and survival. The IRC will provide multi-purpose cash assistance to these vulnerable households with particular focus on Jere LGA. This will primarily focus on the lean season, then food security deteriorates. Across Jere LGA, the IRC will provide comprehensive protection services, across project activities, including protection risk mitigation activities and GBV/Child protection case management.

1 - Needs

In Borno State, Nigeria - the epicenter of the conflict - the protected conflict has cut people off from their primary livelihoods, constrained access to markets and limited opportunities for women, youth and other vulnerable groups. While needs are high across all sectors, the most compelling need remains Food. Recent findings from the March 2021 Cadre Harmonize (CH) analysis project that over 2.04 million people will face a food Crisis or Emergency (IPC phase 3-4) in Borno State by August 2021, a 31.5% increase in food consumption gaps compared to the previous projections. This is compounded by the lack of a robust social protection system both at federal level and state level (Borno) to prevent economic shocks and uplift vulnerable people out of poverty. Conflict and insecurity have also significantly increased the protection risks of IDPs and host communities with 88% of violent incidents recorded in Borno.

2 - Activities

Vulnerable households would be provided with multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA). The rationale for this planned modality of assistance is premised upon the following:- MPCA provides an appropriate, effective and efficient response to meeting the multiple needs of people affected by crisis in a single transfer.- The transfer is unconditional, and so places the emphasis on the crisis affected individuals’ ability to choose and decide how to address their immediate needs.- The cash transfers are conducted in a manner that upholds the dignity of the beneficiaries.- MPCA allows for a more harmonized planning and approach including the potential future linkages with social protection.- Accordingly, the MPCA approach upholds a principled humanitarian response that puts needs at the centre. These advantages position the approach to be suitable for assisting IDP populations in the Northeast who have undergone forced relocations.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The project will involve all partners, volunteers and stakeholders in a PSEA training using IRC's PSEA training guidelines and other standard guiding principles. In addition, the toll-free line will be pen to all participants of the project and be open for report of PSEA related issues. The IRC will also collaborate with the protection cluster through it's protection sector to make referrals for beneficiaries who require protection.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Feedback will be collected from the most vulnerable through physical conversation and the provision of feedback mechanisms in the community. The physical conversation would be through community members on KII conversation with their respective representatives and FGD with community focal groups. The Client Responsiveness Mechanism (CRM) of the IRC M&E unit will also be used to collate feedback while also leveraging on the IRC toll free line made available to the community members. Community structures such as community committees and community engagement meetings will also be put in place to ensure that there is an avenue to communicate and exchange information.
