Project Summary

The Syria crisis is one of the most complex humanitarian crises in recent memory. Humanitarian assistance is provided by a large variety of active actors on the ground, operating both from within Syria and from neighbouring countries. Coordination hubs have been established, in Damascus, Gaziantep, and NES, with WOS in Amman, that ensures common strategies and best use of resources from the different hubs.
WASH needs of affected populations will continue to be high across 2022 and 2023 with sector partners expected to provide quality emergency WASH services and maintain WASH facilities and infrastructure. WASH needs will largely be influenced by, but not limited to:
•Water scarcity and drought-like conditions – The evolution of the situation will heavily depend on the sufficiency of winter precipitation, the ability to fill in the dams along the Euphrates River through an increase in the flow to water, which will impact hydropower generation and functionality of water systems powered by them. Nevertheless, proper aquifer recharge, affected by years of over-extraction and drought-like conditions, will not be achieved by a single year’s sufficient winter precipitation.
•Energy supply crisis – the evolution of the situation will largely depend on the level of winter precipitation and related ability to resume normal hydropower generation as well as on the availability of fuel for fossil-fuel power plants, water supply systems, or solid waste management services. If intermittency and further degradation of electricity supply to WASH systems continue, this will trigger a further increase in the people in need of WASH assistance.
•New displacements: New displacements may also be triggered by hostilities or economic or water crises.
•Population living in IDP sites: population in last resort sites continued to increase despite little to no major hostilities taking place in 2021. This may be partially linked to ongoing economic or water crises, and to the closure of collective centers. Due to funding decrease, trend deterioration of the WASH conditions in IDP sites may be expected.
•Returnee movements: no major change is expected due to political stasis. Nevertheless, there are areas experiencing a higher level of returns (returns from Al Hole camp, returns to the areas around the frontline in the northern part of rural Latakia, Idleb, Aleppo). This trend is likely to continue, with the returnees facing lower levels of access to WASH services than residents
As Sector/Cluster Lead agency for the WASH Sector, UNICEF must ensure that sectoral coordination needs at Hub and at Whole of Syria levels are met through appropriate support. There are also growing needs to create an enabling environment allowing for the Government of Syria and more specifically the Ministry of Water Resources to be more involved with existing coordination structures as the currently the wash Sector is the leading structure that coordinates between WASH partners and managing the sector activities, with minimum engagement of Ministry of Water Resources (MOWR). The sector will try this year to work closely with MOWR to encourage its management team in the ministry for more engagement in leading the sector activities possible and with data collection processes at different levels. This project presents accordingly the activities and related budget allowing to fulfill these coordination tasks and the development of this enabling environment, thus, to ensure following objectives:
• Increased predictability of geographic coverage and sectoral responsibility
• Demonstrated accountability (for sector-wide performance).
• Increased field level capacities to address gaps.
• Quality and uninterrupted WASH services.
• Maximized coordination and partnership and buy-in from the major humanitarian actors to ensure a more strategic and coherent response in support of national led efforts.
• Advocacy efforts to ensure that the sector needs are appropriately funded

4 - Needs

Power outages are common across the country which impacts the functionality of water systems, leading to restricted pumping hours and bypassing of water treatment systems, which reduce water quality. The extremely low water level in the Euphrates affects the production of electricity. Some large water systems are split between different areas of control, which challenges equitable water supply access for hundreds of thousands of people. Alouk water station that provides water to over 460,000 people in Al-Hasakeh governorate, was not functional for a quarter of 2021. The effects of climate change, cumulative drought trend, and aquifer overexploitation, also for irrigation purposes, combined with the lack of agreements between riparian countries on water use in transboundary river basins and aquifersReliability and efficiency of water systems have decreased since 2016, with nearly 2 million fewer people using water networks as the only household water supply modality, in comparison to mid-2020. This means up to 47% of the population is relying on often unsafe alternatives to piped water, to meet their water needs. At least 70% of sewage is discharged untreated and at least half of the sewerage systems are not functional. in NES, no wastewater treatment exists for the IDP sites, resulting in environmental pollution and increased disease prevalence.

5 - Implementation of activities

Activities will be implemented through the WOS WASH coordination group supported by WoS sector Coordination team. To ensure evidence-based planning, the sector will continue to promote and lead harmonized inter hub needs assessments and common analysis, contribute towards intersectoral analysis, as well as strong monitoring of the response. Also to coordinate with specialized agencies to monitor the market of water and essential hygiene items. The sector will also provide opportunities for capacity building in key technical areas, including risk-informed management, gender, and protection mainstreaming. The sector will ensure effective coordination between the hubs and at WoS, as per the IASC standards, including: •Ensuring effective Information Management at a WoS level •Promoting the exchange of best practices and technical standards •Representing and advocating on behalf of, the sector in the relevant fora, at hub and WoS level and at intersectoral level •Effective coordination ongoing in three hubs, and at WoS level, and with relevant sectors (Nutrition, Health, Education and Protection) •Organization of joint WASH assessments between the hubs •Activity monitoring (4W) at WoS level updated monthly •Sector specific needs identified •Common WoS strategy developed •WASH participation in all HPC initiatives, both at Hub and WoS level •Implementation of capacity building strategy based on UNICEF MENA RO (ToT) •WASH bottleneck analysis

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

The WASH Sector will ensure that the ’do no harm’ principle is systematically applied. Potential protection risks related to the implementation of the WASH related activities, along with a description of the relevant mitigation measures and resources required for monitoring those risks have been identified.[1] WASH partners will take adequate measures to minimize exposure of the most vulnerable groups, including women, adolescent girls, children, people with disabilities, female-headed households, to greater protection risks, particularly GBV. WASH staff will be trained on key GBV concepts and referral pathways. Women, adolescent girls and people with disabilities will be consulted during project cycle steps to facilitate feedback. High level of explosive hazard contamination close to water and sanitation infrastructure, or contamination of accumulated garbage/rubbles, which potentially put humanitarian staff in danger during WASH assessments, rehabilitation works, and operation/maintenance will be considered. Where relevant, WASH staff will be trained on explosive hazard risk mitigation, while risk education sessions and awareness-raising materials for beneficiaries will be integrated as much as possible into WASH programming.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

UNICEF as co-lead agency for the inter-agency network (ICN) on PSEA, will continue to support and work closely with other agencies aiming at enhancing the portfolio pf protection from sexual exploitation and abuse. The main goal of the ICN is to enhance the networking for the benefit of people at risk, and ensure access to complaint mechanisms and specialized services for victims. Therefore, continuous capacity building for humanitarian workers on zero tolerance for SEA and awareness raising for the community on rights to humanitarian aid free of any exchange in dignified way is integrated in project activities. Consequently, as per-condition for contracting implementing partners, PSEA assurance and capacity building plan are guaranteed. Close coordination with the Inter-Agency Country Network (ICN) on PSEA will be continuously guaranteed.
