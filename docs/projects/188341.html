Project Summary

In line with the needs and priorities identified in HRP 2022/2023, based on the experience in previous years, COOPI aims to give continuity and expand its interventions to support self-sufficiency and food security at Household (HH) level, working in Aleppo, Dar’a, Hama, Homs, Idleb, Raqqa and R. Damascus. To enforce vulnerable families’ food security, the project targets moderately food insecure families for monthly life-sustaining assistance strengthening benef. self-resilience to adopt more positive coping strategies and through agri best practices sustain HH food production &maximize crops. Projects will target most vulnerable HHs, especially women HHs, HHs with elderly, PwD, vulnerable returnees/IDP, families with high number of children, HHs with pregnant/lactating women, unaccompanied children, severity scale 3 and above. Through activities of agri inputs distribution and the food vouchers, project will strengthen the affected HHs’ resilience and reduce their dependency on aid adopting a participatory approach. Project will contribute to SO1:“Provide life-saving humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable people with an emphasis on those in areas with high severity of needs”, and additionally to SO3:“Increase the resilience of affected communities by improving access to livelihood opportunities and basic services, especially among the most vulnerable households and communities”. Food Vouchers (FV) distribution will be according to the FAS sector recommendation of the feeding period of 30 days, minimum of 8 distributions to the assessed same food insecure HHs, meeting a minimum of 65% daily kcal intake according to the CWG recommendations of the amount of voucher, while agri kits will be as per FAS and coordinated with ERL Sector. Programmatic monitoring will be done through regular field visits, PDMs and focus group discussions. COOPI field staff and MEAL will attend all distributions and redemptions. Three rounds of PDM, including FCS and CSI surveys at each location will be conducted (baseline,mid-project and endline). Traders monitoring and price survey will be conducted before every distribution to ensure that prices are aligned with prices in community (compared to WFP price bulletin). To pursuit these goals, the project aims to strengthen the capacity of communities to restore their livelihoods through enhancing food security and supporting HH agriculture recovery to sustain 71,500 the most vulnerable beneficiaries in need in 2 years. Agro inputs will be delivered to those HHs who have an access to their land, ensuring that lands are free of EOs, while in the areas still EOs affected, or for beneficiaries without land, FV and will be distributed on HH level. Trainings on agriculture best practices will be organized for beneficiaries, to enhance their knowledge and to maximize the food production from the agricultures inputs received.
Furthermore, FV modality, as more dignified form of assistance, will contribute to the development of local market and to enforce the affected communities.
The beneficiaries’ participation will be promoted throughout the project implementation, during the market assessment, trader surveys, focus groups discussion, monthly redemption activities and feedback mechanisms. In order to ensure high quality of the project implementation and beneficiaries’ feedback mechanisms, COOPI staff and SARC volunteers will be trained on PSEA, MEAL tools and Vouchers Assistance (VA) processes.
In order to facilitate the relationship with the several stakeholders and to promote a synergic approach to the challenges, COOPI will ensure the coordination with local authorities, as well as Food Security Agriculture Sector (FSA), ERL Sector, Cash Working Group and international and national actors active in the same target areas. With the aim to adopt an integrated strategy, COOPI FSL project will be implemented considering the projects of other actors and sectors taking place in the same Governorates.

4 - Needs

According to the HNO 2022 Syria remains a complex humanitarian emergency. Substantial harvest losses combined with a chronic economic deprivation have contributed to the mounting food insecurity. HH’s capacity to cope with growing food insecurity has diminished. Project targets the most vulnerable in targeted Districts in R.Damascus, Dar’a, Homs, Hama,Aleppo,Idleb and Raqqa where COOPI is already presence, where is 2,686,127 acute FI PiN (HNO 2022). Vulnerable HH in R. Damascus and Hama are particularly at risk, adopting several harmful changes to their daily food consumption. Conditions were particularly poor for the IDP as close to10% self-reported a least one household member going to sleep hungry due to lack of food, more than half coped by purchasing less expensive food(51%), reducing the size of the meal(20%) daily. IDP HH report negative coping strategies more frequently than other population groups. Majority of IDP households out of sites/camps are concentrated in R. Damascus(24%), in Aleppo(18 %) and Idleb(10%), while new displacements were registered mainly in Aleppo and Idleb as well in Dar’a. One of the most harmful coping strategies – child labor – is also adopted, significantly increased in Hama(42 %) and Homs(40 %), while taking children out of school is highest in Ar-Raqqa(35 %), Idleb(28 %), Aleppo(26%), needing to work to support the HH. HH are continuously changing daily habits to maintain some level of food consumption.

5 - Implementation of activities

Project will be implemented in R.Damascus, Dar’a, Homs, Hama,Aleppo,Idleb and Raqqa.The activity will complement COOPI ongoing interventions of agro kits distribution and FVA based on the successful COOPI program implemented in R.Damascus and Dar’a.Through the strengthening of agriculture inputs and activities COOPI will contribute to reduce the dependency on food assistance of the supported families.Families unable to be involved in the agro activities due to absence of the land or the capacities will be supported by FV. Activity will target moderately food insecure HHs (the supplementary food items will be included in the voucher) for monthly life-sustaining assistance.COOPI will provide 5,000HHs with a monthly distribution for 8 rounds of unconditional restricted FV (50-70USD) per month to cover MEB gap as per FAS and CWG sector recommendation.Through agro kits content will be as per sector recommendation distribution the longer term food security will support 2000 HH in condition of receiving the agro kits.In total 21,700 people will be assisted in 2022, 71500 HHs in 2 years. COOPI staff/volunteers will be trained on PSEA, market assessment, trader survey, distribution and redemption process, MEAL tools to ensure high quality of project implementation and monitoring. Trainings on agro best practices will be organized for beneficiaries to enhance their knowledge and to maximize the food production. Methodologies will include the recommendations of CWG and FAS

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk(R): exclusion of some groups due to the choice of the location. Likelihood(L): medium. Impact(I): high. Mitigation Measures(MM): consult with communities in the choice of the location. R: tension in community, for perceived inequality, discrimination, favoritism, including risk for project staff. L: medium. I: high. MM: establish adequate selection criteria mechanisms based on vulnerabilities; criteria are communicated in transparent manner with all actors.R: the population targeted is put at risk as a result of the facility location; lack of consideration for the accessibility to services (in terms of freedom of movement, safety and security for men, women, boys, girls, elderly, PwD). L: medium. I: high. MM: consider mobility patterns and contextual analysis as part of decisions for establishing a static facility/mobile service provision or combine both; conduct safety audit to ensure safe access (route and location) by all the targeted groups; select staff for all activities with adequate knowledge of the community, but also respecting balance in gender, age, social profile. R: different vulnerable groups might not have the same access to services and assistance. L: high. I: high. MM: make use of existing tools/mapping; coordinate at local level with other protection actors to ensure balanced coverage; avoid overlapping or concentration of activities if possible; ensure consultation with communities that may be at particular risk of exposure and risk of exclusion.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: exploitation or abuse perpetrated by staff members or service providers during delivering assistance. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation measure: establish formal and informal feedback and complaint mechanisms in each area of intervention; provide staff with training on Code Conduct and PSEA; ensure the proper communication to the communities about the gratuity of the services; establish mechanisms and procedures for ensuring compliance with PSEA zero-tolerance policy. Standard operating procedures are in place for submission and receipt of complaints, reporting, investigation and victim assistance. PSEA focal point have been established in COOPI country offices and headquarter. All staff members receive training and information on preventing and responding to acts of sexual exploitations and abuse.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R:FV fraud and identity theft, voucher sold or exchanged for unauthorized items. A: heightened visibility for beneficiaries and voucher spent on non-food items, causing a negative impact on food security outcomes. MM: vendors’ selection at community level with a good reputation. Random quality controls of suppliers. Include in the FV design unique serial numbers and laser tags to avoid copies. Crosscheck of voucher supplied with COOPIs copy. Random monitoring at HHs’ level the quantity and quality of the goods exchanged and utilized (as PDM questionnaires). R: direct access to the targeted areas and HHs is not allowed or has become unsafe for hum. workers. A: escalation of hostilities in the target area.MM: COOPI will communicate constantly with agencies, government and security monitoring networks in the country. All staff will receive trainings on security.R: selected beneficiaries cannot access their land. A: the presence of EOs may hamper access to agri. fields MM: COOPI will organize awareness raising sessions for all staff, volunteers and communities in EOs recognition, will coordinate with SARC and UNMAS on progress of EOs clearance. R: Staff/volunteers/beneficiaries are infected with Covid-19 during implementation. A: staff and volunteers are not properly protected. MM: staff and volunteers are provided with PPE and trained on the best hygiene practice and prevention measures; distribution will be conducted in line with sector's recommendation for safe distribution.
