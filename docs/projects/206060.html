Project Summary

PASS seek to implement front line WASH response to the IDP, returns-from Sudan crises- and host communities affected by multiple hazards including malnutrition in Ayod County. This project will target the specific needs of the above-mentioned vulnerable groups. The project targets a total of 11,111 beneficiaries 60% women and girls, in the 8 payams (i.e. Ayod town, Wau, Mogok, Paguong, Korwai, Pagil, Pajiek, and Kuachdeng). 5,600 malnourished children (under 5 years) will also be targeted through integrated WASH with Nutrition at the community and nutrition facility (OTP/SC) level. Provision of WASH NFI at the nutrition centers will ensure availability of sufficient safe water for SAM/MAM admitted children.
The project will mitigate WASH specific gender based violence (GBV) through conducting safety audits for the WASH infrastructures. PASS female staffs will conduct the audits to determine the protection needs of women and girls. Establishment, design and rehabilitation of WASH facilities will be informed by findings from consultation of women & girls. Culturally gender appropriate toilets will be established/rehabilitated and fitted with appropriate lockable doors to enhance protection. To manage menstrual hygiene, the project will facilitate acquisition and distribution of MHM kits to vulnerable women and girls of reproductive age.

1. Needs

Ayod county has suffered multiple shocks which includes localized intercommunal violence, cattle raiding, effects of climate change (floods) including hanger that has caused malnutrition rates among the infants to be high. According to the interagency spot check assessment conducted in Ayod from 2nd to 9th August 2023. An increase in population (due to influx of returnees escaping conflict in Sudan), the urgent needs of the vulnerable group cuts across all sectors including WASH. The population increase in the area places an extra burden on existing WASH infrastructure and puts a strain on scarce resources for operation and maintenance of WASH services. As per the interagency spot-check, 85% of the population in Ayod has no access to clean water. The population depends on surface water (Jonglei canal) for drinking, domestic use and for their animals. The three-surface water treatment plant (SWAT) that were previously installed along the canal are non-functional. Levels of knowledge on handwashing is low, with 73% of the respondents attesting that they do not handwash with soap. Sanitation coverage extremely low. As many do not practice good hygiene. This is partly due to social-cultural nature: majority in Ayod are not used to good sanitation and hygiene behavior, hence cultural preference of open defecation. The IPC projection period of December 2023 to March 2024 shows that Ayod County in Jonglei State will face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. The prevailing food insecurity situation is driven by effects of significant underlying vulnerabilities that have built up over time due to recurrent shocks. High malnutrition rate is strongly linked to poor WASH conditions demonstrated by diarrhea-malnutrition which is prevalent in vulnerable populations. According to the IPC AMN (July – September 2023) malnutrition level in Ayod is classified as IPC AMN Phase 4 (critical). The major factors contributing to acute malnutrition include high prevalence of diseases (Diarrhea, fever, and cough) attributed to poor sanitation conditions, with open defecation rate. Displaced women and girls continue to be disproportionally affected by protection related WASH issues, with gender-based violence occurring due to insufficient and unsafe access to water and sanitation facilities. PASS -being a static partner in Ayod has experience in WASH programming since 2018. In March 2021 to May 2021, PASS conducted WASH intervention (focusing on hygiene promotion and distribution of WASH NFI’s) targeting 9,726 beneficiaries with support from the Rapid Response Fund (RRF). The emergency project targeted beneficiaries from Ayod town, Wau, Mogok, Korwai, Paguong and Pagil Payams in Ayod County of Jonglei State. The project trained hygiene promoters and supported one thousand nine hundred thirty-six households with WASH NFIs. Five hundred most vulnerable women and girls of reproductive age supported with dignity kits. From July to September 2022 PASS - courtesy of the RRF- PASS implemented a WASH project targeting food insecure population “Emergency WASH response to the most vulnerable population in IPC 4 living in Jiech, Kuach-deng and Korwai Payams of Ayod county, Jonglei State”. The project reached IDPs: 1,501 HHs (9,006 individuals) and Host Community: 1200 HHs (7,200 individuals). The project activities included training of twenty (20) hygiene promoters, conducting outreaches on hygiene promotion and distribution of WASH NFIs. The project also partnered with Nutrition Partners through provision of WASH services to the malnourished. Recently, PASS received another RRF funding targeting returnees (from Sudan crisis) through provision of emergency WASH services in the Same location. The above array of experience gives PASS competitive advantage for this project. This project targets 23,300 vulnerable people (IDPs, Returnees and Host communities) from Ayod with 60% being women and girls. 5,600 are SAM/MAM children.

2. Project activities

PASS will target the most vulnerable IDP, returns-from Sudan crises- and host communities affected by multiple hazards including malnutrition, with integrated WASH, Nutrition and GBV activities. WASH and Nutrition integrated activities will target malnourished children including their caregivers/mothers from thenutrition facilities (OTP/SC) and household level. Rehabilitation of water point and provision of frontline WASH services at the Nutrition facilities will ensure availability of sufficient safe water for SAM/MAM admitted children. To mitigate WASH specific related gender based violence (GBV) the project will involve female staffs in conducting protection assessments to determine the protection needs of women and girls. Establishment, design and rehabilitation of WASH facilities will be informed by findings from consultation of women & girls. Culturally gender appropriate toilets will be established/rehabilitated and fitted with appropriate lockable doors to enhance protection. To manage menstrual hygiene, the project will facilitate the acquisition and distribution of MHM kits to vulnerable women and girls. Activities Summary:•Rehabilitation of non-functional hand pumps/water points •Rehabilitation of water points and distribution of WASH NFIs at nutrition facilities (OTP/SC) targeting SAM/MAM admitted children. •Hygiene promoters training on proper hygiene practices to prevent AWD and other water-borne diseases. •Hygiene promotion campaigns, at household level, in public places/gatherings, food stalls and markets coupled with distribution of WASH NFIs •Focus Group Discussions/safety audits with women and girls on safety, design and appropriate location of WASH facilities •Establishment and rehabilitation of gender appropriate latrines with handwashing facilities •Training of Nutrition workers/volunteers on the WASH minimum package of activities •Distribution of Hygiene kit to SAM (especially children under 5 years) •Distribution of MHM kits to vulnerable women and girls.
