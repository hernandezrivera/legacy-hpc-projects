Project Summary

The project aims to provide mobile protection interventions to People of Concern (PoC) and People with Special Needs (PSN) ensuring that their needs are prioritised. The project will aim to specifically identify and support unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), identify, prevent and respond to GBV; and address general protection issues identified through assessments and safety audits. Over the past two years, the political, security and economic situation in South Sudan has deteriorated with a corresponding increase in violence, a significantly worsened humanitarian situation and massive violations of women and child rights. Since 2015, spreading hostilities have resulted in 4 million South Sudanese people being displaced, including 2.4 million children (UNICEF, 2018). It should be noted that the fighting in South Sudan has two distinct, but at times related, elements: a national political struggle; and intercommunal conflicts over land and cattle. This has precipitated a sustained increase in human rights violations particularly among the refugee and displaced populations. These violations have manifested in the form of increased general protection, child protection and gender-based violence (GBV) cases.
Intersos will target vulnerable groups ‘left behind’ or in isolated pockets in South Sudan where their larger community have fled the area due to conflict or other factors and as a result their traditional community support mechanisms or services no longer exist and/or they no longer have access or ability to access lifesaving humanitarian services / assistance due to their vulnerability or the security situation. General protection interventions will focus on providing both mobile and static response intervention to prevent human rights abuses and providing life-saving assistance to survivors of any form of violence. Focus will be placed on preventive interventions and mainstreaming of protection in other sector interventions. INTERSOS will continue to lead protection assessments and safety audits to ensure evidence-based protection programming. INTERSOS will also focus on strengthening community protection mechanisms through community engagement and capacity building. In addition, INTERSOS will continue to play an advocacy role to ensure that other critical services are provided and that they contribute to creating a protective environment for refugee and IDP communities, including the host communities.
Child protection interventions will contribute to reducing the number of children and young people exposed to, or at risk of, violence, exploitation and abuse in emergency settings. The programme will focus on delivering critical services, promoting positive behaviours and strengthening community child protection mechanisms. Child protection interventions will be aligned with Child Protection Sub-cluster strategy and will include responses to child rights violations, including gender-based violence and child marriage; family tracing and reunification; and safe release, care and reintegration of children associated with armed groups, alongside efforts to prevent recruitment. INTERSOS will provide training and empowerment to demobilized and other at-risk young people linking them to new skills and opportunities and facilitating their reintegration into the community. INTERSOS will advocate for provision of mine risk education through community capacity development and the school curriculum.
GBV interventions will be aligned with the GBV Sub-cluster Strategy and will seek to collaborate with strategic partners to contribute to the provision of the basic package of multi-sectoral GBV services at community level; build capacity of service providers and communities to deliver quality GBV services in line with best practices and minimum standards for humanitarian settings, strengthen GBV prevention and risk mitigation across INTERSOS interventions including through mainstreaming; and strengthen co-ordination, advocacy and collaboration at community level. Through collaboration with other actors, survivors of gender-based violence will be provided with psychosocial support and case management services. The risk of gender-based violence will be mitigated by providing women- and girl-friendly spaces and addressing social norms.

Needs

According to UNHCR (2018), as of 31 July 2018, the refugee population in South Sudan stood at 298,037 individuals, consisting of 69,175 households in 21 different locations across South Sudan. The majority (91%) of the refugees are hosted in Upper Nile and the Unity regions in South Sudan. UNHCR (2018) also estimates that 1.84 million people are internally displaced in South Sudan (UNHCR, 2018). Refugee and internally displaced persons (IDP) are vulnerable to a wide range of protection risks. A Safety Audit conducted by INTERSOS in Jonglei state revealed that physical violence is common in the IDPs settlements, due to limited infrastructure; such as; shelter, and water points. Several physical violence incidents have been reported around the overcrowded water point areas and that creates a tension between IDPs and the host community. Robbery incidents and gunshots could be heard from time to time. Insecurity in Lakes State, for example, is characterized by armed cattle raiding, conflict over scarce resources and tribal clashes (UNICEF, 2015). In the dry season, competition for water, pastureland and cattle campgrounds can precipitate violence and revenge attacks. Like other parts of the country, the Lakes state has seen increased use of more powerful weapons in cattle raiding, which has become increasingly violent and retaliatory. With the proliferation of weapons, conflict in most of the states has the potential to be very deadly. Based on the IPMT implemented in 2017 and in the start of 2018, seven IPMT assessments were produced, which indicate many different protection needs in the communities that were targeted. On GBV, the needs identified are various, and include sexual exploitation and abuse of women and girls, rape, restrictions of movement and domestic violence against women and girls, economic inequalities, such as denial of resources and opportunities, forced and early marriage, and lack of access for women and girls to safe spaces, psychosocial and health services, and justice. The remoteness and isolation of many locations in South Sudan, coupled with a complete lack of communication means in areas that are heavily affected by the crisis makes it often extremely difficult to get reliable information on the humanitarian situation needs on ground. The local population, both displaced and host communities, often face heightened protection concerns caused by a lack of services, such as Shelter, WASH, Nutrition and Health. There is thus an urgent need of increasing access to conflict-affected communities through regular field missions by an Integrated Protection Mobile Team (IPMT) for need assessment for the specific locations and respond to the urgent GBV cases by Intersos GBV mobile teams. The aim of such missions is to conduct systematic context and needs analysis or to provide rapid response to affected population as first responder or surge capacity when partners on ground do not have sufficient resources to deal with a sudden and unexpected increase in humanitarian needs.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

INTERSOS will provide all persons of concern within its areas of focus with timely, accurate, and relevant information on their rights and entitlements, the roles and responsibilities of INTERSOS, and the organization's programmes, commitments and processes. Through community engagement, communication and dialogue between INTERSOS and PoC will be facilitated at key stages throughout the operations management cycle. INTERSOS will share information and communicate in languages and formats, and media, that are culturally appropriate for, and accessible to, all groups in a community.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages are context specific and tailored for gender and age groups. INTERSOS uses facilitation guides for children, adolescents and women which are age appropriate for facilitating dialogue sessions with children in the Women and Girls Friendly Spaces. Specific tailored tools are also used during community engagement and awareness raising activities

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

INTERSOS staff who come into contact with beneficiaries sign a Prevention of SEA Code of Conduct to ensure that they prevent sexual exploitation and abuse of project beneficiaries. Internal trainings are also conducted to refresh staff on their obligations to prevent SEA by timely reporting cases.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Consultations were conducted at community level through focus group discussions, key informant interviews and observation. The consultations covered men, women, boys and girls as well as traditional and local leaders.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

INTERSOS ensures that complaint and feedback mechanisms are in place during the implementation period. This is achieved through the installation of a mobile complaint box in each location where INTERSOS deploys as well as regular FGDs with beneficiaries about INTERSOS service delivery to understand how they are perceived and what can be improved. Furthermore, there will be monthly staff meetings to understand the community feedback on existing services and additional needs identified by those who closely work with members of the community. INTERSOS will use its internal monitoring and evaluation and control project appraisal tool (PAT) as well as field visits to ensure accountability and guarantee corrective measures are taken timely whenever necessary. The PSS intervention will follow a flexible work-plan, which is constantly revised upon collection of beneficiaries’ inputs. During case management, all case management principles will be respected, particularly the confidentiality of survivors will be respected at all stages in field locations. Women will be informed about confidentiality and can agree to what extent they wish to give consent about the release of information in case of referrals to third parties. All case files will be kept in a lockable cabinet and no identifiable information will be recorded.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

INTERSOS integrates community engagement in all aspects of its protection work, and recognizes the capacities as well as needs of communities in humanitarian crise, in order to avoid harm and ensure that humanitarian programmes do not inadvertently leave people and communities worse off. In this regard, INTERSOS will continue to conduct extensive dialogues, focus groups discussions and participatory methodologies to ensure the community actively participates in all the protection activities from start to finish.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Several barriers were identified with regard to access in hard to reach areas which are often inaccessible. Other barriers include exclusion of the most vulnerable groups by the more powerful groups, stigma and discrimination, and gender inequalities within the communities. The project will deploy mobile teams in hard to reach areas to enhance their accessibility to the protection services. To boost access, project will also deliberately target the most vulnerable groups with protection services and through advocacy and social mobilization, the project will address stigma and discrimination for survivors of GBV and gender inequalities.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project seeks to address gender based inequalities and protection threats through addressing the drivers of gender based violence, particularly social norms, gender roles, violence, community conflicts, exclusion and deprivation. Violence will be addressed through community engagement and provision of services to survivors of GBV, child protection services and strengthening of community protection mechanisms.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project utilities protection assessments, safety audits and ongoing monitoring to assess inequalities and protection threats to children, women and their families.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Access to services will be measured through tracking the number of children provided with child protection and GBV services through the CPMIS and the GBVMIS and other monitoring processes. Additional community engagement activities will facilitate identification of access problems.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Through safety audits, the project identifies potential safety threats to beneficiaries and implements mitigation measures.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

The project monitoring process is participatory, age and gender specific, ensuring the dignity, confidentiality and protection of beneficiaries.
