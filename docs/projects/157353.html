Project Summary

Gender based Violence is very common among the IDPS and Refugees living in the four states where World Vision is implementing its activities. Women and girls have suffered rape often by multiple militia men. FGM among women and girls aged 15 to 49 years is rampant while early marriages has continued at an alarming rate. Specialized response services especially psychosocial support for GBV survivors are not readily available or non- existent in the affected states and localities. The community safety nets in the affected states are either weakened or over stretched and the social institutions cannot provide adequate protection services for those affected.
The proposed project will embark on raising awareness against harmful traditional practices to mitigate against rape for women and girls, FGM and early marriage. We shall carry out a mapping exercise to document existing referral pathways and communicate the same to the community so that those affected can easily seek services from those identified facilities. The project will train medical and para medical staffs on clinical management of rape as well as psychosocial support. Community protection support system shall be enhanced and supported t address the weakened community safety nets in the states and who will help reduce the incidences of sexual and physical violence. The project will target 39,500 Boys, Girls, Women and men from the two states of South Darfur and East Darfur.

Needs

Gender-Based Violence (GBV) is common among IDPs and refugees in Sudan. Throughout the lengthy conflict, the rape of women and young girls, often by multiple militiamen, has been reported at staggering rates. So too, the prevalence of female genital mutilation/cutting (FGM/C) was 87% among women aged 15-49 and 32% among girls aged 0-14 in 2014 in the country. Almost 38% of women were first married before age 18 (12% before age 15). There are also severe shortages of specialized response services (e.g. psychosocial support) for GBV survivors, which are unavailable in over 80% of affected localities. Girls and boys (who represent 60% of displaced people in camps), face numerous child protection risks including becoming separated from their families, lack of birth registration, recruitment by armed groups, sexual and physical violence, child labor, child exploitation, and risky coping mechanisms. The report published by the Secretary General on children and armed conflict (May 2018) outlined the following grave violations in Darfur: 24 documented cases of recruitment and use of children in armed groups, 94 verified incidents of killing and maiming children (116 boys, 30 girls), and 36 verified incidents of rape and other forms of sexual violence which affected 44 girls. Community safety nets in the country have been weakened and overstretched social institutions cannot provide adequate protection services for those affected (HNO, 2018).

Activities

- 15 Awareness raising sessions against harmful traditional practices such as Rape of women and young girls, Female genital mutilation/cutting (FGM/C) and Early marriage. - 5 Locality Mapping and documentation of referral pathways to address shortages of specialized response services (e.g. psychosocial support) for GBV survivors.- Formation of 5 community based protection mechanisms to address the weakened Community safety nets in the country.- 5 Trainings on clinical management of rape - 5 Trainings of GBV Capacity building for Staffs and Ministry of Social Welfare at the Locality level- Construction of 10 Girls and Women Centers and running of Psychosocial services at the centers
