Project Summary

SPEDP is proposing an integrated school feeding programme in schools in Renk, Melut, Fashoda and Manyo Counties with the general objectives of contributing to reduction in cognitive underdevelopment and malnutrition, improve attendance and enrollment, and reduce gender disparities. This project will target school going children between 6-18 years in primary schools within Renk, Melut , Fashoda and Manyo for three terms which will expand over a period of 12 months. It targets 16 schools with 10,000 (6500 M, 3500 F school going children, 160 teachers and 48 cooks/kitchen helpers. These 16 schools will be selected based on food security status, literacy statistics, enrollment and attendance data. SPEDP will give due consideration on environmental and economic aspects of the emergency to determine the most affected or most in need of assistance. It will develop programme plans to feed all children within these schools, regardless of their individual circumstances. SPEDP will ensure that protection risks and benefits are considered in emergence school feeding program, as part of all humanitarian assistance and protection. SPEDP promote a community-based protection approach, or meaningful engagement and ideally, leadership, of crisis-affected communities to identify protection risks, self-protection capacities, and joint prevention and mitigation and link to this protection actors seek to mainstream an age, gender and diversity approach to aim for the equal enjoyment of rights by all crisis-affected people, and to integrate this into accountability frameworks.

Needs

SPEDP intends to provide emergency school feeding programme to children in primary schools to improve on under development of cognitive, malnutrition, improve attendance and enrollment, and reduce gender disparities. According to the South Sudan IPC report September 2018 – March 2019, it is expected that 6.1 million people (59% of the total population) faced Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity at the peak of the lean season (July – August), of whom 47,000 were in Catastrophe (IPC phase 5) and 1.7 million were in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). Food security has improved slightly with the green harvest in September relative to July and August 2018, and further improvements are expected in the post-harvest period between October and December 2018. The number of people in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) is most likely to reduce to 4.4 million (43% of the total population), with 26,000 populations in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5), an anticipated earlier than normal start of the lean season will result in an estimated 5.2 million (49% of the total population) people in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity between January and March 2019. Likewise, 36,000 populations in Catastrophe levels of acute malnutrition are expected to deteriorate in the first quarter of 2019 with an early onset of the lean season as most households are expected to deplete food stocks from own production. This has a direct impact on education particularly on enrollment, attendance and performance. According to final draft Nutrition Cluster 2019 HNO caseload and target, 268 persons in Fashoda, 118 persons in Manyo, 636 persons in Melut and 1997 persons in Renk, which are under severe acute malnutrition. The level of acute malnutrition is attributed to severe food insecurity, poor access to health and nutrition services, high morbidity, extremely poor diets and poor sanitation and hygiene. According to the IPC report September 2018 - March 2019, the levels of acute malnutrition are expected to deteriorate in the first quarter of 2019 with an early onset of the lean season as most households are expected to deplete food stocks from own production. SPEDP conducted an education assessment in Manyo, Melut, Renk and Fashoda covering 30 schools from 24th- 28th September 2018. The aim of the assessment was to understand the education situation in Fashoda, Renk, Melut and Manyo Counties , especially related to the functionalities of the schools, the general challenges affecting the education system in the Counties. The findings of the assessments confirmed that 23% of the schools are not functional due to displacement, starvation and hunger at the households’ level, making it difficult for the school children to attend classes regularly among others. These Counties are faced with a myriad of challenges ranging from limited teachers, little and irregular payments, limited scholastic materials, hunger, etc. These needs are greatly affecting the education performance in these Counties and have prevented school going children from attending classes. Therefore, it is of greatest concern are Counties such as Fashoda, Manyo, Melut and Renk where harvests are likely to be poor; access to humanitarian assistance may be limited. Possible returns of internally displaced persons (IDPs) from within and outside of the Country may cause further pressure on already scarce resources. Furthermore, insecurity despite of the recently signed revitalized peace agreement, lack of freedom of movement and extreme depletion of livelihoods prevails. As a result, extreme levels of acute food insecurity are expected to persist in these areas. This calls for immediate and unhindered provision of humanitarian assistance and close monitoring. Hence, this project is designed to reduce on the above factors so as to "increase enrollment rates, reducing absenteeism, and improving food security at the household level".

Activities

This project will target school going children between 6-18 years in primary schools within Fashoda and Manyo for a period of three terms, planned to reach 16 schools with 10,000 (65000 M,3500 F) school going children, 160 (115M, 45F) teachers and 48 (16 M, 32F) cooks/kitchen helpers. These 16 schools will be selected jointly with the county education officials and other partners operating at the ground to avoid duplication and this will be based on food security status, literacy statistics, enrollment and attendance data. Food procurement will be done through tendering and preference will be given to the local suppliers. The quantities required for each selected school will be determined based on enrollment figures (monthly/as school ratios are provided.) School heads/PTAs will receive and manage the food supplies in close coordination with other relevant stakeholders. Community participation integration approach (CPIA) will be applied to ensure inclusive participation. SPEDP will undertake following key interventions: - provide ready-made food (high energy biscuits) to selected schools for one to two weeks as an immediate response before onsite schools feeding programme kicks off -onsite school feeding will follow after set-up of the food preparation facilities. Children aged 6 to 18 years require 2,000 kcal/person. For general populations, the daily energy requirement is 2,100 kcal. Based on this calculation, the food commodities that will be provided at each school will include; Pulses (yellow split peas), Cereals (Sorghum flour), Oil (Vegetable) and Salt. The same ration size of Pulses (30grams), Cereals (150grams), Oil (20grams) and Salt (5grams) totaling to 205 grams ( = 803 kcal or 38% of daily kcal requirements) applies for pupils of all ages in targeted primary schools and for non-pupils (teachers, cooks, kitchen helpers, etc.) on a daily basis for 5 schooling days a week. The selection of food commodities and nutritional requirements puts into consideration of the cultural food preferences and tastes of the school children. There will be minor rehabilitation of schools stores, cooking stove and kitchens. Food aid will be distributed to schools on a monthly basis from SPEDP warehouse located in Renk main office for Manyo, Renk and Melut Counties. A teaching staff will be selected per school to serve as a store keeper and trained in store keeping including record taking and reporting. Whereas 3(2 F, 1M) cooks per school will be hired and trained in hygienic food preparation prior to initiation of onsite cooking, 2 cooks will be hired for food preparation while the other 1 will be hired for fetching water and carrying out other kitchen duties like washing of plates. The PTA/SMCs will be trained on school monitoring, management and hygiene. SPEDP together with education officials will be carrying out joint monitoring to assess the impact of school feeding programme and determine to what extent it has increased enrollment, retention and improved cognitive development of learners. SPEDP will work in close collaboration with education sector partners to establish synergies among the similar initiatives and avoidance of duplication and overlapping. SPEDP will periodically report on FTS and 5Ws. SPEDP will hire a security guard per school to provide security to the children and food items within the school. It will ensure that protection risks and benefits are considered in emergence school feeding programme as part of all humanitarian assistance and protection. SPEDP will promote a community-based protection approach, or meaningful engagement and ideally, leadership, of crisis-affected communities to identify protection risks, self-protection capacities, and joint prevention and mitigation strategies. Hygiene materials such as hand washing facilities and soaps will be distributed to the schools. Hand washing facilities will be placed near the serving place and every child will be asked to wash hands before meals.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

A community committee, made up of diverse representatives such as student representative, PTA/SMCs, Teacher and a project staff will be formed to record and handle complaints and feedback especially regarding the quality of food prepared, hygiene of the cooks and in the kitchen. The committee will be briefed on their roles, how to keep confidentiality. Regular meeting with staff and student representative shall be conducted to review record, and deciding action needed. Making immediate changes to address the complaint will be taken. For example if a complaint was raised about poor disposal of food left over, immediately digging rubbish pit will be done.
