Project Summary

A new national peace accord does offer hope but conflict & displacement is still anticipated due to the fragmentation of military factions vying for political power & persistent localized intra & inter communal level conflict. With conflict, the disruption to livelihoods, poor economic conditions & challenges faced in the delivery of humanitarian assistance has resulted in 59% of the population 935,000 people being severely food insecure during the peak of the lean season (June – July) in 2019 in Unity State. Conflict is still one of the main driver of food insecurity, inflation at 75%, an economy continuing to contract, a population now 82% ‘officially poor ’ & major challenges expected with the arrival of significant returnees, leaving increasing numbers vulnerable to the impact of multiple shocks.
In addition, the economic crisis persists, in an existing context of sub optimal market integration, affecting especially urban based & other market dependent persons (vulnerable food deficit farming households reliant on day labour).The effect is most severe where there is convergence of these multiple shocks that has resulted in very high levels of chronic poverty, vulnerability & acute malnutrition/ food insecurity with populations resorting to the use of negative coping strategies as their livelihoods are disrupted, kinship support mechanisms over stretched & remaining assets depleted. The spread of the conflict which started in July 2016 from Juba city into predominantly agricultural areas in the Greenbelt zone within the peri-urban & beyond has had a particularly detrimental impact on food security in the zones & into the urban centres resulting into a cereal deficit 482,000MT whose effect is mostly felt in urban centres such as Juba. Recent assessments conducted by WFP, FAO & other partners show that 76% of the urban population in Juba is food insecure & 36% of the households live on less than a dollar per day & therefore have limited purchasing power.
While assessing several data such as the most recent national Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) data (South Sudan IPC August 2019, IPC_ Oct-Dec 2019 & projections for IPC_ Jan-March 2020, food insecurity is expected to reach critical levels particularly in Mayendit which is already in crisis phase. Current IPC data indicates Mayendit County as having 40,000 & 21,000 individuals in stressed levels, in 32,000 & 27,000 in crisis levels & 16,000 & 6,000 in emergency phase 4 while the situation is expected to get worse in January to March with a total of 95,000 & 57,000 individuals being in stress, crisis & emergency phases respectively. Moreover, the nutrition data from the most recent SMART surveys conducted reported global acute malnutrition (GAM) prevalence by Weight for Height (WHZ) of 10.1% in Juba classified as serious.
A combination of factors has conspired to consign food & nutrition security to the precarious levels seen in the county in 2018. These factors include the localized conflict between cattle herders & the local population, which has led to loss of lives, property & displacement of people, the current severe economic crisis coupled with the skyrocketed prices of essential foodstuffs has eroded the purchasing power of vulnerable households, the destruction of crops by floods in the lowl& areas & drought in higher areas where people cultivated crops.According to Pictorial Evaluation Tools (PET) crop yield assessment conducted in March 2019 which revealed that the average yield per HH was at 270 kg/HH which is far below the national threshold of 800kg/HH & is also corroborated by the FSNMS round 23,2019 report which reveals that many households in Unity state are resorting to dangerous coping mechanisms including skipping meals, reducing meal portions, sparing food for children only, consuming wild food plants & even productive asset stripping, thus registering very poor food consumption scores.

1 - Needs

There is evidence from recent assessments conducted by WFP, FAO and other partners that show 62.3% of targeted population is food insecure and 37.8% of the households live on less than one meal per day (IPC messages Aug 2019) and therefore are suffering from high malnutrition. UNIDOR‘s proposed intervention will contribute to the clusters stated objectives of changing mindsets of beneficiaries on the blanket food distribution and further reducing dependency on humanitarian assistance. Currently, UNIDOR has a field presence in Both the selected Counties and is responsible for the delivery of WFP&UNICEF supported TSFP interventions in the targeted location, running OTP and TSFP centers. UNIDOR will leverage shared organizational support functions across the projects to deliver the much needed value for money.Over the last one year alone, UNIDOR has successfully implement the FSL, OTP and TSFP projects in target locations. With this proposed intervention, UNIDOR will link their TSFP/OTP intervention with the FSL intervention, creating a referral system that will help stabilize households that were having SAM & MAM cases by targeting them under the FSL intervention. This kind of linkage with reduce consumption nutrition food meant for TSFP/OTP and avoiding relapse cases. UNIDOR has a large social capital in the area with cordial relationships with the local authorities, opinion leaders, and the organization also enjoys large acceptance in the area having run several projects with high community participation in the same county.

2 - Activities

UNIDOR will apply the following considerations during selecting of FSL activities: (1) The period it takes for the activity to impact the target beneficiaries (e.g. the impact of planting vegetables is felt very soon after the activity)(2) How direct is the benefit of the activity is on the target project participant.Initially the FSL implementation is focused on activities that have the most direct benefit for beneficiaries and where the time between engaging in the activity and receiving benefit is short. Over time and with greater community engagement in FSL activities can shift to those activities that have less direct benefits and take longer for the benefit to be realized. This shift comes as a result of community engagement and is fundamental to successful transition.  Activities will be selected by and planned with the target groups themselves, as much as possible. Activities will not be a disincentive to local agricultural production nor will they undermine long-term development projects. At least 70 percent of the participants in FSL activities (at country level) will be women and adolescent girls, i.e. for out of school education, vegetable farming skills training, dyke construction skills training, water pond construction skills and micro enterprise establishment and community leadership training. If less than 70 percent female participation is planned for a given project, an explanation will be provided.  FSL activities will be designed in a manner that facilitates the participation of women and adolescent girls (beyond age 18) as workers and ensures that they will not be overburdened and working for 4 to five days a week (County Level). This is activities are designed to assist in changing mindset of the people on food and reduce dependency on humanitarian. It will be achieved through the followings: 1. Hold stakeholder meetings with Government counter-parts/community leaders and partners to discuss on project objectives, criteria for beneficiary selection and gain community -buy in- into the project.2. Awareness and sensitization creation campaigns through caravan.3. Establish project management committees(PMC’s)at the project level- ensuring a good mix of male and female representation4. Trainings of PMC and/or targeted farmers/households • Trainings on CBPP • Trainings on food security best practices• Trainings on personal hygiene for beneficiaries at individual level (men/women) at home.Training on waste management awareness and disposal techniques 5. distribution  Distribution of tools, seeds, cash, crops and vegetables Conducting post Distribution monitoring  Reporting
