1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

According to CPIMS+ data from January to September 2024, child protection concerns in the camps remain significant, with issues such as neglect, psychological distress, physical abuse, child labor, and disabilities affecting large numbers of children. Neglect alone affecting 31.86% of children (15.92% girls, 15.94% boys), psychological distress affecting 11.57% (6.20% girls, 5.36% boys), physical abuse affecting 10.48% (5.22% girls, 5.26% boys), child labor affecting 7.58% (0.34% girls, 7.24% boys), and 5.49% of children having disabilities or impairments (2.15% girls, 3.35% boys). The Inter-Sectoral Needs Assessment (ISNA) report indicates that 15.52% of households lack access to child protection services. Obstacles such as distance to service centers (27%), fear of abduction (26%), lack of awareness of available services (22%), and financial constraints (5%). Socio-cultural issues such inbuilt prejudice of FDMN community issues also create obstacles.The Joint Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (JMSNA) highlights critical child protection needs across several areas. Community-based child protection program participation shows that while 66.5% of children engage in community activities, 18.3% may lack access to safe spaces with adult supervision, increasing their vulnerability. Concerning separated children, 96.9% of households report at least one child under 18 living outside the household, primarily due to economic migration (43.4%) and education-related reasons (32.1%).Livelihood coping strategies significantly impact children, with 66.4% of households withdrawing children from school due to financial challenges. Additionally, 75.4% of households resort to marrying off children under 18 as a financial burden-reduction measure, while 66.9% of children are engaged in labor to contribute to household income. Psychosocial distress among children is another pressing concern, with signs of distress reported in 61.5% of households. Girls aged 3–17 years exhibit slightly higher rates of distress compared to boys.Barriers to education and early childhood development (ECD) are evident, as only 54.3% of children aged 3–4 participate in ECD programs, and the school enrollment rate for children aged 5–18 is 65.7%, leaving significant gaps in educational coverage. Access to healthcare and safety also poses challenges, with 32.3% of households facing barriers such as transportation issues, costs, and safety concerns. Furthermore, 30% of households expressed a need for information about child-focused humanitarian support to better address these challenges.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project aims to comprehensively integrate protection mainstreaming principles to enhance safety, dignity, and access for children, adolescents, and caregivers in the Rohingya camps and surrounding host communities. By embedding protection mainstreaming within all project activities, the initiative ensures that child protection services are accessible, inclusive, and responsive to the needs of vulnerable groups, including girls, boys, adolescents, and children with disabilities. The project will apply the Protection Mainstreaming Framework’s eight core components as outlined in the CPSS guidelines to create a systematic approach to reducing harm and building resilience. This mainstreaming approach will focus on ensuring dignity, safety, and non-discrimination in all services, from structured psychosocial support to Early Childhood Development (ECD) interventions, and recreational activities for children.

2.3 - Age & Disability

Educo has a commitment to age and disability inclusion, ensuring that individuals across various age groups and abilities have equitable access to services and opportunities for empowerment. Interventions are carefully tailored to meet the specific needs of each group: children aged 3–5 years benefit from Early Childhood Development (ECD) services, adolescents aged 11–18 years who lack access to child protection services are prioritized for activities supporting their protection and development such as PSS support, Case management through referral, A&Y club, Adolescents and youth led campaign, and youth aged 16–24 years are offered life skills sessions and vocational training in areas such as sewing, handicrafts, and solar repair to promote resilience and self-reliance. Adults, and elderly including parents and caregivers, are actively involved in community mobilization to strengthen social cohesion and child protection. The project ensures gender equity with equal male and female participation across all activities, while Community-Based Child Protection Committees (CBCPCs) follow guidelines that encourage involvement from children and adults alike. To support disability inclusion, at least 3% of project beneficiaries will be persons with disabilities, in line with the Joint Response Plan (JRP), with an approximate 12% representation across all activities. Infrastructure, including Multi-Purpose Centers (MPCs) and ECD centers, is designed to be accessible and disability-friendly, while staff receive training on disability rights to facilitate inclusive practices. Educo’s collaboration with NGOs, UN agencies, and program partners further reinforces these inclusive efforts, fostering an environment where all individuals are empowered to fully engage in community life and project activities.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project’s monitoring approach emphasizes both learning and accountability, tailored to the complex operational environment. Throughout implementation, Educo-Bangladesh and project staff will actively engage the target population, conducting formal and informal consultations and data collection with adolescent girls and boys, women, and men. This will be done using a gender-integrated, child-friendly approach, with a strong emphasis on safeguarding.An Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) plan will be developed, outlining expected results, indicators, and activities in line with the project’s Performance Measurement Framework (PMF) align with AAP. The Educo MEAL team, supported by Educo HQ, will lead the design, oversight, and management of the AAP system, collaborating closely with stakeholders. Tools and procedures will be created to ensure timely, accurate, and consolidated data collection, quality control, analysis, and use in alignment with project objectives. All project staff will receive training to effectively use AAP tools and understand their roles within the system.To enhance accountability, a monthly progress tracking system will be implemented. A structured feedback mechanism—including a complaint box, help desk, and quarterly satisfaction surveys and focus group discussions (FGDs)—will allow for continuous community input, managed by both MEAL units. Monthly MEAL team visits will capture project progress, community feedback, and insights for best practices.A dedicated space will enable participants to express opinions on the project’s activities and impact, allowing Educo to make iterative improvements. The national Suggestions, Complaints, and Compliments (SCC) committee at the country office will introduce a formal SCC mechanism to receive and respond to feedback consistently.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

To address Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) as a crosscutting issue, the project will integrate robust safeguarding measures at all levels of implementation. PSEA principles will be embedded in project activities, ensuring the safety, dignity, and rights of all participants, particularly vulnerable groups such as women, girls, and persons with disabilities.Educo and its partners will implement a clear PSEA policy and code of conduct, requiring all staff, volunteers, and contractors to complete mandatory training on PSEA standards, reporting mechanisms, and behavioral expectations. The project will establish confidential reporting channels, including complaint boxes, help desks, and a hotline, accessible to all participants in their preferred languages. A dedicated PSEA focal point will monitor and address any reported incidents swiftly and transparently, ensuring accountability and confidentiality.Community sensitization sessions will be conducted to raise awareness about participants' rights and the reporting mechanisms available to them. Educo will also coordinate with relevant UN agencies, NGOs, and community groups to align PSEA efforts and promote a unified approach to preventing and addressing sexual exploitation and abuse in both refugee camps and host communities.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Educo and its partners are committed to protecting children from disasters and engaging in activities that mainstream Climate Change Adaptation (CCA) and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR). To mitigate the risks of landslides, cyclones, and flash-floods, especially in areas where FDMN cannot evacuate to safe zones, concrete and innovative protective and preparedness interventions are being implemented. This project integrated the seasonal contingency planning for its implementing tenure, which is essential to mitigate risks associated with monsoons, flash-floods and cyclones, ensuring a rapid response. This includes establishing stand-by and roster systems for staff during weekends, holidays, and critical periods, ensuring preparedness during monsoon and cyclone seasons. Multi-purpose centers (MPCs) serve as child-friendly spaces and emergency response shelter for supporting children and their caregivers needs and collaborating with like-minded service providers and the Child Protection Sub-Sector to provide comprehensive support in post-disaster scenarios. Tree plantation, solid waste collection campaign etc. increase sense of responsibility of the children and adolescents about environmental degradation and help to natural restoration. Tree plantation activity will be done in coordination with EEN and ensure proper care and maintenance. As well as CFS/MPC established nature based design and child friendly way and maintain benefiting children mental health. Overall, this project incorporates climate change adaptation and mitigation by addressing DRR, DRM, anticipatory actions, and environmental issues.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

The project has taken Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) measures to enhance resilience and ensure effective response during emergencies. The project allocated contingency funds are integrated into the project’s financial structure, enabling immediate action for urgent needs arising from emergencies such as cyclones, flash floods, or fires. This funding provides the flexibility required to respond promptly to sudden protection needs resulting from crises, whether they are natural disasters or significant social disruptions. The project also emphasizes community capacity building by equipping community-based child protection structures with the necessary skills and resources for effective emergency preparedness and response. Training for child protection actors, community volunteers, and caregivers on disaster risk reduction (DRR) and response readiness is a core component, complemented by awareness-raising sessions focused on building protective capacities within the community to anticipate and manage risks effectively. By embedding these EPR measures, the project aims to enhance the overall resilience of Rohingya refugee communities and their caregivers, ensuring they are better prepared to respond to emergencies. In this project Educo maintaining harmonization and alignment with ISCG and other EPR WG members. If any emergency situation arise Educo targeted 3-5% of children and their families to be served on the basis of critical need. And reserve 3-5% of funds for EPR. Educo ensure no disaster contributes to more distress for kids. Also include child-friendly early warning alerts and advisories, to ensuring child-friendly emergency relocation plans, and emergency center protocols and guidelines.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Youth engagement is a central aspect of this project, aimed at strengthening child protection and building resilience within Rohingya refugee camps and host communities. For adolescents under 18, structured psychosocial support sessions are provided through individual or group activities that emphasize coping strategies, stress management, and emotional resilience, fostering a supportive community environment. Additionally, adolescents participate in life skills training sessions focused on leadership, friendship, communication, and decision-making, which enhance their social and cognitive abilities. They are also actively engaged in courtyard sessions where they discuss and learn about critical topics such as child protection, gender equality, and violence prevention. Beyond participation, adolescents are empowered to lead and organize community campaigns, contribute their perspectives in Community-Based Child Protection Committee (CBCPC) meetings, and take part in advocacy sessions, equipping them to promote safer and more inclusive environments. By engaging in monitoring processes through feedback and consultations, their insights shape project adaptations, ensuring that interventions remain youth-centered, gender-sensitive, and culturally appropriate.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

The project aims to establish and strengthen community-based child protection mechanisms, community members are actively engaged in preventing and responding to violence, fostering a safer environment. The project includes MPC-based life skill sessions for children and adolescents and positive parenting training for parents and caregivers, which increase the self-reliance and community resilience among children, adolescents and their caregivers by increasing their knowledge of protection concerns. Additionally, project includes vocational training and family livelihood support to reduce economic pressures, which often lead to child labor and increase girl’s child marriages, and enable families to prioritize education and protection. Children led awareness campaigns and community dialogues empower youth to advocate for their rights and enhance community resilience. Additionally, mental health and psychosocial support services help children cope with stress, while inclusive services ensure that children with disabilities have equal access, further promoting resilience and self-reliance across diverse needs.
