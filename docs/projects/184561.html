Project Summary

According 2022 HNO, an estimated 2.7 million are in need of protection under HLP rights. The key factor which causes land tenure issues and forced eviction is displacement that is higher as well as landowners who are demanding their land across Somalia with severity score 3, majority in Banadir, Bay, Lower Shabelle regions, further by the end of August 2021, 92,042 persons had been evicted compared to June with a total of 18,278 people evicted. In addition, IDPs in Banadir who experienced multiple forced evictions with 77,487 cases face the constant of secondary displacement as a result of forced evictions. The key driver of forced eviction is informal tenancy agreement causing a precarious situation for thousands of IDPs who are at eviction risk in terms of land tenure insecurity. Importantly, HLP AoR stated the people in need do not have equal or equitable access to basic and social services and face numerous challenges accessing humanitarian assistance. IDPs are more vulnerable to protection threats both from the incident that triggered displacement in their place of origin and the lack of access to basic services in their current location.
Out of the total number of people in need including non-IDPs and IDPs, 63% are children, 17% are women and girls, 17% are men, 4% are older people and 15% are persons living with disabilities. In addition to the total PiN, 1.1 million (41%) are in IDP cams and settlements. The most people affected by the HLP issues include children, women and girls, PWD, minority clans and older persons specifically women and girls face difficult challenges due to inequality and social norms which exclude them from opportunities and decisions-making platforms. The most common problem faced by people in need is ownership issues/disputes in terms HLP and vast majority of households do not have land tenure documents to prove their occupancy arrangement and yet, displacement is in itself a traumatic event for households from both a material “loss of land and goods” and a psychological perspective “isolation and separation from family” (2022 HNO).
As a result, there is a need in protecting HLP rights of IDPs, advocacy strategies, institutional arrangements through addressing land conflict and related disputes, and protection of them from HLP violations such as land grabbing, encroachment and forced evictions to promote and increase access to land for people in need.
Therefore, the proposed project is designed to equally enhance protection and access to HLP rights for displaced populations of 148,160 individuals including women 23,706, men 23,706, boys 47,411 and girls 47,411and Elder people of 5,926 including minority as well as persons with disabilities of 22,224 in Banadir, Lower Shabelle, and Bay regions in Somalia through HLP prevention and services, protection and evictions monitoring and analysis.

1. - Projects activities

1. Provision of information services to increase knowledge and the ability of the project communities in the target locations who are very in need of understanding of legal framework and property rights, HINNA will conduct advocacy and pre-eviction campaigns on HLP issues through information sharing with individuals at high risk IDP sites. During this activity, HINNA will provide them with information on land rights; risk of eviction, policies and procedures that government and other relevant institutions to maintain, land dispute resolutions and the roles of landowners as all these will promote enjoyment and exercise of HLP rights. 2. Provision of specialized counseling and legal assistance services for HLP cases identified through fixed centers and mobile clinics. Access to legal aid services will be unrestricted due to the nature of the service. Through this activity, HINNA will use cash for legal aid services.3. As most of the time eviction occur without an adequate notice period and causes destruction of assets, HINNA will provide HLP Prevention and Response Services through negotiations with the presence of local authorities with landowners, gatekeepers, consultation on alternatives, non-discrimination and negotiation of extended notice.4. As tenure security and documentation is to maintain in order to prevent evictions from the target IDP populations, HINNA will establish a coordination and collaboration linkage to government institutions relevant to issuing tenure security documents to enhance protection and access to HLP rights against forced evictions. Through this activity, HINNA will use cash supports for tenure security and documentation and the evictees to address their basic needs including relocation and post-eviction complications. 5. HINNA HLP team, in close coordination and collaboration with HLP cluster and partners, will conduct periodic assessment, monitoring, documentation and reporting threats of eviction and forced evictions that will cover land tenure use, the status of tenure agreements, current ownership, tenure rights of IDP communities, legal documentation and tenure rights.

The full participation of these people in the project is crucial to the achievement of objectives, the project will give more priority to the equality of gender, and "Do No Harm principleswill be adopted among project stakeholders. 4.5 The project will support vulnerable IDP communities who are mostly living in both public and private lands. The risk of dispute on landowner and land tenure may rise, but the two-side agreement with a specified period of time at least 3 years between landowners and established IDP community committee or representatives from IDP sites in support will be developed in the presence of district commissioners/local authorities to ensure the security of land tenure.
