Project Summary

The project meets strategic and cluster objectives:
Strategic objective 02: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of essential services, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL.
HEALTH AND NUTRITION CLUSTER OBJECTIVE 1: Ensure the availability of acceptable and quality essential healthcare services to vulnerable communities in oPt.
The project’s main two objectives are:
1- Saving neonatal lives by ensuring the availability of life saving items and essential infection control supplies at the neonatal units. MAP aims to achieve the project’s objectives in coordination with the Ministry of Health, UNICEF, WHO, QRCS and other stakeholders.
2- Improving the skills and competencies of staff at the neonatal units on neonatal life support, neonatal safe transport, and infection control.
In general, this project will contribute to addressing the lifesaving needs for high risk babies at neonatal units in the Gaza and improving neonatal care management in order to contribute to reducing mortality, morbidity and long-term disability among newborns.

1 - Needs

The neonates are one of the most vulnerable groups in Gaza, the poor socio-economic status has negative impact on the whole population but specifically on pregnant and consequently on the new borns. While the HNO for 2020 indicated that there were 10,000 neonatal babies at risk; the HNO for 2021 indicates that there are 15,000 neonatal babies at risk (10,000 born by CS and preterm and another 5,000 born with low birth weight). The situation is exacerbated by the ongoing usual and protracted shortages of staff, drugs, disposables, salaries, infection control supplies, skills, etc. However, March 2020 was adding another global disaster to the toll of Gaza, COVID-19. Of the effects of this pandemic on the neonatal service in specific • The redirecting of funds towards COVID-19 activities and response which in turn jeopardizes other vulnerable groups. For example, one of our Key Informative Interview KII conducted with a key official at MoH indicated that MAP was the only INGO supporting the MoH with the “surfactant”; a medicine that used for the serious respiratory issues of premature and extremely premature babies.• Relocating some neonatal services from one location to another, such as the neonatal unit at EGH to Naser, that lead to overcrowdings of the unit• To cope with the pandemic, MoH needed to divert significant resources, including the manpower (doctors, nurses and midwives or infection control team leaders) from regular service delivery to response efforts. E.g. infection control teams transferred to work at quarantine centers• Other staffing shortages due to COVID-19 itself and the MoH’s decision to exempt staff over 50 of age, pregnant women and people with NCDs not to report to work for their safety. In a rapid assessment that MAP conducted, we found out that at least 13 staff have been ordered to stay at home due to the aforementioned reasons which in turn affected the shortages in staffing negatively• MAP also found that at one specific NICU, there were 3 doctors and 1 head nurse who had COVID-19 which in turn made MoH shut down the unit for a period of time and relocated babies at the unit to another major unit in the governorate• Amid COVID-19 pandemic cost of infection prevention and infection control including sanitizes, personal protective equipment raised up due to huge demand worldwideAll the above are reasons why the neonates had been vulnerable during the protracted emergency that Gaza lives in but especially since the discovery of COVID-19. To focus more on the drugs and disposables issue, in September there was a shortage of 47% in drugs in general and 55% shortage in MCH drugs in specific. For disposables, it was not better, overall, there are shortages of about 30% and 21% in ICU disposables specifically. To add, MAP’s own evidence from the neonatal monthly monitoring data shows that neonatal case fatality in 2020 so far stands at 12% compared to 10% in 2019, and that average cases of sepsis per month is 54 cases of the admissions and that incidence of neonatal sepsis deaths from total deaths stands at 17% so far in 2020, some months this rate even reached a staggering 24%. All in which indicates the importance to continue supporting the infection control practices at the NICUs.Another major issues that needs addressing is the skills of staff at the NICUs. With the regular turn-over of staffing and rotational policy that MoH has been implementing to fill in the huge gaps of HR; neonatal units are no exception. That is why it is important to conduct neonatal life support NLS and neonatal safe transfer NST trainings and on-job trainings to raise the skills of staff at these units, also include the new staff at the unit and refresh the knowledge of old staff at the unit too. The trainings will be conducted through local instructors with support from medical missions (physically or remotely). More info under activities section.As for RoP, while MAP introduced the screening for such condition back in 2016; the treatment is still a missing factor in Gaza, but MAP will address this issue in 2021.

2 - Activities

Procurement of life saving neonatal drugs and consumables to be used for 15,000 at high risk newborn babies/year including female and male babies admitted to neonatal ICU to benefit from the neonatal care project’s interventions. This will happen in coordination with MoH, UNICEF, WHO and all related parties Procurement of infection control supplies and printing educational materials (infection control booklets) to be used for 15,000 high risk newborn babies/year including female and male babies admitted to NICU and who will benefit from the neonatal care project’s interventions. This will happen coordination with MoH, UNFPA, WHO and UNICEF. Procure necessary equipment that is urgently needed to address the over-crowding in NICUs. This will happen in coordination with MoH and NGOs, Implement three Neonatal Life Support Trainings NLS and three Infection Control training courses for neonatal and obstetric department for 200 health professionals. 120 female and 80 male health professionals working at the neonatal and obstetric units of MoH will be targeted. These training will be coordinated with MOH different departments. Implement on-job NLS training courses and procure the needed equipment for these courses. These trainings will be also coordinated with the above-mentioned parties. Please note that the usual way that MAP conducted these trainings was through medical mission, however, if the situation in 2021 does not allow for missions to enter to Gaza then MAP will utilize the local cadre of trainers we have been working with (13 in total) to conduct these training courses locally with remote support from our UK consultant. This was implemented by MAP in 2020 and was successful. Plus, all the emergency services that MAP will support will contribute to the reduction of long term disabilities in neonates as these babies are endangered and subject to death or develop long-term disabilities. With regards to gender, the project will target male and female neonates but for the training, more emphasis will be given to female health practitioners as they form the majority of the workers at NICUs, plus, the local trainers’ cadre that MAP has invested in, who help the consultant in provision of all course, are a mixture of both genders to address cultural sensitivities. As for partnership with local NGOs, while we do not have direct links under the neonatal project with any community partner; MAP has other partners involved in its MCH projects focusing on women at reproductive age, MAP is working with these women to support prenatal and antenatal care which have direct impact on the neonatal morbidity and mortality. Please note that the focus of this project is about provision of life-saving items and equipment to the NICUs that are solely by MoH. There is only one neonatal unit in the NGO community which is considered as an intermediate unit. The babies received at this NGO unit, if extra care is needed, they will be transferred to one of the MoH NICUs eventually.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

MAP includes the feedback of beneficiaries as a routine part of our project monitoring and we continually learn from this feedback to improve project design and our effectiveness. For this project in specific the below tools will be used for community engagement: Collection of testimonies from the parents of neonatal children will happen bi-monthly to follow-up on satisfaction of services provided and if MAP can provide any feedback to NICUs and GNN about treatment of babies and parents at the units.Monthly RoP and Sepsis data is collected and this is shared with the stakeholders such as MoH, GNN, UNICEF, WHO, and QRCS to feedback and try and figure if there are any gaps that needs addressing. For example, Sepsis data can indicate if a specific unit needs extra care related to IPC and staff training. This data can also be used to decide on action plans for next year phases of the project.Hand Hygiene compliance monthly data helps MAP and other stakeholders determine the abidance of hygiene in the units and this is extremely helpful right now amid the COVID-19 crisis. A full M&E plan will be created with the custom and cluster specific indicators which will enable MAP to keep a close eye on the project. Management decision will be adapted based on the feedback received from the beneficiaries in an evidence-based manner to implement any necessary changes as deemed necessary.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

MAP has developed an organisational algorithm for responding to Safeguarding concerns and region-specific algorithms for the initial investigation of such concerns. These documents were developed in conjunction with safeguarding experts and are considered fit for purpose and consistent with current sector guidance and the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS).They contain clear lines of responsibility and reporting and, where appropriate, confirm the timelines to be followed when responding to safeguarding concerns. All these documents are reviewed at least every two years. An incident report template is used to report any suspected breaches in MAP’s Code of Conduct or Safeguarding policy to MAP’s Safeguarding focal point.In addition, MAP applies very strict measures ensuring the privacy of the patients’ data by keeping it anonyms or not identified, rather unique codes that are known only to MAP and the relevant stakeholders to protect the privacy of data. Please note that all MAP’s staff have been trained on safeguarding and are fully aware of the chain of complaints and how these are treated inside MAP. For the neonatal project, MAP will take it into consideration that during all its training activities (NLS, OJT, infection control, workshops) we will address the issue of safeguarding and how serious it is to inform MAP of any breaches to such policies at the NICUs and we will educate the attendees briefly on PSEA.As for beneficiaries’ stories and data, no data is taken from parents unless they provide full written consent for the baby and parents can even ask MAP to alter names and not to publish photos.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

MAP is neutral and does not lean toward any group over the other in the context we live in.MAP is impartial, and we provide aid to those who need it regardless of any other consideration such as gender, age, religion, etc.MAP is an independent organisation and is not affected by commercial powers.MAP is humanitarian and provides aid to those vulnerable and suffering all the time.In this particular project, please note that the neonatal babies are all served at MoH facilities that are accessible to all population groups regardless of anything, even insurance status. Neonatal babies are above all other considerations.
