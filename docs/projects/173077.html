Project Summary

The project will provide timely shelter solutions and NFI assistance to people affected by crisis to reduce mortality and morbidity, mitigate health threats, and improving living conditions. The project is locations are directly linked to in-country clashes in the past as well as re-occurring natural disasters like flooding in specific states. The project will operate in West and South Kordofan, in West Darfur as well as in Gedaref and Kassala State.
In West and South Kordofan States the project will support vulnerable households who were affected by climate driven disasters including flooding with 2000 NFI kits and 1000 durable shelters and 500 in-kind partial kits for shelter rehabilitation/upgrade.
In West Darfur the project will support conflict affected communities with 2000 NFI kits and 750 durable shelters and 500 in-kind partial kits for shelter rehabilitation/upgrade.
In Gedaref and Kassala State the project will support vulnerable communities from the host community who are in need of shelter and NFI kits to mitigate the impact related to climate change and the hunger gap with 500 full NFI kits, 250 durable shelter and in-kind partial kits for shelter rehabilitation/upgrade distributed.
The projects plans to provide environmentally friendly, durable and locally sourced shelter solutions and will work closely with key government entities. Trainings will be provided selected community members who support beneficiaries to ensure safe and adequate construction and maintenances of the shelters following the standard cluster design. The development of the budget of the intervention is based on the unit value of $ 700 USD for durable shelters, $ 300 USD for partial kits for shelter rehabilitation/ upgrade and $ 117 USD for Full NFI kits. The training will take place over at least three days and is budgeted for $10 / day and person but may have to be reviewed based on the rising cost of living.

1 - Needs

Re-occurring man-made disasters like tribal clashes in West Darfur and natural disasters like flooding frequently displace people who are in need of timely shelter solutions and NFI assistance to reduce mortality, morbidity, mitigate threats, and improve the living conditions. In addition to that, Sudan is facing multiple other challenges, including over 200% annual inflation, currency depreciation and fuel shortages that is compounded by lockdown restrictions due to COVID-19. All these factors impact the need of support to provide multi-sectoral life-saving assistance to crisis affected people. In West-, and South Kordofan climate driven disasters e.g. flooding is a frequent phenomenon in the region that requires addressing basic needs including shelter and NFI support. Natural disasters are frequently hiding hard the most vulnerable groupings including women and the elderly who are in need secure and safe shelter. In West Darfur re-occurring tribal clashes frequently force cause displacement. The project will support vulnerable IDPs in need of shelter and NFI items. The growing tensions in the Tigray region can cause large displacement and movements across the border into Gedaref and Kassala State. The new refugee arrival can heavily impact the living situation for the host community in terms of food security, hunger or other economic hardships. Tensions may arise which will produce internal displacement. The provision of shelter and NFI material will help vulnerable groupings to restore their dignity and reduce exposure to protection and health risk. Among all vulnerable populations, children, the elderly and people with disabilities are highly vulnerable without shelter to protect them and expose them to further risks. The project targets 55% women and 45% man. To ensure the rights of these particular groups are ensured, protection activities will be mainstreamed across the intervention. The provision of durable shelter NFI kits can support stabilizing the situation and prevent people from moving further. Cultural and environmental friendly solutions will be included which will bring more stability.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project will not have direct significant impact on the environment. However, it is acknowledged that shelter provision and NFI distribution that the project seeks to support sometimes has negative impacts on the environment. For instance, in some of the localities where the project will be implemented, environmental degradation is a major concern. The assessment, planning and implementation of shelter and NFI activities will consider the impact on the environment and will aim to mitigate any negative impact ensuring synergy with afforestation activities and selecting durable shelter/NFI materials. The shelter materials are locally available, are easy to use for self construction and will absorb twelves through time and will not have an environmental impact. During the project design and implementation climate change adaptation approaches will be considered.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection mainstreaming is core to the programme. Avoiding causing harm will be ensured as SNFI will be distributed only in safe and secure areas. Ensuring access to impartial assistance will be given by ensuring that the project reaches the most vulnerable. The community will engaged in the selection process and will be included in each step of the SNFI distribution to avoid violence. Assisting with rights claims, access to remedies and recovery from abuse will be ensured as the community will select the most vulnerable and link them with existing referral systems. As part of the rapid context analysis, the project will identify the most common protection risks, the high risk group and then design mitigation measures. However, the project will take measures to ensure that women and children are protected and are not put in a situation that may increase their protection risks. For instance, the distribution of supplies will take place within the communities, awareness raising will be done and will involve both men and women. Concern’s protection strategy is guided by Code of Conduct and associated policies. Concern’s Code of Conduct sets out all steps that are reasonably possible to protect programme beneficaries– children, women and men. Concern’s approach to protection is based on the key concept of analysis and management of risks: i) the risks faced by the communities we assist; ii) the specific risks of the most vulnerable groups within these communities, iii) the risks our programme may create; and reducing vulnerabilities as a consequence. Concern also updated its Child Safeguarding and Child Protection Policy that will be applied for this intervention. The affected population, who live very far from the health centers will be in focus.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

In late 2017, Concern was the first Irish agency to be accredited with CHS certification based on a rigorous external process. (https://www.chsalliance.org/verify/certification/) As part of accountability to affected people, Concern has put in place a robust Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM). The CRM spells out how the projectbeneficiaries are supposed to their complaints, analysis of the complaints, reporting, feedback and actions to be taken if required. The implementation of the CRM system starts at the project design stage where the beneficiaries are informed about the platforms available to report any cases of misconduct by project staff, including partner staff and contractors. At the implementation stage, Concern usually rolls out the CRM at the lowest level of project implementation, with the establishment CRM committees with membership from the community, including women to ensurethat the system is implemented at the grassroots. The CRM system is monitored and overseen by the senior management staff of the organisation. The feedback from the CRM system are analysed, reported downwards to the beneficiaries, including the actions taken and upwards to the senior management in country and the headquarters in Dublin. Concern’s projects is aligned with minimum standards and accountability mechanism to beneficiaries including CRM, lessons learned and PDM.
