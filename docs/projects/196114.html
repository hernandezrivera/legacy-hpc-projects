Project Summary

In 2022 ZOA implemented projects in multiple sectors including FSA, reaching a total of 185,027 benfs. In 2022 ZOA was able to secure $ 4,185,282 in funding, of which $1,313,293 was SHF funding. ZOA was able to allocate $500,000 for activities under the FSA sector, reaching out to 11,675 benfs. For 2023 ZOA has to date secured $2,085,141 with around $ 525,000 secured for FSA interventions.
Through its 2023 proposal ZOA is proposing an expansion of its interventions to reach communities most in need of food security & agricultural assistance in underserved, conflict affected areas in Aleppo, Dar’a, Deir-ez-Zor, Hama, Idlib, Quneitra & Rural Damascus. Based on community-based needs assessments & sector guidance, ZOA will target the most insecure HHs with an emphasis on those in areas with high severity. Activities are aimed at improving the food security & nutrition status of assessed food insecure HHs through life sustaining food assistance. According to needs, market assessments, Ministry of Agriculture & Agrarian Reform (MoAAR) as well as sector guidance & beneficiary preference, appropriate modalities that include vouchers, cash assistance, grants & other support to small-medium farmers will be used. Assistance will be prioritized to the most vulnerable groups that include female headed HHs, persons with disabilities & elderly. To address contributors to food insecurity & malnutrition in a sustainable manner while also restoring agricultural livelihoods, ZOA proposes the following agricultural activities: rehab. & solarization of irrigation wells, rehab. of irrigation canals, cleaning of irrigation drainage open canals, rehab. of drainage pumping station, climate smart agriculture trainings & rehab. of animal vaccination centers as well as bakeries.
ZOA prioritizes communities with high severity needs & selects vulnerability & selection criteria in line with sector standards. ZOA’s vulnerability criteria also incorporates protection concerns including PWDs. Issues or concerns highlighted during assessments feed into project & implementation planning to ensure a mainstreaming approach & impactful support to HHs. Project committees will be set up to oversee the project implementation & take ownership. Voucher values will be based on Cash Working Group (CWG) as well as Sector recommendations. During 2023 ZOA is planning to target 16,100 HHs (80,500 beneficiaries) & the cost per beneficiary is estimated to be $36.5.
The proposed interventions are in line with the specific objective 2 & 3 of the Sector. The HNO FSA sector chapter is encouraging to move from short-term intervention into more sustainable activities changing the humanitarian work dimensions into early recovery & promotion of livelihoods (HNO 2023). All targeted sub-districts have 3-4 severity score according to the FSA HNO PiN 2023. Before launching any activity a verification of the needs will be done with the sector, local communities as well as authorities, & activities will be altered accordingly.
ZOA has taken into consideration the FSA sector recommendation & is aware of the “Recommendations for Response Packages for Specific Objectives 2 & 3” doc. ZOA will inform its plans through the FSA 5Ws so that duplication of activities is avoided. As for the agricultural input the cap per farmer will be $400 for the whole agricultural season, in line with the sector recommendation for response packages. We are targeting farmers with small to medium capacity, in order to support their livelihood.
ZOA actively participates in the monthly meetings of the FSA sector as well as the Cash Working Group, which is valuable in case any updates are shared by the Sector &/or the Group. Through its flexible programming ZOA will be able to adapt the changes based on the new recommendations. Also, during the past year ZOA closely coordinated its activities with MoAAR, thus establishing a cooperation with the Ministry. The organization is also in process to sign MoU with MoAAR.

3 - Needs

HNO 2023 is indicating 12.1M of the Syrian people is food insecure. Due to the ongoing conflict the destruction of agri infrastructure has severely affected Syrians’ livelihoods & food security. There has been an increase in the PiN, largely due to households depleting their savings & assets & struggling to find livelihood opportunities to meet their food needs. Massive depletion of livelihoods infrastructure, high levels of unemployment & poverty coupled with high food prices, sanctions & inflation have all contributed to food insecurity, as households struggle to meet their daily food needs. Due to devaluation of the Syrian Pound & loss of jobs due to the impacts of COVID & Cholera, including those in agri production, it is likely that food insecurity, malnutrition & adoption of undesirable coping mechanisms will increase, exacerbating existing substantial humanitarian needs across the country. The FAO AICPB of Oct 22 indicates that high cost of agri inputs continues to limit access to quality agri inputs in the local markets, especially for vulnerable smallholder farmers. Apart, the drought-like conditions that certain parts of the country have faced during 2022 will cause additional food insecurity (HNO 2023) and require agri interventions to be climate smart. Scarcity of energy resources such as diesel and electricity hamper the agri value chain from production to sale. All targeted sub-districts have severity score 3-4 according to the PiN SS doc shared by the Sector.

4 - Implementation of activities

ZOA will work closely with the MoAAR, local authorities, FSA sector, CWG & local communities to ensure that assistance reaches the most vulnerable. The activities undertaken to achieve results are: 1 Conducting needs assessment to ascertain the most vulnerable households. 2 Consultations & coordination with affected communities. Consultations with beneficiaries including decisions on the contents of agricultural tools and materials, distribution planning & assistance. 3 Training & capacity building of project staff on accountability to beneficiaries, protection & gender mainstreaming, safety and security as well as COVID-19 and Cholera awareness. 4 Identification and selection of beneficiaries. 5 Provision of agricultural grants to farmers (for seeds, fertilizers, irrigation kits, tools, livestock, livestock vaccinations and poultry farming etc.) to support food production & sustainable livelihoods. 6 Rehabilitation of agricultural infrastructure (canals, drainage pumping stations & canals). 7 Climate smart agriculture trainings. 8 Tender & selection of contractors for rehabilitation works. 9 PDMs. ZOA’s M&E is the responsibility of the country-based Manager of Program Quality, supervising MEAL officers in-country. ZOA will monitor its interventions based on the logical framework indicators and activities with sex, age and disability disaggregated. Field level monitoring will be key in quality assurance, tracking progress, improved accountability, and demonstrating impact.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

R1: Tension in community, for perceived inequality, discrimination, favoritism, including risk for project staff. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigation Measures: 1 Establish adequate selection criteria mechanisms based on vulnerabilities and tools made available by the FSA sector, such as WFP’s beneficiary targeting tool; 2 Consult with actors on the ground through operational coordination mechanisms to establish targeting criteria that are as harmonized as possible throughout protection service providers; 3 Consistent M&E and feedback mechanisms that contribute to project development/ changes. R2: Selection criteria influenced leading to project not accessing most vulnerable. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigating measures: 1 Clear complaint and feedback mechanisms; 2 Ongoing engagement with community leaders and clear community communication; 3 Humanitarian standards, accountability and PSEA training for staff. R3: Physical safety of beneficiaries and risk of exploitation/abuse Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium. Mitigating measures: 1 Liaison with communities and local stakeholders to develop detailed implementation plans; 2 Project coordination, sharing and seeking lessons learned with other actors; 3 Amending project plans based on identified protection vulnerabilities, such as disabilities, age and gender; 4 Regular staff training; 5 Raising awareness for all beneficiaries on complaint mechanisms.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers when accessing assistance and services. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation Measures: 1. Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and for whom they are intended, 2. Reinforce the message that humanitarian services are FREE OF CHARGE and signal the importance to report abuse and exploitation to the service providers ensuring confidentiality of the reporting, 3. Consult periodically with the population receiving the services/assistance, through regular and targeted sessions, including separate sessions with different groups that may be more at risk, ensuring standards of data protection and confidentiality, 4. Implement specific complaint mechanism and assign adequate staff to follow up on complaints, ensuring standards of data protection and confidentiality, 5. Provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA, 6. Perform post-distribution monitoring.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R1 Government entities require agencies to adhere to new regulations or additional approval levels are required in order to be able to implement activities. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1 Coordinate well within the sectorial coordination spaces, to be aware of how other actors deal with the challenges; 2 Continue to engage closely with ministerial entities; 3 Continue to build capacities of local actors & continue to implement through them. R2 Market & exchange rate instability. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1 Continuous monitoring of the markets; 2 Transfer funds into Syrian bank according to the immediate needs. 3 Hold maximum possible amounts in Syrian banks in hard currency. R3 Security related issues during interventions in the field. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1 Continuous assessment of the intervention areas; 2 Coordination with local authorities & community leaders. R4 Fraud & corruption. Likelihood: Low. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1 Clear communication with target populations on their entitlements using multiple channels; 2 ZOA has a clear conflict of interest policy in its code of conduct; 3 Operational policies in place for the mitigation of fraud & corruption risks; 4 All staff are trained on policies that address fraud, corruption & abuse; 5 All financial transactions are documented & filed; 6 Regular finance spot-checks & unannounced audits are carried out.
