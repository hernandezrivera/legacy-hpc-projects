Project Summary

Health needs in South Sudan remains very immerse due to the weak health systems associated with conflict, natural disasters and also COVID 19 pandemic that has ravaged the world since December 2019. The threat of Ebola Virus Disease and other epidemics like measles and cholera continue to affect health service delivery in the country.
The health indicators in the country have not yet improved despite concerted efforts by humanitarian and development partners to support health service delivery in the country. The maternal mortality rates remain very high at 789/1000 live births according to UNICEF. Reproductive health services, mental health services and EPI services are not to the required standards and this has left the country in dire need of humanitarian health services.
Disease outbreaks are rampant with the country struggling to break the chains of transmission for contagious diseases like measles and cholera. According to the IDSR bulleting week 5 2022, there were over 5 counties with suspected or confirmed measles outbreaks. This exposes the vulnerable and affected populations to infections.
Effects of COVID 19 on the health systems in the country cannot be underestimated. The pandemic has strained the very limited health services and has also affected health service delivery as well as the ability of the citizens to contribute to health care through out-of-pocket contribution due to the strained economic situation in the country.
Occasional flooding has in the country has disrupted health service delivery causing displacements and destruction of health infrastructure thus negatively affecting heath service delivery. This therefore calls for efforts to improve access to health services in the country.
TADO will therefore bridge the gaps on the health services in the country through mobile and static health intervention in all the target counties. TADO will support 8 facilities (4 static and 4 mobile) in all the 4 target counties.
TADO will offer routine immunization services as well emergency measles vaccination due to the emergency situation in the country targeting children under 15 years. TADO will ensure that there are reproductive health services through provision of ANC services targeting 1000 pregnant women, PNC services management of STIs, case management of rape, contraceptives to the mothers to support safe motherhood.
TADO will also ensure that there is integrated service delivery in the health facilities through offering nutrition services in the health facilities that TADO will be supporting. This will be through screening of the pregnant and lactating mothers for malnutrition including children under five. The children with malnutrition will be referred to the nutrition sites for management. This will be done in collaboration with the nutrition partners on ground.
There will be integrated WASH services in the health facilities through training the health workers on infection prevention and control, water treatment and health education with key WASH messages passed to the beneficiaries that will help reduce the cases of WASH related infection and other diarrhea diseases among the vulnerable populations. TADO will offer management of non-communicable diseases through the management of hypertension, diabetes and also referral services for the severe cases.
Mental health services delivery is a major issue with limited services being offered in the health facilities. TADO will offer mental health services in the health facilities through provision of psychological and referral of the severe cases to higher level health care facilities for management. TADO will work closely with the county health department and the health cluster both at the national and sub national level to support health information systems by reporting to IDSR and HIMS on weekly and monthly basis.
TADO is targeting 40000 (8000 boys, 10000 girls, 10000 men 12000 women) beneficiaries that will also include persons with disabilities.

1 - Needs

South Sudan continues to struggle to meet the health needs of the vulnerable and affected populations due to the increasing challenges associated with poor health systems, COVID 19 pandemic, natural disasters and intercommunal conflict that has negatively impacted health service delivery and displaced populations. The country also is staring at a possibility of ebola virus disease due to its proximity with Uganda that is a epi centre of the current outbreak.Tonj East County is prone to intercommunal conflict that has affected health service delivery in the county. There has been disruption of health services among the conflicting parties straining the already strained health services. Flooding in the county have also impacted negatively on the service delivery destroying health infrastructure and also cutting health services to the vulnerable population thus exposing them to health risks due to lack of services (Tonj EastIRNA, August 2022).Nasir County had massive flooding in 2021 that displaced over 24000 people and made access to health services very limited. This also made increase in number of patients with Acute watery diarrhoea and other waterborne diseases. Patients were lacking medical supplies and there was urgent need to improve accessibility to reproductive health services (Nasir IRNA July, 2022).Ulang County was as well affected by flooding and intercommunal fighting that has also affected health service delivery. Access to health facilities, immunization services and reproductive health service delivery was cut off from the populations due to the flooding. Lack of medical supplies and displacement of health personnel also affected service delivery in Ulang County (Ulang IRNA, July 2022 ).The country health indicators are still wanting with delivery of mental health services, management of non-communicable diseases, delivery of reproductive health services and referral pathways lacking in many parts of the country thus calling for efforts to support delivery of health services in South Sudan.Only 44% of the total population in South Sudan have access to health services and are able to access services within an hour. The functionality of health facilities as per the basic package for health and nutrition services remains very low with many facilities having low service functionality that has led to low utilization of health services by the affected populations (SARA 2020).

2 - Project activities

• Epidemic disease surveillance, prevention and control including outbreak detection, investigation and response• IPC in health facilities through regular assessment using score cards, provision of supplies and protocols and continuous monitoring of WASH in health facilities• Immunization against vaccine preventable diseases and procurement of outbreak response commodities and emergency antigens for immunization and cold chain commodities.• Community health service provision linking with facility-based services using initiatives such as Boma Health Initiatives and ICCM• Provision of life-saving essential health care services including management of non-communicable diseases through static (Hospitals, PHCC, and PHCU) and mobile healthcare services, procurement of essential medical kits and training of health workers.• Provide technical and operational assistance to heath facilities through supportive supervision visits to ensure quality provision of health services and accountability to the affected population.• Conducting community consultative meetings within the community regularly and implementing feedback mechanism and ensure compliance• Provide technical and operational assistance to heath facilities through supportive supervision visits to ensure quality provision of health services• Promote humanitarian-peace-development nexus bringing humanitarian, peace and development actors together to promote synergies and optimize use of available resources.

7.4. Community Engagement Strategy

Yes

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

TADO will have a focal person in each of the implementing counties to receive and give feedback to the community on the implementation of the project. In addition TADO will use the beneficiary accountability committees that will be key in ensuring that the beneficiaries are able to get feedback on the implementation of the project. TADO will also have suggestion boxes from the community and feedback desks within the health facilities to improve community engagement during the implementation.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

TADO has robust community engagement mechanisms that foster good understanding between the organization and the community. TADO will have beneficiary accountability committees that will be the key linkage between the organization and the community. The beneficiary accountability committees will be trained on the AAP principles and staffs will as well be trained on this important aspect of project implementation.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

TADO has a PSEA policy that ensures the beneficiaries are informed about sexual exploitation and abuse. All TADO staffs involved in the implementation of this project will sign code of conduct that has a very strong component of prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse. TADO has non tolerant policy to sexual exploitation and has a committed email address and a telephone number for reporting cases of SEA.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiary feedback will be used in ensuring that the project activities are aligned to the needs of the beneficiaries. There will be tailored responses depending on the needs and the feedback received including change of design where possible to incorporate the views of beneficiaries.
