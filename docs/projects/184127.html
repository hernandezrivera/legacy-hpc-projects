Project Summary

Despite the signature of JPA, armed clashes have continued to be observed across Sudan, the severe economic crisis continued to negatively impact on the life of the people in Sudan. These situations have been exacerbated by the COVID 19 pandemic. The overall protection needs of the children have grown up considerably. According to the HNO/HRP 2022 figures a total population of 2,447,351 children are need across the different states, including 1,221,786 IDPs, 152,249 South Sudanese refugees, 465,488 returnees and 617,828 vulnerable children among the host communities. International support towards the transitional government has not yet translated into substantive financial and policy support to contribute to a progressive reversal of the situation. As a result, humanitarian needs continued to grow, driven by underlying factors including the economic crisis, food insecurity, flooding, disease outbreaks and intercommunal violence. All these factors have resulted in prolonged displacement in addition to recent fighting in Jebal Marra between different factions of armed groups and intercommunal conflict exacerbated by the deteriorating economic conditions and other political factors. Sudan has and is still receiving additional numbers of refugees from South Sudan, Congo, CAR, Eritrea and recently more than 60000 Newly displaced Ethiopian refugees. The country has also continued to experience rains and floods emergencies in 2021, affecting people across the 18 states of the country. Moreover, endemic diseases such as Malaria, cholera, dengue fever and chikungunya, other novel diseases are also emerging including some which have not been reported in over 10 years. The people in need in Sudan will be the highest over the past decade. Protection of children from violence, exploitation, abuse, and neglect within such complex emergencies is challenged by many factors that child protection partners are working to address. Children affected by conflict, displacement, disease outbreaks and other emergencies are more prone to all types of violations to their rights including their rights to basic services. Many have been separated from their families due to sudden displacement and become vulnerable to further violations including recruitment and association with armed forces/groups. Others who witnessed violence and atrocities during conflict are psychologically affected and need psychosocial support services to avoid further deterioration to trauma and mental health problems that needs specialized services which is not available in almost all conflict affected areas. Displaced children and adolescents specially adolescents’ girls are subject to GBV because of the breakdown of the protective family and community environment during displacement and conflict. Many children in the states affected by armed conflict are victims of UXOs and some of them lost their lives especially in the newly accessible areas in Blue Nile, south Kordofan and Jabal Marra area in Darfur. The six grave violations against children by armed forces/groups is widespread in the conflict affected areas. Together with UNITAMS, the UN Wide MRM-CAAC is being strengthened across the country to continue the monitoring and reporting on grave violations including community-based approaches to response to these grave violations against children are needed basically strengthening formal and informal child protection mechanisms and ensure that they complement each other. Under the strategic objective one of HRP, child protection emergency response to newly displaced people due to intercommunal conflict, flooding, and pockets of violence in Jebel Marra areas as well as in the newly accessible areas in South Darfur, Blue Nile and South Kordofan states will be provision of lifesaving child protection services such as provision of PSS, FTR services, prevention and response to GBV against children and adolescents, UXO risk education, monitoring reporting and responding to grave child.

1 - Needs

People in the Sudan remain extremely vulnerable to emergencies. Deepening poverty iscausing widespread malnutrition; lapses in the rule of law are allowing ethnic tensions to erupt into violence; flooding and disease outbreaks are destroying lives and livelihoods; and education interruptions in 2019, 2020 and 2021 have left 13 million children out of school. COVID- 19 and related mitigation measures are further complicating these existing challenges. These hardships are threatening people's lives, denying children – particularly girls – access to an education, creating serious protection risks and giving rise to violations and abuse, including gender-based violence. Past conflicts in the Sudan andneighboring countries have left thousands of people internally displaced or living as refugees and without basic support, in or out of camps. While the recent steps taken towards securing peace have opened the possibility of humanitarian assistance in previously inaccessible areas in Darfur, Kordofan and Blue Nile, humanitarian needs remain high: 9.3 million people, including 5.4 million children, will require humanitarian assistance in 2022.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The activities include provision of MHPSS services to children and their families, provision of FTR and alternative care services, prevention and response to GBV, UXOs risk education, prevention and reponse to recruitment of children and reintegration of children released from armed forces/groups and establishment of CBCPNs to engage with communities on prevention and reponse to grave child rights violation. UNICEF has long experience in working with the government and civil society partners on all the emergency issues faced by Sudan. UNICEF's response depends mainly on these partnerships and the child protection system that have established and supported during the past 15 years. So, FTR is already established with clear SOPs that identifies the roles and responsibilities of different partners. Social workers in all the states affected by conflict are well trained on FTR issues and the alternative care procedures. For MHPSS, UNICEF in collaboration with the NCCW has been working during the past years on the design and production of a PSS manual for children and adolescents affected by conflict. This manual has been approved and used widely in providing PSS to children and adolescents by trained social workers in all the states affected by conflict. UNICEF has also long experience in reintegration of children released from armed forces/groups. In collaboration with DDRC, a training guide for social workers on working with children recruited or associated with armed forces/groups was designed and used in the reintegration of more than 2500 children after the signing of the CPA. Building on the critical mass of experience and lessons learned, UNICEF together with UNITAMS is engaged with SAF, RSF and Juba Peace Agreement signatory parties on the way to implement a National strategy developed for the release, the interim care, family tracing and reunification as well as the sustainable reintegration of children associated with armed forces and armed groups across the different states across Sudan. For this purpose, road maps have been adopted and a technical level committee and High-level committees have been established to oversee the sustainable reintegration of the children In close collaboration with the UN agencies, International Organizations operational. Furthermore, effective case management system is being strengthening under the case management working group of the Child Protection subsector. This will be the basis to rollout quality case management according to the Child Protection Minimum Standards revised on December 2019. And 100 social workers in the states affected by conflict have received training on how to do case management for children released form armed forces/groups. The same applies to UXOs where a community based MRE manual has been designed and currently used by community volunteers and social workers to provide risk education to affected communities. UNICEF has also supported NMAC and National Center for Curricula to develop a curriculum for MRE for school children and has been used before in Kassala, Red Sea, Al Gadarif, Blue Nile and South Kordofan states.
