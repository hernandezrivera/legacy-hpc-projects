Project Summary

WFP is committed to the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and is guided by a strategic, programmatic and governance instrument to operationalize its plan and achieve its project objectives to:
• End Hunger by protecting access to food and by improving dietary diversity;
• Support the implementation of the SDGs by enhancing the capacities of national institutions to implement food security strategies and operations;
This project directly contributes to strategic objectives two and three of the Humanitarian Response Plan 2018-2020, by meeting the basic food needs of severely vulnerable Palestinians and by improving their dietary diversity.
WFP’s interventions focus on high food insecurity levels, including the malnutrition levels recorded by national surveys. By assisting the poor and severely food insecure Palestinian households in Gaza and Bedouin and herding communities in West Bank’s Area C, and by disseminating information on food and nutrition practices, WFP addresses the basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians and tackles one of the root causes of some of the food insecurity levels recorded by the government institutions.
These interventions aim at improving the overall food security levels including nutrition and health status of the vulnerable Palestinians and therefore at increasing their coping abilities to sudden shocks in the market for access to food.
The project also contributes to the Food Security Sector’s three objectives as well as to the National Policy Agenda 2017-2022 (policies on escaping poverty and strengthening social protection as well as meeting the basic needs of the communities) to ensure that the vulnerable Palestinians households basic food needs are met, by assisting households in enhancing their food security levels and by strengthening national institutions in implementing food security strategies and social security safety net programs to best identify and target food insecure and vulnerable Palestinians.

1 - Needs

According to the latest Socio-Economic and Food Security Survey (SefSec - PCBS, WFP 2018), food insecurity levels remain high among the Palestinian population. The survey found that 31 percent of the Palestinian population is severely or moderately food insecure, respectively 12 percent in the West Bank and 68.5 percent in the Gaza Strip. In addition to the SefSec, a specific survey carried out by WFP in cooperation with UNRWA in 2016/2017, reveals that more than 60 percent of the Bedouin and herding communities living in Area C of the West Bank are food insecure, marking a deterioration in their food security status compared to the findings in a 2012 survey at 50 percent. The survey found evidence of continued use of negative strategies to cope with the deterioration of their livelihoods and socio-economic conditions. Food insecurity is driven by limited economic access to food, arising from restrictions on movement, trade and investment, high unemployment rates, erosion of agricultural infrastructure, land confiscation, limited access to land and water, and an overstretched government safety net. Gender inequalities play a significant role in the food security and nutrition status of individuals; women are especially affected by food insecurity and poverty. The prevalence of food insecurity among households headed by women is 6.6 percentage points higher than that among households headed by men – 39.1 versus 32.5 percent. Women’s economic empowerment is likely to be impeded by lower wages and domestic work. At 30.6 percent, the poverty rate for individuals in households headed by women is higher than the 29.2 percent for those in households headed by men (PECS , PCBS , 2017). In addition to limited economic opportunities and access to food, malnutrition is also an issue. Based on the Palestinian micronutrient survey conducted by the Ministry of Health in 2013 and 2014, Palestinians are facing a double burden of malnutrition: a high level of micronutrient deficiencies alongside growing overweight and obesity. Approximately 50 percent of people assessed had very low levels of essential minerals and vitamins. Depleted levels of iron were found in 20 percent of lactating women in the West Bank and in 28 percent in the Gaza Strip. Prevalence of mild anaemia in children aged 6-59 months averaged 20-24 percent, with moderate anaemia at 8 percent; similar results were found for pregnant and lactating women. According to World Health Organization (WHO) standards, anaemia is a moderate public health problem. Overweight and obesity are moderate public health problems for pregnant and lactating women, with 31 percent of pregnant women being overweight and 26 percent obese, based on body weight and height measured before the 17th week of gestation. In lactating women, prevalence rates are 37 percent overweight and 23 percent obese. Wasting, stunting and underweight are not severe public health problems. On average, 7.4 percent of children under 5 suffer from moderate or severe stunting - 7.7 percent in the West Bank versus 7.1 percent in the Gaza Strip. The rate of stunting among boys is higher, at 8.1 percent, than among girls, at 6.6 percent. Among children under 5, 1.4 percent are moderately or severely underweight: 1.5 percent in the West Bank versus 1.3 percent in the Gaza Strip; and 1.6 percent among boys versus 1.1 percent among girls. Wasting prevalence - moderate and severe - is 1.2 percent for children under 5: 1.7 percent in the West Bank versus 0.7 percent in the Gaza Strip. The rate of overweight is 8.2 percent in the State of Palestine: 9.8 percent in the West Bank and 6.5 percent in Gaza. 18 per cent of pregnant women and 14 per cent of lactating mothers in deprived communities in Gaza are undernourished. A total of 210,000 children in Gaza under the age of five are vulnerable as a result of micronutrient deficiencies, including seven out of ten of children under the age of five.

2 - Activities

WFP will carry out the following activities: Activity 1: Provision of unconditional food assistance - through cash based transfers and in-kind modalities and nutrition awareness sessions to poor and food insecure households. WFP will provide unconditional food assistance to targeted poor and severely food insecure households in the Gaza Strip, in Area C and areas with poverty and high food insecurity levels in the West Bank. The estimated number of beneficiary is based on the needs identified in the 2018/2019 SefSec survey conducted together with PCBS. The target beneficiaries include 404,000 non-refugee poor and severely food insecure people including 37,000 individuals from the Bedouin and herding communities in Area C of the West Bank that will be assisted as part of the joint emergency food assistance programme in partnership with UNRWA. Around 30% of the targeted households are female-headed householdsThroughout the implementation of the project, WFP aims at introducing immediate cash transfer. To ensure the integrity of transfers of intended beneficiaries, WFP will contract shops that are able to offer e-voucher beneficiary’s quality and diverse food at market prices, while providing an extra layer of control. WFP also will, to the greatest extent possible, ensure the inclusion of shops owned by women, to promote women’s economic empowerment. Through the cash-based transfers activity, WFP allows the redemption of all food items except unhealthy items such as sodas, chocolate, chips,..etc. The local food items such as honey, olive oil, olives, and food processed by women and agricultural cooperatives are included and can be redeemed by the beneficiaries. WFP is monitoring the secondary impact of the voucher on the local economy to assess the trickle -downs effect on the local economy along the supply chain. Disability is also a key vulnerability factor taken into consideration in targeting poor and food insecure households and included in the data collection tool that is used in targeting. Disability of food insecure people is an eligibility factor to receive assistance under the National Social Safety Net programme. Operationally WFP considers disability a factor in the selection of distribution points and shops to ensure accessibility. In response to the recommendation stemming from the strategic review, WFP will utilize available nutrition data to design and implement interventions per the nutritional -and not only the caloric -needs of the beneficiary population. This will include developing and delivering Social Behavior Change and Communication interventions. Throughout the activity, WFP will ensure the safety of beneficiaries and emphasize protection and accountability towards affected populations. All women and men beneficiaries will continue to have access to beneficiary feedback mechanisms through secure boxes placed in retail shops for CBTs and through the WFP hotline.

3 - Indicators

In line with the Food Security Sector 2018 Strategic Response Framework and Strategic Objectives 1, 2 and 3, the indicators for the WFP Country Strategic Plan and activities are: - Number of beneficiaries (by gender and age) receiving in-kind food as a percentage of the planned figures (Number). This indicator contributes to the HPC SO 3 and FSS Objective 2. The target set for this indicator is 72,000 (32,568 women and girls, 36,432 men and boys). -Number of beneficiaries (by gender and age) receiving vouchers as a percentage of planned figures (Number). This indicator contributes to the HPC SO 3 and FSS Objective 2. The target set for this indicator is 316,300 (156,252 women and girls, 160,048 men and boys). - Number of beneficiaries (by gender and age) receiving cash as a percentage of planned figures (Number). This indicator contributes to the HPC SO 3 and FSS Objective 2. The target set for this indicator is 15,700 (7,756 women and girls, 7,944 men and boys).- Total voucher amount distributed as a percentage of planned (Percentage). This indicator contributes to the HPC SO 3 and FSS Objective 2. The target set for this indicator is 100%. - Tonnage of food items purchased from local producers (Tons). This indicator contributes to the HPC SO 3 and FSS Objective 1. The target set for this indicator is 9,435 metric tons. - Proportion of targeted households with borderline and acceptable food consumption score (Percentage). This indicator contributes to the HPC SO 3 and FSS Objective 2. The target set for this indicator is 85%. WFP works closely with Food Security Sector stakeholders to continually improve the analysis and identification of the food insecure population in Palestine and to target food assistance support accordingly. The targeting of WFP’s food assistance is based on a Proxy Means Test Formula (PMTF) developed by the Ministry of Social Development. The PMTF takes into account changing socioeconomic factors which influence household expenditures and the determination of households that cannot meet their basic needs. Food assistance will be provided in a manner that reduces the exposure to risk for beneficiaries (especially that of women) and at the same time reduces the burden associated with carrying food items, particularly for female headed households. WFP uses a robust monitoring system to ensure that all beneficiaries are verified and monitored after receiving assistance to ensure that the beneficiaries meet the criteria of falling below the PA’s threshold deep poverty line. The deep poverty line is calculated by taking into consideration household consumption levels of three categories of goods and services: food, clothing and housing. Prior to any activity implementation, WFP will strictly coordinate the final definition and selection of localities and beneficiaries with line ministries offices, and with any other organization working in the same area and sector of activities.
