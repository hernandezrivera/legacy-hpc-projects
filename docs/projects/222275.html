Project Summary

Comprehensive Support for Children and Caregivers in Emergency Situations: Mental Health Services, Protection, Alternative Care and Prevention of Early Marriages, aims to ensure the protection, recovery and well-being of children in crisis contexts by offering psychosocial support services, legal protection and specialized care. The intervention covers a comprehensive set of activities that includes psychosocial support services (MHPSS), child protection case management, alternative care and family tracing and reunification for unaccompanied and separated children, as well as specialized services for children associated with armed forces and armed groups (CAAFAG). It also addresses the prevention and response to cases of child marriage and facilitates access to birth registration, a fundamental right for the exercise of citizenship.
Purpose:
The main objective of the project is to ensure that all children in vulnerable situations receive the necessary protection for their survival, development and social reintegration, through a holistic approach that considers physical, emotional, legal and social aspects. The intervention seeks to mitigate the impacts of crises and ensure that children have access to health services, education, legal protection and psychosocial support.
Target:
The project targets children in vulnerable situations, including those who have been separated from their families, are involved in armed conflict, are at risk of child marriage, or have difficulty accessing birth registration. In addition, the project also includes caregivers of these children, offering support and guidance to ensure that they can play an active and protective role in the development of children.
This intervention seeks not only immediate protection, but also long-term recovery and the promotion of children’s rights, with the support of the community and local and international partners.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The project will focus on a set of priority activities to protect and promote the well-being of children at risk, focusing on both immediate and long-term needs. Activities will be implemented flexibly, adapting to local conditions and the specific challenges of each geographic area, with the aim of reaching children in different contexts of vulnerability.1. Psychosocial Support Services (MHPSS) for Children and Caregivers (Levels 2 and 3)Purpose: To provide psychosocial support to children affected by crisis, trauma or violence, and to empower caregivers to provide more appropriate care.Activities: Conducting psychosocial support sessions for children, focusing on trauma, post-traumatic stress, anxiety and other psychosocial impacts; Training caregivers (family members, teachers, community workers) to identify signs of emotional distress and provide initial support; Establishing psychosocial support centers in the most affected areas, with ongoing monitoring.2. Child Protection Case ManagementPurpose: To identify, monitor and provide specialized support to children in situations of abuse, neglect or exploitation, through a case management approach.Activities:Identifying children at risk through home visits, interviews with family members and collaboration with community leaders; Monitoring individual cases, developing specific action plans and ensuring that the needs of children are fully met;Referrals to specialized services (education, health, legal protection, etc.).3. Alternative Care and Family Reunification for Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC)Purpose: To ensure the protection and family reunification of children who have been separated from their families or are unaccompanied.Activities:Identifying unaccompanied and separated children in shelters, refugee camps or temporary host communities; Processing cases for family reunification, where possible, and providing psychosocial support to help with adaptation; Establishing partnerships with child protection services and local support networks to ensure appropriate care.4. Specialized Services for Children Associated with Armed Forces and Armed Groups (CAAFAG)Objective: To ensure the social reintegration and rehabilitation of children who have been associated with armed groups or military forces.Activities:To provide demobilization, psychosocial support and community reintegration services for children associated with armed forces and armed groups; To develop education and vocational training programs tailored to the social reintegration of these children; To monitor reintegration to ensure that children are not re-recruited and can resume their lives in safety.5. Prevention and Response to Child MarriageObjective: Combat child marriage by promoting education and awareness to prevent this practice.Activities:Conducting awareness campaigns on the risks and consequences of child marriage for communities and families; Working with schools and community leaders to promote alternatives to early marriage, such as education and training for girls; Monitoring and supporting girls at risk of child marriage, offering them access to health services, psychosocial and legal support.6. Identification and Access to Birth RegistrationObjective: Ensure that all children, especially those in vulnerable situations, have access to their birth registration, guaranteeing their civil rights and protection.geographic area:Identification and monitoring of children without birth registration, with special attention to those in resettlement centers, displacement areas and communities; Collaboration with local authorities and registration agencies.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

1. Contextual Analysis: Before any activity, a contextual assessment will be conducted to identify local risks, such as exposure to violence, gender discrimination, or harmful cultural practices (e.g. child marriage).2. Consultations with Communities and Stakeholders: The project will involve consultations with community leaders, local organizations, and experts to ensure that interventions are culturally sensitive and appropriate, minimizing potential negative reactions or stigmatization.3. Ongoing Monitoring: Case management and monitoring of children will be closely monitored to detect risks of re-traumatization or violation of rights during interventions. Harm Prevention:1. Sensitive and Safe Approach: Activities will be conducted by trained professionals, respecting the emotional and psychological needs of children, as well as ensuring a safe and supportive environment.2. Protection and Confidentiality: Confidentiality will be strictly maintained, especially regarding sensitive information of children and their families, to avoid exposure or re-victimization.3. Partnerships with Local Services: The project will collaborate with local organizations and protection services, ensuring that children are referred to appropriate care and avoiding duplication of efforts or misdirection.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Tailored Support for Women and the Elderly: Women, especially mothers and caregivers, will receive psychosocial support and guidance on how to protect their children, with a focus on empowering them to make informed decisions about the care and protection of their children. Elderly people will also be involved in child protection by providing emotional support and helping to care for children.• Community Awareness and Empowerment: The project will conduct workshops and educational campaigns targeting women and the elderly, informing them about their rights, the services available and how to access assistance. This ensures that these groups have clear and accessible information.Legal and Social Support: Accessible legal services will be made available to women, especially in cases of domestic violence, child marriage or family separation. This will help ensure that their voices are heard in legal processes involving their rights and the rights of their children.2. Participation and Empowerment of Women and Elders:• Active Involvement in Decision-Making: Women and elderly people will be involved in decisions about the care and protection of children, particularly in cases of case management and family reunification. This ensures that their perspectives and needs are considered in the planning of interventions.Empowering Women as Community Leaders: The project will encourage women to take on leadership roles in the community, such as facilitating support groups, mentoring and child protection advocates, increasing their influence and decision-making power.• Forums and Dialogue Spaces: Safe and accessible spaces will be created where women and older people can express their concerns, share experiences and influence project activities. This strengthens their active participation and allows their specific needs to be met.• Skills Development: The project will offer capacity building and training programmes for women and older people, developing skills in areas such as childcare, psychosocial support, leadership and advocacy, enabling them to take a more active role in the protection and well-being of children. 3. Promoting Gender Equality and Intergenerationality:• Promoting Gender Equality: Through awareness-raising activities, the project will address harmful cultural and gender norms, challenging practices such as child marriage and domestic violence, while empowering women to become advocates for children’s rights and make autonomous decisions.• Intergenerationality: The project will integrate women and older people into child protection activities, recognizing their fundamental role in society and promoting the strengthening of family and community bonds, with a focus on collaboration between different generations to ensure the safety and well-being of children.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Screening and Initial Assessment: During the initial identification and case management phase, a comprehensive screening will be conducted to identify children and adults with disabilities. This will include interviews with family members, community leaders and caregivers, which will help identify signs of disability, both visible and invisible (such as intellectual or sensory disabilities).• Partnerships with Specialist Organizations: The project will partner with organizations and professionals specializing in disability, such as disability associations and rehabilitation centers, to ensure that children and adults with disabilities are identified and receive appropriate support.• Community Outreach: Awareness campaigns will be conducted in communities, informing about the different types of disabilities and how to identify them, encouraging greater awareness and support for inclusion.2. Removing Barriers to Access to Care: Physical Accessibility: The project will ensure that service locations (such as support centers, shelters, and case management offices) are accessible to people with physical disabilities. This will include adaptations such as ramps, accessible bathrooms, and large, obstacle-free spaces.• Communication Accessibility: For children and adults with sensory disabilities (such as hearing or visual impairments), the project will implement accessible communication measures. This may include the presence of Libras (Brazilian Sign Language) interpreters for deaf people, reading materials in Braille or audio for people with visual impairments, and the use of assistive technologies to ensure inclusion of all in the activities and services offered.Specialized and Adapted Services: The project will include specific and adapted services to the needs of children with disabilities, such as specialized psychosocial support, rehabilitation therapies and inclusive educational support. This will ensure that these children have access to holistic recovery, according to their specific needs.• Professional Training: All professionals involved in the project, including those working in psychosocial support, case management and direct care, will be trained to work with children and adults with disabilities, ensuring that they know how to address the specific needs of this population and remove barriers to communication and access to services.• Sensitive Psychosocial Support: Psychosocial support will be provided that is sensitive to the needs of children with disabilities, taking into account their limitations and capabilities. Trained professionals will be able to apply appropriate care methods, helping to reduce stigma and promoting a safe and supportive environment.3. Ensuring Participation and Empowerment: Active Involvement of Children with Disabilities: The project will ensure that children with disabilities are actively involved in decisions that affect their protection and well-being, whenever possible, respecting their capacity for understanding and decision-making. This will include adapted activities where they can express their needs, desires and concerns in an accessible way.• Strengthening Community Participation: Women, families and caregivers of children with disabilities will be empowered to advocate for the rights of these children, promote inclusion and ensure that they receive appropriate assistance. This may include specific workshops and training on inclusion and the rights of persons with disabilities.

Potential Environmental Impacts: 1. Use of Natural Resources: These activities, such as the construction of support centers, temporary shelters or training centers, may involve the use of natural resources, such as water and energy, in addition to the generation of waste. 2. Waste Disposal: Activities related to case management, health services or psychosocial support may generate waste, such as paper, specific medical materials or electronic waste. 3. Mitigation Measures and Planned Actions: 1. Initial Environmental Assessment: Before starting the activities, an environmental assessment will be carried out to identify possible environmental impacts in the areas where the project will be implemented. This will allow the implementation of strategies to minimize or avoid environmental damage during the execution of the activities. - Example: Assessment of the impact of construction or renovation of child-friendly spaces.
