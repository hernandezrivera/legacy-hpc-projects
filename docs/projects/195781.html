Project Summary

In poultry sector livestock:
The objective for this sector is to contribute to reactivation of poultry farms for reproduction and food security, and to support poultry sector through following activities:
1- SDH will support 100 poultry farms (500 for each) selected in each region according to specific criteria where the farmers will be responsible for raising the distributed chicks until they could financialise their farms by chicken products, (SDH will supoort them by chicks (broiler) to get benifit and empower their poultry farms (broiler)
2- Purchase (chicks - sawdust - medicine (under veterinarian supervision as needed) - fodder supplement - charcoal - power source (solar power)- vitamins and sugar supplements ( SDH will provide to increase the prodiction)
3- The other sub-activities will be (chicks & charcoal distribution, supervise feeding process, monitor the overall condition of poultry, provide healthcare for little chicks, carry out immunization during life of little chicks under 6 weeks according to vaccine program.
* A portion of production estimated at 56K chicken (70 * 800) will be returned and prepared for distribution in camps for IDPs and people in need according to selected criteria by SDH.
4- Support owners of poultry farms by animal (feeding, breeding, diseases) training.
5- Distribute hatchers to breeders working by solar power, which have egg-laying chickens (1 hatchery in each community according to selection criteria)
In livestock sector:
The activities under this section aim to support livestock herders, living in the NWS. which provides to preserve health of livestock, increases production, and grants economic security for working families.
1-support 500 families have livestock according to criteria.
2- SDH will provide these families with necessary supplies for livestock, such as fodder, fodder supplements (Salt and vitamins) for three months, vaccines campaigns, and veterinary services.
Training for herders will carry out by veterinarians; The training provides herders with information about The care for livestock, the methods of forming the fodder mixing, and main diseases that affected livestock and its treatment.
In vocational training sector:
in order to alleviate unemployment rate, which is significantly widespread in NWS, and provide high-quality local products
Training (500) BNFs, especially female ones, through a series of vocational courses in order to meet the local market need, as well as those who commetitted will be supported by business grants limit 2000 USD to 100 BNFs.
In agriculture sector which BNFs are new agriculture sector through selection criteria for farmers to support them to:
1- Cultivation of 500 hectares of wheat, chickpeas, Bean,..ect
2- Cultivation of 4 hectares of various vegetables (cucumber, tomatoes, okra, zucchini, pumpkin green pepper)
3- Cultivation of 150 hectares of mix-fodder for animals. The crops are alfalfa, Jalban, and Barley.
3- Increasing the agricultural experience for the BNFs and reduction of unemployment.
In Food sector:
The objective of this sector is to provide life-saving humanitarian assistance to most vulnerable people to alleviate suffering.
1- Distribute FBs for 6250HH in NWS, for 8 months along with FSL and CWG recommendations to cover Kcal needs for these households to be aligned with food component .The BNFs in this component will be selected from IDPs (including disabled, female-headed households, women and young girls, PLW, and the elderly )The targeted BNFs are 6250HH (25000 individuals) (Men 5000, Women 6000, Boys 6800, Girls 7200) (from them 600 disabled),SDH will be provided food baskets for full 2100 kcal person/per day, Weight of food basket is 78 kg family size 6 Persons.
2- Distribute ready eat ratio for 50,000 families in NWS (for newly IDPs (less than 2 months).
3- Distribute cooked meals for 50,000 families in NWS (for newly IDPs (less than 1 months)
The fund for the Project is secure for 0% and unsecured for 100%.

3 - Needs

12 years of the crisis, the food security situation in Syria has deteriorated compared to last year, with a total of The situation continues to deteriorate drastically with an estimated 14.3M people in need of some form of food and agriculture assistance, compared to 9.8 million in the previous year. The food insecurity PiN has risen by 46 % in the last year. • At least 12.4M people are estimated to be food insecure, an increase of 58 % compared to 2022, of which 1.3M people are considered severely food insecure. Food prices in Syria have increased dramatically in 2022 and WFP’s national standard reference food basket price in November 2022 was 251 % higher when compared to November 2021. The agriculture sector, which was once a cornerstone of the Syrian economy, has been severely impacted, with half (i.e. 50 percent) of national grain requirements met through imports. • According to Food and Agricultural Organization (FAO), high cost and limited availability of quality crop and livestock production inputs, damaged irrigation infrastructure across the country, degradation of livelihoods and income-earning opportunities, coupled with the direct and knock-on effects of Covid-19 and climate-induced shocks continue to drive more people into food insecurity. (2023 Needs and Response Summary)

4 - Implementation of activities

The proposed activities are expected to cover period till the end of Dec23. The project start date has been set taking into consideration urgent need for support livestock and chicken herders during winter months. During lean period, farmers rely on animal feed for their livestock also, cultivate multi-crops for themselves also, we will distribute FB/RTER and Cooked meals. SDH will involve and cooperate with local partners in all stages of the implementation process. A team will be assigned including a project manager and field coordinators (agricultural engineers and veterinarians and distributors, for FB had purchased to deliver the FB monthly for 8 months) and we will work to include employees in the staff for supporting the families (most of which being female-headed). Contracts will be made with specialized instructors to provide support in training workshops. Purchases will be carried out in a transparent manner and in accordance with the procurement strategy approved by SDH and will be overseen by the relevant specialists to get the best quality. The local team will be responsible for the implementation of all phases of the project _ procurement, distribution, monitoring and evaluation, the preparation of periodic reports, and enacting any necessary updates at every stage. Trainers will be hired to add value to the planned business plans and ensure quality standards. Follow-up training will also be undertaken up to several weeks after the training.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SDH a PSEA network member and strives to serve all vulnerable people to attain a better life and maintain their dignity. Through set protection policy and procedure, SDH actively discusses methods to protect the dignity and image of BNs during implementation. BNs are treated equally with respect and dignity, regardless of religion, G, S, or political affiliation. SDH conducting gender mainstreaming to identify how project could impact lives of vulnerable groups in area. The project tries to touch on specific risks faced by boys such as labour and recruitment, by G such as early marriage and labour, and the disabled such as isolation and limited mobility. SDH discovered immense benefit to vulnerable W and (PwDs) SDH is assisting the most vulnerable groups gain access to services. To further protect these groups (C, W...etc) during the implementation SDH will aim to 50-50% F/M representation. SDH will prioritize hiring CFW people who live near Locations—as longer distances often limit W’s ability to travel to work due to cultural norms and/or safety concerns. 9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

SDH has a specialized S&S department to ensure S&S of staff, BNs and volunteers, and mitigate damage when not avoidable. Risk management in SDH is based on a scientific basis through the development of a risk matrix that includes all possible risks as well as risk mitigation mechanisms. Weekly reports to be prepared for management of SDH and partners, in addition of course to security updates and incident reports, if any. S&S follow and conducts a daily risk assessment of area and informs the administration of any possible scenarios and how to deal with them, including evacuation and contingency plans and training the field staff on all expected scenarios. Operation Risks: 1-Closure of borders with Turkey and increase the agriculture inputs prices Likelihood: Medium – Impact: Medium Mitigation: Prepare a list of alternative markets and vendors, and sign long term contract with the current once. 2- COVID-19 virus outbreak Mitigation: Online methodologies are being used to share information.
