Project Summary

The Danish Refugee Council (DRC) in Iraq proposes a 12-month intervention, in line with the HRP strategic priorities, to support families and communities affected by the 2014 conflict. The proposed program will be part of a DRC multisector response to support those who return to reintegrate into their communities with inadequate community infrastructure, lack of available services, and population influx and to continue providing services to most vulnerable communities in their location of displacement and their host communities. Activities will be based on both sector-specific and integrated needs assessments, and build on DRC’s achievements implementing Protection, WASH, Shelter, and Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) activities under previous grant awards in 2015-2018. The program will be implemented in both camp and non-camp settings. Where possible activities will be validated and confirmed by inclusive community committees as part of local development plans.
The multi-sectoral program will be strengthened through further integration of proposed activities, including through beneficiary selection modalities, referrals, and assessments, using an “area-based” approach where possible. In line with this approach, DRC expects a large proportion of the individuals targeted by this project to benefit from multiple DRC services. The program also incorporates increased attention to training of local authorities, civil society, community leaders and other duty bearers to meet the needs of populations of concern as an exit strategy.
DRC intends to incorporate a greater focus on market-based approaches to the delivery of WASH assistance to support the economic recovery of the targeted communities. This is through engaging with local vendors and implementing elements of work through cash for work where possible. DRC will continue to implement a strong protection focused programme to ensure the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, consideration for those with specific needs due to disability, access constraints and gender as well as integrated HLP support for household assistance as part of the multi-sectoral objectives of the program. Household activities will be tailored to the needs of those benefiting, with particular attention to better access for those with disabilities, the elderly and the chronically sick.

Needs

Return movements following the conclusion of military operations are complicated by continued lower-level violence, serious protection risks, lack of infrastructure, shelter and housing issues and disruption of markets and employment options; these issues often lead to secondary and protracted displacement. Both during displacement and upon return, people have acute needs with accessing WASH services, with the HRP recognizing water supply as an essential basic service.Sanitation: SWM is highly problematic due to reduced municipality capacity. DRC's Tel Afar needs assessment (April 2018) found that the municipality employs 10% of the garbage collectors that were employed pre-conflict before garbage trucks were damaged and looted. Waste collection and disposal is often irregular as a result, so community members resort to burning and making ad hoc dump sites, which attracts rats and scorpions. The municipality of Mosul city confirmed that despite 2018 investments in restoring services, service resumption is not equitable, with some neighborhoods not receiving any services. The Municipality and Sewage Directorates share responsibility for the management of grey water, which incurred damage both directly as a result of the conflict and from lack of investment in maintenance. Makeshift shelters have been established and are in many cases connected to the water networks through unsanctioned connections, which lead to water leakages and contribute to standing water bodies. These stagnant water bodies are a potential source of drinking water contamination, they also leach into cesspools and septic tanks, increasing the need for desludging to avoid the overflow posing serious contamination and health concerns to the entire community, especially chronically sick, elderly, infants and pregnant and lactating mothers.In Qayyarah, the local municipality is unable to ensure service provision to the camps. DRC supports the municipalities work by providing collection and maintenance services in the camp through communal bins and daily collection. The DRC Ninewa Plains MSNA (January 2018) found that private household latrines are prevalent, but many incurred war damage to the point they are dysfunctional entirely, giving rise to open defecation practices.Hygiene Promotion: The DRC hygiene promotion assessment in March 2018 among IDP households in Diyala showed that 80% of the population demonstrated a very low level of knowledge of positive hygiene practices. This matched findings in a hygiene assessment in schools in Shirqat the same month which indicated a severe lack of personal hygiene among boys and girls (600 cases of lice and 317 cases of scabies). Water-borne diseases are commonplace in Iraq with outbreaks often due to interrelated factors of low levels of water production, ineffective water treatment, lack of awareness of water-borne diseases and use of unimproved water sources. In Qayyarah, individuals share a community water point with their neighbors, often requiring women and girls to collect, transport and store water in jerry cans and other containers, resulting in contamination. Water Supply: According to DRC’s assessment, 73% of residents have domestic water at a rate of less than 50 l/p/d in Diyala, which is insufficient to cover daily needs in summer. According to the Dec. 2017 REACH Multi-Cluster Needs Assessment, 43% of households in Diyala rely on bottled water, a resource many cannot afford. Many households in Anbar, SAD and Diyala have connected their houses informally to the water network, resulting in reduced quality and quantity of supplied water. To try to meet water demand, water treatment plants are operating for double their designed operation duration, disrespecting pump rest times and increasing the rates of breakdown.A DRC assessment in Baiji in April 2018 found that most of the key water supply infrastructure components have been destroyed, damaged or looted. As a result, water supplies are offline or supplying unsafe water due to lack of adequate treatment, or contamination during distribution due to damaged water networks. The only reliable method for water delivery in Baiji is through private water trucking, which the most vulnerable populations cannot afford. In Qayyarah, safe water is trucked from a water filling point inside the camp, limiting the quantity to an average of 30 l/p/d, in line with the Iraq WASH Cluster standards but below the Iraqi standard (300 l/p/d). Water trucking inside the camp also poses child protection risks, as children are out of school due to the lack of schools and lack awareness of road safety, resulting in a tragic fatality in 2018.In Ninewa water sources have mostly been restored to operation, however, water quality and village pump stations are often overlooked. Water network leakages are estimated to be 70%, and significant works are required to improve water quality, village quantity and prevent water-related disease.

Activities

In line with the first WASH Cluster objective, DRC will continue to operate to sustain quality and standards of water and sanitation services and provide an enabling environment for good hygiene practices in camps through adequate WASH support in the Qayyarah Airstrip IDP Camp, where there is a high population turnover, expected to reach a population of over 75,000 individuals in 2019:- Capacity building and provision of tools for WASH committees- Daily water trucking for 9 months from the DRC-maintained water treatment plant (also supplying treated water to the host community and Jeddah 1-3 camps)- Desludging and provision of tools and equipment to maintain sanitation infrastructure through community volunteerism - Solid waste collection and disposal services- Construction of phase one of the water network: DRC, understanding that the Qayyarah Airstrip camp will remain, will construct phase one of the water network within the camp to phase out water trucking requirements, and provide additional in camp water storage to account for breaks in the supply of water from the treatment plant during times of high water turbidity.In line with the second WASH Cluster objective, DRC will assist highly vulnerable people out of camp through the restoration of basic water and sanitation services and support for good hygiene practices. DRC will facilitate handover of care and maintenance of water and sanitation facilities to local authorities in areas of return in Anbar, Diyala, SAD and Ninewa. DRC will focus on areas not covered by stabilization actors and consider historically underserved small urban towns, peri urban locations and rural populations with the following:- Regular monitoring of community health conditions through coordination with public health institutions to respond to localized water-related and hygiene issues. This includes a prepositioned capacity to respond to disease outbreaks such as cholera;- Behaviour change communication for improved hygiene in schools using the CHAST approach - Provision of hygiene kits for highly vulnerable families out of camps- Rehabilitation of war damage in water treatment plants, boreholes, pipelines, internal water networks, sewage and drainage networks and dump sites. DRC intends to consider use of the local labor and construction market through means such as vouchers, local purchase and local labor to install shelter solutions based on the findings of construction material and labor market assessments for works where appropriate. Gender, age and personal preference will be considered as well as feasibility.DRC intends to support the rehabilitation of WASH facilities in war-damaged public facilities to support the restart of the basic services provided there. This includes basic repairs to schools, public health facilities and community spaces to strengthen the sustainability of returns in the area. Where possible cash for work will be used to provide economic opportunities to the local population. This will be done in consultation with DRC's Shelter and Livelihoods teams, who will provide expertise on the design of the intervention, and the sites will be considered in alignment with Protection activities that provide community messages through these institutions.Household assistance to vulnerable families will include the position of water tanks, garbage bins, water connections and showers and toilets. This will be integrated with DRC Shelter programmes and be inclusive of HLP support for legal support to recover documents where required. DRC will provide support to the directorates of water, sewage and municipalities to strengthen their capacity to implement and return to operation, this will often be through the provision of targeted trainings, provision of tools and equipment and the repair of assets.- One-off solid waste clean-up campaigns to clear areas of accumulated garbage to initiate a more durable method to address the issue In order to respond rapidly to the humanitarian needs, DRC intends to establish Framework Agreements for works and services carried out under this programme where local business strengthening cannot be achieved due to lack of productivity in the local economy.

Mitigation Measures

1. DRC is registered in Iraq and has strong links and productive relations with authorities, particularly at the local level, which allows for a smooth implementation of projects. Regular engagement with these contacts inform them about the humanitarian principles surrounding our works in order to obtain buy in.2. DRC will work closely with the local authorities, host and beneficiary communities and through the humanitarian architecture to ensure that distributions meet safety and security requirements in the target location, including distributions in a safe and secure location and preventing beneficiary crowding at distribution sites. The RPA team, hosted by DRC can influence the decision to desist due to protection risks, and the results from DRC Protection teams ongoing protection monitoring influence all aspects of DRC programming.3. DRC work alongside colleagues at DDG, who will frequently undertake non-technical surveys in areas of infrastructure works and provide mine risk education (MRE) within communities particularly to pump operators. 4. DRC has an extensive security setup utilizing a low-profile approach. Furthermore, security and contingency planning is in place, and staff have the ability to remotely manage and support on-the-ground activities if necessary from the field offices in each of the project governorates and or area offices if staff are forced to evacuate. This setup allows DRC to continue operating even with the possibility of increased insecurity. If the project cannot be implemented in some locations due to access, these activities are in line with the strategy for other locations and can be diverted until a time that implementation in the original site is possible.5. DRC WASH teams operate in the same established locations as DRC Protection teams, both teams have promoted DRC's complaints and feedback mechanism, and IDP call center. All distributions are accompanied by IEC materials that promote the reporting of abuse cases and all post distribution monitoring questionnaires of WASH items include questions on PSEA and ensure that if issues were not prevented then they are followed up and that learnings inform the programme design.

How is your project accountable to affected population

DRC strives to be accountable to those whom we assist. We seek to achieve this through our Accountability Framework, Complaints and Feedback Mechanisms, and certification against international standards. DRC’s global Accountability Framework defines the organization’s commitments, that is, its aims, standards and procedures and how it ensures that it is accountable for them. DRC’s accountability framework comprises a global AF which applies to all international operations in DRC. DRC’s international operations were certified as compliant with the Core Humanitarian Standard, following an external audit in May 2017.A strong monitoring, evaluation, accountability, and learning system (MEAL) has been an integral part of all DRC interventions throughout the different phases of humanitarian response and other programming. DRC’s independent field-based MEAL Officers conduct assessments and monitor activities under the supervision of the MEAL Coordinator, in collaboration with the Project and Area Managers.A MEAL plan outlining outcomes, indicators, baseline, targets, activities, and status of implementation will be created to guide implementation. The MEAL plan will guarantee that the initial project proposal and logframe are not only adhered to in the process of implementation but done so in a timely, effective and efficient manner. A major part of the MEAL plan is beneficiary satisfaction, measured by an independent team. Households also take part in providing data to calculate the impact of the projects in terms of water availability, quality and quantity. Knowledge evaluation questionnaires show how successful the hygiene awareness component has been for men, women, boys and girls. The MEAL team manages the country-wide and multi-channel beneficiary feedback and complaint mechanism including a hotline, an email address, feedback boxes and mobile help desks at distributions. This mechanism provides project participants and other concerned individuals with different avenues to report complaints and submit suggestions.
