Project Summary

The project seeks to improve the health status of 40080 refugee and 10050 host community women and most at girls (engaged in commercial sexual activities) as well as their 4273 partners of both the communities in camps 1E,1W,2E,2W,3,4,5,6,7,8E,9,11,12,13,19 and KRC in Ukhiya and 21,22,24,25,26,27 and NRC in Teknaf and peripheral host communities of Teknaf and Ukhiya through delivery of quality sexual reproductive health services with special focus on family planning. As integrated services, the project plans to building capacity involving in psycho-social supports/social activities, treatment and management of Sexual Reproductive Health(SRH) services including ANC, PNC, family planning/contraceptive services, and support services to survivors of SGBV with appropriate linkages to other services through other sectors and/or service providers, Safe shelter support for GBV survivors, Screening and management of STI, Referral of complicated STI case, Conduct HIV Determine test, Linkage with ART center for HIV positive case, and Mental Health Psycho Social Support (MHPSS) services for men, women, boys and girls from both the host and refugee communities who are at higher risk of STI and HIV transmissions. The project also covers 10000 Adolescents of which 8000 from refugee and 2000 from host communities through awareness raising, referral linkages to WASH sector, and to integrate adolescent friendly services as this is a critical at-risk population. Where feasible, adolescent friendly spaces will be designated for adolescents to attend short sessions, separated by gender and age, on menstrual hygiene management, safe sex practices, early pregnancy, and the psychological and mental developments occurring at this age. Efforts to incorporate adolescent responsive services in other ways such as provision of hygiene kits shall be put in place as approval is provided.

1 - Identified Needs

As of August 2020 , it is reported that a total number of Rohingya Refugee 860000 are living in different congested camps and makeshift settlements with limited sexual reproductive health service facilities in Ukhiya and Teknaf. It is revealed that only 64,143 women of reproductive age are using long-acting, reversible contraceptives methods. According to SRH WG mid-year bulletin there is an overall decrease in the number of using family planning methods during second quarter. Long-acting reversible contraceptives are not widely available. As the target of facility based deliveries are 75%, extended family services is yet to be offered to the eligible couples especially in the camps. Therefore, the achievement is 53.2%. One of the most challenging barriers to scale up family planning methods is the socio-cultural conservativeness of the Refugee communities and lack of awareness. The unwanted pregnancy and birth rate are reported high among the refugee women. As different methods of family planning is yet to be extended to community family level, birth spacing and sexual reproductive health services are reported low. The community consultation conducted by Light House also reveals that vaccinations for both mothers and children as the target of coverage Penta 3 is >75%, in 2020 is only 25% of children completed measles 2 vaccination. There is apparent need to enhance timely childhood immunization uptake and awareness to protect against vaccine preventable diseases. It is also found that due lack of awareness and increase fear and decrease trust within the refugee community population, had a negative impact on reproductive health such as, family planning, birth spacing, ANC/PNC before services. In April, ANC visits dropped by 36.7%, and between January and June, institutional deliveries by 23% (SRH WG midterm bulletin, 2020). There are priorities to enhance communication with communities in all level to rebuild the trust and improve the access to health services including responding to COVID-19 pandemics. Due to pandemic situation and limited access to mobility of the women, negative impact on immunization family planning, maternal health and other NCDs are reported. Ensuring a strong referral pathway for obstetric emergency and other complicated cases there is a need to strengthen health Sector coordination, partner collaboration, information management and monitoring system for standardized and accountable health service delivery. As per the needs, the family planning services inclusive of other associated services to be extended to the beneficiaries. Following COVID-19 pandemic, COVID-19 awareness inclusive extended family planning services to be provided deploying PSEA sensitive CHWs and other staff.
