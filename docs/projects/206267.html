Project Summary

In 12 months, Reach Africa Organization(RAO) will ensure provision of shelter and nonfood items to an estimated 35,361 individuals (5,893 Households) amongst Internally Displaced Persons, Returnees and Vulnerable Host community in Ayod, Akobo, Fangak, Uror, Nyirol and Canal Pigi counties in Jonglei State. The modality of assistance is both in-kind and restricted Cash Voucher (CVA). Out of the 5,893 Households beneficiaries, 1,590HHs will receive SNFIs assistance through restricted CVA modality. A combination of In-kind and Restricted CVA will be conducted in Akobo, Ayod, Fangak, Nyirol and Uror, where some of the affected persons have access to competitive functional markets and people have meaningful access to the markets. Restricted CVA will be preceded with thorough market assessment, targeting market actors and households with a focus on protection risk analysis to ensure that safety concerns and any risks associated with CVA and appropriate risk mitigation strategies are set in place. The CVA assessments will be coordinated with the Cash Working Group (CWG) and transfer values of assistance will be calculated on the agreed Multi-Sector Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB). The proposed project will seek to aid in form of Kanga, blankets, cooking sets, Plastic sheets, non-food item bags, solar lamps and mosquito nets to the affected persons. Priority will be given to elderly (given their advanced age, poor economic status coupled with inability to actively engage in income generating activities); pregnant and lactating women (they are exposed to various risks such as sickness, malnutrition and protection thus making them more vulnerable compared to any other group); children; unintegrated persons; people with no community links; severely sick persons; and individuals living with disabilities. In this project, RAO will adhere to Shelter, Non-food Items guidelines and requirements as well as “Do-no-harm” principle while delivering assistance. RAO coordinate with local authorities, S/NFIs National cluster and focal point, humanitarian partners at the county and state levels to avoid duplication of activities and efforts. The intervention is integrated and will seek to link activities and beneficiaries to nutrition, health, education, protection, FSL and WASH. For durable solutions, community based S/NFIs committees will be trained and supported on effective, sustainable and equitable utilization of local resources to ensure disaster risk reduction and community based shelter and non-food items solutions. RAO will establish and train county based Housing, Property and Land Committees to advocate for non-violent resolution of housing, property and land issues facing targeted communities and access right. RAO is an active member of Shelter and Non-Food Items Cluster at the national and state level in Jonglei State. The organization has conducted SNFIs interventions in Ayod and Uror Counties through Rapid Response Fund and has exhausted its 2023 HRP target. RAO is the SNFIs focal point for Uror County and presence field presence in Pigi, Fangak, Akobo and Nyirol counties where it is carrying out protection and livelihood activities. In light of this, RAO has field presence, has established dependable community networks and is well versed with the social, political, cultural and security dynamics of the target locations.

1. Needs

According to the latest Integrated Phase Classification (IPC) data for South Sudan, Uror, Akobo, Ayod, Canal Pigi, Duk, Fangak and Nyirol are projected to remain in Integrated Phase Classification Four (IPC4) food insecurity or worse during the period December 2023-July 2024. In the same report, the six counties are expected to exhibit critical acute malnutrition (IPC AMN Phase 4 (Serious) or worse) during the same period. Due to food insecurity and malnutrition, humanitarian needs in these counties will remain severe with more than 25% of the population expected to meet their caloric needs through humanitarian assistance. According to IPC data and Humanitarian Needs Overview reports, food insecurity and malnutrition in these sic counties is induced by insecurity, funding gap, flooding, inflation and influx of returnees. The inter-sectoral analysis conducted using cluster people in need (PiN) reveals that more than 600,000 individuals will face extreme needs in the six counties in 2023 and 2024 (HNO, 2023). According to OCHA situation reports, flooding is expected to destroy crops while influx of returnees and displacement of populations will constrain existing resources in the six counties at a time South Sudan is facing a funding gap of 40% in 2023 (Reliefweb and Financial Tracking System data), with Uror, Akobo, Ayod, Canal Pigi, Duk, Fangak and Nyirol being some of the counties negatively affected. This implies 2023 and 2024 malnutrition and food insecurity programs in the six counties which are projected to be in IPC4 and IPC AMN 4 in December 2023 to July 2024 will be constrained leading to amplified humanitarian needs. In the latest IPC Analysis report covering December 2023 to July 2024, Uror, Akobo, Ayod, Canal Pigi, Duk, Fangak and Nyirol that are prone to annual flooding may be flooded within the projection periods leading to disruption of markets, supply routes and in worst case scenario affected communities losing household and shelter assets due to rising water. Already, flooding has submerged roads making it difficult for traders to restock markets with basic non-food items such as nails, timber, cooking and serving utensils, plastic sheets, kanga, mosquito nets and blankets. Where the items are available, the prices are prohibitively high and out of reach for the majority of the Internally Displaced persons, returnees and vulnerable and poor host communities (Joint Market Monitoring Reports 2023). As a result, IDPs, Returnees and vulnerable host communities are forced to borrow utensils for cooking from the few who have them, leading to delayed preparation of meals. Women and girls are forced to prepare food late into the night in open spaces with no lighting exposing themselves to GBV risks. Elderly persons, pregnant and lactating women as well as children are unable to access mosquito nets exposing them to malaria infection. Lack of shelters is exposing returnees to extreme weather and mosquito infestation leading to increased morbidity rates due to malaria and easy transmission of airborne diseases such as measles because of congestion. Women and girls who are living in open spaces or overcrowded lack privacy and are exposed to protection risks such as gender-based violence and abuse. Whereas the Chiefs, RRC, Returnees and host community representatives have been trying to resolve some of the Housing Property and Land issues, no due diligence had been conducted on HLP issues to enhance informed engagement with community leaders and members on HLP rights of the returnees. Occupation of idle land, abandoned shelters and competition for natural resources (forests, water points) is creating tension among owners, host community and returnees. Considering the acute needs faced by returnees and vulnerable households. RAO is proposing SNFI to at least 5,893 IDPs, Host community and returnee’s households, representing 35,361 individuals in six counties to enable affected persons to live a dignified life.

2. Project activities

ACTIVITY 1: Conduct Inception meetings to provide information on project objective, duration, anticipated results, kind of assistance to be provided, category of beneficiaries targeted in the project, modality of implementation, and role of community and project staff in the implementation process. ACTIVITY 2: Selection of local enumerators: Enumerators will conduct mobilization of beneficiaries, communication and sensitization, registration, setting up of distribution points, distribution of non-food items and post distribution monitoring. They will play the role of initiating community level engagements, mobilization of beneficiaries and stakeholders during planning, meetings, trainings, and project monitoring and evaluation. ACTIVITY 3: Selection of beneficiaries: RAO’s project staff will work closely with AAP committee, chiefs, local authorities, site managers, enumerators and existing partners to select 5,893 HHs among returnees, IDPs and host community who will benefit through distribution of S/NFIs. The selection criteria to be applied will include: Individuals classified as IPC 4 and 5, women headed households, and children headed households, elderly persons, severely sick persons, pregnant and lactating women, care givers of orphans, separated and unaccompanied children. ACTIVITY 4: Distribution of S/NFIs: RAO project staff will supervise setting up of distribution venues two days prior to distribution exercise. Each distribution point will be accessible by elderly persons, those living with disability and considered safe for women and girls. The venues will have gender disaggregated latrines, water points and safe spaces where pregnant and lactating mothers can be able to rest, feed their children. Public announcement will be made using public address systems in all distribution locations. On the day of distribution, enumerators will conduct demonstration on appropriate use of the SNFIs prior to handing over the items to beneficiaries. RAO will work closely with health partners who will conduct Measles messaging during distribution days. All distributions will be conducted during light hours to allow beneficiaries sufficient time to walk to distribution centres and return home during daytime. ACTIVITY 5: Conduct Post Distribution Monitoring: The PDM will be carried out using S/NFI’s cluster PDM household questionnaires which contains the question that assesses the appropriateness as well as beneficiary satisfaction with amount & quality of items distributed registration and verification processes. Respondents will be selected randomly among the beneficiary HHs with focus on the IDPs and vulnerable host communities. The project will employ a sample size of 20% (7,072 individuals) of the HHs who will benefit directly through the intervention. ACTIVITY 6: Conduct HLP analysis and awareness to clarify tenure arrangements and authentication of HLP ownership documents/alternative proof of ownership to mitigate HLP cases. ACTIVITY 13: Conduct a thorough market assessment, targeting market actors and households with a focus on protection risk analysis in Akobo, Ayod, Fangak, Nyirol and Uror Counties. ACTIVITY 14: Conduct community consultations with the market actors, local authority and SNFIs cluster to determine transfer values of assistance using agreed Multi-Sector Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB). ACTIVITY 15: Develop and distributed Restricted Cash Vouchers to 1,590 HHs beneficiaries. ACTIVITY 16: Conduct Post distribution monitoring of the CVA assistance.
