Project Summary

ACTED will deliver critical camp coordination and camp management assistance to vulnerable and conflict-affected persons living in camp settings in Mafa, Maiduguri and Dikwa in Borno State. The proposed Action will be delivered in order to deliver robust coordination and information sharing mechanisms at camp level in order to strengthen targeted and evidence based humanitarian intervention activities. The Action will further serve to implement critical risk preparedness, mitigation and response strategies, ensuring immediate access to life-saving assistance for IDPs in the event of a rapid onset emergency, through the development and implementation of emergency preparedness and response plans. The intervention approach is derived from the two core pillars of participatory governance structures in order to ensure the inclusion and elevation of beneficiary voice into humanitarian planning and response is systematically conducted, and on ensuring accountability to affected populations for the delivery of transparent and accountable humanitarian interventions. ACTED will thus deliver a comprehensive CCCM package through static and experienced Teams in the target camp locations, benefitting a total of 83,384 IDPs living in camp settings in Mafa, Maiduguri and Dikwa LGAs. In addition, ACTED will conduct mobile CCCM, enabling emergency response to informal settlements facing critical CCCM gaps, ensuring that basic coordination and management structures are in place. Mobile CCCM services will be delivered across the BAY states, anticipating to benefit up to 20,000 individuals.

1 - Needs

In Dikwa and Mafa, since January 2019, and in Stadium Camp in Maiduguri since April 2019, ACTED’s CCCM Team has conducted regular needs and site monitoring assessments, tracking prevalent gaps in service provision. Rapid assessments have also been conducted by ACTED’s CCCM Team in collaboration with ACTED’s Appraisal Monitoring & Evaluation Unit, and ACTED has also conducted a gendered assessment from 26 August to 6 September 2019, specifically assessing the situation for women and girls living in camps in Dikwa, Mafa and Maiduguri, targeting female respondents in 10 camps. The 2019 rainy season has exacerbated critical risks facing IDPs, with assessment findings highlighting flood and storm damage, loss of shelter and personal properties, latrines in urgent need of rehabilitation, over-congestion rates, and critical NFI needs. Based on these rapid assessments, in Dikwa, an estimated 7,000 IDPs are in need of emergency shelters assistance following flooding. The damage caused by floods throughout this season has increased the threat of sanitation and health crisis, as on 31 July, ACTED CCCM reported 269 damaged latrines due to heavy rain, with the Joint Assessment on 14 August placing this number at over 500. Chronic over-congestion rates continue to affect living standards in camps, compounded by high makeshift shelter rates. ACTED’s CCCM have recorded shelter conditions, and found high makeshift prevalence rates in Dikwa camps: in Shuwari 60%, in Kamchejin 70%, Modu Kasa 90% and in Mohamed Kyarimi 100%. In Mafa, ACTED’s CCCM team reports that over 1,400 households are living in makeshift shelters in the GGASS Camp, with 240 severely damaged shelters reported in October following the rain season. Further, in Mafa GGASS Camp, the number of active humanitarian partners has reduced from more than 20 to only 8 over the past 12 months, significantly reducing the ability for displaced and conflict-affected individuals to access emergency and life-saving assistance. Stadium camp in MMC was initiated in 2019 in order to address the large-scale influx of IDPs following ISWAP territorial gains in Kukawa LGA, resulting in over 34,000 newly arrived IDPs in MMC by mid-January 2019, and currently houses over 11,000 internally displaced and conflict-affected families. To ensure comprehensive assistance is available to all camp residents, strong coordination between government and humanitarian actors in the new site, establishing coherent humanitarian interventions is imperative. Further, ACTED’s gendered needs assessment and collected reports from beneficiaries via the CRM in 2019 reveals continued gaps, including critical NFI gaps, delays in WASH facility maintenance, and gaps in food security. Strong advocacy and inclusion of beneficiary voice into coordination mechanisms is imperative to ensure such gaps are targeted and addressed. Informal camps without access to critical CCCM services remain prevalent across Borno State due to limited humanitarian capacity and active agencies capable of delivering camp coordination and camp management services. Settlements without active CCCM agencies suffer from poor coordination of assistance programs, low functioning camp governance structures, and inability to advocate to the greater humanitarian response system for critical and life-saving humanitarian assistance for the targeting of unmet needs on the ground. It is estimated that over 120 informal settlements remain within Borno State alone, resulting in significant and exasperated vulnerability rates of camp residents. Low maintenance of camp facilities, combined with floods, over-congestion (in Dikwa), and unmet needs, exacerbates the vulnerabilities experienced by IDPs, resulting in sanitation and protection risks, and requiring a robust and coordinated emergency response, enabling the targeting of critical risks and gaps.

2 - Activities

The CCCM intervention is designed to ensure strong coordination structures and information-sharing mechanisms are actively engaged, in order to direct humanitarian system programs in IDP camps to target the most prevalent needs, and that interventions are evidence-based, through daily monitoring and analysis of conditions in camps, integrated with feedback provided by beneficiary communities through AAP mechanisms (CCCM camp CRM). ACTED’s CCCM approach is fundamentally derived from a community-led and community empowerment approach, ensuring capacity building, training, and technical backstopping support for community members to actively partake in humanitarian intervention activities. Further, ACTED’s CCCM intervention will aim to mitigate the most critical risks facing site residents, specifically by conducting camp planning and spatial layout to decrease fire risks and ensure response capacity, and through building capacity and awareness of site residents to minimize fire risks. Additionally, ACTED will develop an emergency preparedness and response plan at camp level, encompassing all humanitarian partners to ensure that critical risks and sudden onset emergencies can be responded to in a timely and efficient manner. To strengthen general protection of IDP camp residents, ACTED will conduct protection risk monitoring, identification, and referrals within camps in Dikwa, ensuring consistent flow of information, access to services, and to mobilise service providers to address protection risks and to ensure Do No Harm. Specific Activities include: * - Conduct regular site monitoring and profiling to facilitate information management, strengthen coordination of humanitarian partners, and to identify critical risks, needs, and service gaps: including baseline, endline, site profiling, demographic tracking, and conduct partner service mapping in order to identify critical needs and gaps.* - Strengthen coordination and camp governance mechanisms through participatory governance structures and communicating with communities (CwC): support camp committees in line with Sector standards, establish safe and accessible platforms for dialogue between IDPs and implementing partners, and to maintain a Complaints and Feedback Response Mechanism ensuring direct, secure, and confidential feedback loops for residents. * - Provision of essential services and site maintenance in target IDP sites to protect the safety and wellbeing of IDPs: identification and prioritization of urgent site care and maintenance needs in line with community and stakeholder priorities in order to increase equitable access, protection mainstreaming in camp settings, and mitigate critical risks. * - Implement mitigation, preparedness, and response to critical risks facing IDPs in the 8 IDPs sites in Dikwa, including fire, flooding, and public health risks: develop and implement an emergency preparedness and response plan (EPRP) inclusive of all actors active in the camps, strengthening coordination of preparedness and response capacity within the humanitarian system. Under the mobile CCCM component, ACTED’s dedicated and experienced CCCM Team will conduct response to under-served and vulnerable informal settlements that suffer from significant gaps in available CCCM services, resulting in diminished capacity to organize camp governance structures, critically low support for vital coordination of assistance programs, and inability advocate to the greater humanitarian response system for vital assistance. ACTED’s mobile CCCM Team will deploy to identified informal and under-served sites on a short-term basis in order to provide critical capacity building and technical backstopping to operational NGOs, and targeting camp residents, to ensure that basic camp governance and coordination mechanisms are set up. ACTED will conduct rapid assessment and site monitoring to ensure unmet needs are captured, and can be relayed to the greater humanitarian system.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

To ensure that IDP camp residents have access to safe, confidential and direct feedback loops to implementing partners and humanitarian stakeholders, and are able to hold service providers to account, ACTED’s CCCM Team will maintain the complaints and feedback response mechanism (CRM) in all target sites. The CRM consists of boxes installed in strategic locations within all sites, enabling IDPs and other stakeholders to submit complaints, requests, and suggestions, which will be collected, recorded, and referred as appropriate to relevant partners by ACTED. Additionally, ACTED CCCM will host one CRM desk in each of the target sites, ensuring illiterate members of the community will have equal access to the CRM. This will complement the central CRM desk hosted in ACTED’s CCCM office in Dikwa. The CRM is designed to provide a communication channel whereby IDPs can record complaints and suggestions, gaps, needs, challenges and any other concern of interest faced by the population in the camp. In this way, the CRM should give site residents a voice, with which they can hold humanitarian service providers to account. Anonymized and consolidated reports will be collated and shared with relevant implementing Sectors and partners, ensuring access to reliable information from the beneficiary communities on their greatest concerns. The mechanism aims to ensure consistent and accessible communication channels between IDPs and implementing partners, enabling informed planning of emergency response interventions in line with community-identified needs and gaps. Information gathered through the CRM will further inform ACTED’s CCCM Project Manager to conduct more accurate, data-driven advocacy efforts as needed, promoting targeted and evidence-based response from relevant Sectors. Further, ACTED’s independent AME Team will conduct a baseline, midline and endline in order to determine beneficiary perceptions on access to assistance, satisfaction with ACTED staff conduct.
