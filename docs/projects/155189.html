Objectives

1) Ensure effective, sufficient and equitable provision of life saving water and sanitation services for targeted women, men, girls and boys. 2) Ensure all targeted women, men, girls and boys have the means and are encouraged to adopt individual and collective measures increasing health seeking behaviours to mitigate public health risks for the well-being of the population. 3) Ensure that all WaSH assistance promotes protection, safety and dignity of targeted women, men, girls and boys with a focus on sustainable and inclusive approaches.

Needs

Despite the achievements of 2018 and the rapid response during the emergency phase, standards were still not consistently met and many refugees and host communities remain without adequate access to water. Water remains a constant challenge; the water table and topography continue to present a challenge as does the physical layout of the camps. Some areas remain without water such as Teknaf Sodor which struggles consistently. In addition, much of the infrastructure that was established during the emergency is not resilient, is often exclusive in its design and increases protection risks. This new phase aims at strengthening and maintaining the facilities in a more sustainable way and empowering the communities – through water management committees (WMC) – in maintenance and repairs. Data shows us that despite huge achievements being reached, still across all camps access to water can still be a struggle, with sources being too far away (23%), long waiting times (21%) and routes of access being barriers (20%) (REACH Data set). This supports the need for a focused response on ensuring access and inclusion for all across WaSH planning. Moreover we can see that the amount of water accessed per person per day is only just over half (56%) demonstrating the need for increased focus on more sustainable systems in geographically sound locations. Internal data for SI still shows that many households rely on unsafe/unprotected water such as pond water or unprotected boreholes for water that is then used for domestic purposes, some of which is not safely stored and some of which contains harmful bacteria. Equally many latrines are not being used because of protection issues for women and children resulting in high levels of open defecation for children and fear amongst female groups at riskier periods of the day such as night time. Open defecation also suggests that hygiene promotion activities are still not targeting correctly the younger population to stress the importance of using latrines. Soap also remains a challenge with many people reporting to SI that they have the knowledge of how to practise safe hygiene and sanitation but they lack the means. Suggesting an increased effort needed in ensuring items such a soap are reaching beneficiaries during distributions of wash items. Increased coverage is also required as large numbers of people report having never received these items. However, in line with ensuring dignity SI will support the move towards voucher or cash systems instead of in kind. The recent reporting of cholera, to which SI was a first responder, also demonstrates the increased need for knowledge and hygiene practices in the host communities and not just the infrastructure activities.

Activities or outputs

SI will continue its work from 2018 in improving the conditions of sanitation and hygiene in the areas of intervention (camps and host communities). SI will be aiming to provide a full WaSH package to those its targets including; bathing cubicles, latrines, handwashing points, desludging and hygiene promotion sessions at both household and community levels. In addition SI will continue to provide water for its target population (which normally exceeds those who receive full WaSH package), the project will aim to uphold standards of 15 litres per day per person. In addition, SI will continue to maintain, upgrade and develop new sustainable water systems in geographically viable locations. SI aims to use new and existing technology to make the most of the challenging water tables and unique topography. FSM sites have been the crux of SI works through 2018, and will continue to be a key focus through 2019. SI would like to build additional locations in camps that currently do not have these facilities, supporting the ongoing desludging that SI covers in all camps we have a presence in. New technologies are also being piloted and supported by SI to find unique ecological solutions to challenges like the water run-off from the sites, as well as testing whether the dried sludge could be used as compost within FSL activities as well supporting one aspect of the integrated approach planned in 2019. All new builds, rehabilitations and upgraded designs as going to be both resilient (thus including DRR planning) and inclusive (thus ensuring an integrated protection approach) to ensure that all WaSH services provided by SI are accessible to all. All activities will be following sector standards. In addition, as per SI mandate the activities will be covered in both camps and communities. However, to be able to retain focus on quality and quantity, SI will strategically withdraw from two camps in 2019 ensuring full handovers with other actors, so that it can instead ensure less people are reached with a higher quality output. To do this SI will withdraw from camp 26 and 22. The budget listed has been created based upon the sector standard of $174 per person in camps and $140 per person in host communities. As a final note, SI also intends to target WaSH in schools for Host communities, developing and enhancing infrastructure to ensure that children have access to water and sanitation. Important to note that some camps have been removed from this application, eg Camp 21 and 27, this is not because SI is not working in these areas, but because these camps are funded through UNHCR funding for 2019 and are therefore reported by UNHCR directly. The host communities have also been reduced due to budget allocation but again this is not reflective of SIs area of intervention.
