Project Summary

This project, as an implementation partner of IOM and aims to enhance equitable humanitarian support in camps 14, 15, and 16 focus on coordination, community participation, capacity building, DRR, climate change adaptation, Shelter/NFI, site improvements, and emergency preparedness. The aim is ensuring households live in safe, dignified shelters through Shelter Upgradation and Maintenance (SUM) and Emergency Support, particularly during monsoon seasons or in response to fires, landslides, or relocations. The cost of all activities includes operational and non-operation of SUM, new shelter, NFI, Emergency support, TDK will be covered by HEKS and materials will be provided by IOM pipeline.
The project planning focuses on the needs stated in the ISNA report, FY24 shelter standard assessments, and internal monitoring. The JRP-2025 includes essential initiatives, particularly regular shelter reinforcement mainly second phase of SUM for camps 14, 15, and 16, with a target of 10,500 HHs. After disasters, affected families will receive emergency shelter and NFI assistance for 10% of households (2,550 HHs) through Rapid Damage Verification (RDV). NFI distribution (2,550 HHs), solar light distribution and maintenance support (1,500 HHs), TDK (26,370 HHs), and construction of new fire and weather-resistant shelter prototypes (15HHs) if approved or mid-term shelter. Continued IOM treated bamboo support (5,000 poles). Families who received shelter support in FY24 will continue to receive regular assistance in FY25, but with priority given to those who did not receive support in FY24.
In camps 14, 15, and 16, catchment area point will continue to provide emergency support and referrals for shelter/protection referrals. Additionally, persons with disabilities and extremely vulnerable individuals, including the elderly will also receive porter and construction support. To minimize multi-hazard risks such as fire, floods, and cyclones, the Government and SCCCM sector is planning to introduce alternative fire and weather-resistant materials for shelters. If approved, HEKS will carry out this in its target camps in line with site plans to support fire breaks, using environmentally friendly and easily dismantled materials.
In alignment with DRR objectives, HEKS will execute out cash-based Shelter/NFI interventions, HHs level site improvements (stairs, retaining walls, slope protection etc) for 100 HHs, using non-durable materials. SI activities will be carried out by HEKS/EPER instead of the IOM pipeline. A community-based integrated approach will involve community members such as women, the elderly, youth, adolescent girls, boys, and persons with disabilities across all program phases (assessment, project design, decision making, implementation and monitoring). FY2025, attention will be given to engaging women and the elderly in light daily labor, as well as ensuring gender-segregated training and volunteer engagement. Special attention will be given to the Youth and capacity building initiative will be taken. The program aims to promote durable, safe, and environmentally friendly shelters while strengthening camp governance and fostering self-reliance and resilience.
The project is in alignment with the JRP'25 strategies and has committed to maintaining key policies such as PSEA, child protection, safeguarding, complaints/feedback procedures, Accountability to Affected Population, protection and gender mainstreaming, and gender-sensitive and transformative Shelter/NFI programming. Technical assistance for accessibility and extra resources for persons with disabilities will be coordinated with the ADWG/Protection Sector. Climate change mitigation efforts coordinated with the Energy and Environment network. Will maintain strong sectoral and stakeholder coordination with the Community, SCCCM sector, CiC, RRRC, CIMO, DC Office, Site Management including active participation in meetings and timely reporting, in order to boost overall project impact.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

In 2025, decreased funding intensifies challenges in camps, where shelter and NFI needs are urgent. Essential materials like bamboo and tarps require frequent replacement due to weather exposure and fire risk. Regular and emergency shelter support, including weather and fire-resistant shelter construction is essential to protect against cyclones, floods, landslides, and maintain safety and dignity for camp residents. Regular provision of shelter assistance through emergency responses, SUM, construction of new alternative shelter models are essential for maintaining decent living conditions. These measures protect the affected population, enhancing security, privacy, and health.The evolving roles of service providers, camp actors, and administrative policies have created a complex humanitarian environment that requires robust referral systems and greater access to services with active community engagement. Timely emergency shelter support and distribution of lifesaving NFIs remain essential for households affected by disasters; Regular and emergency shelter repair activities, listed as firewall priorities in the 2025 JRP, must continue to receive funding. The MSNA’23 found that 72% of households report shelter damage, but in FY24 with 41% still unable to ensure assistance, mainly due to a lack of humanitarian support. Shelter repairs are critical as alternative shelter options await large-scale approval.As of June 2024, only 38% of required funding for Shelter activities was secured, with 41% of households reporting receiving shelter materials a year ago. Shelter Performance Standard’24 report highlighted noting that only 29% of households have fire-protective walls, 30% has no sign of insect infestation in structural bamboo, and 16% has no rainwater entering through leaking. Although beneficiaries’ express satisfaction with shelter/NFI support, but over 40% indicated that shelter materials remain insufficient. ISNA’24 report found 40% of households indicate insufficient cooking space, while 19% noted an unmet need for cooking-related NFIs. Additionally, over 30% reported lighting issues and expressed a need for solar lamps or repair of lamps to ensure nighttime safety. The ISNA’24 report also highlights ongoing deficiencies in NFIs for affected populations, with a voucher or cash approach recommended to provide beneficiaries with choice of flexibility.Community engagement and participation require strengthening, as many reports poor experiences when raising issues, with limited involvement in decision-making, especially among women, youth, and individuals with disabilities. According to ISNA'24, 31% of households report not receiving follow-ups on complaints. Shelter activities in 2025 will ensure inclusion for women, men, girls, boys, LGBTI, and persons with disabilities, incorporating their feedback and boosting women’s cash-for-work and decision making and participation engagement beyond the current 53%. Refugees are still exposed to natural hazards, and in 2024, waterlogging, landslides and heavy monsoons were major concerns. The camps’ hilly terrain site improvements at the household level are one of critical need including drainage, retaining walls, pathways, stairs, and expected use of durable materials. To mitigate frequent hazards, including fires, floods, and cyclones, the Government and SCCCM Sector are planning to implement fire- and weather-resistant shelter materials in the camps. If approved, these environmentally friendly prototypes will allow for easy shelter dismantling and scaling, improving camp site planning, reducing high population density, law maintenance cost and ensuring equitable access to essential services. This new shelter models will also address security and protection risks, reduce environmental impacts, and mitigate natural hazard risks. Investing in resilient shelters now will ensure cost savings and sustain the refugee response amid diminishing humanitarian funding.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Protection mainstreaming will be effectively implemented through the engagement and accountability towards the affected populations ensuring minimum access, safety, privacy, and security in each stage of the relevant project cycle. Selection of Targeted households will be based on specific needs, assessment, referrals from protection sector, and/or concerns raised by FCRM platform. In compliance with the humanitarian response protection principles, ensuring data confidentiality will be prioritized, guidelines and policies of HEKS/EPER for Protection Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), Child Protection and Safeguarding, and the broader protection sector will be followed. HEKS/EPER will coordinate with the PSEA network, CPSS, GBVSS and protection sector through the designated focal point for PSEA. High emphasis on transparency and accountability towards community will be given. Particularly vulnerable groups will be the main focus, such as extremely vulnerable individuals (EVIs), female headed households, households with special needs (HHwSN) and LGBTQI. Initiatives such as stairs with handrails, ramps, partition walls, separate working crews for male and female, training rooms (with provision of a breastfeeding and child-friendly area inside the distribution point),and easy access for people with disabilities will be provided ensuring inclusivity. Training of beneficiaries will focus on the importance of partitioning in shelters for privacy and the knowledge of the FCRM system to refer any protection-related cases if needed. Shelter assistance is needed under the referral of a protection case,a dedicated HEKS staff member will supervise to provide tailored assistance. The project will ensure protection principles, through confidentiality, ensuring coordination with relevant sectors,and upholding transparency and accountability. The distinct needs of vulnerable groups will be met through customized activities, ensuring a comprehensive and inclusive approach.

2.3 - Age & Disability

HEKS/EPER strives to ensure that all groups within SNFI activities have equal opportunities by making sure that active discrimination is avoided through the provision of adequate information and participation in the decision-making processes. HEKS has a zero tolerance policy on the use of child labor (below 18) in the project activities, advocates for children’s rights and also ensures the safety of the children during construction and repair activities. Focus will also go to women who will have with children and pregnancy, elder persons, and persons with disabilities so that they can fully engage in coordination meetings, training, assessments, and decision-making processes of the projects, as inclusivity, Porter and labor support will be provided to the along with mentioned activities. While the formation of community committee participation will be equally consider the youth, women, and elderly people.HEKS values the necessity of SADDD to meet the needs of various groups. Community sensitization and messaging will prioritize the preferences of elderly people and adolescents, with their involvement in decision-making. Camp and host community volunteers and mobilisers will undertake comprehensive training on child safeguarding, child protection, PSEA, and the code of conduct in order to enhance risk mitigation. CFW opportunities will be available to individuals aged 18-59, with lighter tasks offered to those over 59 who are willing and able to participate. HEKS aims to enhance access to services for persons with disabilities through specific activities and referrals, coordinated with the Age & Disability Working Group. An inclusive budget will address the specific needs of persons with disabilities, and at least 3% persons with disabilities will be supported under the project. Persons with disabilities will receive support through disability-friendly access to shelters, facilities, including handrails and household-level improvements.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

HEKS/EPER is committed toward ensuring accountability to affected populations and stakeholders at all stages of the program cycle. In SCCCM activities, HEKS uses a community based approach in which the community participates in needs assessment, project planning, implementation, monitoring and sustainability. To foster transparency and accountability HEKS established camp level Feedback and Complaint Response Mechanisams (FCRM) to ensure gather and address of community feedback. Community members should be able to submit their concerns through feedback boxes, hotline numbers, or FCRM help desks. Submissions are verified by the Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) team alongside FCRM and safeguarding focal points, as well as key community personnel before results are communicated with the complainant. Consistent with CHS standards, HEKS ensure its program plans to the community before the planning of any project activities. Current JRP, the project is based on participatory assessments and joint analyses, e.g. ISNA-2024. We share project information with stakeholders through inception and lessons-learned meetings. The project is evaluated by means of annual consultations, and information obtained during these consultations is utilized in lessons-learned workshops. In order to ensure that the community has an adequate understanding of the materials, all the communication materials (IEC) distributed by HEKS are in English and Burmese. The FCRM and safeguarding focal points verify referrals from Site Management/Protection teams or project participants and communicate decision to Site Management/Protection teams to relay to beneficiaries. Coordination shall be ensured at camp and sector level to achieve the harmony and transparency. HEKS is committed to ensuring that the community is well-informed about the FCRM process, with the MEAL team actively working to promote understanding and accessibility of these mechanisms.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

HEKS/EPER has established a strong Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policy that applies to all stakeholders, including but not limited to beneficiaries, community mobilizers, staff, guards, vendors, donors and partners, and others. PSEA-related issues are given attention in every project HEKS implements, with determination to integrate PSEA strategies into HEKS’s projects and full compliance with the PSEA sector operational directives. HEKS will ensure that there will be regular training conducted with the aim of ensuring that 100% of project personnel (those in the volunteers, staffs, senior management) as well as relevant external stakeholders (vendors, suppliers, third parties) are reached with mandatory PSEA training. Apart from this HEKS will try to engage more female staffs in the team to keep strong focus on PSEA. This will also target direct benefices with a budget for PSEA awareness and training making it more extensive. Complementing PSEA messages, materials like Posters, flyers will be made available in distribution points, training centers, and offices. In addition, separate training rooms as well as additional desks will be set aside strictly for enhancing awareness on the issue. In project areas, HEKS will create an enabling environment in respect to communication and will setup a Complaint Feedback Mechanism (CFRM). PSEA referral and complaint mechanisms will be made available during all interventions and training. A designated focal point for PSEA and risk management will be responsible for these important aspects and reporting. In the management of complaints, HEKS is committed to confidentiality, accessibility and cultural appropriateness. HEKS is also ready to address any incident of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) according the standard operating procedure for SEA complaint referral in respect of the rohingya response. Such extensive measures are intended by HEKS to help to prevent and address any instances of SEA.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes. The interventions by HEKS/EPER mainly shelter, site improvement, and NFI interventions prioritized DRR, climate change mitigation and adaptation strategies. In alignment DRR/DRM, to the HEKS has come up with few activities such as HH level SI, options for alternative shelter materials instead of bamboo and tarpaulin dependency, DRR principles orientation to the targeted beneficiaries by training and orientation, reuse of the old materials etc. Considering climate change mitigation measures HEKS has considered the above activities and has a priority to contribute on climate change mitigation measures. The community committees in each block/sub block shall be formed, consulted and engaged at every stage of the project cycle such as but not limited to risk assessment, site selection, need assessment, implementation, monitoring and strengthen technical know how and share DRR measures. If any new fire- and weather-resistant shelters introduced at sub-block levels will serve as firebreaks, while innovative shelter designs and site planning address the camp’s space limitations. Also proposed newly introduced shelter materials by SCCCM sector high longevity, environmentally sustainable materials long process of production and environmental impact of the materials values is high as well as for the reuse scope. Regular repair and maintenance purposes provision of treated bamboo will be continued to extend the life span of bamboo. Special attention will be given on energy wastage management which includes the less use of energy and solar lighting repair initiative will be implemented. The field team will provide technical support as needed and conduct follow-up to ensure community committees are effectively managing relevant activities at the block/sub-block level. All SNFI interventions along with new construction will follow updated site plans to mitigate hazards, and coordination with the EEN network and relevant stakeholders will ensure alignment with best practices.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes. HEKS/EPER’s shelter, site improvement, and NFI interventions are closely aligned with Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) efforts. Major priority EPR activities within the SCCCM sector activities, include household-level site improvement (SI), distribution and installation of Tie-Down Kits (TDK) with technical support, distribution support of emergency shelter and NFIs, porter support to EVHHs, and special need households, Rapid Damage Verification (RDV), and shelter materials provision. In partnership with IOM, HEKS responsible as the catchment area focal point for Camps 14, 15 and 16. HEKS is responsible for implementing and coordinating EPR measures, with IOM providing contingency resources. Emergency preparedness and response training for each block/sub-block and forming of Community Committees in Emergencies will be organized by HEKS for shelter interventions. A designated project budget will support these emergency preparedness and response measures. HEKS will collaborate with the EPR Working Group (EPR WG) to align strategies and enhance overall EPR synergy. The catchment area focal point will continuously liaise with camp management, site management, beneficiaries and partners and stakeholders for appropriate emergency response coordination and to avoid any form of assistance overlap. All reporting to the ISCG, SCCM sector will be coordinated by HEKS through disaster risk informed approaches supporting the effective EPR and all its components. Additionally, the emergency response team, along with beneficiaries, will participate in regular training and awareness sessions to strengthen preparedness and resilience.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes. This project is closely driven by the principles of youth engagement in that young people will participate fully in the entire program cycle from the design stage to implementation and monitoring. HEKS/EPER focuses on the contributions of the youth in humanitarian actions especially in SNFI interventions where being demand driven and youth adaptability are of main concern. Appreciating this aspect, the concern for youth development promotes participation of youths within the age bracket of 14-17 as community influencers to share innovative insights related to SNFI approach and 18-24 as community mobilizers in conducting shelter needs assessment and solutions and field monitoring. This way, youth involvement is encouraged at HEKS where it allows access and involvement of focused services and strategies that protects the youth even in the shelter and settlement programming. This is geared towards creation of awareness within the communities with regards to social cohesion and peacebuilding within shelter focus especially activities that utilize the youthful population. Young women receive attention because they are more exposed to violence in emergencies. Involvement of such young women is made possible by the provision of special orientation and formation of separate groups. Program also provides for training of the staff and other stakeholders on how to effectively involve the youth who are critical to the program’s success, considering factors such as young people’s energy, leadership and creativity. Because there is an age and gender based data collection, the project is able to mainstream youth issues in its activities and stratifies its activities based on gender. In engaging young people as both beneficiaries and actors of change, HEKS/EPER creates an opening for young people to play a role in the future of their communities in a way that mitigates their risk and facilitates their ongoing participation in the process of rebuilding their own communities.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

HEKS/EPER is actively committed to supporting refugees by fostering self-reliance and resilience at this stage of the response. The emphasis shall be on enhancing capacities in DRR through the shelter interventions. These interventions are meant to satisfy the immediate needs of the refugees but also the need for equipping them with the skills and knowledge that will enable them to be resilience in the long run. The project will also focus on mobilizing refugees of the target areas as volunteers. Involving them in the activities will encourage and enhance ability to make their own decisions at the community level. This is not only helps improve the resilience of the community but also gives them a sense of ownership. Through this integrated strategy, the project aims to enhance the community's capacity to respond to and recover from future shocks. HEKS/EPER makes sure that its programs do not harm host growth and in fact allows refugees to participate in improving their own destinies.
