Project Summary

SOP will conduct protection project for 12 months and will take place in two WGSS with child friendly spaces in two sub-districts in Idleb GOV: Sarmada and Maarrat An Numan districts. The project activities will be:
•conduct protection activities by supporting financially and technically of WGSS in Sarmada, Maarrat An Numan districts
•conduct outreach (non-specialized) activities and center-based including (recreational activities, life skills activities, individual and group counselling (non-specialized), Psychological First Aid (PFA) Which will target hosting and IDPs (men, women, boys and girls) in focusing on vulnerable groups like (PWD, orphans without bread winner, women headed houses, elderly, etc
• provide GBV specialized services like (CM, PSS activities and referral pathway) for men, women, boys and girls in focusing on adolescents and PWD. Conduct capacity building training activities include GBV and specialized actors. Moreover, the trainings may include CMR, SOPs, MISP, GBV and GBV mainstreaming. SOP has excellent capacity to conduct protection projects and has access to the targeted area.
SOP will ensure that children, including children with special needs in Sarmada, Maarrat An Numan, can join child-friendly spaces, which provide comprehensive child protection services and are physically safe. The organization will use these spaces to identify children with special needs who need constant follow-up to achieve greater integration with the other children. In order to achieve this, SOP will improve access to the facility for persons with disabilities such as installing slopes, lateral side ladders and other required modifications. The project will also provide recreational activities and awareness raising and form community committees to activate the role of young adolescents in the society. Parenting session will be provided to both men and women in the targeted societies to mainstream child protection concepts and contribute to reducing the risks of child abuse.
SOP has an independent M&E team, the M&E team will monitor all project’s activities. Monitoring activities will include ongoing assessment and review of the progress being made toward the achievement of intended project outcomes against the project logical framework and budget. Continuous programmatic review alerts staff to needs and opportunities for adjusting strategies, inputs or systems and for providing technical assistance to the centers.

Needs

Regarding protection project, based on result of two projects conducted by SOP these year 2018 in the same WGSS AND CSF centers in Ariha and Teftnaz, the result reveals that the GBV, particularly verbal harassment, domestic violence (including family violence against women and girls), child marriages and the fear of sexual violence, continue to be part of the lives of women and girls in Syria inside and outside the home, resulting in very few spaces where women and girls feel safe. Moreover, the most vulnerable groups are persons with disabilities and the children without bread winner, People affected physically by explosive weapons can present different symptoms of distress including physical reactions (fatigue, tightening in throat, headache etc.), emotional reactions (irritability, anxiety etc.), cognitive reactions (confusion, recurring nightmares, difficulties concentrating or making decisions etc.), and other difficulties (sleep problems, crying easily, hyperactivity, hyper vigilance, social withdrawal etc.).The SOP has reached to more than 50,000 beneficiaries (men, women, boys and girls) in Ariha, Teftnaz districts through prevention and specialized services including( awareness, PSS and specialized activities). Furthermore, we reached to 872 case management (423 women, 240 boys and 209 girls) during 12 months. Sustained access remains challenges in some areas. Distance to service delivery points and lack of transportation, especially in rural areas, family restrictions and a lack of trust or fear of stigmatization are the main barriers for women and girls to access GBV services. While more disclosures of violence are taking place in Syria, the shame and stigma surrounding GBV and in particular sexual violence continue to contribute to survivors not talking about violence when it happens.Through study done by ACU 2018 identified some difficulties that prevented children (girls and boys) access to education in the camps that contain a school. Financial and material difficulties were the most important among the assessed schools. The most influential factor regarding student dropout was child labor because extreme poverty pushed 40% children who dropped out of school to work to help their families. Moreover, extreme poverty prevented parents from sending their children to schools in 21% of dropout cases. The lack of stationary accounted for 15% of dropout cases. The reasons also included lack of certification, school unavailability, or the bad conditions within schools if available, in addition to a social reason, early marriage which accounted for 5% of dropout cases especially for girls between 14 and 18 years old. In normal conditions, children (girls and boys) need psychological counseling in school. This need increases exponentially in war time, especially when, “nearly 3 million children (girls and boys) in Syria have never known peace and suffer from psycho-social distress as a result of experiencing the horrors of war.” Consequently, teachers noted the spread of several psychological cases among students including introversion, shame, violence, and depression. we noticed the need for group and individual PSS activities for children and the need for referrals to specialized services. The most vulnerable groups are children with disabilities and the children without bread winner. Children with disabilities will have improved access to the services after the rehabilitation of WASH facilities and the center infrastructure to be disability-friendly. Children with disabilities will be better integrated with their peers after these improvements. The team noticed that most children who come to the center suffer conflict-related cognitive and emotional disorders (confusion, recurring nightmares, difficulties concentrating or making decisions etc.), and other difficulties (sleep problems, crying easily, hyperactivity, hyper vigilance, social withdrawal etc.).

Mobile resources to reach out and overcome mobility challenges, as well as a gender-balanced staffing able to overcome cultural and other barriers will be critical for implementation. The GBV AoR will continue to provide services through an integrated GBV/RH approach and will advocate for increasingly integrated GBV/RH services to expand entry points for survivors and improve overall access to GBV services without fear of stigma from communities or family members.
