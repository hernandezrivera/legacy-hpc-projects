Project Summary

This project will cover integrated Protection , GBV, and CP and health promotion /awareness respond to protection needs for 7260 among South Sudanese and non South Sudanese refugees and their hosting communities who faced threats of physical violence, exploitation, abuse, neglect in particularly to the current economic and health deterioration in light of COVID 19 in Khartoum, White Nile and North Darfur states. NADA embraces community based protection approach by building capacity of targeted communities through equipping them with necessary skills and knowledge to implement community driven solutions to their local communities. The project will carry out extensive consultations with local community and line ministries as an entry point for smooth project implementation. The project will respond to urgent protection needs by providing psychosocial support to extremely Unaccompanied and Separated children among the emergencies affected population. The intervention will enhance local capacity on CP and GBV through training of 15 community based protection networks (CBPNs), establishment of referral mechanisms, provision of direct support including case management for UASC and GBV /SGBV survivors distribution of personal dignity hygiene kits (PHKs) The project will link the protection, health and legal response needed by GBV survivors through provision of case management and referral services.. The Project team ,social workers and active CBPNs members will identify people of concerns and at risk of violence (including sexual and gender-based violence), exploitation neglect or abuse, in particular UASC and survivors of GBV and work closely with range of stakeholders, particularly in coordination with FRT coordinator appointed by State Council for Child Welfare (SCCW), specializing in family tracing and reunification in identifying UASC who wish to reunify with relatives within Sudan, relatives in other countries. The project also will advocates with national service providers and support for refugee families to obtain birth certificates, for both new born children and for late registration procedures especially for South Sudanese refugees. Project team will conduct services mapping and organize monthly meetings with relevant partners and services providers to ensure better access of the People of concerns including GBV survivors to other services as a number of 510 GBV/SGBV cases will be identified and referred , 1500 will be referred to other services while 100 SGBV among PoC will be provided by direct assistance including case management. 5000 women and girls in Khartoum, White Nile and North Darfur will receive direct support through provision of PHKs. The project team will organize monthly coordination meeting with line ministries, health care units, community leaders and other services providers regarding the coordination and strengthening the reporting and referral mechanism. Project team will provide monthly awareness raising /PSS sessions through microphone in the public places such as community/women centers to ensure social distancing. 50000 individuals will be reached with awareness raising messages on CP, GBV, environmental concern and COVID 19 risks and prevention methods through 22 mobile public awareness campaigns. The project will consider APP through community participation on conducting needs assessments and implementation of project activities

1 - Needs

The political instability and poverty in South Sudan will continue to force approximately 1.3 million refugees to seek asylum in Sudan. Additionally, factors which contributed to political instability and malnutrition are continued, and this may increase influx of South Sudanese refugees to Sudan. Despite the fact that, Sudan maintained opened doors policy for welcoming refugees but refugees are faced by critical challenges including the congestion at reception and transit centers, and the high start-up costs to establish services and basic infrastructure in new refugee settlements, which extend over vast areas. In addition, limited livelihood opportunities, high rate of unemployment, human trafficking and smuggling and grave violation of human rights effect their living conditions, dignity and well being. Various needs assessment reports have shown that most of refugees, particularly women and girls are vulnerable and under-served with regard to basic services. The refugees face major challenges in accessing the limited available services. This is due to many factors, including information gaps with regard to available services, absent of operationalized referral pathways and limited basic services and infrastructures in targeted locations. Refugees struggle to meet their basic needs in an increasingly difficult economic environment, with limited access to livelihood services and assistance. They suffer from limited access or sometimes lack of educational opportunities or vocational skills training, making it difficult for them to improve their living conditions. Women and children are particularly vulnerable in this regard, as they lack the support of structure and protection of a family. They are at a heightened risk of violence, exploitation, neglect and abuse. Girls and boys continue to be exposed to distress, gender-based violence (GBV) and other forms of violence, abuse and exploitation. Children have been separated from their families on an unprecedented scale. Therefore, it's crucial to respond to this emergency by principled and integrated intervention. The intended objective of the intervention is to ensure safe and protected environment for refugee at risk and/or with specific needs, sexual GBV survivors, female households, unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) and persons with special needs through provision of integrated protection assistance including gender-based violence and child protection.In light of absent of support structure and protection of families, beside current situation of COVID -19, refugees suffer to meet their basic needs and have limited access to livelihoods, services and assistance, women and children are at heightened risk of harassment, exploitation, neglect and abuse. Refugees lack development recreational or social activities which are vital for their well-being. The major challenge in addressing the psychosocial needs of refugees is their knowledge regarding their psychological problem. This requires additional attention to improve the living condition and well being of this category.

6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The integrated protection project will adopt community based approach that promotes the active engagement and participation of all community segments in the design and implementation phases to ensure the community by-in and sense of ownership. The project will strengthen the capacity of 15 Community Based Child Protection Networks who will take the lead and core responsibility of the project implementation. NADA team will ensure that the (CBCPN) composition’s considers balanced representation of gender, age, social status and minority to improve the decision making and allow the voices for these groups. The CBCPN will receive training on different protection issues as well as ToT to be able to contribute to sustainability of the protection awareness and capacity among the local community by provision of protection training for community volunteers. This will further support the self-protection capacity of the target communities to run a concrete platform to identify their concerns, needs, challenges and design and implement needed actions to improve the protective environment. To ensure accountability and open channels of communication, a community based complaint mechanism and complaint box will be established through the project.
