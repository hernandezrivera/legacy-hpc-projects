Project Summary

The project aims to enhance resilience, livelihood & improve food security of 1778 ‎farmer's HHs with capita of 8712 (2387 women, 2405 men, 3659 children, 261 ‎elderlies) in vulnerable localities that are threatened with confiscation and settlers’ ‎attacks in Bethlehem, Nablus and Salfit governorates.‎
The design of the project relayed heavily on the Food Security Sector “FFS” Hamartian ‎Needs Overview “HNO”, OCHA’s 2022 Multisectoral Needs Assessment “MSNA”, the ‎available data by other national and international NGOs and bodies, and on need ‎assessments conducted by PARC and ACPP. The project was designed based on the ‎Humanitarian Right Based Approach, in which those in the most marginalized situations ‎who face the biggest barriers to realizing their rights were prioritized.‎
The project is directly aligned with the 3rd SO of the HRP strategy in Palestine, and the ‎FSS HRP 2023 objectives, mainly with the 1st and 2nd objective.‎
The project focused on the governorates that are affected by settlements and settlers’ ‎attacks in the WB. For instance, Nablus is surrounded by 13 settlements and 37 ‎settlements outposts, in 2021 and 2022 around 13,193 in Nablus were injured due to ‎settlers’ attacks, Nablus encountered many episodes of confiscation of Agri-lands and ‎heavy expansion of settlements which led to violent escalations harming local ‎communities. While 70% of Salfit’s land was either confiscated or will be confiscated by ‎the occupation to build more settlements, as currently Salfit is surrounded by 25 ‎settlements that are inhabited with nearly 100 k settlers, and residents in Salfit suffer ‎from settlers’ attacks and the occupation’s unhumanitarian measures.‎
Furthermore, Bethlehem has around 18 settlements that are inhabited with 105k ‎settlers, these settlements separate Bethlehem residents from accessing their lands ‎and livelihoods, and during 2021 and 2022 there were around 946 injuries due to ‎settlers’ attacks there.‎
Through the various need assessment and consultation in each governorate; the ‎localities and the needs in each locality were identified. The proposed ‎project will have two main components
Firstly, agricultural assets and productive capacities of farmers and damaged ‎productive restored.‎
This component focuses on enhancing access of the targeted beneficiaries to ‎productive assets mainly land and water and increasing their productive capacity ‎through provision of inputs and seedlings.‎
Secondly, vulnerably groups in targeted localities increase their economic capacity to ‎meet food security needs. This component invokes the CFW modality and ‎aims at creating a multiplier effect by hiring people who are food insecure and ‎vulnerable in each locality to work on rehabilitation the agriculture roads in return for ‎cash.‎
The project adopts human rights-based, gender mainstreaming and do no harm ‎approaches with respect to core humanitarian standards. To ensure accountability and ‎community engagement project team will announce and formulate Local Committees ‎‎(LC). The project is gender sensitive and takes into consideration the special barriers ‎that face women; in the need assessment workshops women participation was ‎mandatory, and the workshops took place at a time and place that were convenient to ‎women. The needs and fears of women that were stated in the need assessment and ‎in MSNA report were also taken into consideration, and embedded in the project.‎
Furthermore, PARC’s partner ACPP will play a role in documenting the human rights ‎violation and conducts the fast dissemination to international community.‎
Furthermore, while developing the project, MoA was consulted to ensure that no ‎duplication in efforts take place. As indicated in OCHA’s coordination mechanism, the ‎planned activities in addition to targeted locations were send to MoA to ensure no ‎duplication of efforts. While implementing the project MoA will be in LC and the project's steering committee.

1 - Needs

While designing the project, PARC relied on its accumulative experience, lessons ‎learned from OCHA-HF funded projects, FSS HPC need analysis 2023, FSS ‎humanitarian needs 2023, OHCA statistics, OCHA MSNA report and various need ‎assessments to determine the needs of the beneficiaries. Part of the desk review ‎included OCHA’s reports that highlight the vulnerability of targeted communities in ‎Area C. As stated in FSS HNO 2023, communities in Area C are faced with the ‎demolition and confiscation of agriculture structures, and restrictions on the ‎maintenance and rehabilitation of productive assets,‎movement restrictions, and settlement expansion, all contribute to livelihood instability and deterioration of the ‎socioeconomic environment. As of Sept. 2022, more than 220 agriculture structure were demolished, which ‎represent more than 34% of the total structures demolished. In addition, to the destruction of the farmers productive ‎assets through confiscation and demolitions, farmers dignity and livelihoods are also threatened by the Israeli settlers' ‎violent practices, which often results in Palestinian injuries and casualties, or damages to Palestinian property, ‎including vandalizing trees, crops, and other agricultural properties and assets. The project’s design also took into ‎consideration the effects of the war on Ukraine had on Food security in terms of access and price of inputs for ‎agriculture process. PARC followed a rigorous process to determine the targeted localities and the needs for each ‎locality. Firstly, PARC conducted desk research and various internal meetings with PARC’s branch managers and ‎staff in each governorate to determine the priority governorates; based on the governorates’ ‎vulnerability status, proximity to settlements and the effect of settlers’ attacks and the ‎occupations measures on their livelihoods. As a result, Bethlehem, Salfit, and Nablus ‎were chosen. Secondly, PARC conducted meetings with various stakeholders working in ‎these governorates “governmental bodies, Grassroot organization, other NGOs”, and ‎numerous rapid need assessment (RNA), to identify targeted localities and their needs. ‎The need assessments aimed to determine the needs of all vulnerable groups with ‎special attention to women, children, elderly and People with Disability. The need ‎assessment ensured participatory and human rights-based approach in its ‎methodology which was carried out via desk review, phone interviews, meetings with ‎relevant stakeholders, women clubs, and focus groups discussions with beneficiaries. 23 need assessment workshops and meetings took place- in which 138 individuals ‎participated ( from which 31 were women); Furthermore, special attention was given to ‎women and their needs; during the FGD with women farmers, the majority confirmed ‎the need to focus on road, animal barn, and provision of production inputs. As road ‎rehabilitation will ensure their accessibility to agricultural land since women participate ‎with men farmers in collecting crops especially during olive harvest. Increase protection ‎since women will not be forced to walk long distances to reach their lands but can use ‎personal cars. At a community level, road rehabilitation enhance access to ambulances ‎and firefighter trucks. In regards to barns, women confirmed that rehabilitation will ‎facilitate their working conditions in the animal barns and minimize efforts. 23 ‎localities with 130K residents were chosen, the localities particularly suffer from settlers ‎and settlements, as there are 70 settlements and military outposts in these localities, ‎around 100k dunums of agriculture land is not being used due to building settlements, ‎land confiscation to build natural reservice (za’tara in Bethlehem and Duma in Nablus).‎Destructions of agricultural assets like burning of agriculture land, and vandalism is alsorampant in these localities. ‎Bethlehem Governorate The proposed project aims to address the needs of the ‎targeted location to ensure that their food security, livelihood and assets is protected ‎and enhanced through increasing the beneficiaries access to their lands and natural ‎resources (agricultural land and road rehabilitation, rehabilitation of water wells, ect.). ‎Also, in Bethlehem governorate grapes are a source of livelihood for farmers, ‎hence rehabilitation of grape vine structure will be provided Nablus Governorate The ‎proposed project aims to address the needs of the targeted location to ensure that ‎their food security, livelihood and assets is protected and enhanced through increasing ‎the beneficiaries access to their lands and natural resources (agricultural land and road ‎rehabilitation, rehabilitation of water wells, ect.). Lastly, through using the CFW modality in these localities, in which marginalized and vulnerable skilled and ‎unskilled labor will work for cash. ‎Salfit Governorate The following activities will take place; enhancing access to lands ‎and productive assets, through agriculture land rehabilitation, land fencing ‎rehabilitation and opening of agriculture roads, and distribution of olive picking ‎machines and inputs, and grass cutting machines. Improving access/ storage of water ‎resources, through rehabilitation of agri-cisterns wells, and distribution of water tanks. ‎Lastly, employing the CFW modality in these localities, marginalized and ‎vulnerable skilled and unskilled labor will work for cash. Some localities dependent on ‎olive crop production as a primary source of livelihood, however, some of the olive ‎groves are located in restricted areas that farmers are only able to access for a limited ‎number of days during olive picking season. Olive picking machines will expedite the ‎olive picking process and reduce the numbers of days farmers spend in these groves, ‎which can help increase production and mitigate the risk of attacks by Israeli settlers in ‎these areas. Olive picking machines and input will be provided

2 - Activities

In total the project will benefit 1,738 farmers households in the targeted localities (8,516 individuals; males: ‎‎2,350, females: 2,333, children: 3,577, elderly: 255). ‎ In each locality 2 main activities will take place; ‎ Activity 1: Forming local committees (LC) in the targeted communities.‎In each locality LC will be formed. The LCs will include representatives from PARC,MoA, local ‎governmental body, grassroots organization and farmers. Proper representation of women in the local ‎committees will be encourged to ensure gender mainstreaming, and engagement of women in the ‎decision-making process.‎ Activity 2: Selection of beneficiaries.‎The selection criteria will be developed by the projects staff in collaboration with the LCs, ‎and open call for the project will take place at each targeted locality to ensure maximum exposure and ‎outreach. In these announcement sessions, PARC’s complaint mechanisms and safeguarding ‎policies, will be shared with the local community, to ensure safeguarding their dignity and to abide by the ‎do no harm value.The project’s staff will also disseminate and raise the beneficiaries’ awareness about WFP’s PSEA mechanism‎‎ and the communication channels. Prior ‎to the beginning of the project PARC staff will be trained on PSEA so they are better able to‎ prevent, detect and respond to such cases. ‎The location and time of these session will take into consideration the social barriers ‎that women face, and will be scheduled in a way that ensures their inclusion.‎In each locality the beneficiaries will be chosen based on the following selection criteria (will be further ‎developed by the project staff) and in alignment with the identified needs for each locality. ‎‎-‎Resident in the targeted localities. ‎‎-‎Practice agriculture as main source of income for their family. ‎‎-‎Families that lack of water sources and that trucked water prices are notably high. ‎‎-‎Women led households, families with PWDs and large families will be given priority. ‎‎‎‎-‎ the criteria will also include vulnerability due to loss of assets due to outstanding debts or other shocks; or due to any natural or man-made disaster (stormy weather and ‎demolitions/evictions, violations of IHL). ‎Once the main 2 activities are conducted, the following will take place in each governorate;‎Bethlehem: ‎‎A1. 9.1c - How will feedback be used?

PARC has a well-articulated feedback and response mechanism (FRM) ‎put in action from the design phase until closing. FRM at PARC is ‎ensured with respect to Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS). The CHS ‎adopted by PARC will ensure to provide a safe/dignified space for ‎targeted population to express their feedback and complaints. PARC ‎has an updated accountability policies (information sharing, complaints ‎and feedback and participation) and the Safeguarding policy. ‎Accordingly, PARC consulted with local communities to identify the ‎most appropriate channels suitable for them. Hence, a dedicated email ‎‎(Complaints@pal-arc.org), complaint boxes distributed across the ‎governorates, in addition to the MEAL Officer phone number (Whats ‎App). These three channels will be utilized to receive feedback and ‎complaints. Then, based on PARC accountability policies the complaint ‎shall be registered into a well-designed complaint form that will be ‎tracked and documented through the MEAL documentation system.‎‎ Additionally, all project activities will be followed by the MEAL officer ‎within the project MEAL framework. With reference to CHS, complaint ‎mechanism will be elaborated at targeted communities and audience ‎will be encouraged to claim their rights. Field staff will announce the ‎availability of the three feedback mechanisms for the local ‎communities and ensure that any complaint or feedback shall be ‎addressed will be taken seriously including any sensitive complaints. Any ‎complaints received shall be protected in accordance to data ‎protection protocols adopted by PARC. Feedback mechanisms will also ‎be promoted and implemented in coordination with local committees ‎‎(LCs). In regards to safeguarding complaints (SEA), PARC has a well-‎articulated policy with clear standard data protection procedures and ‎will take any sensitive complaint seriously when proven correct no ‎tolerance policy shall be applied.‎

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

‎“Do No Harm" as seen to be part of ensuring accountability towards ‎the local communities used and monitored during any intervention ‎implemented by PARC including participation, information sharing, ‎enabling complaints and "do no harm”, environment protection and ‎gender mainstreaming. The designed project interventions and ‎implementation strategies adheres to the social, political, and ‎economic context of targeted populations and is based on ‎comprehensive understanding of the targeted contexts through the ‎conducted participatory needs assessment and PARC do-no-harm ‎approaches. In regards to social sensitivity, PARC understands the ‎conservative local Palestinian context and will ensure that in all project ‎phases the announcements and meetings will take place in a wide, ‎easily accessed area where women can feel comfortable to join and ‎share their needs. For the political side, all proposed interventions will ‎take into consideration the sensitivity of its location in which a low-‎profile working approach will take place. This will happen by supporting ‎the local community to document the Israeli violations. In addition to ‎that a group of the targeted community will follow up to document all ‎Israeli violations and report back to PARC’s team, other relevant acting ‎organizations and OCHA field office. Finally, at economic level the ‎project interventions aid to support local population to empower their ‎production abilities and access to their assets. Continued consultations ‎will be conducted with the project target groups/stakeholders and HHs ‎to analyze potential risks that the Project team will take into ‎consideration while progressing/implementing the project. PARC ‎believes in equal opportunities for all beneficiaries; ensuring equal ‎access of information, neutrality and assistance through the project ‎cycle starting with the inclusion of all relevant stakeholders in the need ‎assessment. Coordination with the local authorities, CBOs, and ‎community leaders in the project planning and selection process to ‎ensure optimal community participation, outreach, and transparency in ‎service provision. PARC ensures a transparent selection process which ‎avoids social conflict during the implementation of the project. The ‎project is designed based on Human-Rights Based Approaches (HRBA) ‎including women, youth and Persons with Disabilities (PwDs). HRBA; ‎focusing on the actual needs of people and integrating principles of ‎accountability, transparency, meaningful participation, ‎nondiscrimination and linkage to global, regional and national human ‎rights instruments are an integral part of PARC work.‎

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

PARC ensures that aid and impartial access reaches the most vulnerable ‎since the design phase when conducting a need assessment at ‎communities. Gender mainstreaming and protection approaches are ‎well integrated within the project activities. In need assessment PARC ‎ensures to engage local community members, local governmental ‎bodies and other relevant stakeholders to have a comprehensive ‎understanding of the targeted community’s needs, including the ‎participation of women and women led cooperatives and clubs. The ‎findings of the need assessment define the proposed interventions. This ‎is then followed at the implementation phase of the proposed project, ‎in which PARC starts its intervention with formulating a Project Local ‎Committee that represents the targeted population and include key ‎actors from the locality to ensure optimal outreach, transparency and ‎ownership to vulnerable community groups. The project Local ‎Committee and PARC’s team will be responsible in ensuring the access ‎to information to all community members through a clear gender ‎sensitive project announcement in the mosques, village councils, and ‎PARC’s social media platforms. PARC team will develop a well-defined ‎selection criterion that responds to the needs of the targeted ‎population and ensures accessing the most vulnerable (Children, PwDs, ‎elderly, women headed HH… etc). An open call of applications will also ‎ensure the meaningful access, empowerment and transparent process ‎in selecting final beneficiaries which will also be in participation with ‎Project Local Committee. This participatory approach emphasizes ‎ownership, transparency, good governance, gender balance at ‎beneficiary and community levels. Weekly meetings will be held ‎between the municipality, project local committee, community ‎members and PARC field officer for each community. PARC ‎accountability system follows a clear mechanism of complaint ‎registration, reporting and solving, is accessible by the municipality and ‎local community members. Regular follow-up of complaints received ‎from communities, and Municipality are performed with PARC’s M&E ‎unit, including when necessary, addressing OCHA for larger external ‎complaints concerning security and overall occupation related to ‎protection risks. PARC will share any human rights-based violations to ‎relevant clusters.‎
