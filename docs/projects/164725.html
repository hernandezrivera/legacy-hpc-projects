Project Summary

While many components of the Syrian society suffer from the repercussions of the crisis, the category of persons with disabilities (PWDs) and their families is among the most affected. Despite the efforts of multiple governmental and non-governmental organizations to respond to the needs of persons with disabilities, available resources – whether human or financial – are unable to cover the needs during the current time and the near future. In addition to these challenges, there are cases of persons with severe disabilities who severely suffer from lack of access to services – whether basic or medically rehabilitative – not to mention economic and social support services, considered currently a difficult goal to attain under the current situation.
Moreover, the cases of persons with severe disabilities present one of the greatest challenges as they suffer from higher mortality rate and deeply depressed socioeconomic situation.
Within the situation of limited resources and capacities to respond to the needs of persons with severe disabilities, cash-based intervention represents an effective mean to fill the gap and provide important support to persons with disabilities and their families.
The suggested project aims to achieve the following main objectives:
1. Improving the physical and functional health of persons with severe disabilities by supporting them to get rehabilitation treatment.
2. Strengthening the resilience of the families of persons with severe disabilities by improving their health condition and preserving the financial, material and human assets within the family.
Based on the amount requested, the project could target approximately 500 PWDs.
The criteria for targeting beneficiaries is mainly based on the severity of the disability they live with, in addition to the socioeconomic vulnerability of the household.
UNDP has experienced implementing similar project in the governorate of Aleppo where it targeted 500 persons with severe disabilities namely spinal cord injuries with cash assistance for 6 months in the amount of 155$ per month. The cash assistance was conditioned to following a rehabilitation treatment Programme designed by specialist individually for each beneficiary. The project was also coupled with a graduation model to support one household member to secure job opportunity to further support the resilience of the whole households of the persons the severe disabilities.
UNDP has benefitted from this experience and collected lessons learnt that fed in the developed model. This model of the project is able to be replicated in other governorates especially the ones most affected by the war such as Dier-Ez-Zor and / or Arraqa.

4 - Needs

While many components of the Syrian society suffered from the current crises, persons with disabilities and their families are among the most affected and marginalized, of which situation leads to their exclusion from many humanitarian and socioeconomic support. Despite the various efforts of many actors (governmental, UN and NGOs) to respond to the needs of persons with disabilities, the demands are still tremendous compared to the available human or financial resources, resulting in being not fully covered. Moreover, the cases of persons with severe disabilities present one of the greatest challenges as they suffer from higher mortality rate and deeply depressed socioeconomic situation. There are no exact data reporting the exact number of persons with disabilities in Syria. It is estimated that 27% of the population now in Syria live with a level with disabilities of which 30% is linked to permanent disabilities. It is also reported that among these numbers, 5% of the permanent disability cases are for example spinal cord injuries which is one of the most severe cases of disabilities and carries a high level of morbidity and mortality not to mention the negative socioeconomic impact on the households, communities and health system. In a previous assessment conducted by UNDP Syria on the cases of severe disability like spinal cord injuries it was also estimated that there would be 500 PWDs in governorates which were affected by the war reflecting on the number of the population in those governorates. In addition, there are several other cases of impairment caused by the war and or diseases or congenital that also fall under the category of severe disabilities that creates a burden on the resilience of households and communities.

5 - Implementation of activities

The action will be implemented directly by UNDP. The Disability Inclusion Programme manager will provide technical support and oversight on the quality implementation and will lead the overall implementation of the project in close collaboration with community structures, MOSAL, and local authorities. The team will consist of both programme management and technical teams that will develop a detailed plan based on implementation strategies. Moreover, UNDP field staff will share their expertise in community mobilization, beneficiary selection, awareness raising activities. UNDP will follow its cash distribution and procurement protocols and will make use of its partnerships with financial institutions in Syria for cash disbursement. At the start of the project, UNDP will hold a kick off meeting to discuss the overall project strategy, logframe analysis, workplan, and to agree on the roles and responsibilities concerning project implementation. UNDP will meet with local authorities (Governor Office, MoSAL, Area Managers, and national authorities to review the project, secure ongoing support, approve implementation and invite feedback. UNDP will setup a solid, transparent selection criterion of beneficiaries which will be implemented and monitored by the UNDP team in collaboration with stakeholders. Although there may be variances in the vulnerability criteria in different communities, UNDP will work with local communities to prevent discrimination and ensure inclusion of most vulnerable PWDs. To further ensure the participation of most vulnerable groups, UNDP will follow-up with selected beneficiaries who did not benefit from project activities to ensure that selection criteria have been implemented correctly. The project team will provide regular, impartial monitoring through direct field monitoring visits, occasional, remote telephone monitoring, and indicator data tracking mechanisms to provide a regular assessment for appropriate course corrective actions.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Beneficiaries directly targeted in the projects are PWDs with severe disability, it has been noticed that women with disability are more likely to endure violence than those without disability. For that, it is critical to ensure that the risks faced by persons with disabilities are effectively addressed, the elements of protection mainstreaming will be taken into account when designing and implementing the project activities; moreover, the protection and safety of the project beneficiaries will be promoted through applying different protection elements, i.e. individual data collection will be done discretely for each beneficiary, a dedicated team will work on analyzing the data which might help in detecting any GBV cases and\or discrimination acts based on gender and disability. The data collected will be handled carefully to avoid any exposure or danger. The data will be accessed only by the project management team. Moreover, Women will be prioritized in the selection process. The teams working on the project under UNDP and the service provider will have female and male members to handle sensitive cases specially for women and children. Also, awareness sessions will be provided for all beneficiaries to raise their knowledge around persons with disability rights and the privileges the Syrian government provides for them. Adding to tailored PSS activities for PWDs with referring paths for persons in need of more in depth consultations.Finally, UNDP will activate complaint policy and ensure that the beneficiaries could raise their complaint and get feedback indicating that their complaint has been taken seriously, different way will be implemented to activate this system like direct tel NO, complaint box and the monitoring team who will conduct face to face interview with the targeted group and compile their complaint.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: - The existence of a high number of unattended people with severe disabilities in the targeted areas in need of rehabilitation services.- The ability to provide and facilitate access between beneficiaries and service providers- The availability of quality rehabilitation services-The lack of funding dedicated for rehabilitation services to persons with severe disability-Lack of accessibility for PWDs to services centers Risks:- Overlapping with other similar interventions due to lack of national coordination mechanism to track services and/or people deliberately seeking multiple support - The drastic drop in the exchange rate for the Syrian pound that affects the volume of services provided, hence the number of beneficiaries. - The resistance from beneficiaries to reach for the provided services due to personal and/or security reasonsMitigation- Establish early coordination through physical rehabilitation working group and other stakeholders and service providers in order to avoid overlapping, and conduct a thorough assessment of targeted group to identify people who have been already served.- Communicate with donors at the early stages in case there is a necessity for any changes - identify the cases of sensitivity and develop customized solutions and support to access the service while ensuring safety and no-harm
