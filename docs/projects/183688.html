Project Summary

The main objective of this proposed project is improving WASH services in 50 schools located in area C in Hebron, Bethlehem, Salfeet, Nablus, Tubas, Jericho, and Jenin governorates. The project's contributed to SO#2 and corresponds with WASH cluster Objective # 2; Ensure equitable, affordable, and sustainable safely managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinians. This project is designed to be implemented by World Vision and in partnership with the Palestinian Hydrological Group (PHG). It’s composed of two main components; First, improving WASH services of 50 schools through decreasing the gap between available facilities based on MoE standards, while accommodating the needs of the Children with Disability (CWD). This will be achieved by rehabilitating and increasing
the number of available latrines and drinking fountains to cover the number and be sufficient to both genders and various age groups and CWD (especially for schools accommodating primary and secondary age students). Meeting the needs of the Children with Disability (CWD) is an integral part of the project, taking into consideration their gender needs, where activities will be intentionally designed to accommodate their needs to increase their access to services and information and participation including infrastructural work (sanitary units and water fountains), awareness on WASH practices (hygiene practices) and the rest of the activities.
Secondly, in parallel with the infrastructural works, capacity building and awareness sessions for all target school students (the 12800 girls and 16192 boys students) as well as teachers in charge of school health committees will take place in a creative and tailored manner so that to uplift the current hygienic practices so that to ensure that children and schools are protected through the improved hygienic situation in their daily lives. Protection measures will be integrated into the capacity building programs by considering the basic needs of girls, young boys, and people with disabilities to have safe access to the WASH services in their respective schools The overall project will be monitored at different levels, at the national level, the Ministry of Education-School Health department will be updated on a quarterly basis about the project implementation and its impact. At Governorates levels, Directorates of Education will be kept in constant communications
by the respective implementation staff. Locally, the village committee in each locality (as explored below) and Child Health Committee at schools will monitor and lead the implementation of activities so that to guarantee, relevance, compatibility, and impact realized

1 - Needs

Over 60% of the West Bank is considered Area ‘C’, where Israel retains near exclusive control over the territory. Palestinian communities in area C have been identified consistently as a priority area for humanitarian and development assistance in recent years. As the situation of the communities deteriorates, it makes them more vulnerable to shocks with the inability to cope with the existing level of fragility, especially among children. Interventions focusing on increasing the protection and resilience of the local population are essential to enhance positive coping mechanisms of communities in Area C and provide access to all services in dignity. Whilst the level of fragility of communities in Area C is multi-layered, the level of the vulnerability is tied to the different risks/hazards that they are subjected to. The project will target most vulnerable schools located in ‘Area C’ in Bethlehem Hebron, Salfit, Jordan Valley, Jenin and Tubas. The assessment of the WASH facilities at schools carried out in the framework of the project confirmed the generally poor condition of the existing WASH facilities. These targeted areas are subject to constant shocks due to conflict, natural and man-made disasters, mainly because of its geopolitical location. Fragile governance and a lack of services lead to lack of effective preparedness and responses to disasters. According an assessment on risk and vulnerabilities of West Bank and Gaza in 2018 by OCHA OPt, the following main risks had been identified: natural hazards, including earthquakes, floods, droughts and landslides and the spread of pandemics; and manmade hazards, including conflict escalation, severe stress on natural resources, environmental degradation, and rapid and unregulated urbanization. February 2021 witnessed an 88.5 per cent increase in active COVID-19 cases in the oPt, with 29,430 additional Palestinians testing positive, and 21,233 people recovering, according to the Palestinian Ministry of Health (MoH). The cumulative number of cases is 208,349, with 189,154 people recovered from the virus. The project focuses on children, as they are considered the most affected by the protracted crisis and continue to face violence and other access challenges to education, and the realization of their right to education remains severely compromised due to armed search operations in schools; harassment, intimidation, and violence towards students and teachers on the way to and from school; poor education infrastructure; demolition and stop-work orders on school buildings. Additionally, according to the education cluster strategy 2020-2021, children suffer from loss of school time due to firing tear gas into the vicinity or inside schools, delays at checkpoints, and continuous Israeli military presence and operations around schools. In response to these threats, families may adopt “negative coping mechanisms,” such as taking children out of school to limit their exposure to violence and harassment, a practice more common among girls than boys. Dropping out of school is further linked to “early marriage and early pregnancy“ as per the UNWOMEN report published in 2018. Whilst it is the MoE’s strategic education target 2030 to ‘Build and upgrade education facilities that are child, disability and gender sensitive and provide safe, non-violent, inclusive and effective learning environments for all, an estimated 1.2% of six to nine-year-old children, 4.9% of 10 to15-year-olds children and 25% of 16 to17 year-olds in the West Bank are out of school (UNICEF Country report on Out of School Children, 2018). Most schools in Area C don’t meet the minimum standards for safe learning environments. Schools are either in rented residential buildings which lack facilities, safe play areas, and WASH facilities. Other schools in area C are built from caravans or tire structures and don’t meet the safety of the Ministry of Education. This information was confirmed from the feasibility assessment conducted by an external consultant for World Vision, where all 50 schools lack sufficient access to hygiene and healthy latrines at schools, 15 schools have no access to clean water and all schools lack the sufficient numbers of drinking fountains to cover the needs of the students. Furthermore, children had stated that the awareness information shared on hygiene practices are not sufficient nor are presented in an innovative way to attract the children attention, mainly the females. The Ministry of Education conducted a need assessment in 2019 across all schools in the West Bank covering several topics including WASH and DRR. According to the assessment, schools located in Nablus, Salfit, Jericho, and Hebron meet only 50% of the hygiene and health protocols that schools need to adhere to in order to ensure protection and health measures for the children. Furthermore, WASH cluster partners have witnessed a deterioration of WASH infrastructure in institutions, particularly in public health centers, as well as in schools. As per the Covid-19 response plan of the Education Cluster, latrines need urgent rehabilitation in 402 schools and new clean drinking water pointes are needed in 135 schools in Gaza and the West Bank. This is in support of the MoE’s four main objectives under its Covid-19 response plan as well as the WASH cluster, with ‘Hygiene promotion and renovation of schools’ WASH facilities’ being one of them. The ratio of student-to-toilet and student-to-handwashing facilities remains very high with an average of one toilet to cater for 42 students, and one hand-washing facility for 71 students in the West Bank.

2 - Activities

Through this project, 50 schools in the targeted areas, accommodating more than 28,992boys and girls students and 224 Male and Female children with disabilities, will experienceimproved compliance between the available WASH facilities and the Ministry of EducationStandards. The selected schools were assessed thoroughly based on specific criteriarelated to WASH, COVID 19 impact, the occurrence of protection and GBV issues, and accommodation of theneeds of CWD and activities will be set to tackle these issues to ensure the impact is achievedand improve safety and quality access to WASH services in these schools. For example, inthe schools where latrines are shared between boys and girls as well as CWD (whichleads to limitation of girls and CWD access) will have the intervention tailored to grantaccess to girls through the increasing number of sanitary units and/or developing a culturallyand the gender-sensitive ways to guarantee safe access for girls. The same applies to schoolswhere issues of bullying are reported where older (secondary) and younger (primary)children and CWD share the same WASH facilities. Better access to healthy and safesanitary units is a cross-cutting issues across the four geographical locations, where theexact intervention in each school will be carefully tailored with the village committees/Student Health Committees and other stakeholders such as school administration.Students (boys, girls, and CWD) will be taught about adequate hygienic practice, mainly related to COVID 19, in an ageand gender-sensitive manner. Hygiene campaigns, hygiene promotion classes as well asdemonstrations and visuals will also be utilized to optimize students’ grasping ofinformation. The long experience of PHG and World Vision in School WASH will beutilized to implement a comprehensive and sustainable awareness raising campaign.Through close coordination with the Directorates of Educations, the School HealthSupervisors will develop a Training of Trainers (ToT) curriculum that will be used to trainrespective teachers in charge of school health in the 50 target schools. These supervisorswill conduct the interactive ToT which will be concluded by a health action plan for eachschool. Those teachers will be mobilizing and formulating school Health Committeesrepresented by students (boys and girls and CWD where applicable). These committeeswill be trained by Teachers and will conduct a needs assessment to identify key WASHpriorities and solutions will be proposed accommodating the WASH needs of each andsingle targeted school. One of the first outcomes will be designing of awareness-raisingthe campaign that fits with the local capacities and is appropriate to age and gender in each ofthe targeted schools. In addition, the committee will also identify one or moredemonstrations, which will provide visual accessible resources to remind boys, girls andCWD about the main messages of each campaign. Girls will be consulted in a safe andculturally acceptable manner to design and implement awareness-raising interventionsspecific for girls’ hygiene including girls with disabilities. A similar intervention will beconducted regarding the specific hygiene practices for the children with disability (CWD).In parallel with the above, health committees, supervisors, and teachers alongside World Vision and PHG in these locations will be trained on school WASH awarenesscampaigns. They will be responsible to conduct an average of ten classes to cover thespecific awareness needs of each age group and each gender at schools. In order to makethese classes more meaningful and appreciated, hygiene kits will be distributed at the endof each class so that to enable students to apply what they learned during these classes.
