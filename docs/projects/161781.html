Project Summary

DRC’s proposed protection activities will build on its existing response in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states. We seek to address critical protection needs of IDPs and vulnerable host communities. These include people living in both informal/ formal camps and within hosting communities. DRC has a strong presence across 24 LGAs in NE Nigeria through the humanitarian response first established in 2015 by implementing a broad spectrum of programmes. Protection programming will aim at strengthening or establishing protection clinics (not used as a health related term in this project) and protection desks which increase the element of protection by presence, continually identifying crucial protection information, responding to the protection concerns of the IDPS, host communities and newly arrived displaced persons within these LGAs of operation. Through a structured protection monitoring approach, important protection related feedback will be collected by directly engaging with beneficiaries. This information will update the project throughout the year. This will also ensure that the project continues to be relevant and that it delivers appropriate responsive action for vulnerable communities while establishing a beneficiary engagement forum. DRC intends to reach an estimated 17,683 HH ( 123,782 individuals) through the protection programme in the next year. Main protection activities will include; individual protection assistance (IPA) and basic case management for protection cases, awareness activities on specific protection related themes guided by the findings of protection monitoring activities, creation or strengthening of referral pathways, providing information on services provided and important human rights awareness, collecting beneficiary feedback through protection desks and clinics, enhance messaging on human rights through appropriate advocacy channels, ensure sustainable protection mechanisms are in place to sustain promotion of human rights, capacity building of civil societies/ community structures and provide community based PSS for conflict affected communities to improve their psychological and social wellbeing. DRC will disaggregate protection data according to gender, age and diversity by conducting assessments and activities that seek to further understand the needs and viewpoints of women, men, girls and boys with a goal of tailor fitting the programme to meet the specific needs of these categories of individuals. Furthermore DRC, will give special attention and support to vulnerable groups of elderly persons, persons with disabilities, female headed households with large dependents, adolescent parents, UAM and separated children and orphans.

1 - Needs

The crisis in Northern Nigeria have created an array of humanitarian needs that are predominantly protection in nature. The security and operating environment is fluid and fast changing. For those most at risk, immediate and often lifesaving assistance continues to be an absolute priority – particularly for those affected by military operations and/or attacks by Non State Armed Groups (NSAGs). Addressing the ever-growing needs of the affected civilian population (both IDPs and hosting communities) requires flexible resources and rapid response approaches. The ability to respond to numerous contingencies also necessitates a flexible, multi-sectorial approach to effectively respond to sudden, emerging, and evolving needs of affected communities.Now in its tenth year, the conflict in NE Nigeria continues as one of the largest and widespread humanitarian crises, negatively impacting the lives of millions of people affected by the ongoing hostilities, particularly women and children. Deep rooted issues of inequality, political exclusions and social marginalization are key factors fueling the conflict. An estimated 7.1 million (compared to 7.7 million last year) remain in need of humanitarian assistance in the states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) in 2019; out of these 7.1 million, 2.3 million are girls, 1.9 million boys, 1.6 million women and 1.3 million men. While the number of people requiring assistance has reduced over the year, primarily due to efforts of the humanitarian sector coupled with different assistance programs offered by the Government, a great number of people (more than 800,000 people) continue to live in areas controlled by Non-State Armed Groups (NSAGs) and/or hard to reach areas in the North East, where access remains limited. The conflict has generated large population movements with current displacement figure standing at 1.9 million individuals (IOM DTM Round 26).

2 - Activities

Protection programming will commence with Protection stakeholders capacity building. Stakeholders for this projects have already been mapped out from our existing programming in these LGAS. They will include the ministry of women and social affairs staff, key community leaders, traditional/ religious leaders and community volunteers who have historically being supporting the community to address various protection and legal concerns. An estimated 1000 persons will be reached through this initiative. They will be provided with core training on protection principles, mainstreaming and survivor/ victim centered approaches which is informed by the human rights approach model. This will earmark the initial activities of this project as create room for community entry. Working closely with community role models tasked as the as protection community focal persons. DRC will identify the focal points, train them and provide them with necessary materials for their community based protection model within their communities. 42 groups will be strengthened and or formed comprising of 15 persons per group. Their main roles will be to create awareness on key protection topics aimed at positively changing behavior, providing community mediation on dispute resolution and support with advocacy initiatives and build a civil society within their communities. Protection programming will focus on identifying vulnerable persons with protection concerns, through the DRC protection concerns criteria; these individuals will be provided with personalized protection assistance, vulnerable female headed households, young unsupported women and men, vulnerable adolescent girls, elderly persons, people with disabilities and terminally ill adults and children will be supported with essential cash or in kind support and those with further additional risks brought by the lack of income will be trained and supported with income generating activities closely linking the implementation of the IGA to the expertise of the livelihood team. Who will provide the essential training on IGA and business. Protection monitoring activities encompassing focus group discussions, key informant interview, household surveys and community meetings will be conducted with displaced persons and host communities to ensure a constant updating of community protection landscape and mapping out of newly emerging risks. This will dictate the strategic direction of the project’s response and prevention activities. Protection and M& E teams will conduct rapid assessments to communities affected by sudden shocks when they occur. DRC will conduct structured community based PSS activities specifically targeting vulnerable groups; activities for PSS will include sports, basic counselling and PSS support and art activities. DRC acknowledges the gender gap created through the insufficiency of opportunities and barriers to access to services for women, girls and other marginalized groups of people. Consequently women and girls are the most severely affected by this conflict are indeed make the highest percentage of the displaced population. Thus this project will target 40% of men and 60% of women in efforts to empower women and reduce the gender gap in the communities targeted. These particular theme will echo across all stages of programming.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

DRC continually engages vulnerable groups of persons through the detailed protection monitoring activities of FGDs, HH interviews and meetings. Important information is collected from elderly persons, people with disabilities, female headed households, child parents, youth, UAMs. SC and Oprhans on the viewpoints on all matters affecting them. This feedback is then analysed and reintegrated into project implementation plan or strategy. This may or maynot change the response plans or strategy in order to meet their needs appropriately according to the needs identified. Since the protection response firstly targets vulnerable groups this kind of interaction with the project is an ongoing daily activity.
