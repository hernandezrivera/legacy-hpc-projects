Project Summary

YIPDI is proposing an 11-month intervention in Dikwa, jere, Banki and Ngala LGAs,Borno State to respond to child protection needs. The goal of the project is to reduce conflict-related risks and vulnerabilities affecting children, adolescents and caregivers by strengthening access to a range of life-saving, child-sensitive and youth-friendly essential Child protection services reducing and responding to children’ exposure to violence, exploitation, abuse, and neglect there by enhancing the well being of conflict affected children, targeting 10990 beneficiaries that are in dire need of child Protection Intervention ( Boys, Girls, Caregivers, Community Members and Stakeholders) through case management and Psycho-social Support.

1 - Needs

After more than ten years of conflict, the humanitarian crisis in the BAY states remains one of the most severe in the world today. A total of 7.9 million people – more than one in two people in the three crisis-affected states are in need of humanitarian assistance in 2020. The majority of the people in need are in Borno State, the epicenter of the crisis. Women and children constitute 81% of the overall crisis population. The gendered dimensions of displacement is having a significant impact on the status of women and girls especially within the context of mobility, fragmentation of households, demoralization and trauma. The crisis is predominantly a protection crisis characterized by grave violations of human rights and dignity. Protection concerns including killings and maiming, sexual violence and abuse,arbitrary arrests and detention without access to justice and legal services, physical safety and security, forced recruitment, abductions forced marriages and forced displacement remain a major concern for the physical and mental wellbeing of IDPs, returnees and host communities in the BAY states with at least a population of 1.4 million of the children in need have very limited or lack access to core child protection services (27 per cent in Adamawa, 55 per cent in Borno and 18 per cent in Yobe). The threats and risks posed by these protection risks have been exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic which has not only negatively affected delivery of life-saving child protection services but has also weakened the capacities of families and communities to care for children. The COVID-19 pandemic highlighted the situation of children living without parental care, especially those for boys who had been sent away by their families for religious education purposes as well as the situation of children on the streets. There is a strong need to build the capacity of child protection actors, particularly NNGOs, in providing family tracing and reunification services and a strategic approach is required to make child protection services more available at the community level whilst building on and strengthening the community capacities for take prevention and response actions for children’s protection. Protection risks are heightened for girls, mainly, adolescent girls who are most at risk of sexual violence and as a result of the pandemic, are also at a high risk losing out on education opportunities amongst denial of realization of other rights.The estimated population of children aged 6-17 years are in need of protection services in north-east Nigeria is 987,636 (517,249 girls and 470,388 boys) and 20,257 girls and boys are estimated to be children with disabilities. More than 6,000 children (more than 50 per cent girls) are estimated to have been associated with armed groups, abducted, killed or victims of violent attacks by armed groups since the beginning of the conflict. Displaced girls and boys living in camps and girls and boys living in host communities are most vulnerable to protection risks, representing 37 per cent and 36 per cent of the children in need respectively. The ability of an estimated 4,882 or 84% parents and caregivers to care appropriately for children has been negatively impacted, putting children and families at risk of negative coping mechanisms including child marriage and dropping out of school. It is estimated that 17,528 girls and boys (28% girls, 72% boys) are in need of child protection case management and other specialized services to help prevent or address exposure to abuse, exploitation, neglect and violence. Young children and adolescents require life skills education to equip them with knowledge and skills to understand, negotiate and mediate changes, challenges and risks in their lives, and to enable them to participate in society. This is particularly so for adolescent girls who face heightened risks including child marriage and sexual abuse. (Child Protection Humanitarian Response Plan)

2 - Activities

These activities include 1) providing children with quality and tailored case management services,: including support to on-going caseloads and respond to newly identified cases including children of parents affected by Covid; 2) reinforcing the provision of psychosocial support to children, adolescents and caregivers in need (including remote or face to face PSS curriculum for children and adolescent (12-18 years) to improve stress coping mechanism; 3) strengthening community capacities, through training/mentoring of Community-based CP focal points and volunteers, to protect children against abuse and exploitation as well as health related risks, including COVID-19, using an integrated approach with WASH and health teams. Dikwa, Banki, Ngala and some parts of Jere are considered high risk areas for mines and other explosive devices. To improve the protective environment in target locations YIPDI will coordinate with Mine Action Group (MAG) to provide Mine Risk Education to the children attending PSS sessions and the volunteers from the community. The main objective of Mine Risk Education is to reduce the risk of death or injury from mines/IED/ERW. YIPDI is recognized in the NE as a major CP actor with strong expertise and field work experience in MMC and JERE Through its work, YIPDI has developed excellent working relationships with local authorities, and is well accepted by community members. YIPDI will use this leverage to tailor this intervention and maximise its impact and beneficiary reach KEY RESPONSE ACTIVITIES ▪ Identification, consistent follow-up and service provision for children at-risk of, or affected by, protection concerns, including case management for former CAAG and children with disabilities. ▪ Children deprived of parental care, or whose parental care is assessed as inadequate, are provided with timely, reliable and safe alternative care arrangements with prioritization of family-based solutions. Consistent monitoring (minimum once a month) of the situation of children in alternative care arrangements. ▪ Identification, assessment and support to foster parents including supervision during the provision of foster care. ▪ Spontaneous alternative care arrangements are identified, assessed, documented and monitored. ▪ Family tracing and reunification for unaccompanied and separated children. ▪ Referral of vulnerable children to access inter-sectoral and other specialized services. ▪ Structured and age-specific creative, recreational and social activities for children including adolescents. ▪ Mobile and age-specific creative, recreational and social activities for children including adolescents. ▪ Counselling services for children including adolescents. ▪ Mental health services for children including adolescents. ▪ Life-skills education for adolescent girls and boys. ▪ Counselling services for caregivers of vulnerable children or vulnerable caregivers. ▪ Mental health services for caregivers of vulnerable children or vulnerable caregivers. ▪ Positive parenting lessons for caregivers of vulnerable children or vulnerable caregivers. ▪ Raise awareness at community level on child protection with specific focus on relevant child protection issues within a given community.
