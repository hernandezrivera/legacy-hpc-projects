UNRWA in Syria continues to support the education of Palestine refugee students despite the conflict and
displacement. In responding to the COVID-19 crisis, the Agency relied on its Education in Emergencies (EiE) approach, reconceptualised in the following areas:
Continuity of Learning, Psychosocial Support (PSS), Security and Safety, and Health and Hygiene issues. Below is a description of the key elements of the response in Syria for 2021:
1) Continuity of Learning:
Students and Installations
In 2021, UNRWA will continue to deliver basic, quality and inclusive education to almost 51,000 Palestine refugees through 102 schools managed by the Agency across Syria, of which 39 are running on a second shift in buildings on loan from the Ministry of Education, to compensate for UNRWA schools that have been destroyed or severely damaged by the conflict. UNRWA will cover the cost of additional temporary staff hired to address the increasing needs that have arisen due to the compounded crises.
Nature of Learning
Back-to-school kits and stationery will be procured and provided to students at the beginning of the academic year to alleviate some of the economic burden faced by parents. Summer learning activities will also be sustained. These interventions have become an integral part of UNRWA’s EiE modality, delivering academic, extracurricular and psychosocial support activities to students during the summer months. Conducting summer learning will be contingent on the COVID-19 situation in the country during summer 2021. In addition, support classes will be organized for 9th Grade students. While schools in Syria re-opened on 13 September 2020 for in-person education, the growing number of COVID-19 confirmed cases amongst education staff and students across the country over the last quarter of 2020 highlights the challenges of preventing transmission in schools. During emergency situations (including pandemics), Palestine refugee children may be unable to access their regular schools, leading to a risk of interruption of their education. UNRWA has developed a self-learning programme to provide UNRWA students with access to age-appropriate, relevant, and safe self-learning material that is in line with UN values and humanitarian principles. The Agency has recently launched a Digital Learning Platform to enhance this process and materials for the platform are being developed for UNRWA students from Grades 1 to 9 in Syria.
2) Hygiene and Physical Safety:
A number of training sessions, incorporating COVID-19 prevention measures, will be conducted for teachers and principals on topics such as Gender Based Violence (GBV), Child Protection, and Addressing Violence against Children (AVAC). Similar training sessions will be delivered to parents through parent-teacher associations (PTAs), which will continue to play a pivotal role in developing a holistic approach to education for Palestine refugees. Reusable masks will be procured and distributed to students and teachers. In addition, disinfectants and sanitizers will be provided to schools to ensure proper hygiene practices are in place.
3) Psychosocial Support:
UNRWA will also continue to provide psychosocial support to address the additional stress created by the COVID-19 pandemic, by deploying psychosocial counsellors in all of its schools with the aim of providing individual and group counselling and support to students and teachers, in addition to the provision of recreational activities, if the context allows. Psychosocial support and awareness raising sessions on COVID-19 will also be delivered in classrooms and/ or through remote modalities.

4 - Needs

After ten years of conflict, it is critical that formal and non-formal education services are approached as a long-term investment by not only children and their families but by aid actors. COVID-19 and worsening economic conditions have exacerbated the consequences of systemic underinvestment in education service delivery. The consequences of this will continue in 2021.Formal education will continue to have a limited absorption and retention capacity and be carried out in largely inadequate and at times unsafe, learning environments. Education capacity and effectiveness will continue to be constrained by insufficient numbers of paid teachers, lack of teaching and learning supplies and a lack of continuous professional development for teachers and other education personnel.UNRWA has lost 40% of its classrooms due to the conflict. Almost 60% of Palestine refugees, including school children have been displaced at least once since the start of the conflict. Many of UNRWA schools are run in second shift in schools building on loan from Ministry of Education. Schools are in dire need of maintenance and new furniture/equipment. The Agency is striving to deploy and maintain educational staff that can deliver quality education, following the displacement or departure of many qualified staff outside of the country. COVID-19 has added another layer of needs for increased space and provision of sanitization materials. Palestine refugee students are dealing with trauma and stress from compounded crises and require sustained PSS support. The deteriorating economic conditions are severely affecting the families’ capacity to cover educational expenses including stationaries and school bags.

5 - Implementation of activities

The Agency will achieve its objectives through the performance of the following activities: •Direct provision of educational services to up to 51,000 Palestine refugee students, through 102 schools; •Provision of psychosocial support for students and staff by counsellors in all UNRWA schools; •Regular maintenance of UNRWA schools and availing basic furniture where necessary •Provision of support and catch up classes to students, in addition to summer learning activities; •Offering students the supplies and materials they need for learning; •Delivery of training to school principals, teacher and students on safety and security, including ERW awareness) and protection; •Procurement and distributing internet packages to staff and vulnerable students where remote learning becomes necessary due to COVID-19 measures; •Procurement and distribution of disinfectants, sanitizers, and hygiene materials to schools; • Procurement and distribution of masks to students and staff. Monitoring and Evaluation: UNRWA has different structures in place to monitor all its interventions, including the Education programme. There is a dedicated Area Support Team that follows up on Protection interventions and a Programme Support Office(PSO) that handles monitoring and evaluation of activities. UNRWA has an extensive Result Based Monitoring (RBM) systems with indicators for the Education programme and holds quarterly management reviews (QMRs). The Education Programme has staff that monitor the quality of the education through regular field visits and feedback from male and female staff based in the field. The Education programme has an online database that gathers programmatic data. PTAs in schools allow the collection of feedback from parents.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk: Violence against children in and around UNRWA schools • Likelihood: Medium • Impact: Medium • Mitigating measures: 1. UNRWA is delivering training and ToT sessions to staff to address violence against children (AVAC) and to decrease violence in and around UNRWA installations, including peer to peer violence. 2. Self-care sessions are conducted with the UNRWA education staff and counselling and psychosocial support is provided to the students through UNRWA school counsellors 3. UNRWA addresses cases of violence against children from staff members using available investigative and sanctions regimes. 4. UNRWA provides training on positive behaviour management techniques in the classroom to education staff. 5. UNRWA creates child-friendly learning environments. Protection risk: Students/families referring to negative coping mechanisms due to the declining economic situation (drop out, child labour, early marriage, drug consumption) • Likelihood: Medium • Impact: Medium Mitigating measures: 1. UNRWA is aware of the lack of professional support for children and their families in need of PSS. UNRWA is doing the outmost possible to contribute to fill this gap. Psychosocial support activities in schools are enhanced, in close cooperation with the UNRWA protection team. Those activities are mostly of recreational nature, but also include clear protection components, like awareness raising intending to address and prevent negative coping mechanisms. During the COVID-19 pandemic school counsellors and teachers remained in very close contact with their students during closures. 2. UNRWA PSS counsellors provide personalized assistance to students who are identified as having very specific and/or urgent need.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: 1. Humanitarian needs of Palestine Refugees in Syria remain relatively stable 2. Sustained commitment of donors to support UNRWA financially 3. Security situation and active violence do not deteriorate any further. 4. COVID-19 pandemic challenges remain within the scope of the Agency's capacity to respond financially and operationally. Risk: UNRWA is unable to continue providing services and respond to Palestine Refugees needs because of lack of funding or due to overwhelming increase in humanitarian needs. Last year, UNRWA received only 30% of its requirements for Syria. Mitigation: 1. Ensure efficient and sustainable delivery of operations and services prioritizing those most in need 2. Regularly engage with donors and partners to mobilize funding 3. Ensure robust financial, planning and management systems to make best use of available resources 4. Maintain communication with stakeholders on status of funding. 5. Continue to monitor interventions through results-based monitoring framework. Risks are also mitigated by ensuring a sound and solid monitoring of implementation. Risk: UNRWA staff and Palestine Refugees are exposed to violence and security threats. Mitigation: 1. Monitor security updates and conduct security assessments for facilities and missions 2. Train staff and beneficiaries on ERW, and personal safety. Risk: COVID-19 pandemic situation persists/worsens, leading to further operational challenges. Mitigation: 1. Adapt programme modality to ensure sustained delivery of services while prioritizing the safety of staff and beneficiaries 2. Provide PPEs to frontline staff and avail sanitization materials at offices and distribution centres.
