Project Summary

The global outbreak of COVID-19 is putting further pressure to the already overstretched health system in the Gaza Strip. UNRWA continues to provide essential primary health care services to 1.3 million Palestine refugees in Gaza, with adjusted approach with risk mitigation measures to provide services safely amidst COVID-19 outbreak. The public health system is overstretched responding to COVID-19 emergency, and expanding the capacity of the health services to absorb increasing needs is necessary to ensure access of vulnerable population to health services. Meanwhile, UNRWA’s ability to respond to emergency health needs in Gaza is greatly constrained by the tight funding situation compromising its ability to respond if necessary.
In light of these conditions, this proposed intervention aims to support the provision of critical healthcare needs for Palestine refugees in the Gaza Strip, while alleviating the pressure on public health system, through: (1) ensuring access to secondary and tertiary health services for poor refugees, (2) providing life-saving medicines , (3) hiring emergency surged staff to cope with increasing needs and new interventions designed in response of COVID-19, (4) providing healthcare services adjusting COVID-19 emerged needs including telemedicine, the home delivery of medicine, triage system in all health centers and enhanced measures including strict use of PPEs, (5) providing necessary assistive tools to children with special needs , and (6) providing mental health and psychosocial support especially women and people affected by COVID-19 emergency .

1 - Needs

With more than a half of the population identified as requiring health-related humanitarian assistance in 2021 (HNO 2021), Gaza remains under protracted humanitarian crisis. The process of gradual deterioration under the blockade has left essential services in Gaza barely able to function, thus directly impacting people’s basic right to health access, which added further strain from the COVID-19 outbreak since March 2020. The health system is facing challenges to contain the outbreak while continue delivering other essential services to respond both existing and emerging healthcare needs. In the first half of 2020, 43 per cent of essential medicines at Gaza’s Central Drugs Store were completely out of stock. Many of public healthcare centres are converted into COVID-19 quarantine and isolation centres with only 19 out of 51 primary healthcare centres are currently functioning as their staff have been deployed to support COVID-19 response in October 2020. These measures are affecting the postponed care for people with non-communicable diseases as well as for people requiring elective surgeries that are added to the already long waiting list with over 11,000 cases and increasing. Such redirection of resources from primary healthcare services in public health system, an additional 80,000 vulnerable women and 210,000 children including 25,000 neonates are affected by limited access to primary healthcare such as sexual and reproductive healthcare, obstetric and neonatal care and emergency nutrition services. Furthermore, the pandemic and its related measures also resulted in increased need for mental health and psychosocial services, including GBV survivors, adolescent with problematic behavior, and patients and other affected with COVID-19, among others (HNO 2021). While UNRWA continues to struggle with financial crisis compounded with COVID-19 emergency, the health demand continues to increase in a deteriorating socioeconomic condition. In order to alleviate the pressure on the public health sector, strengthening the health services already provided by UNRWA to approximately two thirds of the population across the Gaza Strip is in immediate need in order to cope with the on-going crisis.

2 - Activities

UNRWA provides primary healthcare services to 1.4 million Palestine refugees across its 22 health centres in the Gaza Strip, mandated to provide primary healthcare services to Palestine refugees. In order to respond to existing and emerging needs, the Agency aims to provide further support to poor refugees to access secondary and tertiary health services which would alleviate the burden on the public health services. UNRWA will refer the vulnerable refugees who are not able to afford the private hospital based treatment, partially covering the hospitalization cost of medical treatment received at hospitals that UNRWA has established agreement with, or through reimbursing the patients for the medical treatment costs they incur. The types of covered procedures which are considered life-saving (ERCP), urgent and those with long waiting lists at the MoH hospitals, and that have an impact on the quality of life of the patients. UNRWA Health Programme will work jointly with the MoH and WHO to determine the procedures that will be referred to UNRWA contracted hospitals. UNRWA will procure life-saving medicine through its procurement capacity to respond to refugee needs. In addition, UNRWA will expand its response capacity within its primary health care services in order to cope with increasing demand and emerging needs due to COVID-19 such as telemedicine and adapting a triage system in all UNRWA health centers to separate respiratory patients from others. These emergency surged staff will be able to fill the gap in the area of increased healthcare needs within UNRWA services. Following COVID-19 emergency, UNRWA implemented some adjustments to prevent further spread of the virus, such as use of personal protection equipment (PPE) to protect the health staff as well as the people visiting health centres, triage care and protocols to separate the patients with respiratory symptoms, and telemedicine service including the home delivery of medicine to limit the footprints at health centres as well as to maintain the access to primary healthcare services during lockdown. Furthermore, UNRWA will also support children with special needs, providing proper medical screening of school children and necessary assistive devices that vulnerable families are not able to afford otherwise. Finally, UNRWA’s mental health and psychosocial support at health centres as well as tele-counselling respond to increasing demand targeting vulnerable Palestine refugees identified as in need of support, including women of reproductive age and children, elderlies, people with disabilities and GBV cases.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

UNRWA has a dedicated Protection and Neutrality Oﬃce to guarantee the provision of its services in an impartial and non-discriminatory way. UNRWA regularly assess the level of compliance with Protection standards of the services provided to refugees through a Protection Audit. Staff in health centres, whom are regularly targeted by capacity building activities including on protection mainstreaming and gender, are able to refer speciﬁc protection cases to MHPSS counsellors for follow up. In addition, a new mechanism is developed to support critical and urgent cases of GBV and protection cases. In normal circumstances, the crowd control system is in place and a speciﬁc calendar is set up to avoid and limit potential incidents and/or disturbance. A complaint response mechanism is accessible in health centres and mechanism to provide timely feedbacks are available through speciﬁc response committees.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNRWA is targeting all Palestinian refugees in need for healthcare through its 22 health centres across the Gaza Strip. Location of health centres takes into consideration of safety concerns as well as catchment area, distance and potential risks for health staff to reach the area for different groups of population. In case of lockdown and movement restriction, people in need of primary health care are still able to access UNRWA’s health services through telemedicine. The health centres take into consideration speciﬁc vulnerability concerns and adopted speciﬁc SOPs to provide meaningful access and prioritize healthcare to vulnerable groups (abandoned women, pregnant women, persons with disabilities, elderly, etc.). A speciﬁc follow up system is also in place whenever assistance is not received in order to identify and follow speciﬁc concerns and potential protection cases. Further, health centres are clearly marked and visible, respecting UNRWA neutrality policy.
