Project Summary

This project will promote quality and lifesaving basic education for children and youths from the most vulnerable groups in the 3 communities of Ulang, Nassir and Gogrial East and will use inter-sectoral and integrated approach to serve Returnees, IDPs and host communities in all these locations. The selection of the project sites of Nassir and Ulang is based of the area being a vital route for the returnees from Ethiopia , the present of huge IDPs number and the areas were currently affected by floods incidents according to the recent flood assessment reported conducted in October 2019 in addition to the alarming humanitarian situation in the two counties as part of displacement due to conflict and some natural disasters like floods. Inter-communal fighting and cattle raids in greater Warrap state has contributed to loss of strong education infrastructural in the area. This project is design to improve safe and access to quality education both formal and non-formal and to improve coping mechanism for children and youth in schools. The project would target 3265 (1403 girls) children and youths in five most hit schools by conflict in the 3 locations 1 school in Ulang 2 schools in Nassir and 2 new community initiatives schools in Gogrial east due inter communal conflicts and displacement in the county .These schools were identified to have a very big gaps in terms of service delivery during the implementation of TADO and UNICEF PCA 2019 education in emergency (EiE) intervention in Upper Nile and Warrap state and have not benefited from the project and are not benefiting from the project .
Establishment and rehabilitation of TLS/classrooms and segregated WASH facilities in selected schools fitted with hand washing facilities. Capacity building to teachers to be able to implement the EiE component. To support teachers in delivering quality and continuous services by support their incentives as guided by the cluster. Provide quality education and recreational materials and provision of school meals/take home ration to support coping mechanism among the school-aged children. Capacity building training of SMC/PTAs and county education authorities to be about to implement EiE component, lifesaving skills like PSS, and effective management and coordination of education in an emergency context. The project is also designed to work with other cluster partners in enshrining integrated approaches in WASH, Nutrition, GBV and Health. TADO would work with RI and Nile hope on promotion of hygiene and safe disposal of waste and with Nile hope for referral of cases of malnourished children to the treatment Centers. TADO with its health programming in greater Nasir would provide health related messaging in schools she will be supporting in coordination with UNKEA. TADO would coordinate with CHADO and Non Violence Peace Force GBV related integration.

1 - Needs

Access to equitable and quality teaching and learning for displaced (IDPs), IDP returnees, returnees and conflict-affected children and youth is extremely low in South Sudan, and worsening in the current state of hunger , economic collapse and floods . With low completion and high dropout rates, lack of safe educational environments and poor availability of scholastic materials, high pupil-teacher ratios, under-qualified and demotivated teachers and highly traumatized school communities, the need for a return to normalcy through greater access to education has never been good for conflict-affected populations. This is true for children and youth and particularly for girls. Children are suffering from trauma, malnutrition and illnesses, all of which impedes cognitive development. Internal displacement and exposure to violence means more children and youth are facing extraordinary protection risks, both in and outside the school. This is exacerbated by the high number of over-age children and adolescents in classes, which creates stigma for them, and potential protection risks for younger learners in the same class.The poor educational status of the country has further been eroded by the conflict that erupted in December 2013 and worsened in July 2016 leading to more than 1.17 million children aged 3 to 18 years old have lost access to education. School-aged children represent more than 47.75% of the displaced population. Around 25% of primary schools in are closed due to insecurity and displacement of teachers and students. Approximately 31% of schools currently open have suffered at least one or more attacks by armed actors, with schools in Greater Upper Nile being the most affected. More than 17,000 children are estimated to have been recruited by armed actors in South Sudan. Anecdotal evidence suggests that child marriage is increasing due to conflict and economic pressures, preventing more girls from receiving education. An estimated 1 million children are believed to be in psychological distress.Adolescent girls are at increasing risk of early and forced marriage, as well as sexual abuse and exploitation due to the escalating economic crisis. Reported incidents of possible sexual slavery more than tripled during July 2016, while reported incidents of forced marriages doubled. Additionally, adolescent girls in rural areas may not be allowed to travel the distance to school and are kept home during menstruation, for household duties and due to the cultural belief that school spoils girls, thus creating lower bride prices if a girl is educated. While risks to disruption of children’s education were highest in Greater Upper Nile at the beginning of 2016, over the course of 2017, the risks increased in Warrap state as well, where conflict escalated and the economic crisis hit extremely hard.Cattle raiding, child abductions, inter-tribal conflicts and revenge killings, and violence due to the ongoing conflict continue to force children and young people from their homes and schools resulting in an acute aggravation of chronic situation that intertwines education and protection. Many schools in South Sudan lack girl-friendly school spaces, poor WASH facilities, MHM kits and adequate water supply. These factors affect girls’ enrolment and attendance in schools. According to UNICEF, “more than five million people lack safe water and adequate sanitation” in South Sudan.Given the economic and institutional challenges affecting the education system in South Sudan, there is also a need for new educational infrastructure to complement the distribution of teaching and learning materials to help reinforce learning outcomes. Motivation of teachers and training of education authorities to manage schools in emergencies is a need. Peace building, WASH, lifesaving skills and PSS should be integrated to education intervention to support children that are greatly affected by this condition.
