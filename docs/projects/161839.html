Project Summary

The complex emergency and conflict in North East Nigeria started in 2009 and has resulted in the displacement of over 2 million people (DTM Aug 2019). Overall, 7.68 million people are in dire need of assistance across Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states; In which, 45% being IDPs and returnees, 38% host communities and the remaining part being inaccessible populations (HRP 2019-2021). The situation of host communities and IDPs is critical, as the IDPs are living in severely congested temporary camps, prone to regular epidemics, rain, flooding and fire incidents, while the host communities are having the tension of not properly intervened as a result of prioritization of IDPs and returnees.
Solidarites International (SI) is proposing the continuation of its current provision of WaSH services through a one-year WaSH project that will address critical gaps in WASH activities for a highly vulnerable population among the People in Need (PiN) at both host communities and IDPs camps level in the Local Government Areas (LGAs) of Ngala, Dikwa, Monguno, MMC and Jere LGAs, Borno State, Nigeria.
Due to ongoing conflict and security operation in Borno state; Ngala, Dikwa and Monguno still remain “hard to reach” LGAs with the least access to health services, permanent shelters, safe water, sanitation facilities and the highest rate of malnutrition and protection issues (REACH - November 2018 - April 2019). Some sites in those 5 LGA’s still have access to less than 7L/pers/day of safe water and a huge gap noted in terms of sanitationreferenced tothe WASH Sector Gap matrix in the“hard to reach” LGAs do not comply with the SPHERE standard of 50 pers/functional latrines.
SI is thus proposing to intervene in Ngala, Dikwa, Monguno, MMC and Jere LGAs under this project so as to achieve its specific objective which is “to improve to sustainable Safe Water, Sanitary facilities n and Hygiene Services to the affected population in Monguno, Dikwa, Ngala, MMC and Jere through:
- Rehabilitation, operation and maintenance of water points in the most vulnerable camps as well as host communities in the 5 LGA’s
- Drilling of water points in Ngala and Dikwa LGA
- Construction and rehabilitation of emergency blocks of latrines and bathing facilities in vulnerable camps and host communities in all the locations.
-
- Hygiene promotion activities and campaigns with support of IEC materials
- Menstrual Health Management activities
The annual target population for this project is 506810 individuals in a ratio of 45:55 of male: female gender proportion

2 - Activities

1. Water Supply responseAs part of the Water supply activity for 2020, SI propose to intervene in the targeted 5 LGA’s (MMC, Jere, Dikwa, Ngala and Monguno) to cover the identified gaps, through the following activities:- Two (2) water points rehabilitation each in MMC & Jere- Drilling of two (2) Water points each in Ngala, Dikwa and Monguno- Three (3) water points rehabilitation each in Dikwa and Ngala- Four (4) water points rehabilitation in Monguno- Drilling of one water point each in MMC and Jere- Rehabilitation of 8 Hand pumps each in the 5 LGA’s.- Establishing and training of sixty-two (62) Water Committees each for the established water point at the respective LGA’sDescription: Water points rehabilitation and drilling will be done in order to meet the need of safe water availability at the targeted area. Moreover, in order to ensure enough and also sustainable water supply, the water points will be provided with solar powered schemes system this will allow a constant water supply, independent of time and weather conditions, thanks to the adequate solar arrays and large storage tank for water network distribution to the beneficiaries. Hence, it is possible to meet even a numerous beneficiary demand, high sustainability and maintenance cost very low. Water committees will also be formed, created and trained for each water point. Each committee is in charge of management and maintenance of one water source. Water Committees are made up of a limited number of members (10, on average). Inorder to make the committee effective they will be provided with water management kitsThe allowable number of beneficiaries shall be minimum of 2880 people for one solar powered water scheme; hence estimated total beneficiaries 63,360 considering 22 solar powered water supply and borehole rehabilitation.2. Sanitation responseSI propose to intervene in the targeted 5 LGA’s (MMC, Jere, Dikwa, Ngala and Monguno) to cover the identified gaps, through the following activities: - Construction of 4x2 latrines 60 units in Ngala, 80 units in Monguno and 50 units each in MMC, Jere and Dikwa- Construction of block of 5 latrines: 120 units in Dikwa, 100 in Ngala and 30 in Monguno.- Construction of Multi-family latrine: 20 units in MMC, 20 in Jere, 125 in Dikwa, 180 in Ngala and 300 in Monguno- Rehabilitation of latrines: 200 in MMC, 200 in jere, 350 in Dikwa, 500 in Ngala and 600 units in Monguno- Distributing 50 latrine maintenance kit to each Multifamily latrine beneficiaries in Ngala, Dikwa and Monguno.Each drop hole of latrine and showers are targeting 20 and 50 beneficiaries respectively; 3. Hygiene Promotion activitiesSI propose to intervene in the targeted 5 LGA’s to cover the identified gaps, through the following Activities:- Hygiene promotion in the targeted 5 LGAs with the use of IEC for improved message dissemination.- Drainage cleaning campaign in all the 5 LGAs; 12 communities in Ngala, 8 in MMC, 8 in Jere and 12 in each of the remaining LGAs (Dikwa and Monguno).- Waste management committee training along with waste management kit distribution for the 52 communities identified.- 400 WaSH dignity kit distribution in MMC and Jere and 500 units in Dikwa, Monguno and Ngala.- Menstrual Health Management (MHM) targeting 300 beneficiaries in each of the 5 LGA’sDescription:The proposed WASH hygiene promotion activities will be implemented in an environment that is well known by SI through previous and ongoing operations. This will be done by engagement and training of the Community hygiene promoter (CHP); however, 50 CHPs will be used in MMC/Jere while for the remaining 4 LGA’s 50 CHPs will be used each. During training and daily activities, CHP this will provide the beneficiaries with key messages during home visits and awareness raising campaign. Key messages will also be displayed on IEC materials for proper messages dissemination. 500 hygiene kits will also be distributed to as part of the intervention.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Solidarites strives to include all stakeholders as much as possible in its intervention. Joint planning and review meetings will be held between the project staff and relevant stakeholders involved in the project from the camp and community level to capture lessons learnt for better programming.Solidarites will also involve communities via staff hiring (Daily Workers, CHPs), so that they can benefit from the project interventions, not only as beneficiaries but also actively supporting implementation of activity. Solidarites will mainstream Core Humanitarian Standards to ensure quality and effectiveness of assistance and demonstrate greater accountability to affected populations. Throughout the project, SI will strive to respect the five Accountability to Affected Population commitments, as defined by the IASC. The project beneficiaries are systematically included in the decision making process at each and every step of project cycle. SI is committed to ensuring beneficiaries’ involvement to ensure the project fits their expectations and needs and to avoid negative side effects. The methodology followed is inspired by URD guidebook for participation. SI will ensure the respect of the Do No Harm principle through analyzing the possible unintended effect of each step of the project implementation to better mitigate them (i.e., beneficiary selection criteria etc.) and ensure that distributions do not put beneficiaries at risk during or after the distribution of inputs, through the presence of MEAL team observers during all distributions and PDMs. In addition, SI will ensure that its existing Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM), managed by the MEAL department, will be accessible to all beneficiaries under this project, and that affected communities have been properly sensitized on how to access and use the CRM.
