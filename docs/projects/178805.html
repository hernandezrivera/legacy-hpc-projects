Project Summary

The project main goal is to response to the core needs of the vulnerable displaced families and host communities through provision of water tanks which will lead to safe access to clean water and raise the people hygienic conditions, and will provide long-term sustainable resilience to those most in need for WASH assistance. The targeted beneficiaries are those who have been forced from their homes in Ras Al Ain and its countryside, where around 19,000 families comprise around 84,500 people have fled the insecure situation to southern safe areas mainly to Tal Tamer town and its countryside as well to Hassakeh city and its countryside, and many people families have been displaced multiple times who exposed to grave protection threats, before resorting to schools and other collective shelters, makeshift camps, as well a huge numbers of displaced families have been accommodated by host communities who have been suffering poor conditions of livelihoods, in which they become under further pressure, who have been already exhausted due the Syrian crisis which enters its tenth year, where the displaced families and the host communities continue to bear the burden of ongoing conflict which marked by unapparelled suffering due to the shortage of humanitarian support which they are in need to urgent humanitarian assistance and services. This intervention aims to provide effective humanitarian assistance to meet the immediate survival needs of WASH materials of the most extremely vulnerable people including women and children especially children less five years old to water-borne infections and other diseases related to personal hygiene. The unavailability of, or lack of access to a continuous safe water source put everyone at risk of disease transmission. the adopted criteria stipulates the targeted families will be poor female headed families, poor widows, poor divorced women with her children, people with disabilities and families without incomes who have been originated from Ras Al Ain areas as well to support the host communities who have been accommodating displaced families.
The targeted IDPs and locations has been selected from the database available at the association in close coordination with DoW, DoSA, SARC and NGOs which the targeted families have been living in areas of Tel Tamer town and countryside and in Al Hassakeh city and its countryside.
In this regard, since mid 2013, Allouk water plant in Ras Al Ain city, around 80 km away in the northern countryside of Hassakeh, is considered the only source for provision of drinking water for the population including the displaced families in Hassakeh city and Tel Tamer town and surrounding settlements.
Allouk water station has completely stopped for the twenty times in a row over the last two years depriving more than one million of population including the displaced families from getting their needs of potable water, where the population as a last resort, despite the available ground water has been unsafe, started digging surface well (from 10-70 m depth) to get their needs of water which led to negatively impacts on the health in particular the sick and children including children less than 5, women and other vulnerable groups.
This proposal has been designed mainly in order to provide water tanks to increase the capacity of household storage of potable water that provided by humanitarian actors by water tracking in particularly in the period of cutting off the water that supplied by Allouk water station and to support WASH response to the vulnerable displaced families as well the host community households with access to improved WASH facilities, the reason behind this proposal has been as the most displaced families have no water containers or they have old and dirty small ones, The proposal will focus on WASH sector with longer-term solution with proper effective exit strategy, as it is the main demands of the crisis affected people as well the host communities.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project activities will be implemented by an ad hoc distribution committee composed for this purpose, which comprises association qualified field staff, and representatives of qualified beneficiaries including women which will be selected by the beneficiaries themselves in proper way to represent the targeted families, local community and local municipality at the areas of interventions, DoSAL and SARC and UN agency partner. The distribution operations will adhere to the workplan and timelines as of the PCA which is expected to start at the beginning of next July in case the required formalities have been completed. The association approach that followed to implement the project activities in most of the projects has been as the following: 1. Carrying out the required field assessments to identify the targeted beneficiaries and computerize all the obtained data,2. Purchase the assistance items throughout open tender process.3. Receiving the materials and stockpiled at the warehouse.4. Composing ad hoc distribution committee that attending short awareness session especially on code of conduct. 5. Launching the distribution activities as of the work plan.6. Carry out multiple field monitoring and evaluation visits.7. Reporting and liquidation of the project.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Security risk: the security is unpredictable although the areas of intervention is enjoying calm security situation, Likelihood: low. Impact: medium. Mitigation: BICA closely monitors the changes in the overall security situation in targeted areas and adjust accordingly its operations, adapting its protocols, systems and contingency plans, to minimize safety and security risks for its personnel and beneficiaries, and ensure safe transportation and storage of goods. BICA engages community leaders, authorities in order to determine the most suitable and safe locations for distribution, and to ensure that all target beneficiaries are informed including people with disabilities about distribution dates and locations. BICA ensures that distribution points are accessible to the most vulnerable including women and persons with disabilities.Fraud risk: Aid diversion, fraud, and corruption. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: diversification of supplier, triangulation of information, standard monitoring procedures in place, security plan and risks assessments tables updated and followed. Financial risk: Substantive devaluation of USD against the Syrian pound. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: The eventuality of significant changes in the value of the Syrian currency will be closely monitored and, if materialized, timely communicated with OCHA office in Qamishli and options discussed.COVID 19 risk: Vulnerable households including persons with disabilities are mostly affected and in need of support. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: Support the beneficiaries to implement the health protocol and the preventive measures against the challenges posed by COVID-19and to adhere to wearing masks.
