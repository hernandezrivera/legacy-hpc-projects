Project Summary

Currently 917,884 Rohingya refugees live in overcrowded shelters across 33 camps with limited access to essential services like food assistance, SMSD, Shelter, WASH, Protection, Education and Health care etc. BRAC is providing support from the beginning to these vulnerable groups and will continue the support by addressing their special needs.
Through the proposed programme, BRAC will promote the dignity and well-being of conflict-displaced communities through shelter support offering security, safety, and protection for a targeted 7,742 Rohingya refugees and 2744 host community population. Shelter project activities will be implemented through a community led approach. BRAC Shelter team will collaborate with the community, Site Management Sector (SMS), CiC official in each camp and determine the feasibility, location and requirement of shelters and site improvement interventions such as construction of household level drains, stairs, retaining walls, BFS road, sand bag pathway, ramps, handrails and solar lamps. Also, BRAC will take initiative to distribute solar panel in the host community and carry out repair and maintenance of solar panels in the Rohingya camps. BRAC is proposing interventions in 10 Rohingya camps (Camp-1E, 1W, 3, 4Ext, 7, 21, 26, 27, KRC and NRC) and repair and construction of vulnerable Jhupri shelter in 6 unions of the host communities which are adjacent to camps. BRAC will continue the work in the host communities in collaboration with Assistant Commissioner of Land, Ward members, Para committees, the Union Parishad Chairman, the Upazila Chairman and the Upazila Nirbahi Officer (UNO) in Ukhiya (Haldia Palong, Jalia Palong, Ratna Palong unions) & Teknaf (Hnilla, Whhykong and Baharchhara unions) Upazilla.
Under the second objective BRAC will carry out following activities-
Shelter improvement considering the inclusiveness and climate resilience (Steel Footing, Treated Bamboo, GI Ware, etc.) for 813 HHs

Ramp repair & maintenance (CC) for disable, elder, women and child etc. in CAMPs and Host- 100 meters

Re-construction accessibility of BFS Road (Host & CAMPs)- 25000 sqm
EVI Household repair & maintenance with considering the inclusiveness and climate resilience in CAMPs- for 800 HHs
EVI Household, repair & maintenance with considering the inclusiveness and climate resilience in Host Community- for 90 HHs
Solar panel/lamps Installation including repair maintenance in Host- 490 sets
Solar panel/lamps Installation including repair maintenance in CAMPs- 1613 sets
Slope Protection (Bamboo mate Guide wall) in Host and CAMPs- 2000 sqm
Along with the regular activity, BRAC’s M&E team will conduct Post Distribution Monitoring of shelter/NFI materials distribution and shelter quality. BRAC’s community feedback process collects information from beneficiary households in design, implementation and completion stages based on which the shelter sector responds. The project’s M&E plan will track sector achievement as per target, on-going assessment, data collection and analysis. As per BRAC’s strict safe-guarding policy, all staff will be oriented on PSEA/safeguarding and gender mainstreaming, to ensure a safe environment for both beneficiaries and staff, and interventions tailored as per needs of the vulnerable groups.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Since the refugee influx in 2017, BRAC’s Shelter Sector has conducted periodical and annual needs assessments and in collaboration and close coordination with the Government of Bangladesh, Donors and different stakeholders have strived to provide the shelter and site improvement services.The Population Demographic Data provided by UNHCR shows that 51.4% of the total population living inside the camps are female. Also, 52% of the population is aged under 18. Four percent of the population is aged above 60. These are the most vulnerable groups as they are not able to carry materials and construct their houses. There is a considerable number of people with disabilities living in the camps who can neither repair their own shelters nor volunteer to repair with the NGOs. BRAC is providing support from the beginning to these vulnerable groups and will continue the support by addressing the special needs of the vulnerable groups.BRAC’s shelter sector conducted a needs assessment for 2023 on shelter and site improvement through focus group discussion (FGD) and door to door household survey to identify the needs of the community. The need assessment determined the type of needs and assistance required to address existing gaps and ensure and durable support to the community. Need assessment has identified large areas to improve and strengthen as many of the shelters are on steep and deforested slopes of sand and clay which face additional risks of landslides. The joint assessment of the Shelter and Non-Food Items (SNFI) Sector and Needs and Population Monitoring (NPM) on the Shelter Performance Standards reflects, 40% of the shelters did not have safety from soil erosion and landslides. Only 18% of shelters met the minimum standard for at least one internal partition wall to provide privacy and rain water entered through the roof covering of 56% of the shelters. In the host community, the majority of households (63%) reported having lived in vulnerable shelter types and 33% HHs reported that no improvement has been made to their shelters despite reporting problems (J-MSNA-December 2021). In the host communities, 63% reported that they had no money to buy materials and 24% had no money to pay for the labour. Also, the need assessment revealed that the condition of the current shelters, lack of privacy within these shelters as well as the terrain has a serious impact on the physical and psychological well-being of the refugees, especially for women, children, elderly and persons with specific needs (PwSN). Furthermore, both host communities and Rohingya HHs in camp have reported not having enough light. Consequently, solar light amongst the commonly requested NFI items. Despite the efforts of the humanitarian response participation of women, older and other vulnerable groups is still low. BRAC will train volunteers who will sensitise the community and help the women, girls, elderly and other vulnerable groups participate in the decision making process. As well as ensure their access to the to information about services, complaints and feedback mechanisms so that they can raise their voice and seek redressal.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Rationalisation will ensure that common strategies, systems, protocols, and tools are used across all the camps. This will lead towards a standardised and cost-effective approach to providing equitable services to refugees and also ensure a minimum standard of service delivery by different partners. BRAC has adopted an integrated approach for service delivery in the camps. BRAC's Shelter and Site Management will work in an integrated manner to ensure holistic support through training and awareness sessions for the refugees, especially for these different vulnerable groups. A participatory land and infrastructure upgrade programme will be developed to include refugees of all groups in the maintenance and improvement in the CAMPs. Post-distribution monitoring will be conducted after every distribution to ensure that the HHs have utilized the shelter materials. To equip the community and ensure HHs participation, a technical team will provide support through conducting basic training in shelter construction and repairs. BRAC has also developed a good infrastructure including offices and warehouses and developed the prerequisite relationships with other camp actors, host communities, refugees, community groups/committees and the government particularly the DC, UNO, Chairmen, RRRC and the CICs.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Humanitarian protection is about improving safety, well-being and dignity for crisis-affected populations. BRAC always focuses on non-discriminatory access to support and services, identifying the most vulnerable and their specific needs, safe and dignified access to basic services, community participation and empowerment, strengthening positive community protection capacity. BRAC will address protection issues including child protection, gender-based violence, sexual exploitation and abuse, protection of persons with disabilities as well as specific protection issues that arise in disasters and emergencies through information-sharing, advocacy or referral to specialized actors. The Shelter Sector will identify the needs of all age and gender groups through a need assessment and provide services to populations with identified vulnerabilities.

3.2 - Gender

BRAC ensures that special needs of all gender groups are identified, taken into consideration and guided by the Gender Policy. Following its Gender Policy, BRAC will emphasise empowerment and inclusivity of vulnerable groups, irrespective of gender. BRAC’s Shelter sector will consider how gender and age affect a person's vulnerability, opportunity, and ability to make independent and informed choices. Tolerance and respect for all regardless of race, gender, religion, caste, national origin, sexual orientation, age and physical disability are included in all programmes. BRAC will ensure its all staff are trained on gender mainstreaming and all facilities are gender friendly. To understand the different needs of affected people based on gender, age and other diversity, the shelter sector will conduct a needs assessment with specific questions related to shelter, distribution of household items. The programme will set indicators to measure age and gender segregated data.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

The Shelter and NFI Sector of BRAC will ensure the engagement of different age groups especially children (Age 0 to below 18 years) and elderly people (greater than 60 years). Through Focus group discussions (FGD), and Key informant interviews (KII). Specific needs of different age groups will be identified and will be reflected in program design, development and implementation. Shelter interventions will aim to achieve safe, secure shelter suitable for different age groups such as accessible shelter for children and elderly people. Besides, BRAC has a child safeguarding policy which will be followed throughout this project, which includes avoiding child labour, child harassment and exploitation.

3.4 - Diversity

Diversity refers to the differences based on gender, age, disability, cultural background, sexual orientation, social and economic background. BRAC promotes equity and equality at every stage of project design and provides services to all according to the needs of the people irrespective of gender, age, disability and insists on upholding the rights and dignity of the affected people. BRAC’s Shelter programme will acknowledge diversity through gender, age, religion, sexual orientation, ethnicity, nationality, and disabilities inclusion while selecting target beneficiary as well as the project staff. BRAC has a Gender Response Team (GRT) which will monitor and ensure the inclusion of gender-diversified people in the Shelter programme. Gender equality and diversity practices will make sure that the Shelter services are accessible to people of all gender and age groups, cultural backgrounds and sexual orientations of the Rohingya and host community.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

While designing humanitarian projects, BRAC considers UNHCR’s AAP framework- i) participation and inclusion; ii) communication and transparency; iii) feedback and response; and iv) organisational learning and adaptation. In the designing phase, BRAC’ Shelter team will ensure community participation through consultations. BRAC has a specific Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) to address specific needs of the Rohingya refugees. The shelter sector will collect and analyze gender, age, and disability-disaggregated data, and conduct participatory gender analysis to understand various needs related to shelter, capacities, barriers, aspirations, and identified populations with special shelter support needs. Shelter sector will regularly analyses the feedback/trends/unresolved issues and links with respective organisations/sectors for feedback referral to ensure AAP. Based on the community needs, interventions, planning and priority of the programme can be modified.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes. The project will undertake slope protection activities like household level drum-sheet retaining wall, bamboo made guide wall and Brick road development works and in the host community under DRR/DRM elements. Besides, to protect the environment and make the activities climate resilient, tree plantation and planting grass tarphien, and pigeon peas (Cajanus cajan) will be continued. Furthermore, BRAC has an Environmental and Social Safeguard Framework (ESSF), which applies a science-based precautionary approach to project design and implementation. BRAC’s all interventions anticipate and address any potential harm before it happens. Where there are threats of serious or irreversible damage, circumstances of not having scientific certainty will not be used as a reason for avoiding cost-effective measures to prevent environmental degradation in BRAC’s project implementation mechanism.
