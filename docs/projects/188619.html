Project Summary

Syria remains one of the largest and most complex humanitarian emergencies globally with 13.9 million people in need (PiN) of assistance. At the beginning of 2022 the annual Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for the Food Security and Agriculture (FSA) Sector estimated that around 12 million people were facing acute food insecurity and in need of various forms of food, agriculture and livelihood-related assistance in Syria. The worsening economic crisis, agriculture production losses, erosion of purchasing power due to the weakened local currency, insecurity in some locations, coupled with the knock-on effects of COVID-19 on food systems and agriculture, has seen the number of food insecure people remaining alarming high.
With life-saving emergency agriculture assistance and the recovery of agriculture-based livelihoods remaining very critical, effective coordination through close cooperation with all partners is fundamental in ensuring that food production and agriculture-based livelihoods assistance reaches those in need in a coordinated and timely manner.
The objective of the proposed action is to strengthen the effectiveness and quality of the food security responses (including all hubs) based on evidence, capacity building and strong coordination within the Food Security and Agriculture (FSA) Sector and across sectors.
Project outputs include:
• Output 1: Accessibility of food security and agriculture information for stakeholders response planning improved.
• Output 2: Capacity for national and subnational Food Security and Agriculture (FSA) sector strengthened.
• Output 3: Capacity for subnational coordination of Food Security and Agriculture (FSA) sector built.
This project is expected to contribute to improved planning (evidence-based decision–making) and supporting timely, efficient and effective responses to the needs of affected populations. Furthermore, the project on sector coordination will ensure that duplication is avoided to ensure efficient use of limited available resources. Overall, the proposed project to support the sector will continue to provide an opportunity for FAO to help assure appropriate emergency response in agriculture and promote early recovery in the crop, livestock and irrigation sectors, through mobilizing resources for both the sector as a whole.

4 - Needs

Overall, the Food Security and Agriculture (FSA) Sector estimates that 12 million people in Syria are facing acute food insecurity, which is at least 55.3% of Syria population. While the food insecure figures have slightly declined by at least 4% compared to last year’s record increase, the number of people facing acute food insecurity remains extremely high and 51% higher than in 2019, pre-Lebanese financial crisis period and before the COVID-19 outbreak. The outlook for the year ahead remains dire more than 50% of the Syrian population remains food insecure. Due to the complex challenges facing the country and the sector, regular and extensive evaluation for appropriateness and timeliness of the FSS responses, quantitative and qualitative analysis to better understand the prevailing dynamics of the situation, remain extremely critical to comprehensively address risks to the sector in a coordinated manner. It is therefore important to have a robust coordination mechanism capable of facilitating timely and effective response. Identifying and reaching affected populations, avoiding duplication, minimizing delays and providing appropriate support is key not only for an effective humanitarian response, but for the expansion of recovery and resilience actions. Building on the achievements of the past year, the sector will continue to play a critical role in 2022 – 2023, through ensuring a well-coordinated approach, centered on the commitment of partners for coordinated response
