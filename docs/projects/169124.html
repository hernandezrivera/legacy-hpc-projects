Project Summary

The Project interventions, aims to target the caregivers of children under 5 as well as the pregnant and lactating women affected from the Beirut Blast that occurred on 4th of August 2020, where it is estimated that 81,000 women of reproductive age are affected, and 4000 Pregnant women in needs for further follow up, Hence through the Nutrition activities, UNICEF with the implementing partners aims to provide essential Nutrition supplies, either through community outreach activities, to distribute Micronutrients supplementation for children under 5 with proper guidance on its use, and through the primary health care centers in coordination, namely for the Malnutrition cases to be treated
This will be accompanied with proper Infant and young child feeding (IYCF) counselling sessions targeting the pregnant and lactating women, and Education sessions on Breastfeeding and complementary feeding for caregivers of children under 5, to be conducted by trained Lactation specialists and community health educators.
and to conduct a Rapid Assessment (SRA) on IYCF and food security with affected population focusing on infant and young child feeding practices and access to food, to ensure proper interventions around IYCF and food assistance
Supplementary feeding support for pregnant and lactating women (PLW of children 0-2 years of age should be prioritised for food/social assistance): in close coordination with WFP and social policy to plan a targeted social assistance/food assistance intervention targeting the most affected pregnant and lactating women .

1 - Needs

The blast occurred at a time of acute economic crisis and inflation. According to WFP, the price of the "minimum survival food basket" (consisting of a monthly per capita ration of the amount of food (e.g., rice, bulgur, pasta, white beans, sugar, sunflower oil, salt and canned meat) had already increased by +116% between September 2019 and June 2020 (of which +34% between May and June alone), in a context where the Lebanese pound experienced a sharp depreciation on the parallel foreign exchange market. The main concern related to access to food is the lack of financial resources. Fuel availability and access to cooking facilities/utensils, are also a major concern.WFP announced that the country wheat stock was destroyed in the explosion. Supply of wheat/grains is expected to last between one month and one month and a half. Infant and young child feeding practices in Lebanon fall short of recommendations with only 14.7% of infants less 6 months exclusively breastfed and not more than 13% of infants 6-23 months meeting the minimum adequate diet for complementary feeding. Despite the presence of a National Infant and Young Child Feeding Policy and a national law (Law 47/2008) that legislates the International Code of Marketing of Breast-milk Substitutes and subsequent resolutions (the Code), field reports are still showing the occurrence of practices that undermine infant and young child feeding, specifically breastfeeding. Call for infant formula donations from the community have been reported as well as complaints from mothers who have been affected by the recent explosion dealing with challenges with breastfeeding. Several grassroots initiatives have emerged with willingness to provide support to infants and young children, however they don’t have the capacity or are not equipped to follow global guidance for humanitarian aid and therefore minimise harm. Community capacity has been mobilized including organisations providing support for mothers with difficulties in breastfeeding. There are also efforts to address potential violations via different channels; individuals contacting organisations or other well-meaning individuals to inform them of the Law/Code or organisations providing support to ensure that artificial support is administered in line with the Law. The joint statement on IYCF has also been disseminated via different platforms. Still, initiatives remain ad hoc and there are no standard operating procedures to organize infant and young child feeding support during the current situation in Lebanon.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: To Provision and distribution of essential Nutrition supplies (High energy biscuits, RUTF- RUSF-MNPs- etc): Distribute essential Nutrition supplies to the Primary health care centres and malnutrition management centers, as well compliment with needed distribution at community level through community outreach activity targeting the most vulnerable CU5 Activity 2: Provision and distribution of Vit A and micro nutrients supplements: to help boosting the immune system of Children , where the provision of Vit A will be done at PHCCs level administered with Measles and MMR vaccinesActivity 3: Community Infant and young child feeding Outreach activities: through UNICEF implementing partners, with trained lactation specialists and community health educators, doing Household visits and community events to target caregivers of children under 5 as well as targeting Pregnant and lactating women, of the affected population.Activity 3: Simple Rapid Assessment (SRA) on IYCF and food security: to be conducted with affected population focusing on infant and young child feeding practices and access to food, to ensure proper interventions around IYCF and food assistance Activity 4: Breastfeeding and complementary feeding education and counselling support for Mothers who are breastfeeding ( community-based mother support groups as well as remote and social media platforms )Activity 5: Supplementary feeding support for pregnant and lactating women (PLW of children 0-2 years of age should be prioritized for food/social assistance): in close coordination with WFP and social policy to plan a targeted social assistance/food assistance intervention targeting the most affected pregnant and lactating women .
