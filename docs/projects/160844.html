Project Summary

DRC proposes to implement a rapid WASH response that aims to provide WASH/GBV services to vulnerable populations through time sensitive integrated WASH assessments and responses that aim to mitigate against WASH related gender based violence (GBV).
DRC will increase household’s access to basic services through the provision of lifesaving WASH services that increase households access to safe water, adequate sanitation facilities, and improve their knowledge of hygiene promotion. Using its tried and tested mobile response, DRC will implement a mobile response team based in Juba with a rapid response capability to cover various hard to reach and newly affected areas across South Sudan. DRC will respond to locations prioritized by the cluster and the Needs Analysis Working Group (NAWG).
Throughout this intervention, DRC will focus on the centrality of protection, ensuring the safety and dignity of persons in communities affected by conflict and displacement and that a do no harm approach will be maintained throughout the project. Accountability to affected populations will be ensured through equal participation of all members of the community.

1 - Needs

Despite the advent of the revitalized peace agreement in September 2018, South Sudan continues to experience unprecedented humanitarian need. Almost seven million individuals are in need of humanitarian assistance, approximately 1.5 million remain internally displaced, and human rights abuses continue to be perpetrated by all parties to the conflict with near impunity. According to the 2019 HNO, approximately six million millions individuals in South Sudan are in need of urgent WASH assistance. Through its mobile response team (MRT), DRC has carried out several WASH assessments in 2019, which found high needs among remote communities in South Sudan (Boma June 2019, Koch May 2019, Rubkona 2019, Duk 2019). During these assessments, DRC observed that many of the handpumps and boreholes are non-functional, forcing households to travel long distances for water or access unclean water sources such as rivers. Women and girls are primarily responsible for water collection in South Sudan, and a lack of locally available water means that they are forced to travel long distances in search of water sources, which increases their protection risks. This was observed in the June 2019 assessment completed by DRC in Boma where poor infrastructure meant that women and girls are accessing water from a nearby river and travelling along a route that exposes them to kidnapping and GBV. Poor water infrastructure does not only increase protection risks but also increases the likelihood of households accessing water from contaminated or untreated water sources such as rivers or open dug wells, which exposes them to waterborne diseases such as cholera. A further challenge observed in South Sudan is poor access to adequate sanitation facilities. According to UNICEF 2019, just 10 percent of the population have access to improved sanitation, with approximately two thirds of the population accessing engaging in open defecation. Open defecation poses a protection risk for girls and women, and also a serious health hazard as it risks contaminating the groundwater. Furthermore, many households lack the resources to invest in hygiene items such as soap and menstrual hygiene items for women and girls, which can lead to a deterioration in hygiene practices. Failing to address the WASH needs of affected population can lead to a diarrhea malnutrition cycle that is prevalent in underserved communities in South Sudan.

2 - Activities

Considering the WASH needs highlighted above, DRC plans to implement an emergency WASH response that will ensure the safe, equitable, and dignified access to basic WASH services. Specifically, the project will align the WASH Cluster Objective 2.2. in that the provision of protection sensitive WASH services will mitigate against GBV risks and increase the number of women and girls with access to safe water. The project also aligns with the WASH Cluster Objective 2.1. Equitable access to quality and inclusive cross sectoral basic services, including safe water, sanitation, and hygiene promotion services. To achieve this, DRC will implement the following activities: Activity 1: Through its mobile response team, DRC will conduct seven WASH and integrated protection/risk assessments and analysis in affected areas. By jointly deploying with DRC existing protection teams, these assessments will also highlight key recommendations for response interventions and any associated protection risks and risk mitigation measures that can be undertaken. Assessment tools that will be utilised on the assessment missions include focus group discussions, key informant interviews, direct observation, and service mapping. The deployment locations will be selected in consultation with the WASH EP&R team. Activity 2: Increase the affected populations access to a safe and sufficient water supply. Based on the findings and recommendations from the WASH assessment, DRC rehabilitate 60 nonfunctional water sources to create access to approximately 30,000 individuals. To support the sustainability of the intervention DRC will train local community members on hand pump repair and provide hand pump repair tool kits/ DRC will also promote water treatment at the household level to improve water quality through the distribution of PUR sachets. DRC will also provide refresher training to WASH committees on the community based operation and maintenance of water infrastructure. Activity 3: Increase access to adequate sanitation facilities; Depending on the findings of the needs assessments highlighted above, DRC will construct emergency communal latrines (175 stances) with handwashing stations. DRC will also construct new and rehabilitate existing institutional latrines (100 stances) in health centres and schools. All locations for the construction of latrines will be screened with a GBV risk analysis and include consultation with women and girls. DRC will ensure that any latrines constructed are culturally appropriate and equipped with doors, sturdy internal locks, privacy fencing and other safety measures to mitigate against GBV risks. Activity 4: Improved hygiene awareness; DRC will carry out gender sensitive hygiene promotion campaigns through community volunteers trained under the project. To ensure the active participant of women and girls, DRC will facilitate separate sessions to facilitate their participation and will also ensure that women and girls are trained as hygiene promoters. The hygiene promoters will be trained on topics such as the critical times of handwashing. To address the emergency needs for menstrual hygiene management (MHM) materials for women and girls, DRC will distribute MHM kits to women and girls of reproductive age. To determine the appropriateness of the items in the MHM kits, DRC will conduct separate consultations with women and girls of reproductive age, including a risk assessment/safety audit of sexual exploitation and abuse, and exposure to other forms of GBV. Finally, DRC will distribute WASH non food items (NFIs), including soap, jerry cans, and buckets to targeted households. Activity 5: To ensure that all activities under this proposed intervention are implemented in a gender sensitive manner that aims to mitigate and reduce WASH related GBV risks, DRC will actively coordinate with GBV actors, including its own protection team, in the response locations and throughout project implementation.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Throughout implementation and where possible, DRC will strive to legally recognise beneficiaries' rights based on the principles of the human rights based approach. The human rights based approach include the following principles: non discrimination, participation, transparency, and accountability. For instance to adhere to the principle of transparency under the human rights based approach, DRC will develop a communication strategy that ensures the appropriate information about the project and its activities are shared with all members of the community including men, women, boys, girls, elderly, youth, persons with disabilities etc, this will also avoid creating unrealistic expectations about the project. In addition, for the principle of non discrimination, DRC will ensure that all services and trainings provided under the project are accessible by all. For instance, in the construction of latrines under the project, DRC will ensure that they are accessible by community members with physical disabilities.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

This project will target both displaced and non displaced populations including IDPs, vulnerable host communities, and returnees. DRC will ensure that both displaced and non displaced populations can access and benefit from the project. DRC will include the views of both displaced and non displaced populations in the needs assessments proposed under this project, through the inclusion of representatives from the IDP, returnee, and host community members in the key informant interviews, focus group discussions, and other assessment tools that will be utilised in the assessment missions. Additional measures to ensure that perspective of displacement affected people is achieved will include; the inclusion of both representatives from both the returnee, host and IDP community in the training for the WASH committee members, hygiene promoters, and hand pump mechanics, and selection of beneficiaries for the WASH NFIs. In addition, DRC will engage with representatives from both displaced and non displaced affected communities in the selection of locations for water and sanitation infrastructure to be rehabilitated or constructed under the project and in the GBV safety audit that will be completed in each location prior to the rehabilitation or construction.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

As part of its programme implementation strategy, DRC seeks to engage local community and government level structures. However, as there is often an absence of formal governance structures in some of the locations in which DRC operates in, DRC will focus on strengthening local community systems to contribute to durable solutions. DRC will build the capacity of the local community systems, such as WASH committees, in the community operation and maintenance of the water infrastructure rehabilitated under the project. DRC will also engage with local community systems to build their capacity in hygiene promotion through the selection and training of community hygiene promoters, both the displaced and host community will be targeted under this activity.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Under the South Sudan Water Policy of 2007, the government is committed to improving access to safe water supply and sanitation facilities and promoting hygiene education for all people living in rural areas. In addition, the government is committed to ensuring that the rapidly growing urban population benefits from access to safe, affordable, and reliable water and sanitation services and promotes efficient development and management of these services. While most of the WASH activities proposed under intervention will be lifesaving and emergency focused, which will increase the target communities access to safe water and adequate sanitation services, DRC will also work to build the capacity of the target community in to assume ownership for the operation and maintenance of the water infrastructure rehabilitated under the project.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The project is not focused on protection, however, it will be implemented through a conflict sensitivity approach that aims to reduce and avoid increasing conflict and tension in the local community, thus contributing to durable solutions for displacement affected populations. For instance, DRC will give consideration to the risk that the rehabilitation of a hand pump can act as a pull factor for populations to move into the area which can create conflict and tension over water resources. DRC will ensure that any rehabilitations are carried out with a conflict sensitive and protection lens to ensure that the intervention does not exacerbate existing or create new tensions, and such interventions will be accompanied by community sensitization activities. For example, awareness raising among the community leaders that it not appropriate to lock the hand pump to restrict IDPs access to water.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Engaging the target communities and increasing their sense of ownership of the project activities will be essential for the sustainability of the intervention and to contribute to the achievement of durable solutions. Under the project, DRC will train WASH committees comprised of local community representatives from all segments of society, including IDPs, returnees, host community members, people with disabilities, men, women, the elderly, and youth. The WASH committees will be trained on the community based operation and maintenance of water infrastructure to ensure that the project is sustainable and that community members continue to have equal access to water beyond the project closeout. In addition, DRC will train community members on basic hand pump repair and maintenance and provide hand pump repair toolkits to the community to enable them to carry out hand pump repairs, thus increasing the sustainability of the project. Finally, all the water points and latrines rehabilitated or constructed under the project will be handed over to local WASH committees following project closeout.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

To ensure accountability to the affected population (APP), DRC adopts a community-based approach, where beneficiaries and local authorities are included in the prioritising, planning and implementation of activities as an important measure of rehabilitation and capacity building. DRC will ensure APP to the target communities during the WASH needs assessments by adopting an inclusive approach that engages a range of community members in the assessment tools utilised by DRC, including key informant interviews and focus group discussions, specifically DRC will seek to engage representatives from the IDP, returnee, host community, men, women, youth, elderly, and persons with disabilities. To ensure that the most vulnerable community members can provide feedback on this project, DRC will deploy multiple beneficiary feedback and complaints mechanisms that give consideration to access issues that different beneficiary members may face, such as literacy, persons with disabilities, women and girls. Examples of community feedback and complaints mechanisms that will be deployed under the project will include the operation of complaints desks to enable community members to flag concerns or complaints in person or anonymously, improving participation and accountability for all affected populations. DRC also has a feedback hotline Number (9686) that beneficiaries can call to register complaints, feedback or requests. DRC will also carry out household community post distribution monitoring through surveys, KKIs, and FGDs. DRC will ensure confidentiality of sensitive and personal information, while also ensuring data protection mechanisms are in place. Informed consent and people centred-approaches are sought throughout the activities, this is measured through information management tools, consent forms, and information sharing protocols. Complaints and feedback submitted to the F-CRM will be reviewed to identify any issues in implementation of the project.
