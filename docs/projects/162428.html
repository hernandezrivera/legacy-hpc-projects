Project Summary

In Gao, in September 2018, the score of food diversity among children from 6 to 23 months was lower than the national rate (14.8% vs. 20.4%). That same month, the score for children aged between 24 months and 5 years was also lower in Gao (26.2% vs. 54%). Harvest was from average to good, whereas in the District of Bourem it was qualified as bad (and more precisely average in Tarkint and very bad in Bamba). Nearly 78% of pastoralist households expect pressure on natural resources, versus 70% in 2017. Rising food prices is the biggest shock that households in Mali are currently facing. Gao is the region with the highest rate of risk in the country (29% in 2018). Consequently, households are likely to rely again on coping mechanisms this year: Gao is, with Kidal, the region where households have relied the most on coping mechanisms (76.1%) in 2018. In Gao, more than 95% of households have relied on seasonal migration as a strategy. According to 25.5% of households, the lack of financial resources is the main constraint in livestock practice according to the survey results (versus 24.3% in 2017 and 36.1% in 2016). In terms of WASH, the access to water in pastoral and agro-pastoral areas is also an acute problem for both nomadic pastoralists and sedentary populations. The water coverage rate is above 47%. However, reality characterized by a low access rate due to the high number of breakdowns and dysfunctions (around 25%). The majority of wells is used for both human needs and watering cattle. Consequently, these water points generally contaminated by animal excrement, poor construction and worse hygiene practices. This results in a significant number of diarrhea mainly affecting children under 5 years old. The presence of returnees and IDPs (Internally Displaced Persons). Access to water remains a highly gendered issue, and Sexual Gender Based Violence (SGBV) cases has noted around the fetching points. The results of the latest SMART survey (September 2018) show that the situation remains worrying in the country because of poor food practices, insecurity and diseases that stem from poor hygiene conditions. Poor Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices, strongly influenced by culture and tradition, also explain the fact that household food security levels are still largely inadequate. The project aims to : (1) Maintain and strengthen the Food Security and Livelihoods of very vulnerable households ; (2) Ensure the access to basic needs and rehabilitate community productive assets; (3) Respond to the critical WASH needs for communities ; and (4) Increase nutrition state of children under 5 and PLW. The project will target 16,800 people. According to the extension to August 2021, in addition to Gao, the project will target Ségou at center of the country. At center level, rehabilitations will concern water points in health facilities and strategic sites identified by the district of Bla in order to support nutrition activities. The rehabilitation of the URENI Unit and the management acute malnutrition will complete the intervention. Specific needs by gender and age will be analyze by sector based on the situation of vulnerable groups (women, girls and boys, people with disabilities, the elderly, women heads of households, etc.). Assistance will be tailor to the specific needs of these vulnerable groups in both communities and IDPs. The assistance will be analyzed from the angle of transversal protection so as not to harm beneficiaries (Do not Harm) and respect their dignity. AAH has a feedback management mechanism and will implement activities in coordination with humanitarian actors and regional sectoral thematic groups. The results will be regularly share with the national clusters who will organize regular monitoring missions. The project will be implement with an involvement of communities and Beneficiaries at all stages and a close collaboration with technical services and development actors to lead to sustainability.
