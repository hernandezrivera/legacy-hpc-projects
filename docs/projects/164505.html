Project Summary

Non-communicable diseases (NCDs) are the leading cause of death worldwide, with particularly higher rates in low- and middle-income countries. In Syria, an estimated 45% of all deaths are related to NCDs according to the WHO. In addition, WHO estimates that 600,000 people in Syria are suffering from severe mental health (MH) disorders and another 4 million from mild to moderate mental disorders. In the context of a humanitarian and economic crisis, fragile health care systems are unable to respond to the growing prevalence of NCDs and MH in a timely and proper manner, and where these exist, vulnerable people have great difficulty in affording health services, treatment and medication. Internal studies conducted in Salamieh District indicate that the top the driving factor for households to engage in negative coping strategies is a drastic attempt to access lifesaving health services and medicine.
The purpose of this project is to support vulnerable people with NCDs and/or MH to access quality and lifesaving health services in Salamieh District. To do this, the project will (1) improve the capacity and quality of health service delivery for AKF’s Health Counseling Center (HCC) – the only facility in Salamieh District providing services for NCDs and MH; (2) provide cash assistance for 9 months to 2,400 patients (targeting 50% women) with NCDs and/or MH, prioritizing vulnerable women of reproductive age (15-49), persons with disabilities (PwDs), and the elderly (50+); and (3) educate patients and their families about NCDs and MH through awareness-raising sessions. The project will establish high level of coordination with local stakeholders including health authority, local NGOs, and SARC through establishing a joint committee (with 50% females’ representation) who will be involved in all project stages with supervision over the project implementation.
The project is designed with close attention to the following:
• Active participation of girls and women with representation of females in clinical and administrator positions.
• Establishing protocols for clinical care of GBV survivors and safe and ethical case documentation.
• Raise awareness of all health personnel on basic issues related to GBV, women’s/human rights.
• Ensure safe healthcare environment with close consideration to patients’ privacy and confidentiality as follows: i) providing safe and confidential escorts to HCC; ii) schedule convenient opening times during daylight hours along with installing adequate lighting; iii) ensure non-stigmatizing and confidential place such as planning for same entrance for NCDs & MH services.
• As a part of NCDs services, AKF will provide counseling on healthy lifestyle which cover nutrition recommendations.

4 - Needs

Non-communicable diseases (NCDs) are the leading cause of death worldwide, with particularly higher rates in low- and middle-income countries. In Syria, an estimated 45% of all deaths are related to NCDs according to the WHO. In addition, WHO estimates that 600,000 people in Syria are suffering from severe mental health (MH) disorders and another 4 million from mild to moderate mental disorders.In Salamieh District, NCDs are significantly high, affecting 25% of people within a given household, and particularly those over the age of 50. Based on an AKF study conducted in 2019 in Salamieh District, an estimated 30,000 individuals have one or more NDCs, and/or MH disorders, and are desperately struggling to afford health care. Female headed households have slightly higher percentages of individuals with chronic illness (28.5% for FHHs, 24.2% MHHs), as well as per capita health expenditures. The study indicated that average household income of a vulnerable household was SYP 76,000/month, however the average annual expenses for health services related to NCDs were an estimated SYP 119,000 (SYP 16,000 for the clinic visit, SYP 32,000 for lab tests and imaging, and SYP 71,000 for medications). In the same study, a survey of 324 patients with NCDs indicated that the average number of clinical visits was 10/patient/year; out of which 5 are visits to private clinics due to the unavailability of quality specialized care in public clinics. Only 14% of these patients had health insurance. The same study indicated that 32% of patients sought healthcare services outside Salamieh District, which on average cost nearly SYP 12,000/travel. A separate survey with 279 cancer patients, showed that average annual health care expenses were around SYP 614,000, with the top driving factor for households to engage in negative coping strategies is a desperate attempt to access lifesaving health services and medication, which is also the top households’ expenditure (84% of respondents).

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will support vulnerable people with NCDs and MH disorders to access quality lifesaving health services in Salamieh District through a gender- and protection-sensitive approach. 1- Improving the capacity and quality of health service delivery of the HCC: (a) Support the HCC with human, administrative and operational resources needed for NCD and MH service delivery, which includes salaries for 12 current staff and volunteers, half of whom are female.a) Develop HCC standard operating procedures (SOPs) and referral system.b) Establish a health service point to provide consultation and follow-up services, including home counselling sessions for patients who have difficulty with mobility 2- Providing cash assistance for 9 months to 2,400 patients (targeting 50% women) with NCDs and/or MH disorders, prioritizing vulnerable women of reproductive age, PwDs and the elderly to help cover health costs such as checkups, lab tests, imaging, and medicines, transport, and accommodation expenses. a) Identify list of vulnerable people with NCDs and/or MH disorders using HCC records and AKF’s vulnerability database, and verify in consultation with specialized doctors b) Classify selected beneficiaries based on the following categories: Category A) 1,000 patients with one disease (NCD or MH disorder) to receive SYP 10,000/month; Category B) 800 patients with two diseases (NCDs and/or MH disorders) to receive SYP 15,000/month; Category C) 600 patients with three diseases (NCDs and/or MH disorders) or cancer to receive SYP 20,000/month3- Educating patients and families about NCDs and MH through awareness-raising sessions, which will be preceded by developing materials and conducting ToT for 8 health educators. Process and outcome monitoring activities include baseline and end-line assessments of the target groups, case studies, ongoing feedback with project beneficiaries, post-project monitoring, household surveys and Focus Group Discussions with project stakeholders.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

A: Access to HCC and/or cash distribution site may be physically challenging for patients or may expose them to injuries or discomfort. Likelihood: Medium; Impact: Medium. AKF, in coordination with FMFI, will allow beneficiaries to assign a member of their families to collect cash assistance for them in case of their inability to do so. AKF will assign a voluntary team to assist beneficiaries at the HCC or cash distribution site, and HCC will allow home visit for counselling when patients are unable to come especially for elderly and people with disability.B: Access to HCC and/or cash distribution site may expose patients to safety concern such as theft, GBV, or harassment. Likelihood: Low; Impact: Low. FMFI will ensure cash distribution site has suitable waiting space equipped with sufficient chairs, along with separate lines for women and men to receive their cash assistance. Cash assistance will be distributed in day hours prioritizing people coming from rural areas, people with disability, women, and elders. Compliant reporting mechanisms will be available to report any cases of violence or harassment, followed by appropriate responses.C: Mental Health patients may be subject to discrimination due to negative cultural perception associated with Mental Health diseases. Likelihood: Medium; Impact: Medium. To mitigate such risk, HCC will take special measures to secure the privacy for MH patients and keep their medical records secure with high level of confidentiality. Regular and confidential monitoring and compliant reporting system will help to identify and investigate any cases of discrimination and take actions against it. AKF will facilitate awareness raising on MH as a way forward to change negative cultural perceptions and encourage positive interaction with MH patients.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk1: Difficulty to reach eligible patients identified through AKF list of vulnerable families and HCC records due to change in patients contact information such as changing residence. Mitigation: AKF will work through many communications channels such as key informants, CBOs to reach these patients and update their information contacts.Risk2: Death of patients receiving cash assistance. Mitigation: AKF will prepare additional list of eligible patients to replace the diseased ones and receive cash assistance for the remaining period. Risk3: Lack of local qualified physicians. Mitigation: AKF invest in capacity building of available local qualified physicians. Risk4: Exchange rate fluctuations and/or inflation may affect the level of funds available at the field level. Mitigation: AKF regularly monitor exchange rate gains and losses and market prices to consider the impact on the project implementation. Risk5: The deterioration of the security situation limiting movement of staff and beneficiaries. Mitigation: AKF regularly monitors the local security situation. In the event that mobility of staff and beneficiaries is deemed a high risk, AKFS will delay the implementation of activities until the situation is safe for travel and implementation.
