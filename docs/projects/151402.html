Project Summary

The project will ensure protection mechanism are in place for children including gender sensitive hygiene facilities, psychosocial support, teachers training and communities in identifying and preventing gender based violence against girls in school. The training and support of teacher and community education committee(CEC) will ensure to provide equal opportunity for men and women and will provide critical skills for duty bearers to identify how gender roles create risk for girls and challenge to their enrollment and attendance in schools, inequalities in classrooms and ensure that both boys and girls are able to access safe learning space in which they are protected from violence (both in schools and in the community).The project will employ several strategies to promote girls and boys education among the pastoral communities in Bakol,Bay and Hiran where HIDIG have active presence through social mobilization campaign, gender dis-aggregated WASH facilities in schools. The project will specifically address the issue of equity by targeting IDPs marginalized boys and girls in the community through provision of school feeding and learning materials.Through this project HIDIG intends to ensure the target schools are provided with safe drinking water hence improving schools retention and enrollments.
The project will also focus on pedagogical component related to mobilization, establishments of 24 an/or rehabilitation temporary learning space, training and mentoring of 207 teachers and 90 community education committee, 24 child to child club and delivery of teacher’s incentives including teaching and learning materials with regular monitoring and supervision.
HIDIG project seek to involve officials from both south west and Hirshabelle regional state in the Ministry of education in participating project inception, training, monitoring and supervision in different level. HIDIG will also engage other partners working in these area and education sub cluster on information sharing and coordination to the affected boys and girls in the target regions.
Finally, the target communities remain to be main partners in this project and will be front and centre in driving the activities including establishment of temporary learning, social mobilization, Emergency school feeding, community education committee participation and child to child club.

Needs

Somalia still has the highest score on risk level in the world with long-standing political instability, armed conflict, environmental stress and weak existing governance structures. According to Somalia humanitarian needs over (Somalia education cluster) an estimated 1.8 M School going children are in need of urgent accelerated education response. Ceelbarde, Rabdhure, Baidoa and Baladweyne in Bakol, Bay and Hiran region where HIDIG have active presence indicates that an estimated 184,800 boys and girls in the host communities and IDPs are in dire needs of education intervention in addition most of these areas were highly affected by both drought and flash floods during Gu 2018 that left many exiting ABEs been destroyed, the school going children in Ceelbarde and Rabdhure were mostly affected by lack of learning continuity due to community livelihood nature as well as conflict and schools closure on the other hand Baidoa and Baladweyne were also affected by high number of IDPs in which most vulnerable boys and girls were expose to protection related risk thus in general high number of boys and girls lack access to learning opportunities hence exposes young boys and girls to child labor for their families foods, recruitment to armed militia groups and early or forced marriages for the young school going girls. HIDIG runs 18 schools in Ceelbarde, Rabdhure, Baidoa and Baladweyne,however, lack of trained teachers, poor school infrastructure, and lack of teacher’s incentives as well as community education committee, poor capacity for both teachers and CEC coupled with poor WASH facilities, however, HIDIG intends to provide better immediate access to quality learning to 2700 school going children (1500 girls), trained 207 teachers and 90 CECs as well as provide schools feeding program to 2700 boys and girls (1500) as well as establishing temporary learning spaces and safe drinking water to these vulnerable children boys,girls in Bay,Bakol and Hiran target region.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The project is meant to build community resilience by strengthening local education infrastructure and thus provide timely action, maintaining momentum of the response, and expanding reach in underserved areas promoting equity. Based on the cluster objectives, the response strategy includes the need to deliver a core package of integrated services, where priority will be given to the delivery of equitable access to basic education services, while simultaneously addressing underlying causes of exposure to the risk of school attacks, poor enrollment and retention rate. To increase community uptake of provided services, community level demand creation will be strengthened. HIDIG will strengthen capacity of teachers by training them on life-skill, school management as well as community education committee. Communities will be involved in project designation and will be part of the committees supporting district level education committees. Working with other actors will ensure leverage among themselves and providing maximum support to target communities strengthening their capacities in various aspects.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

This project targets areas with high concentration of IDPs including Baidoa and Baladweyne and pastoralist in Ceelbarde and Rabdhure therefore possible community tensions between IDPs and host communities is a risk for displaced populations who may face animosity from host communities who already feel overburdened and with limited services. With the IDPs living in camps, many of them not legalized, constructing temporary learning spaces may be a challenge exposing them to lack of comprehensive and timely care. To overcome these challenges, HIDIG will ensure IDPs have access to a comprehensive access to services within the IDP camps by establishing temporary learning spaces in consultation with the host communities an education authorities. Where not possible in areas like Ceelbarde and Rabdhure pastoral communities will be provided with mobile school where HIDIG will procure donkey and carts to ensure schools are integrated within the already existing dugsi to enable provide needed services. Based on the education Cluster Guideline on inclusion, both displaced and host communities will be consulted about needs and assess whether education interventions could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the IDPs and host communities.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Lack of desegregated data impedes well planned interventions based on needs. HIDIG intends to overcome this by having a need based assessment and disaggregated data so that the special needs of youth out of school and those with special gender needs are taken care of. Due to poor education infrastructure, many centers/schools do not have proper planning so that those with disabilities can easily seek services. Also, services will be ensured that it reach the most vulnerable pastoralist in the far areas through identification and prioritization so that discrimination and exclusion is avoided. Location, distance and access routes to the education sites and services will be accessible to all members of the boys and girls, including the most vulnerable IDPs children. To support the inclusion of women teachers HIDIG will support training of female members through employment and capacity enhancement. To ensure equity, school mobile/outreach teams will be used to reach rural communities.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Building community structures including local temporary learning space that will ensure tension is minimized. Staff will be trained on human rights based approach where all people should be treated equally, especially those who ran from conflict and violence. Communities fleeing violence will be immediately supported through access to immediate education services without any discrimination. Where IDPs are involved, schools that support them will be built in consultation with them and the host communities. Marginalized groups will be prioritized so that they don’t feel excluded, which can otherwise aggravate their situation if they have run from violence. School seasonality or schools days-This should be considered for the purpose of ensuring vulnerable boys and girls safety are taken care of, poor timing of schools that is evening teaching might endanger learners safety and are susceptible for invasion of militia, HIDIG and consultation with the ministry will ensure timely learning for the venerable children, in addition to this constructing schools in nearby military camps is danger the life of the vulnerable population, hence HIDIG will ensure learning space are constructed in far from military base to avoid the cross fire between the militia group and the military thus reducing the risk of explosion and gender based violence against vulnerable girls.
