Project Summary

The cumulative effects of years of prolonged conflict, the COVID-19 Pandemic, chronic vulnerabilities and weak essential services have left over 1.35 million Palestinians across the oPt being affected by public health risks associated with insufficient access to WASH services and in immediate need of humanitarian assistance in 2022 as they are as per (2022 WASH HNO draft). In the Gaza Strip, the ability of the service providers to ensure satisfactory operation of over 500 WASH facilities has been undermined by funding gaps, also, more than 290 WASH infrastructure were affected during May escalations, constraining access to water and sanitation services for more than 1.2 million Palestinians. This situation has reduced households access to adequate water and sanitation services, weakening Gaza households’ capacity to maintain their health, livelihoods, social and hygiene status, of particular concern in light of the ongoing pandemic. Women, children, elders and Persons with Disabilities (PWD) are the most affected by this shortage in WASH services. Women in particular are affected disproportionally, leaving them in a struggle to maintain minimum hygiene and ensure the availability of drinking water. In line with the WASH cluster objective #2: “Ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable safely-managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinians”, this project is proposed with the overall objective to enhance the protection of the most vulnerable female-headed households at higher risk of protection concerns and exposure to COVID-19, due to having substandard and inadequate WASH conditions. The project will provide emergency WASH assistance and capacity building support to FHHs, prioritizing households with PWDs, elderlies and people with chronic diseases. Specifically, the project intends to respond by providing an appropriate, integrated WASH response for the most vulnerable FHHs which are affected by the loss of sole breadwinners and the protracted humanitarian and pandemic crises, including women, children, elderly and people with disabilities in order preserve the dignity of vulnerable beneficiaries through improving access to water for drinking and domestic use, sustaining access to protected, and gender-sensitive sanitation facilities and promoting personal and environmental hygiene among the targeted Households. The intervention will complement the cash assistance, which these FHHs receive from IRW’s sponsorship programme, contributing to achieving better outcomes for their families’ members. The project will target 50 of the FHHs in Rafah governorate in the Gaza Strip (75 women, 55 men, 60 boys, 60 girls), that are critically underserved in relation to WASH services as revealed in the Rapid Needs Assessment carried by IRW and its partners in June 2021 and found a need for urgent WASH intervention to improve safety and privacy and to address the protection concerns of women, girls and boys members of the most vulnerable female headed households. Based on the findings of the rapid needs assessments, IRW and partner have identified the following locations for 2022 interventions: Rafah Camp, Alshabwra, al'iidarii, Tal Sultan and Rafah Algharbi. IRW is the WASH cluster focal point in Rafah, and it has the staff, experience and practical presence in the area, thus creating a conducive environment for successful operations. Throughout the project life cycle, IRW will coordinate with WASH cluster and partners, the local authorities and other stakeholders including affected community, and will depend on a local partner on ground for implementing the activities ensure maximum utilization of resources. Throughout the proposed intervention, IRW will monitor any protection concerns (GBV, SEA, child protection issues) by building the capacity of project team and partner and raising awareness of beneficiaries and through referral and Complaint and feedback mechanisms.

1 - Needs

WASH sector in Gaza Strip face systematic challenges and particular vulnerabilities driven by the longstanding Israeli occupation and Gaza blockade, the internal Palestinian divide, the COVID-19 outbreak, and most recently the May’s escalation, along with the fiscal and socioeconomic consequences of these factors. The ability of WASH authorities, partners and service providers to upgrade, maintain and operate at full capacity more than 500 water and sanitation facilities have been undermined by significant funding gaps, the Israeli import restrictions on ‘dual-use materials and equipment”, and the poor governance of the WASH sector as well as the limited technical and financial capacities. Moreover, more than 290 WASH infrastructures in the Gaza Strip were affected during the May 2021 escalation, constraining access to water and sanitation services for more than 1.2 million Palestinians, yet the WASH service providers are still struggling to fully restore and maintain the functionality of WASH services in the Gaza Strip. Insides houses, the hostilities also left many families lost essential items such as water tanks, or have their sanitation facilities and networks damaged. People in the Gaza Strip are exposed to public health risks associated with poor water quality, poor wastewater collection and treatment, lack of storm water infrastructure and lack of proper hygiene practices. According to HNO -2022, over 1.35 million Palestinians across the oPt are affected by public health risks associated with insufficient access to WASH services and will be in immediate need of humanitarian assistance in 2022. When adequate and appropriate water, sanitation and hygiene are not available, major health and protection hazards can result. In particular, the lack of access to adequate WASH affects women disproportionally, due to both biological and cultural factors. In addition to meeting women’s specific practical needs, WASH is also essential for their social and economic development, contributing towards gender equality and the realization of their rights. Understanding vulnerabilities and social relations is absolutely essential in assessing, designing and implementing an appropriate WASH programme that is effective and safe and restores the dignity of the affected population, including women, children and PWDs. In its Rapid Needs Assessment Report, which was conducted by the sponsorship programme at IRW in cooperation with its local partners in June 2021, a sample of 1,000 FHHs from the five governorates of Gaza Strip were surveyed to assess their multi-sectorial needs and priority areas of interventions. Among the 140 households participated in the survey from Rafah governorate, 46% of them are identified as needing urgent WASH assistance of some kind due to lacking the basic elements of protection, with disproportionate impact on women and girls, diminishing their sense of physical security, safety and privacy. 65 HHs have inadequate/damaged sanitation facilities and networks, requiring bathrooms to repaired/separated or reconstructed and sanitation items to be replaced. Also, 55 HHs lacks adequate water storage both for drinking and domestic use due to insufficient/damaged water tanks and/or inoperative generators and water pumps. Also, adequate access to water is constrained for 51 HHs by the inadequate/damaged in-house pipelines, networks and connections. In addition to that, 75 families reported their need for assistance for accomplishing their basic hygienic needs in a modest and acceptable way as well as promoting their personal hygiene. This is especially critical for women and girls who reported to find difficulties to get sanitary kits, emphasizing the immediate need to provide sanitary kits and awareness related to menstrual hygiene management on the basis of cultural preferences. Women surveyed reported that they are not able to mitigate the interruption of services and support, they are adopting negative coping mechanisms such as decreasing their water consumption and discharging wastewater and dumping solid wastes to open areas. Also, they stated that the substandard WASH conditions of their families have negative effects on the physical health as well as the mental and psychological health of all members; bringing isolation, despair, stress, and in some cases violence, among the families’ members, exacerbating the needs for specialized WASH services which are adequate, protected, and gender-sensitive. The proposed intervention is expected to respond to the basic WASH needs for the targeted HHs, to alleviate their suffering and improve the quality of their lives.

2 - Activities

The project activities will be implemented through a local partner and in close coordination with the WASH cluster, peer organizations and governmental counterparts to avoid duplication and ensure complementarity. Prior to implementation, the project team and partner will receive information on PSEA and CFM and will be required to sign the Code of Conduct, which directly refers to the PSEA and child protection. Also, the quality assurance unit and the project team will be responsible for the monitoring of activities to ensure gender and age specific WASH interventions as well as integration of safety and mitigation of protection risks especially for females. The project will consider active engagement of the community members in the design, planning, implementation and evaluation of activities. The targeted locations and potential beneficiaries have been identified through the needs assessment, and the project team with partner will be responsible for the collection of data and finalizing the selection processes aaccording to the results of the technical assessment visits. The data from the assessment will be entered and analysed in a scoring system, presenting the most 50 eligible households. The project team will specify the amounts to be allocated for each house according to the actual needs. The final list of beneficiaries will be shared/agreed in close cooperation the relevant stakeholders. Then, the project team will lead efforts with the implementing partner, linked with the provision of the following WASH interventions: (1) The provision of PE water tanks, and (2) The provision of water facilities critical operation and maintenance materials and Tools. IRW and implementing partner will select the HHs of short water supply and will provide cash assistance for the purchase of functional and safe Roof Storage Tanks (1000 liters’ capacity), and functional and safe potable water storage tanks (200 liters’ capacity), also for the water pumps and critical operation and maintenance materials and tools for the rehabilitation of individual in-house water connections and system (Modality for two activities: In-kind at an average of 300$/HH). For improving access to sanitation and hygiene, the project comprises the activity (3): The rehabilitation of household sanitation facilities; IRW and implementing partner will select the HHs in need of activity, conduct technical assessment and provide unconditional and restricted cash assistance for the targeted HHs to conduct the rehabilitation and upgrading of HHs sanitation facilities and networks in 50 homes to meet SPHERE standards (Modality for the activities: Cash at an average of 2,000$/HH, Self-help approach). This includes maintenance of toilets, kitchens and in-house sewage connections. The maintenance works will consider the women security and privacy, child protection, supporting the wellbeing of elderlies and people with disabilities. Activity (4) The provision of standardized household hygiene kit including hygiene health materials, and dignity items, in which all family members: women, men, boys, girls, people with disabilities and elderly’s specific needs are considered, to helps HHs maintaining a healthy and safe hygiene environment and protection of COVID-19, IRW and partner will distribute family hygiene kits to the target families (Modality: Value Voucher at an average of 200$/HH). Activity (5) Hygiene promotion awareness campaigns: IRW through the implementing partner will conduct the hygiene promotion activities will include home visits and delivering awareness messages to HHs with poor hygiene situation. These hygiene promotion activities are to enhance safe hygiene and water management practices (personal hygiene, hand washing especially, and safe water handling), and will consider the specific needs of different target groups including women, girls and PWDs (Modality: Service Provision at an average of 300$/HH). IRW will conduct systematic orientation and regular communication with beneficiaries, and will put in place a beneficiary feedback mechanism, whereby beneficiaries can express their feedback, suggestions or complaints about services, including about the possible negative consequences of its programming or any issue related to PSEA, child protection or other related issues. IRW MEAL department will collect, record, analyze and respond when relevant, to all beneficiary feedback.

3 - Indicators

IRW implements an extensive framework of tools for the monitoring and evaluation of its projects. The Quality Assurance Unit at IRW will be in charge of conducting a range of MEAL activities, gathering and analysing all monitoring data in order to measuring progress against indicators and provide cluster and donor with all data requested. The progress of implementation will be systematically monitored through field visits, following up and reporting mechanisms, addressing any challenges and adopting the necessary amendments. Also, feedback from beneficiaries will be regularly collected and their satisfaction will be assessed in the post distributions. Throughout the implementation, lessons learnt will be documented to inform better future programming and scaling up of WASH-related interventions. MEAL activities will enable reporting detailed gender and age disaggregated data and measuring project outcomes of different targeted groups. The project team will monitor progress against indicators and verify the validity of reported data through reviewing the means of verification. The WASH cluster indicators are designed in order to closely monitor the meeting of the project objectives, especially the WASH cluster objective 2: Ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable safely-managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinians. The following are the expected results and indicators of the project: - The outcome indicator for Activity (1) and Activity (2) is # of people benefiting from improved access to safe drinking and domestic water services, while the output indicator for Activity (1) is # of PE tanks provided and the output indicator for Activity (2) is of water facilities provided with operation and maintenance materials and tools. The outcome indicator for activity (3), Activity (4) and activity (5) is # of people benefiting from improved access to sanitation, solid water and hygiene practices. The output indicator for Activity (3) is # of households sanitation facilities rehabilitated, while the output indicator for Activity (4) is # of household hygiene kits provided and the output indicator for Activity (5) is # of WASH awareness campaigns conducted. The proposed intervention is expected to improve the overall protection of the targeted 50 HHs (including 250 people: 75 females, 55 males, 60 boys, 60 girls), through improving their access to basic WASH services. To further ensure protection, quality and accountability are mainstreamed throughout the project implementation, the following indicators will be monitored: % of project beneficiaries (male/female) reporting that humanitarian WASH assistance is delivered in a safe and dignified manner. % of the affected population (disaggregated by sex and age) reached through consultation in the establishment of community-based complaint mechanisms (CBCMs), awareness activities and community mobilization interventions on PSEA including how to report SEA-related complaints (disaggregated by type of PSEA communication materials developed for each population group identified). %of HHs reporting satisfaction about the intervention. %of HHs report knowledge/access to complaints mechanism.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

The project adopts the cash modality (when applicable) in the rehabilitation/maintenance works. This modality proves to be efficient and it maintain the dignity of target households when reflected in using self-help approach that empowers households to resume their capacity in leading the solution to their concerns rather than being considered as a mere recipient of support, especially when the approach is complemented with mentoring and training on the use of cash. The project will use in-kind modality for the provision of water tanks and operation/maintenance tools and materials because they need technical specifications which may not be noted/identified by the beneficiaries. IRW through implementing partner will contract a vendor company to supply and install standardized plastic water tanks for domestic water to replace the substandard tanks or to add to the insufficient ones and to provide maintenance/operation tools and materials. The hygiene promotion awareness activities will be delivered through service delivery modality through the hiring of health promotion. The hygiene kits will be delivered using the voucher modality, as restricted scheme for the purchase of hygiene materials.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

IRW has an effective and reliable complaint and feedback mechanism that will be applied to provides a chance for all beneficiaries especially the most vulnerable to express their concerns freely and provide their feedback on the delivery of project activities. Before the implementation of activities, IRW will organize an inception workshop and introduce the complaint and feedback mechanisms to all stakeholders and target group. IRW will determine to what extent it is necessary to adapt the project and messages to the needs captured through feedback and complaints. 9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

Ensuring all different groups of beneficiaries and communities have access to safe complaint and feedback mechanisms is one of the first steps in mainstreaming protection across the activities of the project. IRW is committed to implement a beneficiary Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its project to allow beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on IRW work, and to rely any arising issue, including potential PSEA or other protection issues. The project beneficiaries will be engaged in all phases and their needs, preferences and priorities will be considered. During the preliminary stages, IRW provides accurate, timely and accessible information to affected people about its project objectives and intended outcomes in the orientation sessions. Information will be clearly understandable by everyone, irrespective of their age, gender or other characteristics. During implementation, regular meetings and sessions will be held with different community groups to solicit their feedback about any necessary changes or adaptations needed to make the project a success. IRW owns a range of communication tools which are designed in participation with the targeted communities, including the most vulnerable groups. Whenever possible, complaint boxes will be set up, as well as contact information (mobile, WhatsApp and e-mails and online communications) will be shared and disseminated to the project beneficiaries through SMS and will be on banners in the project locations. If the beneficiaries cannot write their feedback or issue their complaints, they will be able to reach the project staff and partner staff who received CRM training and will be available to receive complaints and feedback during collective activities and household visits. In all monitoring and evaluation exercises, IRW monitors the proportion of assisted people informed about the intervention.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The intervention has been developed with comprehensive understanding of the protection principle, especially “Do No Harm”. The project design had considered protection risks analysis and the activities are screened not to increase risks for all especially women, girls and children. IRW will ensure that women, PwDs and elderlies are represented in to ensure that their rights, needs, and views are well taken into consideration. During the verification and final selection of the target groups, all project information will be shared with the target communities to inform them about the provided assistance, and the project will prioritize the FHHs occupying the vulnerable individuals at risk of protection issues (including GBV and child protection issues). GBV and PSEA issues, will be the specific focus of capacity building for staff and partners to ensure that they are able to detect cases of abuse. The project includes effective use of referral pathways for specialist support, in areas related to gender-based violence (GBV), child protection and psychosocial support. The project staff and partner are required to sign the Code of Conduct which directly refers to the PSEA, GBV and child protection. IRW will first do a conflict analysis, understanding the context where we will be intervening and identifying the relations between the different groups that will be targeted. IRW will act upon understanding these interactions in order to avoid negative impacts. IRW staff will perform interviews with beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries to ensure that tensions are not rising in any way as an outcome of the project. Also, local cultural practices and gender sensitivity will be respected at all times by the project team and partners during implementation of activities. Especially, the hygiene awareness will be conducted by female promoters to ensure privacy and dignity of female members. Accountability to the affected population has been well mainstreamed in the project design, implementation, and monitoring. A set of mechanisms will be established to ensure accountable implementation of the project activities. Beneficiaries and stakeholders will have a voice in the project through complain mechanisms held in place. A satisfaction survey will be completed at the end of the intervention to assess the final valuation of the beneficiaries toward the project works. Regarding specific harm related to COVID-19, emphasis will be put on applying protective measures and behaviors to ensure the safety and protection of all people involved in the project (HHs, project team and laborers). Eventually, the project will increase proper access for adequate WASH facilities, and thus contribute to increase access to human right and ensure beneficiaries safety and dignity through improving their life conditions.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

IRW seeks to prevent discrimination and to ensure equal and impartial access though transparency and accountability to affected populations. The project directly improves access for people with specific vulnerabilities in terms of socioeconomic and protection to basic WASH needs. A participatory approach was used to identify the needs and requirement by including participants at all levels and in all phases, starting with the design phase (appropriateness analysis) followed by appropriateness and adaptation planning to ensure adapted assistance accessible to all people of all genders, ages and disabilities, based on previous experience and primary assessment results to ensure equitable access of the marginalized groups. The beneficiaries' selection process is need-based and will consider transparent and accountable processes including the feedback and complaint mechanism. Women, girls, PWDs and elderlies are disproportionately affected by poor WASH conditions, and will be consulted and their decisions will be considered. The technical WASH needs for selected HHs will be evaluated individually and customized to meet the needs of the groups in terms of access, safety, protection and privacy rather than a standardized implementation. In cases where HHs are unable to effectively conduct the upgrading/rehabilitation works, IRW technical team will directly implement the work for the disadvantaged households. Further, the implementation team will ensure all planned rehabilitation work by the beneficiaries through Self-help approach, and the targeted HHs will receive a customized training on the use and management of cash. Safe access of groups at-risk of COVID-19 complications will be ensured through considering the precaution safety measures and protective tools in all sites. The hygiene kits and materials will be provided to HHs based on the rapid technical assessment, and the awareness activities will promote equal knowledge and participation between female and male of good hygiene practices in the households. The protection approach will be followed throughout the whole implementation phase to ensure a solid engagement and ownership at community level.
