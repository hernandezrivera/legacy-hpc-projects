Project Summary

Street Child will enhance the protection of 45,000 (55% girls) conflict-affected children directly or indirectly impacted by COVID19 through the provision of targeted face to face/remote case management and MHPSS services as well as through the strengthening of community-based preventive and protection mechanisms. Key child protection needs and gaps across high-priority LGAs in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa states will be targeted, through a holistic and integrated approach. Specific focus will be on the most at-risk children, including unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), children associated with armed groups (CAAG) and children at-risk of gender-based violence (GBV).
To prevent and mitigate the impact of COVID19 spread, children and communities will be equipped with key age appropriate life-saving knowledge .
In Yobe, LGAs which will be targeted are Gujba and Gulani. In Adamawa, the targeted LGAs will be Mubi, Michika and Madagali. MMC, Jere, Gwoza, Damboa, Monguno, Biu, Michika and Gujba. In Borno the targeted LGAs will be MMC, Jere, Gwoza, Damboa, Monguno, Biu, Mobbar, Dikwa, Hawul and Konduga. This will maximise the efficiency and the impact of existing programmes.

1 - Needs

As of 25 April 2020, in Nigeria, confirmed cases have increased to 1,182 with 35 fatalities across the country. Cases have been registered in twenty-seven states and the Federal Capital Territory (FCT), with Lagos and the FCT reporting the highest number of cases. 30 positive cases with 3 deaths reported from Maiduguri while one positive case reported from Adamawa Satte while no case reported so far from Yobe state.With cases reported in NE Nigeria children losing or being separated from their primary caregivers due to quarantine or confinement measures are at increased risk of neglect, abandonment, violence and exploitation. They may also lack access to health treatment, and suffer mental health and psychosocial impacts, as well as malnutrition. An infectious disease outbreaks as COVID19 is going exacerbates the existing risks and needs in NE Nigeria.An estimated 1.8 million children and adolescents in North East Nigeria are in need of child protection services. At least 6,000 children (over 50% girls) have been recruited, abducted or victims of violent attacks since the start of the conflict, and at least 15,250 children (over 50% girls) have been affected by risks including abuse, exploitation, family separation, neglect and violence. Girls and boys may be exposed to different types of risks, with girls more likely to face SGBV and boys more likely to be at risk of recruitment. Restriction os lockdown will bring to loss of income of caregivers with repercussion on risks of child labour and increased domestic violence, with parents under additional constraints and stress.The risks faced are also age-specific; particular risks are heightened for adolescents, such as underage recruitment into armed groups, sexual exploitation, child labour, early pregnancy, early marriage and other reproductive health issues. There is therefore a need to design more gender-specific initiatives which meet to the different risks and capacities of girls, boys, women and men. Case management of acutely at-risk children, including UASC and CAFAAG, has been identified as a significant gap in the current response in the region. There is a need to improve FTR services, inter-sectoral referrals and follow-up, and the provision of alternative care, especially to anticipate secondary separation due to COVID19.Analysis of cases management cases at the sector level has indicated that children living with vulnerable caregivers are amongst the most likely to face protection risks. An estimated 110,700 parents and caregivers are in need of support to be able to care appropriately for their children and avoid negative coping mechanisms. Certain groups face heightened risks and are more in need of support than others: female-headed households, for example, are amongst the most vulnerable. Street Child's integrated approach of working holistically with families on child protection, education and livelihoods and seeking to build capacity of caregivers to provide for their children is therefore relevant and timely. School closure also represent a Child Protection risk factor. It affects not only the gains made in education in the region over the past years but will negatively affect mental health. This will result in negative psychosocial and security ramifications on children, families and communities. The absence of physical attendance in schools’ leaves girls at a disproportionate level of risk of exploitation due to forced labor, sexual harassment and early childhood marriage. Finally, evidence has demonstrated that violent extremist groups have used moments of disasters – natural or otherwise - to enhance their recruitment through propaganda aimed at exploiting marginalized portions of the population, including children.

2 - Activities

Street Child will provide psychosocial support (MHPSS) to 23,000 children through after-school recreational activities and peer clubs facilitated by trained PSS facilitators, if government restrictions will allow again small groups to gather in cluster. The activities will be tailored to the different gendered and age-specific needs of different children and will address topics such as awareness and prevention from COVID19 to reduce the stress, health and hygiene, GBV prevention and child rights. Caregivers will also be engaged in PSS activities especially if activities will be home based to respect social distance and isolation with simple instruction to organize activities from local culture. Prevention will be considered along the whole implementation, with a minimum of 45,000 beneficiaries (children and caregivers) receiving child friendly messaging on COVID 19, on facts, prevention and myths.140 children affected by protection risks (including GBV survivors, CAAG and UASC) will be targeted for tailored case management, including a tailored care plan and regular follow up from dedicated case workers. Street Child's services include mental health and psychosocial support, family tracing and reunification (FTR) for UASC, socio-economic reintegration of CAAG, the provision of protection kits and GBV case management. In addition, referrals will be made for specialised services including medical support and legal support and follow-up conducted for all referrals made. It is important to note that Street Child has already existing case management files and will continue during COVID19 scaling up to address additional needs but according to government restriction. This means a thorough prioritization with high risks treated immediately against medium and low risks. Specific case of COVID19 will be treated with same approach together with existing cases. In all target communities, Street Child will build community-based protection and response mechanisms, establishing or strengthening community-based child protection committees (CBCPCs) and mapping referral pathways to empower community members to be able to identify child protection concerns and respond.Street Child is committed to localisation and will work through local partners to implement project activities and provide tailored mentoring and capacity building to strengthen technical and organisational capacity. This will be intensified also to provide local organization a timely understanding and capacity around COVID19 prevention and response.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

SC will utilise proven mechanisms for engaging stakeholders and beneficiaries throughout all stages of project planning, implementation and evaluation. A needs assessment will be conducted before implementation, and will include focus group discussions (following social distancing measure and restrictions) will different community groups - boys, girls, men and women. Households interviews will be also conducted, face to face or by phone. A dedicated complaints mechanism (CRM) officer will be identified for all targeted communities. This person will be responsible for collecting feedback and complaints, and liaising between community members, partners and Street Child to share this feedback. Street Child will provide a toll-free line which can be called at any time by community members to provide feedback or report urgent concerns (such as safeguarding concerns). All feedback received through these diverse mechanisms will be fed into review sessions and management decisions, allowing activities to be adapted to respond to feedback where appropriate in line with Street Child's commitment to accountability and evidence-based learning.During COVID19 Street Child will also introduce remote mechanisms to collect feedback from communities through continuous engagement through phone or door by door with appropriate Protective and Preventive Equipment of staff. Key will be the continuous assessment of effectiveness of COVID19 preventive messages, analysing emerging needs and identify gaps to tailor and strengthen our COVID19 response
