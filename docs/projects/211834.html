Project Summary

With 16.8M people in need, the humanitarian crisis in Syria showcases the demand of sustained intervention in 2024. With 15.3M people in need of Protection (of whom 6.6M children IN of child protection, and 8.5M women IN of GBV) the economic hardships, conflict, and climate shocks amplify vulnerabilities and the lack of access to primary resources (HNO 2024). Protection services remain essential in all the related aspects, particularly for women, and children with people disabilities.
With a meaningful intervention in protection, AVSI aims to enhance GBV, CP and inclusivity in Daryya, Rural Damascus. Contributing to Protection cluster O1, O2, O3 and O5, the ultimate goal is to create a safer and more inclusive community. To do so, the intent is to raise awareness, encourage behavioural change and support individual facing trauma or PSS issues. In this direction, PSS individual sessions will be provided to people with psychological disorders, and specific cases will be identified and referred to experts. At the same time, recreational activities and group session will target children aged 6-15. The project promotes community response and aim to engage Daryya people in local initiatives such as community focus groups and positive parenting sessions. In addition, AVSI will commit to the establishment of a safe space for women within a community centre to enhance GBV services.
To provide adequate response, AVSI’s applies population consultations, investment in monitoring resources, and feedback mechanisms. AVSI will identify (or create) safe spaces for the implementation of activities to provide individuals with a sense of security, hospitality, attention, and care. One protection risk is bond to the violence that people, particularly women, experience moving from one place to another. In response to this, AVSI conducts an attentive selection of quality services and transportation support. Regular monitoring will boost timely prevention measures against protection threats and ensure “Do no Harm”. In addition, community engagement prevents the risk of exacerbating disagreement and strengthens the sense of ownership, hindering discrimination among beneficiaries.
AVSI’s PSEAH strategy reinforces the commitment to safeguarding personnel, beneficiaries, and communities contributing to the HRP SO2 and the creation of a secure environment. AVSI's PSEAH policy, Child Safeguarding Policy, and Code of Conduct follow IASC guidelines and DAC recommendations. The measures in place include rigorous staff screening, comprehensive awareness training (last one in January 2024), risk assessment and a transparent reporting mechanism. Thus, community engagement remains pivotal, empowering people to report misconduct and reinforcing a zero-tolerance approach. The identification of beneficiaries, particularly women, girls, and children, will be guided by the analysis of needs, exposure to protection risks, and individual or family vulnerability.
AVSI can count on the solid relationship with local authorities, enhancing access to the implementing areas and facilitating clearances. Political and social situation will be regularly monitored to adapt strategies and readjust activities. Socio-cultural barriers will be addressed through cultural-sensitive training to staff to engage within diverse communities. In parallel, logistical challenges are overcome through adaptive planning, regular risk assessments, and transparent communication. Exchange rate volatility and irregular in-country cash are actual risks. Measures such as expedited procurement schemes and payment procedures helps the organization in minimizing delays and inconveniences. Moreover, the risk of fraud or corruption is mitigated through internal procedures (DIGE 8/2020) that set high standards of accountability, transparency and regulatory compliance.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The project has been designed based on the achievements of previous interventions. AVSI acknowledges the importance of promoting Protection services to respond to the in-country great need, both in the short and the long term. The identification of PIN of protection is based on AVSI manuals and procedures, and conducted by social workers in tight relation with the community. The PSEAH Policy sets out AVSI’s approach to prevent and respond to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and Sexual Harassment. This policy and its obligations bind all AVSI employees and all AVSI associated personnel, irrespective of their contractual relationship, regardless of status, payment, or length of contract. AVSI organizes in-country training sessions to employees, partners, and beneficiaries to identify GBV and harassment cases, and promote a safe and more inclusive work environment. PSS activities will be performed at the individual and group level, so to enhance the focus on specific cases and sharing, particularly with children. Furthermore, AVSI intervenes delivering in-kind assistance for 50 people with disability - clutches, hearing and vision aids, pads, bedpans, etc. Likewise, aiming to complement the project's GBV response, AVSI will provide vulnerable women with 320 dignity kits (the content of the kits is aligned to UNFPA specifications). In addition, a few education components will be performed to beef up the impact at the community level. AVSI will 1) organize PSA training sessions for 30 teachers (including PSEAH components), 2) reach 150 out-of-school children through a BTL campaign and 3) provide them with school bags and supplies. Last, pursuing the current intervention in At All, and as for the other project presented this year, AVSI aims to strengthen its support to national exams. Hence, 300 students enrolled in 9th and 12th grade in national exams, will receive support to cover food and transportation expenses. All the activities provided by non-AVSI staff (SARC volunteers and community teachers) will be performed after completion of specific training, enabling them to acquire new skills and share knowledge. Constant monitoring, and follow-up meetings will allow AVSI to verify the success of the activities and promptly intervene in case of needed adjustment. AVSI's international experts and Thematic Focal Points will provide technical support during the entire project cycle. AVSI MEAL staff will monitor the activities on a regular basis with tools such as distribution lists, attendance lists, etc; and evaluate the impact of the provided through satisfactory surveys, pre-post test, and establishing feedback mechanisms. Accountability and Learning remain essential to be judged on the performance of the project, engaging stakeholders in the planning and reporting phases, and adjust the interventions accordingly. *The budget presents the costs of a one-year intervention.
