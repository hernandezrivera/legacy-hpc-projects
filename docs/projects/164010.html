Project Summary

As a response to the needs of breeders which have been identified while conducting the technical needs assessment in the project areas, and based on the results of the implemented projects by IYD during last two years and FSS priorities, this proposed project will assist the targeted vulnerable categories through lessening their financial burdens, contributing to establishing stable monthly income, correcting the improper livestock practices, and dissemination the recent and best livestock knowledge to the specialists and breeders as a whole. To reach this end, this project will support 1500 cows’ breeders- 500 females, and 1000 males who own no more than three cows. Accordingly, one of these cows will receive healthcare and reproductive follow-up treatment and artificial insemination with the semen of high specifications and quality to improve the productivity and reproductivity of the local cows’ breed with a parallel increase of the livestock numbers in the targeted areas. Moreover, each cow will be provided with10 kg of pellet fodder daily for five months and 5 kg of green fodder as hydroponic barley for five months, supplied from pre-established hydroponic barley rooms by IYD in the previous projects. Also, the beneficiaries’ cows will be supported with premix fodder which embraces all needed vitamins, minerals, and production modifiers.
Secondly, IYD will support the targeted breeders with technical training to improve the community's skills in breeding livestock. The training program to the breeders will discuss many significant aspects, and the nutrition section will be at the forefront of issues to be considered since all problems in breeding animals are directly or indirectly linked to animal feeding. Based on the pre-post tests' results, IYD will assist the top 200 breeders, who excel in the training, with the needed equipment for producing the hydroponic barley. The HB production capacity of each unit of equipment will be approximately 15-20 kg green fodder daily.
IYD, throughout this project, seeks to develop the capacity of fifty unexperienced vets who graduated during the conflict period and couldn't have a chance to improve their skills in the field. This will be done through intensively theoretically and practically trainings. In this way, the vets’ chances of entering the labour market will considerably increase. By the end of the training, each trainee will receive a medicine bag include the needed medicines and equipment to start with their jobs after establishing the link between the cows' breeders and the trainees.
From another similar angle, IYD under this project aims to use modern ways to control the reproductive cycle of 5,000 sheep ( 500 breeders) by conducting a hormone program outside the reproductive season to have 3 births in two years instead of 2. To translate that into practice, IYD technical team will inject the selected sheep with PMSG hormone to stimulate the sheep's ovary for releasing more than one oocyte during the oestrous cycle in order to get twins. The same 5,000 selected sheep will be also supported daily with 1 kg of pellet fodder for five months during the breeding time to stimulate the sheep and their reproductive system.
An important resilient component will be supplying 300 vulnerable HHs of specific criteria ”100 % HHs headed by women” with the daily ASF intake by purchasing 30,000 chicks of a Turkish type called Attack-S. These chicks will be fed, vaccinated and bred for about four months. Following that, IYD will distribute 8 hens and 2 roosters for each selected HH, thus enabling them to obtain a part of their needed daily requirement from ASF through consuming eggs daily. The distributed roosters inseminate the hens to give more chicks, and the HHs will, therefore, be able to gain the maximum benefit. Regular visits will be conducted to follow up on the health of these flocks and give the needed medicines.

4 - Needs

Accodıng to HNO 2020, an estimated 7.9 million people in Syria are food insecure and an additional 1.9 million people are at risk of food insecurity. The same report also says that at least 69 per of the people living below $1.90 a day, more than double 2011, and with an estimated cumulative GDP loss of $226 billion between 2011 and 2016 (out of which $16 billion in losses and damages are attributable to agriculture sector alone. Based on Crop and Food Security Assessment Mission CFSAM Published by FAO and WFP in 2019, Prior to the crisis, livestock accounted for (35- 40 %) of the country’s total agricultural production and occupied about 20 % of the labour force in rural areas. In addition, 35 % of rural households raised livestock, which is considered their main source of food and income. Before the crisis, MAAR supported services to the livestock sector such as extension, fodder rations, cultivation of pasture seeds and seedlings, digging of wells to provide water for the population and livestock, grading and improving activities, artificial insemination, vaccination, livestock treatments, veterinary drugs, improved breeds, and research services. However, with the outbreak of the Syrian crises, the livestock contribution in agricultural production has decreased in the context of the collapse of the veterinary services.The key findings of the technical assessment conducted by IYD in 2019 indicate that the cows ageing (2-6) years have health diseases of foot-and-mouth, smallpox, food poisoning, indigestion, emphysema, ulcerative mammalitis,in addition to having reproductive disorders and diseases. The assessment also says that the pellet fodder is available in the targeted communities but with high prices which the breeders can’t afford whereas the green fodder is not available there at all. Besides that, the assessment notified to the absence of any cow, sheep, and poultry support by the humanitarian bodies despite the importance of implementing such support

5 - Implementation of activities

As soon as the project fund is secured, IYD, in coordination with the local communities and leaders at first will place advertisements for the benefiting from the project components. Upon the expiration of the advertisement period, the applicants will be registered and then verified. Following the selection of the beneficiaries of the cow breeders, sheep breeders, vets to be trained, and the vulnerable HHs to benefit from poultry, IYD will apply well-studied schedule on a scientific basis to organize the process : - Applying the artificial insemination for the targeted cows-Applying the hormone program for the targeted sheep. -Conducting the reproductive and health following up for sheep and cows-The theoretical and practical training will be conducted for the targeted vets. -Conducting creative conditions which will lead to the success of procuring and breeding chicks. -Empowering the trained vets, after the completion of the training, to launch or create their businesses especially in supporting the current livestock situation in the targeted areas. Scientifically, using the artificial insemination protects the cows from the uterus inflammation, which results from the natural one.The sperms used into artificial insemination collected from good specification bulls which lead to improving the local cows’ specification.Furthermore, the distributed green food and pellet fodder will contribute to improving and increasing the productivity of cows and sheep as these kinds of fodder include necessary items such as corn, barley, wheat, soybean…etc. Another important dimension of this project is the health and reproductive follow up for the targeted cows and sheep, and by applying that, the spread of epidemics and diseases will decrease. This project will provide the targeted HHs with ASF intake which will be reflected in improving the health status of these HHs’ members. IYD's skilled and trained technical team will ensure the success of the project.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

• Institutional: Low collaboration between IYD and the actors involved in the projectleading to a slowdown in the implementation of activities. However, IYD’s strong presence and good name will enable it to overcome this challenge• Operational: Limited capacity of service providers leading to a delay in the supply process. To mitigate this, IYD will keep following up the service providers day by day, to ensure their commitment to the contracts signed with them. • Social risks: From a social standpoint, this project presents no risk to the IYD and local communities. Although women are still underrepresented in decision-making bodies, their concerns have over time been taken into account, and this project will seek to ensure their better presence and representation. •Security Risks: Interference from the military controlling groups or the local councils in the targeted areas, and deterioration of the security situation.IYD has a strong relationship with neutral communities leaders, and will highly coordinate with them for the prevention of any interference attempts in the project activities or IYD’s work as a whole. IYD will coordinate with the active actors on the ground to select the safest area to implement this project; additionally, IYD’s safety and security team will keep following up the security situation and send updates to the field team to ensure their safety• Risk Management StrategyTo effectively manage the risks that may arise during the implementation of the project, key stakeholders will be capacitated and empowered. The list of current risks will be reviewed and discussed with the stakeholders at the start of the project. Subsequently, the project team including the program coordinator and the human resources and the supporting structures will monitor the advancements in response to the principal risks identified. These advancements will be discussed during the project team’s coordination meetings, as well as the Steering Committee’s meetings.
