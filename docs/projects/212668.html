Project Summary

In 2024, it is estimated that approximately 6.8 million people in Syria will require shelter support
The series of earthquakes, in conjunction with situational displacement, has also increased the number of people forced to seek emergency shelter in Collective Centers 92,538
Of the 21.3 million people in housing/apartments, 38% are estimated to live in partially safe or unsafe conditions.
Among the top shelter adequacy issues identified are lack of access to electricity/lighting (24%), insulation from heat and cold (22%), and leakage from roof and ceiling (20%)
Such findings, across all contexts, are extremely concerning as they reflect the dire need for hundreds of thousands of housing-related humanitarian interventions
There are considerable Housing Land and Property issues across Syria, in Aleppo Governorate 87% of the population experiencing concerns (post-earthquake)
To address these challenges, the Shelter project focuses on improving level of safety, privacy and protection in shelters and communities. It includes implementation of minor repairs to damaged housing through a contractor modality for 250 HH and provision of cash for rent for 500 HH in Aleppo,to reduce insufficient privacy and GBV risks. The project prioritizes areas with the greatest needs based on severity scores.
Implementation of the project follows established SOPs, including planning, reporting, procurement, and quality assurance. Continuous monitoring and evaluation are conducted to assess the effectiveness of interventions and gather feedback from stakeholders. The EPDC has extensive experience in similar projects and has strategically located offices to effectively reach communities in need.
The project aims to improve housing and living conditions and help individuals and families secure safe and stable housing. Stakeholder engagement is crucial, ensuring interventions meet the specific needs of the target communities. EPDC commits to providing monthly reports.
Shelter project prioritizes the rights and safety of vulnerable individuals, including women, girls, young boys, and the elderly. Gender equity is promoted, and measures are in place to protect against PSEA. A code of conduct and Complaint Response Mechanism are implemented to address these issues. Data collection tools consider gender and age, and community participation efforts incorporate gender considerations. EPDC aims to hire a gender-balanced team and collects protection-related data disaggregated by sex and age. Child protection and safeguarding standards are upheld, and a safe environment for children is ensured. A comprehensive risk assessment is conducted, and mitigation measures are implemented for different age groups. Monitoring, evaluation, and beneficiary feedback are utilized to assess services. The project follows the principle of 'Do no harm' and promotes inclusive participation
external risks include conflict and insecurity, international sanctions and continued instability, further depreciation of the Syrian currency, and intervening events as COVID-19 resurgence and EQ. The measures for mitigating these risks involve conducting security assessments, maintaining communication with local authorities, identifying alternative routes, advocating for streamlined fund transfers, and monitoring prices and markets. risks related to interventions and the organization itself, include increased violence against women and girls, gender-based discrimination and exclusion, limited access to information and services for women, barriers to accessing resources, extortion and embezzlement, weak accountability and complaints mechanisms, and exclusion of vulnerable groups. The suggested risk-reducing measures involve inclusive response design, participatory activities, cultural sensitivity, gender analysis, staff training, internal controls, robust complaints mechanisms, transparent selection criteria, coordination with relevant stakeholders, and implementation of accountability mechanisms.
