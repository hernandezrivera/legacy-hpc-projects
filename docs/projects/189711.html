Project Summary

This project aims to provide protection, assistance and solutions to 13,750(8300 female and 5450 male) Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and host communities in Pibor, Nyirol, Ayod and Tonj North Counties in South Sudan. Specifically, the project will provide protection services and strengthen community resilience to Persons of Concern (POCs), through the provision of protection services, CVA, shelter assistance and distribution of CRIs among the affected communities.
HRSS is currently providing protection assistance to persons with specific needs and monitoring the movements of Spontaneous refugee Returnees in Leer, Mayendit and Panyijiar Counties and currently has presence in Ayod, Pibor, Nyirol implementing the CCCM and women economic empowerment activities and will expand its interventions to Tonj North. HRSS will also mainstream GBV and offer camp coordination and camp management activities by engaging with persons with specific needs (PSNs), Community sensitization and capacity building on conflict resolution mechanisms/ techniques, referring PSN cases and high- risk clients to other services through the referral pathways.
Direct service provision will be conducted through mobile and static teams throughout Tonj North, Pibor, Nyirol and Ayod Counties. HRSS will continue to prioritize and respond to highly sensitive protection issues including forced recruitment into armed groups and detention, and coerced sexual exploitation. In addition to the efforts to increase documentation. This will be aimed at supporting the establishment of effective partnerships and frameworks for actions for responding to challenges of protection and finding solutions for persons internally displaced due to conflict and abuses of human rights and bridging the gap between relief and development. This project will support and continue to coordinate with local authorities and lead efforts to provide protection and solutions for persons internally displaced due to conflict and abuse of human rights as part of the collaborative response to IDPs in South Sudan.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan is likely to continue to worsen in 2022, The continued displacement of 1.64 million people living in these conditions exacerbates the ongoing humanitarian situation in South Sudan. Conflict and flooding have forced hundreds of thousands of people to relocate and remain in displacement sites across the country. Protracted displacement is a major driver of humanitarian needs, as significant coping strategies of Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) are eroded, and unresolved security and livelihood obstacles mean that women, men, boys and girls are likelyto remain vulnerable and in need of humanitarian assistance. Women, girls, and people with disabilities in particular, who comprise 15 per cent of the total IDP population in camps and camp-like settings, are even more vulnerable. Women and girls continue to face threats to their safety and security arising from early marriage and sexual exploitation/Gender- Based Violence (GBV) as they lose livelihoods and accessto education, which is not prioritized by their families.Information gathered from IDPs through complaint and feedback mechanisms identified people with specific needs(people with chronic illnesses, people with disabilities, the elderly, female-headed households, child-headed households,and minority groups), as being disproportionately affected by the crisis. According HNO 2022, 5.6 million people are in needs of protection service.Competition over already scarce resources has caused tension between displaced persons and host communities. Maintaining harmonious co-existence between the two groups is a necessity. Maintaining the civilian and humanitarian character of asylum requires constant engagement acknowledging the presence of combatants in/around sites/camps which also leads to increased tensions and exacerbates existing vulnerabilities of refugee populations, exposing them, particularly women and children, to increased levels of violence and insecurity. Children (0 to 17 years old) constitute 61 per cent of the refugee population. They continue to face protection risks including neglect, abuse, exploitation, child labour, forced recruitment, SGBV, and lack of access to services. Protection concerns are especially high for girls risks for early marriage, early pregnancies, and denial of education particularly for girls and children living with disabilities. Decades of conflict, poverty and poor access to services made people with disabilities (PWD) particularly fragile. PWD are often stigmatized, marginalized, excluded and left unable to access the support needed to live in dignity as equal members of society

2 - Project activities

Objective 1. Ensure the provision of critical protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas 1. Provision of survivor center psychosocial support focused on healing, empowerment and recovery. HRSS will incorporate psychological first aid into a standard package of post-rape care offered by first point of contact. Provide in-depth training on psychological first aid to a selected group of focal points and train care coordinators in established protocols at county and payam level for help-seeking survivors of sexual violence to link to relevant services and support, including provision of survivor-centered information. 2. Provision of GBV case management services including coordinated care and support. HRSS will train 3 community based case managers to carry out Screening to determine if the survivors would benefit from Case Management Services. During the screening phase, information will be reviewed to decide appropriateness for Case Management. 3. Establish and / or strengthen GBV referral systems and linking GBV survivors to available response services and support. HRSS will conduct 36 trainings on building on and supporting local initiatives and structures, rather than creating parallel initiatives to develop local competency, including skills for leadership. HRSS will ensure there is local Leadership and ownership of the project to address gender-based violence to reflect and support the strengths, resilience, coping mechanisms of affected individuals and communities. 4. Implement Women and Girls Friendly Space (WGFS) programming to provide services, information and activities that promote healing, well-being and empowerment. HRSS will carry our 3 initial assessment to help determine the feasibility of establishing safe spaces and support the establishment of 3 women- and girl-friendly safe spaces for girls and women to receive basic psychological and social support and referral for health care and other services. 5. Provision of legal services that protect the rights of GBV survivors and promote their access to justice. HRSS will Provide technical support for establishment or improvement of 12 inter-agency referral system to link survivors with additional MHPSS providers as well as health, security and legal support and include provisions for safe and ethical data collection, management and sharing as well as strategies that address barriers to accessing support. 6. Provision of livelihood and economic support including through Cash and Voucher Assistance to vulnerable women and girls and GBV survivors as part of a multi-sectorial GBV response. HRSS will adopt Cash Based Intervention through cash and vouchers which will target 1125HHs equivalent to 1220 individuals. Objective 2. Prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience 1. Implement community engagement and awareness raising activities on GBV risk factors, mitigation measures and on how survivors can access support and services in line with the “do no harm” principle. HRSS will conduct 60 public awareness raising campaigns, 20 radio talk shows and 60 community dialogues and information sharing sessions. 2. Engage men and boys for accountable practices to transform harmful social norms that perpetuate gender inequality, and to promote the health and safety of women and girls. HRSS will conduct 60 monthly meetings, 20 community debates and 30 community outreach programs. 3. Establish and / strengthen support to safe house to provide immediate security, temporary refuge, and support to GBV survivors who are escaping violent or abusive situations. HRSS will establish 3 shelters and strengthen the capacity of 2 shelters for survivors of gender based violence. 4 Provide capacity building trainings on various topics of GBV to front line serve provider.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

HRSS has designated trained PSEA community focal points in each location to disseminated information about PSEA. The project also incorporates PSEA in all its activities including trainings and awareness raising in to the protection activities. HRSS will use Community-Based Complaints Mechanism (CBCM) approach which is a system blending both formal and informal community structures, where individuals will be able and encouraged to safely report incidents of SEA. Local communities will be involved in developing and approving the CBCM so that the structure is both culturally and gender-sensitive. The mechanism should have multiple entry points, allowing both beneficiaries and staff the opportunity to report at the organizational level – internally through the network’s and field agency focal points at the community level. The primary Concern of the mechanism is to aid known and potential SEA survivors, and also fulfill a prevention function through awareness-raising efforts. A PSEA CBCM will not be a separate, parallel system to other complaints and feedback structures but rather link to and build on existing structures to create one system for handling feedback and complaints. 12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

The project will contribute significantly on prevention and reduction of protection risk in project target area.HRSS will design questionnaires to assess the needs of the community and to receive feedback from stakeholders to be considered in the Trauma and Psychosocial Support delivery and the Trauma and Psychosocial development of the curriculum training materials. This will include Trauma/Mental Health and Psychosocial Support for the community and Trauma/Mental Health and Psychosocial Support Curriculum Training/Trainer of the Trainerson building their capacity. On case management, HRSS will work with health centres and also set up as an alternative to enable identification of cases, take complaints, guarding against the possibility of missed diagnosis of more urgent conditions that will potentially result in increased fatalities. In areas where the treatment capacity for serious and critical cases is extremely limited, HRSS will support and advocate for facilities be reinforced to enhance in-patient capacity so as to provide palliative, end of life care. Screening sites will be established close to designated Ready Facilities and will be undertaken by health professionals to prevent missed diagnosis. Screening sites will implement Infection Prevention Control (IPC) measures to protect staff and patients

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

This proposed project will contribute to this outcome through implementing risk mitigation and response to protection needs of the target populations. The relationship between the host and IDPs is usually normal but tensions are expected over grazing land as people continue moving to highland areas with their livestock every day. HRSS wil target beneficiaries in newly displaced IDP population with few hosted by host communities. HRSS will also carry out registration of continued displacement of population by flood. HRSS will respond to flood-affected people’s urgent needs include food and livelihood support, emergency shelter and non-food items (ES/NFI), and water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH), health and protection services. HRSS will lobby for additional logistic support with dedicated air assets and riverine transport to transport cargo to multiple locations prioritized for flood response.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

This project seeks to respond to chronic food insecurity in target locations which is a result of combined pressures as a result of poor production methods, environmental degradation and Climate Change. HRSS intends to contribute to enhancing long-term sustainable household food and income security through addressing environment drivers of food insecurity in the target counties. HRSS will also work with existing community structures using a multi-sectoral approach by building on the already existing programs implemented by HRSS through food distributions targeting food insecure households especially women, girls and persons with specific needs hence reducing IPC level by 10%
