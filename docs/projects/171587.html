Project Summary

The purpose of the project:
The space for individual Human Rights Defenders (HRDs) and civil society organizations (CSOs) in the occupied Palestinian territories (OPT), speciﬁcally those working to end the Israeli occupation in Palestine, or to promote human rights (HRs) in the OPT, is increasingly shrinking. The project responds to an increasingly dangerous environment for HRDs in the OPT. The vision of this project is that HRDs will be free to carry out their peaceful and legitimate work, and to promote human rights and justice. The goal is defending HRDs and minimizing the “chilling effect” that targeted harassment has on their work. The project intervenes by building resilience of local HR communities to withstand the persecution they face.
Proposed Outputs/target Include:
Direct Services for individual HRDs (men and women and, where applicable, children) and organizations at risk, aimed at mitigating these risks (legal, ﬁnancial or others) to their body, person or work. These services focus on coordination—via a 24-hrs hotline—and ﬁnancing of legal counselling and representation, in order to increase likelihood of early release from detention/arrest and avoid indictment and/lawsuits. Other services include capacity-building training and media support for HRDs (men and women) and organizations. Advocacy activities that address the root causes of persecution, and develop strategic action to prevent and minimize it. In addition, outreach to the international community to leverage its capacity to enforce international HR law.

1 - Needs

This project responds to the need of protection for individual human rights defenders (HRDs) and human rights organizations, mitigating threats through direct legal support, resilience building and advocacy. In recent years, we are witnessing a growing systematic attack on HR organizations and individual HRDs (men, women and also children) who are peaceful protesters, intended to delegitimize and deter them from working, and silence criticism over Israel’s violations of human rights. Concerted campaigns and incitement by the Israeli government and non-state actors against HRDs is endangering the space for opposition of human rights violations. This project also will have different challenges that will include the following: (A) Legal Risks: Israel violates HRDs’ rights and freedoms by issuing arbitrary military/police orders, using police/military force, and surmounting legal procedures - criminal or administrative. Men, women as well as children are affected. Palestinian HRDs in the OPT face greater risk since they are subjected to a military regime with little access to justice within the conﬁnes of the military court system. HRDs and organizations are not equipped to legally defend themselves, and the costly defense demands resources that could otherwise be used for activities. (B) Public Targeting and incitement: Coordinated efforts to discredit organizations who oppose the occupation including attempts to restrict their ability to fundraise and label CSOs as ‘foreign agents’ or ‘state enemies’. Without rapid professional response to counter attacks, such allegations may actually turn into restrictive legislation or initiatives endangering HRD's funding sources. (C) ‘Chilling Effect’: The intimidation resulting from the targeting leaves activists (men, women and at times children) in a state of emotional ‘burnout’; without investment in activists’ wellbeing, this leads to a ‘chilling effect’ against HR activism. The work of women human rights defenders (WHRDs) underlies not only the power dynamics between Human Rights Defenders and state representatives, but on top of this, women face the pressure of gender dynamics of society in general, creating a two-fold burden. Incrimination, arrests, investigations, imprisonment, legal proceedings and trials create a complex reality that is different for women to cope with. Many women human rights activists have testiﬁed to being verbally, sexually and/or physically harassed by soldiers/police during protests, arrests or investigations, as well as being subjected to humiliating treatment based on gender, sexuality and appearance. The HRDF constantly considers special needs of female HRDFs in its work, including in this project.

2 - Activities

Activity cluster 1 –Risk Reduction 1.1. Legal Aid including direct representation, advice, and counsel to HRDs and organizations. Legal representation will be provided in cases of criminal prosecution (detention/arrest, interrogation, indictments) for men, women, children HRDs and organizations. Aid is rendered by contracted top attorneys. 1.2. Urgent aid will be facilitated through 24-hours phone hotline to HRDs (men and women) and organizations, to provide timely and effective legal counselling. Activity cluster 2 - Resilience Building 2.1. Workshops to inform HRDs (men/women) on their special rights and provide them with tools to better prepare activities, to minimize risks, and to defend against different threats and considering speciﬁc gender needs. The workshops are designed together with expert lawyers and activists (men and women). 2.2. Media crisis management for HRDs who are victims of public smear campaigns or of incitement to violence. The HRDF contracts/outsources media experts to provide consultations to HRDs (men and women) and push to include the voices and perspectives of targeted HRDs and HR organizations in mainstream media. Activity cluster 3 – Advocacy 3.1. Outreach activities to disseminate information to international community members through meetings, briefs, regular newsletter, and urgent updates. 3.2. Using mechanisms of international monitoring and action, such as ﬁling formal “urgent complaints”, issuing reports, submitting opinions to the UN state review, and so on. As a result of COVID 19 pandemic crisis, HRDF activities had been affected as other human rights organization, due to the increase in infected people and different waves of the virus. During the lockdown in Israel (March to May,) the Israeli courts were closed and most of HRDs cases were postponed. In the second lockdown (September to October), the courts are open and operating, but with a slow pace, and the lawyers are working according to the COVID 19 regulations presented by the Israeli government. Therefore, HRDF had taken the current situation under COVID 19 into account, where several adjustments were taken to the implementation of the activities. In case of another lockdown, HRDF will continue providing legal counselling to HRDs and human rights organizations through 24 hours hotline, as it has been done in the past, but with more preparedness during COVID 19 and the continuous demonstrations taking place. If courts are open, HRDF lawyers will be able to attend court sessions physically in different legal cases, taking into consideration the COVID 19 safety regulations. Furthermore, all capacity building trainings will be shifted and will be conducted online / virtually depending on the different digital platforms available. As for media crisis management, HRDF will continue to provide media consultations to HRDs including human rights organizations and activists groups online. As for advocacy activities, newsletters, legal updates, legal developments, etc. will continue via different digital means, as well conduct different briefings to diplomatic missions, international organizations, human rights organizations and groups online.

3 - Indicators

The success of the proposed project will be measured through reaching the following indicators: Activity Cluster 1: • Direct legal aid to HRDs (men and women; children if necessary); hotline calls answered to individual HRDs or human rights organizations per year.• Overall good legal results for the large majority of legal aid beneﬁciaries, such as: - early release from detention/arrest (after no more than 2 arraignment hearings); - reduced and/ dropped charges from the indictment; - lighter sentences/punishments. • Activists’ groups will contact the HRDF prior to organizing actions, to get legal advice or counsel and/ to arrange to have standby lawyers in advance. Activity Cluster 2: • Number of trainings held for an average of 10-20 HRDs per training. • Media discussion of HRDs and HR organizations will include the voice of at risk HRDs and/ their lawyers. • Public discussion will include perspectives that counter-act the targeting of HR community. Activity Cluster 3: • Brieﬁngs held to international diplomats; 2-3 newsletters or press releases sent to over 650 recipients, in addition to providing legal updates about different cases of HRDs. • Advocacy work and activities to mobilize the International mission representatives and international community to attend trial hearings of local HRDs, to be more informed about the situation on the ground and show more support for HRDs.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

DCA/NCA and partners apply Do No Harm (DNH) approach that is integrated in the project at all levels. Both partners are trained and aware of the approach which is part of the human rights-based approach adopted by DCA/NCA and HRDF.This project works on bringing social change to the lives of human rights defenders throughout their different activities including awareness raising and medica crisis management. Both DCA/NCA and its partner (HRDF) prioritized the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and considered the principles of do no harm. It is worth mentioning that due to COVID 19 Crisis locally and globally, HRDF had made adjustments to continue their activities in supporting HRDs. Like in many other human rights organizations, HRDF have embraced flexibility while making the required adjustments, some of which are necessary technical adaptations designed to protect the health and safety of staff members, lawyers, volunteers and human rights defenders who reach out to us for assistance. HRDF are implementing a policy that includes working from home, meeting online and avoiding public transportation. Even some court sessions are currently held with no physical presence in the courtroom, but over the phone or by other digital means. Other adjustments are complex programmatic changes. These are challenging times for the entire public, but they present particular challenges to human rights defenders working in the Occupied Palestinian Territories (OPT). Every day, we see how the need for the role that HRDF plays, for the ability to counsel, support, back and protect human rights defenders not only persists but grows, and how the work must continue, in an enhanced format, despite the many difficulties and challenges. DCA/NCA is a Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) certified agency (www.hqai.org) and works continuously to improve the quality and effectiveness of the assistance it provides to the affected population. PSEA mainstreaming will be part of this project in the following ways:• DCA/NCA implements a code of conduct that is signed by all its staff that reflects among others the values and standards regarding PSEA. DCA/NCA ensures that the cooperation agreement with implementing partners consists of an article that requires its partner to have an internal staff code of conduct in place that reflects as a minimum the values and standards of partners regarding PSEA.• DCA/NCA will ensure that the target groups and communities have an accessible, a confidential and a safe complaints handling mechanisms during the implementation of the project, where the sensitive complaints are dealt with and acted upon with confidentiality ( either by using DCA’/NCA’s already functioning complaints mechanism or using the partners complaints mechanism)• DCA/NCA will conduct a PSEA training to its and partners’ staff at the beginning of the project and will ensure that a plan is put in place on how the partners will raise awareness about PSEA among their targeted communities.• DCA/NCA staff will monitor closely the practices of partners to reinforce the PSEA and ensure that the targeted communities are aware of PSEA and the available functional complaints mechanism.• DCA/NCA’s PSEA local focal point is an active member in the oPt-HCT-PSEA Network. Accordingly, DCA/NCA will continuously update its partners with relevant and needed PSEA information especially on available referral pathways for sensitive complaints and will continuously seek support from the available network services when needed.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project enables access to assistance and services and targeting of vulnerable groups. This will mainly focus on providing assistance and services to HRDs. To add, the protection mainstreaming that the project is highlighting, comprises the principles of prioritizing safety, dignity, avoid causing harm, participation, and empowerment.This project is creating equal and impartial access to aid through the concept of inclusion: thus the main focus of this project is targeting human rights defenders including vulnerable groups, activists, human rights organizations, men, women, children, and others. Due to COVID 19 crisis, HRDF has made the needed adjustments as previously mentioned, to provide equal and impartial access to aid. This includedcontinuing providing our legal counselling over the HRDF 24 hrs hotline. Additionally, network of Lawyers on behalf of HRDF remains on stand-by, and are ready to provide legal counsel, representation, and aid to different HRDs, HR organizations, and activists’ groups. Furthermore, changes has been made to the provision of media crisis management, by providing services and consultation overthe phone or though digital platforms but with caution as a result of the sensitivity of information provided, and to avoid being totally censored. Also, adaptations have been made to the training courses for online delivery. As a result, all the necessary adaptations and changes were made with a sole aim of providing HRDs, human rights organizations, and activists groups with an equal and impartial access to aid under these current unstable circumstances. With regards to gender and age mainstreaming, HRDF has been taking into account and will continue doing so with issues related to gender and age of the targeted HRDs. HRDF provides its services including legal aid, media crisis management, capacity building trainings, in addition to advocacy and lobbying activities, to all HRDs including individuals, activists groups, and human rights organizations, without any discrimination to any gender, race, color, etc. For gender analysis, HRDF provides services to all HRDs, as they have male, female, and other (such as LGBTQ groups) HRDs in their targeted beneficiaries. In certain cases, for tackling gender mainstreaming, HRDF purely provides direct services to women HRDs and women human rights organizations. Furthermore, in the work HRDF takes the disaggregation of needs and assessment data and identification of gender and age groups that are most vulnerable. Giving an example, HRDF provides legal counseling and representation to Israeli and Palestinian HRDs who participate in nonviolent protests against the Israeli occupation in Israel and the oPt; HRDs fighting for LGBTQ rights; Palestinian inhabitants residing the Jordan valley and many more. To add, HRDF works with lawyers who are HRDs by themselves, and most of the volume of the work is done by female lawyers, who are prominent HRDs.With regards disability mainstreaming, HRDF doesn’t have any limitations to respond to the specific needs and vulnerabilities of persons with disabilities. Services are provided to all HRDs, including individuals, groups, human rights groups, men and women including persons with disabilities. Being aware that the needs for legal protection provided to persons with disability are higher than the ones provided to regular HRDs, as they more marginalized and they need more attention and support. Therefore, disability mainstreaming is an important part of HRDF’s legal aid work on the ground, especially focusing on legal cases dealing with persons with disabilities.
