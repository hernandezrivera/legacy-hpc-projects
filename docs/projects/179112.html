Project Summary

Project interventions are designed to:
Rehabilitation of 4 water stations supporting them with the operational costs include (Fuel, oil, chlorine, salaries) for 6 months which are located in 4 communities ( Selwa, Shallakh, Jisr-Ash-Shugur, Upper Shghur) to serve 62,500 BNFs.
Rehabilitation of 4 sewage systems in 4 communities ( Sarmada, Haranbush, Ein Elsoda, Shaturiyeh) to serve 48,200 BNFs.
Provide full emergency WASH services for the IDPs in informal camps located in northern Syria. to serve 131,793 IDP's living in 92 settlements and camps in Idleb and Aleppo governorates with basic WASH services, adequate drinking water via water trucking (35ltr/PD/pp) while constantly assuring the quality of the water through chlorination and tested, solid waste removal, waste container, storage water tank,de-sludging latrine septic tanks, addressing the gap in latrines numbers in the targeted camps by constructing 79 new latrine blocks (each block consists of 2 cells; each cell serves 25 people) and making them gender-sensitive and to address the needs of elderly and disabled beneficiaries, additional consumable hygiene kits will be distributed to displaced families in all target locations, also IRD will hold segregated sessions for men, women, and children to raise personal and environmental hygiene awareness.
The intervention has put strong emphasis and providing emergency WASH service that can meet the specific needs of the most vulnerable groups: women, children, elderly and persons with a disability, also, to apply mainstream protection principles throughout the project cycle by considering the different needs of men, women, boys, and girls. The provision of sanitation services (latrines, washing facilities, etc), will be consulted with women/men and boys/girls to ensure they serve the communities equitably and take into account the specific needs of these groups. IRD has refined the content of hygiene kits in consultation with women and teenage girls through community meetings.
IhsanRD will hire female staff in distribution and monitoring teams to facilitate communication and consultation between IRD’s staff and women and girls. IhsanRD will hire women and men and will provide them with cleaning tools and materials to clean latrines in the targeted camps to sustain those latrines and keep them clean for use by the whole community.
IhsanRD has a well-established Complaint Response Mechanism in place, with a full team of staff who have been training on its use and function. The CRM operates online and offline with various communication channels available to project participants (WhatsApp, phone, email; physical boxes). During all Interventions, complaint boxes are provided and CRM materials are distributed which outline the CRM process and its confidentiality. In addition to the CRM, IRD has PSEA and Child Safe Guarding policies and mechanisms in place to ensure every effort is made to protect all program participants.
Finally, all staff working on these interventions will be given the needed information about any other services in the area (protection, education, etc.). They will also be trained on safe identification and referrals so as that when faced with any case on the field they will be able to correctly handle it and refer or point beneficiaries to the correct needed

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2021, WASH services in IDP sites require significant and continuous investments. IDPs living in sites across Syria are amongst the most vulnerable groups often solely relying on humanitarian aid (77% of interviewed residents of IDP sites received some kind of WASH humanitarian assistance, with 11% not being satisfied with it, mainly due to insufficient quantity or low quality of assistance). 69% of interviewed HHs living in IDP sites exclusively depend on water trucking services and often reported water insufficiency (20%) to meet daily household needs, 27% of interviewed HHs in IDP sites in north-west and northeast Syria indicated they are forced to purchase water to meet or complement their water needs. 63% of those households spend more than five percent of their incomes on water, and those that rely on paid water trucking as the main water supply modality spends on average 7.2% of their income on water.The proposal has considered locations at severity scales of 4 and 5. according to 2021 HNO.According to an assessment take up by IhsanRD in April, families who have no option but to purchase water from commercial water trucks spend on average 25% and in some cases as much as 40% of their income on water (compared to 10% on average in the rest of the country according to HNO 2021).Most of the sewage systems deteriorated due to damage and lack of maintenance which resulted in a leakage of wastewater into roads, agricultural land, and often caused contamination of drinking water sources. In the project area, 80% of sanitation pipes need to be replaced, also there is a need to extend the sewage networks to connect new neighborhoods.

5 - Implementation of activities

This intervention aims to restore WASH services through rehabilitation and support operation of 4 water systems and 4 sewage systems in 8 locations in Idleb and Aleppo governorate.Restoring water stations and distribution networks intervention is based on detailed bills of quantities and needs assessments of the water systems by providing the required mechanical rehabilitation and the operations and maintenance costs for 6 months as the efforts will be made to establish/re-activate a tariff collection and accounting system, and the sustainability of the water system in these communities. The work will include the establishment of a Services Unit (SU) per water station to manage the timely operation, maintenance, and repairs for long-term sustainability for water systems.The rehabilitation of 4 sewage systems will include installation of concrete pipes with excavation and backfilling, cleaning, and unblocking sewage pipes and manholes, an extension of sewage networks, in addition to repairing the concrete in existing drainage and manholes. Emergency services will be done through the provision of adequate drinking water via water trucking (35l/p/d) while constantly assuring the quality of the water through chlorination and tested, storage water tanks, solid waste removal, provision of waste containers, constructing latrines blocks (each1 cell serve 25 people),de-sludging latrine septic tanks. Hygiene kits and hygiene promotion awareness. Beneficiary selection and verification. - Procurement phase. - Distribution phase. - Monitoring and closure - monthly monitoring report will be conducted through all activities. The project activities will be implemented in line with the COVID-19 guidance of the WASH Cluster.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

• Movements of water trucks in congested, camp like settings L:H, I: L Mitigation: Consult community (separately women and girls) regarding location of distribution points, delivery routes and its schedule; Avoid location of water points in narrow streets, congested areas; Establish clear routes and schedule for water trucking that use wider access routes, • Long distance to water collection points and route to be travelled not safe (exposure to GBV risk, UXO/ERW hazard). L:L, I: H Mitigation: Ensure agreed context/SPHERE standards (200-500m) and closely monitor these aspects during implementation; Consult separately girls, boys, women and men, including older people and those with disabilities in the assessment, • Toilets present GBV and/or health, safety risk; Access to toilet is not available to all 24/7;Access to toilets not available for all; L:H, I: H Mitigation: Ensure agreed context/SPHERE standards and closely monitor these provisions/aspects during implementation. Ensure women and adolescent girls are consulted during design, implementation and monitoring phase of projects. Accountability mechanisms are put in place and received feedback from users is followed up. Lessons from previous experience are shared and learned.• Risk of theft of WASH rehabilitation/construction materials such as generators and construction materials (during delivery/storage/ after installation) by armed groups and used in conflict activities. L:L, I: H Mitigation: MoUs to be agreed with local authorities/councils to ensure local ownership of materials and rehabilitated/new facilities.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

•Community acceptance for the project. Likelihood: Low. Impact: High Mitigation: Building the intervention based on needs assessments and consultations with local communities for their needs and priorities. Signing MoUs with local authorities in order to define the roles and responsibilities of each side.•Environmental risk, Bad weather. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Medium Mitigation: Continuous monitoring for weather forecasting through the available resources and postponing the activity accordingly and continuing when good environmental conditions.•Delays in transferring money to Syria through PTT Bank whereas it takes a lot of time and the Turkish Lira fluctuations during this period. This let some vendors withdraw their BIDs. Likelihood: High, Impact: medium Mitigation: Informing the vendors about transferring procedures and times and getting written approval from them prior to contracting.•Losing Access to targeted location due to the bad security situation. Likelihood: low. Impact: High Mitigation Measures: No operations without Risk Assessment” Safety and Security Policy, and contingency plan and New Area Security Assessment SOP•Personnel security: Likelihood: Low, Impact: High Mitigation: all staff will be trained on the contingency plan and SOP of field movement. •COVID-19 Outbreak in the target location. Likelihood: Low - Impact: High: Mitigation: Ensuring that all protective measures had been carefully considered and applied to avoid COVID-19 outbreak during implementation, social distancing at least 2 meters, providing masks and gloves for all persons involved in project activities (BNFs, field staff, vendors, drivers..etc). Applying Preventive all Measures for COVID-19.
