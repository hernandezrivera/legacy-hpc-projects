1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

World Vision will aim to reach 56,490 affected people in Nampula city and Erati Districts, in Nampula, Montepuez and Metuge Districts, in Cabo Delgado. The intervention will address the immediate food needs of the vulnerable communities and address sustainable livelihoods needs. The selection criteria will target female-headed households, elderly persons (>65 years), child headed households, disabled headed households and the chronically ill.The goal of the project is to reduce vulnerability to shocks by providing food/cash assistance and supporting rebuilding their livelihoods in Nampula and Cabo Delgado Provinces. The goal will be realized through utilization of low-tech-low-risk assets building approach knitted within traditional knowledge and practice.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

In addition to children, there are a number of other groups who have dire protection needs in communities affected by the disaster/crises. Displacement or relocation contexts pose heightened risks of gender-based violence to women and girls, of exclusion of elderly people and persons with disabilities, and can create high levels of stress among all affected persons. To address these risks and needs, and to support the rights of all people to live in safety and dignity, WVMoz will seek funding to conduct community-level protection monitoring in relocation sites, areas of return, and in other cyclone-affected communities. Dedicated protection staff will monitor risks and trends, provide targeted support to vulnerable individuals, and make referrals to specialized service providers. This monitoring will also facilitate proactive advocacy on behalf of vulnerable groups with duty-bearers – including the Government, community leaders, and other humanitarian responders.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

World Vision has an accountability mechanism hinged on 4 Program Accountability Framework pillars, including community consultation, participation, providing information, and receiving and responding to complaints. WV will conduct community consultations that will include the most vulnerable groups to establish feedback mechanisms. Community consultations and learning from previous projects will help to design accountability mechanisms for 1) information provision like banners, use of megaphones, ration boards, visibility materials; 2) complaints and feedback through suggestion boxes, linha verde, help desk. Awareness on the existence of mechanisms will be reinforced in pre-activity meetings (registrations, verifications, distributions, asset creation). Pre-distribution and mass awareness meetings with beneficiaries, community and local leaders will explain beneficiaries’ rights, PSEA, responsibilities to curb any gender-based related violence and abuse and how to access reporting channels in case of abuse and infringement of rights. Affected populations will have opportunity to provide project related feedback to mitigate risks of fraud, corruption or sexual exploitation and abuse. A committee of about 6 to 8 key members with 60% women. The committee will be composed of different socioeconomic group members i.e. representative of girls, boys, men, women, chiefs, youth, elderly, religious leaders, secretary, chiefs of blocks, extensionists and will be engaged in asset identification; Beneficiary identification/registration; food distribution, redemption of vouchers, monitoring assets and trainings and marking attendance registers. Project will consider distribution time for women protection.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

World Vision uses the rights-based social model of disability inclusion which recognizes and partners with women and girls, men and boys, elderly persons and persons with disabilities and addresses barriers. Building on World Vision’s Guidelines for Inclusion of Persons with a disability (PWD), and individuals from minority groups all face distinct risks in crises settings and have unique needs. WV response teams will proactively seek to understand the specific experiences of these groups, and will ensure that the assistance and services are accessible to the most vulnerable. This includes ensuring a gender perspective, disability awareness, and proactive identification and support for individuals with specific needs. World Vision will partner with PWD organisations and specialized organizations, including Humanity & and Inclusion (formerly known as Handicap International) in Mozambique and other inclusion specialist agencies to ensure disability is streamlined across programming and to ensure accessible to people with disabilities. in-depth understanding and mainstreaming of disability awareness and inclusion.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

WVMoz is aware of environmental considerations and will act appropriately to implement activities that are not harmful to the environment. The projects will also support the community to promote appropriate environmental hygiene and sanitation practices.
