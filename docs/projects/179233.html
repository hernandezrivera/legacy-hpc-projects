4 - Needs

According to the data collected for this project in May 2019, South Syria appears to be one of the most underservedareas of Syria: the average population per functioning PHC is 11,307 in Dar’a and 2,127 inQuneitra. Dar’a has 3 hospital beds per 10,000population and Quneitra has 13 per 10,000. Dar’a has only 13% (14/105) of PHCs fullyfunctioning, while an additional 69% (72/105) are partially functioning. In Quneitra, only49% (29/59) of PHCs are functioning. The majority of fully functioning PHCs areconcentrated in the north and central areas of Dar’a governorate (As Sanaman and Izradistricts) and in northern Quneitra. Secure access is challenging for some areas, with 18 %of pre-crisis PHCs in Dar’a not being accessible at present. Health facilities (HFs) cannotdeal with the huge need due to a very limited number of medical staff and lack of suppliesrequired for treatments. The situation is much worse in the areas that came back underGoS control, where almost all PHCs are not functioning, equipment has been stolen, andhealth referral systems are weak or non-functional. There is only one public referralhospital and remaining cases are being referred to private hospitals where patients mustpay high costs for services, or to the public hospitals in Damascus, which are notaccessible for people in all locations. People who are trying to access health services facehigh travel costs, security concerns, and risk of detainment while travelling through thecheck-points. Ambulances are also in short supply and insufficient to meet needs. There is also a shortage of medical professionals and specialists, especially physicians, as many have left due to ongoing insecurity and low salaries.

5 - Implementation of activities

This project will build on current programming, community-level linkages, and establishedrelationships, including MoUs and agreements with the Ministry and Directorates ofHealth, to enhance the availability of health services through rehabilitation, re-equipment,and training support to non-functioning and partially functioning HFs, in coordination withother health actors also doing health programming in these governorates. In addition,we will use existing experience in South Syria and other parts of Syria toextend the reach of health services through mobile clinics and community healthinterventions. The primary activities are rehabilitation of health centers, provision of primary care services, and outreach via mobile health units. 4 PHCs will be rehabilitated and equipped, in each center the actual number of health staff is as follows : Ain Al Tina : 18/ Qarqas : 16/ Naba Al Fawar : 14/ Breiqa: 10 but the MOH intend to increase the staff number in each PHC after rehabilitation. Under this project, outpatient visits are a visit by a patient to a health care provider in which the patient presents a problem or issue and the health care provider provides medical evaluation, diagnosis, treatment, and/or referral for that person. For all protection concerns, Dorcas will have social workers and psychosocial counselors tethered to PHCs and mobile units to provide referral pathways and support. The monitoring and evaluation will be done by Dorcas' monitoring and evaluation officer following a plan established for the project. The monitoring of health activities will be done jointly with the health coordinator, as for the MHPSS activities will be done jointly with Dorcas MHPSS officer. Dorcas uses a robust 2-way CFM.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

In South Syria, traditional social norms reinforce discriminatory expectations of women’sbehavior leading to gender inequalities in movement, access to and control overresources, empowerment, and safety. The project will apply agender lens across all phases of the project to understand the differing needs of men,women, boys, and girls and ensure gender integration is mainstreamed throughoutactivities. The project will also look at where this lens intersects with other forms ofdiscrimination such as ageism or against people with disabilities to identify barriers thatprevent individual and community participation to humanitarian programming, and alsoidentify capacities for self-reliance. Female CHWs and health educators will be prioritizedduring recruitment to ensure that household visits to women are culturally appropriate andeasily accepted and include targeted awareness to women on health services andimportance of seeking health care. Inclusion and accessibility will be applied across allsectors and sub-sectors for this project, ensuring universal access to information andservices.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Access and security: As the proposed activities rely on accessfrom organizations implementing within South Syria, the project iscomprised of partners who already have access or have the relationships to enableaccess to planned project locations. Adhering to GoS protocols and approval processeswhile observing core humanitarian principles will be imperative to project implementation,as will the assumption that the security situation remains relatively stable for partners toaccess and operate in proposed locations, particularly in Dar’a, safely. • Availability of andability to train staff: the project will aim to hire Syrian staff and ensure gender equalitywherever possible (in particular, aiming to ensure inclusion of female staff for communityhealth work). Recognizing the need to predominantly work with MoH staff in relation toservice delivery and training, any training of MoH staff will need to be coordinated withand approved through the MoH. Currency flux: : Internal mitigation is to have a small amount ofSYP at any time and keep funds in hard currency. In addition, there is a contingencybudget in the SJR which could be released in the case of hyperinflation.
