Needs

The humanitarian situation in Mali has significantly deteriorated over the past year due to conflict and increasing intercommunal clashes in the northern and central regions, as well as a poor agro-pastoral production in certain regions. The security situation remains unpredictable and could continue to deteriorate in northern and central Mali if a sustainable solution is not found for the conflict and intercommunal clashes in the northern and central regions of Mali. Although the prospects for the current agricultural season are average to above-average, food insecurity will continue to affect hundreds of thousands of households in areas with poor agro-pastoral production due to conflict, rainfall deficit in certain areas and crop losses from flooding. The results of the 2018 SMART survey show that four regions surpass the WHO Global Acute Malnutrition alert threshold of 10 per cent: Segou, Tombouctou, Menaka and Gao. The severe acute malnutrition rate remains at a worrying level as the national rate of 2 percent is still above the emergency threshold. Seven active clusters (Education, Food Security, Health, Nutrition, Protection, Shelter and NFIs and WASH), sub clusters and dedicated working groups (Access, CMCoord, Accountability, Gender and Cash) are in place at national and subnational levels and were involved in the development of the 2019 HRP. Based on their analysis, conflict/ insecurity, drought, floods and their combined effects will affect 6.7 million people with 2,8 million people in need of humanitarian assistance (still provisional). The affected people are mostly located in the conflict-prone areas in the northern and central regions of the country as well as areas in the southern regions affected by recurrent food insecurity and malnutrition crisis and floods. The most vulnerable groups include internally displaced persons, returnees, refugees and their host communities, and agro-pastoral households. Among them, women and children are considered as the most vulnerable groups as they are more exposed to protection abuses in conflict situations and to severe malnutrition in the Sahel region. Considering the above context, OCHA Mali will continue to focus on strategic coordination under the leadership of the Humanitarian Coordinator and the Humanitarian Country Team. Through coordination and advocacy, OCHA will improve disaster response preparedness and early warning and ensure that timely assistance reaches the most vulnerable population (women, men, girls and boys). OCHA will also support the implementation of the New Way of Working and the preparation of a multi-year HRP for 2020-2023 to “move from delivering aid to ending needs”, in line with the SG’s Agenda for Humanity, by addressing the structural causes of the crisis. OCHA Mali will enhance common situational awareness and support decision-making by providing high quality information management services and products to enable an objective analysis of the situation based on reliable figures, facilitate rapid response and highlight gaps. OCHA will advocate for access, protection of civilians and resource mobilization. At the end of 2018, OCHA Mali had a total of 43 staff, with 14 international staff (6 women and 8 men) and 29 national staff (5 women and 24 men), located in Bamako (31) and in field offices in Mopti (3), Gao (6) and Timbuktu (3). In 2019, OCHA will reinforce its team, maintain its presence in these locations, and continue to remotely cover Taoudenit, Ménaka and Kidal. For areas where OCHA is not present but humanitarian needs are important, 14 humanitarian focal points cover four districts in Timbuktu (Niafunke, Dire, Goundam, Gourma-Rharous), two districts in Gao ( Ansongo and Bourem), four districts in Mopti (Tenenkou, Youwarou, Koro, Douentza) and the regions of Menaka, Segou, Kayes and Kidal. More focal points could be appointed if required by the humanitarian situation.

Activities

-> Support the HC, the HCT and the cluster coordinators to effectively implement the Humanitarian Programme Cycle;-> Ensure that early warning mechanisms and other minimum and advanced preparedness measures (for floods, epidemics) are in place as part of emergency response preparedness;-> Organize and facilitate multi-sector gender-sensitive needs assessments and other field missions as required;-> Engage with armed actors to protect and promote humanitarian space, and protection of affected populations;-> Ensure that a collective approach to accountability to affected people is in place, in line with Core Humanitarian Standard and IASC commitment to Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA);-> Publish regularly information products to better understand the humanitarian situation, identify gaps and constraints, and highlight ongoing or future responses;-> Facilitate the development of a high-quality needs-based and gender-sensitive multi-annual HRP;-> Advocate for predictable, timely and flexible funding to respond to identified needs;-> Brief and train humanitarian/ armed actors and other key stakeholders (Government, local NGOs and journalists) on Coordination and humanitarian principles, including at regional and community level;-> Contribute to reflexions on the Nexus and New Way of Working (NWoW), work with humanitarian and development partners to put in place and implement the NWoW and improve strategic and operational linkages between humanitarian and development interventions, and contribute to the formulation of the Integrated Strategic Framework, ensuring that humanitarian issues are fully reflected and considered.
