Project Summary

According to the recently updated Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP, August 2020), as many as 12.9 million people are affected by the crisis, of which 6.8 million are in need of urgent humanitarian assistance to meet basic needs. The combined effects of conflict, multifaceted insecurity and the repetitive cycle of shocks will accentuate pre-existing vulnerabilities, forcing some populations to adopt negative coping or survival mechanisms.
The project covers three countries (Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger). For the present HRP Mali, only the part concerning Mali has been put online, both for the achievements and the beneficiaries. It will be implemented in the regions of Gao and Menaka, bordering Burkina Faso and Niger.
WASH will improve hygiene practices, such as handwashing, safe excreta disposal, safe water management through activities and messages adapted to the evolving context.
NRC's Shelter programme will provide adequate shelter assistance through the distribution of shelter kits, NFI kits or cash, as well as technical support.
NRC protection programme will strengthen its durable solutions approach to reduce vulnerability and enhance community resilience and ICLA will improve access to civil documentation and HLP rights, .
Livelihoods and Food Security will focus on the provision of immediate food aid to address acute and chronic food and nutritional challenges and support livelihoods and early recovery to achieve sustainable solutions.
NRC in Mali will target vulnerable IDPs, returnees and refugees as well as host communities with an integrated response, creating a package of activities to a specific need within the targeted regions. NRC's response will target the most vulnerable groups, including women, girls, children, elders and people with disabilities.
The action will be implemented in coordination with NRC's other programmes in Mali and with all humanitarian actors.

1 - Contexte

Since 2012, the humanitarian situation in Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger continues to deteriorate and it is often described as a situation of “no peace no war”. The cumulative effects of climate shocks as drought and floods, armed conflict and widespread insecurity, mainly affecting northern and central regions, exacerbate pre-existing vulnerabilities and have caused the displacement of an additional 311 000 people as of otober 2020 in Mali. In addition, the health emergency linked to the Covid 19 pandemic exacerbates pre-existing vulnerabilities with significant socioeconomic consequences in the short, medium and long term. Following several weeks of political unrest and demonstrations, in mid-August, elements of the Malian Armed Forces began a mutiny, ultimately leading to the resignation of President Ibrahim Boubacar Keita (August 2020). Inter-community conflicts are fuelling displacement and many communities are struggling to access food, water, healthcare, education, shelter and protection services. Human rights violations are common, while women and girls also faced increased risks of gender-based violence (GBV). Failure to meet humanitarian needs will worsen the impact of the crisis in Mali and the wider region and could perpetuate inter-community violence, while exposing youth to a higher risk of recruitment by non-state armed actors and criminal groups.
