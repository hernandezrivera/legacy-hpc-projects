Project Summary

This project addresses the health needs of a population in northwest Syria, including host communities and IDPs, out- and in-camps, with a special focus on IDP,s. The project aims at continuing and expanding the life-saving hemodialysis services for more than 472 patients with end-stage renal failure (non-communicable diseases), which can be divided into 2 main components:
1. Maintain the quantity of dialysis service. This component will focus on providing medication and medical consumables, excluding dialysis kits, to cover the urgent needs of 9 dialysis centers targeted for 6 months.
1. Maintain dialysis service provision continuity. This component is subdivided into to:
- Repair and provide regular maintenance for the dialysis machines.
- Repair and provide regular maintenance for the water treatment units.

4 - Needs

By reviewing recent assessments done by the dialysis taskforce on current dialysis needs/services in Idlib and Aleppo governorates, the total number of registered cases of patients with renal failure was 472. These patients are currently served by 9 dialysis centers run by different NGOs (7 in Idleb governorate and 2 in Aleppo governorate). Our proposal targets the 9 NGO-run centers. In these centers: 1) Medications and consumables: The assessments also showed that one of the main challenges faced by the dialysis facilities in Idleb governorate is the shortage of medications and consumables, which is due to the high prices of dialysis items and difficulty of securing them; these items are unaffordable by the patients and the only source for securing them is the humanitarian assistance. The last campaign of distributing dialysis kits (run by WHO) did not include some of the key medications, such as erythropoietin, which would decrease the need for blood transfusions and corresponding risks (blood-borne diseases). The anemia is one of the most important manifestations of a patient with chronic kidney failure and giving folic acid or iron as a treatment for this phenomenon and its benefits are very limited, and the only treatment previously followed was blood transfusion or the transport of concentrated red blood cells. However, blood transfusion has many risks for the patient and the medical and nursing staff. Patients with kidney failure, unfortunately, have weak immunity, and with them, they need blood transfusions, the possibility of infection increases during blood transfusions, and transfusion blood may not be subject to careful prior examination so it helps to transfer serious diseases such as hepatitis or AIDS, for example, if the person from whom blood transfusion was infected or Carrier of these diseases.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

All proposed activities will be delivered in adherence to the principles of humanitarian neutrality and ensure equal access to all beneficiaries in need without discrimination. All staff will be trained on principles and standards of humanitarian principles, preserving dignity of the beneficiaries. AL Resala Foundation (RF) will work with staff stakeholders, and other actors to ensure gender, as well as protection considerations are mainstreamed into all activities by prioritizing consideration of the specific and often sensitive issues related to gender, disability, age, and protection among the beneficiary populations. This will be emphasized in the collection and analysis of datadisaggregated by age and sex and key informant interviews conducted by relevant staff throughout the project cycle with both mixed and single-sex groups. This information will help in adjusting the project’s emergency program design to meet gender-specific needs, disparities, and vulnerabilities affecting targeted populations. In this way, RFs will continue to ensure the impartiality of program services while enabling the project team to identify individuals and families with specific vulnerabilities related to age, sex, disability, family size, health status, socioeconomic status, or other factors
