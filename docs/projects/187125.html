1 - Needs

The humanitarian crisis in North-East Nigeria, now in its eleventh year is a protection crisis compounded by challenges in the availability of and access to basic services. Violent conflict continues between non-state armed groups (NSAGs), including ‘Boko Haram’ and the Islamic State’s West Africa Province (ISWAP) and the Nigeria Security Forces (NSF) resulting in continued conditions of insecurity, waves of displacement of children and families, violations of human rights and destruction of life and property. In addition to the general needs of children impacted by the conflict, girls and boys have also been subjected to grave child rights violations. Many have been separated, recruited, abducted or held by non-state armed groups (NSAG), during which time they have experienced sexual violence, while some may have participated in physical violence. Humanitarian response plan estimates that 1.7 million people (1.6 million children and 100,000 caregivers) need child protection services; this includes an estimated 21,000 children and caregivers living with disabilities. due to the complex and protracted conflict, with greater vulnerabilities anticipated in inaccessible areas. Community resilience to protect children has been weakened, resulting in children and families adopting negative coping mechanisms. Girls and boys continue to bear the brunt of the violence that has continued throughout 2020 and children already affected by conflict faced challenges to access services due to COVID 19 pandemic. The protection and well-being of over millions of children and adolescents in the north-east Nigeria remains seriously at risk mainly due to the complex and protracted conflict, with a weakened level of resilience of communities for the protection of children and worsened by the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic. According to the Gender-Based Violence Information Management System (GBVIMS), 99 per cent of GBV incidents reported between January-June 2019 were made of women and girls, although boys and men have also been subject to GBV. Twenty one percent of the reported incidents were on children, out of whom 35 percent were unaccompanied or separated. Due to their vulnerabilities, children, especially adolescent girls and unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), are particularly at-risk of sexual abuse and child or forced marriages .In 2020, 99% of the reported incidents of GBV were perpetrated against women and girls across Adamawa, Borno and Yobe states, with 80% of incidents of GBV perpetrated against adults over the age of 18 and 20% against children (below the age of 18 years) compared to 2019 where with 81% of incidents of GBV perpetrated against adults over the age of 18 years and 19% of the incidents. Additionally, girls and boys affected are increasingly at risk of being engaged in hazardous forms of labor with over million girls, boys and caregivers in need of psychosocial support services due to severe distress, hardship and displacement. An estimated 800,000 children were at risk of being injured or killed by unexploded devices and remnants of war across north-east Nigeria . Most children have been exposed to one or more of the following: violence, loss of friends, family members and neighbours, accumulated stress, deterioration in living conditions, inability to provide for one’s self and family, increased military presence, divisions in societies, and lack of access to services. It is estimated that over 6,500 children have been separated from their families and are either unaccompanied or separated. These experiences have a significant impact on the psychosocial wellbeing and development of children and have both immediate and long-term consequences for children, families and communities. They can also hamper their ability to access other services, such as education, further damaging their life chances. These children are in need of psychosocial support to build their resilience, help them to recover.

2 - Activities

Sector Strategic Objective 1: Girls and boys facing protection risks, including adolescents and children with disabilities, have access to comprehensive case management services. Key activities under Sector Strategic Objective 11. Identification, consistent follow-up and service provision for children at-risk of, or affected by, protection concerns, including case management for UASC including COVID19, former CAAG & GBV and children with disabilities.2. Children deprived of parental care, or whose parental care is assessed as inadequate, are provided with timely, reliable and safe alternative care arrangements with prioritization of family-based solutions.3. Identification, assessment, train and support to foster parents including supervision during the provision of foster care. 4. Family tracing and reunification for unaccompanied and separated children and referral of vulnerable children to access inter-sectoral and other specialized services.Sector Strategic Objective 2: Conflict-affected children, including adolescents and children with disabilities and caregivers’ well-being is enhanced through provision of quality mental health and psychosocial support services and information on childcare and protection.Key activities under Sector Strategic Objective 2 1. Provide psychosocial services and Life-skills education for children and caregivers of vulnerable children through Structured, mobile and age-specific creative, recreational and social activities for children including adolescents.2. Mental health and counselling services for vulnerable children including adolescents and caregivers.3. Educate on positive parenting lessons for caregivers of vulnerable children or vulnerable caregivers.4. Raise awareness at community level on child protection with specific focus on relevant child protection issues including grave violation against children, GBV and protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) within a given community. Sector Strategic Objective 3: Conflict-affected boys, girls and young people benefit from strengthened community-based services and prevention actions including reintegration services. Identify (existing and new), train, mentor and work with community-based mechanisms for child protection including Child Protection Committees, camp management committees, children’s groups, youth groups and school-based management committees.5. A system developed to identify and report on grave rights violations as described under the Security Council Resolution 1612 mechanism;7.
