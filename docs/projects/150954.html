Project Summary

The project aims to improve the living conditions of IDPs in sites and settlements and to ensure equal access to services through site management, coordination and information management activities in areas that have the highest levels of displacement. ACTED intends to prioritize areas where it has established presence and working relationships with stakeholders and authorities, such as Jubbaland (Kismayo), Bay (Baidoa) and Nugaal (Garowe), with expansion to new areas based on identified needs.

Needs

Although the drought has receded, at least in certain areas of the country, the situation remains critical as households' resilience and coping mechanisms have been severely affected by years of protracted crisis (FSNAU September 2018). Returns to area of origin are very limited: majority of the affected population lost source of livelihood so they remain in urban areas where humanitarian aid is concentrated due to lack of access to rural areas; in other cases, insecurity still does not allow safe and dignified returns for the affected population. Evictions continue to be a major protection issues for displaced population and in many cases prevent to work toward local integration. Over 204,000 people were evicted between January and July 2018, almost double the 122,000 evicted over the same period last year (HLP July 2018). Even where evictions are less rampant, lack of coordination and credible discussions on durable solutions are an obstacle to local integration. Sites are for the highest part self-settled: apart from eviction risks, this poses major protection concerns related to site layout that increases the risk of fire and disease outbreak, floods but also other protection concerns related to overcrowded spaces and lack of privacy. Presence of camp management is key to ensure coordination of service provision, underpinned by reliable strong and regular information management. Participation of the affected population is lacking in many aspects of the delivery of humanitarian assistance: Camp Management is therefore crucial to empower affected population through dissemination of information on delivery of humanitarian assistance; to support inclusive and representative community governance structures; as well as to monitor that the community is engaged in service delivery by other partners.

Activities

Coordination and Information management: Coordination will aim at monitoring service delivery and standards in the sites, identify gaps and needs, maximize protection impact of partners programming over available resources. Coordination will be underpinned by reliable, up to date information management on population data and service delivery. Information products produced and circulated with partners, clusters, local authorities and communities will include but will not be limited to: 3Ws, service mapping, Site Profile, Eviction risk monitoring (in coordination with HLP), CCCM Snapshots, new arrivals monitoring. Site Care and Maintenance: Establishment of Site Maintenance Committees, provision of tools and skilled training supervision of their cash for work activities. Implementation of emergency site improvement projects to address project risks in the sites. These activities will be connected directly to GBV integration. Communication with Communities and Community Participation: Mapping of existing governance structures and their integration to make them inclusive of all the different components of the communities, capacity building towards community self-management, integrated and multi sector CFM for the sites, establishment of info desk and information boards to increase community awareness on their rights and services available.GBV Integration: GBV will be actively integrated in CCCM interventions particularly through safety audits, GBV risk mitigations through site maintenance activities, focus on women empowerment and women participation, coordination with GBV partners disseminate GBV Referral Pathways within the partners and the community. Durable solutions: link to durable solutions will be ensured through coordination with existing durable solutions forums, consultation on intentions of the affected population (whenever is appropriate for the context), livelihood assessments, and cooperation with HLP for negotiating stable land tenure agreements.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

In areas where evictions are less rampant and public land is available ACTED will promote transition to local integration as durable solutions, particularly through: - Promoting and conducting consultation with affected population on intentions; - Participating in durable solutions forums at the local level (and advocate for their creation), inclusive of all the stakeholders , with the local authorities playing a key part in the planning for durable solutions;- Advocating for land and resilience services for affected population;- Integrating CM and Shelter component and, once land is secure with a stable formal stable agreement, built sustainable transitional shelters with active involvement of the local population; - Linking construction of Transitional shelters to other existing long term and resilience building interventions;- With its Community Participation approach, ACTED will build community capacity working towards community self-management and future handover of camp management activities to the communities themselves. As a contribution to durable solutions through information management and assessment services, ACTED will also partner with REACH (ACTED and IMPACT’s common initiative), which will work on data collection for the Detailed Site Assessment (DSA). The DSA brings together tools developed by different agencies into a shared site identification system, designed to gather key-informant and observation site-level data on basic informal site characteristics and needs, and feed into the Somalia Site Master List. In linkage with ACTED’s CCCM intervention, REACH will intervene for data collection aiming to cover all accessible urban and peri-urban areas throughout the mapping process. The assessment will be conducted on a bi-annual basis. Based on extensive consultation with the CCCM cluster and partners throughout the first year of the programme, the aims of the DSA have evolved to reflect the extremely fluid displacement context in Somalia. The key outputs from the assessment will focus on providing a snapshot of humanitarian need through a severity ranking at site, district and sectoral level, in order to assist the cluster with targeting.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Risk: Assistance and services do not reach the most vulnerable categoriesLikelihood: Medium Impact: HighMitigation measures: Project aims to target IDPs settlements. ACTED strictly applies the 4 core humanitarian principles plus Do Not Harm in all its interventions. Through regular physical presence in the sites and service delivery monitoring activities, ACTED will ensure that principle of impartiality is respected by partners working in the sites and that beneficiaries are prioritized only on the base of needs. Inclusive Community Governance structures representative of the all the different component of the affected population will help in identification of most vulnerable and more in need HHs to be targeted with the interventions. Community Governance structures and communities will be empowered through capacity building activities and dissemination of information on services available as well as rights of the affected population. Site maintenance activities will be conducted to ensure that services are also accessible to people with limited mobility. An integrated and multi sector CFM mechanism for the site will be in place and give the chance to every individual to present complaints, including on beneficiaries’ selections and verification processes, and share feedback which will be used to inform programming.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Risk: Community governance structures are not representative of different age and gender groups Likelihood: MediumImpact: HighMitigation measures: ACTED will map existing community structures in place and if community governance structures in place are not inclusive, they will be integrated in order to equally represent different ages and gender of the affected population. Particular attention during training and capacity building will be given to empower women and youth in the community, including the possible implementation of specific skill-training activities for them. An integrated and multi sector CFM mechanism for the site will give the chance to every individual to present complaints and share feedback, including reporting discriminatory delivery of humanitarian assistance. The project will have a strong GBV integration component, with focus on safety audit and women participation as well as mitigation of GBV risks faced in the sites. Trainings conducted by ACTED in CCCM have specific modules on GBV mainstreaming.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Risk: High needs vs. limited resources/services provided Likelihood: MediumImpact: HighMitigation measures: ACTED will target new arrivals and evicted communities with emergency and immediate cash assistance: this will provide at least short term relief to HHs in serious distress because of displacement, often connected to widespread violence, especially in south central Somalia. New arrivals will also be referred to specialized protection partners for immediate protection assistance, following referral pathways in site, and to other services available, such as nutrition and health. Through its community mobilizer, ACTED will pay attention to the arise of possible conflicts within the community or between community and host community, including clan related tensions or tension over resources, and act in a timely manner to mitigate their impact on the affected population including setting up peaceful coexistence forums with host communities or with different clans in the community. In line with cluster strategy, ACTED will advocate for shared infrastructures with local population, especially with regards to schools, primary health care centers, Child Friendly Spaces and Women Friendly Spaces. Risk: Project operations are hampered by volatile security environment Likelihood: MediumImpact: HighMitigation measures: ACTED closely monitors the security dynamics liaising with the local communities including traditional elders, local authorities and regional state government representatives. Over time, ACTED has developed a low profile access strategy with solid security measures. A participatory approach is used to update security risk analysis and triangulated with other sources to provide regular security briefings to staff. Working closely with targeted communities, engagement with local partners and prioritizing community sensitization and participation, ACTED and partners build acceptance of activities from the bottom up.
