Project Summary

According to 2022 HNO, an estimated 3.6 million people are in need of shelter and NFIs assistance resulted by recurrent conflict, drought, flooding, evictions and cyclones as key drivers of humanitarian issues and caused vulnerability and migration from rural to urban and peri-urban centers resulted in inadequate shelter conditions, overcrowding, economic hardship, and lack of security of tenure. Those in need of shelter and NFI assistance include displaced and non-displaced populations. A total of 30,800 refugee and asylum seekers and 90,583 refugee returnees need shelter and NFIs assistance in 2022 as they do not have sufficient access to basic and domestic items and shelter assistance as well as financial capacity to build adequate shelters. As per UNHCR post return monitoring data, there is a high proportion of returnees live in housing they do not own and have no documentation as this leads to protection concerns, including housing insecurity and risk of eviction. On 2022 HNO, a total of 593,058 IDPs are to settle in informal and largely unplanned settlements facing unsafe living conditions, they do not have financial capacity to build adequate shelters. They usually do not own the land they occupy and also are not allowed to build any permanent structure. Due to lack of tenure agreements and housing options, minority and marginalized groups including women headed households as the main income earner have no alternative residence options.
Importantly, Women and girls living in sub-standard and overcrowded shelters are particularly vulnerable. They tend not to feel safe in makeshift shelters which do not offer privacy and sufficient protection from weather elements and increase the risk of gender based violence. Over a quarter, 27%, of IDPs reported that their bathing facilities are not safe as their main shelter concern. In addition, marginalized groups and people with special needs also require additional support and should be prioritized as resources are limited.
As a result, this project is to address shelter and NFIs needs of IDP families and refugee (90%), while poor host communities (10%) will receive NFIs only in Kaxda and Daynile districts of Banadir region, Afgoye and Marka districts of Lower Shabelle region, Baidoa district of Bay region, and Beletweyne of Hiiraan region in South and Central Somalia including women-headed household families, extended families, families with disabled and elderly persons who are most in need of humanitarian assistance to mitigate risk and its consequences on crisis-affected populations at the target areas. The main objective is to improve the living conditions and safe access to services of IDP families including refugee returnees and poor host communities in the targeted locations. HINNA aims at providing 5000 ESKs and NFI kits to 5000 HHs (30,000 beneficiaries/Individuals) while 3000 (10%) people of them will be poor host communities through in-kind distribution to improve the living conditions of the target beneficiaries who faced inadequate shelter/NFIs conditions. The reason we can't use cash transfer programming for this project is that we have had several meetings with the communities in some of the project target areas and most of them have told us that cash transfer program can cause insecurity, family violence that can lead to family breakdown and other things including missing kits or rising market prices.
Disaggregation of gender will be promoted in full consideration with protection mainstreaming. The total beneficiaries of the project (30,000 people) of the drought, floods, and conflict-affected populations including (4,800 women, 4,800 men, 9,600 boys, and 9,600 girls) including 1200 elderly people (4%) in the project target locations will directly be receiving any benefits of the project through selection criteria set by the shelter cluster.

1. - Projects activities

1. Construction of 4500 ESKs for 4500 HHs (27,000 individuals) of the Crises-Affected Populations including IDPs and returnees in terms of their needs for shelter and NFIs assistance through selection criteria of the cluster specifically new arrivals, recently forced evictees, and protracted IDPs including minority and marginalized groups of those in need of privacy and protection from weather conditions in the project target regions in full consideration with women headed-households, extended families, and people with special needs - Banadir, Lower Shabelle, Bay, and Hiiraan. 2. Distribution of 4500 NFI kits available to 4500 IDP families and returnees (27,000 Individuals) of the same beneficiaries who received ESKs as well as 500 NFI kits to 500 vulnerable host communities families (Non-IDPs) (3,000 individuals) in terms of their vulnerability criteria of NFIs. Further clarification on vulnerability criteria in terms of shelter and NFIs needs include: families with female headed-households, minority clan families, child headed-household families, extended families, families with specific needs (persons with disabilities), and households with older and sick people who are new arrivals due to drought, flooding and conflict and also recent forced evictees. In addition, families with specific needs in IDPs and host communities will be part in the families to whom the project is targeting who will be given higher priority as they are the most vulnerable in the project target communities. 3. According to site decongestion guidance and Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs), the project will implement site planning and decongestion in full consultation with representatives of target communities including CMCs, site leaders, and landowners, local authorities to reduce or prevent risks such as transmission of the virus of COVID-19, outbreaks, and issues of GBV and flooding. 4. For accountability improvement, the project will conduct PCM/PDM exercise four to six weeks after distribution to ensure the quality and/or issues related to services provided for future intervention.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

(4.1)-Minority clans/ethnic groups are the main part of project designing and implementation and they are included during the consultations and decision-making process. Minority clan members in IDP sites and host communities who experience structural and distinct forms of exclusion and discrimination with elevated needs that different from the population at large and also have different sources of problems for their communities and different security risks than the general population will be targeted. Apart from these, any important information about the whole project will be shared with them to avoid any action that may possibly make them excluded. The full participation of these people in the project is crucial to the achievement of objectives, the project will give more priority to the equality of gender, and "Do No Harm principles will be adopted among project stakeholders. (4.5)-The project will support the most vulnerable IDP communities and returnees who are mostly living in private lands. The risk of dispute on landowner and land tenure may rise, but the two-side agreement with a specified period of time at least 3 years between landowners and established IDP community committee or representatives from IDP sites will be developed in the presence of district commissioners/local authorities to ensure the land tenure.
