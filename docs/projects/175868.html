Project Summary

In 2021, IOM South Sudan's Shelter and Non-Food Items Unit (SNFI) seeks to fill immediate and long-term housing and household needs across the country. IOM plans to respond to the multi-faceted needs of displaced, returnee and host populations through the provision of life saving and life-sustaining SNFIs, as well as community support services such as capacity building disaster preparedness, dispute resolution and referral services. Support will be provided through numerous modalities, such as in-kind distributions and cash- and voucher-based interventions, while ensuring vulnerable populations are prioritized and reached in every response activity. IOM's responses align with cluster priorities and objectives and are targeted to geographic areas experiencing the most significant shelter and NFI needs. IOM plans to target 154,592individuals with the provision of shelter and NFI support and flood mitigation programming. IOM continues to support communities that include new and protracted IDPs, returnees, host communities, and people affected by floods. As site focal point for IDP Naivasha camp and collective Centers in WBeG, IOM will provide SNFI assistance, shelter upgrade to new and protracted IDPs. IOM plans to expand its cash-based interventions in areas with functional markets. In 2021, IOM aims to assist 28 per cent of its target beneficiaries through cash-based modalities in Wau, Bor and other areas. Being Cluster lead, IOM as last resort will deploy teams to any location in the country, if no partner is available.
Using the Linking Relief with Recovery and Development (LRRD) framework, IOM undertakes long-term settlement programming alongside its emergency interventions. Since 2019, IOM has facilitated the formation of seven village development committees and constructed more than 500 permanent, low-cost shelters for returnees and host community members. IOM plans to expand and strengthen its settlement programming through enhanced coordination with other units and humanitarian partners, linking essential services such as WASH infrastructure, livelihood resources, mental and physical health services. Focusing on areas of return and locations with relative stability and security, this settlement planning model allows communities to build their resilience and access a network of services, creating enhanced stability peace and prosperity.
Communities in the Nile basin experienced extensive property losses, livelihoods and life due to the second year of intense seasonal flooding. In anticipation of future flooding events, IOM will strengthen and expand community-based preparedness and planning mechanisms while linking community members to early warning systems and data-based predictive modelling. IOM plans to construct flood mitigation infrastructure in Bor and other flood-affected areas that will benefit 80,000 individuals. IOM will establish and strengthen DMCs at the Boma level and, form new committees at the county and state levels. Parallel to IOM's work undertaken at the community level, the SNFI team also works with national policymakers and national political leadership structures to advance progressive and meaningful South Sudan legislation. IOM has been deeply engaged in the process of developing and finalizing a National Land Policy. IOM has also aided in the review and finalization of a National Disaster Risk Reduction Policy. IOM is dedicated to continuing in its commitment to support national and sub-national governmental authorities working in matters of land, housing, shelter, displacement, and disaster.
Lastly, IOM continues to lead the Shelter and NFI Cluster, a role it has held since 2011. IOM also hosts and co-leads the National Cash Working Group and the Housing, Land and Property Technical Working Group. IOM mainstreams HLP, AAP, CfM, GBV and numerous other programming elements into every programmatic activity and seeks to foster community capacity and resilience in every intervention.

1 - Needs

Over two-thirds of the total population of South Sudan are projected to need humanitarian assistance in 2021, with an estimated 2.1 million people experiencing inadequate access to non-food items and safe shelter. Over one million people have been affected by ongoing and intense flooding since July 2020. When overlayed with the country’s weakened economic situation and the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic, the nation’s humanitarian needs remain extremely significant. A detailed description of state-specific S-NFI needs are discussed below: Jonglei: An estimated 243,989 IDPs are reported in Jonglei state – the second-highest among all states. Some 495,000 individuals were affected by floods in Jonglei and Greater Pibor Administrative Areas. The entirety of Jonglei State is classified as IPC Phase 4 and 5, with 84.7% of the population facing emergency-level food insecurity. The DTM assessment found that 30.9% of IDPs and 33.7% of returnees are living in damaged shelters which need immediate repairs. Shelters damaged by flooding displaced people to other areas, including Mangala. An IOM needs analysis found that populations in Bor South, Pochalla Pibor, Uror and Akobo, need S-NFI support. Most flooded areas are still inaccessible, preventing humanitarian support from reaching people in need. Western Bahr el Ghazal (WBeG): IOM needs assessments in all three counties of WBeG state-reported urgent need for shelter and NFIs. The entirety of WBeG is categorized as IPC Phase 3, with around 43.3% of the population considered to be living in ‘Emergency’ conditions. There are12,676 IDPs residing in the Naivasha IDP camp and Masna collective centres. As the site focal point, IOM is responsible for shelter upgrades and repairs. Continuous shelter construction and repair needs are anticipated throughout 2021 as future conflicts and upheavals may result in new arrivals to these sites. DTM reported 64,096 IDPs in WBeG. In 2020, clashes in Tonj South and North caused IDPs to flee to Wau. IOM assessments determined that shelter and NFIs are among this population’s most urgent needs. There has also been a significant increase in returnees in Wau (+16,810 individuals), Jur River (+7,442) and Raja. Most of these returnees will need shelter and NFI support to settle. According to DTM reports, 47% of IDPs and 34.8% of returnees in WBeG are living in collapsed or dangerous shelters.Warrap: Warrap state hosts the highest number of IDPs in the country – 287,355 individuals (DTM data). Spikes in the localized conflict in Tonj North resulted in huge displacements to bordering counties and states. Food insecurity projection places 61% (294,000 individuals) of the state’s population in ‘Emergency’ (IPC 4) and ‘Catastrophe’ (IPC 5). DTM reported that 23,186 individuals (4,219 households) were displaced between July 10 and September 10 2020 to neighbouring payams within Tonj North. These clashes affected Awul, Kirik, and Rual-Bet payams. Current reports name shelter and NFIs among the population’s most urgent needs. Some of these locations remain accessible due to insecurity.Unity: Severe flooding caused by heavy rainfall in Unity State caused temporary displacements and disruptions in livelihoods and service provision. IRNA reports recommended shelter and NFIs as the most urgent needs in the state’s flood-affected areas. Additionally, 196,666 IDPs in and outside the camp will require SNFI assistance to meet their acute needs. The humanitarian needs in other states like Upper Nile, Lakes, Central, and Western Equatoria are similar to those named above. Armed clashes have caused continuous displacement in Central Equatoria– hundreds of people fled the Kuda area when their shelters were burnt. Around 11,555 IDPs moved from Terekeka to Juba due to violence against civilians in Tali payam, Central Equatoria. DTM reported 32,660 individuals (6,608 households) were displaced to payams in Awerial County, Lakes State

2 - Project activities

IOM maintains frontline teams in Juba and Wau and can deploy to any location within South Sudan contingent on security and access constraints. 1. The most appropriate modality for emergency support is determined through comprehensive needs and market assessments. IOM will undertake joint assessments involving CBOs and national NGOs, thereby building their capacities, and maintaining standard SNFI Cluster tools for data collection. Assessment findings are shared with the SNFI cluster, including consultations with local authorities, men, women, people with disabilities, youth and minority groups. 2. Following needs analyses, IOM starts the verification and registration processes for eligible beneficiaries. In consultation with beneficiaries, we will ensure equitable distribution approaches in locations most suitable for safe and dignified access to shelter. Distribution committees are formed and share information about the date, time, location and items to be distributed. The distribution process includes queuing points, a registration desk, separate lines for vulnerable people, item receiving points and a complaint desk. SNFI will assist 180,343 individuals through in-kind and CBI support. IOM follows WHO guidance and the SNFI Cluster COVID-19 response strategy. 3. IOM will respond to shelter rehabilitation needs in IDP camps and Collective Centers. Given the pandemic, SNFI will construct new shelters to avoid congestion in the camp if required. 4. In areas where markets are functional, SNFI will use CBIs such as multipurpose cash assistance and conditional grants for shelter and NFI support. SNFI plans to reach 27% of its beneficiaries through CBIs in 2021. 6. IOM also undertakes long-term settlement programming alongside its emergency interventions. Village development committees will be formed and 400 low-cost shelters in Wau and local high return areas will be constructed. SNFI will provide vocational training to 100 youth (50% women) for livelihood and skills development. IOM adopts an area-based approach to ensure that programming is geographically targeted, participatory and multi-sectoral. This approach advocates a new type of relationship with the community by encouraging engagement, consultation and bottom-up methods. It is based on the understanding that people do not perceive their needs in sectoral terms, but from a holistic perspective. It promotes strong inter-cluster coordination and collaboration and brings together the Protection, GBV, CCCM, WASH, FSL and Solutions Clusters and Working Groups. y. 7. IOM has created a slate of settlement-centric programming that incorporates disaster management committees (DMCs), building DMC members' capacity, constructing resilient flood shelters and flood mitigation infrastructures such as dikes, access pathways and evacuation centres. IOM plans to expand this program, which includes developing contingency plans, emergency preparedness, awareness-raising and linking national early warning systems (EWS) with community-level EWS. 8. IOM also provides HLP support to returnees, local leaders, families experiencing lost documentation or secondary occupation, and anyone seeking information or counsel about their HLP rights, all while maintaining strict adherence to the HLP Due Diligence Guidelines. 9. IOM has served as the Cluster lead agency for S-NFI since 2011, State Focal Point for WBeG and has co-led and hosted the National Cash Working Group for a year. IOM is ready to support Cluster through SFP Jonglei, based in Bor. IOM hosts the four SNFI cluster staff members and engages directly in the utilization and standardization of cluster tools, mapping, information gathering and data sharing. We also co-host the CWG and co-host the national HLP Technical Working Group. IOM's Core Pipeline will continue to provide in-kind supplies to partners for humanitarian responses through pre-positioned stocks in strategic locations.

3 - Project indicators

IOM has established monitoring systems and processes to ensure delivery and monitoring of expected results. These include the M&E tools and the online Integrated Project Management System. The M&E team is responsible for the overall monitoring of SNFI program activities. IOM's Programme Support Unit provides technical backstopping and conducts regular review of the project progress. IOM has developed different data collection tools and has a roster of trained enumerators to collect data in deep field locations. IOM uses a standardized checklist for rapid monitoring of distributions and cash-based interventions. The M&E team conducts exit interviews with beneficiaries, inquiring about the entire distribution process, including selection, communication, accessibility and quality of the intervention. IOM will also conduct post-distribution monitoring in 50% of randomly selected distributions. IOM uses standard Cluster tools to gather and record this data. Household samples will be identified based on Cluster guidance, with a 95% confidence level and 5% margin of error. PDMs will be conducted 2-4 weeks after the intervention, following standard Cluster guidelines. For CBIs, IOM (in collaboration with CBM Global) will pilot new data collection tools for access to cash. IOM will select intervention for PDM based on the following criteria:1. Rapid monitoring results indicating poor distribution process or beneficiaries not satisfied with the process2. Number and status of unresolved complaints received through CFM3. Any other complaints received through partners or other stakeholders. 5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

The use of cash or voucher assistance will be informed based on the findings of the market and needs assessment. Cash-Based Interventions are dependent upon the functioning of local markets, availability of financial infrastructure for secure/accountable/responsible cash delivery and the preference and financial literacy of target populations. IOM programs do regular response and feasibility analysis for different humanitarian assistance choices. Through Joint Market Monitoring Initiatives, IOM monitors market status, functioning, items availability and does a comparative analysis of in-kind vs CBIs. IOM intervention considers market behaviour and ensures that there is no adverse effect of CBIs on the overall market system

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

IOM selection for the most appropriate modality is based on solid reliable analysis, understanding and assessment of needs, and context and operating environment. IOM considers cash or voucher as a complement to in-kind assistance, capacity-building and technical support. The most appropriate modality for emergency support is determined through a comprehensive needs and market assessment. In areas where markets are functional and accessible, cash-based interventions (CBIs) are preferred, taking the form of multi-purpose cash assistance and conditional grants (such as cash-for-work and restricted vouchers). Cash assistance will allow IOM to enable accessibility and availability of goods and services in local markets, bringing sustainable solutions to the communities and address different type of needs of different type of communities IOM believes that cash-based interventions (CBI) will empower the affected population to decide how to meet their individual needs using available local resources and contribute in improved resilience.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

IOM overall objective is to support crisis-affected persons in accessing life-saving assistance and support by integrating the principles of do no harm, promoting safety, participation, non-discrimination, and empowerment across all programmes. In line with the GBViC framework, IOM will implement the following activities: 1. Ensure all IOM staff and implementing partners are trained on IOM's Code of Conduct, PSEA and AAP. PSEA training for all staff, enumerators and casual labour is mandatory. IOM will dedicate time and resources to ensure all staff are trained on PSEA principles, protocols and reporting mechanism 2. Uphold PSEA protocols and reporting mechanisms throughout the humanitarian system. 3. In coordination with IOM GBV, translate PSEA educational material in IOM offices (into English and Arabic). 4. Educate beneficiaries (women, girls, boys and men) about their right, how and where to report SEA incidents and confidentiality of information.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

All SNFI activities hinge on thorough comprehensive data collection and assessments in advance of intervention. Utilizing findings from this data collection, IOM prioritizes equitable service provision to the most vulnerable populations, ensuring the dignity, safety and well-being of these individuals is prioritized. Community members and IOM staff are trained and available to meet the specific needs of vulnerable beneficiaries throughout the response

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

IOM will ensure that beneficiaries are allowed to participate in the processes and activities that affect them actively. Response teams will conduct needs analyses through a combination of qualitative and quantitative data collection tools. SNFI teams will continue to use Cluster recommended AAP tools to mainstream AAP throughout the process, including a) checklist for needs analysis and distribution; b) rapid monitoring tools; and c) debrief tools after each intervention. The use of AAP tools will help IOM teams form Community Committees involving women, men, boys, girls and elders representatives of different groups for assessment and distribution, ensure appropriate targeting, and identify needs and context-specific risks and enable community feedback. Throughout implementation, feedback from beneficiaries will be used for comparative analysis and to establish baselines. At all stages of project management, the beneficiaries will have the opportunity to provide feedback and raise complaints through CFMs that will be established at each distribution site. IOM will keep a record of each complaint and update the status of the issue. PDMs tool has an important section to monitor the CFM desk's performance, which will inform on the performance and recommend measures in case of any changes. IOM will also continue to explore the use of technology for CFM in locations where phones and the internet are functioning. In the settlement program area, IOM established village development committees and places Complaint Boxes to gather feedback on service provision. Following Do No Harm principles, IOM will prevent and mitigate any negative impact of its actions on affected populations.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The need assessment process is entirely driven by beneficiary feedback. IOM consults affected communities through household interviews and FGDs before and after the interventions. Information gathered during community engagement will inform IOM project design and implementation. In the past, IOM in consultation with the communities' design and made changes in the shelter infrastructure project. IOM Settlement and DRR program are participatory and designed based on beneficiaries feedback. IOM continues to engage communities in the design, implementation and M&E phases of the project cycle.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

IOM is committed to ensuring a safe environment for the registration and distribution process. IOM conducts safety audits and select distribution site based on accessibility, safety and availability of other services. To ensure the safety of beneficiaries, IOM selects locations that are close to the majority of the potential beneficiaries and easily accessible to all beneficiaries IOM follows the cluster methodology for beneficiaries' registration and distribution. To mitigate the risk of violence, IOM ensures clear channels of communications with the communities. IOM established distribution committees before each intervention and inform local authorities before each intervention. Before any response/intervention, IOM conducts safety audits and collaborates with protection and GBV partners to ensure that the GBV associated risks are identified and incorporated into the overall project cycle. IOM works with communities to build confidence and regain trust through equitable access to services and gender. Community participation is ensured at every stage of intervention. IOM share selection criteria with the community and ensure that vulnerable households are included in the selection process – qualifying the selection criteria. IOM purposively select persons with disabilities, women-headed households and other minorities group during needs assessment and post-distribution monitoring. IOM also establishes a CFM desk during assessment and distribution. The CFM desk is placed in a prominent location and every hour, the distribution team, through the announcement, inform beneficiaries to contact CFM for any complaint. IOM conducts rapid monitoring during the distribution and refers issues to CFM desks. IOM conducts safety audits prior to any intervention and by ensuring strict adherence to the HLP Due Diligence Guidelines through assessment and response. IOM also incorporates HLP related issues in the response planning and beneficiaries' selection criteria. IOM employed a full-time HLP expert to support on HLP related issues both and the field and policy level.

The disaster risk reduction component will improve resilience, and communities will be better prepared to cope with climatic shocks. Similarly, the IOM settlement project theory of change entirely based on a risk-informed approach and well-integrated with other sectors like WASH, Health, food security and livelihood.IOM works in close coordination with solutions working group at the State level and encourages area-based coordination for integrated multi-sectoral projects. IOM will prioritized women, children, the elderly and persons with disability during in-kind distributions and cash-based intervention. Wherever markets are functional, IOM depending on the needs of the vulnerable group will tailor the interventions as per the target group needs. Elderly people will be assisted with provision of SNFI support at their households level so that they do not have to travel to the distribution location.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The project targets areas fall in the category of IPC 3 and above. Cash-based interventions are the key modality for IOM proposed activities. The cash-based interventions include both conditional and unconditional grants e.g. multipurpose cash assistance to vulnerable households. Our monitoring activities indicate that a significant amount of multipurpose cash assistance is used to buy foods and medicines. The cash intervention will improve the household's affordability to purchase food, contributing to the improved nutritional status of children under age 5.
