Project Summary

Despite the end of large scale military operations in December 2017, humanitarian needs persist in the country. Some 6.5 million people are in need of some type of humanitarian assistance. Of these, the humanitarian community will target 1.75 million people through the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) for 2019. This includes one million people displaced in 125 camps and out-of-camp locations (concentrated in Baghdad, Dahuk, Diyala, Erbil, Kirkuk, Najaf, Ninewa, Salah al-Din, Sulaymaniyah), half a million returnees who are in locations with high and very high severity of conditions (concentrated in Anbar, Baghdad, Diyala, Erbil, Kirkuk, Ninewa and Salah al-Din governorates) and most vulnerable residents living in communities hosting a large number of IDPs.
OCHA presence is important to foster responsible transition of the response from humanitarian relief operations towards recovery, stabilization and development solutions. OCHA will support this evolution by coordinating effective and principled humanitarian action, advocating for the rights of people in need, promoting preparedness and contingency planning and facilitating sustainable solutions for displaced people. OCHA will continue to provide advocacy and strategic guidance to the Humanitarian Coordinator (HC) and the Humanitarian Country Team (HCT) and work with the Inter-Cluster Coordination Group (ICCG) to advance leadership protection priorities. These include protecting women and children with perceived affiliation, enhancing assistance to gender-based violence assistance (GBV), working to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and intensifying accountability to affected populations (AAP).
OCHA also plays a crucial role in facilitating coordination and cooperation among all relevant stakeholders who work to serve Iraq’s most vulnerable people. The 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and HRP corroborate the need to maintain a sizeable humanitarian footprint during the coming year. There are currently 13 clusters/sectors operating in Iraq, of which only three indicated their intention to scale down their operations in 2019 as a result of declining needs – Emergency Telecommunications, Logistics and Rapid Response Mechanism. According to data reported by partners to the Iraq Humanitarian Dashboard, nearly 170 organizations - 85 national non-governmental organizations, 74 international NGOs and eight UN entities have been operating in 420 locations spanning across 107 districts out of 109 districts in 2018. OCHA needs to maintain a presence in the country throughout 2019 to be able to provide humanitarian coordination and information management support to as well as to facilitate engagement with a wide range of counterparts, including national, regional and local authorities, military actors and development actors.

Needs

The humanitarian situation in Iraq is in a transition phase evolving from the response to the conflict with Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant (ISIL) to a humanitarian context with unforeseen protracted displacement and delayed implementation of recovery and resilience activities. Vulnerable people are faced with multiple humanitarian needs that are expected to intensify until families can rebuild their lives and achieve sustainable solutions. Of the six million people displaced by ISIL, approximately four million have returned home more than 1,400 areas of origin across 35 districts and seven Governorates of Iraq. However, since early 2018 returns have steadily decreased and levelled off. More than 1.9 million internally displaced persons (IDP) remain displaced - over half for more than three years. It is anticipated that a majority of those displaced cannot or will not return to their area of origin in the immediate future. The 2019 Iraq Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) estimates that 6.5 million people are currently in need of some type of assistance. Nearly 2.4 million people are vulnerable to food insecurity; 5.5 million people require health care; 4.5 million people need protection support; 2.3 million people require water and sanitation assistance; 2.6 million children require access to education and 2.3 million people need shelter and non-food items. These figures include 500,000 IDPs in camps and 1.5 million people out-of-camp locations, more than 3.9 million returnees and close to 400,000 vulnerable people in communities hosting large numbers of displaced people. Reasons for continued displacement include destroyed, occupied or disputed housing, a lack of livelihoods and services, and social cohesion, security concerns (blocked return or perceived insecurity), and mental health and trauma issues. Although major efforts are underway to rebuild the country and jump-start local economies, significant barriers to return remain. With protracted displacement expected to endure in 2019, humanitarian assistance must continue targeting IDPs both in camps and in out-of-camp settings, and returnees in locations where conditions are severe. The scale of the operation and the nature of issues described above call for an efficient and flexible coordination mechanisms, information management provision and evidence-based situational analysis, advocacy support as well as effective, innovative humanitarian financing system. OCHA plays a crucial role in providing this support. Since its establishment in 2014, OCHA Iraq has expanded, and continuously adjusted its operational presence to effectively cover the coordination needs in all humanitarian hot spots. In 2019, OCHA aims to maintain an agile and strategic footprint in Iraq and provide support to partners across these four areas where needs have been identified: (1) coordination;(2) information management and needs analysis;(3) humanitarian financing; (4) advocacy for the centrality of protection in humanitarian programming. Support for effective and innovative humanitarian financing system, and associated activities in the OCHA annual work plan, will be covered through complementary funding sources. The current proposal seeks to ensure funding to address the remaining three needs related to coordination, needs analysis and advocacy.

Activities

COORDINATION • Support and strengthen the inter-cluster coordination mechanism at national and sub-national level in support of 2019 HRP implementation and HCT decision making.• Ensure the Information Management Working Group (IMWG) and the Assessment Working Group (AWG) work plans are complementary and provide efficient support to the ICCG in implementation of the 2019 HRP and 2020 HNO (including 2019 HNO assessment gaps). • Strengthen NGO collaboration on humanitarian access gaps through the peer-to-peer forum of the AWG.• Periodic review of the humanitarian coordination structure to ensure “fitness for purpose.”• Clarify boundaries and strengthen coherence and linkages between humanitarian, recovery, stabilization and development programing in support of durable solutions for six million IDPs.• Implement the Emergency Response Preparedness (ERP) approach at national and sub-national level to strengthen humanitarian system preparedness to respond to natural disaster, disease outbreak and other contingencies.• Incorporate the feedback of affected IDP and returnee communities, especially vulnerable groups, on the needs and priorities included in the HNO and HRP. • Incorporate the feedback of affected IDP and returnee communities received through Multi-Cluster Needs Assessment (MCNAs), IDP call centre and targeted Accountability to affected populations (AAP) initiatives on the reach, quality and timeliness of response and update on action points arising.• Report on the achievements and access, operational and financial gaps encountered with HRP implementation through monthly snapshots and six-monthly monitoring reports. INFORMATION MANAGEMENT AND NEEDS ANALYSIS • Strengthen the humanitarian programme cycle to produce higher quality HNO, HRP, Periodic Monitoring Report (PMR) and monthly response products to inform HCT decision making on priority needs, response and implementation gaps.• Produce corporate communications and information products (humanitarian bulletins, flash updates, situation reports, websites, snapshots, dashboards, maps) in accordance with corporate policies and guidelines, including regularity, timeliness, adherence to templates, and of high quality.• Improve coordination of needs assessments to develop high quality HNO endorsed by HCT based on shared data and analysis, ensuring the response is informed first and foremost by the needs of people and risks.• Produce humanitarian dashboards and PMR, presenting the humanitarian needs, response and gaps against planned target. ADVOCACY/CENTRALITY OF PROTECTION • Strengthen the integration of the Centrality of Protection and protection mainstreaming elements – women and children with perceived affiliations, GBV, PSEA, AAP - across the humanitarian response.• Support HC advocacy efforts on Centrality of Protection and protection mainstreaming elements with Government of Iraq authorities at all levels.• Strengthen the promotion of durable solutions for IDPs and related protection protocols across the humanitarian response.• Support HC advocacy efforts on durable solutions and implementation of related protection protocols with Government of Iraq authorities at all levels.• Strengthen support to existing GBV, PSEA and AAP initiatives including better resolution of cases and problems identified.• Strengthen internal communication through enhanced engagement with all humanitarian system actors and constituencies on priority needs, response strategy and gaps.• Strengthen engagement with the Government of Iraq and other key interlocutors on priority needs, response strategy and gaps. Support for effective and innovative humanitarian financing system, and associated activities in the OCHA annual work plan, will be covered through complementary funding sources and are therefore not listed here.

Protection Risk Analysis

OCHA does not engage directly with affected population. However, addressing protection concerns within Iraq remains an overarching priority for OCHA and the humanitarian leadership given existing social, ethno-sectarian and political tensions, particularly in disputed or heterogeneous areas. OCHA will continue to advocate for putting protection at the centre of the response through exiting coordination mechanisms (e.g. HCT, ICCG, Clusters). OCHA will continue to provide advocacy and strategic guidance to the HC and the HCT and work with the ICCG to advance leadership priorities. These include protecting women and children with perceived affiliation, enhancing assistance to survivors of gender-based violence assistance (GBV), working to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and intensifying accountability to affected populations (AAP). Through the provision of information management services and through facilitation of coordinated needs assessment, OCHA aims to increase the quality of humanitarian analysis, including of analysis related to protection risks.

How is your project accountable to affected population

OCHA as an organization providing services in support of the humanitarian community, is not providing direct assistance to people in need. While OCHA is accountable to put people at the centre of the response, it does this through engagement and advocacy with a wide range of clients, including the Clusters and the NGO community, as well as local and national authorities, military actors and donors. OCHA is also providing overall support to all AAP mechanisms, including the IDP Call Centre for and the PSEA network. It does this through the CCS Cluster chaired by OCHA and NCCI , and through regular organization and/or facilitation of HCT, ICCG, cluster / sectoral, NGO and other relevant multilateral meetings where AAP and accountability mechanisms are standing agenda items. OCHA will also advocate for the inclusion of AAP-related questions in relevant joint assessment forms.
