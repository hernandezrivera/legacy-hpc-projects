Project Summary

"AF will provide support and skill development training to home gardening and demo farmers, Livestock owners, and fishermen of host communities/local communities of all unions of Ukhia upazila to enhance the livelihoods and resilience of vulnerable host communities in cooperation with the Government of Bangladesh, FSS, and other counterparts. Moreover, IGA practice will ensure diversified and climate-resilient trades. At the same time, the implementation of small mitigation scheme work (based on the Risk Reduction Action Plan-RRAP of respective union, repairment of culvert, guide wall, drainage system, refurbishment of connecting road either Brick Flat Soling- BFS or Hering Bone Bond- HBB) which will generate income directly for the skill and unskill labor will be instantaneously involved to accomplish these works which will create the opportunity for CfW. Indirectly the community will become more resilient, and access to amenities will increase. In addition, the awareness-raising activities on disaster preparedness and environment conservation will directly capacitate the participants for disaster preparedness, resulting minimize the climate disaster loss and maximize the conservation of the environment. AF proposed activities are based on the communities’ feedback received during the running project implementation from the targeted communities and local authorities and will be accountable to them for the decisions made during the project implementation. The intervention was designed to ensure a long-term positive impact on the food security of the targeted beneficiaries and to increase market accessibility of the neighboring households and communities not directly targeted through the project. This will be ensured through business training of the beneficiaries and the provision of organizational support to enhance the linkages between the beneficiaries and neighboring markets. This community-based approach and market linkage assistance will ensure the acceptance and sustainability of the AF project. Thus, these activities will enhance the food security resiliency of host/local communities through climate-sensitive food production in a stressed environment.
In addition, AF has planned to work at host communities/local communities of all unions of Ukhia upazila through natural resource management activities; AF will ensure more plantation/reforestation (In accordance with the guideline of EETWG, local leaders, UNO), Land stabilization for forestation, and landslide prevention (Based on the suggestion of EETWG, SRDI, UNO, Chairman of Upazila and union to prevent landslide Arannayk will implement Brashwood Check Damn/Gabian Check Damn along with Contour Grassed Waterways). In addition, in the host community, specifically surrounding the Sheikh Jamal Inani National Park (SJINP), after collaboration with IUCN, NACOM, and EETWG AF will form/reform Elephant Response Team (ERT) to mitigate the human-elephant conflict.
Beneficiary selection will be based on vulnerability criteria (i.e. Female headed HH, presence of a disabled person in the HH, elderly-headed HH, HH affected by disaster recurrence, level of poverty, etc.) for IGA, livestock, and agricultural activities. Females will be favored for the home garden, livestock, and IGA activities as they face more barriers to accessing training and livelihood opportunities. Youth groups will be prioritized for the veterinary agent, local service provider, awareness sessions, and ERT formation to ensure sustainability.
AF has a well-established Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) and whistleblowing policy in Ukhia to receive beneficiary feedback regarding the assistance provided. The MEAL team will ensure that the appropriate mechanisms are in place through which the affected populations can measure the adequacy of the intervention and address their concerns. In case of protection needs, the case will be referred to relevant protection actors working in the area."

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"Refugee Influx Emergency Vulnerability Assessment (REVA-5) in June 2022 revealed that due to economic constriction, less scope of work, and high dependency on daily wage labor resulting increasing trend of vulnerability, inadequate food consumption, economic vulnerability, use of coping strategies for host communities. Moreover, climate Vulnerability, insufficient livelihood support, absence of a multi-dimensional income source, lack of source to receive training on skill development, and scarcity of water for both drinking and irrigation are common problems of the Ukhiya host community people. This makes this population highly vulnerable to climate change and natural disaster impacts. Human-elephant conflict (HEC) is the major crisis for the survival of Asian elephants in Bangladesh. Cox’s Bazar Forest division is one of the country's last remaining productive elephant range areas, where at least 153 elephants have been killed in the last two decades by electrocution (70 elephants) and indiscriminate shooting (The business standard 2021). The existing HEC in Cox’s Bazar South Forest division has increased manifold with the arrival of forcefully displaced Myanmar nationals in the Cox’s Bazar region in 2017. About 38 elephants are entrapped in Cox’s Bazar-Teknaf area as the Ghumdhum corridor (for transboundary movement between Bangladesh and Myanmar) is blocked due to the Rohingya refugee makeshift settlements (IUCN & UNHCR 2018). Community-based response and rescue teams are required and have already proven successful in Bangladesh. However, the strengthening of Elephant Response Teams needs to replicate in the wider landscape of refugee-prone areas. The formation & expansion of Elephant Response Teams (ERTs) is identified as one of the actions under the six problem pillars of the BECAP (2017-2028). It is suggested to be done immediately as its implementation scope is high. The project will ultimately assist in implementing the Bangladesh Elephant Conservation Action Plan (BECAP) 2018-2027 and international agreements such as the Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora (CITES), the Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD), Monitoring Illegal Killing of Elephants (MIKE), and the Convention on the Conservation of Migratory Species of Wild Animals (CMS). "

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The proposed project by Arannayk Foundation will ensure an integrated approach during the village selection process; suggestions from local leaders, Government officials, and counterparts stalk taking will be considered to avoid the same work in the same area. Moreover, AF will consider improving working relations with Government agencies, food security sector, IUCN, NACOM, and EETWG and building up an operative bridging among Government officials, service providers, and beneficiaries to ensure the best use of resources in a coordinated way for the betterment of beneficiaries in a sustainable manner.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

AF is fully compliant with safeguarding communities and stakeholders, having relevant policies in place to ensure safety and dignity, and apply to do no-harm principles to the communities concerned. AF ensures meaningful access of all concerned beneficiaries and stakeholders and project staff/volunteers to the opportunities/facilities of the project and the organization itself, giving due consideration to AGD-related tailored risks. Moreover, the location, distance, and access routes to the distribution points will be made accessible to all members of the community, including the most vulnerable groups. AF will establish mechanisms to prioritize (e.g., priority lanes for the big que) those who cannot stand in line for long periods, such as older persons, persons with disability, unaccompanied children, and pregnant and lactating women, including alternate collector mechanisms.

3.2 - Gender

Likewise, the other projects of the Arannayk Foundation Gender and youth issues will be considered for the food security sector for host communities. The project would ensure the participation of girls and women in the home garden, livestock, and IGA activities as they face more barriers to accessing training and resilient food security opportunities. Youth groups will be prioritized for the veterinary agent, local service provider, awareness sessions, and ERT formation to ensure sustainability and the best effective results. In addition to that, the participation of the members of families and persons with disabilities and LGBTQI would be prioritized. The project would have a strong team of GESI (Gender Equality and Social Inclusion) and a gender mainstreaming team to ensure the participation of women and persons with disabilities. AF will conduct periodical community-based inclusive approaches (e.g., PRA) and gender analysis to understand and identify the potential gaps.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

AF has safeguarded its people (participants, employees) in the first place by making their safety a primary consideration already at the level of the design of programs and enterprises. The prevention of sexual harassment, child and adolescent abuse, intimidation, violence, bullying, humiliation and discrimination, neglect, and exploitation is, as far as possible, built into the work practices. Where the design and its implementation cannot reduce risks sufficiently, AF has established secondary safeguarding mechanisms, like selecting and training personnel, supervising and monitoring, establishing multiple reporting channels, investigation, and punishment. A dedicated PSEA committee is formed in AF, having a focal person to deal with all cases, including child safeguarding issues. AF has the mechanism to receive feedback from the children as part of accountability to them, maintaining separate complaint boxes and providing child-friendly information and avenues for reporting.

3.4 - Diversity

AF supports its project participants by following humanitarian standards and principles. Therefore, prioritizing staff diversity and having a culturally relaxed and inclusive work environment is an integral part of its program. The project will also include Gender Diverse Population (GDP) considering their needs and interests, and training modules will be developed and facilitated accordingly. To ensure diversity, AF will be disaggregated all collected data by age, sex, and other diversity considerations based on the context; it will follow the participatory methodologies at each stage of the operations management cycle to incorporate the capacities and priorities of women, men, girls, and boys of diverse backgrounds; AF will approach to communicating with them, that are appropriate and accessible to all groups in any given community, and will establish and promote feedback and response systems, including for confidential complaints.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

AF always ensures its accountability to the affected communities and keeps them at the center of AF’s work, decisions, and actions. AF will ensure two-way dialogue with the community, even during crisis responses. AF determines demands for information from the community and their presentation styles/formats in consultation with the clientele communities and accordingly establishes the information channels. Further, the AF implementation teams work closely with the PQ teams, which ensures appropriate tracking and monitoring of activities being delivered to the beneficiaries. For this purpose, AF uses various tools like complaint books/boxes/channels, feedback, and response mechanisms such as establishing a help desk, post-distribution monitoring (PDM), hotline, feedback hub, participatory review sessions, etc. AF recognizes that all relevant groups have a voice, including men, women, children, older persons, minority cultural groups, and people with disabilities.
