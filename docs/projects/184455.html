Project Summary


In line with the 2022 education cluster response strategy, the proposed project will ensure that 5000 crisis-affected children ( 3500 displaced and 1500 non-displaced children of which 3000 are girls and 2000 are boys) have access to safe, protective, inclusive learning environments and are retained in their schools. Among these are 500 children living with various forms of disabilities such as learning disabilities and physical disability who require teachers with skills of handling them and facilities meeting their unique needs. The project has two results that seek to improve access and retention of crisis-affected children in 10 schools in Gedo and Banadir Region, and result two will ensure crisis-affected children in Gedo and Banadir Region access quality education in a safe and protected learning environment. This project also integrates improved education infrastructure, provision of food aid, provision of WASH services and protection services that encourage retention in school for the crisis-affected children. NAPAD targets 10 schools each with various needs. Therefore infrastructure development will target some schools and not others. 2 schools located in Banadir IDP camps will be supported with the rehabilitation of one classroom each and the construction of a latrine in each of the schools. The 2 latrines will be fitted with handwashing facilities and all these facilities are designed to be age, disability and gender-sensitive. 4 schools in Gedo will each benefit from the construction of a barkaad for water storage. To ensure general and proper hygiene is maintained in 10 targeted schools 5000 will participate in hygiene promotion awareness-raising events. 5000 students in 10 schools in the target regions will receive monthly food vouchers worth $7 for 6 months. Foods purchased with these vouchers will contribute to household food and other basic need and encourage parents to send their children to school during the crisis period. It is also assumed that during this time parents and guardians will have an opportunity to restore their assets and improve the standards of living of their households. 5000 students will benefit from the provision of learning and teaching materials. To improve attendance and retention rates of adolescent girls, 1008 adolescent girls will receive sanitary pads on a monthly basis for 12 months and the constructed latrines are designed to be lockable from inside and are also gender-sensitive.
To ensure that the 84 teachers and head-teachers are engaged and retained in the delivery of quality services, they will be facilitated with monthly incentives of $90-$100for 12 months. To ensure the proper management of these assets for longevity, 10 CECs (84 persons -58M and 26 W) will be trained on school safety, with topics including hygiene promotion, maintenance and management of learning spaces. To improve the quality of education during the crisis, the project and line with the Integrated Education-Child Protection Response Framework for Somalia the project will on capacity building 84 (58 M and 26W) teachers on pedagogical skills psychosocial support and psychosocial first aid as well as teacher training in inclusive, protective and socio-emotional teaching and learning, and implementation of MoE teachers’ codes of conduct. The project will facilitate the MoE to conduct awareness sessions for 10 head teachers and 74 teachers (58 M and 26F) on the teachers' code of conduct (TCOC) and ensure they sign and adhere to it. The Ministry will also play a key role in periodic and planned monitoring and supervision of the performance of teachers for quality assurance. These visits will be vital to ensure that children with disabilities have equal access to education, for instance, ensuring those who have learning disabilities such as dyslexia, dyscalculia and ADHD to mention but a few are identified and supported accordingly. This project will be implemented in Gedo and Banadir.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The project will support children with a voucher that will provide thebeneficiaries with the power of choice on the purchase of the preferred food andat what quantities at a time. The unconditional support is also safe and doesnot present any risks to the beneficiaries, unlike in-kind support. The redeemingof these vouchers will also help in strengthening local markets as thebeneficiaries will purchase the preferred foods from local suppliers. This will notonly avoid disruption of the food markets that may be occasioned by in-kindfood supplies from outside but will also inject cash in the local markets. Thisapproach was selected as it benefits the whole family and the vulnerablefamilies will be encouraged and obliged to ensure their child attends schoolconsistently. The voucher is also physically manageable and non-disruptive andthe child will be able to deliver it to the household head as opposed to carryingin-kind support that is heavy and affects their security.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

NAPAD staff working and residing in the targeted project districts continuously provide information on the local markets during the design of the project. The staff monitored the trends in the availability of different essential foods in the local markets and the prices. Additionally, they gatheredinformation on the quality of different foodstuffs in the market. Observations in the target district markets are that the markets have experienced minimal fluctuations despite the movement restrictions brought by COVID 19 outbreak. Additionally, NAPAD continuously read the WFP Joint Market and Supply Chain Update. The generally stable markets informed the choice of support in theproject.
