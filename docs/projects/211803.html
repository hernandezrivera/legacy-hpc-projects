Project Summary

Over 14.9 million people in Syria are estimated to be in need of life-saving primary and secondary care health assistance in 2024 (Humanitarian Needs Overview 2024). The erosion of basic service capacity has continued, with water and sanitation systems and public health services under immense strain (HNO 2024). Access to and functionality of basic health services remain a huge challenge; almost 40 percent of primary and secondary care health facilities - serving over 4.8 million people in need of life-saving health services - are either partially functioning or not function. According to the Health Cluster EWARN, as of 30 December 2023, 110,060 suspected and 581 confirmed cholera cases were reported, with 15 cholera associated deaths. Moreover, in 2023 in NWS, 14,390 Acute Jaundice Syndrome cases, 1,464 suspected meningitis cases and 7,088 suspected measles cases were registered and influenza like syndrome is the leading cause of morbidity across NWS HFs. These trends can be attributed to the decline in living standards, poor water and sanitation conditions and lack of preventive health services.
In response to the identified needs, RI will provide healthcare services in Idleb Governorate, and Aleppo governorates, supporting 18 HFs ( 4 general hospital, 2 maternity & pediatrics hospital, 2 BEmONC centers, 4 PHCs, and 6 mobile clinics). Healthcare services include primary and secondary health care, mental health (MH) care, preventive and curative nutrition services, and physical rehabilitation services Within the supported HFs, healthcare services are integrated with Gender Based Violance (GBV) and psychosocial support (PSS) services. Furthermore, RI will provide WASH in health services, including sanitation and hygiene activities, water safety in HFs, health care waste management. RI will also assist the supported HFs with repairs and rehabilitation to maintain the health infrastructure (installations/repairs of latrines, handwashing stations, other sanitation systems are urgently required to meet the WHO's minimum standards for WASH in HFs).RI will also support a central medical waste management zone
Protection risks identified: Safeguarding incidents (SEA and GBV) could increase due to a rise in negative coping mechanisms as living conditions deteriorate. As a mitigation measure, RI will continue raising awareness on PSEA and other types of exploitation and abuse by mainstreaming protection across health program activities, as well as ensure that service-users/staff are aware of their rights, what constitutes exploitation and abuse, and how to report safeguarding incidents and violation of Code of Conduct provisions
In terms of safeguarding, RI has zero tolerance toward safeguarding incidents with its robust system, enabling the safe submission and handling of sensitive information, and assuring that service-users/staff are aware of how to report a violation of safeguarding or Code of Conduct (CoC). To prevent and mitigate SEA risks, RI will: provide HR orientation/refresh sessions for staff on CoC and Safeguarding mechanisms; deploying a PSEA focal point; develop and disseminate informational materials for program participants, outlining their rights, available support services, and avenues for reporting concerns; conduct surveys to assess the effectiveness of safeguarding measures.
Risks, assumptions and mitigation measures: Assumptions: Unimpeded and safe access to the targeted health facility for all populations, including women, girls and person with disabilities; Border crossing remain open for humanitarian access and delivery of goods; Targeted communities positively accept proposed health intervention R1: Increased military activities leading to a surge in trauma cases, disruption of healthcare services, and displacement of people.
