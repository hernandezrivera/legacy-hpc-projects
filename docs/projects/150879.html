Project Summary

WRRS will seek to offer response to the needs that has affected both the host community and the IDPs who have sought to take refuge in these proposed areas. This will be attained through improvement of HH access to food by provision of unconditional and conditional cash transfers to the beneficiaries of the targeted district of Sool and lower Juba. Besides this those who are pastorals will receive restock of animals offered with the vaccination, and poultry support with major focus of the female headed HH which will be accompanied by capacity building on best farming practices; veterinary care and pest control. The need to offer sustainable access to livelihood assets in hard to reach areas of Lower Juba and Sool will remain crucial and need urgent humanitarian response especially after the flood that has caused a lot of damage to the previously restored livelihood.

Needs

Somalia has been one of the most affected country with volatile needs with disasters varying from season to seasonA recent forecast issued by the Greater Horn of Africa Climate Outlook Forum (GHACOF50), indicates a greater likelihood of normal to above normal 2018 Deyr (October-December) rains across Somalia. As a result, pasture and water availability, crop cultivation, livestock reproduction, access to agricultural employment, water and food prices are expected to continue to improve through late 2018. However, the expected average to above average rains may also cause flooding in some low-lying and riverine areas.In the absence of humanitarian assistance, food security outcomes are expected to deteriorate to Emergency (IPC Phase 4) in Guban Pastoral livelihood zone and to Crisis (JPC Phase 3) in Northern Inland Pastoral of Sool and Sanaag regions. In summary, over 1.5 million people will face Crisis or worse (IPC Phases 3 or higher)1 between now and December 2018 which represents a significant improvement in the overall food security situation in Somalia compared to the 2.7 million people projected to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse between February and June 2018. Through December 2018, an additional 3.1 million people are classified as Stressed (IPC Phase 2), which brings the total number of people facing acute food insecurity across Somalia to 4.6 million. Results from 30 separate nutrition surveys conducted by FSNAU and partners between in June and July 2018 indicate that the overall nutrition situation in Somalia has continued to improve due to a combination of improved food security conditions, reduced outbreak of diseases and sustained humanitarian interventions. However, high level of acute malnutrition tends to persist in several population groups due to underlying/structural causes. The overall, level of acute malnutrition at national level has improved to Serious (5-9.9% Global Acute Malnutrition-GAM) in the current 2018 Gu 2018 (median GAM of 14.

Activities

Provision of emergency unconditional cash transfer and safety nets to 800 (300 IDPs and 500 HC) vulnerable IDP and host community households considering 60% of the beneficiaries registered as vulnerable women household in Bakol and Hiran region south central Somalia. Each household will received 81 USD per months for 3 months period. •Provide cash for work activities to 300 men and women household heads and enable create short term job opportunities and create income to the vulnerable drought displaced and AWD affected IDPs and host communities to enable improve the community livelihood structure hence provide alternative livelihood •Sensitize vulnerable household benefiting from unconditional cash transfer on nutrition, health and hygiene promotion for food preparation to enable reduce malnutrition and disease hence improve community living standard. •Conduct training to capacity building farmers (48 men and 62 women household headed) on proper preparing, harvesting as well as management of farm inputs storage hence enhancing their knowledge and thus inculcate skills for their livelihood in futures

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Accountability to affected population is a key aspect in the trans-formative agenda. The project intends to involve the community in all stages of the program cycle, so that they understand the aim and the outcome of the entire project and embrace its as it rolls, they will also understand the guiding principle behind the decisions made.The project intends to use already existing community governance structures and use them as community committees. These will be involved in recruitment of staff, community mobilization and monitoring of the project.Their presence will represent the interest and role of the community in successful completion of the project. The project will also have a feedback mechanism , from which complaints and comments from the community will be collected, and fed back to the same. This will be done in a highly confidential manner with very few staff in the management allowed to interact with this process. WRRS will address the complains as they come and embrace the comments for the sustainability and acceptance of the services within the community since these are live saving interventions that will help the households have a sustainable livelihood income.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Gender disparities are on the rise in the operational communities, where women usually take a back seat in issues of community development. WRRS through the project, through community mobilization and awareness, will ensure women are empowered and represented to influence decisions and outcomes. A Do No Harm approach will be adopted in all our interventions. Contextual analysis is important in the implementation of this project considering the various potential sources of conflicts in the target locations both at leadership and community level. Regular assessments and community engagements will be done to learn if the project activities, approaches and methodologies are sensitive to the local context. Vulnerable community members generally refer to those disadvantaged with compromised access to resources to meet their daily basic needs. The project staff will ensure that during community mobilization and sensitization, all community members are given equal opportunity to participate.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

WRRS has been working in areas where security and conflict has been a major factor affecting the beneficiaries. Accountability for violations is limited, with traditional or formal mediation and justice mechanisms having been disrupted, or having become disrespected or inaccessible. Building community structures including local temporary learning space that will ensure tension is minimized. Staff will be trained on human rights based approach where all people should be treated equally, especially those who ran from conflict and violence. Communities fleeing violence will be immediately supported through access to immediate food security and livelihood services without any discrimination. Where IDPs are involved, sites that support them will be built in consultation with them and the host communities. Marginalized groups will be prioritized so that they don’t feel excluded, which can otherwise aggravate their situation if they have run from violence.
