Project Summary

This multi-year project is going to contribute to the education cluster and HRP objectives through improving the safe access of 25,732 school-aged children (13044 boys & 12688 girls) host and IDPs including 373(0.01) % CwD in 51 schools located in high severity locations in Idleb to quality formal and non-formal education services by providing a package of education services that target the gaps and needs of the most vulnerable groups of hosts and IDPs children in 3 sub-districts in the camps and off camps.
This multi-year project will help 22721 (11238 boys & 11483 girls) school aged 6-17 years old to access formal education using the SIG curriculum and 3011 children(1806 boys & 1205 girls) of law achievers at the risk of drop out of schools and out of schools children to access non-formal education(1020 children in catch-up & 1991 in remedial) including 373 children with disabilities (205 boys & 168 girls) with preventive measures of Covid-19 during 2022 and 2023 through establishing 50 new classrooms in tents , conducting light repair to 123 classrooms and wash facilities taking into consideration the needs of CwD and gender sensitive issues , providing 28 schools with school furniture to increase the absorption capacity , conducting 51 back to Learning campaigns in 51 different locations targeting 5100 beneficiaries(2550 children and 2550 caregivers and parents)to reach out to the children dropped out of schools and re-enroll them and increase the awareness of parents and caregivers towards the importance of education and CP basic rights , providing 96,822 liters of heating fuel and 295 heaters to 51 schools , procuring and distributing 25,732 school bags and education kits to 25,732 children in 51 schools ,providing 51 schools with operational cost(drinkable water - cleaning tools & materials - buffet materials- stationery) to work properly , providing transportation for 373 CwD in 51 schools, providing 51 schools with safety equipment(first aid kits and fire extinguishers), paying the incentives to 957 teachers(573 males & 384 females) and supporting staff working in the 51 schools in formal and non-formal education , providing light professional development package including training on (Child Protection Principles and Child Safeguarding –Integration of Children with disabilities in Education Response - Modern Teaching Methodologies– Education in Emergencies ) , providing 51 schools with recreational materials , printing 3011 copies of non-formal textbooks , providing teaching materials kits to 51 schools , providing 25,732 children in formal and non-formal with school-based psychological, support conducting safety training to the children and teachers ,provide cleaning and disinfection materials kits to 51 schools ,provide 26,689 PPE kits to the teachers and children in 51 schools ,provide awareness raising sessions on the preventive measures of Covid-19 to 25,732 children , 957 teachers and 2550 parents and caregivers
The affected population will be involved in all the stages of the project planning , designing , implementation , Monitoring , Evaluation where there will be FGDs , PDMs after each distribution, satisfaction surveys on the provided services and distributed items and field monitoring visits.
All the activities of the project will be implemented in 2022 and 2023 except for the rehabilitation activities will be in 2022.
In order to make sure that suggestions and complains of the beneficiaries are heard during and after COVID-19, there will be posters clarifying the objectives of the project with Facebook, what’s up, email, accounts will be posted outside and inside the schools (After re-opening the schools) and on the WhatsApp groups to the caregivers and children in the distance learning in COVID-19 as plan(B)

4 - Needs

Basing on the HNO 2022 final draft, there are over 7 million children (3-17 years) in need of education support, Idleb has a critical confluence of negative factors: low attendance rates, the highest classroom to school aged population ratio, the only governorate with triple and quadruple shifts, nearly half the schools are non-operational and the largest proportion of children traveling +45 minutes to school and the highest rates of attacks on education. Basing on “Schools in Syria report” that published from ACU(edition 06) 2021, 2568 classrooms needed desks and chairs, and minor repairs of windows and doors, 63% of children aged 6-18 are out of school (drop out) and 12% of formal teachers don’t receive salaries. Basing on the need assessment that SCA conducted in 3 sub-districts in Idleb in December 2021, the key findings were: 1-There are 206 functioning schools, 51 schools out of them need support.2- There are 22721 children (11238 boys & 11483 girls) enrolled in formal education in 51 schools 3- There are 957 teachers working voluntarily without incentives in the 51 schools 4- There is a need to establish 50 new classrooms in tents in 3 sub-districts 5- 123 classrooms need light rehabilitation in the 51 schools 6- The wash facilities in 28 schools need light rehabilitation 7- 51 schools need support with heating fuel and heaters during winter 8- All the children in the 51 schools need support with school bags and stationery kits

5 - Implementation of activities

This multi-year project is going to help 25,732 children (13044 boys & 12688 girls) school aged children host and IDPs to have access to quality formal education opportunities using the SIG curriculum and 3011 children(1806 boys & 1205 girls) who are late in their education and children dropout of schools to access non-formal education1020 children in catch-up & 1991 in remedial) including 373 children with disabilities with preventive measures of Covid-19 during 2022-2023 in 51 schools through establishing 50 new classrooms in tents , conducting light repair to 123 classrooms and wash facilities taking into consideration the needs of CwD and gender sensitive issues , providing 28 schools with school furniture to increase the absorption capacity , conducting 51 back to Learning campaigns in 51 different locations targeting 5100 beneficiaries(2550 children and 2550 caregivers and parents)to reach out to the children dropped out of schools and re-enroll them and increase the awareness of parents and caregivers towards the importance of education and CP basic rights and child safe guarding , providing 96,822 liters of heating fuel and 295 heaters to 51 schools during the winter , procuring and distributing 25,732 school bags and education kits to 25,732 children in 51 schools ,providing 51 schools with operational cost(drinkable water - cleaning tools & materials - buffet materials- stationery) to work properly and providing transportation to 373 CwD in 51 schools.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: Teachers and other supporting staff participate in physical punishment or bullying of children Mitigating Measures: 1- All the teachers and supporting staff in the schools will sign on a code of conduct on physical punishment and bullying 2- The parents and the children, teachers and other supporting staff in schools will be encouraged to report any issues to physical punishment or bullying Protection Risk: Children are over-exposed to the risk of, or experience, harm by teachers, school personnel or organization staff Mitigating Measures: 1- There will be Child Safeguarding policy signed by all the project staff , teachers and supporting staff in the schools and monitored on annual bases 2- SCA will provide sufficient safe and clean bathroom facilities that segregated basing on sex and age Protection Risk: The schools don’t provide sufficient protection from the elements (rain, snow, cold, etc.) Mitigating Measures: 1- SCA will do rehabilitation to the wash facilities and classrooms including Painting, blacksmithing, Electricity, building works, building works for CWD)


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

PSEA will be mainstreamed in all the phases of the project When hiring the staff of the project and teachers in the 51 schools, The PSEA officer in Syria will provide an orientation to them about the PSEA policy and code of conduct used in SCA (what are the main 6 principles of PSEA, what are the channels for reporting violations …etc. During the academic year , PSEA officer in Syria will provide awareness sessions to the members of PTA in the 51 schools on the definition of sexual exploitation and abuse as per the SC’s Bulletin and Saed code of conduct , tell them they have the right to be free of PSEA , how can they complain including the stages of time-frame ,to whom they complain , what steps they can take to ensure confidentiality and what step that SCA will take to ensure safety and confidentiality. Protection Risk: Abusing or exploiting the children by teachers or other supporting staff Mitigating Measures: 1- SCA will make sure that PSEA policies are in place, practiced and monitored 2- 2- SCA will ensure that targeted communities and staff have access to safe and effective reporting systems including when they will get response


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: All the planned schools are accessible safely without any restrictions Risk : The schools are no longer accessible due to the change in the maps of control MM: 1-SCA will monitor the situation on the ground closely 2-SCA will track the movement of the teachers and children and coordinate with the local education authorities to accommodate them in the new destinations to access their education after displacement Assumptions: 100 classrooms in tents are established and 376 classrooms are rehabilitated in 79 schools as well as provided with furniture and safety equipment without the interventions of head of the camps or any concerns related to HLP. Risk: The intervention of some camps' authorities in rehabilitation works and some land owners aren’t giving the permission to establishment of new classrooms MM: SCA will discuss these activities in advance with all the relevant stakeholders and will coordinate closely with the local education authorities (Directorates of Education in the areas of operations and will keep them updated on the progress of activities in schools) Assumptions: The CwD are enrolled and attending the schools where all their needs are met and there are no barriers that prevent their access to schools in the classrooms and wash facilities. Risk: The CwD are facing some barriers that prevent their access to learning facilities in entrances of classrooms and wash facilities. MM: SCA will consider the needs of CwD when assessing the needs
