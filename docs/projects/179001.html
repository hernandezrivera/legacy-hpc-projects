Project Summary

The Syria crisis is one of the most complex humanitarian crises ever. Humanitarian assistance is provided by a large variety of actors, operating both from within Syria and from neighbouring countries. Several coordination hubs have been established, in Damascus, Gaziantep and Amman as Whole of Syria overall coordination system, that ensures common strategies and best use of resources from the different hubs. As Cluster Lead agency for WASH sector, UNICEF must ensure that sectoral coordination needs at Hubs and at Whole of Syria levels are met through appropriate support. There are also growing needs to create an enabling environment allowing for Government of Syria and more specifically Ministry of Water Resources to be more involved with existing coordination structures where possible and with data collection processes at different level. This project presents accordingly the activities and related budget allowing to fulfil these coordination tasks and the development of this enabling environment, thus, to ensure following objectives:
• Predictable sectoral leadership and coordination
• Increased predictability of geographic coverage and sectoral responsibility
• Demonstrated accountability (for sector-wide performance).
• Increased field level capacities to address gaps.
• Maximized coordination and partnership and buy in from the major humanitarian actors to ensure a more strategic and coherent response in support of national led efforts.
• Enhanced ability of the Strategic Steering Group to lead a more strategic and coherent response, and better influence how available resources are prioritized.
• Advocacy efforts to ensure that the sector needs are appropriately funded

4 - Needs

The Syria crisis is one of the most complex humanitarian crises ever. Humanitarian assistance is provided by a large variety of actors, operating both from within Syria and from neighboring countries. Three coordination hubs have been established, in Damascus, Gaziantep and Amman, as well as a Whole of Syria overall coordination system, that ensures common strategies and best use of resources from the three hubs. At Gaziantep level, there exists largest WASH cluster coordination arrangement with about 104 active humanitarian actors mostly comprising of national and international NGOs. The cluster coordination team here guide management of Humanitarian Fund of worth USD25 Million for OCHA mainly for emergency WASH programming. As Cluster Lead Agency for WASH sector, UNICEF must ensure that sectoral coordination needs at Hubs and at Whole of Syria levels are met through appropriate support, this according to Global Wash Cluster and IASC requirements. There are also growing needs to create an enabling environment allowing for Government of Syria and more specifically Ministry of Water Resources to be more involved with existing coordination structures where possible and with data collection processes at different level.

5 - Implementation of activities

Activities will be implemented through the WoS WASH coordination group (Hubs cluster coordinators, Hubs IMs and Hubs Cluster Co-lead, Syria Field offices coordinators) supported by WoS sector Coordination team. To ensure evidence-based planning, the sector will continue to promote and lead harmonized inter hub needs assessments and common analysis, contribute towards intersectoral analysis, nutrition, health, education and protection sectors, as well as strong monitoring of the response, via the WoS 4Ws. Also to coordinate with specialized agencies to monitor the market of water and essential hygiene items. The sector will also provide opportunities for capacity building in key technical areas, in coordination and in programmatic aspects, including risk informed management, gender and protection mainstreaming. The sector will ensure effective coordination between the hubs and at WoS, as per the IASC standards, including:•Ensuring effective Information Management in all hubs, and at a WoS level. •Promoting the exchange of best practices and technical standards, as well as gender and protection mainstreaming.•Representing and advocating on behalf of, the sector in the relevant fora, at hub and WoS level and at intersectoral level.•Effective coordination ongoing in three hubs, and at WoS level, and with relevant sectors (Nutrition, Health, Education and Protection).•Organization of joint WASH assessments between the hubs.•Activity monitoring (4W) at WoS level updated monthly.•Sector specific needs identified.•Common WoS strategy developed.•WASH participation in all HPC initiatives, both at Hub and WoS level. •Implementation of capacity building strategy based on UNICEF MENA RO (ToT)•WASH bottleneck analysis

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Full WASH Protection Risk analysis from last years has been updated and completed at Cluster level. Nevertheless, it concerns essentially field related activities and not coordination related activities. UNICEF is the cluster lead agency for the WASH sector. UNICEF must ensure that capacity is in place for the Whole of Syria WASH cluster coordinator, Information Manager and Cluster Co-coordinator based in Amman, as well as for each of the hubs in Damascus, and Gaziantep. For WASH cluster leadership accountability, UNICEF led the Humanitarian Programme Cycle (HPC) for the development and review of the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and the Humanitarian Response Plan. This approach will ensure that assessments, gap analysis, responses and preparedness plans are provided fully online with humanitarian principles in general and in considering centrality and mainstreaming of protection.
