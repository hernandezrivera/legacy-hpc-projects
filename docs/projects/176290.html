Project Summary

LWF intends to support the affected school age children from most vulnerable IDPs, Returnees and Host communities who have been negatively impacted over the past one year by flood, COVID 19 , compounded by inter-clan, inter-tribal violence and cattle raiding. The project will reach 5000 (2800boys, 2200gilrs) beneficiaries of which 4,500 (2500boys, 2000girls ) primary school students ,500 (30boys, 500girls) ECD pupils and 98 teachers and 147 SMCs/PTAs of Twic East and Duk. The project will address three key areas of education in emergency corresponding to sectoral objectives 1, 2 and 3 of the cluster.
The project will focus on infrastructure improvement with the establishment of ECD and learning spaces for primary students; the construction of gender disaggregated and disable friendly school’s pit latrines. Support school WASH and hygiene activities in collaboration with the WASH cluster to better coordinate the work. LWF activities will also focus on creating an inclusive and safe scholastic environment, through the provision of learning and recreational material, training to teachers on psychosocial support, support to incentive teachers and equipping education structures with skills to better manage schools during emergency situations, but also in the day-to-day activities.

1 - Needs

due to the COVID -19 pandemic education has been interrupted since March 2020 with a reopening of schools only for cadet students in October (class 8 and senior 4) to prepare for the exams. Unfortunately the unprecedented flood in Jonglei led to a complete disruption of the education system with schools and latrines being submerged by water and destroyed. In Duk County there has been torrential rain from July to October 2020. As a result of the flood, significant number of house/Tukuls were submerged and collapsed, schools were affected by flood and most toilets were damaged, people affected by flood faced displacement and loss of livelihood and livestock. During the flooding period all schools (11 Primary school, one secondary school and one ECD center) were under water for months to January 2021) Due to flood and inter clan conflicts in Duk over 50,000 individuals were in sever condition (RRC Inter Agency Rapid Assessment October 2020). Similar, from 20th of July 2020 to December 2020, Twic East county had been under flood due to torrential rains across the upper Nile basin that increased the water in the river. The primary dyke got broken, and the water reached Pakeer, Ajuong, Nyuak, Lith and Kongor payams of the county. Except few spots, all areas had been under water. The flood caused, huge damage to schools. All school were under water up to January 2021. The flood affected all schools in Twic East (three ECD centers, one secondary school and 40 primary schools), except Panyagor primary school and Golden Star ECD center the pitl latrine and toilets were damage across the payams and school, which till standing are full of water. As of February information from the Department of Education of Duk County report and LWF staff physical observation of schools among 11 primary schools , the schools that are going to open on its place are Patuenoi, Payuel ,Dongchak and Padiet Primary Schools. Some primary schools which are still under water and community and pupil of the area moved to canal side as IPDs , then temporary learning space with the name of the following schools , Ayueldit , and Poktap Primary schools recommended to address pupils of these schools at the IPDS settlement and move back to original school when the condition improves. One Childhood Development center ( Jual ECD center) can also be operational in 2021. Similarly one secondary school at Duk Payuel will reopen in April. As per LWF observation with Education department of Twic East county, out of 40 primary schools the schools which are going to reopen on its original place are 14, these are Wernyol ,Marial ,Pageer ,Pakuor ,Pamot Paliau ,Pongborong ,Panyagor ,Apiir , Mabior Academy , Payom , Ameer Duarbek St. John and Primary School. Similarly John Garang secondary school and three ECD such Golden Star Wanglei ECD center and Yith ECD center will reopen. Remain primary schools will be reopening case by cases TLS following the settlements of communities in IDP centersAs the water level is decreasing rapidly the schools which are still under water will dry up fully by April. The project will target two primary schools and one new ECD center in Twic East and three Primary schools and one new ECDs centers in Duk countyThe impact of COVID-19 and the flood with the prolonged closure of schools could lead the most at-risk children into adopting negative coping strategies such as child labor or early marriage, preventing them from returning to schools once they reopen. Additional support is required to improve the provision of water and sanitation services, the installation of handwashing stations, and gender disaggregated latrines, teacher training and community support to build trust, and reassure parents that it is safe for children to return to school. COVID-19 is affecting the mental health of children and adolescents and depression and anxiety are affecting many.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project will mainstream Protection in the implementation to ensure that the right holders (school children) are protected and sexual exploitation and abuse controlled with zero tolerance. LWF will inform beneficiaries through awareness on PSEA using meeting , and pamphlets. In addition, LWF will inform beneficiaries on its own PSEA policy

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Children with disability and their families will be consulted periodically to get their feedback on the project service

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Beneficiaries will be introduced to the project through kick start workshops to gain full information about project , the project planning and implementation will be done with their full involvement as right holders. The project will engage SMCs/PTAS to address complaints

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project will consider the feedback from beneficiaries through reorienting or revising the operational plan and revising where possible the key project delivery mechanisms. Feedback will be gathered through SMC/PTAS and the project staff on the ground.
