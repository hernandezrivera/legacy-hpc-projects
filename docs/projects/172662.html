Project Summary

The project aims to tackle two main sets of needs of the education sector in Libya.
The first one is related to the protracted crisis, insecurity and humanitarian situation that has negatively affected education opportunity of Libyan and foreign children. Teachers need specific skills to be able to foster learning in a situation of protracted crisis, and this project seeks to address this need through teacher training on child-centered pedagogy for 500 teachers. Moreover, refugee and migrant children often do not have access to Libyan schools; this project will support Arabic speaking migrant and refugee children with the required documentation (250 children), as well as with cash assistance to enable them to access education (1,000 children). The project will also provide non-formal education both for foreign children who do not have access to the national system, but also in the form of remedial classes for children at risk of dropping out of school (5,000 children). Learning material will be distributed in formal and non-formal education settings to 40,000 children to improve the quality of the education provided.
The second set of the needs that the project addresses is related to safe return to school under COVID-19 circumstances. This entails preparing the school infrastructure and supplies (disinfection of 400 schools, provision of PPEs to 100 schools, rehabilitation of WASH facilities in 10 schools), as well as conducting awareness with teachers and families on safety protocols to follow in returning to school. The aim is to reach 100,000 individuals with awareness activities.
As school closure has highlighted the need to develop distance learning in the country, the project seeks to collaborate with the Ministries of Education to scale up the use of online platforms across the country, especially for catching up for missed learning opportunities as schools reopen in December 2020. A target of 60,000 children will benefit from the online platform.
The project targets both children in school and children out of school, in the latter case with a specific focus on migrant and refugee children. Most components in the project are implemented through the Ministries of Education, and therefore Libyan and foreign children enrolled in the national education system will be a primary focus on the education interventions related to safe return to school. The project will ensure gender parity in its education activities, in particular in the provision of non-formal education in community centers. A focus on disability is ensured through WASH rehabilitation that is accessible to everyone and purchase of supplies that specifically support children with disabilities.

1 - Needs

Since the start of the armed conflict in 2011, Libya continues to experience armed clashes, insecurity, political crisis, a collapsing economy and displacement. In spite of recent political steps towards an agreement between the parties, the complex political and economic crisis continues to negatively impact education, both in terms of children’s access to schools and quality of the education provided. Internal displacement has led to overcrowded schools, with currently 34 schools still used as IDP shelters (UNICEF SitRep, June 2020). 18 schools remain damaged since the conflict (UNICEF SitRep, 2020). The latest Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA 2020) data show that 9% of interviewed households have faced issues (e.g. functional WASH, learning space, learning materials, and violence in school….) to send their children to school. Sub-standard WASH facilities affect especially girls' attendance in school, and are therefore a priority intervention to ensure gender equality in education. Moreover, since March 2020, the COVID-19 pandemic has led to the closure of schools across the country, which have only re-opened for exams between September and November 2020. The new school year is set to start in December 2020, which means that most children have not been in school for eight months in 2020. According to MSNA data, 32% of children are not enrolled or attending school, compared to 3% in the previous year; while this might be prevalently due to school closure, it is possible that safety concerns and deteriorating economic situation related to COVID-19 contributed to higher-than-usual drop-out. The closure of national schools to reduce the spread of COVID-19 pandemic has disrupted learning for 1.3 million students, affecting especially younger children. Schools serve as an access point to gain entry to various services including access to teaching and learning materials, recreational activities, and psychosocial support, especially targeting girls. While the Ministry of Education has developed an online platform and recordings of classes related to core subjects have been screened on TV and made available online, only 17% of the interviewed households in MSNA data collection (2020) reported access to distance learning opportunities during school closure. Moreover, the possibility of benefiting from distance learning varies depending on age, with younger children strongly depending on their parents' ability to support their learning during school closure. In different conversations between UNICEF and Ministries of Education in the Western and Eastern part of the country, the Ministries have highlighted the need to focus on safe school re-opening through distribution of personal protective equipment (PPEs), school disinfection and awareness raising on health precautions in schools. Moreover, they have added that distance education is a new teaching modality in Libya, and therefore it needs to be supported with teacher training. Another main need in terms of teacher training is inclusive education for children with disabilities, as well as enhancing the use child-centered pedagogy both in formal schools and in non-formal education. A group of children particularly affected by both the humanitarian situation and the COVID-19 pandemic is migrant and refugee children. Migrant and refugee children mainly participate in education activities through non-formal education opportunities in community centers, which have also closed across Libya in line with school closure decided by the Ministry of Education. The community centers are crucial as entry point not only for education opportunities, but also for access to information and services to protect children and families from COVID-19. They especially represent a safe space for out of school girls, being able to target young females through gender appropriate activities and interventions.

2 - Activities

Activities implemented through NGO/INGO partners for access to formal education and non-formal education: 1) Distribution of cash to support access to education, especially for migrant and refugee children (ensuring 50% female participation). 2) Support for missing student documentation, especially for migrant and refugee children (ensuring 50% female participation). 3) Provision of distance and in-person non-formal education, with a focus on children who cannot join distance learning (ensuring 50% female participation). A specific focus in this activity will be on children with disabilities. 4) Distribution of essential learning materials (ensuring 50% female participation). UNICEF direct implementation or through service provider:1) Teacher training on child-centered pedagogy, child safeguarding, and remote learning (ensuring 60% of female participation). A specific focus of the training will be on inclusive education, to enhance understanding of how to include children with disabilities in education programs. 2) Orientation to Education Sector Members and stakeholders on HRP process, EiE, and Information Management.3) Provision of distance and in-person formal education (including early learning). This mainly entails support to Ministry of Education in the development of its online platform to support learning. 4) Rehabilitation of school WASH facilities. 5) Distribution of school furniture (i.e. whiteboards, student desks).6) Disinfection of schools and learning centers 7) Distribution of school kits including cleaning materials and/or PPE. 8) Awareness raising on Covid-19, Infection prevention and control measure, safe return to school, hygiene and sanitation, and distribution of awareness raising materials on COVID-19.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

Barriers for beneficiaries to report concerns through complaint and feedback mechanisms Measures: 1) Complain and feedback mechanism is in place in UNICEF projects; 2) Communication materials on feedback mechanism/ awareness campaign. Increased tensions and conflicts in the community due to perceived or actual unequal distribution of assistance Measures: Community engagement in project development and implementation to ensure fair distribution of assistance. Increased tensions and conflicts in the community due to perceived or actual affiliation to one group or anotherMeasures: Community engagement in project development and implementation to ensure UNICEF and partners are not perceived as affiliated with sides in the conflict. Project results in Increased exposure to COVID-19 transmissionMeasures: education projects implemented will have a blended in-person/distance approach, so that if COVID-19 transmission increases, the education can be moved completely to distance modality. Moreover, IPC protocols are in place where education activities are conducted. Deprivation of access to services of persons with different (visual, physical, intellectual, psychosocial/mental, hearing, speech) disabilities Measures: the project includes targeted interventions for children with disability, for example in the rehabilitation of WASH facilities and in the distribution of desks for children for disability. Exposure of individuals to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance Measures: UNICEF and partners abide by PSEA regulations to ensure beneficiaries are protected from sexual exploitation or abuse. Reporting mechanisms are in place to encourage reporting of cases. Barriers for beneficiaries to report sexual exploitation and/or abuse in a safe and confidential manner Measures: Training to partners on PSEA and awareness campaign among beneficiaries on ways to report sexual exploitation and abuse. Risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure in targeted project areas Measures: when the project entails construction work, legal property of the land will be required in order to implement the construction work, if there is a dispute over the land.
