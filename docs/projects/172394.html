Project Summary

The project seeks to ensure that women and girls are central to humanitarian action. This shall be achieved through building the capacity of humanitarian actors to address gender, protection GBV and SEA concerns in humanitarian action. The project shall focus on training inter cluster humanitarian actors and community cadres. Linked to this is the focus on increased access to information by women, men, boys and girls on humanitarian actions, their role and how they can benefit

1 - Activities

The plan is to train 2140 protection actors (including community cadres) in gender, protection, GBViE and PSEA response X 8 districts, and 100 humanitarian actors across clusters at national level. 36602 men, women, boys and girls per district shall be reached with risk communications. Key activities will include the following; • Train humanitarian actors to integrate gender, protection, GBV and PSEA in their humanitarian response plans, and implementation strategies. • Training on gender sensitive monitoring and evaluation in humanitarian programmes. • Training existing community outreach groups, including women groups to undertake community-led gender based advocacy, research and monitoring of humanitarian action • Training community actors to play a key role as disseminators of risk communications that takes into account differential needs of women, men, girls and boys. (train community actors as agents of change in their communities) • Develop information and advocacy materials to strengthen the gendered response to humanitarian disasters.

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

In designing the project a Zimbabwe humanitarian needs overview assessment was conducted in the proposed districts. The assessment focused on determining key GBV issues related to COVID 19 impact on GBV risk exacerbation, access to GBV services at community level, and availability of community based security structures and complaints mechanisms. UN Women will support the development/strengthening of a community feedback mechanism model to allow programme beneficiaries to provide feedback on key implementation issues. UN Women proposes to train the already exiting community structures and mechanisms such as women peace committees, to become focal points at community level to link the project beneficiaries to the GBV cluster members as well as linkages to the gender based humanitarian monitoring system that will be developed.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Research demonstrates that when environmental conditions worsen, women and girls’ experience and interaction with the environment also worsens. For example, Cyclone Idai’s devastating impact on the environment increased women’s burden as home managers and carers, in that access to water, sanitation, cooking energy was limited or non existent. As communities in environmentally-precarious situations face stress, gender-based violence (GBV) is a negative coping strategy that perpetrators sometimes use to reassert control over women’s bodies and minds. GBV is often an effort to enforce gender norms and roles during unstable times. The project will address these issues in the planned capacity building activities for IPs and communities, also drawing on existing environmental impact assessments that highlight gender issues.
