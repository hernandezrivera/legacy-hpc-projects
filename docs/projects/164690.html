Project Summary

The project seeks to establish a community center in one of these cities( Afrin , Azaz ,Albab , Sarmada) , so in the Humanitarian Needs Assessment 2020 issued by the United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (UN OCHA) It is reported that hundreds of thousands of people have moved to areas such as Afrin, Azaz and Al-Bab in the northern province of Aleppo and the northern regions of Idlib ,SCA Seeks to continue empowering communities and individuals in That cities and support their role in building their society and determining their future. The community Center will provides full protection services& Children Friendly Space and case management services, including vocational training, youth programming, legal assistance, and psychosocial support for more than 17,600 individuals (host community/IDPs). and referral services for children at risk .Provide systematic awareness campaigns to protect children Educating children about the dangers of explosives and mines Training humanitarian workers on child protection issues.
The community center creates safe public spaces in which conflict-affected Syrian populations, including IDPs & host community and Children, can interact, express feelings and thoughts, and build confidence and a sense of belonging through supportive methods led by the center’s team and outreach teams. As the Syrian conflict heads into its Ninth year, 13.1 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance, with over 5.6 million living in acute need. Individuals in Syria face increased protection risks in their daily lives while the capacity to positively cope with these risks continues to be stretched. Indiscriminate and targeted attacks on civilian areas and facilities, grave rights violations, denial of humanitarian access, and violations of international law persist. Violence at household (HH) and community levels continue, and vulnerabilities based on gender, age, disability and other factors often intersect. Humanitarian actors remotely managing emergency programming in conflict-affected northern Syria face significant access challenges, resulting in unclear understanding of contextual protection issues.The project takes into account the inclusion of children Persons with disabilities in society through specialized activities including psychological rehabilitation, provision of PSS activities, life skills and awareness. And provide sessions on their rights within the community and work will be done to improve their access to the services provided by the project by providing their transportation to ensure their safety.The project was designed to be based on gender equality in the number of jobs and the number of beneficiaries and to determine the priorities for employing women.as the project achieved the proportion of girls benefiting more than the proportion of male children.

4 - Needs

As the crisis continues, the protection and well being of Syrian populations is drastically deteriorating. SCA assessments that as a result of the ongoing conflict, many Syrians have suffered severe and interrelated stressors especially children such as loss of their homes, livelihoods, material belongings, and community or social support systems. Some have witnessed horrific events and lost loved ones, become separated from family members, and suffered physical assault or psychological . Children and adolescents have bear the burden of the crisis with grave violations perpetrated against children and adolescents by all sides of the conflict.The concept of targeting the elderly in certain recreational and PSS activities is quite new in the Syrian context and most of them tend to stay at home than participate in such activities. SCA will include elderly and disabled people to the project through its outreach activities.The majority of individuals& Children affected by extreme events experience acute stress reactions including nightmares, sleeping problems, anxiety and worry, hopelessness, as well as behavioral problems, anxiety and bedwetting among children. While these conditions resolve themselves over time for a large portion of the population, some affected by extreme events or with pre-existing conditions require psychosocial support and other protection services like legal assistance, case management, one-on-one sessions or assistance from a social worker. Furthermore, as many conflict-affected Syrians are experiencing loss of hope, lack of motivation, boredom, social isolation, weight-loss, and frustration by their lack of opportunities, community-based psychosocial programming and protection services are essential in strengthening social networks and promoting protective and supportive environments for the boys, girls, men, women and people with disabilities’ of both IDPs and host community members.

5 - Implementation of activities

SCA will respond to protection concerns through a community centers and community outreach teams. The community center staff will be composed of 9 staff members:4 social workers, 2 case managers (who can handle the full cycle of case management),1 community center officer [responsible for admin and management], 1 community network coordinator,and 1 center assistant. The two mobile outreach teams, who will work in schools, IDP camps, and communities , will be composed of 4 staff members each: 1 team leader, 2 social workers (responsible for PSS, recreational and awareness-raising sessions, and assistance with community protection networks), and 1 caseworker (responsible for identification of vulnerable cases, including Protection, CP, etc.).In addition to these full-time staff members, a lawyer will be contracted to handle legal assistance, and trainers will be contracted to conduct vocational and technical training.SCA will deliver social and protection activities to children, adolescents, people with special needs and older women and men.It will use the youth empowerment curriculum and other protection guidelines from the CP Sub-Cluster, UNHCR, UNICEF, and UNFPA. The center will be accessible five days per week (Sunday-Thursday); while the centers is primarily reserved for boys, girls, and women, adolescent boys and men can access activities conducted in other contracted spaces, for example for vocational training, awareness-raising sessions, and recreational activities.The mobile teams conduct activities in locations including schools,camps, and markets around targeted Cities and surrounding communities. Activities will emphasize equity and non-discrimination, reinforcing the basic social and human rights of participants while taking into consideration the cultural, historical and social specificities of targeted populations.All activities in Field will be monitored by M&E team on a monthly basis, Use the kobo tool in M&E.Complaints system.traning staff.FGDs.RNA
