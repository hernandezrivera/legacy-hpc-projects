Project Summary

Somali children are described to live in one of the least protective environments in the world due to the cyclical occurrence of the man-made and natural disasters. This scenario has broken down the child protection systems and structures and weakened the capacity for delivery of services in spite of the concerted efforts of community, government, donors and partners. COVID-19 pandemic, floods and the locust invasion in 2020 has further exacerbated child protection risks due to their impact on household livelihoods thus exposing children to negative coping mechanisms like child labour, separation, FGM and child marriage. Access to CP services such as psychosocial support activities ceased with government sanctioned lockdowns to prevent COVID 19 spread. The goal of this project is to ensure boys and girls including those with disabilities, facing life- threatening risks of abuse, neglect, violence, exploitation, and severe distress have access to well- coordinated and disability, gender- sensitive quality child protection services by 2021. To deliver on this, WVI seeks to focus on prevention, mitigation and response interventions in Somaliland, Puntland, South West State and Jubaland where the organization has a physical presence with ongoing interventions on CP, health, nutrition WASH and FSL. Under prevention, awareness raising and capacity building of child protection structures at the front line is emphasized while creating a linkage with the formal reporting and referral pathway. Dialogue forums with different community people groups such as caregivers, teachers, faith leaders as well as duty bearers will be used to strengthen child protection awareness at the community level. Child to child interventions including school club activities will be used build lifeskills and increase knowledge on child rights. Skills for self-protection in emergency situations will also be imparted through IEC materials and school club activities. Caregivers, parents and teachers will be targeted with positive parenting messages and non-violent discipline techniques to address the corporal punishment and other forms of violence at the household level and at school. Negative coping mechanisms to emergency situations such as child marriage, separation, child labor and trafficking will be prevented through cash based assistance to households with children at risk of abuse including those headed by caregivers with disability. Through the Channels of Hope Child Protection model, WVI will continue to work with faith leaders to tackle difficult topics around cultural practices that disadvantage the girl child such as FGM and child marriage. In close collaboration with existing local networks, policy advancement in child protection with different government line ministries at state level will be supported. Under mitigation, community-led safety audits and mapping of most vulnerable households with children at risk of violence will be done with an aim of ensuring risk mitigation mechanisms that are disability-sensitive are in place. Community level disaster risk reduction plans with an early warning system embedded alongside a communication plan targeting children and caregivers including those with disabilities will be disseminated through IEC materials and community meetings. Under response, individual specialized services through case management e.g. for Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC) will be done while collaborating with existing mechanisms for family tracing and reunification efforts. To strengthen reporting and referral, the use of the toll free line 364 will be popularized alongside setting up child help desks and dialogue forums with children at school level to identify those in need of care and support. Mental health and psychosocial support interventions will be prioritized for children and care givers including child friendly spaces, support groups etc.COVID-19 control measures will be integrated during activity implementation

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

In line with the standards for infectious disease outbreaks response, cash and vouchers will be used as a means to prevent exposure to COVID-19 through in kind distribution. Cash and voucher assistance directly to beneficiaries will prevent delays in access to essential commodities due to logistical hurdles that could arise as a result of another COVID-19 state and country level lockdown leading to delays in procurement and distribution. WVI will prioritize cash transfers to enable the families providing alternate care to UASC meet the specific household supplies needs and unique gender needs of the children as opposed to providing a standard in kind package. Cash assistance also stands to revitalize the local economy by promoting local businesses and providing a source of livelihoods which is a factor in prevention of violence against children. Distribution of in-kind supplies to the UASC is more cost intensive as compared to mobile cash and reduces the risks of aid diversion.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

WVI is a member of the cash working group in the country and participates in market functionality assessments conducted by the FSL projects already existing in the proposed districts covered by this project.

Mitigation Measures

1. Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.) This risk will be addressed by factoring in mobile outreaches to far and hard to reach locations to ensure timely access to services. A mapping of the available CP and GBV service providers alongside the telephone of their focal points will be displayed in health facilities and other social amenities like schools in the Somali language.2. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities This project The project has considered working with disability persons organizations for consultation and make a provision for service delivery tailor made to reach marginalized communities and PWD e.g. community outreaches, remote service delivery e.g. for MHPSS and individualized follow up. School clubs will be used to reach children with GBV prevention, mitigation and response activities including creation of women and girl friendly spaces.3. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms WVI already has an existing community response mechanism which will be integrated in this project to increase access to provide feedback and complaints. The toll free number 364 will be displayed in all service delivery sites in the Somali language. Monitoring of the response to complaints in this system usually forms part of the senior leadership team monthly updates until the issue raised is resolved. Suggestion boxes will continue to be used in schools alongside the setting up of help desks where boys and girls can walk in and share concerns about WVI service delivery.4. Risk that the project does not have sufficient internal mechanisms to combat and prevent child safeguarding and PSEA WVI has a strong policy of Child and Adult Safeguarding Management that also states our zero tolerance to SEA by our staff and affiliates which include contractors, consultants and volunteers. In line with this policy, all staff and affiliates will be trained on DO No Harm and the safeguarding standards which include behavior protocols while interacting with project beneficiaries. In compliance with our safeguarding standards, IEC materials in Somali language communicating our zero tolerance to SEA will be distributed at project sites with a strong message to beneficiaries' right to provide feedback or/and complaints about our services as well as send reports about incidents anonymously through our toll free line 364. In response to safeguarding incidents, a survivor centered approach will be used to ensure the safety and well being of the survivor during investigations. Project staff will also undertake the mandatory PSEA training annually and participate in the compulsory safeguarding training once every 2 years. During recruitment of project staff, screening including background checks with previous employers and criminal background check will be done to ensure only candidates that are suitable to work with vulnerable adults and children are hired.
