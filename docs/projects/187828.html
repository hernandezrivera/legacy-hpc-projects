Project Summary

Child Protection:
During 2022, Shafak organization will respond to the increased child protection concerns of the crisis affected children including CWDs through center-based activities in five Child-Friendly Spaces and 19 community outreach teams deployed in Harim, Jisr-Ash-Shugur, Ariha, and Idleb districts in Idleb, and Azaz, Al-Bab and Jarablus districts in Aleppo. Total number of BNFs targeted 45000(4000M, 6500W, 17250B, and 17250G).
Shafak will provide CP case management and referral in the targeted locations targeting children with CP concerns. CM services will be complemented with the provision of emergency case funds and CVA to respond to the immediate and urgent needs of children including CWDs. Shafak will submit the CM tracker to CPSC on regular basis. The following CP vulnerability criteria and concerns will be considered for CM and IPA support:
1- UASC
2- Child labor including its worst forms
3- CAAFAG
4- Early Marriage
5- Child headed household
6- Domestic violence
7- Severe Psychological distresses
Moreover, age and gender-appropriate MHPSS and structured and non-structured PSS activities for children and adolescents will be organized in and out of the CFSs. Different curricula will be used such as; IRC PSS manual, I DEAL, and Child Resilience.
Positive parenting skills sessions will be provided to caregivers to reduce the physical and humiliating punishment of children in the home, and improve the capacity of parents and caregivers to practice positive parenting.
In addition, Shafak will target the communities with awareness-raising activities on PSEA, child rights, and CP issues. Thus, messages will be tailored to gender and age needs in target locations to ensure gender sensitivity and also that messaging is relevant to children at special age levels.
Finally, Shafak will strengthen the capacity of local communities about child concerns by establishing 20 CPNs covering all age and gender groups in the targeted communities to prevent and respond to CP needs at the community level. And secondly, train key persons in those communities and the workers in care centers, schools, hospitals who are in direct contact with children about the CPMS, PFA, and Safe Identification and referral. Shafak will adapt the CFSs and the static facilities to be accessible and friendly for CWDs through establishing ramps and toilets for CWDs.
As for 2023, according to Shafak's strategy and fund availability, it is expected to implement the same activities with the same size and budget.
Mine Action:
Shafak will deliver a comprehensive Risk Education. This project will be a continuation of Shafak's work in the Explosive Remnants of War Risk Education ERW - RE program in Idleb and Aleppo.
During 2022, and under the ERW-RE part of the project, 4 RE teams who have already been trained by Shafak, will target 5200 individuals (400M, 1000W, 1900B, and 1900G) including IDPs and host communities in Idleb and Aleppo providing school-age children with RE activities, and materials to inform them about the dangers of ERW. Shafak will also provide RE material to adults, putting up posters about the ERW risks in the public streets and communities to increase awareness of ERW threats.
Moreover, the RE (ToT) Trainers will provide RE training to Community Focal Points (CFPs) in affected communities, and RE teams and other workers from NGOs across all humanitarian sectors, to improve the relevance, integration, and reach of RE messaging across NW Syria.
COVID-19 precautionary measures will be taken in line with WHO, Global Protection Cluster, and the Protection Cluster’s Guidance, including strict enforcement of physical distancing, minimizing of participants per activity, and maintaining the social distancing between BNFs and staff, and the use of PPE.
Shafak plans to implement similar activities during 2023 and reach the same target.

4 - Needs

Syria remains one of the world’s most complex humanitarian emergencies characterized by ongoing hostilities which have killed hundreds of thousands of people triggered one of the worst displacement of our time According to Draft HNO 2022, 14.2million people in Syria are in need of humanitarian assistance including 6million children. Shafak as part of the PMTF observed CP concerns as Kidnapping/Abduction as occurring in 26% of communities Child labor as 84% of communities child marriage continues to form an issue for adolescent girls in 71% of communities family separation in 36% communities 41% assessed HHs reported lack of birth certificates Children without birth certificates, especially children with disabilities are denied to access basic rights The situation for many children and families remains insecure with nearly 90% of children in need of humanitarian assistance According to latest Shafak NA in Salqin and Aghterin Child marriage is a common concern for girls in NWS, with 81% of the KIs are aware of girls in their community who had been married off at an age below 18, with the vast majority of the cases to be girls at the age of 14. School dropout is a major issue in NWS, 72% of KIs said children drop out of school due to financial reasons have to work to support their families EO contamination continue to present a risk for populations living in areas having witnessed hostilities will remain the top access constraint for the safe delivery of assistance to PiN

5 - Implementation of activities

CP activities will be delivered through five CFSs, that will be rehabilitated and equipped for CWDs, and 19 CP mobile teams and will include: -PSS: Age and gender appropriate structured-PSS activities for children will be delivered, each group will receive 6-8 sessions. Adolescents boys and girls will be also targeted with MHPSS and PSS activities to improve their mental health wellbeing.Target:15000(7500B,7500G) -Parenting Skills:1500 (750M,750W) parents/caregivers will be targeted with this activity divided into groups 6-10 participants,6 to 8 sessions for each.-Case Management: Shafak will provide CP CM, referrals, and IPA/ ECF in the targeted locations to 3000(1500B,1500G), these services will target children with CP concerns (e.g.PSS distress,UASC,etc).IPA will be provided through in-kind and Cash modalities -AWR: Sessions will be conducted on the impact of key CP issues on children’s life and PSEA to 25000(3000M,5500W,8250B,8250G) -CP Networks: establish and support 20 CPNs.-Training of CP staff ,CPNs and humanitarian workers on CP topics: CPMS, CPIE.500(250M,250W)MA: -Deliver RE sessions to 5200 individuals by the RE teams.MEAL activities will be conducted to measure the impact of the delivered activities through 1-Sessions feedback for AWR activities 2-Pre/post-test for training and PSS activities. 3-CM will be monitored and evaluated by trained CM team ensuring confidentiality principle through satisfaction survey tool and checking the files by the CM supervisor

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

CP Risk1: Doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls, and boys (and their caregivers) through the provision of specialized child protection services if service providers are not adequately trained/ services are of low quality Likelihood: High Mitigation-Ensure training packages are in line child protection minimum standards and consider remote managed context-Services should not be provided by any staff who have not received and demonstrated proficiency in training modules-Include a practice element to training modules that can be overseen through remote mentorship arrangements to allow for practice prior to service provision.Risk2: Exploitation or abuse by staff members’/service providers if access to services or information are linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups. Likelihood: High Mitigation-Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and who services are for -Provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA. MA Risk1:Familiarisation may cause the civilian populace to become overconfident or complacent to the threat of explosive hazards Likelihood: Medium Mitigation: Conduct regular KAPB surveys to monitor changes in risk behavior Risk2: Familiarization may cause the civilian populace to become overconfident or complacent to the threat of explosive hazards Likelihood: Medium Mitigation: -Emphasis on "Stop, Warn, and Report" messaging in risk education


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk Identified1: the risk of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse by the project personnel on project BNFs and women staff. Mitigation: 1- provide awareness raising sessions in the targeted community on PSEA 2- train the new staff on PSEA, CSG and to have refresher trainings to the old staff. 3- Distribute banners, PSEA reporting mechanism including the suggestion box, WhatsApp number...etc. Risk 2: No reporting from SEA survivors: Mitigation: 1- Raise awareness between project BNFs about PSEA and reporting mechanisms. 2- Apply various reporting channels that are accessible to all BNFs. 3- Emphasize on the principle of confidentiality in dealing with SEA cases. 4- Assign gender-balanced PSEA focal points to encourage women and girls to report


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1:Limited/ interrupted access to intervention areas due to increased insecurity and/ or loss of access due to change in control; Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High. Mitigation measures:1.1 Close monitoring and participation in info-sharing mechanisms and meetings.1.2 Security-related SOPs in place. 1.3 Remote monitoring mechanisms in place.1.4 Maintain strong lines of communication with Shafak HQ security in order to preempt any potential increase in hostilities, takeover, or areas through agreements, and be prepared to react accordingly. Risk 2: Disputes between host communities and IDPs; Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 2.1:Targeting host community, 2.2: ensure Complaint mechanism is accessible by all people. 2.3 integrity and clear verification process conducted according to announced criteria. 2.4 Prior coordination with local actors and stakeholders in the community to ensure the process.Risk: Outbreak of COVID 19; Likelihood: High; Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 3.1:Daily check of staff to ensure their safety is a major symptom of the Coronavirus.3.2:Emphasis during work on the need to leave a distance of at least 1 between workers.3.3:The necessity not to exchange equipment and tools before sterilization. 3.4:Ensure the presence of awareness brochures within the work area and in a clear place.3.4:In case of imposed curfew. Suspend the activities and change the modality to be through service providers and distribute the dues of the staff
