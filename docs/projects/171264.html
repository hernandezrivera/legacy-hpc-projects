Project Summary

Through this project UNRWA aims to scale up its COVID-19 response in the West Bank through procurement of PPE and infection control supplies, awareness raising on COVID-19 prevention measures among Palestine refugees and finally, enhancement of UNRWA’s rotation and triage systems along its network of 43 health centers and Qualqilyia hospital.

1 - Needs

Through its network of 43 health centres and health points and Qualqilyia hospital, UNRWA provides quality health services to fulfill the health needs of Palestine refugees in West Bank, in partnerships with the MoH and other stakeholders. 667,215 refugees (approximately 57percent female) have active files with the UNRWA West Bank Health Programme, and directly benefit from UNRWA’s health services. UNRWA’s Health Programme provides services to Palestine refugees at different stages of the life cycle through preventative care, maternal and child services, reproductive health, and treatment of non-communicable diseases. As the primary humanitarian organization providing health services to Palestine refugees in the West Bank, UNRWA health centres and health points register more than 1,000,000 patient visits every year. The COVID-19 emergency has put an increased pressure on UNRWA’s already overburdened health system. As of 22 October 2020, 30 per cent of the 254 COVID 19 infected UNRWA staff were health workers. Despite the pandemic, the introduction of triage and rotation systems in UNRWA’s health centers have allowed UNRWA to provide over 447,005 consultations in the West Bank and East Jerusalem (20 March 2020-10 October). It is estimated that in 2021, over a period of 12 months, UNRWA will continue providing over 1,000,000 consultations even while the COVID-19 pandemic continues. It is believed that while the number of visits to health centers was reduced during initial lockdowns, patients are only holding off their visits to health centers. Thanks to provision of PPE to all Health workers and a steady supply of infection control material to sanitize health centers , UNRWA has been able to continue provision of basic health services during the first wave of COVID-19. Scaling up supply of PPE and infection control material in 2021, continuation of the triage and rotation systems and finally awareness raising among Palestine refugees on COVID-19 prevention measures are critical for ensuring UNRWA’s uninterrupted provision of primary health services to Palestine refugees in the West Bank.

2 - Activities

UNRWA provides primary healthcare services to Palestine refugees across its 43 health centres and Qualqilia hospital in the West Bank. In 2021, UNRWA will scale up its response capacity within its primary health care services in order to cope with increasing demand and emerging needs due to COVID-19. In response to the pandemic, UNRWA is implementing all necessary adjustments to prevent further spread of the virus. The activities of this project include a) procurement of PPE and Infection Control Material for the safe operation of UNRWA’s 43 health clinics and Qualqilia for a period up to 12 months; b) hiring of daily paid health workers to replace health staff falling sick and to continue with UNRWA’s current triage and rotation systems for 10 months, while alternative measures are identified. The proposed budget estimates 60 health staff for 43 Health centers (an average of 1.4 additional health staff per health centre); 13 additional cleaners and 7 health clerks for checking temperature and other tasks. In addition, 5 medical staff is budgeted to support the rotation system at Qualqilia Hospital. Finally, c) Risk Communication and Engagement Campaign activities are planned to raise awareness on COVID-19 prevention measures among the Palestine refugees community. The campaigns will include distribution of printed material, spots at UNRWA's health centers tvs and facebook posts.

3 - Indicators

The success of the project will be measured by continuous provision of health services to Palestine refugees. It will be measured by the following indicators: 150,000 printed prevention messaging material distributed; 30,000 views through social media platforms and other media channels with communicable diseases prevention measures; 44 healthcare facilities with propositioned items to enhance preparedness within the health sector;723 healthcare workers protected with the provision of PPE; 667,215 people have access to L2 PHC services; 15,129 women received SRH services. In normal circumstances, satisfaction surveys and exit interviews are conducted on a regular basis by UNRWA health officers to ensure optimal delivery of health services to Palestine refugees. In emergency situation, the beneficiaries may address any concerns to hotline staff, and such feedback will be followed up. UNRWA also runs its own portal where beneficiaries are encouraged to raise any general query with regards to any issue including the quality of services delivered.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

In UNRWA Protection Strategy (2012), the prioritisation of safety and dignity is explicitly addressed as a key protection standard where UNRWA programmes deliver services in a manner that ensures the safety and dignity of beneﬁciaries and staff. UNRWA ensures that its actions do not cause unintended harm or result in reduced protection of beneﬁciaries, communities or staff. Further, UNRWAs Medium-Term Strategy 2016-2021 explicitly prioritises the "do-no-harm"-principles by stating that UNRWA will address protection challenges that arise from its health service delivery. This will include focusing on instances when UNRWA, through its actions or policies, unintentionally undermines the realization of speciﬁc rights such as the socioeconomic rights. By addressing such concerns through ‘do-no-harm’ and other approaches, UNRWA service delivery promotes and respects the rights of beneﬁciaries and ensures the security and dignity of beneﬁciaries. Protection needs are addressed in all aspects of regular UNRWA programme and project design, policies, protocols and procedures, as well as in staff training. In all contexts, UNRWA will work to ensure that its assistance and service delivery is carried out in accordance with internationally agreed standards and its own minimum protection standards. UNRWA will regularly measure its level of compliance with these minimum standards and take necessary corrective action.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNRWA as a service provider is dedicated to the application of the humanitarian principles in all of its service delivery, and takes it responsibility in this regard very seriously. Specific measures are taken to ensure that UNRWA's services are neutral and impartial in nature, including through regular monitoring throughout its operations. In planning UNRWA engages with a range of community representatives, including protection committees, village councils, camp resident committees – as well as holding numerous focus group discussions including for women, children, youth and the elderly in communities that we are working with. In the context of the COVID-19 response, in case of lockdown and movement restriction, people in need of primary health care are still able to access UNRWA’s health services through telemedicine. The health centres take into consideration speciﬁc vulnerability concerns and adopted speciﬁc SOPs to provide meaningful access and prioritize healthcare to vulnerable groups (abandoned women, pregnant women, persons with disabilities, elderly, etc.). A speciﬁc follow up system is also in place whenever assistance is not received in order to identify and follow speciﬁc concerns and potential protection cases. Further, health centres are clearly marked and visible, respecting UNRWA neutrality policy.
