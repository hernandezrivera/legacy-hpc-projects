Project Summary

The population of Somalia consistently experience some of the worst health outcome indicators in the
World, the average life expectancy is estimated to be 51.5 years. Somalia is among the countries with the highest rankings in infant and child mortality rates of, respectively, 76.6 and 121.5 per 1,000 live births. The maternal mortality rate (692 deaths per 100,000 live births) and the fertility rate (average of 6.9 children per woman) are also among the highest in the world, 79 per cent of all births are home delivered without skilled assistance. These is further compounded by the prevailing prolonged humanitarian crisis characterized by ongoing conflicts, climate-related shocks, communicable disease outbreaks and weak social protection mechanisms (Humanitarian Needs Overview -2022). Since the beginning of 2020, four additional shocks have contributed to a deterioration of humanitarian conditions: Extensive floods, Desert Locust infestations, the COVID-19 pandemic and ravaging drought currently been experienced in Gedo region particularly in Bardhere, Dollow, Ceel-Waaq and Garbaharey districts of Gedo region.
According to Humanitarian needs overview 2022, Over 6.56 million Somali people require lifesaving essential Healthcare in 2022. Excess mortality in Somalia is driven primary by malnutrition, disease outbreaks (e.g., COVID-19, AWD/cholera, measles, malaria) and violence; as well as non-communicable disease including complications of pregnancy and chronic disease. These are compounded by displacement due to conflict and natural disasters and lack of preventative and curative healthcare. Lifesaving physical trauma care services are scarce, and the available mental health and psychosocial support services do not keep up with the substantially increasing need. Availability and access to appropriate health care for persons living with a disability, estimated to be at least 15 per cent of the population, remain dramatically insufficient.
HDC proposes to undertake a life-saving emergency primary and secondary healthcare services to Disaster-Affected Communities (DACs) IDPs, returnees, and host communities in Baardhere, Ceelwaq, Garbaharey, Dollow in Gedo region, aimed at reduction of excess morbidity and mortality due to disease outbreaks driven communicable diseases and injuries by climatic shocks and conflict among IDPs and host communities: by providing Immunization services to children under one year and women of reproductive age, Support early detection, investigation and response to disease (e.g. outbreak) through public awareness, health staff training, monitoring (EWAR) and supplies, Provision of antenatal with iron/folate supplementation (pregnant women) and post natal care for lactating mothers, provision of safe delivery -BEmOC/CEmOC , nutritional counseling, pregnancy monitoring, and early detection and management of pregnancy complications through one-stop center within the health center for the pregnant women, Provision of basic lifesaving medical services to sick patients including AWD/Cholera, upper respiratory infections such as pneumonia, Identify and Refer patients with severe medical cases to secondary and tertiary health facilities in the region.
Proposed project location usually experience cases of gender based violence mostly against women and girls. There is heightened gender based violence (GBV) risks against displaced women and girls in Ceel Waaq, Baardhere, Dollow, Garbaharey and Banadir districts who are currently living in makeshift shelters and have no adequate food, water and sanitation services. The major GBV risk to IDP women and adolescent girls is a threat of sexual violence. Health services for GBV services is only available in the main towns which is distant from the IDP locations where the recently displaced population live. Major gaps in GBV services includes lack of safety and security services safety facilities are cited as being unavailable. Priority needs are safe shelters

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Mitigation MeasuresHDC will provide refresher training to all its staff on PSEA involved in project implementation on preventive measures against sexual exploitation and abuse/ protection mainstreaming and ensure they sign the code of conduct on physical sexual exploitation and abuse as a commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries, including the confidentiality of patients. HDC will ensure the selection/targeting criteria is shared with local communities/IDP management committees while staffs will ensure that the selection criteria’s adhered to all the time. Committees must be selected based on AGD criteria to ensure all affected groups are involved in the intervention HDC will involve all the stakeholders and target beneciaries at all stages of the project so that there are sense of ownership and inclusivity right from the beginning. HDC will involve local leaders, women, men, youth and community elders during beneficiary identification, selection and registration. This will allow the community to do vetting and identify the neediest community members. It will also reduce risk external interference as well as exclusion of marginalized community members. Community will shared with complains and grievance feedback mechanism as well as opportunity to engage the organization to identify most suitable and friendly way of raising their complains.
