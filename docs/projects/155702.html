Project Summary

SDO will target 1500 most vulnerable returnee households in this project. SDO will provide them with agricultural inputs for Kharif season including maize (organic certified variety from government), agricultural tool kit, kitchen gardening kit and trainings on modern techniques of agriculture and kitchen gardening. SDO will provide women beneficiaries with kitchen gardening kits having vegetable seeds of eight types (organic recommended varieties from government). Male and Female agriculture staff of SDO will train men and women beneficiaries on modern techniques of agriculture farming and kitchen gardening respectively. They will also follow up the beneficiaries at farm and household level to correct their practices and advise them where necessary. Apart from that SDO will assist the beneficiaries to reclaim/level/prepare their lands. For this purpose SDO will hire tractors for the beneficiaries. Each beneficiary family will be serviced for 3.5 hours to utilize tractor for land reclamation, levelling or preparation. Agency Agriculture department will be one of the stakeholders of the project where the department will guide SDO in selection of recommended varieties of organic seeds. The agriculture department will also train beneficiaries on modern techniques of agriculture farming. Project will rehabilitate 10 critical agriculture related infrastructure for enhanced access to water. SDO will link project beneficiaries with agriculture department during project duration in the capacity building sessions. Later on after SDO evacuation, the beneficiaries will access agriculture department as per their needs.

Needs

Agriculture is the main livelihood and lifeline of local economy. Small landholders make up the majority of farmers engaged in agriculture mainly at the subsistence level. Maize and vegetables is the major portion of daily food which is not cultivated in the project area since displacement and return. Wheat and maize are the two main crops produced in the Agency. However at project area Potatoes cultivation was carried out by many farmers. Productions of wheat and maize are insufficient to even meet the local food requirements. Average yield of wheat in SWA last recoded in 2008-09 was 1.33 tons per hectare which is half to the other areas of Pakistan and the same trend was there is case of maize and average yield of SWA is 1.56 tons . Since the militancy production of both crops was zero and same is the case with potatoes. According to figures for the year 2000, the majority of farms (33.9%) are between 0.4 and 1 hectare in size, followed by smaller agricultural holdings of less than 0.4 hectares (22.8 per cent), and slightly larger holdings of between 1 and 2 hectares (18.6 per cent). Most farmland produces a single crop each year and a very small percent of cultivated land is put to use during two seasons. By far, the largest majority of farms are cultivated by owners. People especially ultra vulnerable return families; do not have farming toolkits, seeds and resources to restart agriculture as per pre conflict situation. SDO based on previous projects experience in SWA will involve agriculture department, FDMA, Political Agent office and Army. Project will use vulnerability criteria will identify vulnerable 1500 families, provide agriculture inputs and trainings. Agriculture in majority of the area is irrigation dependent which is carried out through a combination of tube wells, dug wells and traditional communal canals. The agriculture land was barren and abandoned due to non-cultivation while orchards were completely destroyed. Cemented irrigation channels were badly damaged and silted, thus were not able to irrigate the catchment lands. Seasonal rain, snow and floods damaged almost irrigation channels and people do not enough tools, machinery and economic resources to rebuild damaged resources. In the absence of irrigation the future of any agriculture related intervention is fade. At project area after displacement population of wild boars, porcupine, foxes and other wildlife increased incredibly. At all villages majority of the arable land was attacked by wild boars and porcupine. Ban on all type ammunition and other substance to repel wild animal further aggravated the situation. Fencing and protection from wild boar is imminent to save crops and agriculture land in villages. The returnees lost income due to displacement for the past six to eight years. At majority of villages community members stayed outside SWA to earn livelihood and pace of the agriculture farming and remaining activities remain slow and still almost no livelihood opportunity is available and people cannot rehabilitate agriculture related infrastructure and restart farming on their own. In selected villages’ project will facilitate community to prioritize 15 irrigation or fencing or mix schemes. Project will provide material, kits and training, to encourage people to return to agriculture again and support livelihoods. Besides emergency food assistance, the affected people will need diversified livelihood support and additional income sources to transit from relief assistance to self-sufficiency. MUSAFR reports 85% of irrigation infrastructure is damaged and 75.5% losses in livestock. During displacement a shift from green to other labour sources was reported. Project will contribute to lifesaving food assistance, livelihoods support for sustainable income generation, and strengthened preparedness and resilience to future disasters remains critical food security needs .
