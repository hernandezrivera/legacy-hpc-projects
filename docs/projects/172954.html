Project Summary

Multi-sectorial emergency response in Eastern Sudan (Kassala/Gedaref) and North Darfur with regard to WASH, FSL, Protection, NFI-Shelter assistance. This project includes Covid-19 response activities in all locations. This project mainly targets refugees, but also IDPs and vulnerable host communities members. In the East Sudan, the project will specifically target the refugee influx from Eritrea. In North Darfur, the project interlinks with a planned BMZ project to operationalize the Triple NEXUS.

1 - Needs

With this project WHH undertakes to respond to the needs of refugees (incl. South Sudanese), IDPs and host communities in different sectors across 3 states of Sudan. See below detailed description for each State. According to the CRP locality priority list 2021, Al Lait (North Darfur) and Wad El Helew (Kassala) are #1 priority in terms of needs and El Fashaga (Gedaref) ranks #2. North Darfur: Localities Al Lait and KebkabiyaIn comparison to other localities within North Darfur, both Al Lait and Kebkabiya localities are remotely located. Even though the two localities host high numbers of displaced population, a lot of unmet humanitarian needs still exist in the two localities due to limited funding opportunities. Despite the efforts already put by various partners to support the local population, there is still a huge gap in access to drinking water, shelter and NFI as well as agricultural inputs that can contribute to saving lives of the IDPs and the SSR. Existence of both the SSR and the IDPs within their host communities is exerting pressure on the existing limited resources such as water points due to competition between the displaced population and the host community members over access to drinking water. The land for crop cultivation is fertile both in Al Lait and Kebkabiya localities therefore there is need to support the target group with agricultural inputs for short-term production. Kassala: Localities Wad El Helew and Gedaref: Localities El FashagaAccording to UNHCR, an estimated 46% of the refugees in the camps live in extreme poverty. Family poverty and lack of livelihoods expose refugee children to protection risks, including child labour, early marriage and dropping out of school. In addition, there is a need for semi-permanent family accommodation, the provision of basic hygiene and household items, especially for new arrivals, and child-friendly and safe housing. In 2020, there will be a continued high demand for drinking water infrastructure and sanitation facilities, such as household latrines, the extension of water pipes and accessible tapping points. In addition, there is a need for support for short-term food production and food use and processing (preservation) to help meet frequent supply shortages. The living conditions of the host communities are equally dire as the majority of refugee camps are located in areas of Kassala and Gedaref with IPC Level 2 (stressed) or 3 (crisis) acute food insecurity (FEWSNET Sudan Food Security Outlook Feb-Oct 2020). In addition, the project also targets the needs of the Ethiopian refugee influx in the areas of WASH, SFNI and Protection. The aforementioned conditions make the target group particularly vulnerable to diseases, especially in view of the current COVID-19 pandemic in relation to which on 16th March 2020 the Sudanese government declared a national health emergency.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The project will address the needs of refugees, IDPs and vulnerable host communities through a multi-sector response. In the area of WASH the project aims to improve the access to save and dignified sanitation and improved access to safe and sufficient water supply. Hygiene and COVID-19 awareness raising is part of this component. In the area of FSL, the project aims to provide timely food support and enhance the self-reliance of refugees by providing training in agriculture and livelihood activities. In the area of SNFI, the project aims to provide refugees with Refugees and asylum seekers are provided with adequate shelter solutions and essential NFI support. Refugees, IDPs and host communities alike are targeted for protection sensitization while the project supports the establishment of child friendly spaces and women space as well as distribution of cloths to the most vulnerable among the Ethiopian refuge influx. Under the area of energy, the target population´s access to clean sustainable energy sources (fuel-efficient stoves) and solar lighting in public spaces is improved.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The intervention area is characterized by resource pressure and degradation of natural resources. Water is a key natural resource which is scarce in all the targeted areas. The project aims to enlarge access to save drinking water through rehabilitation/construction of water sources and reduce the potential for deforestation through promotion of fuel efficient stoves among the target groups. Access to clean energy is further improved by providing solar lighting in public spaces.
