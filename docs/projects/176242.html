Project Summary

Food Agriculture and Disaster Management (FADM) is a national NGO working in Upper Nile, Warrap, Northern Bahr El Ghazel and Western Bahr El Ghazel States and is currently implementing WASH, Nutrition and livelihood programs funded by UNDP. OCHA and USAID/RRF.
Food Insecurity persists in the locations targeted, with the majority of those affected females from especially female headed HHs. Food Consumption Score and HH hunger indices indicate a dire need for humanitarian support to avert a slide of the population into worse food insecurity levels. Over 50% of the populations in these areas are projected to be under IPC phase classification of 3 and above in the first quarter of 2021

The main drivers of food insecurity have been identified as protection and safety issues in accessing livelihoods, loss of livelihoods, disease, and economic challenges, lack of inputs, asset ownership, and erratic weather patterns culminating into floods amongst others.
The project will target persons through cluster objectives CO2 (Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19 (compulsory use of new SOP & guidelines) and CO3 (Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19 (compulsory use of new SOP & guidelines)
FADM plans a 12 month response targeting 8,000 people with food security and livelihood support inline with cluster frameworks and plans, with a focus on reaching women, the elderly and people with disability.

1 - Needs

Malnutrition continues to be a challenge in the locations of Western Bahr El Ghazel States (Wau) with levels of acute malnutrition are attributed to severe food insecurity, poor access to health and nutrition services, high morbidity, extremely poor diets, poor sanitation and hygiene services. In the projection period of April to July 2021, an estimated 7.24 million people (60% of the population) are likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. Multiply displaced IDPs have significantly higher levels of food insecure households (over 53%) than host communities (35%). Female-headed households also have higher prevalence of food insecurity (45% versus 33% for male headed households), a possible indication of gender-based vulnerabilities. At the household level, food availability is now expected to be lower than previously projected due to crop damage from Fall Armyworm which has spread among nearly all the counties as well as damage of seedlings by floods that have affected vast locations in Western Bahr El Ghazel States. Loss of livelihoods, economic challenges, lack of tools and inputs, post harvest losses, livestock diseases have all contributed to the dire food security situation. Annual Consumer Price Index (CPI) increased by 88.5 percent between June 2019 and June 2020; the increase was mainly driven by high prices in non-food items. Households in both urban and rural areas remain unable to afford the minimum food basket. The latest economic update indicates a significant decrease in the number of households that receive income from casual (skilled and unskilled) labor in Western Bahr El Ghazel States according the latest FSNMS. Disease outbreaks such as AWDs have had significant effects of food security. Livestock conditions are also impacted. Changed migration patterns due to conflict, insecurity, and concentration of animals in small areas have raised concerns about disease outbreaks, reduced access to resources and veterinary extension services, as well as increased tensions between herders and farmers. Importantly, the terms of trade between livestock and cereals have drastically declined across all markets, undermining purchasing power of agro pastoralists in Western Bahr El Ghazel States. Food consumption scores as well as household hunger indices indicate variances from accepted thresholds immensely manifested in the lack of dietary diversification. Protection issues related to access to livelihoods further hinder access to livelihoods, with the project targeting mainly women and the elderly to foster HH production while reducing dependency of food aid.

2 - Project activities

• Carry out, participate and contribute to food security and other multi sectoral assessments including IPC assessments, FSNMS and other WFP, FAO, led FSL fact finding missions. • Enlist and enroll 8,000 persons in the locations of Wau Counties as direct beneficiaries of the project including 60% of these female, 40% IDPs, 5% elderly and PWDs. • In partnership with FAO and WFP, distribute inputs including seeds, tools livelihood kits and vouchers to vulnerable communities in Wau County. • In partnership with FAO, carry out emergency livestock initiatives amongst vulnerable communities involving vaccinations, case identification, surveillance, treatment and training of CAHWs amongst other initiatives.• Carry out plant and animal disease surveillance and early warning systems in collaboration with state line ministries and lead agencies.• Conduct seed fairs in locations with functional markets and apparent surplus produce to support the trading farmers and local economy while supplying locally adapted seeds to the beneficiaries.• Establish, rehabilitate and revive community owned post-harvest handling facilities.• Rehabilitate and revive markets through Emergency Market Initiatives aimed at strengthening livelihoods, directly targeting IDPs and vulnerable host communities.• Carry out trainings of agriculture workers, community animal health structures, county FSL structures, as well as integration of ongoing WASH and Nutrition programming in Wau County funded by UNDP, OCHA and USAID/RRF.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

FADM will provide public information to the beneﬁciaries about the programmes provided through local government, community outreach and health facility-based awareness and health promotion activities. FADM will consult with local authorities, community leaders, Department of Water and sanitation, CHD and health staff throughout the project implementation regarding decisions to commence, adapt or complete programmes. Emergency Rapid Team’s assessments include key informant interviews and focus group discussions amongst community groups. In static facilities, FADM will use annual household surveys to assess programme coverage and post exit interviews are used at the facility level for monitoring the quality of health service provision. Every staff member who joins FADM will get orientation on the Code of Conduct and has to sign upon it, together with a “Summary of Minimum Standards for the Protection of Women and Children Against Sexual Abuse and Exploitation” which form part of the Staff Guidelines.
