Project Summary

In line with objectives 1,2 and 3 of the HRP, the purpose of this project is to strengthen the socio-economic resilience of crisis affected vulnerable households and communities particularly women, youths and local vulnerable population by enhancing their agricultural production and income-generating activities through the food for assets (FFA) creation and development of agricultural value chains. These will improve the living conditions of beneficiaries, reduce their vulnerability, create the conditions for their smooth integration, rebuilding their productive capital and set in motion the process of early economic recovery. The Smallholder Agriculture Markets Support (SAMS) activity will be used as a lever to boost the local economy and absorb the surplus production that will be generated for other food assistance activities.
A. Objectives: Specifically, the project aims to:
1. Provide capacity building and skills (agricultural training in poultry, table birds, piggery, fishery agriculture, resilience building activities, and access to markets), as well as adequate equipment and agricultural input support, to vulnerable household, rural smallholders’ organization especially women and youths to facilitate their professional integration through activities related to food security and nutrition and the development of agricultural value chains;
2. Connect trained IDPs, returnees and vulnerable local population to market opportunities linked to food programs and networks (school feeding and other potential buyers), for improved income and livelihoods ; and
3. Strengthen the livelihoods and resilience capacities of vulnerable local population, particularly women and youths, to reduce, in a sustainable manner, their vulnerability to food and nutrition insecurity caused by various shocks, by focusing on the empowerment of IDPs, returnees and vulnerable local population through synergies between multiple sectors including agriculture, health, education, social protection.
B. Expected Results:
Immediate results:
1. The food and nutrition security of targeted IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host population is preserved during the lean season, through the distribution of food vouchers;
2. Targeted IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host population received adequate capacity building on local poultry; table birds, piggery, fishery and agriculture, as well as support equipment and agricultural inputs, access to reliable markets and take necessary measures to organize themselves as Economic Interest Groups (GIE);
3. Targeted IDPs, returnees and vulnerable local population have improved production and therefore increased work opportunities and income.
The project will target 1,500 beneficiaries 60% being women and girls and 40% being men and boys. Who are IDPs, returnees and vulnerable local population ( male and female, aged from 18 to 50 years), unemployed with limited economic prospects and weak ability to seize business opportunities; wishing to develop / consolidate their economic activity; Smallholder farmers groups, cooperatives, women groups and youth associations with a know-how that can be nurtured through training and eventually lead to job creation.
The targeted North West and South West Regions are considered areas of high food and nutrition insecurity. Overall, the project will support IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host population from food insecure households, without adequate training and reliable economic opportunities, tempted by migration and other negative coping strategies.
WESAD will continue to prioritize the quality of its assistance through improved targeting, conflict- and Gender-sensitive programming, increased community engagement, and increased Protection and monitoring activities. WESAD will ensure the convergence of its activities in the same communities and those of other partners around shared objectives for better complementarity for more impact.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

In 2023, an estimated 4.7 million people across the country will need humanitarian assistance. This figure, which increased by 20 per cent compared to 2022, means that one out of six people living in Cameroon requires humanitarian assistance. The HRP is targeting a total of a total of 508,480 people (273,568 in the NWR and 234,912 in the SWR) in need of food assistance. While the Northwest and Southwest regions continued to be affected by humanitarian crises, the food security situation and livelihoods activities also continued to be negatively affected by insecurity and the impact of climate change, including floods and irregular rainfall distribution. Conflict and insecurity continue to disrupt livelihood activities, markets, and trade across Cameroon’s Northwest, Southwest, and Far North regions, resulting in reduced access to food and income and heightened staple food prices, according to the Famine Early Warning Systems Network (FEWS NET).In the Northwest and Southwest regions, sporadic clashes between government forces and non-state armed groups continue to impede households’ access to income-earning activities and inflate production and trade costs. The price of maize remained elevated during September despite the seasonal harvest; specifically, maize prices increased by an estimated 14 percent in areas of Northwest and 35 percent in Southwest, FEWS NET reports. As a result, most households resolve to negative coping strategies like reducing the number of meals per day, eating malnourished food, begging for money to feed, stealing of food items, become delinquent, sex workers, increase in migration etc. Road insecurity has presented movement restrictions of both people and goods, contributing to increases in food costs and at times the spoilage of food when restrictions make transport and delivery to markers impossible and widespread loss of income has meant that families struggle to have sufficient income to meet their families’ food needs. All these factors create need to improve food security (access and use) of internally displaced persons, returnees and vulnerable local populations (men and women) to provide a sustainable source of livelihood. Recent discussions with the government have revealed that the return of displaced persons to their places of origin is among the most important priorities of the government but loss of livelihood and scarcity of food still remain a major challenge. The project will strengthen the socio-economic resilience of crisis affected vulnerable households and communities particularly women, youths, PLD by enhancing their agricultural production and income-generating activities through the creation of assets and development of agricultural value chains in line with the HRP: CO1, CO2 and CO3. This project activities will focus on; capacity building on agricultural livestock and fishery support (local poultry, table birds, piggery, fishery and agriculture) , access to market opportunities and complementary assistance through food vouchers during the lean season which are inline with the 2023 Cameroon HRP activities CA1: Provide unconditional food, cash transfers to IDPs and local vulnerable population, and CA3: (Agriculture) provide assistance to IDPs and vulnerable local population to improve their food and nutrition security through agricultural livestock and fishery support (local poultry, table birds, piggery, fishery and agriculture) on the Cameroon HRP. WESAD through this project will therefore provide assistance to IDPs, returnees, and vulnerable local population that have been affected by crisis through Food For Assets creation and will strengthen resilience-building activities as a key component of food assistance.Due to the powerful impact this project will have in the North West and South West Regions of Cameroon, these are optimal activities for any donor organization to partner up with WESAD.
