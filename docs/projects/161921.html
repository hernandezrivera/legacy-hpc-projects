Project Summary

Friendship in partnership with NCA will provide people-centred services to SGBV survivors and engage the wider community in primary and secondary prevention. Friendship will work with NCA to provide quality and compassionate SGBV case-management services. Case workers will receive training and information about referral pathways so they can refer to other providers of protection and legal support services. Since mobility for women and girls, and especially people with disabilities, is a major hindrance, Friendship/NCA will organise home-based discussions and advocacy sessions. Friendship/NCA will work with community ‘changemakers’ and traditional birth attendants serving as community advocates, thus ensuring local ownership. Regular sessions will be conducted to build their capacity in facilitating community conversations and provision of safe referrals. Furthermore, Friendship/NCA will deliver comprehensive community-based psychosocial support, focusing on creating peer networks with women and girls at risk and connecting survivors to social structures, such as support groups for intimate partner violence survivors. Frienship/NCA will continue working with BBC media action as a strategic partner to reach the wider community with culturally appropriate information on SGBV. Discussions with different groups in the community will address sensitive topics such as early marriage, intimate partner violence and modelling healthy relationships.

Needs

The Rohingya crisis continues with more than 900,000 Rohingya refugees living in 34 congested camps in southern Bangladesh exacerbating the vulnerabilities of women and girls. The scale of the refugee influx has put great strain on the host population in one of Bangladesh’s poorest districts leading to tension in sharing services. Since arriving in the camp in 2017, women and girls continue to be at risk of SGBV, exploitation and trafficking. According to the 2019 GBVIMS report, 78% of the SGBV cases were perpetrated by an intimate partner in the form of physical assault (52%), psychological/emotional abuse (23%) and denial of resources, opportunities and services (16%). Harmful cultural norms create barriers to women and girls to access services with fear of stigmatization and retaliation either from the perpetrator, his family and/or the community. Sexual violence is rarely reported, and rape and forced/early marriage cases are often solved at home by elders which explains why only 1% of the reported cases are of early or force marriage. This calls for more strategic ways to create awareness and find allies in the community to encourage help-seeking behaviours. Survivors of SGBV are at a greater risk as access to essential psychosocial services is currently limited, with nearly 56% of sites lacking required services. Friendship/NCA aims to respond to the latest reports of suicidal behaviour in a structured manner that integrates mental health as part of protection measures for women, men and adolescents at risk. In 2019, only 13 adolescent girls received safe delivery services at the Friendship birthing centre and health centres in camp 11 and 12. More adolescents seek information on menstrual regulation when they are already pregnant or after trying unsafe ways of abortion at home, this paired with restricted movement has denied most adolescent girls a chance to enjoy their sexual and reproductive rights.
