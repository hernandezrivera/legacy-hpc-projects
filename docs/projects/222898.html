Project Summary

UNHCR will strive to ensure that Rohingya refugee and host community children aged 3 to 18 years can access safe, inclusive, and equitable quality education opportunities by continuing existing programs in registered camps and adolescent interventions across the refugee camps in Cox’s Bazar. This will involve maintaining existing learning facilities and WASH facilities, establishing additional facilities based on Pearson’s recommendation, recruiting, training, deploying teachers, and procuring teaching and learning materials. Considering the Government of Bangladesh (GoB) directives on the non-use of the Bangla language and the resumption of the resettlement program targeting Registered Refugees, UNHCR started the implementation of the Pearson Edexcel curriculum, which the RRRC authorizes. The implementation of the Pearson Edexcel Curriculum will be in a phased approach, starting with KG and Grade 1 in 2024 and will be scaled up in upper grades in 2025.
To ensure the provision of quality education, UNHCR will collaborate with the British Council and The Pearson Team to facilitate an English language acquisition program; comprehensive Teacher Professional Development, including subject-based training, will be provided for all teachers from registered camps to ensure educators achieve the required language and pedagogical competencies. The continuation of regular training on cross-cutting issues (e.g., child protection, gender and disability inclusion, gender-responsive pedagogy, PSEA, and MHPSS) will also be provided. The project will recruit 50% female teaching volunteers, train, and deploy to ensure gender parity and focus on disability inclusion through identifying CWD, ensuring accessibility to the learning facilities, and providing individual support for those most vulnerable. Additionally, two months of certified training on pedagogy will be continued to train 1000 teachers from a range of education partners. In addition to mainstream education for 17,195 refugee and host community children, UNHCR will continue providing Early Childhood Education (ECE) for 2300 children aged 3-4 years in registered camps through community-based Learning facilities aligning with sector standards. Additionally, UNHCR, jointly with BARD College (in partnership with BRAC University), will implement a Language, Images, and Analytical Thinking (LIT) youth empowerment and upskilling program intended to promote the English language and soft skills related to analysis and communication, including basic ICT skills and cultivate leadership skills for those who have previous lower secondary level education but not attending in either MC or registered camp secondary schools. After completing the LIT course, students will have the opportunity to move either into the bridging program or skills training program. The bridging program is the only opportunity that could offer higher education accessibility and create a pipeline for third-country pathways. Moreover, adolescents, especially girls who have completed the highest available level of education, shall be organized into groups through literacy boost activities, which will boost their life skills and literacy skills and keep them safe.
UNHCR will continue ensuring community engagement in the overall project cycle through regular community consultations, monthly parent meetings, and monthly school management committee and Education Support Groups (ESG) meetings while maintaining Age, Gender, and Diversity (AGD) principles. UNHCR will continue its host community support by maintaining and operationalizing the existing community technology Access, renovating classrooms and WASH facilities, and providing classroom furniture and supplies. To ensure social cohesion and peacebuilding, the project will observe different days, including 16 activities, literacy days, and refugee days, and organize annual cultural events and sports competitions.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The Inter-Sector Need Assessment (ISNA) Report 2024 demonstrated that 59% of school-aged (3-18 years) children are attending any form of education, and access to education for Rohingya children in the camps remains challenging. In Nayapara and Kutupalong registered camps, only 40% of school-aged learners are attending. Most school-aged learners from the registered camps are not attending schools due to a lack of an accredited curriculum, while in other camps, 24% of respondents indicated that their adolescent child is either of menstruating age (9%) or too old to attend the learning facility (LF) (15%). Additionally, The Multi-sector Need Assessment Report 2023 indicated that the lack of appropriate curriculum is the main reason (65% of respondents) for not attending school in the registered camps. The lack of qualified and trained teachers remains a major barrier in providing quality implementation of the Pearson Edexcel curriculum. A teacher competency assessment (using the British Council Apps) result demonstrated that only 22% of teachers have B1 level English proficiency, with this challenge further amplified amongst female teachers. Whilst all teaching and learning materials are in a non-native language (English), teaching in a multilingual setting is very challenging, especially for facilitators/teachers with fewer qualifications and limited training. Additionally, several consultations with stakeholders and study findings demonstrated that the education sector partners providing ongoing professional development training, including UNHCR, led to providing a two-month-long certified training on pedagogy for teachers. However, there is a need to take the Myanmar language acquisition program and scale up training on pedagogy and subject-based training in other camps. In addition, UNHCR has received reports of abduction, fear of armed groups, forced recruitment, early marriage, teen pregnancy, child labor, and children engaged in harmful practices. The ISNA report indicates that 7% of children do not feel safe while they are traveling, and the vast majority of them are afraid of abduction (32%) and armed groups (21%). The consequences of the above could lead to student dropout and mental health and well-being challenges, making it difficult for children to focus on lessons and make the necessary progress to advance their education. As per the education sector dashboard (as of 30 September 2023), less than 1% of children with disabilities are enrolled in learning facilities, while the target was at least 3% for 2024. These reports indicate a need to improve access and identification of children with disabilities and ensure learning facilities and educators are well prepared to meet the needs of this group. Additionally, most (44%) of children not enrolled in the learning facility (LF) reported a lack of interest in enrolling. Among those, the majority are adolescents aged 15-18 (92% of girls and 70% of boys are not attending). Thus, this highlights the need to prioritize education activities for this group, focusing on transferable skills and life-long learning opportunities. UNHCR will work with its partners and communities to remove barriers to girls' education and promote gender equality in education by setting only girls' sessions. At last, certification and clear pathways for higher education remain significant challenges that need cross-border and regional engagement.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project will strongly keep the following FOUR PRINCIPLES in all of its activities. The project will strongly prevent and minimize any unintended adverse effects of its intervention as much as possible, which can increase people’s vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. Access to assistance and services will be ensured proportionally to need and without discrimination, gender, disability, or age-related barriers. It will pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services, e.g Persons with disability, elderly, children, etc. Specific objectives for IPs are included for teacher training on key protection areas (such as Child Protection, PSEA, GBV) to ensure safety and dignity are prioritized and maintained. IPs will ensure PSEA and child protection referral mechanisms will be in place, and awareness-raising activities will be conducted with students, parents, and teachers on how to access these. UNHCR will monitor this through its multi-functional reviews in conjunction with protection colleagues. Each learning facility will have a PSEA focal person (including one female). Child Protection will be mainstreamed throughout the project by training and sensitizing the project and partner staff. Community-based CP and GBV mechanisms to monitor, identify, and address CP and GBV concerns will be supported and strengthened, and community/parent support groups will help monitor, report, and refer to identified protection cases within education interventions. Safe learning spaces will be provided, and facilitators/instructors will be trained in CP principles and ways to identify and refer cases. SEL and life skills interventions will equip girls and boys with key knowledge and skills to help them mitigate, prevent, and respond to GBV.

2.3 - Age & Disability

UNHCR will provide education interventions for children, adolescents, and youth ranging from 3-18 years, tailoring support to meet the needs of each specific group. UNHCR's IPs will continue School Management Committee (SMC) and Community Education Support Groups (CESG) made up of a range of community members of a range of ages, following age, gender, and diversity (AGD) principles, who meet every month. For children aged 15+ years, adjustments will be made for them to participate in age-appropriate and need-based Language, Images, and Analytical Thinking (LIT) and Bridging youth empowerment and upskilling programs that enable them for their learning continuation for higher education or other shorts of livelihood/labor pathways. UNHCR acts to protect children (persons who are under 18 years of age) by responding to their specific needs and the risks they face. It protects and campaigns against all forms of discrimination; prevents and responds to abuse, neglect, violence, and exploitation; ensures immediate access to appropriate services; and provides durable solutions in the child's best interest. UNHCR will ensure its IP will follow sector guidelines on new classroom designs which ensure disability access to students. Teachers will receive integrated models on disability inclusion as part of their induction and training packages. UNHCR will request IPS to use Washington Group questionnaire tools to identify CWD, and act appropriate to make sure the classrooms are inclusive and refer the individual cases to the specialized organization for assistive devices if required. The project will emphasize community-based learning /blended learning approaches to minimize access barriers, especially for the most vulnerable including children with disability, adolescent girls as well as students of religious believe from religious institution.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

UNHCR has provided a helpline (+88 017 359 3300) for Rohingya to speak directly with a dedicated UNHCR staff on various issues and also has a UNHCR BGD Cox’s Bazar and UNHCR BGD Dhaka email line which is used by many refugees to raise their concerns. Additionally, UNHCR recently developed a comprehensive feedback and response mechanism, titled “Centralized Automated Information Processing Platform- CAIPP” to address the issues raised by the beneficiaries within a specific timeline. The CAIPP will be used across the intervention to ensure AAP in the project implementation cycle. The issues raised are then channeled to various sections within UNHCR to provide an appropriate solution. The community are consulted periodically through focus group discussions (FGDs) and key informant interviews (KIIs) to identify their opinion on the services provided, identify the areas of improvement as well as they are consulted in project planning and evaluation process. UNHCR monitors and evaluates its intervention through mid and year-end multi-functional reviews in which the parents/SMC members/ CESG members play a vital role in providing feedback. IPs will also have their own complaints and feedback mechanisms in place (such as suggestion boxes at each school) to ensure their programs are adaptive and responsive to the needs of the community. The IPs will organize and engage SMC/CESGs and ensure monthly meetings and capacity building sessions are maintained for this group to encourage ownership by the community and to inform them of the community’s rights with regards to education.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

UNHCR is fully committed to gender-responsive safeguarding and has policies and practices to reduce the risk of, prevent, and respond to SEA by UN personnel, implementing partners staff and other relevant persons who are involved in humanitarian response. UNHCR will continue training and refreshers for teachers on PSEA and SGBV. UNHCR IPs will run sex-disaggregated schools for secondary education and sex-disaggregated LIT and bridging centered for adolescents and ensure at least 50% female teachers (where possible) and 100% female teachers for ECD to increase female participation and community acceptance. Separate WASH facilities for boys and girls will be available at all schools and menstrual hygiene and sanitation emergency kits and bins will be available in all learning facility latrine facilities. In 2025, UNHCR will continue to monitor the safety of its learning centers through the continuation of its PSEA/gender-based violence (GBV) Safety Audit which was started in 2022 and being conducted across all IPs' learning facilities.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Each school has a small-scale tree plantation plan, considering the available space. Furthermore, a solar panel will be set up in each school to provide renewable energy instead of electric supplies. All teachers, project staff, and SMC members will receive training on DRR. Furthermore, a community awareness session on DRR will be continued during and before the monsoon season.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

UNHCR, through its partners, keeps a budget for a contingency plan to procure teaching-learning materials and tiedown learning center materials for use during emergencies, including monsoons or any other form of natural disaster. Additionally, an alternative plan for business continuity engaging implementing partners and community has been developed for learning continuation during monsoon or any other form of political instability.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

The LIT and Bridging courses equip refugee students with highly transferable skills to promote self-reliance and to prepare a pipeline for potential third-country pathways programs. After completing the bridging course, students may opt to participate in 360 hours of skills training, equivalent to Level 1 of the Myanmar National Qualification Framework (MNQF) or the ASEAN Qualification Framework (AQRF), in collaboration with UNHCR livelihood partners.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

The project intends to renovate/reconstruct classrooms and build gender-segregated toilets for host community schools. Additionally, four Community Technology Access Centers will continue to provide opportunities for adolescents and youth to participate in basic and advanced ICT training, which is a priority for the host community's development plan.
