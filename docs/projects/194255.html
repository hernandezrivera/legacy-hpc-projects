Project Summary

Ruweng Administrative Area currently hosts about 124,000 Sudanese refugees and at least 4,868 internally displaced persons(IDPS) who have moved from Benitu due to the flooding. This has posed increased challenges to the already stretched service delivery point in the area. Available records from the Area Ministry of Education indicates that 83 learning facilities in the Administrative Area are currently not operational due to lack of paid teachers. Additionally safe learning environment remains a huge challenge with over 90% of all learning centers in the area are temporary structures.. This intervention is geared at providing education for learners in the host communities of the Ruweng Administrative Area (Parieng). Below are the goal and three principal objectives of the proposed intervention:
Goal: Increase access to inclusive quality education for host community children especially girls and children with disabilities
Objective1:
Improve equitable access to safe and protective education services for 4550 emergency affected IDPs, returnees, spontaneous refugees, and host community children including children with disabilities and youth (girls and boys).
Objective 2:
Improve the quality of gender and disability-inclusive education services and teachers' capacity to protect the wellbeing of all children and prevent the negative impact of the emergencies on learning environments.
Objective 3:
Strengthen/enhance the education system’s response capacity of the school community and national/local education actors to mitigate the impact of existing emergencies on learners, teachers, and communities
Target:
Some of these refugee learners will be hosted by within the existing refugee schools located in the Ajuong Thok and Pamir refugee camps and will therefore together with the refugee learners directly benefit from this intervention
• 4,264 learners (2,132F, 2,132M) at ECD, primary & secondary levels
• 76 (20F, 56M) teachers and head teachers
• 200(100F,100M) executives and members of PTAs/SMCs/BoGs
• 10 (3F, 7M) Area Education leadership members

1 - Needs

According to the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview on South Sudan anchored by OHCA, a deterioration of people’s physical and mental wellbeing, living standards and coping mechanisms is expected in 2022. Findings from the various data review processes indicate that 8.9 million people are estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2022, compared to 8.3 million in 2021. This increase is largely driven by compounded shocks triggered by continued conflict, widespread flooding, deepening food insecurity, inflation, high food prices, and lack of access to basic services. An accompanying info graphic of this overview of needs amongst other things reveals that about 50% of classrooms and 60% of latrines in South Sudan are not accessible by children with disabilities (CwDs). On the protection front, the assessment shows early pregnancy as the top most amongst reasons why girls do not enroll in or drop out of schools while 64% of schools reported that children do not receive meals amidst a slash of food ration by 50% for refugees in various locations including Jamjang. Emerging from a prolonged civil conflict coupled with an influx of refugees across most of the north, central, west and eastern parts of the country has made an already dire situation worst. The Ruweng Administrative Area(Parieng) in the far north of South Sudan in the greater Unity States becomes one of the worst affected areas of South Sudan as it currently plays host to more than 124,000 Sudanese Refugees from the South Kondofan region of the Republic of Sudan. In 2021, an assessment conducted in Jamjang camps and host community identified over 600 children with varying special needs, the majority with physical impairments. These numbers are expected to increase in 2022 with new arrivals and outreach activities, requiring specialized educational services and material support to facilitate access to inclusive learning opportunities. Most teachers are untrained refugee incentive teachers and lack the professional competency required to identify and support children with special learning needs.. Girls education is also a very critical challenge as most girls do not transcend primary education largely due to early marriage and teenage pregnancies. This creates a serious gender disparity at the secondary level of schooling. The presence of these refugees in the stated figure in two big refugee camps and a transit center around the borders with Sudan has placed a severe strain on the already limited capacity and resources in the area. LWF is currently providing education for about 26,000 refugee and host community children at various levels—ECD, primary including ALP and secondary. In addition to the challenges this is posing to the already scarce local resources, there are at least 4,868 internally displaced persons (IDPs) have also moved from the Bentui side of the boundary into the Ruweng Administrative Area due to flooding. About 90% of all learning centers in the area are temporary structures. About of a total of 83 learning facilities in the administrative area according to local education records are currently not operational due to lack of paid teachers. This speaks to the massive shock of the prolonged conflict in the country. For example, out of the eight schools in the Jamjang County which is the seat of the refugee response, only four are currently operational. These are operational as a result of LWF’s support in hiring and incentivizing teachers and providing teaching and learning materials amongst other support. Most of the current response has been focused on refugees. Being fully cognizant of the tenants of conflict sensitive education in emergencies (EiE) programming, the organization intends to foster education for host community children at ECD, primary and secondary levels. Some of these refugee learners are in existing refugee schools in the Ajuong Thok Pamir camps and will dierctly benefit together with the refugee learners there

2 - Project activities

In order to fully implement this proposed intervention to the maximum benefit of the target host community learners, teachers, parents and community members, below activities will be undertaken in three separate categories. The first set/category of activities are designed to support increased access/enrolment of learners into existing, established and rehabilitated learning centers, the second category captures those activities that support the quality improvement of the education services provided. Thirdly, the last category then embodies those activities that are designed to support capacity/system strengthening and sectoral governance and management I. Enrolment/education access Activities • Enroll children in Pre-primary, Primary and secondary schools/learning centers: A back to school campaign will be planned and conducted as a way of boosting enrolment and getting back to school all children of school going age who are not in school. A proper registration process will then proceed to proper track the enrollee by age and gender. • Establish temporary learning spaces and classrooms. Following the project baseline survey, a technical assessment will be carried out by a LWF hired engineer to ascertain any construction of temporary learning spaces. This will then clearly establish areas where construction is needed or rehabilitation. • Rehabilitate temporary learning spaces and classrooms: Like that of the construction, a technical assessment will be carried out by a LWF hired engineer to ascertain any learning spaces that need rehabilitation. This will then clearly establish areas where construction is needed or rehabilitation. • Establish gender segregated latrines, water points: an assessment on the existence and sufficiency of WASH facilities especially those of latrines but also water points and hand washing facilities. Following the findings form this assessment, a determination will then be made into locations where these facilities are needed to be established from scratch. • Rehabilitate gender segregated latrines and water points: Like that of the establishment of gender segregated latrines and water points, LWF will also conduct a technical assessment on the existence and sufficiency of WASH facilities especially those of latrines but also water points and hand washing facilities. Following the findings form this assessment, a determination will then be made into locations where these facilities are needed to be established from scratch. • Distribute or provide sanitation and hygiene materials (hand washing soap, sanitizers, masks, etc.) and PPE for students and teachers: In order to remain compliant with the COVID-19, LWF will procure and distribute personal protective equipment (PPE) to include those of face masks for teachers and learners, hand washing soap, senitizers and other necessary protective materials in addition to regular awareness raising on the prevention protocols.
