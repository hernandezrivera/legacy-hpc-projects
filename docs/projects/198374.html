Project Summary

UNHCR will work through a Community-Based and Right-Based approach in Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) targeting 145,121 (52% women and 48% men) IDPs living in camps and camp-like settings. The project has four main components i.e. (i) Enhancing community participation, site level coordination and information management, (ii) Support site planning and site development, (iii) Capacity-development and (iv) Quick impact Projects (QiPs). UNHCR aims to build on the gains made in 2022 in fostering localization through partnership with the relevant local government departments, national NGOs and civil society to enhance meaningful community ownership and self-management of the IDP sites in the targeted districts. The CCCM projects will complement UNHCR’s Protection, Shelter and NFI programmes to provide a holistic intervention in the targeted locations. CCCM services will integrate centrality of protection, age, gender equality and diversity in programme planning and implementation through regular consultation (FGDs) with all community groups i.e., women, men, youth, PWDs and other most at-risk groups and involve the IDPs and host community in the project implementation. UNHCR CCCM will target IDP sites that the local administration considers villages (also referred to as Aldaires or Relocation sites). The Aldaires or Relocation sites are considered part of the local municipality plan and provides an opportunity for a local integration solution to IDPs.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

In close coordination with the CCCM Cluster, local administration and the communities (host and IDPs), the CCCM project will primarily target 145,121 IDPs (52% women and 48% men) in the district of Montepuez, Mueda and Nangade. The project priority activities include:• Enhancing community participation, site level coordination, and information management- UNHCR will support community self-management structures in the IDP sites by establishing and or: (i) support established community structures, working towards inclusive representation with an Age, Gender and Diversity (AGD) lens. (ii) conduct intention surveys, Focus Group Discussions (FGDs), household level surveys, and service mapping to identify needs and priorities of women and men in their diversity that inform service delivery and response, (iv) support registration, headcounts, and identification of persons with specific needs to facilitate reception, plot allocation and assistance delivery.• UNHCR will establish Community-Based accessible and adapted Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CBCFM) in the sites and reinforce existing one. CFMs will adopt both static and mobile outreach. Site Management Committees (SMC) and sectoral committees will be established where absent and promote an AGD representation in decision-making. • Site planning and development- UNHCR will support small scale care and maintenance needs that mitigates protection risks ensuring safe and dignified living conditions while fostering meaningful community ownership in the process through regular community consultations in planning and implementation. Needs assessment will be conducted through FGDs with different age groups and persons with specific needs, undertake perception surveys and map community capacities, and conduct household level surveys targeting all groups (women, men, youth and other most at risk-groups) to facilitate tailored response. These activities will include but not limited to: (i) care and maintainace of site infrastructures (ii) opening/ upgrading access roads and drainage systems in the sites to mitigate flood risks during the rainy seasons and facilitate service and assistance delivery, (iii) support connection to the main electricity grid- providing streetlights to enhance safety and security in the sites, and (iv) upgrade the communal facilities i.e. reception centres for IDPs, community centres, recreational facilities, and support Quick impact Projects (QiPs). • Capacity-development- continued capacity-building on camp management and protection mainstreaming targeting partner agencies; national NGOs, local government, IDPs and host communities.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

UNHCR maintains a strict zero-tolerance towards sexual exploitation and abuse. Through the code of conduct, project staff are continuously sensitized on the Prevention from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) while ensuring that any sexual exploitation and abuse complaints are channelled through the appropriate internal mechanisms.UNHCR staff and its partners are trained on humanitarian principles and the key elements of the centrality of protection: do no harm principle, safety and dignity, effective access to services and assistance, accountability with the affected populations. UNHCR has developed a protection monitoring tool for identifying risk profiles and collecting protection incidents, with indicators of age, gender, and diversity. UNHCR will ensure CCCM programmes mainstream protection delivery throughout its project cycle. UNHCR will also ensure CCCM interventions undertake due diligence including on issues around Housing Land and Property (HLP) associated disputes and risks are mitigated and in close coordination with Protection Cluster and HLP AoR. In addition, to guarantee the dignity of people, UNHCR’s CCCM will ensure timely and quality services are delivered while taking into consideration the specific needs of the affected populations, the respect for their culture and religion and, respecting their freedom of choice.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

UNHCR considers the affected communities as the centre of its operation. UNHCR programming will build on the findings of the 2022 Participatory Assessment (PA) exercise conducted across the Northern Mozambique and the numerous focus group discussions and meaningful community consultations (women, men, youth, PWD and other at-risk). The PA exercise involved consultation with IDP women, men, boys, girls, persons with specific needs, host community, partner agencies; I/NGOs, CSO, and government officials. Throughout the programme cycle in 2023, UNHCR CCCM will conduct regular gender responsive community discussions and assessments to engage affected population in the selection, design and implementation of programs and to further ensure they provide feedback to the monitoring and evaluation process, undertake household survey to identify gaps at household level, support UNHCR protection monitoring activities and GBV safety audits in ensuring an evidence-based data is gathered while at the same time having an active and meaningful participation of women, boys, girls and other at risks groups. UNHCR will enhance the established complaint and feedback mechanisms by deploying dedicated CCCM personnel to follow-up on CFM cases, undertake regular review of the CFM functionality and data analysis and where absent create such mechanisms to ensure complaints from all population groups are recorded, referred, and addressed while feedback is provided timely to the affected population. UNHCR CCCM will continue to deploy both static (information desks) and mobile teams to support outreach activities including key messeging on services available in the targeted locations. UNHCR will ensure that persons with disabilities have access to all relevant information by preparing gender responsive key messages in multiple and accessible formats.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

UNHCR will build on the gains made in 2022 including partnership with Humanity Inclusion in ensuring tailored assistance to persons with disabilities is provided. UNHCR CCCM in close coordination with the protection team and specialized partners, will undertake regular registration and identification of persons with specific needs including persons with disabilities to ensure assistance is provided timely and where required referred for specialized assistance. UNHCR will ensure that persons with disabilities have access to all relevant information by preparing key messages in multiple and accessible formats. UNHCR CCCM will also ensure site infrastructure are accessible to persons with disabilities including opening of pathways (feeder roads) within the sites and ensure community centres include disability friendly entry/exit points. Community centres and community meetings will be in places which are accessible to persons with disabilities.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

As part of its CCCM strategy, UNHCR ensures that the communities are sensitized and engaged on the protection of the environment, emphasizing their responsibility for the local environment where they settle. Through community-led initiatives, UNHCR will prioritize the reforestation of sites by providing the necessary resources required. IDP and host community led initiatives in mitigating environmental impacts including flood risks mitigation measures such as constructing drainages and tree planting will be supported. Community engagement in eco-friendly activities will also aim to enhance peaceful co-existence between the IDPs and the host communities with key messeging provided throughout project implementation
