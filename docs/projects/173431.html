Project Summary

The educational programming will specifically target Rohingya and Host Community (HC) children that have limited access to schools in the COVID situation focusing on:
a) children 3-5 years old;
b) adolescent girls and boys between the ages of 13-24 years; and
c) children and youth with disabilities.
The program will provide foundational education to children using community based facilities, aligning with the education sector and government approved framework on digital literacy while also providing innovative digital learning initiatives in the camps as well as providing technical support to the sector through the YWG and TTT.
For continuing education during the pandemic, in addition to use of community based facilities, the program will deliver education through digital Home-based Learning (HBL) initiatives in a holistic manner by ensuring community buy-in through advocacy with parents and community leaders.
The HBL program has a two fold approach with the use of gamified applications aligned with LCFA and MCP core competencies for numeracy and literacy as well as development of audio and video materials and e-assessment tools that can be deployed by sector partners to monitor HBL. The HBL efforts will target Rohingya and HC children and youth including disabled children.
To address youth development, the program will provide targeted and specific vocational training according to needs based on mapping of all ongoing Sector coordinated Youth-Activities and Trainings, including numeracy, literacy, vocational and livelihoods skills, which will be compiled on an online digital knowledge platform; which will include a repository of contextualized ToTs and help develop the standardization for Youth-Activities in coordination with the YWG, creating a harmonized cross-sectorial approach for youth initiatives, providing market linkages within the sector and in the HC, with a core focus on social innovation and personal development based on humanitarian principles as its foundation.
Ultimately, the program will support strengthening coordination between Sector, the YWG and Education partners to move towards a harmonized/standardized approach to deliver education opportunities.
The cost of beneficiaries is not limited to the provision of services, but also takes into account the budget that would be going towards coordinating efforts across sectors via needs assessments and content development, specifically to support Sector Working groups, such as TTT and YWG.

1 - Identified Needs

Rohingya Community Needs Despite significant progress during the last two years in providing safe and systematic access to learning opportunities, more than 30 percent of Rohingya children and youth aged 3-24 years old still require access to education. 69 percent of Rohingya refugee households reported at least one 5-17 years old aged child not benefiting from any learning opportunities. (JRP-2020) Furthermore, an alarming 83 percent of the adolescents and youth aged 15-24 years old don’t have access to any educational or skills development activities, including some whose limited education was interrupted by displacement from Myanmar. Education in emergencies provides children with a positive sense of purpose and equips learners, of all ages, with the life skills, knowledge, values, attitudes and behaviours that foster critical-thinking, empathy and the ability to build communities; while also providing, life-saving information aimed at strengthening disaster awareness, hygiene, and other survival skills. In the absence of education and other opportunities, children, adolescents and youth are more vulnerable to the risk of early marriage, child labour, trafficking, sexual and genderbased violence, exploitation and abuse, and increases the likelihood of engaging in harmful activities including criminality CHILD PROTECTION & GBV Mainstreaming in Education (Refugee Needs) Equitable consideration of all children’s protection, safety, and other vulnerabilities, including the specific needs of children with disabilities, will facilitate the implementation of key elements of protection mainstreaming: safety, dignity and avoiding causing harm; accountability; participation and empowerment; and meaningful access. (JRP-2020) The need to scale up structured psychosocial support and access to effective mental health support (MHPSS) for those most severely affected remains urgent. Gender-Based Violence continues to be a constant threat, especially to women and girls in the camps. The reported GBV incidents include rape, physical assault, sexual violence, forced marriage, denial of resources, and psychological abuse. According to the GBVIMS data, domestic violence constitutes 76 percent of total reported GBV cases. (JRP-2020) Adolescent girls, who are more vulnerable to child/forced marriage, trafficking, and sexual violence face heightened obstacles to access services. Host Community Needs The rapid influx of August 2017 has stretched the resources of the communities and schools of Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazilas, where enrollment and retention are among the lowest in the country. Only 60 percent of children and youth aged 12-24 reported having completed primary school in Teknaf and 75 percent in Ukhiya. (JRP-2020) One-quarter of households reported that the COVID-19 outbreak disrupted education. At the same time, 31% of households reported that school-aged children were not attending any formal learning before the COVID-19 outbreak. Households with secondary education and above were found to be significantly less likely to report out-of-school children. 27% of households reported loss or diminished access to education as an impact of the COVID-19 outbreak (MSNA-2020). COVID-19 Context Two recent policy briefs, ILO highlights the fact that migrant workers and refugees face specific challenges in accessing social protection, including health care and income security, making them more vulnerable to the health and socio-economic impacts caused by COVID-19. ILO calls for integrated approaches that include migrant workers and refugees in national social protection responses – based on the principles of equality of treatment and non-discrimination – to mitigate the effects of COVID-19 and support a swifter recovery. (ILO 2020) There is also a need for vocational training and home-based digital learning to upgrade their non-formal education system during Covid-19, with linkages to mainstreaming Child Protection and GBV.
