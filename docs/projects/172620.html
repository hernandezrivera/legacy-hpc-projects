Project Summary

The planned project will contribute to the Sudan humanitarian response plan focusing on addressing life threatening needs through timely provision of life saving food and/or cash/vouchers assistance to reduce mortality and morbidity of the affected people. Most vulnerable households will be provided with food kits or cash/vouchers to enable them access food to meet their minimum food requirements. Analysis of the situation in terms of gaps, capacities and market functionality will precede response in order to inform the project on the most suitable modality. This activity will precede and overlap with the food production activities in order to build an exit strategy that is based on self-reliance of the targeted households. Nutrition awareness messaging with special focus on food hygiene and diet diversity will be developed and shared with the target groups.
Secondly, the project seeks to address life-sustaining needs to improve vulnerable people's access to livelihoods focusing mainly on food production and related value chain. The project will promote activities that support production of food at household level. This will range from small backyard nutrition gardens to field-based staple and other crops production, food processing and preservation. The project will consult the target beneficiaries and other stakeholders in order to find appropriate farming methods and promote technologies that will allow those with limited access to land for production to participate. Nutrition information will be used to influence the type of crops that will be grown in order to contribute towards improvement of the household’s nutritional status. Food processing and preservation training will be conducted in collaboration with organizations such as Food and Agriculture Organisation (FAO) and the state Ministry of Production and Economic Resources and relevant local organisations to promote preservation of food to cover the normal food deficit periods. The project will use market-based approaches to provide target groups with agricultural inputs including inputs for reforestation . The food production activities will be preceded by training in the correct and improved practices using extension methods that support local structures. NRC will not replace the local extension system but instead work to strengthen especially the community level extension services through training of community based extension workers.
The project will be implemented in South Kordofan targeting new and old internally displaced persons (IDPs) and the most vulnerable host community households. The project will target households that are most vulnerable in terms of demographic and socio-economic characteristics. The project will engage with the target communities in deciding on the vulnerability criteria for beneficiary selection. NRC’s general selection criteria includes age, gender, disability and nutrition status. Children under the age of five, pregnant and lactating women, all gender types, the youth, women, girls, people with disabilities, the elderly and chronically ill people make up a special target group for NRC’s food security projects. The project will design in collaboration with the target populations, strategies for complaints and feedback handling in order to be accountable to the affected people and also to ensure recording and follow-up of sexual exploitation and abuse cases related to the project implementation. The monitoring and evaluation activities will also be designed to capture achievements not only on outcome and output indicators but also on target beneficiaries’ satisfaction of their engagement and treatment during project implementation. The project seeks to contribute to durable solutions by building self-reliance of the target groups so that they are ensured of an adequate standard of living, including at a minimum access to adequate food, water, housing, health care and basic education.

1 - Needs

According to the Sudan Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) report of November 2020, about 7.1 million people in Sudan are projected to face high levels of acute food insecurity between October to December 2020, an increase from the earlier projection of 6.4 million people made in June 2020. The projected quarter is normally comparatively better than others because it is the post-harvest period hence increased food availability. The Food Security and Technical Secretariat (FSTS) of the Sudan Ministry of Agriculture’s preliminary findings of the mid-season assessment for 2020/2021 agricultural season, indicated that the performance of the season was expected to be on average. However, the situation continues to be compromised by the macro-economic conditions in the country, where the prices of food and non-food household commodities increased by more than 500% compared to the five-year average. According to the World Food Program’s Market monitor of October 2020, the cost of a local food basket increased by 221% in October 2020, compared to the same period the previous year. This situation will continue to affect access to food by the most vulnerable households especially those affected by displacement due to conflict and flooding. Use of consumption coping strategies including reduced number of meals, reduced meal portions and consuming foods of less quality will compromise the nutritional status of the most vulnerable members of the affected households, specifically children under the age of five, pregnant and lactating women, the chronically ill, elderly and people with disabilities. South Kordofan state is The Norwegian Refugee Council conducted multi-sector rapid needs assessments in South Kordofan in October 2020 where food insecurity featured as the greatest need as households struggle to meet their daily food requirements. The acute food insecurity drivers in 2021 will include localized conflicts, climate shocks, pests and diseases, slow recovery from prolonged asset depletion, poor macro-economic conditions and sub-optimal market functionality. Food access will remain a big challenge which has always been exacerbated by the harsh economic conditions as a result of high inflation currently being experienced in the country. Food security and livelihood support will remain critical for most vulnerable populations to prevent asset depletion and reduce their adoption of negative coping mechanisms as well as reduce the number of people relying on food assistance for their survival in 2021. Household members, especially women, are exposed to negative coping strategies as they search for food and try to meet basic household needs. Chronic pattern of food insecurity has eroded livelihoods, disrupted social safety-nets and negatively affected the resilience of affected populations. This is compounded by already existing factors of low seed and tools access, mono-culture, flooding, dry spells and low area under production. As a way to cope, households (mainly female-headed households) engage in negative strategies such as reducing number of meals, eating less preferred foods, sending children to beg and selling livelihood assets. Income generating options are scarce and unprofitable due to inflation and most vulnerable households engage in casual jobs, selling firewood/charcoal and petty trade. With projections of more returns of both IDPs and refugees in 2021, if the peace agreement holds, the country needs to prepare for increased humanitarian needs in order to support the returnees. Their needs therefore will range from immediate food/cash needs to support to livelihoods establishment. Areas of settlement will require increased social and economic services in order to meet the increased needs.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Community mobilisation and sensitization activities will be conducted before start of activities in order to ensure information about the project activities reaches all community groups especially the minority gender groups, the elderly and people with disabilities. Safety net activities will be implemented to enable target households access food, diversify diet, and reduce negative coping strategies. Market based programming will be used to identify relevant modalities which will include food distributions, cash transfers and a mix of the two. The intervention will contribute and utilize the monthly market monitoring information coordinated by the World Food Program (WFP). This information will be used to adjust the modalities and value of transfers in order to prevent negative impacts on the local markets. Awareness creation on the use of cash and household gender dynamics will be addressed through consultative focus group discussions before and after distributions. This will be complimented by post distribution monitoring and establishment of a complaints and feedback mechanism. The activity will target recently displaced populations, food insecure households and vulnerable groups during the lean season to cover a percentage of their annual household food gap. These activities will prioritize localities in IPC level 3 and above. Complementary emergency food production: Households who are willing and able to participate in asset creation and public works will be targeted for conditional food and cash for asset interventions. These complimentary livelihoods activities will be preceded by basic knowledge and information regarding correct use of the inputs and kits. This will be done in order to promote correct usage of the inputs to increase production. Post distribution monitoring will be conducted complimented by participation in country wide assessments led by Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) i.e. Post-Harvest Assessments. This activity will cover locations in IPC 3 and above as per Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) objectives. The same locations will be targeted for activities that reduce dependency on food and emergency livelihoods inputs by engaging in activities that build the target households' capacity and strengthen their ability to produce food and earn income that will enable them continue with their livelihoods. Support to value chain addition e.g. milk, groundnuts and fish, preservation and marketing. Training on farm and off farm activities will include small food processing and preservation skills. Natural resources management and climate smart agricultural practices will be promoted in order to reduce negative impacts on the environment. Communities will be engaged in identifying negative impacts on the environment and come up with mitigation measures that will include but not limited to rehabilitation of gullies, forest regeneration and sustainable use of the natural resources. NRC will be responsible for community engagement and sensitization with consecutive meetings with local authority to ensure they take ownership of the projects for sustainability. The implementation methodology will be based on group formation for value chain activities. The project will promote the use of supply markets for agricultural inputs and services. This will be complimented by intensive skills training and knowledge transfer for relevant on and off farm topics. The project will target all gender and age groups that can engage in work but will have special focus on women and youth (girls and boys) to reduce their engagement in negative activities that have protection concerns. NRC’s main role will be to facilitation a linkage with private actors and beneficiary communities for sustainable transformation. All activities will be implemented through gender and protection lenses.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Assessments and field visits to South Kordofan conducted by NRC in October and November 2020 have indicated that the target communities are already depending on the natural resources for energy and income (charcoal and firewood), wild fruits and foods, farming and grazing of the livestock. The project plans to engage the target groups in food production, mainly agricultural activities. All these if they go unchecked can have negative impacts on the environment and continue to affect climate change. Therefore, the project by analysing the baseline practices that have impacts on the environment, has managed to understand practices that need not just awareness raising of their impact on environment but to engage with the affected communities in order to come up with mitigation measures that will enable reduction of the impact. The project will work with women, men, girls and boys, local leadership and authorities by engaging them in identification of the problems and designing the mitigation challenges. NRC had initial meetings for technical collaboration with the Reducing Emissions from Deforestation and Forest Degradation (REDD+) Unit that is under the Forest National Cooperation (FNC) which is part of the federal ministry of Agriculture and Natural Resources. The plan is to tape into their technical resources in supporting awareness raising and training of staff and communities in community-based forest management initiatives. The project will promote climate smart agricultural practices through its collaboration with the extension staff from the State Ministry of Production and Economic Resources. Food/cash for assets interventions in partnership with the World Food Program (WFP), will incorporate activities that contribute to environmental protection and conservation. Inputs for community mitigation measures such as reforestation, gully reclamation, and planting of fruit trees will be provided by the project after consultations with the target communities and authorities.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The protection environment is not sufficiently safe or stable. The project has therefore taken into consideration risk analysis and mitigation measures in order to reduce the impact. The target communities were involved in identification of protection and security risks in their locations and the analysis has been captured in NRC's area and country strategies. These risk assessments remain living documents as they are continuously updated during specific periods of the year and when certain events take place in an area. NRC practices safe programming across all its projects through training of project staff to be able to engage with communities in identifying protection issues for the different gender and age groups. Awareness raising of communities is carried out in order to enable them understand protection issues that can affect the different people by gender, age and displacement status. Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) training is mandatory to all NRC staff and this is cascaded to all casual laborers, incentive staff and project management committee. Basic do no harm principle and accountability to affected populations will be upheld ensuring all aspects of the project take into consideration the needs of the elderly, the sick, people with disabilities, women, youth, men and children. The project will set up complaints and feedback mechanisms in collaboration with the target groups in order to enable them report on issues including PSEA and other protection concerns. Two-way communication will be established between NRC and the target communities using diverse methods in order to capture issues from all groups.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

NRC conducts needs assessment before designing of any intervention in order to engage and capture target beneficiaries’ needs. The project will conduct community mobilization and sensitization sessions in order to inform the target communities about the assistance that will be provided. Feedback on how target beneficiaries prefer to receive the assistance will be recorded and used to inform the modalities and response strategiesThe project has a complaints and feedback handling mechanism that includes different ways of collecting information from target community members including people living with disabilities, those who are illiterate, women and girls. The mechanism provides for anonymous complaints and feedback to be captured and uses different media and methods that the affected can choose to use. The feedback mechanism will allow the project to adapt project assistance based on information received. Monitoring and evaluation activities will be able to measure if beneficiaries felt well-informed and respected during project delivery and their overall satisfaction with the project. The evaluation will reflect how the project tracked and responded to beneficiary complaints and how the feedback shaped the project delivery. NRC will contribute to food security cluster reporting mechanisms and participate in any other AAP efforts by the humanitarian sector including referral systems for SEA and abuse complaints.
