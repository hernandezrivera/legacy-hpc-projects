Project Summary

According to the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 8.7 million people currently require humanitarian assistance across NE Nigeria - a 10% increase since last year, and the highest ever recorded figure since current humanitarian operations began (excluding COVID-19).The majority of those in need of aid live in Borno State; and women and girls account for 20% of those affected by the crisis. The COVID-19 pandemic has further exacerbated the situation by placing additional strain on already weak services. Over 1.5 million people in NE Nigeria require Gender Based Violence (GBV) prevention and response services (HNO 2021). Women and girls, who are disproportionately affected by GBV, make up over 99% of the survivors of GBV incidents reported between January and June 2021 (GBV information management system mid-year report 2021). Additionally, 1.7 million children need access to core child protection services. These risks are further exacerbated by a lack of adequate services and facilities. An estimated 2.8 million people across NE Nigeria remain in critical need of sustained, equitable access to clean water and dignified hygiene and sanitation services (HNO, 2021).
In line with the 2021 Standard Allocation Strategy, the IRC will implement a 10 month integrated water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) and protection response across Borno and Yobe States in Konduga, Monguno, Damaturu, Potiskum and Gujba local government areas (LGAs). The core objectives of the project are in line with Sectoral objectives and priority approaches.
The proposed project will provide protection services for vulnerable internally displaced persons (IDPs) and host communities, including general protection support, protection monitoring and mainstreaming, GBV and Child Protection response services. The WASH component of the project will provide support across water and sanitation infrastructure as well as hygiene support. The project will build the capacity of new and existing community structures, to support implementation and sustainability.
The IRC will utilize well developed monitoring guidelines and tools to support project measurement. This will involve a variety of monitoring methods including quantitative and qualitative surveys customized to enable collection and collation of data for reporting and electronic data collection applications such as Kobo collect to enable faster capturing of data for post distribution monitoring and other assessments.

1 - Needs

The protracted nature of the conflict continues to exacerbate the negative effects of gender inequalities across Northeast Nigeria, with women and girls including children being disproportionately affected by the conflict and incidents of GBV. According the 2021 HNO, more than 1.5 million people—IDPs, members of the host communities, and returnees—need GBV prevention and response services across north-east Nigeria. All forms of GBV continued to be underreported. Reporting an incident of sexual violence (rape or sexual assault) is hindered by the risk of stigmatization, retaliation, shame or marginalization and oppression from the community, as well as lack of survivor-centered service provision after referrals. Physical assault, sexual violence, denial of resources opportunities and services, and psychological and emotional abuse are among the violations most frequently reported, recurrently perpetrated by intimate or former partners (domestic violence). Girls and boys under the age of 18 years are also at risk of GBV, mainly occurring within the context of child sexual abuse and child/early marriage. The GBVIMS 2021 mid-year report shows that women and girls were the victims of more than 99% of reported GBV incidents between January and June 2021. 70% of the survivors are adults, whilst 21% are children. Additionally, 2% of the survivors are women and girls with disabilities. On average, one in five reports of GBV alleges child survivor, and three in five reports concern the context of intimate partner violence. Overall, women, girls, children, and persons with disabilities (PWDs) remain the demographic groups most at risk of GBV in Northeast Nigeria. According to the MSNA, at least 1% of the surveyed households report having children (either gender) under 17 married off in the three BAY states. Adolescent girls, female or child-headed households, and orphaned girls living with foster carers are at particular risk of GBV. Nigeria ranks 139th out of 156 countries in the Global Gender Gap Report 2021, clearly showing the magnitude of gender-based disparities that continue to place women and girls and children in the crisis-affected region at risk. For many survivors of gender-based violence (GBV), reaching available services such as the police, the judiciary and healthcare requires an untenable investment of time, resources, and determination to overcome socio-cultural barriers.

2 - Activities

The IRC will provide comprehensive case management for survivors of GBV (including psychosocial support services) in women and girls' safe spaces. This will include social networking activities like recreational activities and literacy and numeracy classes. Additionally, life skills classes will be provided for women and adolescent girls, covering sexual and reproductive health, GBV education, safety planning and communication skills. This activity will target 350 clients in Monguno, 175 clients in Potiskum and 175 clients in Gujba. GBV prevention and Response Training: The IRC will target key government stakeholders for capacity building training focused on the prevention and response to GBV. This will target 30 stakeholders each in Monguno, Potiskum and Gujba LGAs. Dignity kits and psychosocial support materials will be provided to vulnerable households based on need. The activity will prioritize GBV survivors, widows, lactating mothers, single mother and women with disabilities. 200 clients will be targeted in Monguno, 115 in Potiskum and 85 in Gujba. Community outreach: The IRC will leverage existing community structures and networks such as women's action groups (WAGs) to conduct community outreach through information sessions. This will focus on GBV prevention and awareness of available services, targeting Monguno (1050), Potiskum (1050) and Gujba (900) LGAs. Also, The IRC will identify safe and unsafe places in communities and engage communities at the local level to identify and execute risk reduction activities. This will include periodic safety audits across activities in Monguno, Potiskum and Gujba LGAs. : Engaging Men in Accountable Practices (EMAP) groups will be formed, engaging more individual men in personal transformation to mitigate the risk of violence against women and girls. Additionally, Start Awareness Support Action (SASA!) groups will be empowered to lead outreach, awareness-raising and action development and implementation to improve the condition of women in their communities. This will target 70 clients each in Monguno and Gujba LGAs.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

This project is designed to reduce gender inequality by supporting women and girls to recover from violence and abuse, through engaging community activists to challenge social norms which perpetrate GBV and by increasing women and girls’ social networks, skills and decision-making power. Dedicated prevention and response services for women and girls will be provided at women’s safe spaces, providing accessible assistance tailored to women’s and girls’ needs. Utilizing EA$E and EMAP evidence-based approaches, this project will positively affect relations between girls/women and boys/men, promoting more equitable gender dynamics while minimizing any adverse effect upon women and girls. Ongoing community sensitization and meetings with stakeholders and community members will ensure local support for this project and minimize risks for women and girls’ participation. Established women’s networks will be leveraged to continue to advocate for the rights of women and girls in their communities. The IRC with support from the protection team will train those involved in the WASH component to strengthen and promote a shared understanding of protection mainstreaming, child safeguarding principles, prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse. The IRC will uphold the do no harm principle by ensuring that all services and structures are meaningfully accessible, safe, promote beneficiary dignity and promote ownership of the project by the beneficiaries through constant community engagement/participation and functional feedback processes throughout the project period. Through FGDs and key informant interviews (KIIs), the IRC will collect information from different groups (women, men, girls and boys). The IRC will also gather information through existing structures (i.e. community leaders, PAGs, etc). This will help the IRC assess the specifics that exist among the needs of the population, depending on age and sex. This regular protection risk analysis will enable the IRC to identify protection risks specific to women, men, girls or boys. Protection responses will be specifically tailored to reduce the vulnerabilities of the specific segments of the population, while simultaneously increasing their resilience.
