Project Summary

The purpose of this project is to promote optimal nutrition practices, prevent and treat acute malnutrition in the most vulnerable population groups i.e. children under five years, pregnant and lactating mothers through implementation of community management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) and maternal infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN) activities in Rumbek Centre and Pibor counties. The project will be implemented from Jan 1, 2021- Dec. 31, 2021 and shall benefit the target groups in IDPs, returnees and host population in Rumbek Center and Pibor counties. PI will ensure people most affected by a crisis and marginalized groups (like those with disability, minority ethnic groups and other groups due to economic, political, social and cultural factors) effective participation in the planning and execution of the project.
This project will focus on the management of children with moderate and severe acute malnutrition and pregnant and lactating mothers with acute malnutrition. It will be implemented using the national CMAM guideline/ Guidelines for Nutrition Service Delivery in COVID-19 Context, MIYCN guideline and other necessary national guidelines. Children with SAM with medical complication will be referred to nearby stabilization centre (SC). To ensure quality and good coverage of the program, CNVs and mother support groups (MSG) will do continuous screening, referral and follow up cases at community level. Furthermore, preventive activities like MIYCN counselling, health, nutrition and Wash education /promotion, backyard gardening, complementary food cooking demonstration and distribution of wash and other preventive (like Mosquito net) NFIs will be conducted at nutrition sites and at community level. As part of treatment protocol, all children (if there is enough supply) with SAM will be screened and treated for malaria according to the national protocol / or referred to health facilities. Furthermore, preventive kits (jerry cans, Mosquito nets, soaps) shall be provided to mothers /care takers of children who are enrolled for treatment in OTP/TSFP sites as these children are at higher risk of getting infected and also to promote proper personal hygiene and sanitation practice.
All children who will be discharged cured from OTPs will be enrolled to TSFP programs for full nutritional recovery and those deteriorated will be linked to SC. Besides, if a child in TSFP is nutritionally deteriorated, he/she will be enrolled/referred to OTP/SC program and those who recovered shall be given protection ration and referred to other (FSL, BSFP) services and will be provided counselling. When there is deworming and vitamin A supplementation campaign, this project will support to ensure children under five in the county get the services in collaboration with SMOH/CHD, UNICEF, WFP and the health partner in the county. Furthermore, PI will participate in FSNMS, SMART and other joint assessments happening in the counties of operation. PI will implement OTP and TSFP programme together in Rumbek Centre (20 nutrition sites) but only TSFP programme in Pibor county (21 static sites). In addition to the above mentioned interventions, PI will implement BSFP in five payams of Pibor county. In total, this project will provide quality treatment service for 29155 children 6-59 months (Pibor: 10,830 MAM children 6 - 59 months; and Rumbek Centre: 4230 SAM and 14545 MAM children 6 - 59 months) and 23,613 Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW), (Pibor: 10,401, Rumbek Centre: 13,212) in TSFP programme in Pibor and OTP and TSFP sites in Rumbek Centre counties using the national CMAM guideline. Furthermore, 45754 mothers /care takers (Pibor:25397, RC: 20357) will receive MIYCN counselling service. This project will be implemented in integration with health, WASH, and food security and livelihoods activities in Pibor and Rumbek Centre counties. Besides, COVID-19 preventive measures will be in place in all service provision activities.

1 - Needs

Based on the Integrated Phase Classiﬁcation (IPC) for Acute food insecurity and acute malnutrition (AMN) analysis that was conducted in South Sudan from October 26th to November 16th, 2020, 6.3 million people or 53% of the people analysed were estimated to face high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above) period from October to November 2020. Moreover, in the projection period between December 2020 and March 2021, estimated 5.8 million people or 48% of the people and from April to July 2021, 7.2 million people or 60% of the people analysed will likely face high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above). In the same report, Pibor was among the 6 counties with some estimation of populations in IPC Phase 5 (others were, namely: Akobo, Aweil South, Tonj East, Tonj North and Tonj South). The Famine Review Committee concluded that Pibor payams (Gumuruk, Pibor, Lekuangole and Verteth) where PI operates TSFP were in IPC Phase 5 (Famine Likely) food insecurity for October 2020 – November 2020 and the same classification is projected (IPC Phase 5 (Famine Likely)) from December 2020 - July 2021. Moreover, Lakes state, where our county of operation found (Rumbek Center) has 42% and 57% of its population were estimated to face high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above) during the projection periods of December 2020 and March 2021 and April to July 2021 respectively. Based on the FSNMS round 25 ﬁndings, Malnutrition rates estimates remained at serious levels in the post-harvest season since 2017. However, the increase of one percentage point from 2018 assessment to December 2019 is not significant. Overall, the prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) and Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) in December 2019 was 12.6 percent and 3.3 percent respectively, reflecting a slight increase over the same period in 2018 when the prevalence rate was 11.6 percent for GAM and 2.3 percent for SAM. Several aggravating factors, explain the high malnutrition rates and these include; high morbidity rates in almost all states, compounded by poor quality of diet with only 4 percent of children achieving the required MAD (minimum Acceptable diet) and low WDD; The other aggravating factor is flooding that contributed to internal displacement in some locations, disrupting households’ livelihoods and water. Malnutrition situation in Pibor County continued to be high as shown by the recent SMART survey, May 2018, post-harvest season. The survey found a high GAM rate of 20.7% [17.6- 25.0, 95% C.I.], of which the prevalence of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) was 4.9% [3.1 - 7.4, 95% C.I.] based on weight for height z scores and/or edema. The overall GAM prevalence is indicative of a critical malnutrition situation based on the WHO standards. Moreover, the service coverage is low in Pibor as assessments show. A MUAC assessment conducted by ACF in Pibor and Likuanguke Payams between June 4 - 13th (2019) found that only 24% of children identified as SAM and MAM were in existing programs. Meanwhile the PLW MUAC assessment found a proxy GAM rate of 36%, showing a high incidence of acute malnutrition beyond critical thresholds. Another follow-up MUAC survey by PI in March 2020 also showed low service coverage; only 36% of the detected moderately malnourished children were found enrolled in TSFP program while 64% were not enrolled. Given the continued food insecurity, instability that hamper food production, the poor health infrastructure, frequent diseases outbreak and relatively low optimal breast feeding and complementary feeding practice, there is the need to continue large scale humanitarian response including nutrition services in Pibor and Rumbek Counties. This can help to prevent morbidity and mortality due to acute malnutrition. Plan International South Sudan by this proposal therefore expresses its commitment to continue strengthening, scaling up and improving quality of its nutrition program in Pibor and Rumbek Ce

3 - Project indicators

Monitoring and reporting activities are planned and included in this project. Monitoring of the activities and budgets will be undertaken at all levels of project implementation. Project nutrition staff will be responsible for day to day management of the project with support from the M&E officer, Program Implementation Area Managers and nutrition coordinators at county and head office levels. The standard nutrition programme (CMAM and MIYCN) reporting and recording tools will be used at each level (i.e. nutrition site, county and Plan’s head office in Juba). Furthermore, PI will select key indicators and monitor the progress throughout the implementation of the project Reports, (weekly and monthly) shall be collected from each nutrition sites (BSFP, TSFP) by nutrition assistants and Field monitors/supervisors who shall provide the services and will be compiled by senior nutrition officers /nutrition coordinators at county head quarter. There will be continuous analysis of collected reports, community feedback and correction measures will be taken accordingly. Monthly project performance trackers will be filled and shared by the M&E officers to inform the project management on the performance of the project and quarterly review meetings will be conducted to share successes, challenges and lessons learnt. Quarterly, midterm and final project reports will be written and submitted to donor (s) as per the agreement. In addition, Plan International will submit biweekly and monthly reports to the nutrition cluster and SMOH. Plan international will also give updates on the project performance/progress when requested. PI will do joint monitoring and supervision of project activities with UN agencies (WFP, UNICEF), partners implementing similar programs (JAM, MEDAIR), SMOH and NC (sub cluster. 7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

PI has code of conduct on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse. Every staff recruited will be given orientation on code of conduct and will sign it to confirm they will abide by it. Furthermore, there will be continuous sensitization. Also, beneficiaries will be oriented about PI code of conduct and they will be notified any concern should they encounter. There are established complaint and feedback mechanisms in place.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

PI will ensure fair representation of different beneficiaries group in the community. Special consideration will be given for people with different vulnerability criteria to be represented in the community level feedback meeting.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

There will be regular meeting with beneficiaries and the beneficiaries will be told about the project and their complaint and feedback will be collected through direct discussion, using suggestion book, suggestion box and complaints will be addressed immediately.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Plan International will ensure that a mechanism has continued for the beneficiaries to participate in all processes including the design, implementation, monitoring and close-out stages of all projects. As part of ensuring accountability, communities will have means to provide feedback and complaints about the project continuously through the existing different mechanisms. Community can inform positive and negative aspects of the project, which can help to improve projects. This will be achieved through use of suggestion book, suggestion boxes, community feedback meeting with selected community leaders/members at each nutrition sites during service provision days and afterwards by Monitoring and Evaluation officer. Furthermore, communities can directly notify Plan staff or pass their issues through the community leaders about the project and user satisfaction surveys will also be conducted once a quarter to collect feedback. Also, Plan will provide information about services provided at nutrition sites and the requirements for getting services to beneficiaries during the routine morning health, nutrition and WASH education sessions.
