Project Summary

Providing Shelter and NFI emergency assistance for the most vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host community families in conﬂict-affected areas of Afrin district, reaching a total of 10000 conflict-affected individuals. Through the provision of winterization and NFI assistance to IDPs in informal camps and settlements as well as host community.
NFI, and Winterization support will comprise two related but distinct components. First, supporting IDPs living in camps, informal settlements and collective centers to survive the harsh winter months with the provision of 1000 kerosene or diesel heater kits with three months cash for fuel, supporting 5,000 individuals. Second, essential NFI in-kind kits and vouchers will also be distributed to the same population and to other new IDPs or returnees, targeting 5,000 individuals.

4 - Needs

The 2022 HNO estimates that in 2022, around 5.92 million people will be in need of shelter assistance and 4.91 million in need of NFI support in Syria, an increase of 5% since 2021 despite an improved security situation. It is estimated that of the 6.92 million people displaced, 3.73 million reside in substandard, damaged, and/ or inadequate shelter conditions, raising serious health and protection concerns. These are primarily being driven to move to such locations due to conflict, security concerns, and economic deterioration. In Afrin district there are a large number of IDPs seeking adequate shelter in urban centers, residing in unfinished/damaged buildings. At the same time, many returnees reside in inadequate shelter options with many homes damaged due to years of violent conflict and the inability to undertake essential maintenance on housing due to years of displacement. In Afrin, half of the population is estimated to be living in damaged or inadequate shelters (HNAP 2021) with 47% of structures damaged, destroyed or demolished (REACH 2021). The number of IDPs living in NWS's ten formal camps is above 200,000 while many more IDPs are scattered in urban and rural areas – in informal settlements, collective centers and in urban centers – across NWs. As the economy deteriorates and prices rises, the majority of basic NFIs continue become increasingly unaffordable to vulnerable IDP and host families

5 - Implementation of activities

This project comprises 2 core activities. First, for Sector Objective 1 (Provide life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support), this project will provide (Sector Activity 1.1) core NFI kits to 5000 IDP, returnee and vulnerable host community households in need (1000 HH). This kit will be provided either in-kind or through a voucher modality. The second activity also under Objective 1 (Sector Activity 1.2), comprises seasonal support in the form of winterization kits (heaters, jerry can, fire extinguisher) and cash for heating fuel for three months. This support will be provided to 1000 vulnerable households (5,000 individuals). Kits will be distributed in November and cash for fuel starting December.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

The SNFI cluster Protection Risk Analysis identifies over 50 key protection risks specific to the sector. Based on this, andIHR's own internal protection analysis, identifies the key risks as follows: inter-community tension because of inclusion/exclusion, unclear selection criteria or varied assistance packages by different actors; increased tension between host and IDP communities; and risks particularly associated with distributions. To mitigate the first risk, IHR adopts SNFI cluster guidance in its vulnerability assessments and its assistance packages to ensure harmonization with other actors. Similarly, the IHR hires local staff (M&F) to carry out activities to ensure all staff have strong local knowledge, extensive information networks and good community acceptance. This ensures conflict sensitivity as our staff have an awareness of the social, cultural, and political dynamics of the area and are representative of the program participants. In terms of tension between host and IDP communities, IHR conducts extensive consultations with both groups before commencing any activities. IHR does not target based on status, and thus supports IDP and host communities. To mitigate risks during SNFI distributions, they will be carried out early in the day to ensure beneﬁciaries can come and go with daylight. Women and men have separate lines at distribution points that are staffed by male and female ﬁeld workers. Besides giving Special consideration for PWD during distribution.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The IHR's Code of Conduct and Associated policies are consistent with the six core principles of IASC Task Force on PSEA in Humanitarian Crises. Risks related to this project have been identified in a separate multi-sectoral strategy with appropriate mitigation measures identified. The latter includes, but is not limited to, the appointment and training of PSEA focal points in all project sites and offices and trained with supported from the Syria PSEA network. It also includes the use of the PSEA networks developed checklists for the observation of operations to identify, prevent and mitigate potential SEA and SEA risk factors in ongoing field activities.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks- Further escalation of the conﬂict, or deterioration in the security situation, in NW as a result of any of the following: -Escalation in the ongoing conflict between SDF and Government of Turkey or GoS, extending beyond the current areas of operation. -IS resurgence with extension of attacks and activities to Afrin and surrounding areas. -Tension between authorities and communities or inter-community conﬂict, jeopardising access to affected project areas. -Markets affected by continued currency devaluation, border closures or escalation of conflict, leading to shortage of supplies and market price fluctuations. -COVID-19 pandemic escalates, new lockdowns restrict access. Assumptions- This project will be able to access beneficiaries, assess their specific needs and implement the program accordingly. There will be no signiﬁcant shifts in control over key locations in Afrin district - This project will be able to access beneﬁciaries, assess their speciﬁc needs and implement the program accordingly - Markets continue to function, internal and regional borders remain open and goods are available locally/regionally. Mitigating Measures- Strengthen operational resilience. In the event of increased insecurity in an area, shift operations to nearest unaffected base of operations. -Flexibly shifting modalities as required depending on cross-border access, the state of markets and availability of key goods. -COVID-19 SOPs, activity level SOPs. -Build local capacity and staff
