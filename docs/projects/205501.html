Project Summary

In response to the continued education needs in South Sudan, Safer Community South Sudan (SCOSS) is proposing the following interventions to expand quality and equitable education response services to vulnerable children, adolescents and youth populations in IDP camps and host communities in Mundri West, Mundri East, Mvolo and Maridi counties of Western Equatoria state. The overall objective of the proposed one year Education in Emergency intervention is to contribute towards improving equitable access to safe and protective education services for all children (3-17 years) including those with disabilities. The complexity of the operating environment requires multifaceted approaches that are tailored to each location including; enrollment and retention of children in schools; establishment/rehabilitation of education infrastructure, including temporary learning spaces (TLS) and gender-segregated WASH facilities; and enhancing and building the capacities of teachers, SMCs/PTAs and education authorities.
The project will contribute to the education cluster sectoral objective 1 and 2 in Western Equatoria state through supporting the enrolment of 4,273 (2,137G and 2,136B) children including those with disabilities in pre-primary, primary and secondary schools in the targeted crisis affected counties; construction or/and rehabilitation of 26 Temporary learning spaces in line with Inter-INEE's Minimum Standards for Education in Emergencies and Standards in Education Cluster Strategy; construction or/and rehabilitation of 20 gender sensitive WASH facilities (latrines) that are accessible to all children including those with disabilities; enhancing and building the capacities of 90 (16 Female and 74 Male) teachers on Education in Emergency, subject matter and pedagogical skills to support provision of educational services to children; distribution of 10 learning and recreational kits for adolescents/youth and 20 ECD kits for children (3-6 years) which will support the provision of Mental Health Psychosocial Support Services (MHPSS) for children in schools; and training 16 (12 Males and 4 Females) SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management, governance, climate change and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR). In addition, to contribute to the psychosocial and physical well-being, minimize school dropout, and promote retention, the project will distribute dignity kits for adolescents and youth school girls for menstrual health management. The project will also prevent, mitigate and respond to protection concerns for children in schools through provision of age appropriate IEC materials and establishing referral pathway mechanisms in the schools. In addition, teachers and staff will be trained on child safeguarding, GBV and CP risk mitigation; MHPSS and referral mechanisms and Back to Learning (BTL) Campaigns will be conducted aimed at addressing forced and early marriages, teenage pregnancies, and other negative practices hindering girls from achieving their educational potentials.
Therefore, 4,379 people (Maridi county 1,088 [534B, 534G, 4W and 16M], Mundri West 1,089[534B, 535G, 4F and 16M], Mundri East 1,100 [534B, 534G, 5W and 27M] and Mvolo county 1,102 [534B, 534G, 6W and 28M]) have been targeted to benefit from the project.
Importantly to note, at the core of this project design, SCOSS has taken into consideration gender, age, disability, and diversity mainstreaming to cater to the needs of the diverse community groups targeted. Accountability to Affected populations has also been taken into consideration through the establishment of complaints and feedback mechanisms (CFM) to receive, process, and respond to concerns from the community on the education services and conducts or behaviors of SCOSS staff. Risk analysis was also conducted to identify potential areas of risk that may negatively impact the organization’s capacity to fully implement the proposed project and mitigation actions were incorporated into the project.

1. Needs

3.4 million girls and boys between 3-17 years of age are estimated as in need of education and over 42,000 teaching personnel needed to support provision of education to the learners(South Sudan Education Cluster Mid-Year Dashboard [As of 24th July, 2023]). This has been attributed to the increasing effects of climate change, food insecurity and localized conflict exacerbating the existing barriers, including inadequate school infrastructure, costs associated with accessing quality education, untrained education personnel and irregular and insufficient teacher payments (HNO 2023). With the already dire humanitarian crisis in south Sudan, the influx of nearly 293,000 people, mostly South Sudanese returnees, since the start of Sudan crisis in April (South Sudan Humanitarian snapshot, as of September, 2023) has further worsened education needs for children in South Sudan. With a particular focus on Mundri West, Mundri East, Mvolo and Maridi counties of Western Equatoria State, the education of children has been further impacted by unprecedented food insecurity levels, floods, economic hardships and protection concerns including gender based and sexual violence among others, which have been rampant. These counties are classified in crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity (IPC Acute Food Insecurity And Malnutrition Analysis July 2022 - July 2023), meaning, low-income households spending on food hinders their ability to support their children’s education through tuition fees, uniforms and school materials. In this situation, as families face mounting food insecurity, girls are increasingly called upon to care for younger siblings so parents can work or seek food, forcing them to miss or drop out of school and as a result, girls’ education are further deprioritized, worsening the education gender gap. In addition, parents with school-aged children bear the brunt of the burden by adopting negative coping mechanisms, such as child marriage, child labour that prevent school enrolment and attendance. Importantly to note, floods due to heavy rain in Mundri West County in Western Equatoria State destroyed houses and household properties and displaced an estimated 3,200 people in Kotobi, Mundri and Amadi in Mundri West County (South Sudan Humanitarian snapshot, as of September 2023). In addition, the IRNA report in Mundri West County highlighted that classrooms, WASH facilities and scholastic materials (exercise books, text books, pen and pencils, blackboards) of children were totally destroyed in schools affected by floods (IRNA on flood in Mundri West County, Western Equqtoria state, as of 18th September 2023). The lack of access to safe drinking water and sex-segregated latrines in the affected schools increases the risks of communicable diseases and resulting in irregular attendance for both children and teachers. The report highly recommended construction of temporary learning space (TLS); provision of scholastic materials for children and construction of WASH facilities (latrine) in the affected schools.An Education assessment conducted by Safer Community South Sudan (SCOSS) in Mvolo, Mundri East and Maridi counties identified inadequate latrines and handwashing facilities in schools, inadequate teaching, learning and recreational materials, few qualified teachers, delayed payment of teachers’ salaries and inadequate learning spaces/classrooms as the main issues facing education. The report also highlighted that 70% of the schools assessed never received dignity kits for girls and female teachers. The lack of menstrual health management poses a significant barrier to the attendance, learning and progression of female students. Besides, these locations have inadequate organizations providing education services. According to OCHA, there are 2 Education partners (1 is SCOSS) with operational presence in Mundri West, 3 in Mundri East, 2 in Mvolo and none in Maridi (South Sudan Humanitarian Operational Presence [3Ws, August 2023]).

2. Project activities

The project will provide access to quality education services through supporting the enrolment of all children (3-17 years) in pre-primary, primary and secondary schools in the IDP camps and host communities targeted. In order to increase enrolment and sustain attendance in school, particularly for girls, the project will also establish school gardens and provide children with school meals. By providing healthy balanced meals, the school feeding programmes will improve overall micronutrient status and reduce the prevalence of anaemia in primary school-aged children and adolescent girls hence limiting the negative consequences on health, nutrition and education thus lowering the barriers to accessing and completing education, especially for girls. The project will also prevent, mitigate and respond to protection concerns for children in schools through provision of age appropriate Information and Education and Communication (IEC) materials (i.e. posters, leaflets) and establishing referral pathway mechanisms in the schools. In addition, teachers and staff will be trained on child safeguarding, GBV and CP risk mitigation; MHPSS and referral mechanisms to strengthen the protective environment for children in schools. And Back to Learning (BTL) Campaigns will be conducted in all the locations as part of the enrollment drive for all children aimed at addressing forced and early marriages, teenage pregnancies, and other negative practices hindering girls from achieving their educational potentials. Temporary learning spaces will also be established or/and rehabilitated by the project in line with Inter-INEE's Minimum Standards for Education in Emergencies and Standards in Education Cluster Strategy. In order to reduce the risks of communicable diseases and improve retention of children in schools, the project will construct or/and rehabilitate gender sensitive WASH facilities that are accessible to all children including those with disabilities. In addition, assistive devices will be distributed for children with disabilities to enable them to carry out daily activities and participate actively and productively in school life and achieve their fullest potential in academic performance. Notably, the project will also distribute learning and recreational kits for adolescents/youth and ECD kits for children (3-6 years) which will support the provision of Mental Health Psychosocial Support Services (MHPSS) for children in schools. SCOSS’s own experience testifies to post-traumatic distress occurring in high numbers in children living in IDP camps and deprived host communities. The MHPSS through Physical Education (PE) sessions will facilitate full recovery, restore a sense of normalcy, and promote the cognitive, physical, social and emotional development of children affected by crisis. In addition, to contribute to the psychosocial and physical well-being, minimize school dropout, and promote retention, the project will distribute dignity kits for adolescents and youth school girls for menstrual health management. Hence, this will create an enabling environment for girls’ enrollment and retention in schools. The project will also enhance and build the capacities of teachers on Education in Emergency, subject matter and pedagogical skills to support provision of education services to the learners. In addition, the project will also train the SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management, governance, climate change and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR). The project will also support volunteer teachers with financial incentives aimed at attracting and retaining them in schools.
