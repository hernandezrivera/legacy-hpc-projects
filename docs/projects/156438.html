Project Summary

Homs city has undergone a huge amount of damage due to mortar, shelling and looting. Many homes are structurally sound but uninhabitable. This project aims to work with local community to rehabilitate damaged private homes of host and previously displaced households in order for them to live in safe and adequate housing. The project will take 11 months and target 300 Households with basic rehabilitation including plumbing, rubble clearing, electrical work, installation of windows and doors etc.

Needs

Due to an overall lack of affordable, available and adequate shelter in Syria, there are an estimated 4.7 million Shelter PiN in 2019. Throughout the conflict, shelter and infrastructure have been damaged on an enormous scale which is far beyond the scope of humanitarian actors alone. With an expected increase in returnees this year, there are many factors which inhibit or prohibit displaced families from returning to their communities. According to the Shelter Sector figures in HNO, 61% of PiN are at a severity level 4 for shelter. The 2019 HNO states that a significant proportion of the population are living with at least one shelter adequacy issue and that 6.2 million long-term IDPs continue to face a range of barriers regarding return. Approximately 680,000 households cannot carry out necessary repairs to their homes, most frequently because they cannot afford the materials and/or professional assistance required to carry out the rehabilitation. When asked why people are unable to make repairs in their community, cash (and the lack of it) consistently was the top reason for inability to make repair. The documentation/authorization, materials and professional labour are all available, but unattainable due to a lack of cash capital on the part of the household. The overall impact of lack of income and high prices result in a considerable number of households currently living in rental accommodation unable to afford rent costs, thus leading to increased vulnerability. Additionally, HLP issues remain a challenge on many fronts. In the 2019 HNO Shelter severity comparison, the number of PiN with major (level 3) need severity in Homs more than tripled in 2019 compared to 2018, clearly indicating that a shelter rehabilitation project addressing inadequate yet structurally sound shelter would bring much needed benefit to the community. The most commonly cited shelter inadequacies by communities are those which can easily be addressed through a shelter rehabilitation project (eg lack of lighting, lack of insulation from cold etc). In Homs, WRGs partner identified shelter rehabilitation as one of their primary needs in the community. Many previous Homs city residents have been living in displacement for multiple years and are now seeking to return back to their cities and apartments. Upon return they find their houses uninhabitable (burnt out, blasted windows/doors, damaged walls and ceilings, etc) and looted down to the pipes in the wall and electricity sockets. This project addresses the needs of displaced and host communities living in inadequate housing. It serves to address their needs by bringing substandard private living accommodation up to standard and enabling people in need to live safely and with adequate shelter protection. WGBM all have distinct needs in this project, as do individuals of different age groups. All genders and ages are subject to protection risks due to a lack of adequate locks and/or security due to broken windows and doors. GBV, Child Marriage and provacy concerns are issues affecting WGBM but particularly women (single & adolescent), girls, mothers (especially heads of households), babies, PwD and the elderly. Child and FHH may be at extra risk due to their reduced ability to perform repairs. Additionally, FHHs are subject to potential protection risks and cultural stigmas to have an outside man entering the household to perform repairs. The privacy and dignity of women must be highly considered in a project such as this where multiple families may share the same areas. Even in households with just 1 family, adequate privacy to WGBM is not always provided. Women and girls (specifically adolescents, young and middle aged adults, mothers, pregnant & breastfeeding) are particularly exposed issues of privacy in these instances. Incidents and tensions occur due to a lack of privacy, often affecting girls and women more than men and boys. Needs Assessment Sources: SNFI HNO 2019 Partner Findings

Implementation of activities

Activity: 2.1 Support to sustainably repair/rehabilitate housing and related community/public infrastructure and facilities to owners/tenants/host families (can include: materials, labour, etc.). World Relief Germany (WRG) will work closely with the National Baptist Church of Homs in order to implement this project. Beneficiaries will be targeted according to vulnerability criteria and the repairs to be made with be documented. A Chief Engineer will work with a team of skilled and unskilled labourers in order to carry out the necessary repairs in each household, including but not limited to electrical work, plumbing, carpentry, installation/repair of windows/doors/walls/ceilings, painting. Repairs and rehabilitation will be carried out by paid construction workers under supervision and to private homes of IDPs, returnees and host families. These individuals can be property owners, tenants and host families and HLP checks will be made for all households. The project will take place between January – November 2019 with thorough monitoring and evaluation during the project cycle.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk/Threat Mitigation Measures: Conflict resumes in project area: Due to the fluid nature of the conflict it is not impossible for fighting to resume in multiple areas. However, Homs city has been stable for a significant period of time now and is deemed to stay that way for the foreseeable future. Should conflict resume, partner, staff and beneficiary well-being would be paramount and any project activities would be delayed or ceased should there be any danger to stakeholders. Partner cannot complete project: This project has been selected specifically and tailored in order to meet the ability and capacity of the partner. Support has been and will continue to be given in order to equip them with everything they need to implement the project. Government/authorities close/delay project: It is not unknown for authorities to close/delay projects. This project has full approval from SARC and WRG have collaboration from the local Homs SARC team in order to coordinate the technical aspects of it. The partner has experience in this sector (privately). Open communications exist between WRG and government authorities.
