Project Summary

The proposed project aims to address emergency and more-sustainable shelter and NFI needs of vulnerable households with acute needs (both IDPs and host community members) in Northwest Syria. The intervention will target a total of 120,820 individuals through rehabilitation of damaged houses and distribution of clothes vouchers in communities, and distribution of fuel and heaters in camps.

Needs

According to HNO 2019, 4.7 million people are in need of shelter support whereas 4.4 million people require NFI assistance in Syria. More specifically, the estimated people in need according to HNO 2019 in Idleb governorate is 847,579 individuals for shelter and 1,028,750 individuals for NFI. Financial Tracking Services data indicate that only 30% of shelter and NFI needs were covered within the scope of 2018 Syria Humanitarian Response plan. HNO 2019 identified clothing items as the highest reported NFI need in Syria (14.78% reporting frequency). Other items which ACTED plans to distribute (heating fuel, winter clothing, and winter heaters) were also frequently reported as crucial needs; all of these items were reported as unavailable and/or unaffordable in many communities. ACTED conducted a rapid shelter and NFI assessment in 25 communities Idleb governorate (Al Ma’ra, Jisr Ash-Shugur, Harim, and Idleb districts) in September 2018. The results indicated that shelter and NFI needs remain pressing. All interviewed local councils’ members in Idleb and Al Ma’ra district cited high need for insulation for cold weather, improved privacy, security, and fire protection. In Harim district, the two highest cited needs were privacy and security; this is due the fact that a large amount of households share their home. 68% of the survey participants in assessed communities cited financial reasons as the reason for not rehabilitating their shelters. Concerning NFIs, across all assessed communities, fuel was highest cited need, followed by hygiene items, and blankets for the winter season. 71% of the participants cited that fuel is available in the market but is unaffordable by the communities.

Implementation of activities

ACTED will implement a multi-faceted intervention to respond to IDP and host community needs in Al Ma’ra, Jisr Ash Shugur, and Harim districts of Idleb governorate. All implemented activities, both shelter rehabilitations and distributed items as NFI, will be conducted as by the SNFI cluster’s guidance. As part of the shelter assistance, ACTED will rehabilitate 800 damaged houses and 5 collective centres in communities to ensure safe and dignified shelter solutions for 5,334 individuals and prevent potential protection issues that would result from inadequate shelters. ACTED will register the beneficiaries with a door-to-door registration and verification, during which the rehabilitation needs of a household will be evaluated with specific price estimation conducted for each household. All shelter rehabilitations will be conducted in line with the HLP guidelines and shelter rehabilitation guidelines as shared by the SNFI cluster. Shelter Beneficiary Agreements will be signed with each household to ensure HLP-relevant criteria is respected. ACTED will also address NFI needs of individuals living in communities and in camps through voucher and in-kind modalities. Under this component, ACTED will distribute a variety of in-kind kits including heaters as well as restricted value vouchers for winter clothes, fuel, and other winter items. The NFIs will target specific people who are in high need (e.g.: fuel for IDPs in camps, but also vulnerable host community members and returnees).

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Non-inclusion of special needs during project design/implementationLikelihood: Low – Impact: HighMitigation measure: The proposed intervention will be implemented in accordance with ACTED’s gender approach, which follows the Gender Handbook for Humanitarian Action reviewed in 2017 by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC). ACTED incorporates gender analysis and considerations into the full project management cycle, from project design to implementation and monitoring. During the community sensitisation, ACTED encourages the identification of special needs of most vulnerable groups including women, children, elderly, or people living with disabilities and conducts FGDs and/or community meetings prior to implementation. ACTED addresses where possible gender and protection concerns. In areas out of the scope of programming or expertise, ACTED relies on referral procedures to specialized agencies./ Risk 2: Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuseLikelihood: Low – Impact: HighMitigation measure: All ACTED staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct which directly refers to the Protection of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and to Gender Based Violence (GBV). As part of on-going efforts to improve accountability, ACTED is committed to implement a beneficiary Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its projects to allow beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on ACTED work, as well as the work of the contractors, and to relay any arising issue, including potential PSEA or GBV issue. When selecting beneficiary shelters’ households, priority will be given to address the needs of unaccompanied women and households with particularly vulnerable individuals such as young girls, pregnant or lactating women, and elderly and disabled persons will be favoured. Activities will be tailored to the specific needs of these groups by providing one-on-one in depth information session with each beneficiary household to mitigate any potential protection risks. During NFI distributions, special attention will be given to minimize safety concerns and to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse during beneficiaries’ selection and distribution. This will be achieved through ongoing monitoring of distribution, careful triangulation of information on beneficiaries and effective communication with communities on the modalities for the targeting of beneficiaries and vulnerability criteria. ACTED will distribute in small groups to reduce the risk to beneficiaries and limit queuing time to less than 20 minutes. Daily workers will be hired to support households to carry the kits, in particular for extremely vulnerable individuals. ACTED will ensure that there is a protected humanitarian space, and that no armed group is present during distributions, and no weapons will be allowed into the distribution./ Risk 3: Inadequate intervention to meet the needs of the most vulnerable groups Likelihood: Low – Impact: HighMitigation measure: ACTED will ensure to tailor shelter rehabilitations to the needs of the most vulnerable groups such as PLWDs, women, and girls to maximize their safety, privacy, and dignity. In individual shelter rehabilitations, ACTED staff take beneficiary needs and requests into account in developing Bills of Quantity to address specific beneficiary needs as much as possible. For NFI distribution, ACTED will constantly monitor the process for protection and gender issues to ensure they are adequately identified and addressed.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The major risk for the proposed project is a limited or major escalation of security situation or a shift in the control over target areas which could prevent access to project area. To mitigate any potential security issue, ACTED identifies alternative locations wherein the intervention can take place in all targeted hubs and activities can be shifted to these locations if needed. Moreover, ACTED security focal points in all target areas assess the security context on a daily basis and take precautions when needed including suspension of field movements or activities. Another risk for the project is target populations becoming hostile towards INGOs due to a perception of compromised neutrality in the intervention. Even though this risk is considered unlikely, it would have severe consequences. To mitigate this risk, ACTED clearly communicates to its beneficiaries and local authorities on its humanitarian values and principle of neutrality. Furthermore, ACTED staff are systematically signing a code of conduct guaranteeing their neutrality. To ensure the host communities that ACTED’s neutrality is not compromised, ACTED works closely with local authorities and obtains all necessary authorizations needed to conduct the activities. ACTED has an established complaints and response mechanism (CRM) inside Syria, particularly through WhatsApp, which is highly used as a method of communication between ACTED and external stakeholders. Through CRM, beneficiaries can safely provide feedback, report any project issues, and easily express concerns regarding the neutrality of ACTED. A change of government/policy attitude in Turkey or in Syria could lead to closure of border or imposition of other practices which could negatively impact humanitarian access to Syria. To prevent this risk from materializing, ACTED continuously builds and develops its relationship with the relevant authorities. If the general attitude towards cross-border humanitarian assistance should change, ACTED will adapt its modus operandi to new requirements that may arise to ensure a continued assistance. In order to mitigate any potential risks related to HLP guidelines in shelter rehabilitation, ACTED will create awareness and understanding among key staff and partners on the links between HLP rights and shelter assistance. Before any rehabilitation takes place, ACTED will identify the ownership status of each shelter to be rehabilitated to ensure the rights of the beneficiaries can be properly protected. Moreover, Shelter Beneficiary Agreements are signed with each household to further avoid any HLP related issues.
