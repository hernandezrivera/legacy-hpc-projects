Project Summary

The project will address the urgent health needs of both IDP and Host Communities in Northern Aleppo with an emphasis on continuity of current projects and ensure available medical services through providing equipment, medications, supplies and operational costs (salaries, fuel, etc.) for seven primary health care facilities, one maternity and child hospital, one dialysis center, one rehabilitation center and three immunization centers in addition to provide emergency patient transportation and improve referral system through twelve ambulance vehicles. also participate in improving infection control through operational central incinerator for medical wastes for various health facilitates in northern Aleppo.

Needs

Programming proposed is based on the analysis of the following Assessments,-Syria Humanitarian Needs Overview 2018,- HNO 2018 report- HNO 2019 draftand - Households Multi-Sectoral Need Assessment, Aleppo Governorate (Azaz, Albab and Jarablus), October 2018, by SRD.which stated that the Key health needs and priorities are “Access to primary health care through an Essential Package of Primary Health Services–to cover essential health care needs of vulnerable populations, Maintenance of medicines’ supply chain for treatment of trauma, obstetric care, infectious diseases, and chronic conditions, Access to Reproductive health services and child health care (immunization, integrated management etc.), Maintenance and upgrade of epidemiological surveillance and capacity to detect, investigate, communicate and contain threats to public health security with early warning systems to minimize the impact of epidemics related to unsafe water, poor sanitation and hygiene, overcrowding, low vaccination coverage and other related factors in addition a dire need to increase availability of mental health services”.SRD has conducted Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment in order to capture the needs on the ground. The data was collected through Surveys, Key Informant Interviews, Clinic Assessments and Focus Group Discussions in Aleppo governorate (Azaz, Albab and Jarabluse ).The analysis of the information collected from the targeted communities showed that the most important health problems highlighted by the discussion group were Lack of medicines, Lack of financial and operational support to health centers, and the communicable diseases as the most critical factors contributing to poor health outcomes requiring interventions by the actors in Health Sector.Also there were major obstacle in access to health care, the lack of medicine being the second major issue followed by lack of health staff, lack of transportation and lack of specialized services. Women have additional constrains in accessing services, most likely due to restriction/danger of movement and lack services corresponding to their specific needs. Newly accessible areas require a higher number of equipped mobile clinics/ambulances and fixed PHCs, to improve access to primary health care services. There are only limited health care options for patients with tuberculosis and leishmaniosis and no facilities at all for mental health and physical rehabilitation.

Implementation of activities

SRD planned activities are in line with Health Sector Objectives which include: 1. Increase access to humanitarian life-saving and life-sustaining coordinated, equitable health services for those most vulnerable and in need. 2. Strengthen health system capacity to support continuity of care, strengthen community resilience, and respond to IDP movements and changes in context SRD will support existing health facilities in northern Aleppo including 1 Maternity and Child Hospitals ( in Aghtrin), 7 PHCs (Qabasin, Bazagha, Tal Elhawa, Sandi, Kafr Ghan, Aghtrin and Albil),1 Rehabilitation center in Albab, 1 Dialysis center in Aghtrin and immunization services through 3 mobile teams and 3 fixed center in Bazagha, Aghtrin and Sandi. in addition to operating 1 central incinerator for existing health facilities to improve infection control. the planned support for all above HFs including salaries, running costs, trainings and other needs for the provision of primary and secondary health care services including minor & major surgeries, deliveries, c-section, ANC, PNC, in patient services as well as rehabilitation, immunization and dialysis services. A set of medical equipment is available at the health facilities. To ensure access and continuity of care w, SRD will operate 12 Ambulances for emergency patients' transportation. SRD field offices will monitor activities on the ground. SRD has mapped out planned services in accordance with existing collaborative sites that have been in place for months.This information has been crosschecked with the OCHA Health Cluster along with other peer agencies. SRD planned activities will meet specific needs to enable access for people with disabilities to health services in accordance to their specific situation. SRD will modify its HFs to ensure access for all beneficiaries with disability including ramp entrance with handles, WCs for disabled people and trained its staff to provide services to patients with disabilities. From GBV perspective, SRD will ensure the integration of GBV in Health services as there will be Women Waiting area within SRD HFs, ensure that SRD HFs has available Females staff and training HFs staff on GBV principles. in addition to that, provide CMR services when needed.

Protection Risk Analysis

Violence against Health Facilities and Healthcare Providers: Although the rate of attacks against healthcare declined significantly over the course of 2018, violence is still a main issue facing healthcare providers and affecting their ability to respond in contested areas, with a long term effect hampering the scale up effort in newly accessible areas that witnessed recent change of control. Mitigating measures: - Applying the designated de-confliction mechanism for health facilities, and increasing advocacy efforts by the health sector with all parties to the conflict to engage them on health protection topics and respect of international humanitarian law (IHL). - Continued reporting on attacks against healthcare, and enlisting support of the Strategic Steering Group (SSG), and humanitarian leadership in their efforts to highlight protections challenges facing health workers across Syria. - Reporting from relevant hubs regarding health situation in reconciled areas with continuous monitoring of health status of population in these areas especially in the immediate post-reconciliation phase. Gender Based Violence GBV: Health services are often the first, and in many occasions the only point of contact for GBV survivors seeking assistance, or coming for health care for GBV related emotional or physical conditions, including injuries, without necessarily disclose the abuse due to shame or fear. In order to facilitate the required care, survivors must have safe access to health facilities, and it is critical that health providers are equipped to offer survivor-centered, right-based, non-discriminatory, quality health services. Mitigating measures: - Prevention and management of sexual violence and Intimate Partner Violence is considered to be a life-saving intervention. Accordingly, affected population should have access to the reproductive health services of the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) as part of the essential health package. - Specialized health staff should be trained on providing first-line support, clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals. - Identification of safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases connecting health services and clinical management with social services, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), in a manner guarantees safety, confidentiality, and privacy of the survivor. - Presence of female staff in health facilities increase access of women and girls to health services. Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA: The health sector and healthcare providers should strive and commit to preventing sexual exploitation and abuse both on organizational and programmatic levels. Mitigating measures: - A PSEA policy describes standard of behavior for organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and inclusive to accountability measures. - Effective community based complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance. - Effective and comprehensive mechanisms should be established to ensure awareness-raising on SEA amongst personnel, with continuous training on the topic. - Effective recruitment and performance management of all humanitarian staff. - Provide information to communities and people affected by crisis about the organization, the principles it adheres to, how it expects its staff to behave, the programs it is implementing and what they intend to deliver. - Design and implement appropriate programs based on an impartial assessment of needs and risks, and an understanding of the vulnerabilities and capacities of different groups, clear reporting and feedback mechanisms.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The risks include: deterioration of the security and political situation in northern Aleppo, New arrivals from other areas, Lack of skilled staff required to operate the health facilities, Lack of financial resources, Delayed access and transfer of materials, Lack of cooperation of local councils in the implementation of project activities.Assumptions and Mitigating measures are: suspension of activities affected by the deterioration of the security situation and resume after the situation stabilizes, Rent warehouses in targeted areas and store necessary materials, More coordination and contracting with alternative medical bodies.
