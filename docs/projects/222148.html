Project Summary

The Logistics Cluster is a service-based cluster and acts, through WFP, as a provider of last resort for common logistics services. The Logistics Cluster aims to augment the capacities of humanitarian organizations and facilitate the timely delivery of humanitarian assistance to vulnerable populations through logistics coordination, information management, capacity building and facilitation of common services to the entire humanitarian community in South Sudan.

1. Needs

South Sudan suffers from a lack of basic road and river infrastructure throughout the country. The road network is among the most underdeveloped in the world, with only around 400 km of the total 20,000 km of roads paved. Lengthy rainfall seasons coupled with poor upstream water management exposes South Sudan to flash flooding phenomenon which compromises the durability of infrastructure development making it very difficult to access areas that are in dire need of food and other relief items. In addition, community dike embanks need rehabilitation to protect the communities in flood prone areas within the Jonglei state and parts of Upper Nile. Rehabilitation of roads and opening of river water ways tops the list of priorities for effective prepositioning of relief cargo as well as for emergencies requiring a timely intervention, such as disease outbreaks, rapid missions, or response scale-ups. Roads significantly deteriorate during the rainy season making many locations remain inaccessible by any other means, and humanitarian organizations are reliant on costly air transport for cargo transport to reach populations in need of timely life-saving assistance. Additionally, due to low maintenance and very limited infrastructure, most airstrips are only accessible to helicopters during the rainy season, which have limited cargo capacity and are an extremely costly method of transportation. In addition to the static responses, this year the conflict in Sudan resulted in the influx of returnees and refugees putting additional strain on resources. This has further impacted South Sudan stretching the funding to meet the demands of humanitarian community with emergency assistance and transportation of essential lifesaving items required at the border locations. With the rainy season affecting logistical movements by road, humanitarian response in the border locations prioritized cargo needs given the increased demand required to assist refugees, returnees, and third country nationals at borders and transit facilities. Efficient common logistics services including provision of storage facilities are critical to enable humanitarian actors to reach vulnerable people in need and implement programs and projects in remote and isolated areas.

2. Project activities

The Logistics Cluster addresses the logistical challenges of the humanitarian response in South Sudan by providing logistics coordination, information management, and capacity building services to the humanitarian community. Where gaps are identified, the Logistics Cluster also facilitates access to common services, including cargo transport and common storage space. The Logistics Cluster serves as a coordination body for humanitarian actors to identify logistics gaps, minimize duplication of efforts, and ensure logistics efficiencies. To achieve this, monthly coordination meetings are held in Juba, and on a semi-regular basis at sub-national level at the Logistics Cluster hubs in Bentiu, Bor, Malakal, and Wau, while also supporting partners in deep field locations with more localized coordination. The Logistics Cluster provides information management to support operational decision making and logistics planning. This includes the identification, consolidation, and dissemination of essential logistics information, as well as GIS support to partners. Information is shared on the dedicated webpage where all products are publicly accessible and through the mailing list with more than 800 active subscribers. The Logistics Cluster enhances logistics preparedness among its partners through capacity strengthening. In 2025, the cluster will provide capacity building and training services to the entire humanitarian community operating in South Sudan, with a special focus in deep field locations prioritizing national actors and female logisticians. The common transport services that the Logistics Cluster facilitates access to include road, river and air transport. To maximize the efficient use of limited resources, the cluster advocates for the prepositioning of cargo during the dry season, making use of the short period each year that roads are accessible. This is enabled by the Logistics Cluster through the coordination of road convoys along all major supply routes in South Sudan. The cluster will increase the frequency of road convoys during the short window of opportunity when key routes are open to allow humanitarian actors to safely transport their cargo with the most cost-efficient transport method. Common storage is offered in locations lacking sufficient capacity to meet storage needs, and minimum stock of Mobile Storage Units will be maintained to loan to organizations in need of storage space in deep field locations. The Logistics Cluster also works closely together with IOM’s Common Transport Service project to offer road transport to locations reachable by road from Bentiu, Wau and Malakal, further reducing the reliance on air operations. As a last resort only, the Logistics Cluster will facilitate air cargo transport to destinations not reachable by other means or in case of emergencies.
