Project Summary

The effects of conflict in South Sudan continue to affect the humanitarian response as
the effects of widespread violence and the deteriorating economic situation will be
aligned to Nutrition Cluster specific objectives, namely 1. To increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition-specific services for children, adolescents, and women in prioritized locations by the end of 2024. 2. To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under five years of age and PLW affected by acute malnutrition in prioritized locations by the end of 2024. 3 To enhance the nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis, and utilization of nutrition information by the end of 2024. 4 To increase equitable access to nutrition-sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education, and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and integrated programming targeting vulnerable groups in prioritized locations by the end of
2024. The project will complement the ongoing intervention in selected priority Counties,
narrowing the nutrition care provision gaps in the most remote sites and for the most
neglected groups for what concerns MAM/SAM treatment but also the prevention and timely
detection of acute malnutrition. Nutrition services and related activities will be aligned with international and South Sudan guidelines. Different service delivery modalities will be considered, from
static to mobile/community-based, as a proactive and articulated strategy to overcome
the accessibility issues affecting current nutrition care coverage and to reach both hosts
and displaced populations. The proactive involvement of local communities or key stakeholders and health authorities will facilitate the selection of the most responsive, feasible, and acceptable
option, while orientating the intervention on the most serious and urgent gaps, promoting better outcomes, and ensuring the due accountability to beneficiaries. Nutrition services will be provided at the Health Facility level or through mobile teams and community-based initiatives in their catchment area and under their supervision and guidance, resulting in highly comprehensive, addressing both health and nutrition conditions and the interrelated complications. In this way, the project will contribute to enhancing the institutionalization of the management of acute malnutrition in the health system as suggested by the Cluster. The cornerstone of the proposed nutrition intervention will be its integration with other sectors as well. WASH & FSL will be given high attention, with the result of the applied strategy to be wide enough to tackle some of the key acute malnutrition drivers, such as poor hygiene access to clean water, and limited food availability knowledge. Being aware of the higher exposure to malnutrition in certain population groups, whose particular conditions (disabilities, mental health disorders, exposure to SGBV) make them particularly vulnerable and their access to services more challenging, besides requiring specific response routine interventions are not used to provide, the project will guarantee adequate integration with the Protection sector, through the opening and sustain of reciprocal referral channels for people to be offered both nutrition and clinical/psychosocial support.
In conclusion, people people-centered approach will be adopted and mainstreamed to guarantee sector-wide responsiveness and acceptability quality of provided care will be strengthened through technical assistance and supportive supervision and monitored through an enhanced data collection and analysis, functional to the informed decision-making process to improve the current situation.

1. Needs

The first Humanitarian Need Overview (HNO) released projected in the period of May 2022 to July 2022, an estimated 8.3 million people will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity in South Sudan, with an estimated 2 million people likely to be in acute malnourished state. 46 counties have reported a GAM rate of above 15% above the emergency threshold compared to 31 counties across the country in 2022. In Lakes State, The 2021 Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring Survey (FSNMS) revealed that the prevalence of wasting among young children is estimated at 10.6%, lower than the WHO emergency threshold of 15% but this will deteriorate in 2024 as revealed by FSNMS round 29 which indicate 17.8% and 24.8% GAM rate in Yirol East and Awerial counties respectively. Furthermore, the 2022 food security Inter-Phase Classification (IPC) analysis projected that an estimated 749,000 people (62.0% of the population) are likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse with 264,000 people likely to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity between April and July. According to the IPC released in April 2022, an estimated 93,208 children are projected to be wasted in 2022 out of which 23,398 are estimated to suffer severe wasting. The expected caseloads in 2024 for Yirol East, Yirol West and Awqerial Counties are disaggregated as; SAM U5: 10,739; MAM U5: 27,647; MAM PLW: 23,462; MIYCN: 59,051; VAS: 79,404, and Deworming 65,392. Key drivers of malnutrition among vulnerable populations in Lakes State, as well as Yirol East, Yirol West and Awerial counties are widespread food insecurity, poverty, inadequate infant and young child feeding and caring practices, high prevalence of malaria, diarrhea, and ARI, limited access to quality health services, and lack of safe water and inadequate hygiene and sanitation. Localized conflict and climatic shocks are also aggravating factors. This proposed project will be delivered through promotional, preventive, and curative nutrition interventions, integrated with the promotion of selected WASH and health services, which will contribute to reducing morbidity and mortality due to wasting among young children in the state. HELP has been implementing an integrated program in Yirol East, Yirol West, and Awerial combining Nutrition, Food Security, and WASH since 2016. This project is therefore a continuation of similar interventions to reinforce the nutrition practices already being adopted by the community while at the same time addressing new cases of malnutrition caused by food shortages, recent flooding, and other shocks

2. Project activities

The intervention will strengthen Acute Malnutrition management institutionalization into the Health System with CHD and communities’ involvement. It will complement the ongoing support to the 17 Nutrition sites and 5 outreach sites. The existing CHD and SMoH structures that support basic health and Nutrition packages will be incorporated to support these outlined activities. Further, these activities will be implemented in health facilities and in the community level. At the Health facilities, the CHD existing structure will be used for the implemenation, while at the community level, the nutrition-sensitive packages and outreach sites will be provided by national non governmental organizations, this will strength knowledge transfer, and leading to sustainability of the project. The activities are outlined below. Activity 1. Staff Training & Capacity Building Nutrition teams will receive pieces of training, and regular refreshers from Help’s supervision staff. Regular field visits by Nutrition Program Managers & field Coordinators. Activity 2. MIYCN. Caregivers at the OTP level will be trained in inappropriate IYCF practices. Nutrition education sessions will be implemented by health promoters at the Static and Mobile OTP levels and will be organized weekly. Activity 3. Management of complicated cases and Referral System: a. A tailored referral system will be an essential component of the PHCC, and Mobile sites. Patients in need of urgent care will be internally referred to SCs. b. Help teams will carry out door-to-door visits to analyze the overall vulnerability situation of the households in regard to barriers to access to services. Activity 4. Nutrition Education and Promotion at CMAM Sites, promotion sessions will be implemented in all health facilities. Awareness of key community persons will be built on major health and Nutrition topics as well as information on the availability of Nutrition Services. Activity 5. Community outreach and mobilization. a. CNVs will oversee the door-to-door visits, screening, and sensitizations while Community Nutrition Workers will oversee animating and following up the Mother Care Groups and ensuring IYCF messages. During the community outreach and mobilization, preventive messages on malnutrition will be passed as well vitamin A supplementation and deworming will be conducted at the household level. b. Community awareness sessions will be provided gathering all key messages and including protection awareness. Besides, Help outreach teams will study the possibility of implementing mass awareness sessions at the community level. Activity 6. Management of Outpatient Treatment Programs Contributes to catering to the needs of children with uncomplicated severe acute malnutrition cases (SAM) which either come spontaneously to the facility or are referred by others (and particularly by the outreach team). Activity 7. Awareness Sessions and Provision of IEC materials: The CNWs will organize two types of sessions. Door-to-door sessions, meeting the community directly in their houses to provide health, hygiene, sanitation, and nutrition advice. Activity 8. WASH Key messages and engage CNVs to disseminate key messages, and provision of handwashing facilities, Latrine maintenance, and management, and Food preparation. Activity 9. Protection mainstreaming training: Following the Global protection cluster guidelines, Help's staff will receive training from the Protection and Nutrition Project Manager on how to incorporate PSEA and GBV.
