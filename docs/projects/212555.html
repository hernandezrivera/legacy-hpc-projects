Project Summary

Children and young people in Syria have often been through several traumatic experiences due to years of war. Non-formal schooling and the desired change to a public school as well as psychosocial support should help to make children and young people more resilient to the above-mentioned risks and hazards. There are more than 2.45 million Out of School Children (OoSC) in Syria (46 per cent girls, 54 per cent boys) and over a million who are at risk of dropping out according to the HNO assessment of needs. The selected area of intervention, Ar-Raqqa district, is the second highest amongst governorates Ar-Raqqa with 48 percent. 70% of the sub-districts in Raqqa, amongst them Ar Raqqa district, are rated with extreme severity Next to a lack of qualified teachers, the lacking financial capacity of the Self-Administration to maintain formal education sector or support economically strained households as well as missing rehabilitation on formal schools highlight the missing capacities to offer educational services at large. 470 schools are still estimated to be damaged in North East Syria according to the HNO 2024.
Access to school education and the acquisition of basic numeracy and literacy skills help to alleviate the psychological effects of the war, give them hope for the future and restore a sense of everyday life to the schoolchildren and their parents.
The regular school attendance of the children contributes to a positive everyday structure, enables the parents to work regularly and to know that the children are well looked after during this time. It is important for parents to enable children to attend school. Re-establishing a school routine reduces the stress and psychological burden on parents of not being able to offer their children this.
Overall objective: Strengthening the resilience of Syrian children and youth and Syrian teachers through education in the communities most affected by the armed conflict. Syrian children and youth aged 8 - 14 years, especially in rural communities without public schools, have improved literacy and numeracy skills to complete primary school and teachers have improved methodological and technical skills, especially in psychosocial care.
Outcome 1: The reading, writing and maths skills of around 1,200 children and young people who have not attended school for a long time have improved.
5 training centres are set up at suitable locations based on a set of criteria (chief amongst them the distance to formal schools, population structure and accessibility). Up to 200 pupils per training centre can take advantage of the offers. The offered non-formal education programme consists of
- Mathematics (matching numbers, adding balls, ones and tens numbers, subtracting and adding numbers, comparison, even numbers, geometric shapes, arranging numbers,)
- Arabic language (analyzing and composing words, writing letters and their shapes, singular and plural, nominal and verbal sentences, word competition)
- English language (matching lowercase letters with uppercase letters, the four seasons, colors)
- Science (parts of the body, drinking water, respiratory system, five senses, food contamination, plant parts, energy sources, the four seasons, states of matter, wind, correct eating habits).
with the aim of closing the knowledge gaps of children and young people in these areas and thus enabling them to (re)enter regular schools. The curriculum is based on the SLP approach and is paired with daily PSS classes that are centered around sports, arts and directed coginitive exercises.
Outcome 2: Teachers' methods and didactic skills have improved.
The activties focus on training of teachers on the implementation, content (Mathematics, English, PSS and Arabic) and methods of the Non-Formal Education Programme. This includes a week-long review and methods traning after each four months, teacher evaluations and specific coaching where required. Experts support staff in PSS evaluations of students.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Each center is equipped with 6 container-based class- and meeting rooms as well as a separate tent for PSS sessions. Additionally, all centers are fenced for child protection and has a soccer pitch, swing sets and slide, as well as volleyball nets for play-based interventions.Centers have following enrolment numbers (55.6% male and 44.4% female): Al-Rawda - 210, Marj Al-Zohor - 200; Al-Jamelyah -190; Al-Turky - 200; Al-Khaldiyah - 200The quality of teaching at the TLS is enhanced by the use of activity-based teaching methods, demonstrated through pictures and videos. Students' talents and teachers' creativity to develop teaching aids is being refined by their interest of creating teaching learning aids. The model lesson competition, where teachers showcase engaging and fun lessons, enhances their motivation and dedication. Additionally, field visits, particularly in science lessons, provide a practical perspective that complements theoretical learning. Education officers continued support and monitor progress with extended support from center supervisors and MEAL team has enabled teachers to deliver good quality teaching at their centers. Among the educational activities that were implemented during this batch are the following: Children have been provided with meal three times per week that used to consist of healthy snack and milk. One of the main focuses is on personal and general hygiene at the centers. Students' nails and personal cleanliness are regularly checked, and awareness sessions are held. Play-based sessions for PSS are conducted daily consisting of the main session blocks:- Structured Psychosocial Support Sessions: children with behavioural issues are identified to participate in regular sessions in the next batch to help improve their behaviour, develop their skills, and boost their self-confidence. Common behaviours observed in these children include introversion, shyness, excessive movement, aggression, and bullying.- Directed Activities: This type of activity aims to instil principles, values and behaviours in children, and create a safe and sound environment for children to play and learn easily.- Recreational, Lyrical and artistic activities: With the aim of enhancing children's intellectual, social, emotional, motor and linguistic abilities, recreational activities helped in the learning process.
