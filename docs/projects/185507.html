Project Summary

This project aims at building the resilience of the poorest, most-in-need and hardest-to-reach children. It targets 780,000 vulnerable children in 3,452 schools (50% girls) with below breakdown:
• IDPs & Returnees Children: 312,000
• Vulnerable Residents: 459,000
The project provides lifesaving interventions that targets provision of formal and informal learning opportunities in a safe operating environment during COVID-19 pandemic.

1 - Needs

Currently, Sudan is struggling to meet its obligation to support the children that call it home. While rapid onset emergencies such as floods, conflict and epidemics continue to draw attention, the exposure to prolonged crises such as displacement, economic deprivation, malnutrition and failing service infrastructure traps people and children in a perpetuating cycle of need. In 2021, each of the 18 states has been beset by one crisis or another and this trend will continue into 2025 . Most of the 3.04 million children in need for educational support will remain unreached without adequate resources. Political stability since the 2019 revolution has allowed steps toward economic recovery and internal peace but these are unlikely to benefit today’s children before they reach adulthood. The transitional government lacks revenue, is battling crippling inflation and the COVID-19 pandemic and is struggling to implement the rule of law. Regional turmoil surrounds Sudan and is likely to trigger further refugee crises beyond the 55,785 Ethiopian refugees, and 784,860 South Sudanese refugees, that are amongst the 1.1 million refugees already hosted. Internally, there are 3 million IDPs in camps awaiting resolution to current and past conflict, and solutions that span the peace, development, and humanitarian spheres. Sudan remains a junction for irregular migration and must reckon with both new and old internal complexities. Even before the COVID-19 pandemic, 36.5 percent of children were out of school, dispossessing young Sudanese of education, the safety of the schoolyard and school feeding. According to JENA 2021, 22% of classrooms were damaged or destroyed. The most commonly reported cause of damage was rains / wind / weather (40%), as well as strong wind alone (31%) and flooding alone (22%). Community structures are ill prepared to mitigate the risks of floods and conflict. Social welfare does not reach deep enough to provide necessities for those worst affected by economic troubles.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The activities will be implemented under three pillars as follows: Pillar 1: Access to formal or non-formal education, including early learning.To increase enrolment and reduce dropout of children from schools, learning spaces and gender-segregated WASH facilities will be provided with community mobilization and sensitization to enroll out of school children. Target beneficiaries are 110,750 children (50% girls). Pillar 2: Teaching and learning materials to attend schoolSchooling supplies will be provided in form of students’ kits, blackboards, teachers’ kits recreational Kits, uniform sets, and school furniture for a total of 303,819 children to attend schools. Pillar 3: Implementing safe school protocols (COVID-19 prevention and control)At least 3,452 teachers will be trained on Teacher Preparedness Training Programme TPTP to oversee implementation of COVID-19 school where 1 teacher will be identified from each school to be also the COVID-19 focal point.
