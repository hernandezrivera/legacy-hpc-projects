Project Summary

The Sudan PSEA Network was established in 2019 under the leadership of the RC/HC and the UNCT as part of the IASC Plan for Accelerating Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse in Humanitarian Response at Country-Level. Since then, the PSEA Network has been implementing key activities under a Joint Framework of Action endorsed by the UNCT and focusing on 6 key priority results and 8 outcomes as below:
Priority Result A: PSEA Policy/Operationalization
1. Outcome 1. All development and humanitarian actors have increased knowledge on standards of conduct, SEA/safeguarding and of the systems in place on prevention, reporting and survivor-centric response.
2. Outcome 2. All development and humanitarian interventions in Sudan reinforce compliance with PSEA safeguarding commitments and policies

Priority Result B: Coordination and Cooperation
3. Outcome 3. The Resident and Humanitarian Coordinator and the UNCH-HCT are supported at senior management and technical-levels to lead, oversee and deliver on the PSEA commitments visa the Sudan Joint Framework for Action
Priority Result C: Prevention & Internal Mainstreaming
4. Outcome 4. Every child and adult within communities receiving humanitarian and development services are aware of their rights, expected standard behaviours by development/humanitarian actors and know where/how to report SEA incidents.
5. Outcome 5. All member organizations of the PSEA Network create and maintain an environment for prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse.
Priority Result D: Advocacy
6. Outcome 6. Increased commitment and cooperation by government, civil society, donors and other key actors in response to PSEA in Sudan
Priority Result E: Complaints and Response Mechanisms
7. Outcome 7: Every affected child and adult recipient of development support and/or humanitarian assistance has access to a safe, gender and child-sensitive pathways to report SEA (through community-based complaints mechanisms) that reach where aid reaches, are appropriate to the context and are accessible to the most vulnerable).

Priority Result F: Support to Survivors
8. Outcome 8. All child and adult SEA survivors receive effective context relevant healthcare, mental and psychosocial, and safety and security services.

Under the new project, the Sudan PSEA Network seeks to evaluate the success of the network and indeed the humanitarian response in so far as implementing the 2019 – 2021 Joint Framework of Action, use the lessons learnt to update the country level 2022 action plan while filling in any gaps identified and through federal and state level consultations, sensitize all stakeholders on revised priorities for the country and roll out implementation of these prioritized activities and outcomes.
Focus will be centered around state level capacity development of PSEA focal points, engagement with local populations (which includes communication, consultation and community-based complaints mechanisms) in collaboration with the AAP Working Group, and survivor support in collaboration with the GBV and CP subsectors.

1 - Needs

In Sudan, existing gender analysis provides some understanding of the relevance of gender roles and power dynamics in communities and the linkages between inequalities at different societal levels. Even in the post revolution era, Sudanese women remain underrepresented in the political, economic, and social spheres and are faced with significant challenges owing to outstanding repressive laws and policies from the previous regime. In such an operating environment, sexual misconduct (even when not acknowledged as such) and a strong culture of silence and shame associated with sexual violence can be rife. The gravity of the situation is further compounded when victims or survivors of sexual exploitation and abuse are denied access to safe, dignified, and timely reporting and response services. This project seeks to address the following needs identified in Sudan’s humanitarian response: • There is a below average awareness on PSEA and accompanying policies or codes of conduct amongst humanitarian/peace/development workers in Sudan while the awareness of SEA and their rights to be protected from abuse and exploitation amongst programme participants is less than 25%. • National organizations and civil society actors who form the frontline responders in instances of emergency response (as was seen in the case of COVID 19 responses) are not fully compliant with the minimum standards for PSEA as outlined in the IASC Joint Capacity Assessment for Implementing Partners, which may expose programme participants to SEA for lack of adequate measures for protection, awareness or due to ignorance. • It is common for emergency responders to assume that SEA risks are nonexistent especially in a context such as Sudan, where discussions involving sexual practices remain taboo. However, as the space to approach these discussions with communities open up, it is evident that even existing laws, cultural norms or practices cannot fully insulate affected communities, especially when they are confronted with realities that increase their vulnerability such as poverty, conflict and displacement, inflation and high cost of basic needs, and others. It is therefore important to continue engaging persons of concerns in these conversations to uncover the interrelatedness of their harsh realities, emergency response and sexual exploitation and abuse. • Sudan is still yet to establish a strong and effective community-based complaints mechanism making it very difficult for programme participants and/or affected communities to report SEA concerns/reports/incidents/suspicions or access information that would facilitate timely intervention (including clinical management of rape). Even as we pursue an interagency mechanism, the network will work hand-in-hand with the AAP working group to map existing complaints and feedback mechanisms, and develop guidance on how to build in the principles of accessibility, safety, confidentiality, informed consent and effective referral into such mechanisms. • As yet, there has been minimal engagement with the government on the issue of PSEA and how to handle it in country, yet the Government of Sudan remains our host, is involved in policy and decision making, is a key implementing partner for many if not all UN agencies and also hosts the law enforcement agencies that could hold perpetrators and agencies accountable. It is therefore important to initiate this level of engagement and advocacy for a multiplier effect and sustainability.

2 - Activities/Outputs

1. Development of a country level PSEA Action plan for 2022 – A 2022 PSEA Country action plan endorsed: During the first quarter, the network will conduct consultations workshops with all stakeholders both at the federal and state level to agree on priorities for implementation in country, based on our identified needs and gaps. This will culminate in the development and endorsement of a 2022 PSEA Action Plan at the UN/HCT level. 2. Trainings on PSEA for staff and PSEA focal points: # of persons trained to increase knowledge and awareness on PSEA: The Sudan PSEA network will take stock of staff implementing humanitarian projects across various sectors and develop a mechanism to roll out the IASC Learning Package – “Say No to Sexual Misconduct” – most likely through a dedicated Capacity development consultant amongst staff effectively. Priority will be given to government actors, NGO staff and grassroot or civil society organizations with an objective to reach 75% of those that have not received any PSEA training. Additionally, the network team will directly undertake training of PSEA focal points both at federal and state level focusing on the IASC learning package, their terms of reference, survivor support and strategies to advance PSEA policy implementation and compliance within their respective organizations. The trainings are aimed at reaching 100% of PSEA focal points in country. A more practical package will also be developed for community based focal points for roll out at the community level. 3. PSEA Compliance - # of organizations that benefit from PSEA mentorship and % improvement in compliance: The network will use a mentorship approach between organizations at different risk levels of PSEA compliance to support those with weaker mechanisms, to reduce their risk level by developing codes of conducts and checklists for reference checks or vetting procedures, administering a PSEA staff induction package, appointing PSEA focal points, maintaining an SEA risk register, adhering to their obligations on PSEA in partner and contract agreements, and establishing internal reporting mechanisms to be complemented by interagency complaints mechanisms. 4. Enhance PSEA awareness at the community level - # of affected populations reached through SEA risk assessments and sensitization campaigns: The PSEA network (working in close conjunction with appropriate coordination mechanisms at the state level) will continue to engage with local populations by implementing SEA risk assessments in locations with humanitarian responses as well as disseminating PSEA/AAP/Inclusion information (in various formats and languages) to community members through a variety of channels to communicate. 5. Strengthening implementation of interagency community-based complaints mechanisms – 1 Interagency CBCM established, # CFM mapping conducted, # of CFMs linked, Interagency CBCM SOPs developed: this activity will including mapping and linking existing complaints and feedback mechanisms, developing guidance and protocols for on how to build in the principles of accessibility, safety, confidentiality, informed consent and effective referral into such mechanisms. Additionally, the network will continue to advocate for funding through the UN/HCT for funding “Salaam Sudan” or invest in now up and running GBV and CP call centers/hotlines being managed by CVAW supported by UNFPA and UNICEF. 6. Emergency Case Fund - 100% of victims/survivors are referred for assistance and received support within 48 hours: The network will host a contingency fund to facilitate victims’ rights support and assistance, to ensure that 100% of victims/survivors are referred for assistance and received support within 48 hours.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

• Considering the sensitivity, the project fully intends to prioritize the safety and dignity of affected populations especially when working on survivor support and the Community based complaints mechanisms • The project will ensure meaningful access to affected communities according to needs and without discrimination as it is aimed at enhancing the protective environment of all. • The project will ensure the participation and empowerment of affected communities through stakeholder consultations to prioritize activities, assessments to identify needs and gaps and engagement through awareness raising and sensitization campaigns. • Accountability is one of the pillars of PSEA in terms of access to justice, investigations and holding perpetrators accountable for any identified form of misconduct.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The Sudan network will continue to engage on an active basis with the AAP Working Group, the Protection Sector and the GBV/CP Areas of Responsibility to incorporate mainstreaming principles, coordinate on a victim assistance mechanism which incorporates GBV/CP referral mechanisms, and establish interagency community based complaints mechanism or link existing complaints mechanism for timely reporting and referral. To the extent possible, the network will also support state level coordination forums/mechanisms in advancing PSEA mechanisms in various localities. The network will also extend support to the Interagency Sector Coordination group to provide guidance on integrating PSEA measures at throughout the humanitarian programme management cycle.
