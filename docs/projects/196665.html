Project Summary

The Voices from Syria 2023 report describes the lives of Syrian women and girls as marked by mutually reinforcing forms of GBV and gender discrimination which embeds risks of both GBV and CP.
According to HNO 2023 the People in need 15.3M. 46% of them are children and 17% PWDs.Highly unequal gender norms, often exacerbated by discriminatory attitudes based on age, displacement status, disability and/or marital status, create an environment in which women and girls are devalued, controlled, and blamed. Population groups, especially children, such as adolescent girls continue to be vulnerable to specific forms of GBV and CP risks including sexual violence, early marriage, denial of education, and increasingly, child labor, psychological distress, and mental health issues.The deteriorating economy and widespread poverty, destruction and loss of housing, and multiple cycles of displacement, substandard living conditions exacerbate protection needs for adults and children like, the separated children getting recruited into armed forces, economically exploited and increase reliance on negative coping mechanisms for both adults and child . So, SDI will not only respond to CP risks, but also will be accountable to children and their caregivers. Interventions for CP will include conducting assessments/research, community awareness raising, training social workers, non-protection workers and other stakeholders on CP minimum standards, provision of MHPSS services integrated with CM for children and in SDI’s health facilities depending on international guidelines, support referral of CP. Increase children’s, especially for CwDs resilience and inclusion within the community, and conduct parenting activities on topics like, Role of parents in protecting children from child labor. In addition, strengthening positive relations between the host community and IDPs. Furthermore, SDI will support the empowerment and protection of women and adolescent girls and work to implement protection and prevention mechanisms based on gender-based violence through conducting assessments/researches, distribution to dignity kits, and awareness raising on GBV, Colera and covid-19, provision of trainings & skill/vocational and income generating activities. Provide support to increase women and girls' resilience & livelihood opportunities, and dissemination of information related to GBV, besides the provision of CM survivors and referrals, provision of training for counselors, provision of training on GBV and Cholera.

Moreover, establish WGSSs, and workingagainst economic, social and gender injustice.The project will implementprotection and prevention measures against SEA by conducting assessments/researches/consultations/FGDs for this purpose, and applying mitigation measures for all activities inthis project, besides holding awareness sessions and raising awareness campaigns. 4 - Implementation of activities

SDI offices in Syria & Turkey will coordinate for best implementation practices. Implementation will be led by protection team in Syria, supervised by the protection officer. Support departments will work in accordance with SDI's procedures &policies. Beneficiaries chosen without discrimination & of the most vulnerable. Employees receive GBV, and PSEA training. Gender consideration be taken in recruitment. M&E will be present at all stages and will conduct regular field visits to the project sites. M&E team received training on protection projects monitoring and will use GBVSC M&E Toolkit for monitoring the activities. SDI, will ensure smooth implementation of the project according to the approved plan. Reporting will cover all phases of implementation. Implementation of activities will be coordinated with local authority/community leaders. Accountability to communities will be established through feedback & complaints mechanisms clearly announced. SDI staff will sign the CoC that includes HLG 2023. Activities will be done using GBVsc, CPsc and GP cluster tools, like the GBV SC Awareness Raising Toolkit and (IASC) MHPSS Guidelines in Emergency Settings, & UNICEF Guidelines on Community-Based MH & PSS in Humanitarian Settings for the provision of MHPSS for children. SDI's Vendors will sign CoC & receive sensitization on GBV and child protectionand on PSEA. PSEA will be mainstreamed. moreover under report tools SDI will share 4Ws report with protection cluster and doner.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Risk of mistreatment of children in friendly spaces and women &girls in WGSS. Level: low. Impact: High. Mitigation: do training for staff, well established M&E plan. 2. Risk of children receiving child protection services, feeling ashamed/stigmatized. Risk: low. Impact: high. Mitigation: Staff trained to protect the children’s privacy&security. 3. Risk of no adequate locations to establish the friendly spaces for children. Level: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation: early coordination established. 4. Risk of children accessing the services provided to them, like MHPSS safely, especially CwDs &girls. Mitigation: centers modified to be accessible & safe for children & CwDS. The place will be guarded &in an area is accessible &safe for all beneficiaries. 5. Risk of women receiving GBV services feeling ashamed/stigmatized. Risk: low. Impact: high. Mitigation: Staff trained to protect the women’s privacy&security. 6. Risk of women & girls getting harassed or exploited or feel ashamed during distribution of dignity kits. Level: Medium Impact: high Mitigation: select a place that is safe for women, put complaint box, employ women staff, M&E be present at the distribution. 7. Risk of women getting harassed or exploited during income generating activities or skill/vocational training. level: low, Impact: High Mitigation: select a place that is safe for women, complaint box, M&E regular visits, and female staff deal with women.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Beneficiaries are at risk on being exposed to sexual misconduct and they might not report the incident due to: 1. lack of knowledge of reporting mechanisms, especially for PwDs and women living in faraway areas. 2. feeling afraid of retaliation. 3. fear of stigma. 4. fear of deprivation of assistance. 5. being unable to understand written banners for being illiterate and/or for having a visual disability.6. for children, language might be complicated. To mitigate these risks, SDI will : 1.aim at clearly announcing the hotline number and email of PSEA of SDI and UN through many means, like, banners, social media, and messages to beneficiaries’ phones through telegram channel to ensure reaching to the biggest number of beneficiaries including women living in faraway areas and people with disabilities. 2. Conduct PSEA training for all staff members and contractors/suppliers dealing with beneficiaries and make them read and sign CoC that state Zero Tolerance towards SEA violations. 3. Conduct community awareness raising sessions on PSEA reporting any violations and stress on the confidentiality and on eliminating any other fears. 4. Conduct awareness raising sessions for children in an appropriate language. 5. complaint boxes 6. Using PSEA field and organizational checklists tools. 7. Approach PwDs with appropriate means to be informed of PSEA complaint mechanisms. 8. Conduct consultations and FGDs with community members and leader on PSEA mitigation measures.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Risk of not acquiring permission which will impede implementation of activities. Level: low, Impact: high Mitigation: working in location were SDI has strong contacts to acquire permission from local authorities/community leaders. 2. Risk of worsening of security situation is a continuous risk for the overall project, Level: medium, Impact: high Mitigation: choosing locations that are safe and train our employees on retreat and evacuation plans and the correct practice during emergencies. 3. Risk of local authorities/communities impeding awareness campaigns or dissemination of information related to the protection of women and children especially against GBV risks and parenting programs. Level: low, Impact: high Mitigation: working in location were SDI has good relations and strong contacts to acquire the needed permissions and were communities are more open to such activities. 4. Risk: Closure of Border Crossing Points, Likelihood: High; Impact: Low. Mitigation: This will have a low impact as all needed materials are available in Syria and will be procured in Syria according to SDI's experience. SDI will keep informing humanitarian actors and advocating through advocacy channels to continue the response in Northwest Syria.
