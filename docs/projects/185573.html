Project Summary

Health is one of the most critical area of operation that should reflect the exact needs of the people. Uninterrupted access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH), and proper medical waste management and cleaning in healthcare facilities is a key to ensure quality care and achieving Universal Health Coverage (UHC).
To improve the overall situation and increase the quality of healthcare services and ensure safe healthcare environment, this project aims to enhance infection prevention and control as well as the hygiene situation in health facilities.
For this purpose, HEKS/EPER:
- Will visit health care facilities (HCF) in host communities and collect information based on the WASH-FIT indicators through observation and interview methods by using Kobo Platform.
- Will conduct series of WASH-FIT training programs, as required, in order to build/enhance the capacity of HCF Managers/Doctors/Nurses/WASH focal point. Including installation of handwashing facilities, upgradation of waste management systems and distribution of hygiene kits.
- Will strengthen the existing monitoring mechanism by incorporating the HCFs and conduct follow-up monitoring visits/ supportive supervisions using at least one monitoring visit of each HCFs.
- Will organize trainings at Upazila level on different components, procedures, and importance of Healthcare waste management.

1 - Identified Needs

The district hosts more than 900’000 Rohingya Refugees. The local communities (Bangladeshi) are strongly affected by the influx. HEKS/EPER has implemented a WASH FIT program starting with Health facilities in the Rohingya camps in Mai 2019 and added Host Community Clinics in September 2019. In its third phase, which started in Mai 2020, the project was scaled to cover all 439 health facilities in the camps and the host community of the district. According to the WASH FIT (Water and Sanitation for Health Facility Improvement Tool) baseline data conducted at the beginning of the third phase in August 2020, only 40% of health facilities already met the water-related indicators, 33% met the sanitation-related targets, 39% met healthcare waste management targets, 35% met hygiene and IPC related targets, and 38% met the management targets. Good WASH services serve as barriers to human-to-humantransmission of the COVID-19 virus in homes, communities, health care facilities, and other public spaces. Improved WASH services in healthcare facilities can contribute towards sustainable improvements in the quality of healthcare services.In Bangladesh, there is almost no medical waste management system in place, especially in the primary healthcare facilities. Before COVID-19 hit Bangladesh, the country was struggling with the management of huge waste generated from healthcare facilities. COVID-19 has increased this burden dramatically. According to a newspaper article, at least 14500 tons of medical waste were generated in April 2020 across the country. As the management of medical waste is poor, this increased amount of waste poses a profound threat to the environment and might create a prolonged public health hazard. The national Medical Waste Management and Processing Rules of Bangladesh was introduced in 2008, but no systems were developed for the proper management of medical waste. Medical waste are often collected without proper segregation by untrained cleaning staff or waste handlers without wearing proper personal protective equipment (PPE). For Cox’s Bazar, the medical waste management system is not any difference from rest of the country, although waste management zones are available in Sadar Hospital and other Upazila Health Complexes. Nevertheless, the practice did not advance e significantly. According to the WASH FIT baseline data of government primary healthcare facilities in Cox’s Bazar, only 22% facilities had some types of waste management systems. According to WHO, many factors are responsible behind the absence of or poor waste management system including lack of awareness about the health hazards related to health-care waste, inadequate training in proper waste management, absence of waste management and disposal systems, insufficient financial and human resources and the low priority given to the topic.The key steps in proper medical waste management encompasses segregation of different waste, storage in color coded bins, collection, transportation, storage before final disposal and proper disposal as per standard protocols. And this must be done is a manner that does not pose risks of injury or infections to the healthcare workers, waste management workers, patients, cleaning staff and the community. In doing so, it is necessary that all the responsible parties including healthcare workers, waste management workers, and cleaning staff understand the importance of proper waste management and have adequate knowledge how to do it. This is where training comes. Such training of people related to medical waste generation and management is essential to ensure not only the safety of the patients and healthcare providers from infections, but also to stop the cycle of infections around it. Absence of proper training and guidelines result in improper waste segregation, handling, transportation, and disposal; and therefore, result in injuries and infections.
