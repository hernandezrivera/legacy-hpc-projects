Project Summary

The ongoing Israeli blockade has devastating consequences on all aspects of life, OCHA estimates 1.3M people in the Gaza Strip are in need of multisectoral services. The Gaza Strip has one of the highest unemployment rates in the world, and World Bank projections (2020) indicate that 64% of Gaza's population will live in poverty in 2021. On top of the chronic crisis, the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic and the recent May hostilities have further compounded the already fragile situation leaving families and caregivers unable to provide for their children’s basic needs, increasing the number of children displaying high levels of stress-related symptoms, requiring additional protection interventions aiming at reducing vulnerability and increasing protection. It is estimated that some 678,000 children in the oPt are in need of protection and MHPSS services (MSNA, 2021), corresponding to more than half (53%) of the children in Gaza. A RNA conducted by Tdh in June 2021 has shown that children in Gaza are exposed regularly to traumatic events, which may result for them in conditions such as anxiety disorders, PTSD, depression, and dissociative disorders.
The Protection Cluster has also identiﬁed provision of MHPSS services and case management as key interventions needed in the Gaza Strip. This Action is a response to such needs and it is consistent with HRP's second Objective of the Protection Cluster which is "Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conﬂict-related violence". The proposed activities include awareness on MHPSS aspects, the provision of child protection services, including case management, MHPSS services, and awareness-raising activities focusing on reducing the prevalence of GBV and on how to deal with a traumatized child. Structured MHPSS and case management for GBV survivors is also included. Overall, 12,550 persons will benefit from the project (2,500 girls, 2,500 boys, 3,775 women, and 3,775 men).
Of the targeted children, 1,000 will benefit from MHPSS services and PFA sessions, 300 children will benefit from case management and 100 will benefit from MHPSS and case management provided to children survivors of GBV. Community and family members and caregivers will also be included, through awareness activities. Workers in the child protection field will also benefit from this Action with trainings and specific helpers sessions. Of the total targeted, the provided protection services will be timely and good quality protection responses that are tailored according to children's needs and the best interest of boys and girls, equally. Under this Action, a total of 7,000 persons will beneﬁt from awareness-raising for persons at risk of GBV (4,000 persons) and how to deal with traumatized children victims of the current situation and general child protection (3,000 persons). In order to ensure a sustainable impact on the beneficiaries’ wellbeing, a cash support to meet urgent needs will be provided to 100 vulnerable families benefiting from protection services. The total allocated budget for this project is USD 400,000. FGD were conducted with the targeted beneﬁciaries, including children and adults from different age groups and gender, including seniors. They indicated the high needs in protection services, and they were involved in the design of the project, and proposed activities. During the implementation, beneﬁciaries will be involved in monitoring the project activities. To closely monitor and follow up on the results and achievements of the project, a Project Monitoring and Evaluation Plan will be developed at the start of the Action with the participation of the five partner organizations, including indicators, assessments, and means of veriﬁcation tools deﬁned in the logical framework. The Project team will coordinate with the relevant authorities, child protection actors, the Protection Cluster, GBV-sub-cluster, CP-AoR, FSS, and Cash Working Group.

1 - Needs

The ongoing 15 years of blockade had caused irreversible damage to the Gaza Strip’s economy and overall deterioration of social determinants of wellbeing. This situation has been exacerbated with the covid-19 pandemic and the May escalation, resulting in critical humanitarian consequences, and pushing families to use negative coping mechanisms. According to OCHA, about1.3M people in Gaza are in need multisectoral services (MSNA, 2021). Gaza Strip has one of the highest unemployment rates in the world (48%) (Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics-PCBS-, 2020). According to the World Bank, in the Economic Developments in the Palestinian Territories report, 24 November 2020, the poverty rate is planning to increase from 53% to 64% of Gaza's population. Before the recent escalation, based on the WFP Palestine Country Brief July 2021, 1.6 million people were not having access to adequate water, sanitation facilities and hygiene items. And it was estimated that 60% of the population was food insecure or vulnerable to food insecurity.Based on the Response to the escalation in the oPt Situation Report No. 8: 8-28 July 2021 by OCHA, during the last escalation, 260 Palestinians in Gaza have been killed by Israeli forces including 66 children, and 1,948 people injured, including 610 children. The Israeli retaliation resulted in people displacement, whose 8,220 people remain displaced in host families or rented accommodations. Damages reached also an important extend with the destruction of health and WASH infrastructures, schools, and various building destructions. The livelihood opportunities have been importantly affected by the destructions and the blockage affecting more than 62% of the households in Gaza, according to the Flash appeal 27 May 2021, Escalation of hostilities and unrest in the oPt. Tdh conducted a RNA in June 2021 showing that this deteriorated situation has exacerbated the need of protection and especially for children regarding psychosocial distress, mental disorders and a higher risk of GBV across the Strip. Already during the covid-19 pandemic, 67.7% of the participant to the Tdh’s survey (Impact of covid-19 on vulnerable children and their families in the Gaza Strip, Oct. 2020) reported an increase of the violence against children. More than half (53%) of all children in Gaza are in need of protection services (CP-AoR, Guidance for MHPSS aspects of child protection in the HNO/HRP 2021). Based on the Tdh’s RNA, it is found that war leaves enduring episodes in children and adolescents. During may's escalation, children face (to differing degrees) exposure to two types of traumatic events: type I (sudden traumatic event) and type II (protracted exposure to adverse events resulting in dysfunctional coping mechanisms). Children in Gaza are exposed to both, regularly. Notwithstanding their existing coping mechanisms, it is shown that children may suffer from conditions such as anxiety disorders, Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD), depression, dissociative disorders, behavioural disorders (especially aggression, asocial and violent behaviour) and substance abuse, more than their counterparts. In addition to the war consequences, according to the Palestinian Multiple Indicator Cluster (2019-2020) conducted by PCBS and UNICEF, more than 92% of the children (aged from 1 to 14 years) have experienced at least one type of violent disciplining methods during the last month, including 90% psychological aggression, and 28.5% subjected to severe physical punishment and 75% subject to any physical punishment. It is estimated that some 678,000 children in the oPt are in need of protection and MHPSS services (MSNA, 2021). It is estimated that some 198,000 children in the Gaza Strip alone are in need of structured protection and psychosocial support, while nearly 36,000 children require, while nearly 36,000 children require individual case management (HNO, 2021). The prevalence for mental health disorders is higher for girls and women than for boys and men, however the numbers from the governmental sector for 2019 and 2020 show that in oPt almost 60 % of mental health service users are male (MoH, 2020). Many respondents during the Tdh’s RNA (2021) pointed to the different impact of war on women, men, girls and boys and a perceived fear that this could exacerbate existing gender inequalities. Consequently, mental health interventions should take a gendered approach. The stigma around mental health is still higher for women and threatens their status in society. This also explains that according to a report from the PCBS, in 2019 only 1% of GBV survivors asked for assistance due to the lack of confidential services and fear of stigma. In general, children with disabilities and girls are more subjected to GBV, however, boys are at higher risk to be engaged in child labor. According to the Violence Survey in the Palestinian Society, from 2019, 35% of married Palestinian women have been subjected to violence by their husbands in the Gaza Strip (PCBS, 2020). Regarding the service providers in Gaza Strip, in the Assessment of MHPSS interventions in the West Bank and Gaza Strip, According to UNFPA, it is mentioned that level 1 and 2 services are provided in all governorates, while level 3 non-focused specialized mental health services and level 4 specialized services are more difficult to find (UNFPA, 2021). The Gaza Community Mental Health Programme (GCMHP) and the MoH provide those services, but they fail to answer the high demand (Child Protection Working Group 2019). All these factors illustrate the importance of providing children and youth with integrated protection services, including structured MHPSS and child protection case management. During the conducted focus group discussions, the high needs for protection services were also indicated. During the implementation, beneﬁciaries will be involved in monitoring the project activities.

2 - Activities

This Action aims to increase access to protection services including case management, MHPSS services, individual counseling to vulnerable children who have child protection concerns, including children display symptoms of psychological distress, children survivors of GBV, and children victims of different forms of child abuse and violence against children, and children with disabilities. Family engagement will be an integral part of the project activities to improve the protective environment of the children. The proposed activities will include: Activity 1: Awareness sessions on MHPSS available services that aims to decrease stigmas, increase utilization of available services, and to provide information on how to contact and benefit from MHPSS services. In total, 4,000 persons will be targeted (1,000 boys, 1,000 girls, 1,000 women, 1,000 men). Different approaches and methodologies will be used to reach the different groups across the society, especially targeting youth, women, vulnerable groups. Activity 2: Provision of MHPSS services (structured and non-structured MHPSS) and psychological first aid (PFA) sessions to 1,000 children (500 boys, 500 girls) and 500 caregivers (250 women, 250 men). Different methodologies developed by Tdh or other specialized organizations will be used for the structured MHPSS support, depending on the needs and age of the targeted groups, like: “You create”, to create a space for youth affected by adversity to strengthen skills to lead their peers in arts-based activities that support wellbeing, resilience and social cohesion, for youth between 15 to 25; or for children from 4 to 14, the Movement, Games, Sport, and Creativity (MGSC) methodology which uses sport, games and creativity as a psychosocial tool to ultimately develop the personal and social skills of vulnerable children, and therefore improve their resilience and psychosocial wellbeing. Activity 3: Provision of case management to 400 children (160 boys, 240 girls), including 100 children survivors of GBV (10 boys, 90 girls). In total, the provided MHPSS services and case management will target both, boys and girls. Activity 4: A total of 30 animators (15 women, 15 men) will be trained on these methodologies and on the safe identification of signs of psychosocial distress such as behavioral changes and in the identification of patterns of exposure to protection risks affecting children. Children will be referred for case management and/or other MHPSS service providers, as needed. Activity 5: Self-care and stress management sessions will be organized on a quarterly basis for all partner’s staff and service providers’ staff (30 persons: 15 women, 15 men), as staff needs to release the pressure and vent the daily stressors they face. Activity 6: A total of 4,000 persons (1,000 men, 1,000 women, 1,000 boys, and 1,000 girls) will beneﬁt from awareness-raising for persons at risk of GBV and more broadly awareness on women rights and PSEA, as a remedial activity to tackle the issue of GBV, since the prevalence of GBV is higher for girls and women. Activity 7: Referral pathways will be also done in case of a specialized support is required, and 40 dignity kits will be distributed or cash for dignity kit will be provided based on the most relevant modality. Activity 8: Under this Action, awareness sessions on how to deal with traumatized children- victims of the current situation and general child protection will be conducted targeting 3,000 members (1,500 men and 1,500 women). Protection services will be provided to all children in need, regardless of their age groups and their ability status; services will be also provided to their families regardless of the gender and age of the head of the household and their localities. Activity 9: Training will be provided to 50 persons (25 women, 25 women) from Tdh and partners on PSEA and child safeguarding and protection mainstreaming. The training will be based on national and Tdh PSEA material. The training will be conducted by either Tdh expert by an external expert. Activity 10: To increase the impact of the protection services, a total of 100 vulnerable families (560 persons: 180 boys, 180 girls, 100 women, 100 men) who have children with protection concerns will benefit from the cash support to meet the basic needs and to avoid resorting to negative coping mechanism such as early marriage, engaging children into labour, and withdrawal from schools. Each family will receive a total of ILS 1,046 unconditional cash support to meet the basic needs, per the recommendation of the Cash Working Group. A customized version of Unified Vulnerability Assessment Tool (UVAT) will be used to select the most vulnerable families. Tdh will coordinate this component with the Cash Working Group, the Food Security Sector, the MoSD, and other relevant actors.

9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

During the course of the project, measures will be put in place to ensure that targeted communities can provide feedback on all the proposed services in a secure and confidential manner. Complaints boxes will be/ are available at different locations in which activities will be conducted, so beneficiaries can anonymously provide their comments and feedback. Beneficiaries' feedback will be taken seriously and will be used to steer the implemented activities. Due to COVID-19 related restriction measures, if any, Tdh and partners will put in place feedback and complaints mechanisms that beneficiaries could access remotely. The Meacham will involve sending SMS messages to beneficiaries that explain how they can contact Tdh and partners either by phone or through a generic email. Tdh and partners will run randomly selected phone calls to collect feedback from beneficiaries. The feedback will be collected on a monthly basis by Tdh teams. Mechanisms of collecting feedback will involve data collection through (1) pre and post questionnaires will be used to evaluate the effectiveness of activitiesimplemented, (2) periodically, intervening beneficiaries to ask about the perceived quality of the provided services, and (3) conducting focus group discussions to assess the quality of provided services from Beneficiaries perspectives. Protection beneficiaries from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse will be considered a priority. Along with the abovementioned feedback and complaints mechanism, awareness-raising of PSEA and reporting mechanisms will be conducted to increase community awareness about preventing and reporting Sexual Exploitation and Abuse cases.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Tdh and partners will fully respect, protect, and full beneficiaries’ safety and fully respect their dignity. Tdh and partners will achieve this by protecting beneficiaries’ personal and sensitive data such as names and ID numbers. Tdh and partners will provide services that are trailed according to identified needs of beneficiaries, taking into account the context, and will have in place all the mitigation measures to ensure beneficiaries' safety. As a good practice for Tdh, no action would be planned and/or implemented without the child’s agreement, under the supervision of her/his legal gradient. Additionally, Tdh will not publish or report any data that could reveal any identification data, all publications/reports will include only aggregated data. Tdh and partner organizations will also involve beneficiaries in as many decisions as possible, including monitoring the project implementation. All interventions will seek to avoid exacerbating the situation for children by ensuring properly planned and implemented interventions that take into account children’s and their family's needs to access to humanitarian services. All services will be provided in a safe protection environment. To ensure the beneficiaries' protection related to the cash assistance, tailored household financial management visits, and financial management coaching for the family will be conducted ensuring that the cash intervention does not cause harm. In addition, a communication channel in case harm resulted from the cash intervention will be available. Tdh and partners have zero-tolerance for exposing beneficiaries to violence and or harmful practices, all measures will be taken into account to grate this, including full compliance to Tdh Child Safeguarding Policy (CSP), PSEA Policy, Code of Conduct, and confidential mechanisms. All staff members are trained on the CSP, PSEA, and the Code of Conduct and have signed the policies.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In respect of equality and impartiality, all identified cases, regardless of their gender, age groups, and disability status will receive the protection services according to their assessed and identified needs. All Project beneficiaries will have equal access to services, including beneficiaries with disabilities and beneficiaries living in the Access Restricted Areas and the most vulnerable areas like camps. Tdh will also target equally men and women without any regard to any discriminatory factors such as age. The proposed activities are based on the different needs that were identified through different methods, mainly focus group discussions, monitoring visits, and interviews with key informants. During the design phase, project beneficiaries were engaged heavily and all their needs were included in the project activities. During the project design, beneficiaries were also consulted through conducting a series of FGDs and interviews and in-depth interviews. Beneficiaries will also be part of the project monitoring. Children were also involved in the assessment and design of the project and will be actively engaged in monitoring the project activities. Finally, as with any advocacy product of this project, children will propose, design, and implement such products. To overcome access problems related to COVID-19 mitigation measures, Tdh and partner will ensure access to children living with disabilities through obtaining lists of children with disabilities from organizations that provide services to children with disabilities. Also, Tdh and partners have already a list of children with disabilities in need or previously bene ted from MHPSS services. Those children will be contacted using different blended methodologies that involve in-person modalities and remote modalities. If needed, Tdh and Partner might conduct MHPSS services in places that are accessible to children with disabilities that could involve home visits and provision of services in grassroots organizations. Also, Tdh and partners will develop the MHPSS manual that could be used by children with disabilities themselves. Atfaluna Society for Deaf Children has already developed such tools for children with hearing disabilities.
