Project Summary

The aim of the project is to strengthen health information systems for emergency response and resilience across the country. The information generated from the health
information systems provide up-to-date information on functionality of facilities, availability of services, and accessibility of beneficiaries. All health partners are/would
report including those operating public, NGO, private health facilities. To ensure quality health information products; WHO conducts joint monitoring visits with MoH to health facilities to follow up on HIS issues at health facility level (reporting, challenges, needs…) in addition to HeRAMS internal and external evaluation exercises.
In northwestern Syria the information system HeRAMS is used to track the functionality, accessibility, infrastructure, services and staffing levels of hospitals and health care facilities. WHO uses this information to strengthen facilities in areas shown to be in greatest need. The information generated from the health information systems provide up-to-date information on functionality of health facilities, availability, and accessibility of services. It is planned to ensure that all healthcare Cross-Border actors will be able to report to the system, including field implementing NGOs and health facilities operated by private sector. As the result of this project, it is important to ensure quality health information products available for the analysis purposes, what will be ensured by WHO/Health Cluster joint monitoring visits with local health authorities and applying available HIS tools, including the HeRAMS.

4 - Needs

The war challenges of inaccessibility and the deteriorating security situation, have made it difficult to monitor the availability of health resources and delivery of essential healthcare services to the people in need in the affected areas. The COVID-19 pandemic is putting extra burdens on the already fragile healthcare system with additional emerging repercussions. Preparedness and response to the pandemic across Syria is affected by limited information and a lack of trained health workers, facilities and medical equipment necessary to respond to the increase in infections, especially among the most vulnerable populations.The health information system is designed to collect data and generate information to assist managers to carry out evidence-based decision making at all levels of health service delivery. The needs of information are defined by the routine functionality of the health system and the extra needs of information of multiple stakeholders involved in the delivery of humanitarian interventions to address the needs generated by the war. In this regard, since 2011 the HIS has kept adapting to the new emerging needs generated by the different actors involved in the health sector such as NGOs, multilaterals, donors and other local and international institutions. HeRAMS (Health Resources and Services Monitoring System) has been established and implemented since 2013 in Syria. The HeRAMS and 4Ws which have been implemented by WHO was used to support the cross border operations of WHO and health partners covering public health and NGO structures; it has led to the development and implementation of a HIS system. The HIS implemented in limited health facilities requires further expansion to all health facilities.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Throughout the Syria crisis, national health information systems were disrupted, and the need of information has increased. The most affected areas, such as hard to reach and areas with severity scale more than 3 were considered the most challenging in terms of providing timely, relevant and reliable information on the available health resources and services for decision making.2. The COVID-19 pandemic has only worsened the situation, adding new layers of complexity and hardship. Therefore, the availability, access and sustainability of essential health services and medical supplies was and continues to be always in risk due to the security situation, internal displacement and donors’ support3. Lack of data on disability and sensitive gender issues may impact proper planning and response to the needs of such vulnerable groups. 4. Violence is still a main issue facing healthcare providers. According to the HNO 2020, Attacks on health care have left 14.5 million people in 42 districts below emergency standards for hospital beds.5. up to 70% of health workers has left Syria and many others have been injured or killed. 6. Regarding the health system risks the recent assessments indicate the need for referral capacity, including ambulances to overcome gaps in secondary care and connect patients to life-saving care. 7. Information systems’ data is stored in WHO servers. While data high availability is taken into account. Most of the systems either Microsoft programs or in-house developed system 8. As for mitigating the shortage of information, some information systems, tools, and assessments were initiated. 8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: - Lack of information about the health situation in some hard to reach areas.- Limitation of financial support to meet the needs of strengthening the health information systems with health partners (Public, SARC, NGOs, and other health partners) - Lack of professional staff in hard to reach areas which may result in reduce the quality of information. - Difficulties in obtaining timely approvals for comprehensive assessments in high risk areas. - Limitations on inter-hub information-sharing is an ongoing risk. - Loss of trust/ confidence in WHO and/or health cluster by stakeholders if data is not well-protected and/or information-sharing protocols are not upheld. - Failure to get approvals for assessments, missions or monitoring exercises.- Non-renewal of this resolution may therefore affect cross-border delivery of medical supplies and equipment, Mitigation: - Conducting site visits by HIS-FPs especially hard to reach areas, reconciled areas, and regained areas to assess the health situation with continuous monitoring of health status of population in these areas especially in the immediate post-reconciliation phase. - Allocating sufficient resources to meet the needs of strengthening the health information systems - Using standard tools developed by WCO; such as “monitoring tool of delivered assistance by WHO” and others. - Strengthening the capacity building of the staff across the country focusing on hard to reach areas at different level on HeRAMS and other HIS areas. - Linkage of the emergency health information systems into the planned national HMIS strengthening initiative to collect health burden in all areas.- WHO is in continuous discussions to ensure its mandate is maintained.
