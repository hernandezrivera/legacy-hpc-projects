Project Summary

As per OCHA flash floods report, Since July 2021, up to 166,379 people have been affected by flash floods in Afgooye, Marka and Wanla Weyn districts in Lower Shabelle region when riverbanks broke along River Shabelle. The floods have displaced in Afgoye alone more than 82,000 people, inundated homes, wells and schools and destroyed large swathes of farmland and crops. Eight villages in Afgooye have been severely affected by the flash floods creating a dire humanitarian crisis in the area which has exacerbated the already delicate protective environment of caregivers, boys and girls. The impact of the ongoing flooding has also been compounded by economic and social impacts of COVID-19. Floods displaced hundreds of people and destroyed at least 60,000 hectares of farmland in Somalia's Middle Shabelle and Hiraan region around Jowhar, the regional capital, when the Shabelle river burst its banks last week, Officials from office of the Prime Minister said on Thursday, 3rd June 2021. Women, adolescent girls and children continue to hardest hit by flooding, droughts and displacements in the first and second quarter of 2021. In total 66,000 individuals have been totally displaced from their dwellings out of which 45% are women and girls, while 28% are children. For Beletweyne in particular - Out of the 3,593 individuals that were displaced by the Shabelle river floods, 2795 individuals of the displaced (77%) are female, children and girls
On the last CP and GBV integrated SHF project, SOYDA identified and supported reintegration of 896 unaccompanied and separated children (517 boys and 379 girls) to their traditional and relatives households, this was done through the active community volunteers, project staff as well as the trained formal an informal child protection focal points within Merka, which was 23.6 % more than the anticipated with same resources.
In Benadir, Hirshabelle and Southwest state, SOYDA will support prevention and response to child protection) incidences and separation of children through comprehensive Identification, Documentation, Tracing and Reunification (IDTR) and case management interventions among Internally Displaced Persons populations and host communities. SOYDA CP rapid assessment report (June 2021) show that there are significant barriers for affected children seeking support that needs to be improved: 1) Limited awareness of the consequences of Child Protection and available services. Gaps to address are: 1) strengthen community engagement and awareness of CP and services available, and promote community-based mechanisms for; 2) improve the quality of response services, particularly medical care, Psycho social support, CM and child safe spaces; 3) Engage with formal and informal security and justice actors on human rights and protection of children and support in referring cases; 4) strengthen knowledge and practice of humanitarian actors on child protection minimum standards. Displacement and family breakdown which means children are at high risk of separation which puts them at risk of trafficking, Child labor, sexual exploitation and recruitment in armed groups. Therefore, there is a crucial need to increase and sustain the provision comprehensive case management, psycho-social support, interim care (including the integration of kinship care and kafalah sytems ehre family reunification is not possible) and tracing services.
Through non-discrimination principle will ensure procedures wich support and advocate for the access all, displaced and including children to protection systems, regardless of their age, gender, ethnicity, religion, nationality, or disability. Approach ensures that all persons of concern, including boys and girls children and adolescents, enjoy equal rights, recognizing diversity.
