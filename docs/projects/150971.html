Project Summary

This project is an integrated response around Nutrition. It is intended to mitigate the physical and protection consequences of drought on populations and communities directly affected in Bakol, Bay and Hiran region, in particular, Ceelbarde, Rabdhure, Baidoa and Baladweyne town. The main objective of this project is to provide integrated life-saving emergency Nutrition services to 19,700 drought affected direct beneficiaries and intends to achieve the following, capacity building of nutrition staff on IMAM. Referral of malnourished children with complicated cases will be done to the Outpatient Therapeutic Programme (OTP) centers and directly linked with primary health care services managed by HIDIG and other actors in the program areas thus providing more holistic opportunity to the target beneficiaries HIDIG will closely coordinate with Local MOH as well as Federal level MOH and social mobilizers, elders all involved in activities on mobilizing communities. To support increase access to basic nutrition services in the area

Needs

Food Security and Nutrition Assessment Unit Somalia - Famine Early Warning Systems Network (FSNAU-FEWSNET) post-Gu assessment3 indicates sustained high-level risk of famine in some parts of Somalia by the end of the year. This is due to a combination of severe food insecurity, high acute malnutrition and high burden of disease. The projected number of children who are, or who will be acutely malnourished, has increased since the beginning of the year to 1.2 million, including over 231,829 (including IDPs) who have or will suffer life-threatening severe acute malnutrition (SAM) over the next one-year period from Sep 2018-Sep 2018.Malnutrition in Somalia has deteriorated with around 1.4 million children suffering from GAM, of which 173,594 have SAM. Somalia continues to have one of the worst infant and young child feeding and micro-nutrient indicators in the world. In addition to the 1.1 million protracted IDPs, approximately 954 007 people have been displaced due to the drought. IDPs represent three quarters of the people in acute livelihood and food crisis in the country. However, post Gu august,2017 nutrition estimates indicate a total of 16,355 <5 children boys, girls and 4,089 pregnant and lactating in need of urgent intervention in Rabdhure and Ceelbarde district of Bakol region with an emergency caseload of 2,297 SAM and 7,952 GAM as well as having high GAM of 18.7% and 5.4 SAM prevalence’s cases in both district, on the same note Baladweyne district have also emergency caseload of 4,678 <5 children boys, girls and 2,421 PLW with a SAM and GAM of 3.2% and 16.5%. The high morbidity reported two weeks prior to assessment with the highest prevalence reported in Baidoa IDPs (34.9 %) and (30.6%); Baladweyne district (41.8% and (42.8%) respectively with most of these rates below 50 percent. Consequently, any measles outbreak will further escalate the morbidity and mortality rate found from the current assessment. There is need to scale up the existing intervention

>Scale up admission, treatment and management of 10100 moderately malnourished boys, girls <5 children and 6000 pregnant and lactating women in all target OTP site through coordination and referral with health and livelihood programs in Bakool,Bay and Hiran regions.>Provide preventive and therapeutic treatment to acutely and moderately malnourished children 5500, boys, 5500 girls and 6000 pregnant and lactating mothers linked with health in Elbarde, Rabdhure,Baidoa and Baladweyne district.>Establish and support 10 mother to mother support group comprising of at least 400 mothers.>Conduct 6 community awareness campaign through OTP outreach services to vulnerable 5500 boys, 5500 girls and 6000 pregnant and lactating women.>Capacity build 30 community health workers/volunteers and promoters (15 men and 15 women) to provide active case finding, and referral services.>Train 20 HRDO technical nutrition staff (10 male and 10 female) on effective management of IMAM/BSNP and monitoring and evaluation in the project target areas.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

This project targets areas with high concentration of IDPs including Baidoa and Baladweyne and pastoralist in Ceelbarde and Rabdhure therefore possible community tensions between IDPs and host communities is a risk for displaced populations who may face animosity from host communities who already feel overburdened and with limited services. With the IDPs living in camps, many of them not legalized, constructing MCH/nutrition center may be a challenge exposing them to lack of comprehensive and timely care. To overcome these challenges, HIDIG will ensure IDPs have access to a comprehensive access to services within the IDP camps by establishing mobile OTP teams in consultation with the host communities and local authorities. Where not possible provided mobile outreach. Based on the nutrition Cluster Guideline on inclusion, both displaced and host communities will be consulted about needs and assess whether education interventions could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the IDPs and host communities.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Lack of desegregated data impedes well planned interventions based on needs. HIDIG intends to overcome this by having a need based assessment and disaggregated data so that the special needs of IDPs beneficiaries and those with special gender needs are taken care of. Due to poor health and nutrition infrastructure, many centers do not have proper planning so that those with disabilities can easily seek services. However, HIDIG will ensure services provided reach the most vulnerable IDPs and pastoralist in the far areas through identification and prioritization so that discrimination and exclusion is avoided. Location, distance and access routes to the education sites and services will be accessible to all members of the boys and girls, including the most vulnerable IDPs children. To support the inclusion of women teachers HIDIG will support training of female members through employment and capacity enhancement. To ensure equity, mobile/outreach teams will be used to reach rural communities.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Building community structures including local temporary learning space that will ensure tension is minimized. Staff will be trained on human rights based approach where all people should be treated equally, especially those who ran from conflict and violence. Communities fleeing violence will be immediately supported through access to immediate nutrition services without any discrimination. Where IDPs are involved, sites that support them will be built in consultation with them and the host communities. Marginalized groups will be prioritized so that they don’t feel excluded, which can otherwise aggravate their situation if they have run from violence.
