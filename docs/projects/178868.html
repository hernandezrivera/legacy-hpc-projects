Project Summary

The project will address the needs of both IDP and Host Communities in the countryside of Idleb and Western and Northern countryside of Aleppo with an emphasis on continuity of current projects during COVID19 pandemic. SRD will provide integrated protection activities within health facilities, prevention and risk mitigation activities through awareness-raising sessions, in addition to response activities through case management approach and solid referral pathway. Psychosocial Support Services (PSS) will be provided in the currently functioning safe spaces to enhance the resilience of survivors and disabled vulnerable men, women, boys and girls. All activities will be delivered considering prevention measures recommended by WHO, health cluster and protection & COVID-19 Task forces guidelines.
SRD will provide protection, child protection, and GBV services through women and girls safe space (WGSS), Community Centers or child friendly spaces associated with community-based outreach services in rural Idleb (Termanin, Aqrabat, and Kelly communities etc) and Northern Aleppo (Albab, Alsalamah, and Qabasin communities etc). While SRD provides integrated GBV, Psycho-Social Support, and Early Childhood Development services, Emergency services and pro monitoring in currently functioning SRD health facilities. Integrated protection and shelter teams are collaborated to improve living conditions integrating protection mechanisms to ensure accessibility, privacy, and safety for persons with disabilities and vulnerable groups. SRD provides center-based services and outreach services in the affiliated areas including community-based protection, CP and GBV structured awareness sessions through outdoor activities.
Integrating the two and streamlining outreach activities will increase access and reduce barriers for vulnerable groups. Health centers proved to be a safe and confidential access point to access GBV services and this successful model is therefore replicated and continued under this project. SRH services will include a focus on adolescents and family planning utilizing young mothers club through raising awareness of relevant services available which include psychosocial support, clinical management of rape cases and providing safe spaces for women and girls.
The objectives of this project are aligned with the most crucial protection needs as indicated by the Protection Cluster, as it will increase and ensure continual access to GBV services and alleviate suffering of those requiring critical care in countryside Idleb, Western and Northern countryside Aleppo.
Qualified and trained psychosocial workers will be present in the safe spaces and PSS rooms to manage the activities and provide private sessions for GBV survivors, referred cases, or other individuals seeking focused specialized PSS services. The work will include instruction on integrated case management, communication skills, community protection practices, behavior change communication, maternal and child health, hygiene promotion, nutrition, GBV survivor support, reporting techniques, cultural sensitivity, and referral pathways. Protection awareness messages will be delivered to beneficiaries through daily household visits, prioritizing messages of civil documentation, HLP, child labor, child marriage, and GBV. Referrals will be made for those with protection needs or other forms of support to facilities and services providers within the referral pathway to minimize the risks of vulnerability. Provision of specialized and community-based child protection services considering what shall be in the best interests of the child through case management services, integrated Emergency Response Outreach Team, structured and unstructured PSS, parenting skills, early childhood Development, referrals, and addressing children and caregivers’ vulnerabilities that are related to disability, physical/psychological injuries, severe neglect, lack of appropriate care.

4 - Needs

Psychosocial distress is reported in 23% of surveyed households demonstrating the toll on child mental wellbeing with immediate. Children comprise half of affected population. Grave child rights violations remain a concern, with children at risk of being killed or injured, recruited and used in hostilities, tortured, detained, abducted and sexually abused HNO21. Children are going to schools and, on the way, they are at risk of being kidnapped or harassed. Girls are not accessing schools because families fear harassment and exploitation due to long distance between the camp and schools MSNA. Women can’t proof their current marital status unless she gets a separation document and has civil doc to enter a camp. Economic deprivation, lack of livelihood opportunities, discrimination, exclusion, and socio-cultural norms have led to continued normalization of GBV and severely impacted the PSS wellbeing. W&G continue to confirm that GBV services have a positive impact on their wellbeing, with Women and Girls Safe Spaces and community well-being centers often serving as the only place to seek safety and receive essential life-saving services. Based on surveys led by SRD, findings showed COVID-19 restrictions affected employment, income, education, availability of and access to services, inc. Pro. Thus there is a need to target marginalized women and girls with the empowerment trainings besides income generating activities. NWS wide, 74% of IDP HHs have at least 1 member with disabilities HNAP. As of Feb 2021, approximately 1.7 million IDPs were living in 1385 camps. 5 - Implementation of activities

Centers continue offering the following services: Case management and referral, vocational training and prevention empowerment activities, GBV awareness sessions and protection monitoring. In order to remove physical barriers for women and girls with disabilities, in coordination with the local council, an additional caseworker is recruited that is specialized in disabilities. Plus, elderly and disabled people will be identified and helped with IPA cash module. Case management has in-kind/cash funds to cover the needs of vulnerable beneficiaries who cannot be referred to other services. Un/Structured PSS activities, CPM, parenting skills, and early childhood development are provided to children and their parents, identifying children with vulnerabilities to be referred to CP case managers. All the mobile staff will be trained on emergency plan and therefore ready to expand coverage in order to conduct rapid protection assessment and protection monitoring to identify risks faced by WMBGs, providing emergency service package for newly displaced households. persons with disabilities and older persons, persons with protection concerns; particularly woman- and child-headed households or recent IDPs are served through integrated community-based approach. Protection-specific M&E tools and SAG data segregation is in place led by trained ME team, referring to GBV ME Tool adopted by SC. That includes but not limited to (M&E of outreach activities, kits distribution, PSS structured activities, IPA , WGSS checklist and learning process. Feedback and complaints mechanisms are obviously present in all delivery points. Specific M&E tools developed for PSS structured activities and Child Friendly Accountability Tools for children.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The population displaced and specifically IDPs women and girls might face challenges in accessing protection services due to multiple barriers on where/why to access. For PSS: lack of sufficient support/supervision for specialized staff; limited specialized mental health professionals inside Syria. Referrals from and to other services might be disrupted due to the security situation. Adolescents with disabilities might face challenges in accessing the center to attend young mothers’ club sessions. Women might face challenges accessing the WGSS due to child rearing responsibilities. Focus on quantity of service providers trained instead of on quality of training, relevance and impact on knowledge and improved service provision. High turn over and loss of capacity . Increased risk of inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages across members. Privacy concerns along with confidentiality and safety might prevent women & girls to access services. Negative coping mechanisms are increasingly being reported due to the economic downturn and COVID-19. Boys are usually facing child labor/begging and girls are facing early marriage and suicide cases. There is a risk of discrimination or perceptions of discrimination if a service is provided to specific groups or individuals at the exclusion of others. risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (harassment) of women and girls. Exclusion of marginalized groups (elderly, disabilities, adolescents) to receive comprehensive support. Risk that existing traditional practices and community structures conflict with child protection principles (e.g. child rights messaging is perceive as a threat).

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

The likelihood of Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information are linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups and its impact is rated high protection risk analysis. Moreover, Exploitation by service provider or by other actor in the community. And low knowledge on PSEA and other types of exploitation and where to report. SRD has policies, procedures, and other measures in place that relate to sexual exploitation, abuse, and harassment.Sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment are never acceptable, under any circumstances and SRD has a zero-tolerance policy. SRD recognizes that achieving a significant reduction in SEAH is a long-term mission. Zero tolerance is not the same as zero incidents. Reports of incidents may increase as organizations improve safeguards and reporting channels. Increasing reports may indicate a growing awareness of SEAH and changing attitudes, with survivors feeling more comfortable to report and organisations more likely to take action. The reporting of incidents and responses is an indicator that the risk of SEAH is being managed. Such measures taken are train staff/service providers regularly with context appropriate information on code of conduct and PSEA, Raise awareness on PSEA and other types of exploitation reporting ways. Establish good relation with local authorities, clarify humanitarian principles and stick to them. SRD is an active member in UN SEA Network.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Mobilization activities take place to share information with the community focusing on IDPs women and girls and boys about the GBV services safe spaces. Regular meetings are conducted with NGOs services providers to facilitate referral pathways and project staff are regularly updates on the up-to-date service map. One of the GBV mobile outreach clinic trained on GBV emergency response to respond rapidly to new displacements with safety Audit, PFA and dignity kits. Specific budget is allocated for transportation costs for the most at risk individuals. Tensions linked to overcrowding may lead to an escalation of partner violence and other forms of domestic violence these risks might not be identified and rectified. Advocating for appropriate and adequate household rehabilitation to be provided considering privacy. We will include women, girls with disability and at-risk groups to determine such things. Designated car will be provided to facilitate the access of adolescents with disabilities to the girls’ club at the WGSS. In consultation with female community members ensure mobile tents or any other safe space that will mitigate the risks of violating confidentiality and privacy. Protection officer in charge of case management supervision. Budget and well trained SRD staff ensured for sustainable capacity building strategy that shows impacts with pre and post assessment, follow ups, mentoring, coaching initiatives and structured supervision of case managers and midwives. Non-protection Staff is sensitized around attitude in terms of sensitive service provision. Children cases with high protection concerns like CAAFAG will be referred to specialized actors and support with IPA when needed.
