Project Summary

This project is aligning with cluster objectives that aiming to ensure crisis-affected population will have access to basic water services, adequate (basic or limited), protected, and gender-sensitive sanitation services, and hygiene promotion services to reduce mortality and morbidity, and to contribute to building resilience to recurrent shocks and improving vulnerable people’s access to basic WASH services, as well as enhancing the prevention and mitigation of protection risks and respond to protection needs, strengthen community resilience, and enhance risk mitigation measures. The project will target 248880 (66003 girls,63415 boys, 62120 women, and 57342 men) individuals in need living in 19 localities within 8 SCI priority states including Blue Nile, North Darfur, North Kordofan, South Kordofan, Centeral Darfur, West Darfur, Sennar, and Red Sea states. The project will be focusing in provision of water supply at community and institutional level including construction, upgrading, improving, and rehabilitation of water sources, in addition to connection of water to schools, health facilities, and child friendly spaces. The project will contribute to elimination of open defecation within our operation areas through enhance the capacity of local communities and mobilizing targeted communities to construct their own latrines, this in line with the construction of emergency latrines during emergencies for people affected by crises. The project will also contribute to solid and medical management efforts through provision of garbage and collection tools, support disposal of solid wastes, construction of medical incinerators at health facilities to facilitate safe disposal of medical wastes. The project will focus on improving hygiene situation and status within the targeted communities to mitigate and reduce the risk of diseases outbreaks as a result of poor WASH services through general hygiene campaigns, awareness sessions, home visits as well as strengthening the capacity of local community committees such as hygiene promotors, conduct training and establishing hygiene clubs at schools and child friendly spaces, this will be accompanied with provision of hygiene items such as soap to promote hand washing practices as mitigation measure to prevent disease outbreaks as well as supporting female with basic menstrual hygiene items to facilitate safe and dignified menstrual management for female at menstrual age. Mover over the project will support families who has malnourished children with hygiene items including soap, jeri-cans and aqua tabs to address underline causes of malnutrition.The project will create synergies ,complement, and integrate with Health, Nutrition and Food security within the targeted areas and worked with other sectors to address root causes of malnutrition and factors contributing to diseases outbreaks. The project will be implemented in 12 months with estimated budget at $5,089,220.

1 - Needs

As per Humanitarian Response Plan 2021, WASH sector estimates that about 8 million people need water, sanitation and hygiene services. These people include IDPs, returnees, non-displaced and host communities including conflict-affected people, people at risk of epidemics and floods, and other vulnerable residents. Out of 8 million people in need, WASH sector will target 4.7M with the highest severity of needs including 2.2 M IDPs in (Darfur’s, Kordofan, and Blue Nile states), 657,960 Returnees, and 5.09 M. are volunerable residents. 3.9 million lack access to improved water sources, 5.8 million people lack access to improved sanitation and 7.9 million people lack access to Hygiene services. South, North, Central Darfur’s, North and South Kordofan’s, Red Sea, Sennar , and Blue have very acute as well as chronic WASH needs (severe, very high, high and medium on severity scale). Unprecedented levels of rainfall began in Sudan in July and have continued to adversely affect population across Sudan Blue Nile, River Nile , and Sennar are amongst the most affected states, while floods have also affected vulnerable populations in Red Sea. Nearly 875,000 people have had their houses damaged or destroyed and over 150 died. The destruction of sanitation facilities, alongside the lack of access to clean water, creates a severe risk of increased infection, waterborne and vector-borne diseases, as well as undermining measures designed to control the transmission of COVID-19. Malaria, dengue, and cholera are endemic in several parts of Sudan, and the risk of these and other waterborne and vector-borne disease increases with floods and stagnant water. Over 10 million people are now at risk of contracting waterborne diseases, and 4.5 million are exposed to vector-borne conditions, North Darfur and East Darfur, Red Sea and Sennar shows very acute as well as chronic WASH needs (severe, high and medium on severity scale). Access to WASH in Institutions (schools and primary health centers) is also limited. Two thirds of the existing schools do not have improved sanitation facilities and a quarter of them do not have access to clean water, this is further compounded by the lack of gender segregated sanitation facilities including for menstrual hygiene management; and ease of access to the physically disabled children. This negatively impacts children’s attendance and enrollment, especially girls.AS per HNO2021; North and West Kordofan, East and South Darfur, White Nile and Red Sea states have very acute as well as chronic WASH needs (severe, very high, high and medium on severity scale), 32 locality including Al Gonub, and Sinkat in Red Sea, Abu Gebaiha in South Kordofan, Sodari in North Kordofan, Altadamon in Blue Nile severe and acute WASH need. According to Federal Ministry of Health (FMOH); by 28 November 2020, 17810 confirmed COVID-19 cases and 1249 deaths were reported in the country since the first case confirmed in March 2019, cases are increasing within the second wave of COVID-19 which requires extra efforts from all WASH and health partners to enforce prevention measures at community and institutions to mitigate infections.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The planned WASH interventions under this project will include provision of safe drinking water for both households and institutional level through construction, upgrading and/or rehabilitation of water sources, water connections, and installation of water containers, and water quality monitoring including provision of water purifications. Also, the project will focus on elimination of open defecation practices among communities through construction of emergency and permeant HHs and intuitions latrines, raising the awareness and build the capacity of local communities to promote hygiene practices and to be more capable to manage their facilities. The project will contribute to solid and medical waste management through support affected communities with appropriate tools and facilities as well as raising the awareness initiatives. Hygiene promotion will be more focusing on improving hygiene status and prevention of diseases outbreaks. Menstrual hygiene management for women and girls will be supported through ensuring female at menstrual age have access to appropriate menstrual items to restore their dignity. The project will contribute to capacity building of WASH community structures through training and strengthening existing committees, and /or establishing new community committees such as hygiene promotion volunteers, water quality monitoring volunteers, WASH committees, and hygiene clubs at schools and child friendly spaces targeting children as an entry points to deliver key hygiene massages for their community and siblings. The project will also be contributing to capacity building of WASH staff at locality level through engaging them in capacity building programs, follow up and monitoring of the project activities.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project designed to consider protection issues and concerns especially for female. Latrines will be designed to ensure maximum protection, privacy and dignity for women, and will be located near to dwellings to mitigate exposure to SGBV incidents especially during nights, also the needs of people with disabilities and eldery will be considered during establishing water points and other community facilities such as communal latrines. The project will ensure adherance to child safe guarding protection, and core humanitarian principles during implementation of the project to ensure all community members and especially children were protected, not harmed by our actions. The project considers conflict sensitivity by understanding the situation, examining how each activity may influence the conflict and work to maximize the positive and minimize the negative impacts of the proposed interventions. Both the newly IDPs and host communities will benefit from the project interventions. Save the Children will strive to ensure that the proposed interventions will minimize unforeseen or unintended negative effects on the targeted population which could potentially expose them to further physical and psychosocial risks. Save the Children will work closely with its various stakeholders (community members, government partners, local NGOs) to analyze the security situation and to avoid tensions between host communities and IDPs through its holistic approach to projects whereby both old and new IDPs as well as host communities are targeted based on the prioritization of needs.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Stakeholders’ participation and accountability to affected people is part of SCI’s core values. In practice, our participation and accountability approaches covers the following:• Regular, timely and accessible information sharing• Participation – discussing together, deciding together and working together• Proactively seeking feedback from children and communities and handling their complaints• Generate data and evidence for continuous learningSCI is signatory to Core Humanitarian Standard, and considering this SCI Sudan has already established strong accountability mechanisms which include three critical components of information sharing, participation, and complaints and feedback for the targeted communities and children. SCI will pprovide regular, timely and accessible information to targeted communities about:• Who we are and the principles we adhere to; • Project interventions and what SCI intends to deliver (e.g. what people are entitled to receive and when); • Our staff and how we expect our staff to behave (including our Child Safeguarding policy and Code of Conduct); • How communities can be involved.This information will be communicated in the local language and medium that are easily understood, respectful and culturally appropriate for different members of the targeted communities, especially vulnerable and marginalized groups.To ensure accountability to the beneficiaries during the consultation phase, SCI has will consult and engage the existing community structures/bodies (traditional leaders, representatives from IDPs, returnees, refugees, host communities, youth groups, women groups, and local government authorities. During the implementation phase, SCI will ensure that targeted communities have been provided with opportunities to express their views and influence decision-making through discussing together, deciding together and working together. Particular attention will be given to the vulnerable groups to enable for active participation in the implementation of the project activities. Key stakeholders such as partners and the community (women, men, boys, and girls of diverse backgrounds; beneficiaries' committees; and local authorities) will be involved in the monitoring of the project activities. SCI Sudan will establish a Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) with multiple community friendly channels. This will include both reactive (hotlines, suggestions box) and proactive (community satisfaction consultations, focus group discussions) channels. The complaints and feedback channels introduced in the projects will allow targeted communities and children to safely and confidentially report serious complaints (including child safeguarding concerns, sexual abuse and exploitation concerns and breaches of the code of conduct). Any serious complaints will be immediately elevated to the designated focal point (e.g. child safeguarding focal point, HR Manager, Director for Program Development & Quality, Country Director
