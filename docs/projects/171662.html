Project Summary

According to September 2020 FSNAU combined effects of widespread and severe flooding, Desert Locust infestation, socioeconomic impacts of COVID-19 and the cumulative impacts of previous shocks, up to 2.1 million people across Somalia are expected to face food consumption gaps or depletion of assets indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3)1 or worse outcomes through December 2020 in the absence of humanitarian assistance. In addition, 849 900 children under the age of five are likely to be acutely malnourished through August 2021. Desert Locust continues to pose a serious risk of damage to both pasture and crops at least until the end of 2020. The 2020 Deyr (October-December) rainfall season is likely to be below average to average across the country, which could lead to drought and trigger a worsening of the humanitarian situation if the 2021 Gu (April-June) season rainfall is also delayed or performs poorly. Internally displaced persons (IDPs), a majority of whom are poor and live in urban areas in desperate conditions, have limited livelihood assets and coping options and rely more heavily on external humanitarian assistance. As a result, a significant proportion of IDPs continue to face moderate to large food consumption gaps. Some of the urban poor across Somalia who struggle to make ends meet also continue to face moderate to large food consumption gaps. For both groups (i.e. IDPs and urban poor), the socioeconomic impact of COVID-19 was a factor, associated with a decline in remittances, increased food prices, and a decline in employment and other income-earning opportunities in urban areas
The purpose of this intervention is to build on the immediate relief and short term gains of SAACOMs/WFP emergency and relief efforts and move forward to build livelihoods that will enable the targeted households to build resilience. The intervention is a bridge from relief to future development interventions. This aims to move HH beyond relying mostly on aid and build capacity and resilience through agricultural and non-agricultural livelihoods to support household food security. The resilience will further be achieved through the start-up and development of Farmers Savings and Loans (FSLA) model adapted to the local context and capacities.
SAACOM intends to achieve this to complement existing cash programming in the same area with distribution of agricultural livelihood kits with seeds and tools supported by demo plots allowing beneficiaries to plant food crops. Non agricultural livelihood kits will be distributed according to the livelihoods that women participate in strengthened by the organization of trained community business volunteers. SAACOM intends to keep moving forward as the situation improves and eventually organize FSLAs to consolidate the gains and move full cycle from relief to development thus achieving a durable solution milestone and outcome targeted by both SAACOM and the beneficiaries and in support of NDP and the SDGs.
SAACOM will promote the realization of the human rights of persons with disabilities in line with the CRPD by empowering persons with disabilities to claim their rights on an equal basis with others and through advocacy efforts directed at changing attitudes and practices so as to eliminate discrimination and promote respect and understanding of the needs and capacities of persons with disabilities.
During project implementation SAACOM will endeavor to adhere and advocate for WHO Covid 19 awareness and preventive measures by involving and including communities directly (including women, children, older people, persons with disabilities and other often excluded groups), listening to them and understand perceptions, social norms and beliefs to avoid the spread of rumours and misinformation about Covid SAACOM will also provide the necessary PPEs and CT safety nets to most affected HH to enhance Covid resilience.

Project Activities

Activity 1: Community entry meetings and monthly feedback meetings. These meetings will explain to the community the project and develops champions from within the community.Activity 2: Covid 19 awareness and prevention through community conversations and engagement Activity 3: Beneficiary targeting, selection and registration exercises - SAACOM utilizes a criteria that allows of targeting and selection of the most in need or vulnerable, The vulnerability criteria includes variables such as female and child headed households, vulnerable members such as pregnant and lactating women, elderly, PLW, PWDs, and chronically ill, large household members, and dependency and in cases such as food security, access to land and productive assets. A socio-economic score is determined and a cut-off to determine the number. In the case of livelihood, the ability to engage in livelihood will be part of the selection criteria. SAACOM is a partner of WFP and an active participant of the Food Security Sector Cluster and Cash working groups and regularly submits reports. Activity 4: Support Relief distribution through Cash to 200HH- (Mobile Money Transfer) 60.00 USD per person per month for 2 months-Identifying mobile money service provider and signing partnership agreement on Mobile Money transfer to the beneficiaries. Activity 5: Rehabilitation of water catchments in Garowe and Galkayo villages under district through cash for work targeting 400 of the most vulnerable beneficiaries. 60.00 USD per participant and per month for 2 months each Activity 5: indentify and train 50 women on FSLA model and simple business skillsActivity 6: Agricuture kits distribution- distribution conducted with beneficiaries separated in smaller groups. SAACOM distribution system through voucher system and different distribution dates by batch of beneficiaries to avoid drawing crowds and reduce security risks as part of SAACOMs commitment in protection mainstreaming by ensuring the safety of beneficiaries at distribution sites. The integrity lay-out of distribution sites also ensures appropriate sections and priorities for vulnerable groups such as elderly and pregnant and lactating women. Activity 7: IYCF/HDD sessions- women beneficiaries will be selected and grouped. Bi-monthly sessions will be conducted in groups to enhance the nutritious preparation and feeding of food. Community nutrition mobilizers and lead mothers together with SAACOM staff come together for regular sessions designed to increase the knowledge of mother and child pairs in proper feeding practices and preparation of various foods that diversify the household diet. SAACOM does not do nutrition and considers this activity as a form of labelling to strengthen the use of cash for food. Activity 8: Post distribution monitoring , community feedback sessions and operationalizing CRM- The SAACOM CRM system is operationalized on field through help desks during distribution, face to face conversations, suggestion boxes and hot lines Activity 8. End-line and project evaluation and project hand over to community.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

SAACOM through focus group discussions held between the beneficiaries ranked Cash and vouchers as the most preferred method of support that the beneficiaries voted and requested to be employed by this project. SAACOM is in agreement of the beneficiaries since the Cash and voucher based assistance from SAACOM point of view also is that cash based intervention gives the beneficiaries the variety and ability to choose between the most pressing needs to be attended to in the HH hence giving a sense of dignity to the beneficiary. Local economy was also found to thrive once the cash is injected to the markets thus contributing to economic growth creation of locally available jobs. Other factors are cash transfers through mobile phones are more secure from many risks like theft, looting and corruption, increasing women’s influence in household decision-making over finances as both man and wife will be registered as a unit HH, cost effectiveness of cash transfers compared to other forms of assistance(Cash programmes are likely to have lower transport and logistics costs than in-kind assistance), and timeliness of delivery.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

SAACOM considered the following aspects during this project design -Is the market functioning? • Are basic items available? • Are there government policies that restrict the movement of goods? • Is the market competitive? • Are markets integrated? • Will traders respond to an increase in purchasing power, and how quickly? • Is there a risk of inflation in the prices of key commodities?

Mitigation Measures

Exclusion and marginalization: Members of marginalized community’s/ minority groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance The following mitigation measures will be applied to address this risk. • Project committees will be developed in the community to enhance feedback between SAACOM staff and the needs of the beneficiaries. Vulnerable and marginalised people including those living with disabilities, the elderly, women, boys and girls, and those from minority clans will be prioritized to participate in project committees, where they will be able to contribute to project decision-making. The participation of these individuals will be routinely encouraged so that their specific needs can be addressed, and their knowledge can also be used. For example, the elderly will bring their knowledge and experience with the success and challenges of current community infrastructure. • SAACOM will also support community outreach meetings, during which all members of the community including vulnerable individuals and also the representation of women will be encouraged. At these forums, the community will discuss and contribute ideas on how to improve project operations. • SAACOM will also adopt a participatory monitoring and accountability approach for comprehensive feedback, including from vulnerable individuals, on project interventions. 4b. Protection mainstreaming: Beneficiaries risk exposure to violence during registration/distribution. The following mitigation measures will be applied to address this risk. SAACOM will carry out it's beneficiary selection process in a transparent and open way based on a pre-agreed vulnerability selection criteria. SAACOM ensure verification of beneficiaries is carried out to avoid conflicts. – SAACOM prioritizes the protection of the affected population by ensuring beneficiaries are served under sufficient shed during the beneficiary registration, male and female beneficiaries will have access to latrines and drinking water during beneficiary registration exercise. – SAACOM promotes the dignity, inclusive participation, and diversity within the affected population by keeping the data of the vulnerable population, by considering both IDPs and host communities, by including the elderly and disability in the programme. SAACOM has a CRM polices well established and aligned with key standards such as code of conduct, accountability to affected populations. Community feedback meetings are held regularly to ensure that communities are both beneficiaries and active agents of their own development and a committment of SAACOM to insure transparency. The community feedback sessions are reinforced by the presence of help desks, hot lines and suggestion boxes. This is a committment to be accountable populations in insuring their voice is heard.
