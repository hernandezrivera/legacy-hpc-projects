Project Summary

The Common Transport Services (CTS) is a free-for-user service that transports key humanitarian supplies on behalf of humanitarian actors. CTS serves as a critical link in the supply chain, enabling products and materials that arrive from regional and international suppliers to move onwards to partners located deep in the field rapidly. IOM has been managing the CTS on behalf of the Logistics Cluster since 2011. During this time, the project has significantly grown from delivering 2,000 MT of humanitarian cargo in 2011 to a total of 17000 MT by the end of 2021, making a yearly average of 25000 MT since the beginning, highlighting the increase in the use of road transport as the most cost-efficient mode of transportation as access improves. IOM has continued to maintain flexibility and adaptability to the operating environment in South Sudan. Through CTS, IOM works with more than 100 unique agencies in key operational locations in Jonglei, Unity, and Greater Upper Nile Region, in Humanitarian Logistics Hubs (Malakal, Bentiu), and Rumbek, Bor, Juba, and Wau. IOM will maintain heavy vehicles and transport cargo from the main ports of dispatch via air, road, and river including LC beyond responses and will continue support for common warehouse management in key operational locations.
Through the effective management of the CTS, IOM aims to support organizations' preparedness measures to mitigate risks associated with increased insecurity, looting/theft of humanitarian supplies, critical access impediments to vulnerable people, and reduce expensive air and road transport costs, which ultimately supports the timely response to the needs of the affected populations. With this funding, IOM will directly work with 100 plus humanitarian organizations located in different areas, enabling them to deliver critical life-saving humanitarian services and assistance items.

1 - Needs

South Sudan is one of the most complex humanitarian operating environments globally, with persistent insecurity, poor infrastructure, and seasonal hazards. Poor road infrastructure in South Sudan is one of the main challenges for people in need of access to life-saving assistance and services and for humanitarian organizations to reach the people. Access impediments cause delays and add substantial extra costs. Humanitarian needs are dispersed across the country, often in areas challenging to access due to geographical remoteness and lack of infrastructure. Affected populations in hard-to-reach locations face considerable protection risks, with many not provided with consistent humanitarian assistance. Of the 2 million IDPs in South Sudan, most live in remote and hard-to-reach areas. Due to climate variability, South Sudan experienced exceptionally short dry seasons in 2019, 2020 and 2021. Heavy downpours, beginning as early as April and continuing as late as December, led to flooding and damaged major supply routes. This resulted in a shorter window for humanitarian agencies to preposition essential commodities ahead of the rainy season. Access to markets, which has already been limited by poor road infrastructure and transport options, further deteriorates during the long rainy season. The majority of the rural population is far from or unable to reach markets for a large proportion of the year. In late 2021, access to many areas affected by flooding remained challenging, with roads washed away and locations inundated with water. As such, major roads are expected to become physically accessible later than usual. This has shortened the window for humanitarian cargo prepositioning during the dry season. Violence and insecurity could further impact access to life-saving assistance and services and the ability of humanitarian organizations to reach populations in need. This could result in humanitarian organizations increasingly relying heavily on costly air transport. In 2022, the humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that 8.9 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 on 2021 (HNO 2022). It is imperative that support continues for the prepositioning of relief items in the field by road where possible, which remains the most cost-effective means of delivery. The CTS project will ensure cargo is transported from warehouses to airstrip/riverside docks for onward transport to priority locations – Bentiu, Bor, Juba, Malakal, Rumbek, and Wau – or to priority locations through the Beyond Responses, which enables time-critical, life-saving frontline multi-cluster activities through a cost-effective and reliable logistics resources provision at ICCG prioritized counties. This project is key in assisting more than 100 humanitarian organizations to deliver life-saving and emergency materials and products to areas where they are most needed across the country through a fleet of 18 trucks, with common storage facilities where needed. This project ensures beneficiaries can safely access services and resources with dignity, including in hard-to-reach/newly accessible locations, and reduction in the scale of CTS will reduce the humanitarian response and put already vulnerable lives in danger. The CTS fleet of trucks ensures the provision of cargo transportation by road, while optimizing cost-effectiveness, and improving the efficiency and timeliness of the wider humanitarian response in South Sudan.CTS also contributes towards making the overall humanitarian response 'as local as possible' through enabling equal and impartial access to assistance and services. In this context, CTS ensures the timely delivery of humanitarian supplies to priority locations served by Logistic Cluster partners, including international and national (I/NGOs) in South Sudan. Therefore, CTS can be considered an enabler towards frontline activities being carried out by I/NGOs where the capacity exists.

2 - Project activities

The CTS will continue to provide support to more than 80 agencies representing all relevant clusters in transport, airport/river port clearing, loader management, and warehousing. In response to the logistics challenges faced in critical areas in South Sudan, IOM (in collaboration with the Logistics Cluster) will continue to provide reliable and readily-available assets to transport humanitarian supplies intended to provide relief to affected populations. IOM will maintain sufficient technical staffing, office, and mechanical workshop facilities to provide adequate logistics support to partners and counterparts, through allowing the full-time availability of 18 dedicated trucks that will meet the cargo movement capacity necessary to transport relief items to various NGOs and humanitarian agencies serving the critical areas in South Sudan. IOM will continue to position the 18 trucks in Bor (3 trucks), Bentiu (4 trucks), Malakal (4 trucks), Rumbek (3 trucks), Juba (2 trucks), and Wau (2 trucks), reflecting the operational requirements of humanitarian agencies. The geographic distribution of transport assets is based on priorities and subject to change when necessary. IOM has continued to maintain flexibility and adaptability to the operating environment in South Sudan. IOM will also adjust the service provision to accommodate COVID-19 and flood response preparedness efforts. CTS will encompass six mutually reinforcing activities, as follows: 1. Provide common transport services to the humanitarian community (INGOs, UN) to facilitate the rapid deployment of essential humanitarian cargo to field locations in coordination with humanitarian partners (through the ICWG). 2. Manage a fleet of 18 IOM managed CTS trucks to provide timely overland transport of humanitarian cargo, including air cargo handling, and maintain effective Fleet Management System. 3. Maintain and improve Humanitarian Logistics Hubs (Malakal and Bentiu), including increasing the storage capacity of humanitarian actors operating in these locations. 4. Provide Common Warehouse Management in Malakal and Bentiu, and other priority locations, namely Bor and Rumbek. 5. Handle cargo effectively and professionally, through consolidation and loading at hubs, and cargo handling until the final destination. 6. Ensure adequate staff to accompany project vehicles, monitor cargo handling, and maintain communications with the control center in Juba.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

CTS is a support service for the humanitarian community. Ensuring accountability to affected populations and protection risk analysis and mitigation is the responsibility of organizations accessing the CTS. This project will assist beneficiaries through humanitarian organizations who have already conducted assessments including protection risk analysis and planned humanitarian assistance based on the identified needs. This project will enable equal and impartial access to assistance and services and the targeting of vulnerable groups and people with specific needs. This will be achieved through the Cluster, which directs where humanitarian supplies are to be sent, in coordination with humanitarian agencies operating in key areas.
