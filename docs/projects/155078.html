Project Summary

The intervention will contribute to the child protection sector objectives to strengthen the prevention and response systems to ensure children and adolescents are protected from violence, abuse, neglect and exploitation. The interventions will adopt a holistic approach to ensuring the needs of the most vulnerable refugees are met, in particular the needs of girls, boys, and adolescents.

Needs

Since August 2017, extreme violence in Rakhine State, Myanmar, has driven an estimated 706,000 Rohingya population across the border into the Cox’s Bazar district of Bangladesh, bringing up the total number of Rohingya refuges in cox Bazar to about 901,000 (UNHCR, 15 November 2018) . The refugees are more concentrated in two sub-districts, which are Ukhia and Teknaf. Children and adolescents under 18 years compose 55% of the Rohingya displaced population. In total, 284,119 refugee children and 107,162 children from host communities have been identified as being in need (52 - 48% female and male ratio). Concerns and risks for children and adolescents are manifold. The large displacement of children and their families have caused distress, and has led to multitude of child protection issues. 6,185 unaccompanied and separated children have been registered, who are at further risk of child trafficking, abuse and exploitation in the camps (ISCG Sitrep, 15 Nov 2018). Girls, who represent a larger proportion of this vulnerable group, are particularly at risk of child marriage, sexual exploitation, abuse and neglect. Adolescent refugee girls are profoundly affected by the crisis,where they are faced with lack of freedom of movement, adverse camp condititions, limited to no access to education or health services. This is underpinned by social norms, attitudes and practices that negatively impact adolescent girls. Psychological distress continues to be a pressing issue. The target group hightlighted the importance of family and friends as key individuals they turn to for support. Observation ally, interpretations of Islamic beliefs among the Rohingya community presents a wide gap between the role, rights, and entitlement of males and females. Similarly, in the host community, adolescent boys face risks of child labor and drug abuse. (Adolescent Girls in Crisis, Plan 2017) (Host Communities Review, acaps, Jan 2018) For girls’ issues are related to memories of violence before movement and exposure to sexual violence before the flight, and for boys, memories of violence before movement and missing family members (Sector Needs Analysis, Nov 2018). In particular for younger children, the lack of routine, familiar environments, and a change in community support networks and dynamics can lead to additional distress and feelings of unsafely for children. While children can access temporary learning spaces, other services that provide quality, age-appropriate and structured activities for recreation and play are limited (Adolescent Girls in Crisis, Plan 2018)

Activities or outputs

Output 1: access to quality, structure psycho social services (PSS) for children and adolescents in need of structured PSS services is increased 1.1: maintain child friendly static and mobile spaces and establish additional mobile spaces for PSS for children and adolescents 1.2: provide structure, inclusive, age and gender-appropriate sessions in static and mobile Child Friendly Spaces (CFS) including children with disabilities1.3: identify, support and refer children with protection concerns/at risk of abuse, including survivors of gender-based violence (GBV)1.4: train and mentor CFS facilitators on key CFS competencies, including on safe identification and referral, psycho social first aid (PFA) and play and recreation for children and adolescents. Including children with disabilities an other vulnerabilities. 1.5 explore opportunities for increased community ownership including possibility of handing over activities as appropriate based on developed exit strategy. Output 2: children harmed or at risk of harm due to violence, abuse, neglect or exploitation (VANE) receive a continuum of case management services 2.1: identify, register and provide support to children with protection needs, including children affected by VANE2.2: scale-up alternative care system for children without appropriate care, including unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) and other children in need of emergency temporary care2.3: provide emergency assistance to families with critical child protection needs, based on established protocols2.4: establish and support internal regular child protection case meetings Output 3: the protective environment is strengthened through enhanced capacities of community-based child protection mechanisms and stakeholders 3.1: support and mentor existing child protection committees (CPCs) in their work to advance the protection of children in their communities in a community-driven, sustainable way3.2: incorporate a child protection monitoring system in existing community networks, to inform coordinated actions for communities and service providers3.3: provide training and raise awareness to caregivers who have been identified with concerns and challenges at family level3.4: strengthen Disaster Risk Response (DDR) capacities among adolescents and youth including projects that target inclusive, social cohesion and strive for sustainability3.5 contribute to sector-wide capacity building and partner mentoring to increase ability of all actors to deliver quality CP activities Output 4: Adolescent girls and boys have access to life-enhancing and gender-trans formative activities which reduce their risks and vulnerabilities Act 4.1: run creative and age-appropriate recreational activities targeting adolescents aged 10-18Act 4.2: carry out participatory risk-assessments with adolescentsAct 4.3: engage caregiver with adolescent children in specialized, community-based positive parenting sessionsAct 4.4: support adolescent-led advocacy and community-based life-enhancing activities, based on identified gaps and needs, including projects targeting social cohesion Additional activities:Plan will continue its active and regular participation and coordination in Child Protection Sub-Sector meetings and working groups, including Youth Working Group, Protection Working Group, and Education Working Group. Plan will continue its active and regular participation and coordination in camp level and government meetings regularly to mainstream and complement other sectors. Plan’s programming will continue to link to sector materials and training opportunities. Plan staff will coordinate and communicate as per the standards with relevant government officials, such as the RRRC, CiCs and other agencies.Plan will enforce child safeguarding and COC to mitigate risks and "do no harm" based on Plan International internal polices and procedures. This includes PSEA.
