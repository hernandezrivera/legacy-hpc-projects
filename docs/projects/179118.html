Project Summary

Syria Relief SR aims to continue providing General Protection and Child Protection services in NW Syria in Dana, Maaret Tamsrin, Kafr Takharim, Afrin, and Aghtrin Sub-district . The project aims to deliver planned services to most vulnerable Girls, Boys, Women and Men GBWM in Harim, Afrin , and A'zaz distrect through 5 static community centres CCs within 5 relatively densely populated communities in addition to 10 mobile units.
SR will ensure the services are accessible by PwDs and will consider all possible precautious measures (social distancing and using the PPE) and Cluster guidelines related to adapting Protection services
delivery modalities in light with COVID-19, to ensure the safety of staff and beneficiaries and avoid causing harm (see attached SR COVID-19 Response Plan). The services will be delivered for 12 months starting from 1st of Jan-21, addressing the identified
Protection and CP needs of targeted individuals. The Overall Project Objective is designed to provide integrated protection and CP services and coordinate closely with local service providers from other sectoral services (FSL & Early Recovery, WASH, and Health-Nutrition actors) to facilitate access of persons with specific needs to those services. The outcome of the proposed intervention will be; conflict-affected and most vulnerable GBWM,
including PwD, have a better understanding of their rights and better resilience through benefitting from legal Protection, Protection and Child Protection services.
SR aims at the continuation of protection activities within areas of operation. Besides, the protection teams will extend their reach into new subdistricts in Idleb and Alepo governorats. Protection services are intended to provide a package of essential preventive and responsive services to affected Host communities, IDPs and Returnees through expanding the capacity of mobile outreach
teams, launching new Protection activities in parallel with the existing Education activities that SR implements in Dana and Maaret Tamsrin. In addtion to deliver an integrated project with WASH sector within Dana sub-Distrect.
The project is designed to respond to most affected populations through 5 static facility and 10 mobile units will be attached to those centres to reach out PwDs or those practising voluntary quarantine so they cannot visit the centres. The gender-mixed mobile units will also deliver the project services within 10 neighbouring communities. The proposed CCs and mobile units will provide many general protection and child protection services including awareness-raising about legal protection, general protection. In addition to Child Protection awareness rasing sessions about CP concerns. SR Teams will also provide case management and IPA to the identified most vulnerable GWBM. PSS will be delivered to girls and boys. Besides, parents will have the opportunity to join positive parenting skills sessions. Training about HLP/CSD will be delivered to concerned humanitarian workers to ensure they are well capacitated. Other Training in line with CPMS will be provided to front lines responder and Humanitarian workers. Also, Community-based Protection committees will be supported. Furthermore, the Protection Teams will be part of the current Cluster taskforces and collect data related to Protection Monitoring and Child Protection Situational Monitoring surveys. Emergency focal points will be assigned to contribute to Cluster efforts in coordinating the Protection response. Protection services will provide a secure and supportive environment, building on existing capacities within the local communities and expanding the population of the SR response team. SR Protection Teams will implement all Project activities directly with support from SR Turkey Office.

4 - Needs

Syria is the second least peaceful country globally according to the Global Peace Index although a reduction in active hostilities can be observed following the Russian Federation- Government of Turkey Idleb ceasefire agreement of March 2020 with active ghting having largely subsided compared to the peak of the crisis, and as a result of large-scale displacement also slowing. A decade of confict has trapped civilians in a pervasive protection crisis where people’s rights to safety, security, and well-being are regularlyviolated. The overall protection situation defined by the protracted crisis and characterized by complex and interconnected protection concerns remains dire (HNO 2021). SR MEALthe team visited the locations of planned targeted camps/communities in Idleb and Aleppo and conducted KIIs to collect data and analyze the needs. Regarding key informant assessment Findings, all respondents said that lack or loss of personal documents and Housing/land and property issues is among the most important risks that both males and females are facing, more so for adults and men in particular. In addition to both restrictions in movement and psychological distress were reported by interviewed KIs. Same to men and women, besides lack or loss of personal documents. According to SR protection needs assessment conducted in April 2021, more than 80% of community leaders said CP services were not available. 100% of them reported an increase in child marriage. Only 33% reported PHC services available for PwD. The most prominent coping mechanisms were dropping out of school, relying on humanitarian assistance, and begging, both economic exploitation, child labor, neglect, and early marriage are being faced by boys and girls.

5 - Implementation of activities

Generally, there will be community centers within relatively densely populated locations. Mobile units will be attached to those fixed centers to reach out to smaller populations and random IDP camps. SR will coordinate with local authorities to ensure access and community acceptance of the proposed activities. Besides, SR plan to deliver the proposed services as integrated services within the location needed locations. The proposed interventions will focus on improving the protection of the affected population through community-based and individually targeted interventions and increased and equitable access for boys and girls to quality child protection intervention. These efforts will focus on the Host community IDPs and people of concern addressing both protection awareness and services while enhancing the COVID-19 protection understanding at the community level. Awareness-raising for children will be delivered online. While trainings and awareness-raising sessions for parenting will be delivered physically with reducing the number of participants to 8-10 individuals per session. PPE will be provided and physical distancing will be considered. Case management will be delivered in a mixed manner with considering COVID-19 precautious measures during physical follow-up sessions.The project will target the most vulnerable persons with an emphasis on boys and girls, CwD and duty bearers (women and men) in line with the cluster SOPs, with the Central Emergency Response Fund CERF life-saving criteria (UN 2010) and the UNICEF Guidance for Inclusion of Children with Disabilities in Child Protection Interventions (UNICEF 2018), the child protection case management SOPs, and the alternative care guidance for NWS. 6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: CM documents' confidentiality is not maintained which puts them under threatMitigation: SR CM Team will be trained on CM principles. Hard copies of case files will be kept in lockable cabinets. they will be destroyed every month after being uploaded into electronic storage units (SR One Drive). All electronic data will be protected by passwords. Case trackers are also anonymized.Risk: SR will not be able to reach children remotely to conduct Structured PSS activities/ awareness-raising sessions due to the lack of smart devices in HH.Mitigation: SR will move to apply the physical sessions modality for these children affected taking into consideration precious measures against COVID 19 (using PPEs and minimizing the number of each session to be 10). Risk: The risk of project staff exerting physical or emotional violence against children.Mitigation: all SR field staff will be inducted on CSG Policy, which prohibits any type of violence against children. The policy also indicates what types of disciplinary procedures should be taken against abusers. All Syria Relief field staff will sign the CSG policy.Risk: People with disabilities will not be able to access project services. Mitigation: SR will rehabilitate the Community center to be suits and accessible for PwD through establishing (ramp, latrine). Additionally, the Mobile team attached with CC will reach PwD in camps.

7 - Does the project include a PSEA strategy ?

Yes

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Risk: A member of the SR team uses their position of authority as a method of exploiting vulnerable populations. Their actions cause significant long-term damage to this individual or group. All trust between SR and the affected community is damaged. SR faces severe reputational damage and loss of funding. Mitigation: All staff who join SR Project are going to a thorough andtransparent recruitment process. References are obtained for all staff during the recruitment process. The Project Team will sign SR PSEA and CSG Policies SR have developed PSEA, Code of Conduct, and Child Safeguarding policies and mechanisms in place to ensure every effort is made to all SR staff and Protection benefciaries.SR PSEA Mechanism is designed around four core components: Awareness Raising (for staff regarding power dynamics, risk and vulnerabilities, etc.), Prevention (awareness regarding the existing controls which are in place and how PSEA is prevented), Reporting (how participants can report and what are the specifc steps which need to be taken regarding SEA cases) and Response (how and what SR will do when cases of SEA are reported). A similar mechanism is available for CSG where the staff are familiar with how to approach and deal with CSG cases of child abuse, exploitation, or violence. All community centers and mobile units’ staff will sign SR CSG and PSEA Policies to ensure a safe environment for all Project beneficiaries
