Project Summary

CEDS proposes to implement WASH interventions aimed to increase access to adequate safe water, hygiene and improved sanitation to 181,806 flood, drought and conflict affected people in Cuiebet, Awerial, Aweil North, Aweil South, Tonj North and Tonj South counties in Lakes, Warrap and Northern Bahr el Ghazal states through rehabilitation of non-functional community and institutional water points and sanitation facilities, construction of flood resilient sanitation facilities, hygiene promotion awareness, community led total sanitation initiatives and provision of essential hygiene materials. As a result of this project, it is anticipated that the WASH conditions for supported communities will improve, and their capacity built to sustain the WASH infrastructures.

1 - Needs

According to the 2022 HNO, the well-being of 6 million people in South Sudan will be impacted in 2023 as a result of inadequate or lack of access to safe water and improved sanitation. The WASH Severity Classification (WSC) 2022 findings highlights Warrap, Lakes and Northern Bahr el Ghazal States as locations with high WASH priority needs with all 19 counties classified in severity level 3 and 4. The drivers of increased WASH needs are attributed to seasonal flooding, insecurity and dry spells experienced. High levels of food insecurity, IPC phase 3 and beyond has also contributed to acute malnutrition in most of the counties. In fact, based on the analysis and estimations from the results of the SMART nutrition surveys, FSNMS and program admission trends approximately 1.4 million children <5 years between January - December 2023 are expected to suffer from Severe and Moderate Acute Malnutrition. The major factors contributing to acute malnutrition include high prevalence of diseases. 2 weeks prior to assessment, 62.5% of children were reported to have been ill. Diarrhea, fever and cough being the leading symptoms reported. Additionally, existing poor hygiene and sanitation conditions contributed to high incidences of illness, with open defecation reported by as high as 50% of households in 56 counties. The IRNA (Sept. 2022 – Oct. 2022) further indicates that more than 312,050 people in Warrap, Lakes and Northern Bahr el Ghazal were affected by floods. Recurrent flooding in Northern Bahr el Ghazal-Aweil East, Aweil North, Aweil West, Warrap-Tonj South, Tonj East and Lakes-Awerial, Cueibet counties and insecurity has resulted in mass displacements, disruption of livelihoods, destruction of shelters and WASH infrastructures. Inadequate, poorly functioning or non-functioning water hand pumps has prompted community members to resort to use of surface water or unimproved water sources as their main source of water. The surface water is often untreated prior to consumption and this, along with the common practice of open defecation poses a considerable public health risk, especially during floods when latrine pits seep into water ways. Women and young girls frequently tasked with fetching water for household use are forced to walk long distances due to limited water resources and/or queue for longer periods to access safe water exposing them to risk of GBV and conflicts associated with sharing of water resources. In addition, majority of the flood and conflict affected communities who migrate to safer and dry higher ground areas largely depend on this very same limited available water resources. The surge in population increases WASH needs and with time, over reliance on these limited hand pumps puts a physical strain on them, resulting to damage and also limits individual access to adequate water as per the standard sphere recommended 15L/p/day. Inadequate access to improved sanitation facilities and strong cultural and traditional beliefs that hinder the use of latrines has led to a rise in widespread open defecation practices in the communities. Lack of awareness on appropriate hygiene practices and limited household access to hygiene materials due to low purchasing power or competing basic priority needs such as foods. Even though many gains have materialized over the past few years as a consequence of multiple WASH interventions, a huge gap still prevails due to the impacts of flooding, conflict and looming drought. There is an increasing need to construct climate resilient WASH infrastructures and step-up efforts to increase uptake of hygiene promotion awareness including developing innovative contextual communication messaging in line with the WASH technical standards. This project seeks to provide access to (1) sufficient and safe drinking water (2) adequate, safe and dignified climate resilient sanitation facilities, (3) information about good and appropriate hygiene practices and (4) essential WASH NFIs and MHM supplies.

2 - Project activities

- Water and Sanitation resource mapping of non-functional community and institutional boreholes and latrines to identify and select WASH infrastructures that require rehabilitation/construction. - Safety audits of WASH infrastructures selected to identify any protection concerns and develop mitigation measures. This will be done through focus group discussions with women and young girls. - Train hand pump mechanics on borehole repairs and maintenance.- Rehabilitate and elevate platforms of non-functional community and institutional boreholes to withstand flooding.- Conduct water quality testing for all the rehabilitated boreholes to ensure safety prior to human consumption. - Train community Water Management Committees (WMCs) on basic borehole operations and management.- Construct and rehabilitate flood resilient community and institutional latrines. - Install handwashing stations to public areas of mass convergence i.e. water points, churches, markets to promote handwashing practices.- Train Community Led Total Sanitation (CLTS) facilitators on CLTS approach.- Carry out CLTS activities and routine monitoring and support supervisions to CLTS triggered villages.- Train community hygiene promoters on hygiene promotion activities.- Secure and distribute WASH NFIs (soap, buckets, water treatment tablets, cloth filters) to vulnerable identified and registered households- Secure and distribute Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) kits to registered women and young girls of reproductive age.- Carry out onsite and Post Distribution monitoring of WASH NFIs and MHM kits- Conduct hygiene promotion campaigns integrated with Covid-19 messaging through door-to-door and public mass awareness sessions on hygiene promotion.- Distribute hygiene promotion IEC materials to community members. - Train community leaders on community-based disaster risk reduction- Carry out a Knowledge Attitude and Practice survey to determine uptake and utilization of hygiene promotion interventions.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CEDS will put in place measures to ensure that the views of most vulnerable community members are taken in account during project implementation. CEDS will make use of the routine community feedback meetings to pass on information and collect feedback about the project from the neediest community members. In order to increase meaningful participation from the at-risk groups, CEDS will purpose to hold separate feedback meetings for people with disabilities, women, girls and men. This will provide an opportunity for self-expression devoid of social and cultural beliefs that look down upon these vulnerable groups on voicing out their individual thoughts. To ensure that illiterate community members’ voices are heard, CEDS will use alternative feedback collection channels involving one-on-one personal contact such as helpdesks, project staff and through community feedback committees (CFCs) established to obtain feedback. CEDS will also make efforts to ensure women and people with disability have representation in the established CFCs (5 women, 2 men) equally and relevantly participate in decision making and day to day management. All feedback received will be channeled to the AAP focal point for action taking and a response provided to the beneficiaries.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

CEDS recognizes the positive significance information sharing and feedback collection from beneficiaries can have on the project and in ensuring its primary objectives are met. For this project, CEDS through the project staff will use all avenues including but not limited to community meetings and dialogues involving direct contact with beneficiaries to pass on information about the project and collect beneficiary views on the project. CEDS will also establish a 7-member village level Community Feedback Committees (CFCs) in each of the targeted villages to share information about the project, project objectives, target beneficiaries, beneficiary entitlement, complaints and feedback channels in place, the project activities and how the community can be involved throughout the project implementation. Prior to formation, CEDS will hold a consultative meeting with the community members to self-nominate and choose suitable CFC members based on agreed upon stipulated selection criteria whilst encouraging more women and people with disabilities to participate and be members of the CFCs to ensure equal, active and meaningful participation. Each of the committees formed will be trained on how to conduct information sharing meetings and supported with materials to facilitate documentation and on a weekly basis share all complaints and feedback received to the project AAP focal point for action taking. Complaints that require the attention of the project implementation team will be directed to the Project Management Team for redress in a period of not less than fourteen days, upon which the feedback will be given to the beneficiaries on the complaint logged. CEDS will also set up suggestion boxes in strategic public locations to collect feedback from literate beneficiaries. The feedback will be collected on a weekly basis, analyzed and response shared back. CEDS will purpose to ensure that all feedback and complaints mechanism in place are safe to access and complaints received are treated with utmost confidentiality, respect and fairness it deserves. All logged complaints and feedback logged will be entered into CEDS database for future use. Complaints and feedback obtained will be used to make improvements to the project during implementation in an aim to ensure that beneficiaries receive assistance to their satisfaction and also feel dignified in accepting assistance. Lessons learnt or best practices used to shape the project implementation that accrue from beneficiary feedback will be shared by the cluster for use in similar contexts.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CEDS will use its existing organizational PSEA policy to guide service delivery to affected communities throughout the project cycle. In regard to this, CEDS will train project staff on SEA policy and procedures and ensure that all staff commit to adhere to these policies by signing formal agreements. CEDS will utilize the set-up Complaints and feedback Committees (CFCs) to share information about beneficiary entitlements and key messages about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse to target beneficiaries at village level. All beneficiaries will be informed about their right to receive supplies and services without sexual favours, exchanges or coercion from anyone including project staff and suppliers. Beneficiaries will also be informed on the available reporting channels and referral pathways and encouraged to report any observed and experienced SEA incidents. All SEA complaints received will be fast tracked and handled in a sensitive manner and the survivor referred for immediate medical support as the investigation is conducted. CEDS staff will also be instrumental in sharing information and collecting SEA complaints during any direct engagements with the community. CEDS will endeavor to ensure that all complaints made are confidential and that the processes are transparent and responsive. All of CEDS contracted suppliers will also mandated to commit to abide by CEDS PSEA policy and made to sign it.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

CEDS will ensure that beneficiary feedback is taken into consideration throughout the project cycle. CEDS will also make use the feedback mechanism in place to gather beneficiary suggestions and feedback on the project to get beneficiary views about the assistance provided and then use this information to mold project delivery. Prior to implementation, CEDS will use beneficiary feedback to determine the actual needs and the kind of assistance the beneficiaries require. CEDS will further conduct consultative meetings with the community leaders and vulnerable community members to get their insights about the locations they deem safe and accessible to rehabilitate boreholes and construct latrines and also make consultations with community members in the target catchment areas on women and men, including people with disability who qualify to be members to the Water Management Committees subject to the stipulated selection criteria. CEDs will also make community consultations on additional beneficiary selection criteria for target WASH NFIs based beneficiaries based on community perceived vulnerabilities and also in developing the distribution locations and timelines. In the routine monitoring of project activities, CEDS will seek to incorporate beneficiary inputs on the project progress through focus group discussions with target beneficiaries. CEDS will utilize feedback obtained to better improve the project assistance and share learnings across other networks.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

CEDS will put in place concrete mitigation measures during implementation to ensure that the project does not pose a protection risk to target beneficiaries. To mitigate the risk of beneficiaries being exposed to violence during registration and distribution, CEDS will hold consultative meetings with the community members and leaders to help identify the most vulnerable members to be registered and jointly agree on beneficiary preferred distribution locations and schedules. All distributions will be conducted in open common areas deemed safe to access and concluded by 3pm to allow marginalized groups ample time to get back home in broad daylight. CEDS will also spread out the distribution dates to avoid overcrowding and long queues associated with distributions that result in frustrations and sparks conflict. To minimize the risk of women, girls, boys and people with disabilities being exposed to sexual violence including SEA, CEDS will ensure project staff are trained on SEA and uphold this commitment by signing a formal agreement. CEDS contracted suppliers who will come into direct contact with target beneficiaries will also be made to sign the agreement. Prior to provision of any assistance, CEDS will make the beneficiaries and community members aware about SEA and also inform them of their entitlements to assistance without having to trade any sexual favours, exchanges or being coerced to do something in return. They will also be made aware of and encouraged to use the SEA reporting channels to report any suspected, witnessed or experience SEA. Additionally, to ensure that the project has sufficient internal mechanisms to combat and prevent child safeguarding and PSEA, CEDS will make sure all staff are well conversant with organizational child safeguarding and PSEA policies and the referral pathways. To counter the risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents, CEDS will map out which organizations offering protection response and through information meetings with the beneficiaries create awareness on accessing protection services available in the area. Protection-related cases will be referred to the GBV, mine action and child protection partners. During the implementation, CEDS will hold consultative meetings with community members including the marginalized groups to ensure they have equal opportunity to participate in project discussions and their inputs are used to inform decision making. CEDS will also make it a point to ensure that marginalized groups take up leadership positions in community-based structures charged with managing community WASH resources. To guarantee that beneficiaries will be accessing and using the CFM in place, CEDS from the onset of the project will share information about CFM, its importance to the community members and how it works. CEDS will avail feedback channels that are accessible by both literate and illiterate community members i.e., use of suggestion boxes, walk in help desks and use of Community Feedback Committees. Feedback collected will be utilized to adapt project assistance provided. To lessen external interference with the beneficiary selections, CEDS in the project initiation meeting will clearly outline the roles of stakeholders in the project implementation. The project implementation team-lead will be informed of any misdeeds relating to beneficiary selection and distribution to ensure fairness and transparency in the process. For land disputes, CEDS will purpose to conduct project activities in open common grounds considered communal and engage community leaders prior to settling on a specific location to undertake activities. Lastly, CEDS will use its existing Data Protection policy to guide in data collection and staff will be required to adhere to this policy making sure that all beneficiary data is kept confidential and stored digitally in the database and only accessible to authorized personnel.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

This project through the project activities will provide access to safe water for human consumption, animal and domestic use. The water will also be used to irrigate vegetable gardens established around the homesteads and the harvests obtained consumed at household level. Surplus vegetables will be sold to generate some income that can then be utilized to purchase basic non-food items. For women, the time consumed in travelling and queueing to collect water will now be used to care for infants and young children and engage in other social and economic activities while the young girls of school going age can dedicate their time to attend classes or complete school course work. Access and uptake of improved sanitation and good hygiene practices will result to a reduction in open defecation practices leading to a reduction in water borne related diseases and infections such as diarrhea and typhoid associated with poor hygiene and sanitation practices ultimately resulting to improved health outcomes. In providing WASH items to the affected communities, money that would have been spent on buying these items will then be channeled for other household pressing needs such as medicine and other essential items. It is expected that through these interventions household food security, health and nutrition outcomes will improve resulting to improved living conditions.
