Project Summary

This project will support the delivery of quality, integrated child protection and psychosocial support (PSS) services to the most vulnerable boys and girls aged 6-18 in the Gaza Strip, especially those affected by the 2021/2022 escalations. The escalation has affected all aspects of life in the Gaza Strip, including child protection needs and services.
The project will ensure that 12 family centres and 30 outreach sites in Gaza are fully operational, responding to the pressing mental health and psychosocial and child protection needs of 11,900 children (50% girls) and 4,300 caregivers (20% males). Additionally, 9,900 children and adults will be reached with Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) awareness messages, while 300 non-child protection professionals and community members will benefit from training and coaching opportunities on child protection, detection, identification and referral.
Service delivery will be directed to the most affected locations and the most vulnerable children through a coordinated continuum of services in Gaza. Each family centre will be connected to a community-based child protection committee that identify the child protection concerns and refer identified children to the family centre within that community. Family centres will operate through established Community-Based Organisations (CBOs) and outreach sites and will assess/screen and refer children for further specialised services through:
1) a case management approach for the most affected;
2) referral of severe and complicated child protection cases to child protection services (managed by relevant stakeholders and Civil Society Organizations (CSOs); and
3) referral of children with further psychosocial support needs to more specialized psychological support services.
Services will also strengthen outreach capacities to detect and link children in need with service providers while providing mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) through the family centres. A total of 11,900 children (50% girls) and 4,300 caregivers (20% males), as well as 300 community members will be reached through the project.
This project aligns with and contributes to the Protection Cluster Objective no. 2, which calls for putting in place protection response mechanisms to prevent and mitigate occupation and conflict-related violence.
Ensuring compliance with the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) principles and procedures, project beneficiaries will be oriented on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse. They will receive information on UNICEF and other interagency channels, including SAWA Free toll number, social media platforms, and WhatsApp to report on PSEA.
At least 70% of the project budget will support the two project implementing partners to ensure that timely services are provided to children with protection and MHPSS needs. The funds will be transferred in line with UNICEF Harmonised Approach to Cash Transfer (HACT), which ensures close follow-up on expenditures. Partners are normally micro-assessed and spot checks are regularly conducted to ensure compliance with UNICEF regulations.

Implementing Partners

UNICEF will partner with two Civil Society Organization (CSO), who are members of Child Protection Area of Responsibility (AoR), with proven track record in child protection. The partners have at least 12 years of experience in running child friendly spaces and have been part of the development, implementation and review of the case management standard operating procedures. UNICEF partners have been already assessed for PSEA and acquired a low PSEA risk rating.
The two local partners will be providing individual and group counseling services, individual case management, awareness raising for caregivers and ERW risk education. The services provided will be in line with the IASC guidelines on MHPSS and Child Protection Minimum Standards (CPMS), as well as the case management Standard Operating Procedures. This is to ensure the quality of the interventions and avoid inflecting harm on benefiting children. The partners use the services of trained counselors and case managers, who receive continuous monitoring and supervision at the hands of experienced professional supervisors. Both counselors and case managers will ensure referrals of children in need of further support to needed services in line with the CP AoR/MHPSS referral pathways, as well as guidance given by the Protection Cluster. Referrals to other organization outside the CP AoR will be done based on the referral pathways of relevant clusters.
Under this project, one of the family centres partners is supporting five Governorate Protection Focal Points, who conduct regular situation monitoring, as well as response to major emergencies and local child protection incidents. Following the August escalation, the focal points together with Ministry of Social Development Child Protection Counselors and active members of the Child Protection Area of responsibility visited 339 families with injured children or whose houses were fully or partially demolished.
The Other local partner has been actively supporting UNMAS to refine their ERW materials, so they are more interactive and hence child friendly. UNMAS has also piloted its newly developed materials in the family centers and organized brainstorming sessions with corner facilitators on the best ways to use the materials with different age groups of children to ensure massages give their intended outcome.

1 - Needs

The May 2021 escalation resulted in large-scale loss and human suffering, aggravating the already fragile situation preceding the conflict. 242 Palestinians, including 67 children (44% boys), were killed, and 2,200 people were reportedly injured (685 children). In 2022, only 14 months after the May 2021 military escalation, the Gaza Strip witnessed renewed casualties and, according to the Palestinian Centre for Human Rights (PCHR) and the Ministry of Health, the 3-day escalation has seen the killing and injury of 17 and 151 children respectively. Although the escalation lasted three days between 5-7 August, it overwhelmed the already fragile Palestinian communities struggling to heal from psychosocial distress, especially children and significantly impacted the mental health and wellbeing of children and families. In 2023 the Child Protection Area of Responsibility estimates that some 584,395 children (296,277 Boys, 288,118 Girls) across the Gaza Strip are in need of child protection, of which children in need for MHPSS in Gaza are 543,000. Negative coping strategies including school drop-out, child labor, children in conflict with the law; pressure in families, which has increased with job losses, cramped home living conditions and socio-economic pressures compounds risks for children. There is a significant erosion of family coping mechanisms especially in marginalized families and those in precarious socio-economic situation and a deterioration in services is impacting children’s wellbeing, learning and development. Boys and girls particularly young ones whose primary caregivers were hospitalized or died due to the escalation may also face neglect or lack of support, particularly emotional, from fellow household members. Orphan boys and girls remain among the most vulnerable. In Gaza children’s exposure to violence remains widespread with 92.3 per cent children aged 1-14 reporting experiencing some type of violent discipline, while some 75 per cent boys and girls aged 1-14 reported experiencing physical punishment. Out of this, 28.5 percent boys and girls experienced severe physical violence by their caregivers (MICS 2020). On the child labor front, 9.3 per cent of children aged 5-17years in Gaza are engaged in economic activities or household chores above the threshold or working under hazardous conditions. In Gaza 4.2 per cent of children work under hazardous conditions, with for example 2.1 per cent of children working under conditions where they are exposed to dust, fumes or gas. On the child marriage front, of women aged 14-49 in Gaza, 2.3 per cent were married before the age of 15, while 24.4 per cent were married before the age of 18 (MICS 2020). The recurrent escalations have further complicated the humanitarian situation in Gaza already affected by a longstanding closure with intensified access and movement restrictions, ongoing political tensions, and fuel, water, and electricity crises. Men and boys face higher threats when it comes to the risks of exposure to Explosive Remnants of War (ERWs). With this backdrop, the proposed project aims to address MHPSS and protection, needs of children affected by the recent escalations, through the provision of psychosocial support , as well as case management services, and referral to available services (such as education, social protection, WASH, etc.), psychological first aid, group, and individual counseling, in addition to other indirect briefing and awareness-raising on positive parenting and protection from ERWs. Case management of CP cases is undertaken by trained case managers, who assess the needs and address child protection risks. Recommended response varies depending on risk level, with cases referred to the Ministry of Social Development or family centres as per Gaza referral pathways. Unless critically under-resourced CP services are funded, children will remain at significant risk with child protection needs unaddressed. Between January and September 2022, family centres reached 10,545 children and 1,946 children, equivalent to 40% and 65% of all Protection Cluster child beneficiaries for PSS and case management in Gaza respectively, reflecting their vital role within the child protection community. However, intermittent funding to the family centres limits their capacity to meet the spikes in needs. Beneficiary consultations will be undertaken at various stages of the project to ensure their participation in the planning, design, implementation, and evaluation of the project; and information about channels to report on sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) will be communicated to the beneficiaries, to ensure compliance with accountability to affected population principles and prevention and response to SEA.

2 - Activities

The proposed project aims to address the protection and PSS needs of children and caregivers, mainly those affected by the recent escalations. The project covers activities for children including the provision of psychological first aid, specialized group, and individual counseling, as well as other indirect psychosocial activities. Case management and referral to available services as per the children needs (such as specialized PSS, GBV, education, social protection, WASH, etc.) will also be provided. Caregivers will also receive awareness-raising on child protection and positive parenting. Awareness-raising ERW risk education will also be provided to both children and caregivers. The project fits under the second strategic objective of the Humanitarian Response Plan; “Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence”; and the Protection Cluster specific objectives of: 1. Provision of emergency and specialized child protection responses and services aimed at supporting vulnerable children and families affected.2. Awareness raising for vulnerable communities' members on CP issues.3. Capacity building for non-child protection professionals aiming to mainstream CP in other humanitarian action.4. Community based ERW risk education.Five Governorate Protection Points will be covered under the project, who will undertake situation monitoring, as well as assessment of children affected by emergencies, providing them with Psychological First Aid (PFA) as well as referring them to the services they need. Child protection needs will be addressed through case management of children affected by PSS distress, domestic violence, and child labor. Priority will be given to support children impacted by the latest escalations. In-house counseling manuals are developed by the partners and reviewed by UNICEF. Case management will help address the gender-specific needs of boys and girls, in particular older boys affected by child labor, and younger age boys and girls subject to PSS distress, neglect, violence, and harmful practices. To support Children with Disabilities (CWD), implementing partners will target them in coordination with disability organizations they outreach to or partner with. In 2022, UNICEF supported more than at least 436 children with disabilities through these twelve centres. In order to ensure the quality of provided services, UNICEF and its partners will utilize the guidance included in the IASC MHPSS Guidelines and the also the 2019 CPMS in the design and implementation of their activities. The Case Management Standard Operating Procedures will also be followed, observing the principles of engagement with children and ensuring referrals are made to other services, including specialised MHPSS, as needed. The child safeguarding policy of UNICEF and that its partners will also be observed. Referrals of children in need of further support to needed services will be done in line with the CP AoR/MHPSS referral pathways, as well as guidance given by the Protection Cluster. Referrals to other organization outside the CPAoR will be done based on the referral pathways of respective clusters.Project outputs and activities include:Output 1: Holistic child protection services equally provided to boys and girls aged 6-18 through 12 family centres.Activity 1.1: Provide structured child protection and psychosocial support programs, including for children with disabilities (Target 8,900, 50% girls, including 250 CWD)Activity 1.2: Provide case management and referral to appropriate services (including but not limited to social protection, WASH, health, and education services) for boys and girls with protection needs. Priority will be given to children who have been directly affected by latest escalations through coordination with MoSD, especially those who lost caregivers, injured children and those whom their houses damaged (Target 3,000, 60% Boys, 50 CWD). Children with protection needs, including psychosocial distress identified through other UNICEF programs, including Social Protection will be referred to receive counseling and/or case management support as per their identified needs. All children joining group activities, as well as caregivers will receive one session on ERW risk education, especially in areas close to the borders, hot zones or where incidents of ERW accidents have taken place. Output 2: Integrated child protection services supported to strengthen parental capacity to respond to children's needs, including those with disabilities.Activity 2.1: Organise awareness-raising sessions for male and female parents on gender-specific child protection issues and positive parenting (including a session on ERW awareness) (Target 4,300, 20% Males).Output 3: Capacity for professionals working on MHPSS support strengthened. Activity 3.1: Conduct training and coaching on child protection, detection, identification and referral to non-CP professionals (Target 300, 50% females).UNICEF and its partners will contribute to the implementation of the community engagement activities and provide technical support during the design of the activities. Also, they will monitor and supervise the progress of the works daily and share issues and concerns with UNICEF. UNICEF developed an online platform for monitoring the projects to oversee the implementation closely. All partners have been trained and have to upload the information in the system. The two partners will coordinate response with each other, with MoSD and other members of the CPAoR, MHPSS and GBV Working Groups. Information about the channels to report on sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) will be communicated to all project beneficiaries, to ensure compliance with AAP and SEA principles and prevention. and response to SEA. All the 12 family centers will be supported with PSEA communication materials including how to report SEA-related complaints

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The safety of children, caregivers, and family centres’ staff will be ensured through the selection of safe and child-friendly spaces. To ensure the dignity of beneficiaries, family centres staff will be observing the way they approach beneficiaries, which will respect their privacy and ensure the confidential handling of their information. Additionally, the family centres will use context-appropriate manuals for group counseling. The child protection case management Standard Operating Procedures will govern the work of the case managers in the family centres, which prioritize the needs and the best interest of the child and takes into consideration their holistic welfare and protection without compromising their safety. The principles of inclusion of children with disabilities will also be taken into consideration when designing the various family centres' activities. Within the children corners and caregivers awareness-raising sessions, all project beneficiaries will be oriented on sexual exploitation and abuse and will receive information on UNICEF email to report on PSEA. They will also be made aware of the other interagency channels including the SAWA Free toll number, social media platforms, and WhatsApp.UNICEF safeguards beneficiaries’ rights to safe and dignified interventions. Consent of beneficiaries is usually considered and obtained through partners and implementing agencies joining training and community interventions. UNICEF ensures applying international standards, for working with children. UNICEF developed feedback mechanisms from beneficiaries to ensure accountability toward affected populations and to mitigate any risks identified by end-users, including SEA and toll-free. UNICEF also conducts end-user monitoring field visits and focus group discussion to ensure the safety and quality of services and supplies provided.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Individual protection needs of both girls and boys (6-18), especially those directly affected by the latest 2021/2022 escalations, and its repercussions on families’ protection and wellbeing will be addressed through the project. This will be done through the provision of psychosocial support services to girls and boys impacted by the aggression, as well as case management of female children who might be affected by violence and early marriage and boys affected by violence, child labor, and injuries by (ERW). Both males and females will be targeted through the project, making sure that they understand their contribution to their children’s wellbeing and ensuring their safety and protection, especially during limitations of movement. Both male and female case managers will be available to provide support either face to face or remotely over the phone or through social media platforms.
