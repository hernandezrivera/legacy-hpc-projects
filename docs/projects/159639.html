Project Summary

Gender-based Violence (GBV) remains a serious concern, particularly for women and girls in Somalia.
The Somalia humanitarian situation has continued to worsen in 2019 as a result of severe climatic conditions combined with other persistent drivers such as armed conflict. This has resulted in protracted and new displacements.
According to UN OCHA Somalia there are 2.6Million IDPS (internally displaced People) 700,000 of that number reside in Banadir District.
There continues to be a need for the protection of vulnerable groups. Women and children who are affected by displacement are at particular risk of exposure to gender-based violence (GBV), including rape.
The objective of the project is to ﬁll the existing critical gaps in protection targeting 23,300 beneﬁciaries in Banadir district. The purpose of the intervention is to significantly reduce the incidents of SGBV, promote awareness on prevention and response to GBV and provide child protection services to the community living in Banadir.
This project intends to meet the immediate, lifesaving needs of women and girl survivors of such violence and support them, their families and communities to recover. Protection activities include provision of psychosocial support including specialized counseling sessions, group therapy, support groups, Medical referrals, legal aid counseling, distribution of emergency social assistance (dignity kits), awareness and sensitization on GBV prevention and response, Capacity building for community structures on GBV prevention and response and comprehensive case management services at the Women Wellness centers and Safe houses.
Establishment of Child friendly spaces, facilitation of psychosocial support for children including play therapy, art work , drawing, recreational activities and basic literary and numeracy.

Project Activities

The project will address the needs of the community through the following activities: Provision of Psycho social support. Survivors of GBV require appropriate and timely support to deal with the psychological and mental health consequences of violence – which may include depression, post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), anxiety, and thoughts of suicide or self-harm – and the potential social consequences, including stigma, discrimination and rejection. Mental health and psychosocial support are essential components of the comprehensive package of care and aim to protect or promote psychosocial well-being and/or prevent or treat mental disorders among survivors of GBV. Psychosocial support, as part of a holistic and community-based intervention, can provide crucial assistance to survivors, as well as their families, social networks and communities, and help to prevent violence in the future. Strengthening of community based protection mechanisms in the IDP camps. SSWC will strengthen existing community structures on GBV prevention and response by training selected community members on community based protection mechanisms and also conducting awareness sessions at IDP sites to promote awareness on GBV prevention and response. Training and building capacity of community leaders and key gatekeepers is important so that they can be champions of change. Facilitate distribution of emergency social assistance (Dignity kits) to GBV survivors and vulnerable children in the IDP sites. The kits will contain hygiene and sanitary items explicitly tailored towards the local needs of women and girls of reproductive age. Dignity kits help women and girls maintain their dignity during humanitarian crises. Preserving dignity is essential to maintaining self-esteem and confidence, which is important to cope in stressful and potentially overwhelming humanitarian situations. Supporting women’s self-esteem and confidence also assists them in providing care and protection to their children and reducing their risk to GBV. Carry out community based awareness sessions and forums at IDP sites to promote awareness on protection risks faced by women and children. SSWC will Involve the IDP communities through community forums and associations and initiate dialogues on GBV to change attitudes, perceptions and behaviorson a societal level. Develop and disseminate messages on GBV Prevention and Response through radio spots in local language. In most IDP Camps communication is by means of radio. SSWC will partner with a local radio station with wide coverage within the IDP camps and disseminate GBV radio spots at prime hours to reach masses with the messages. Provision of case management services to GBV survivors including children through Women wellness centers. SSWC manages two women wellness centers in Banadir district where survivors of GBV are attended to and receive comprehensive services including admission to safe houses for beneficiaries with heightened protection concerns, Clinical care which is done by trained nurses and medical referrals and legal aid counseling. Facilitate psychosocial support sessions for children at child friendly spaces (CFS). SSWC Will Construct child friendly spaces for children in the IDP Camps. Counselors and educators will facilitate psychosocial support sessions for children in CFSs. The Psychosocial sessions will target children in the IDP camps and host community who experience low to medium psychological distress. The sessions are aimed at providing children with a sense of normalcy, enhancing their ability to cope with the situation and fostering their long term emotional and social wellbeing which can mitigate long lasting psychological damage.

Mitigation Measures

Exclusion and Marginalization of Women, girls and Boys. Women and children might face risks of being excluded in decision making processes on issues that affect them. In the Somali community,men are considered the key decision makers and give women and children no room for contributing their views and opinions. Save Somali Women and children will ensure that their is equal participation of women, men and children in all activities. Through outreach and awareness sessions, SSWC will sensitize and empower women and children on their rights. SSWC will develop a strong criteria for beneficiary selection that will include women, girls and boys. Risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure in targeted project areasThere is the high risk of displacement and evictions of the most vulnerable persons in the IDP camps. SSWC will encourage the government to address the challenge through the land tenure system, lease land for more than 10 years or buy permanent land for them. Barriers for beneficiaries to report concerns through complaint and feedback mechanisms Ensure accountability to affected populations Be transparent with the affected populations by providing them with accessible and timely information on selection criteria for targeted assistance, organizational procedures and processes that affect them. Set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. E.g. place feedback surveys in dignity kits, feedback box at service centers, phone lines. Set up mechanisms for submitting feedback that does not require the beneficiary exposing themselves to project staff. Respond to complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can/need to be put in place. Organize awareness raising sessions so that people know how it works. Consider a joint feedback mechanism with other sectors to minimize confusion. Protection staff and committees should be representative of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, disability) and all staff and members should be given protection mainstreaming training. They can play a key role in identifying issues related to exclusion and discrimination and be proactive in ensuring the voice of marginalized groups is represented. Ensure protection staff, implementing partners and volunteers working with affected populations understand, sign and adhere to a Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries. Ensure all staff, implementing partners and volunteers involved and in direct contact with beneficiaries have received training in prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) Provide clear information to protection actors and affected population explaining that beneficiaries do not have to pay or provide services/favors to anyone in exchange for receiving services. Ensure that this information is communicated to women and girls. In cooperation with protection actors, enhance the capacity of community workers to monitor, report and refer protection concerns (such as abuse and exploitation) in accordance with standard operating procedures

Please provide further explanation

The Project will focus on Strengthening existing community based Protection mechanisms and structures and linking them with local authorities who will closely work together with them in the IDP Camps even after the project closure. The service provision enters are locally owned and government provides oversight role in all its activities making it recognised legally. The project will also focus on building capacity for members of the community structures, training them on protection and equipping them with knowledge and skills that they can always use. The project encourages community ownership of the properties being used in supporting the vulnerable population. The sense of community belonging and acceptance will encourage community structures to continue providing protection services.
