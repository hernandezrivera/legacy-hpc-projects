Project Summary

In 2021, SARD’s conducted a YNA (Yearly Needs Assessment) which observed the needs in various sectors including Food, Non-Food Items, Shelter, and Livelihood sectors. Findings showed that 25% of households reported that their food consumption decreased in the last month. Furthermore, the main challenge relating to food access pertained to thehigh price of food items in local markets, as reported by 78% of households, the low quality of available food (36%), and the lack of variety of goods in the local market (14%). Additionally, safety and security concerns in markets, particularly for women, were reported as an impediment to access.
SARD is committed to assisting the most affected people in northwest Syria, despite unpredictability and the risks of working in areas with security threats. The response plan with a budget of 2,000,000 USD will address the needs of more than 16500 people over the course of 15 months, providing life-saving and life-sustaining assistance. The action contributes to improving shelters and providing life-sustaining assistance with Non-FoodItems (NFI) for selected most vulnerable persons including IDPs who have been recently displaced or displaced multiple times.
Implemented with a protection lens, and addressing gender and disabled-specific needs, the shelter response is aimed at providing housing options in emergency and life-sustaining situations. The activities include emergency shelter in camp settings,
temporary shelter solutions in open fields, life-saving shelter upgrades, and life-sustaining house rehabilitation through a self-help approach. The modality of intervention will be adapted to the urgency, situation, needs, and HLP situation of the beneficiaries. SARD is committed to promoting social cohesion including shelter programming based on community approach. The shelter activities are detailed as follows:
-Upgrade of IDP sites/camps including infrastructure improvements, Road graveling, tent insulation, and tent replacement to cope with flood season. Upgrade of IDP sites/camps will also include the shift to dignified shelters where SARD will replace the tents with better shelter solutions that will provide safe, secure, and longer-term accommodation to the beneficiaries. SARD’s team in coordination with CCCM, SNFI clusters, and local authorities proceeded with the assessment and the selection of the most vulnerable IDP sites/camps camp.
-Emergency shelter option with the distribution of shelter kit by SARD’s team.
-Collective shelter upgrade and unfinished building repair by contractors under SARD’stechnical supervision.
-House rehabilitation through provision of cash grants under SARD’s supervision.
The shelter response proposed in this action is integrated with WASH and basic shelter-related NFI support adapted to the needs of the assessed, eligible families. The modality of NFI interventions will range from in-kind to cash/voucher according to the market accessibility and availability, family capacity, and needs. Door-to-door distribution is the preferred option to ensure specific needs and security are addressed to girls, women, the elderly, and people with special needs.

4 - Needs

According to the yearly need assessment (YNA) implemented in 2021 by SARD, The second-highest proportion after food are living in formal camps (30%) and shared housing (24%) revealing critical living conditions, particularly for IDPs group. Furthermore, it is estimated that at least 31 % of the population live in inadequate shelter conditions, with IDPs and returnees disproportionately affected.At the household level, 93% of those households live in private homes, while the remaining 7% are hosted by other families. On the other hand, only 39% of displaced households live in private houses, while 47% live in official camps and 10% in unfinished buildings. Among the returnees interviewed, 78% live in private houses, while the remaining 19% live with another family. On average, displaced households share their dwelling with two other families and residents with one other family, while the number of families in unfinished buildings rises to three. The number of households can increase up to 22 families in shared settlements such as unfinished buildings.

5 - Implementation of activities

To address the shelter needs as described above, shelter assistance will be provided ranging from emergency interventions with shelter kits, road graveling (include tent insulation, and tent replacement), and core NFIs to house rehabilitation, shelter upgrade, and community projects. SARD will ensure newly-displaced families have access to life-saving assistance through emergency basic shelter kits in the open field and quick-fi xing at collective shelters/unfinished buildings. The action provides also life-sustaining shelter support to most vulnerable families including house rehabilitation through cash grants. In addition to this, SARD will be upgrading IDP sites/camps on both the site infrastructure level and the shelter unit level. Activities will take place as follows: assessment of the security, locations, tenders, and markets’ surveys will enable SARD to determine the modality of response (cash, voucher, or in-kind). Identification and targeting of beneficiaries, registration, preparation of BoQs, distributions/implementation, monitoring, and post- monitoring, and close coordination with relevant clusters, including sharing the4Ws matrix and identifying gaps. The selection will be based on eligibility criteria and scoring mechanism, taking into consideration HLP documentation. Bill of Quantities will be prepared for shelter upgrades in close consultation with the beneficiaries. For self-help modality (cash grants).

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: Under the current context, the most vulnerable categories, such as women-headed HH, PwD, may be excluded from accessing the service due to socio-cultural barriers.Likelihood: medium, Impact: HighMitigation Measures. a)SARD increases visibility for self-referral to ensure the inclusion of the most vulnerable individuals. b) SARD applies a scoring system to all selected beneficiaries in order to categorize them according to their vulnerabilities. Protection Risk: The high target ofthe project may lead in some cases to reduce the quality of the service.Likelihood: low, Impact: medium.Mitigation Measures: a) Prioritization of safety and dignity and avoiding causing harm: in IDP sites/camps, the program and MEL teams constantly monitor the quality of the assistance delivered, ensuring that only quality activities are provided. b) SARD has a strong CRM in place that is regularly advertised to local communities, currently, due to Covid-19 prevention measures, beneficiaries, and non-beneficiaries can register any feedback/complaints via SARD’s WhatsApp number. Protection Risk: The blanket approach of the program limits the possibility of providing tailored service to persons in Need.Likelihood: Medium, Impact: MediumMitigation Measures: SARD coordinate with protection actors to enhance referral pathways and ensure providing the service based on the needs of beneficiaries.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

I. Safety and Security Risk The volatile and often unpredictable nature of the Syrian conflict continues to impose significant risk on humanitarian staff and beneficiaries' safety. Risks could include but are not limited to a sudden escalation in hostilities, airstrikes,armed clashes, and kidnapping. The likelihood of increased conflict hostilities isconsidered moderate in northern Aleppo compared to opposition-controlled areas of Idleband western Aleppo. The impact of this risk is assessed as high in both areas. SARD hasdeveloped and adapted tools to ensure a sufficient level of standard of care to safeguardemployees and beneficiaries from a reasonably foreseeable risk of harm is maintained. II.Operations and Program-related Risks The high-risk environment of northwest Syriacontinues to impose significant risk on the continuation of humanitarian activities. Risksto humanitarian programs implementation in the context of northwest Syria include butnot limited to partial or complete closure of border crossings with Turkey; disruption of thesupply chain; fluctuating exchange rate; and community acceptance. The likelihood andthe impact of operations and program-related risks in areas of northern Aleppo, Idleb, andwestern Aleppo were assessed as high. SARD has developed and adapted tools to ensure its readiness to cope and adjust with sudden unexpected risks as they arise. III. Financial andReputation Risks The absence of a fully functioning banking system in NWS.
