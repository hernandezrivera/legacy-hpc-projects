Project Summary

The project will directly contribute to support the local community by provide a safer environment for children. The project contributes to qualitative and integrated education services, also contributes towards maintaining and enhancing equitable access to education services.
This project will be implemented in Northwest Syria, Idleb in Dana (89.43% Severity 4 / 10.01% Severity 5) and Ariha (72.81% Severity 4 ) sub-districts Locations have been identified based on accessibility and Severity scale 22-23, however, this might change due to situation on the ground.
The education objective is to increase access and quality of education to IDP children and host communities. The project intends to operate in area with high severity ranking (Ariha 5 / Dana 4) and with high numbers with Children in need (Ariha 43,509 / Dana 457,971) around 7% of the PiN in the targeted areas are from CwDs The main goal of this project is to scale up safe and equitable access to formal\ non-formal education for school-aged girls and boys between ages 5-17 by:
1. Provide operational and running costs (such as fuel for heating, salaries) for the targeted schools, hygiene and classroom’s stationary which includes adapted tools for children with disabilities;
2. Minor rehabilitation and maintenance of classrooms which includes ensuring accessibility for children with disabilities;
3. Provide school furniture and safety equipment, including white boards, desks, equipment, etc.;
4. Conduct trainings for teachers and education personnel; Teachers in Crisis Context (TICC), PSS, Safety trainings and training on working with children with disabilities;
5. Provision of non-formal education: Accelerated learning programs (ALP), Basic Literacy and Numeracy (BLN), Catch-up classes, self-learning program (SLP), Remedial classes, support to ensure that these programs are accessible for children with learning and intellectual disabilities;
6. Provide education kit (school bag and stationary) for children which can include adapted devices to enable learning for children with disabilities and kits for teachers;
7. Provide recreational materials which are diverse to ensure that materials are accessible for all children;
8. Payment of incentives to teachers and education personnel;
9. Provision of psychosocial support for children in both formal and non-formal schools which is accessible for all children;
10. Referral of children to relevant services (Health, Nutrition, mental support, etc.).

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project will contribute significantly to the gender marker as it specifically addresses services to vulnerable population. The project will specifically address the cross-cutting issues that will contribute to gender equality. One of the main protection risks or this project is that student undergoes bullying by other student or teachers. SOP will ensure that all learning facilities have an enforced code of conduct and policy on bullying and all teachers are trained on prevention and reporting of, and issues related to, physical punishment and bullying. It will also provide awareness sessions for both students and their caregivers. Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information are linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups. SOP will provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA. Another risk is the Privacy concerns along with confidentially and safety which could accessed by people who could pose a threat to beneficiaries. SOP ensures that confidentially principles are in line with global standards and will adhere to all protocols for risk mitigation, personal safety, movements, communications, and operations within Syria. We also have robust contingency planning and incident response protocols in place.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

PSEA will be mainstreamed in all the phases of the project as the following: 1- When hiring the staff of the project and teachers in the schools, The PSEA FP in Syria will provide an orientation to them about the PSEA policy and code of conduct used in SOP (what are the main 6 principles of PSEA, what are the channels for reporting violations …etc. 2- Provide awareness sessions to the members of PTA and community on the definition of sexual exploitation code of conduct, tell them they have the right to be free of PSEA, how can they complain including the stages of time-frame ,to whom they complain , what steps they can take to Protection Risk: Abusing or exploiting the children by teachers or other supporting staff Mitigating Measures: 1- AiBi & KP will make sure that PSEA policies are in place, practiced and monitored SOP will ensure that targeted communities and staff have access to safe and effective reporting systems Based of SOP's CRM policy.A compliant box will be placed in each school and there will be a WhatsApp number for receiving complaints and complaint will be handled in confidential manner , only concerned staff will have access to complaint system and information will be sorted and categorized based on the sensitivity scale to ensue rapid response to the received complaints , Additionally a compliant FP will be assigned to receive the feedback directly from the BNFs.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures
