Project Summary

The project aims to provide a wide-ranging support from emergency to regular food assistance as well as household based agricultural and livelihoods assistance. The project aims to target populations who are both food insecure as well as at risk of food insecurity through multiple but mutually reinforcing activities.
The project will contribute to the following two objectives of Food security cluster:
Objective 1: Improve the food security status of assessed food insecure people through life-saving and life sustaining food assistance.
Objective 2: Support self reliance of affected households by protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income generating opportunities to save and sustain lives.
A total of 30,000 households (150,000 individuals) will be supported through multiple but integrated activities.
Under first objective for life-saving and life-sustaining food assistance, the project will aim to meet the immediate food needs and also seasonal/supplementary food assistance. The Emergency Response will aim to reach crisis-affected people within 72 hours of the onset of a crisis to cover a minimum of one to four weeks of their immediate food needs through short-term support such as Ready-to-Eat Rations, cooked meals and/or baked bread.
Baked bread will also be provided to the displaced families living in the camps. this will help to stabilize the market prices.
further supplementary food assistance will be provided to improve the dietary diversity. such as Ramadan food distribution and Qurbani meat distribution.
under the objective 2, appropriate agricultural inputs (cereal as well as vegetable production) will be provided along with technical capacity building at household-level to ensure that most vulnerable farming households receive timely inputs as per seasonal calendar to be able to produce and contribute to domestic production requirements. further asset building and asset protection support mainly for livestock keepers and poultry will be provided. This include but not limited to provision of EWE sheep, poultry feed, and vaccination.
Finally, individuals including youth and people with disability will also be targeted for small scale income generation activities. such as barber shop, female saloon, grocery store etc.
Local procurement will be encouraged to promote local markets, especially where markets have the capacity to absorb such demands.
This objective also aim to restore and strengthen productive assets and create livelihood opportunities for the targeted populations, thus contributing to the overall food production and subsequently to food availability in Syria.

Needs

6.5 million people are estimated to be food insecure in Syria and a further 2.5 million people is at risk of food insecurity. - From the assessed households, an estimated 40 percent of Syrians spend more than 65 percent of their expenditure on food, 57.2 percent of food insecure households reported to have outstanding debt and 65 percent of food insecure people are adopting crisis or emergency negative coping strategies. Those who are categorized to be food insecure are reporting large food consumption gaps, depleted coping strategy and large food expenditure share. Those who are considered at risk of food insecurity are the “worst off” in the moderately food secure category and may be falling into food insecurity if their livelihoods are not supported. The main drivers of food insecurity in Syria are a set of interrelated factors related to the deteriorating economic environment as well as food insecurity stemming from massive displacements. Almost 40 percent of the surveyed households reported to be an IDP or returnee of whom 74.5 percent reported to be displaced for more than 12 months. Other key drivers include loss of livelihoods and reduced production capacity, limited physical and financial access to food, high prices and inflation, reduced purchasing power and continuous livelihood depletion of the most vulnerable populations. With an 83 - 89 percent people living below poverty lines , and with cumulative GDP loss of $226 billion between 2011 – 2016 out of which $16 billion in losses and damages attributable to agriculture sector alone, the relationship between poverty and food security is becoming inseparable. A monthly food ration with staple items costs at least 80 percent of an unskilled laborer’s monthly salary and 50-80 percent of a public service employee’s monthly salary thus demonstrating the existence of “working poor” in Syria. The worst drought in 30 years affected cereal production in 2017/2018 winter season in Syria. This has come as an additional burden on the prolonged crisis with the lowest wheat production at 1.2 million metric tonnes since 1989. There is a shortfall of wheat to meet domestic requirements (food use) of 3.4 million metric tonnes. Unprecedented livestock asset losses estimated to about half of the pre-crisis situation affecting livelihoods leading to irreversible coping strategies.food insecurity contributes to the risk of Gender Based Violence in situations of crisis. Lack of information or access to food security related can cause household tensions leading to negative coping mechanisms including tensions and early marriage. Qualitative and quantitative data provides support to the vulnerability of female-headed households as prevalence of food insecurity from the assessed households indicate that 28.1 percent of the male-headed households are food insecure, compared to 32.4 percent of the female headed households. Furthermore, from the assessed households, 44.2 percent of the male-headed households adopted crisis or emergency livelihood coping strategies compared to 47.3 percent of the female-headed households, who are often placed in a situation where they are at risk of being exploited due to their status. Furthermore, divorcees or widows have difficulties to access assistance because of customs and traditions at times and harassment may occur at distribution points. In some areas, females’ salaries working as agricultural labor are lower than males’ earnings (CFSAM). Child labor and early dropouts of school continues to be a major coping strategy of farmers and displaced households. In conclusion, the humanitarian needs in Syria are expected to remain high in 2019 and multiple actions are needed to ensure that all four pillars of food security related to access, availability, utilization and stability of food are adequately addressed through lifesaving, life sustaining, appropriate resilience, and early recovery interventions.

Implementation of activities

The following are the main project activities. A total of 30,000 households (150,000 individuals) will be targeted. 1.Emergency response to crisis affected vulnerable people with short term food assistance through appropriate modalities. This intervention will target 5000 households (25,000 people). 2.Supplementary food assistance to Persons with Specific Needs (PSN) through appropriate modalities (complementary to above and inter linkage with nutrition sector). This also include seasonal food support such as during Ramadan and Qurbani. This intervention will target 17,000 household (85,000 people).3. Support to bakeries (wheat flour, yeast, etc.) through appropriate modalities. This intervention will target 3,500 households (17,500 people).4. Distribution of agricultural inputs, such as seeds, fertilizer, pesticide and equipment. This activity will target 1,500 households (7,500 people).5. Support to small scale food production ( poultry egg laying hens). This activity will target 1000 households (5,000 people).6. Support to asset building and asset protection (small livestock and animal feed distribution) including winterization activities. This intervention will assist 1,000 households (5,000 people).7. Emergency livestock treatment, and training for veterinary services. (same as of activity 6)8. Support Income generating activities including vocational training. This activity will target 1,000 households (5,000 individuals).Most of the Youth are already economically active in Syria. The project will target the youth and people with disability for income generation activities that include vocational training to build the human capital. youth and people with disability will also be targeted for the support including provision of small enterprise grants or tools or equipment to start their own business and establish a livelihood base. Islamic Relief will promote gender sensitive programming at all stages of the project cycle (i.e. in beneficiary targeting, prioritization of distributions, participation in activities and monitoring). Every effort will be made to promote gender equality and ensure that men and women are able to influence beneficiary selection, the type of aid provided, and equal access to assistance provided, and that they are included in consultations that will inform programming decision making and monitoring. When distributing assistance, the specific mobility challenges faced by female and child heads of households and those with disabilities will be factored in. This will be done by setting up the distribution points close to the villages whereby these household are residing. Que time will be reduced by informing the households exact date and time of the distribution. Also where required, door to door distribution will be ensured for the vulnerable groups having mobility challenges. Islamic Relief will promote and strengthen the collection and analysis of sex and age dis aggregated data throughout the project cycle to facilitate robust programming. The project committees will be formed for males and females. The project will target the men and women equally as the primary recipient of the support. since women and girls have an important role in household food and nutrition and traditionally rural women have experience of being engaged in household level activities like sheep breeding and medium scale farming. thereby women and girls will be provided equal access to productive assets, and vocational training.since some of these activities will be implemented in the same areas thereby Food security cluster integration strategy will be applied. whereby the people benefiting from food assistance will be targeted for livelihood to promote early recovery. similarly, vulnerable farming households will receive protection food rations so that they are able to protect their assets up to harvesting.

Protection Risk Analysis

The following are the main protection risks and the mitigation measures:1. In provision of general food assistance there is a risk of violence from the beneficiaries among themselves as well as the humanitarian staff. Also there is a risk of SEA by humanitarian workers male dominated, gate keepers and potential other beneficiaries. This risk will be mitigated through establishing a robust complaint and feedback mechanism so that any such cases are appropriately reported and followed up. The organisational staff will be trained on the Inter Agency Coordination in Community Based Complaint Mechanisms as well as code of conduct. To the extent possible, female staff will be recruited and community committees will be formed for women. post distribution monitoring will be conducted. it will be ensured that the distribution is well organised. For example identification of sites first, set up several distribution points , do joint distribution with other partners. 2. The beneficiary lists are not updated by the local council. Interference with beneficiary targeting. To mitigate this risk the staff will be well trained on the beneficiary selection criteria and mechanism to cross verify the vulnerability of the population. The communities will be informed of the beneficiary selection criteria and a through community mobilization will be conducted to ensure that the communities are sensitized and are able to voice their concerns in a timely manner. The local councils will be supported to prepare the beneficiary lists. Regular meetings will be organised with the community leaders or gate keepers on the targeting criteria. Community feedback system will be established. Well trained and gender balanced outreach team will be used so that they could check if some locations are not included in the beneficiaries list.3. Diversion of aid is another possible risk. This will be mitigated through staff training, consultation and coordination with all the relevant stakeholders including local council, other humanitarian organizations, and the targeted communities. Complaint and feedback mechanism should be established and should remain functional. 4. For market/cash based responses, risks include heightened visibility for beneficiaries/exposure, Cash/voucher overly spent on non food items, thus negatively impact food security outcomes. Cash for work opportunities may increase the risk for female to sexual exploitation. As a mitigation, conditional cash assistance will be provided through vouchers. Vendor monitoring system will be emplaced. Vary the payment days. The food security assessments will be conducted to ascertain/inform needs. It will be ensure to have close monitoring by female aid workers.5. Targeting for IDPs versus Host communities. The risk includes inclusion and exclusion errors leading to tensions and sexual exploitation of IDP female. This will be mitigated through local beneficiary selection committees with equitable representation of both IDPs and host communities. Clear/precise guidance to the staff on the beneficiary selection process and criteria. Effective communication as regards the guidelines include communication materials in local language.6. Targeting for IDPs/Returnees for livelihood projects. The risks include access to land/assets. Risk to secondary rights holders e.g. female headed households. This will be mitigated through engaging with local authorities to verify status. Work alongside other complementary programs on HLP.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

some of the major risks and the mitigation measures are as follows: 1. Potential conflict between the beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries.Mitigation measure: To prevent conflict between the beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries, IR will ask the local councils and the residents of these areas to nominate a community representative to represent the people in the area. IR, in cooperation with the Community Representative, will choose the beneficiaries according to specific criteria to serve the community and will adopt a clear and transparent policy in the implementation of the project. 2. Outbreak of major livestock /poultry disease.Mitigation measure: The livestock and poultry will be vaccinated against the major diseases before distribution to the targeted families. The veterinary officer will ensure to keep a record of animal health and report immediately about any major diseases outbreak so that appropriate action is taken. 3. Destruction of cultivated lands by air strike.Mitigation Measure: This is an external factor beyond the control of the project. In case of any such security incident donor will be informed in timely manner. Actions will be taken in consultation with local community, M&E and programme team and donor.
