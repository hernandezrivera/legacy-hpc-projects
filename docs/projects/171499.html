Project Summary

The 2020 HRP estimates that overall 5.2 million people are in need humanitarian assistance (compared to 4.2 million in 2019). CoDHNet will improve the access and utilization of health services for women, men, boys, and girls including people with disabilities living in the most crisis-affected populations in IDP settlements and the vulnerable urban and rural poor in Dolow, Belet-Hawa and Luq Districts of Gedo Region of Jubala State of Somalia. Static facilities and mobile outreach teams will be set up and strengthened through provision of medical supplies and the improvement of staff capacity. Remote villages will be reached through mobile clinics; community mobilizers and Community Health Workers (CHWs) will be recruited and trained.
The proposed activities will include supplemental vaccination campaign to under5 children (boys and girls) and women of child bearing age (WCBA); Provision of antenatal and post-natal care to pregnant and lactating mothers, strengthening physical and mental healthcare system. Through this intervention in the target locations will strengthen and improve access to essential lifesaving health services aimed to reduce morbidity and mortality, maternal and child morbidity and mortality among. The activities will seek to contribute to improvement in the physical and mental wellbeing of the population affected by conflict and displacement among IDPs and non-displaced; including people with disabilities. Further, seek to improve equitable access to quality emergency and essential lifesaving health services for the crisis-affected population IDPs, and non-displaced population. Health care services through consultation and management of childhood illnesses with an aim f reducing maternal and neonatal mortality will be given focus. ANC and BEMONC with referrals of the CEMONC cervices will be provided to the pregnant mothers.
Treatment and control of communicable diseases targeting the most vulnerable boys, girls, PLW, WCBA, and men; 27690 (6,646 female, 4,707 male, 7,476 boys and 8,307 girls, and 554 elderly) beneficiaries from both host and IDP communities with healthcare services, out of which 2,769 beneficiaries are people with disabilities; Strengthening of a referral system between Primary Healthcare facilities, the community (remote villages) and the regional hospitals; Establishment of mobile clinics; Creating Disease Surveillance and Response plan; Training of health workers on clinical management of childhood illness, Basic Emergency Obstetric Care and disease surveillance and response. Training will be conducted for community health workers in integrated community case management on communicable diseases such as pneumonia, diarrhea, and malaria.
CoDHNet has been operating in the project locations, thus the positive relationship between CoDHNet and the community in the district and enjoys full access to both districts. CoDHNet will undertake stakeholder engagement targeting beneficiaries in the target villages, their leadership, local authority and Ministry of health officials to provide an overview of the intended project including the objectives, key activities, indicator and the target beneficiaries. Participatory monitoring and evaluation will be employed with regard to frequency, keeping track/ assessing the effectiveness of the interventions with the aim of working within and where necessary adjusting the work plan to enhance efficiency and realization of project objectives. Proper coordination with partners, the respective focal point representatives as well as the zonal and the national team will be adhered to avoid overlap and share the 4W Matrix report to the Health Cluster respectively.

Mitigation Measures

1) Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services: Mitigation measures: During the implementation of the project, the mobile team units will operate near the beneficiaries' settlements to avoid gender-based violence, while are seeking the health services that the mobile team units are extending. CoDHNet will give priority vulnerable and marginalized people including people with disabilities. 2) Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.). Mitigation: CoDHNet Community Health workers (CHWs) especially Female Health workers and Community Liaison focal person will engage community stakeholders on its available health care services and as well enhance community education, mobilization and awareness on available Protection services in its areas of operation. CoDHNet protection sector will ensure referral pathways are available for protection survivors. 3) Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys, and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. Mitigation measures: CoDHNet will ensure that the marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys, and girls, participate in consultations or decision-making processes during the project design, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation. CoDHNet will put robust measures to address barriers that hinder access to health services or prevent health-seeking behaviors especially among youth, disabled, illiterate and poor, GBV and protection affected beneficiaries. CoDHNet will coordinate its activities and identify disadvantaged and vulnerable groups (IDPs, minority clans, other marginalized) that may face unequal access to health, preventing unintended harm working in close collaboration with other community resource persons. To meet the specific needs of vulnerable people separate meetings will be held with the women and girls including those with disabilities, facilitated by female staff. During the sensitization meetings, action plans will be drawn with a clear division of roles and responsibilities. The capacity building of the beneficiaries is planned to empower them to participate fully in the implementation process. The project will promote participatory monitoring and monthly feedback to all stakeholders to enhance accountability. 4) Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms Mitigation measures: CODHNet will develop elaborate beneficiary complaint and feedback tool, project beneficiaries and stakeholders will be given a 1- day workshop on how to use the complaint /feedback system to enhance transparency and accountability. CoDHNet will ensure that the project implementation process is accountable and transparent to the community and to the stakeholders including women, people with disabilities, and underserved communities. Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) will also be used to make sure that the beneficiaries are satisfied with services provided and complaints can be channeled in the right direction. One of the ways of achieving this is through the establishment of a feedback and complaint mechanism to record and act on expression of satisfaction or dissatisfaction about the quality of the project services.
