Project Summary

The development objective of the provision of essential health services project is to increase access to an essential package of health services in the Republic of Sudan, with a particular focus on Khartoum , West, North, East and South Darfur States. The project will focus on the delivery of essential preventive & curative health services where it will support the provision of a defined package of essential health services, targeting the populations of the selected states and other vulnerable communities affected by conflict and violence. The project will also strengthen the capacity of PHC workers and community health workers to investigate and detect disease outbreaks in the target states at locality and state level. HDPO will collaborate and coordinate with locality and state development committees in identifying and mobilising people for project intervention. At locality and state level, the already established networks and links with the committees mentioned will be strengthened by HDPO in all stages of the project like from designing, implementation, monitoring and evaluation.

1 - Needs

Since the beginning of the armed conflict is Sudan between SAF and RSF have been ongoing since 15 April, services delivery has been heavily affected. As of 13 May, the Federal Ministry of Health reports that at least 700 people have been killed and over 5,200 injured since the onset of the fighting. The number of casualties is likely to be higher, as many individuals were reportedly unable to reach hospitals due to movement constraints. In a region already significantly affected by conflict, insecurity, displacement and high levels of humanitarian needs, civilians have been caught in the crossfire of ongoing violence and face targeted attacks, including reported cases of sexual and gender-based violence. The sudden armed conflict has resulted in the killing and injury of civilians, as well as emotional and psychological distress for the affected people. The ongoing violence is also hindering families from organizing dignified burials. The health service provision across the country and specifically in the country’s capital Khartoum and Darfur; has been heavily impacted with discontinuity of several critical programs due to violence and attacks on health, disruption of supply chain, and poor accessibility by the population to facilities that can provide needed services. Due to heavily centralized health system and reliance on the country’s capital for medical supplies distribution, performing laboratory tests, funding, and other aspects of the health provision across the country, all states have been negatively affected by the violence that erupted in Khartoum. Health facilities and systems in Khartoum and in the greater Darfur are either not functioning or closed, which limits access to care, malnutrition treatment and vaccination for children under five. In Khartoum, 61% of health facilities are closed and only 16% are operating as normal, leaving millions of people without access to health care. Since the start of the conflict, the World Health Organization (WHO) has verified 30 attacks on health care facilities, resulting in eight people killed and another 18 injured. A maternity hospital was attacked in Khartoum on 4 May. Power shortages, limited medical supplies, and infrastructure damage are severely hampering the delivery of essential health care. The national amidst an ongoing polio outbreak reported across the country before the crisis. Several cold chain facilities have been looted, damaged or destroyed, including the loss of over 600,000 vaccines intended for children thus disruption of the vaccination programme. Urgent assistance is required to restore critical emergency care and provide the Essential Health Services in Khartoum, North, South, East and West Darfur States.

2 - Activities/Outputs

For health systems to be resilient against health crises like during conflict, strong primary and community health care the frontline of all health systems is essential. Primary health care delivers the first line of care in communities during the acute phase of a health crisis, and helps maintaining continuity of care for people with chronic conditions. This project aims to the support the provision of essential public health functions with a focus on strong primary health care in Khartoum, North, South, West, and South Darfur States.The project will comprise of the following activities and outputs: Activity 1: To provide essential primary and secondary health care services, including trauma care, EmONC and referrals. Output 1.1: 40000 outpatient consultations conducted in the 12 health facilities in the 4 selected states; Output 1.2: 6,000 people enrolled on National in need for health assistance in the national health insurance system (1,500 per state); Outputs 1.3: 10,000 trauma consultations supported and provided in locality and health facility level; Outputs 1.4: 10,000 mental health consultations conducted; Outputs 1.4: 2400 vaginal deliveries attended by a skilled attendant in the four states; Outputs 1.6: 12,000 antenatal care sessions conducted where pregnant women benefited from maternal, newborn and child health services; Outputs 1.7: 6000 postnatal sessions conducted in 12 health facilities and outreaches; Outputs 1.8: 4000 cases referred for specialized treatment (among levels of care across Sudan); Outputs 1.9: 6000 admissions to receive inpatient treatment; and Outputs 1.10: 80 physical rehabilitation and psychosocial support sessions conducted 20 per state. Activity 2: To expand the reporting capacity of the early warning systems. Output 2.1: 12 sentinel sites submitting weekly surveillance reports two per state established and strengthened. Activity 3: To strengthen capacity to investigate and detect disease outbreaks. Output 3.1: 400 community-based surveillance officers trained, deployed and reporting (100 per state) to identify people who at high degree of exposure to disease. Activity 4: Support health authorities to carryout timely response to emergencies and disease outbreaks. Output 3.1.1: 300 rapid response teams members trained and deployed (120 medical & 180 community health workers). Activity 5: Increase access to health services by supporting mobile medical units and referral pathways. Output 5.1: 4: mobile medical units supported to provide PHC services at high-risk localities of the selected Darfur states; Output 5.2: 4 ambulance and four motorcycles bought to increase access to health services by supporting mobile medical units and referral pathways. Activity 6: To strengthen the capacity of health care providers and community health care workers to provide essential health services. Output 6.1: 800 community health workers trained/re-trained on different health topics; and Output 6.2: 800 community health workers trained/retrained on different health topics (in PHC awareness campaigns and referrals). Activity 7: Increase access to health services by rehabilitating and refurbishing health facilities with provision of medical equipment. Outputs 7.1: 12 health facilities refurbished or rehabilitated to improve and supported to provide quality health services in the project areas (selection will be based on HDPO assessment findings and MoH priorities) will comprise of 8 PHCs and community-level health facilities and 4 stated level hospitals.

With regard to ecologically unfriendly activities like environmental pollution due to conflicts and congestion, HDPO look to have a more stable role in environment protection or management activities and governance to help protect the health risks. During the implementation of this project, environmental preservation practices will be considered by HDPO. Therefore, this project will respect the rights of the beneficiaries, partners and donors to information on the environment and natural resources; and also, will also consider their right to be consulted and participate in decision-making on activities that have a significant impact on the environment; and their right to legal remedies and redress when their health or environment may be affected. 6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

While there is great uncertainty about the current conflict in Sudan, particularly in Khartoum and Darfur regions, there are several lessons for provision of essential health services strategies based on experiences from past disastrous. This is an important moment to bring in that learning and ensure that risk communication is evidence based and does not reinforce stigma or discrimination. HDPO will expand the consideration of who is at risk, while focusing on targeted community engagement. This project will focus on health promotion-based strategies and work with community-based leaders and organisations with histories of outreach and engagement in Darfur. Since health resources are limited, community participation will be encouraged during the implementation of this project conflict or remote areas like in IDPs and host communities. Here effectiveness of community-based outreach program will be designed to improve the preventive health services in different localities in the Khartoum, North, South, West and East States who lack health services.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) is a term used by the UN and NGO community to refer to measures taken to protect vulnerable people from sexual exploitation and abuse by staff and associated personnel. During the course of their work, aid providers come into contact with vulnerable people. Humanitarian workers have access to goods and services that put them in a position of power over the community. Unfortunately, some humanitarian workers have used this imbalance of power to exploit and abuse vulnerable members of the community. The health sector and healthcare providers should strive and commit to preventing sexual exploitation and abuse both on organizational and programmatic levels.
