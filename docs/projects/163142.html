Project Summary

This project aims to provide assistance to the populations affected by the Lake Chad Basin crisis in the Far North region of Cameroon, particularly in the divisions of Mayo Sava, Mayo Tsanaga, and Logoné-et-Chari. The ongoing violence carried out by armed opposition groups has had widespread impact on livelihoods and food security, limiting communities’ ability to meet their basic needs and exposing them to various protection risks. The proposed intervention will to continue the implementation of activities already underway by the IRC through previous support from OFDA over the past two years, aiming to provide direct support to beneficiaries to meet their basic needs across multiple services areas. These include provision of drinking water, promotion of hygiene and sanitation, the restoration of livelihoods and the introduction of actions to promote protection and prevent gender-based violence (GBV). The previous OFDA funded projects have allowed IRC to develop an in-depth knowledge of beneficiary communities and the humanitarian needs confronting them. Based on the successes and lessons learned from its experiences working with these communities, the IRC plans to support and strengthen established structures and its local relations in order to improve the quality and scope of actions, while maintaining its capacity to react to spontaneous movements with mobile services that enable access hard-to-reach areas in persistent and precarious environments. The activities proposed by IRC are part of the mandate and mission assigned by OFDA/USAID for the response to the needs of the Lake Chad Basin.

Besoins / Needs

Over the past year, with OFDA support, the IRC has supported 600 households (among which 50% are IDPs) with agricultural support to restart agricultural related livelihoods and improve food security among these conflict-affected communities in the Far North. The IRC’s continuation of this activity under the proposed project builds on lessons learned over the last year to support a new cohort of households. In particular, to address land access issues for IDPs, the IRC has found with the local communities a strategy to provide them access to land. The main livelihood activities of the target population are agriculture and “petit trading”/IGAs. To support livelihoods restoration the IRC will furnish 300 households with business grants to carry out income generating activities. Based on competencies, beneficiaries will present business plans which the IRC will review and accept to fund approximately $1,200 per group to purchase their inputs for the IGA selected.The IRC will provide clinic care through established referral mechanisms for 100 sexual assault survivors in 10 targeted health facilities in Mozogo, Mora, Afade and Fotokol. In order to make sure that sexual assault survivors receive safe and appropriate clinical care, IRC will strengthen mechanisms for referral of survivors to the government health centers by providing trainings, updating service mapping, and distributing Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP) kits to health centers. The IRC will train 30 government health professionals on Clinical Care of Sexual Assault Survivors (CCSAS). Under the proposed project the IRC will continue to provide a set of activities aimed at provided quality care to survivors and to tackle the root cases of abuse through awareness raising and capacity strengthening of community protection mechanism. This set of activities will include: case management through Safe Space and mobile teams; community-based psychosocial assistance; promotion of community mobilization through community action plans & safety audits to reduce GBV risks and distribution of dignity kits; conducting Engaging Men through Accountable Practice (EMAP) groups; supporting adolescent girls through the IRC’s Girl Shine curriculum.project the IRC will continue to provide a similar package of protection services in the same 4 PICS to strengthen community capacity to address protection needs. In addition, the IRC will provide these services to beneficiaries in hard to reach areas, or who are less mobile, through its protection mobile team. The IRC’s proposed WASH programming aims to reinforce and increase sustainable access to safe water supply and sanitation facilities, including a shift away from emergency latrines and towards more durable family latrines. IRC will continue to construct 10 solid waste disposal pits and provide communal waste collection bins in consultation with IDP representatives to maintain cleanliness across the targeted IDPs sites (Kolofata, Kerawa and Igawa-Meme in Mayo Sava, and Kessawa and Sotrahy in Logone et Chari). The IRC plans to train 40 new CHWs for the new locations hosting IDPs in Mayo Sava and Logone et Chari that IRC will expand into. The IRC will also engage small groups in Educational Talks (ET) geared specifically towards small groups of men, women and teenagers to address their particular needs and concerns. The IRC WASH team will work through CHWs, community mobilizers and community leaders to mobilize and encourage community to participate in the project. The IRC will train beneficiaries on how to dig pits and at what dimension, and will provide toolkits (each containing: crowbar, shovel, bucket and pickaxe) to households to facilitate latrine construction. Given the fact that there are still unmet needs in villages hosting IDPs with non-functional water points and water access below SPHERE standards, the IRC will continue to rehabilitate another 20 new water points in Mayo Sava and Logone et Chari to improve access to water.
