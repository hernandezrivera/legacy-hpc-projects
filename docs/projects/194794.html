Project Summary

This is a continuation for the 2023 project putting spotlight on the arrangements to resume critical life-saving activities in Sudan, as and where security allows. When the resumption of operations becomes possible, WFP will engage in three key pillars of work:
A. Resume life-saving assistance to some or all of WFP’s pre-existing 4.9 million relief caseload. Targeted beneficiaries include food-insecure IDPs, refugees and resident population.
B. Meet new food security and nutrition needs, where feasible.
C. Provide critical common services to humanitarian partners (Emergency Telecommunications Cluster, Food Security Cluster, Logistics Cluster, UNHAS).
WFP resumed critical food assistance on 2 May 2023 to a proportion of the existing relief and nutrition caseload, where WFP has assured security, capacity, access, stocks and partners. The focus is:
Gradual provision of general food assistance (GFA) with the aim to reach 4.9 million food-insecure IDPs, refugees and resident population, over the next 2-3 months.
Given the highly volatile context, a gradual approach will be applied. As a first relief response, a two-month in-kind food ration covering May and June will be provided to refugees (100% ration) in White Nile, Kassala and Gedaref States.
There is a possibility that food commodities in warehouses may be looted if not distributed urgently. Accordingly, only in-kind food will be distributed in this preliminary response (no CBT at this stage with a particular concern on liquidity). While in-kind food relief is being prioritized for the initial response, CBT preparedness options are being concurrently developed. g and fosters gender equality.

1 - Needs

Before the current crisis, 15.8 million people were already food insecure across Sudan (57% female, according to CARE), and WFP was planning to reach, based on available resources, the most food insecure 11.7m IDPs, refuges and residents with food relief. However, according to food security information gathered just before the conflict, the number of acutely food insecure had already increased to 16.8 M (Comprehensive Food Security and Vulnerability Analysis - CFSVA March 2023). Since the conflict began (modelling based on the impact of previous shocks on food security in Sudan), WFP can conservatively estimate the number of acutely food insecure to increase to at least 19 M (or nearly 40 percent of the population) over the next 3-6 months. It can be anticipated that women and girls could comprise more than 50 percent of the food insecure. For the next 6 months of 2023 WFP implementation plan is targeting those severely food insecure following the recent conflict targeting 5.9 Million as per the attached to this project locations.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Under this project, food assistance package is provided to people affected by recent conflicts (displace and severely food insecure residents or host communities) to address their basic food security needs in the form of life-saving food assistance. Where WFP has assured security, capacity, access, stocks and partners. The focus is: • Gradual provision of unconditional general food assistance (GFA) in the form of in-kind Full ration for Refugees and half rations for IDPs and Residence with the aim to reach 4.9 million food-insecure IDPs, refugees and resident population, over the next 2-3/6 months. Given the highly volatile context, a gradual approach will be applied. As a first relief response, a two-month in-kind food ration covering May and June will be provided to pre-existing IDPs (50% ration) and refugees (100% ration) in White Nile, Kassala and Gedaref States. WFP is also exploring options to distribute food to IDPs in the Darfurs and will integrate this and other locations in the CONOPS depending on access. Households with children under 5 and/or PBW will be included in relief (independent of their nutritional status). There is a possibility that food commodities in warehouses may be looted if not distributed urgently. Accordingly, only in-kind food will be distributed in this preliminary response (no CBT at this stage with a particular concern on liquidity). While in-kind food relief is being prioritized for the initial response, CBT preparedness options are being concurrently developed.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The programme will involve unconditional food assistance to affected population in targeted locations in across Sudan. The unconditional food distribution will result in waste generation requiring a simplified waste management plan. The environmental significance is however very low, and the impacts can be mitigated/eliminated with the application of best waste management practices. And the partners implementing following WFP Environmental Policy and Guideline. In cooperation with other agencies, WFP contributed to the climate vulnerability assessment that examined climate vulnerability with a focus on the implications of vulnerability to climate change on the environment, energy and food security situation of targeted communities. The methodology for the assessment was based mainly on remote sensing techniques. The types of data and information used included historical records on precipitation, temperature, vegetation, land-use/land-cover (LULC), flood events and population. The idea is to use the long-term mean and coefficient of variation of the inter and intra-annual (e.g., monthly and annual) assigned climate parameters to detect the potential impact of climate risks and vulnerability. The assessment identifies the climate risks based on observed climatic trends and variability that have contributed to the vulnerability of the target areas. The analysis established that some states are prone to high rainfall variability. Vegetation productivity proxies showed a variable trend. Understanding of the vegetation dynamics is expected to help for better planning of livelihood activities to a wide array of beneficiaries in regions where the majority of the population depends on natural vegetation and agriculture to secure their livelihood. The general pattern of the LUL changes that took place is rapid increase in bare land and a reduction in natural vegetation. The increasing trend of bare land is expected to increase the risk of erosion of fertile soil from farmlands and might create hotspots of desertification. The concentration of the humanitarian population in the most populated parts of the states is expected to create high pressure on the natural resources and the surrounding environment. Based on the findings, a set of recommendations was made to be taken in project design. Under the current variable climate conditions careful management of WASH intervention is needed that take into account the amount and quality of water supply that might diminish due to droughts. Local population should play an active role in managing and maintaining water facilities.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

In alignment with the WFP Humanitarian Protection Policy, WFP remains committed to ensure that the assistance does no harm to the safety, dignity and integrity of the people it serves. In the design phase, WFP conducts in-depth consultations to key stakeholders for needs collection and inform beneficiaries and other relevant individuals of their entitlements (e.g. what to be served at what time for how long) to ensure that everyone is aware of the WFP’s assistance. In the implementation phase, regular visits to and monitoring at project sites will take place throughout the project period by dedicated staff including senior gender and protection associates who are based in Area Offices with support from four officers in gender and protection team based in Khartoum.
