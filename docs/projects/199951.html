Project Summary

GRI will improve access to quality, protective education for out-of-school children, quality of teaching and learning for formal schoolchildren to address acute learning and retention barriers for 4,600 conflict-affected children in northeast Nigeria. The project will mainstream protection and Safe School Declaration.
Through this project, GRI intends to work within selected locations in Borno state with the aim of building resilience of conflict-affected children through quality education. The project will expedite access to education for vulnerable children to enable them to obtain be enrolled and retained in schools.
In synergy with current efforts GRI will provide scholastic martials, teachers training, access to basic education to conflict-affected children.
GRI also aims at strengthening the capacity of education activities at the community level and ensuring the protection of children and adolescents.
The project location are Maiduguri, Jere, Monguno, Ngala, and Konduga In Borno state targeting 4,600 individuals

1 - Needs

The 2022 Nigeria Humanitarian Needs Overview targets 1.2 million out of 2.1 million people who need education support, the majority being children with adult teachers requiring training and professional development. According to the EiE Working group strategy 2021-1023, the number of children with chronic needs in education remains high across the BAY states and the crisis has put additional pressure on an education system that was already fragile. Estimates indicate that nearly 60% of primary school-age children are not attending school, with girls disproportionally affected. 41.8 % and 42.2% of primary school age children are not attending school in Adamawa and Yobe states respectively, compared to 27% at national level. Children and teachers in the BAY states have also a continuing need of psychosocial support to cope with the negative effects of the conflict. Poverty remains the greatest barrier to access education, with parents being unable to afford education fees and levies. The poor quality and lack of infrastructure such as classrooms, school furniture and WASH facilities, are also critical barriers. The insufficient number of qualified teachers and lack of teaching and learning materials have also contributed to poor attendance, retention and learning outcomes of learners in schools and learning centres. Dropout is particularly pronounced among adolescent girls, who are often expected to be married or carry out housework. Children with Disabilities (CwD) also experience low rates of access to education due to physical restrictions (school infrastructure being inadequate to facilitate learners with disabilities), untrained teachers who cannot effectively teach CwD, social stigmas, and high costs for specialized/accessible materials.

2 - Activities

The project will support access to quality education for 4,000 children in northeast Nigeria as well as supporting the development and strengthening of 200 teachers and 150 SBMC/CEC members. Key activities will include construction of safe and accessible semi-permanent learning centres or classrooms, in alignment with state government requirements, to support formal schools which do not have space to absorb newly enrolled out-of-school learners (or communities where no formal school exists). Learners will receive accelerated learning programmes in local language via GRI trained and supported Community Volunteer Teachers. The project end cycle will facilitate mainstreaming activities for both children and learners to fully re-enter the formal system. Government teachers and CVTs, together with School Based Management Committees (SBMC) or Community Education Committees (CEC) will be supported to form communities of practice and create spaces for regular review and reflection to identify best practices. GRI will also deliver targeted training sessions in protection, gender, and CwD. Schools and TLSs will also be supported to develop clear, comprehensive safeguarding mechanisms that facilitate childrens’ safety in school.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The project will benefit children from IDP, returnee and host communities, teachers, school based management committees and indirectly benefit the surrounding communities. Protection will be mainstreamed in all aspects of the project. GRI has developed a Safe and Inclusive Programming Action plan to ensure that programming promotes various aspects of safety and inclusion in all phases of the project cycle. Consultation and feedback mechanisms will be established to enable beneficiaries share their concerns or feedback related to the services provided. Conflict sensitive education principles will be applied during the implementation of the project so that all interventions minimize the risk of causing conflict among the beneficiaries. Education staff, Teachers and school based management committee will be trained on child protection, prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, and safe and inclusive programming so that all education interventions will promote safety and protection of all beneficiaries GRI’s existing child protection and safeguarding measures will guide the project; and all staff, teachers, consultants and any other persons engaged in the education programme will sign the code of conduct and child safe guarding policy as a commitment to protect children and other rights holders that GRI will be supporting during the period.
