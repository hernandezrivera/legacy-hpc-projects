Project Summary

This project will deliver lifesaving emergency health services including maternal, neonatal and child health through a fixed health facility targeting seven settlements in Balcad district Middle Shebelle namely Waberi star, Qalimow, Hawo tako, Ceel Gelow, Farax gololey, Horseed and Halgan with a total population of 87438 persons. The health facility will be equipped with essential medicines and equipment and trained staff to provide quality services. Emphasis will be laid on Maternal and Child Health, including Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BEmONC) and Expanded Programme on Immunization, EPI, to beneficiary communities. This action will focus on provision of both preventive and curative health services at primary level integrated with nutrition, WASH and protection components. Attention will be paid on application of treatment guidelines as per Essential package of Health Services, EPHS at the Primary healthcare, PHC. Building the capacity of health workers through training on integrated Management of childhood illness (IMCI) and infection control, Antenatal care,ANC and BEmONC will be done to equip health workers with pertinent skills to deliver quality health care services to the affected population. 7 staff, 1 for each of the targeted settlements, will be trained on emergency/cholera preparedness and response to equip them with skills and knowledge to be able to effectively respond to epidemic outbreaks. This project will deliver comprehensive reproductive health care to women of reproductive age among the target beneficiaries and promotion of health seeking behavior including adoption of preventive practices through campaigns, health and hygiene education sessions. Health and hygiene promotion will mostly be done by 12 trained CHWS, 2 from each of the targeted settlements. The project will also contribute to strengthening early warning disease surveillance through weekly submission of CSR reports from the health facility. Referral system for patients seeking secondary healthcare services will be facilitated to the recognized referral hospitals on pregnant women requiring more specialized emergency services, victims of armed conflicts, casualties from accidents, trauma cases among others. The project will also refer GBV victims to the wocca health facilities or any of the above mentioned facilities for comprehensive care and psycho-social support. This project will complement a Wocca run nutrition project, funded by SHF and UNICEF but now in final stages of being fully funded by UNICEF, Children with severe acute malnutrition will also be referred to nutrition centers for nutrition care and rehabilitation. Finally, Wocca will coordinate with Health, WASH, and Nutrition clusters to coordinate response activities for maximum impact to beneficiaries.

Needs

The health sector in Somalia is in critical situation with one of the worst health indicators in the world. The high risk groups are 2.4 million children under five and more than 3 million women of child bearing age. About 3.3 million girls, women, boys and men in Somalia need emergency health services. Secondary healthcare service provision remains dismal with a glaring gap in hospital based service provision. These include vital hospitalization services for mothers and children, treatment of the increasing number of patients suffering from chronic and non-communicable diseases, and inadequate referral capacity to hospitals, stabilization centers and laboratory facilities.The Shabelle regions, have insufficient number of medical facilities which compounded by the re-curring drought conditions has overstretched the already inadequate facilities, staff and services. The Balcad Healthcare sector has potential for scale-up at facility level as well as upgrading staff skills to handle existing and forecasted emergencies. Wocca with the approval of the ministry of health (MOH) of the Hirshabelle government will upgrade the facility hosting woccas Stabilization center, SC, in Balcad to MCH level offering a wide range of mother and child services and other health services to the host community. The facility will provide integrated Health services including EPI, Referral Services support, basic emergency obstetric care services, Provision of basic primary health care services in Balcad district of middle shabelle region with a view of addressing these health gaps. It will provide integrated lifesaving response by supporting a health clinic equipped with essential medicines and trained staff to provide emergency lifesaving health services including maternal, neonatal and child health

Activities

Activity 1: Equipping the supported facility in Balcad with essential drugs, vaccines, basic medical equipment to deliver lifesaving health services.Activity 2: Providing Curative consultations through the health facility supported through this project. Included in this activity will be the training of staff from the MCH on integrated management of childhood illnesses, IMCI, and infection control.Activity 3: Provision of Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BEmONC) in the supported health facility. Cases requiring Advanced/comprehensive emergency obstetric care will be referred to recognized referral hospital.Activity 4: Support the referral of obstetric emergency cases from the MCHs in Balcad to recognized referral hospitalsActivity 5: Provision of Immunization services (EPI) within primary health services integrated with nutrition services through Woccas nutrition sector. Children and pregnant women with malnutrition will be referred to nutrition centers for nutrition care and support.Activity 6: Provision of prenatal care services for pregnant women.Activity 7: Training of key field staff on emergency preparedness and response including on cholera preparedness and response planActivity 8: Receiving and Pre-positioning of emergency supplies for prompt response to epidemic outbreaks like AWD/CholeraActivity 9: Surveillance of epidemic diseases including, AWD/Cholera, measles through case registration and sharing reports with Health clusters, WASH cluster, sub-clusters and MoH for prompt response

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Wocca will promote equitable and impartial access to all; we will Consult both displaced and host communities about health needs and assess whether assistance could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the two communities and make decisions on how to mitigate possible conflicts. We will apply a systematic outreach process in registering beneficiaries to ensure the displaced as well as the mobile populations are not excluded. To further avoid community tensions between displaced and host communities we will provide the local/host community with access to services and assistance in situations where they are experiencing similar concerns.The project will also prevent risk of exclusion by promoting an accountability mechanism for effective protection of affected populations consisting of complaints and feedback mechanism.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Wocca will employ staff representative of all groups in the community by gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group and disability. Gender balance of the teams and implementation committees will be considered to ensure that there are enough women available as a contact point for women in the community. Clear information will be provided to the affected population explaining that they do not have to pay or provide favors to anyone in exchange for project service. Deliberate efforts will be made to ensure that this information is communicated to women and girls. While gender disparity in Somalia often exclude women from accessing information and making decision, the project will not only ensure health and hygiene promotion will include both men and women in the event, it will also intentionally include both male and female in the various decision making units so that they can have equal opportunities to take part in decision making on health issues affecting their community

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Somalia has been affected by conflict for a long period of time. Wocca as a local NGO is very conversant of the target areas and has more than 25 years of experience in the Middle Shabelle where the project is going to be implemented. Wocca is familiar with the administration and other actors in the area. our staff will follow the normal security arrangements and policies of the organization and minimize risks by sticking to these procedures. The majority of the staff that will implement this project are drawn from the area and are familiar with the target communities. They will lead the project and ensure project ownership of the communities to reduce security risk to this intervention. Furthermore, the community elders and local authorities are encouraged to participate in all steps of the implementation. No major safety risk is expected during the implementation of this project as WOCCA will engage with the local communities and encourage project ownershipAccess is generally unlimited in the Middle Shabelle specially Jowhar and Balcad. The Hirshebelle state government is based in Jowhar and Balcad is the gateway to Middle Shabelle and adjacent to Mogadishu. Both areas are controlled by the Somali government. women and child care organization has already several projects running in Middle Shabelle were access is granted by the local authorities. No expectation of limited access in the project area. WOCCA on the other hand has been working in Middle Shabelle for the last 25 years and have had always access in Jowhar and Balcad developing a close relationship with the community. The project staff are members of the local community and threats to their lives are very minimal. Wocca assumes that the security situation in the area remains stable or improves so that the project can be executed according to plan.
