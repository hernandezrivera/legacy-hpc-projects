Project Summary

Somalia has been impacted by multiple shocks in 2020, including desert locust invasion, flash floods and COVID-19. This has negatively affected the ability of vulnerable households to meet their food security needs. The urban poor, including IDP households, have been disproportionally affected due to the socio-economic effects from COVID-19 due their heavy reliance on food purchases from the market and casual labor as the main sources of income. These households are likely to face food consumption gaps due to declines in employment and income-earning opportunities. The desert locust caused significant damage to cereal crops, fruits, vegetables and pasture in the northern and central regions. Control efforts of the government and partners in Somaliland and Puntland made progress, but an increasing number of adult locust groups was reported in central regions. In addition, in the northeast, remained swarms have matured, and some have moved towards central areas. As per FAO, the hatching and band formation is occurring now and more swarms are likely to move to other areas in central Somalia and at later stages are expected to move towards crop producing areas in the south; which can have some negative impact to the crops and pasture up to early next year.
The impact of localized flooding particularly in riverine areas destroyed farmland and crops and caused population displacement, leading to significant crop losses and the loss of income from agricultural employment.
Jointly with the Government and other humanitarian actors, WFP Somalia has supported communities and vulnerable houses impacted by the multiple shocks. The high levels of sustained humanitarian assistance in 2020 have played a critical role in stabilizing the situation. The immediate food security outlook for Somalia however looks bleak despite the efforts sustained in 2020. According to the latest Post-Gu Assessment, 2.1 million people are expected to be food insecure (IPC 3 and 4) across Somalia through December 2020. An additional 3 million people are expected to be Stressed (IPC 2), bringing the total number of people facing cute food insecurity to 5.1 million until the end of this year. Based on results of the 2020 Post Gu assessment, an estimated 849,900 children under the age of five years face acute malnutrition between September 2020 and August 2021, including 143,400 likely to be severely malnourished.

1. - Projects activities

WFP plans to reach 2.25 million people with relief and conditional assistance to support its lifesaving and life changing food assistance as Somalia is currently in the grips of severe hunger crisis driven by prolonged drought period. The relief component is planned to address short term hunger and meet food consumption gaps of most vulnerable households with in-kind or cash unconditional transfers. While with in-kind or cash conditional transfers, WFP will support the rehabilitation of productive assets (water catchments, canal rehabilitation, farmers training and skills training activities aimed at promoting recovery and resilience capacities of communities affected by natural and manmade calamities, based on seasonality. A projected 84% ( 1,900,000) of the planned 2.25 million people will be supported through unconditional relief interventions, while 16% ( 350,000) will be supported with conditional transfers .1. Locations will be selected through food security and nutrition needs assessments and targeted at the household level using vulnerability criteria that reflects the needs of men, women, boys and girls. At geographical level - targeting will be informed by critical situation analysis of multiple factors include the effect of climate change, causes of conflict, socio-economic conditions, gender roles and protection matter. While at household level it will drive from community-based targeting process in which the recipient population will be involved in the definition of eligibility criteria with attention to monitories, specially v 2. Biometric registration of beneficiaries will be conducted through SCOPE, and socio-demographic profiling and assistance will be monitored through the SCOPE platform. 3. The unconditional relief assistance will target most vulnerable communities identified through the seasonal assessments and aims to address food consumption gap of poor households through in kind or cash unconditional transfers ( max target: 1,900,000 beneficiaries from January to June and 1,720,800 from July to December 2022). 4. Rehabilitation of productive assets and skills training that will enhance resilience through food/cash transfers based on seasonality (target 350,000 beneficiaries) 5. Monitoring progress of implementation of unconditional and unconditional activities, provision /distribution of food commodities/cash/vouchers to targeted beneficiaries (target 30% of Food Distribution Points monitored monthly) 6. Calls to beneficiaries for feedback on targeting, entitlement and to understand if they face any challenges to receive entitlements (cash or food). Beneficiaries receive messages on programmes and entitlements as well as information on the hotlines and Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms (CFMs). 7. Monthly reporting to the food security cluster 8. Development of early warning bulletins, participation in regular food security assessments, regular participation in FSC meetings. 9. WFP will create an internal pathway under different programme activities relief, nutrition, livelihood and safety net fostering complementarity and mutual reinforcement of efforts. 2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

WFP will use both modalities; i.e in-kind and cash-based transfers depending on the most appropriate modality based on context. WFP selects transfer modalities by analyzing food security and nutrition needs, assessing supply chain capacity, and comparing the efficiency and cost-effectiveness of modalities across various population groups, activities and locations. Decisions will reflect nutritional needs and the capacity of financial service providers such as mobile money service providers, traders and retailers in the targeted geographic locations WFP is working in. Due to COVID, WFP has suspended cash through the bank to avoid the risk of spreading the virus, opting for a contactless means of transfers. In areas with functional markets WFP will prioritise cash transfer programming. In areas without functioning markets, WFP will provide beneficiaries with in-kind food assistance. Besides market functionality assessments and cost efficiency analysis to decide on whether or not to implement CTP, WFP also considers protection concerns such as security, distance to the markets, and the beneficiary preference.. Over the years, WFP has conducted supply chain, financial, IT capacity and security assessments in most parts of Somalia in order to determine the feasibility of CTP. WFP started implementing CTP is Somalia in 2014, and over the years, has established contracts with over 1,000 retailers in Somalia, with some of them also offering mobile retailing to some communities that face challenges of accessibility to markets .These retailers offer services to WFP e-voucher beneficiaries, who receive a cash voucher that is redeemable in any of these retailers. The e-vouchers are restricted to food commodities, and the beneficiaries can choose to purchase from a wide choice of food commodities provided by these retailers. Besides this, WFP also provides multipurpose cash, that provides choice to the most vulnerable people, who can choose how to prioritise their multiple needs. This is done through mobile money, which is now the most preferred mechanism due to the COVID 19 challenge, as it allows for contactless assistance to beneficiaries, hence reducing risk of transmission. WFP has established a contract with 3 telecom companies for the provision of mobile money. Generally, multi-purpose cash is a more dignified and effective alternative to traditional in-kind humanitarian assistance as it provides the beneficiaries to have a choice on how to use their cash assistance according to their personal needs, ranging from health, education and food needs. WFP’s monitoring on its cash voucher programmes, where beneficiaries are restricted to buying food commodities only, have also generally shown that despite this restriction, beneficiaries still have a choice to buy different food commodities, and this improves their dietary diversity. The voucher programmes also ensure that the food security status of the Households improve, as all the transfer is used for food security needs, and not diverted to other things. Cash transfer programming in general therefore provides a choice to beneficiaries, therefore empowering them to make decisions on how to use their assistance, whilst at the same time also contributing positively to the local market capacity by providing opportunities to retailers in the country.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

WFP conducts regular market assessments in the target areas to check the capacity of the potential service providers to provide a service to the beneficiaries. These assessments gather the necessary data for WFP to make an informed decision about the most appropriate transfer modality for the specific contexts/locations, and identify the risks associated with the use of CTP for each sector (market, supply chain, finance, IT, etc.) The assessments also consider distances, and security issues, as well as possible mitigation measures beforehand. The assessment evaluates the functionality and trends of food and labor markets, including their regional integration, supply chains, capacity to respond to an increase in demand, the availability and quality of the food accessible at reasonable and stable prices, and the opportunities for beneficiaries to access them safely. This phase supports WFP’s decision to intervene in any given context to address identified needs and determine the suitability of a market-based response option. The aspects to consider as part of the market analysis include: a. Analyze the current and historic availability of both staple and fresh food commodities in local markets, including potential recent changes and patterns of seasonality.b. Analyze the overall market environment in which food commodity trade takes place, including recurrent- socio-political situation, security, road and transport and infrastructure. c. Demand conditions of affected populations: their physical and economic access to local markets (including inflation patterns of food and non-food commodities, households’ purchasing power, livelihood and market participation behaviors, self-sufficiency and resilience statuses, and preferences.The objective of the procurement options analysis is to understand the implications and opportunities of sourcing decisions on the efficiency and effectiveness of the transfer modality options in achieving the objectives of the intervention. In compliance with Joint its directive and as a preparedness measure, based on the information collected through the Micro Financial Sector Assessment, Procurement creates a roster for FSPs that qualify for WFP interventions, according to roster specifications provided by Finance in collaboration with Programme. The MAITA also informs: the consolidation of risks into the country office Risk Register carried out by the sectoral assessment coordinated by programme, logistics, procurement, security, and IT.
