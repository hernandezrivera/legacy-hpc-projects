Project Summary

In line with the South Sudan HRP and cluster objectives 1 and 2, the project will save lives by addressing the acute severe food insecurity of 57,000 (6840 Men, 14820 Women, 17100 Boys and 18240 Girls) conflict and natural disaster affected people in IPC4+ in Jonglei (Pigi/Canal) and Unity State (Rubkona) through distribution of food vouchers to 3,000 HH and enhanced and sustained food production through distribution of seeds, crop and fishing kits, training and cash for work to 9,500 HH. In support of nutrition centers, kitchen gardens will be established to improve the nutritional status of people. This will allow severe and acute food insecure people in IPC4+ to meet their immediate food needs whilst allowing them to rebuild their assets. In addition, by improving people's food security, acute malnutrition will be reduced. All the selected locations are affected by different natural and man-made disaster, showing high and multiple vulnerabilities and are priority areas as they are in IPC4 or worse, confirmed by FSNMS. Assessments also showed that women (incl. PLW), children (incl. children under 5), PWD, elderly and chronically ill are impacted differently and are bearing the brunt of conflict droughts/floods and covid-19. The project will therefore target these most vulnerable groups through inclusive assistance, and will ensure that protection, gender and COVID-19 is mainstreamed in the proposed activities. Protection will be mainstreamed throughout the project cycle to ensure beneficiary safety & dignity, avoiding doing harm as result of the intervention, providing meaningful access to all sets of beneficiaries and ensuring all groups are actively involved and participate in the project in an inclusive manner. Additionally, the project will ensure all necessary Covid-19 measures and protocols will be adhered to; for example, during distributions, beneficiaries will be encouraged to maintain safe social distance, always wash their hands and wear face masks.
Cordaid has been responding to the increasing humanitarian needs in the 2 areas providing WASH, FSL and Health assistance and is fully operational in the 2 areas with offices in Rubkona and Pigi.

1 - Needs

An estimated 8.2 million people in South Sudan need humanitarian assistance, 7.8mln- two thirds of the population in 2023 during the lean season (April – July) are expected to face severe food insecurity (IPC analysis, 2022/2023). Pigi/Canal and Rubkona are among the counties of extreme concern for food insecurity during the lean season, with 45% of the population of Pigi projected to be in IPC4 and 5% in IPC5 while 35% in Rubkona are projected to be in IPC4 with non in IPC5 (FSNMS,2022). According to the stratified data analysis of the FSNMS, it is more likely for displaced persons to be severely food insecure with 61.1% of the IDPs been food insecure with 61.2% particularly significant in unity state (Rubkona) whereas in Jonglei state, the residents (Pigi) tend to be more food insecure. FSNMS results also show that age of head of households also have strong link to food security with HH headed adults aged between 18 to 60 years more likely to marginally to be food secure compared to the young and the elderly headed households. Gender plays a significant role in the security status of households with women headed households slightly more food insecure across South Sudan. The food insecurity in Pigi and Rubkona has been as result of protracted conflict and flooding which has limited the flow of both commercial and humanitarian goods and services affecting access to markets. Sub-national violence continued to be experienced in both locations in 2022 and is expected to continue until an inclusive peace process and governance is integrated. Flooding in its fourth year has caused displacement, damaged homes and has affected agricultural livelihoods and household’s ability to produce their own food. This has been compounded by the effect of water logging and water borne diseases on livestock and human health. Persistent high food prices have also contributed to the heightened food insecurity and poor nutritional status in the locations. Despite the key drivers of food insecurity in Pigi and Rubkona as highlighted, other factors have also contributed to these dimensions of food insecurity in the two counties. Malnutrition has significantly affected mental and physical growth, educational attainment, and later livelihood opportunities of the people; poor access to health services has affected the ability of the people to carry out manual livelihood tasks and to cover the long distances necessary to herd livestock or collect wild foods and firewood; Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) have influences on food preparation and safety as well utilization when contaminated water is consumed. Protection concerns such as conflict, displacement, and gender-based violence disrupts people's livelihoods and mental well-being impacting food security. Poverty and vulnerability, due to the protracted nature of the crisis in South Sudan have resulted in significantly greater levels of food insecurity and lack of livelihood opportunities, forcing many families to resort to negative coping strategies, (some of which are harmful to children). Strategies include, sale of productive assets, early/ forced marriage and forms of child labour. The analysis of the correlation between food security and the education level of the household’s head reveals that investing in education is key to future economic well-being, income and livelihoods as the higher the education the more likely the household is to be food secure. Households whose heads have no, or low education levels tend to be more severely food insecure. 120,000 Men, Women, Boys and Girls in Rubkona and 53000 Men, Women, Boys and Girls in Pigi will be severely affected. Women and children mainly bear the brunt of the crises and are mostly affected. Pigi and Rubkona have high number of IDPs with 64% and 56% respectively as newly displaced. Cordaid will provide humanitarian life-saving assistance and capacity building to 57,000 vulnerable people in Pigi and Rubkona in line with FSL cluster objectives.

2 - Project activities

In line with the cluster strategy the project will save lives by providing emergency food assistance (cluster objective 1) which is complemented by livelihood support (cluster objective 2) contributing directly towards the first strategic objective of the HRP. Protection, gender and COVID-19 will be mainstreamed in the activities: Cordaid will implement the following;1. Provision of in-kind food and Cash & Voucher assistance in Canal/Pigi and Rubkona respectively to 3,000 HHs. Cordaid will distribute unconditional/restricted cash or in-kind food to vulnerable HH in areas in Rubkona and Pigi/Canal respectively. The approach may vary depending on context and market structure/functionality. In Pigi, markets will not cope with an increase in demand and thus in-kind food will be distributed. Cordaid will select and contract local vendors through a tendering system who will procure and distribute food to selected HHs. In Rubkona, Cordaid will distribute cash with food security and nutrition objective, as the market will be able to meet demands. Beneficiaries will receive 100USD per HH per month 3 months period. However actual transfer value will be determined based on CWG guidelines and prevailing market prices. Training of the beneficiaries on food utilization, including hygiene promotion, use of safe water, appropriate feeding practices, efficient and renewable energy sources, and technology for adequate processing, cooking and conservation of food will be included. Selection criteria are based on FSL cluster. Within 4 weeks after distribution a PDM will be conducted. 2. Distribution of emergency livelihood inputs (Vegetable, crop seeds and fishing kits) and training for 9,500 HHs (57,000 Men, women, children and the elderly) in IPC4+ Emergency livelihood input will be distributed including distribution of fishing and crop kits. Fishing kits: Potential fisher folks will be identified and supported with fishing kits and training on fish preservation. Fishing kits will be distributed, each kit consisting of twine (2 spools), monofilament (1 coil), and hooks (2 boxes) following FAO guidelines. Distribution of vegetable & crop seeds and tool: In close coordination with the state ministry of agriculture, we will conduct a seed needs assessment for seed viability. A Seed germination test will be carried out to determine the viability of the seed before it reaches in the hands of the farmers, to ensure high quality. Following FAO guidelines, each seed kit will include one tool (hoe/maloda), and a variety of crop seeds and vegetable seeds: Crop Kits (10-13 kg) existing of Maize (5kg), Sorghum (5kg) and Cowpea (3kg) Vegetable Kits (7 to 9 varieties including Amarant (20G) , Onion(20G) , Tomato (20G), Eggplant (20G) , Collard (20G) , Pumpkin (30G), Watermelon (30G), Kudra (20G), Carrot (20G) and Okra (50G). Group formation will be facilitated through awareness and guidance from Cordaid and provide training on vegetable growing/production and agronomic best practices for harvesting and marketing. This activity will also include key nutrition messages to improve the food security situation and the nutrition status of beneficiaries. One month after distribution, a PDM will be conducted to obtain feedback from beneficiaries on the distributed items and the trainings conducted. 3. Cash for work activities and distribution of conditional unrestricted cash. Cash for work Cordaid will target 700 households mostly areas much affected by the flooding to support and repair their community assets. In agreement with the locally community roads, market drainage systems, dykes and bridges will be repaired. Cordaid will provide tools and following CVWG and FSL guidelines, number of working days and day rate will be used. A PDM will be conducted at the end. 4. Establish kitchen gardens in nutrition centers in Rubkona and Pigi. 8 kitchen gardens will be set up at nutrition facilities to facilitate food and nutrition education to SAM children/caretakers.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

In-kind food: Cordaid is proposing to provide targeted in-kind food to complement WFP’s food distribution efforts. Local vendors will be selected through tendering (incl but not limited to visits and quality checks). These vendors will procure and supply the food to selected beneficiaries. This way market functionality will not be jeopardized, and markets will not suffer from the food distribution and will benefit from the assistance as well. Cash for food: In Rubkona, the market will cope with increased demand and as such, people will be provided with the same amount in cash. Beneficiaries will be encouraged to use the money for food. Overall, local traders will benefit from the cash assistance. Cash for work: the amounts for cash for work will be small and are complementary to the food assistance. Based upon markets assessments, markets in the proposed areas will be able to cope with small cash injections that are unrestricted and can be used based on people’s needs. Markets in Pigi and Rubkona will be able to cope with this

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

In addition to the HH assessments, Cordaid also conducted market assessments to measure market functionality, prices and feasibility to meet increased demand. Besides the JMMI and Reach assessment reports. The market assessments show that although markets are moderately functional, they are not able to cope with large or increase in demands in Pigi/Canal. This is why Cordaid is proposing to use food vouchers in combination with local vendors who procure and distribute the food. In Rubkona, the market will be able to cope with increased demands in food and as such, Cordaid will distribute cash with food security objectives.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism will be provided to beneficiaries through Project inception meeting where information about the project scope, Cordaid and expected contribution by the community is explained; Focus group discussion with different groups, community meetings with community leaders and community representatives, Post distribution monitoring; communication materials like banners/posters which is translated in local language and through one-on-one interviews.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The Cordaid has PSEA policy in place and will use her PSEA policy throughout the project. PSEA is based on communities preferred ways of communications, assessed by Cordaid. In line with Cordaid's PSEA policy, communities will be informed verbally and in writing concerning expected behaviour of Cordaid staff. Information will be provided during community meetings, project inception meeting through the project management committee, and during training of community based groups like farmers group etc. For this a specific plan is drawn up – Specific communication plan on PSEA: 1). Stakeholders – by name if known. (In the department, Organisation, partner organizations, wider community, service users?) 2). Purpose of communication 3) Key messages to be communicated (To do this, what do they need to know? Which questions should be answered?) 4) Timing of communication (Stages of project, specific times, frequency) 5). How are you going to communicate? (eg routine report, flyer, newsletter, poster, case study, social media, video, blog). 6) Who is responsible? (Who will do the communicating? What do they need to know?) Through the communication plan: - Communities understand their rights, the role of Cordaid, expected behaviour of Cordaid staff and how to make complaints - Ensure appropriate investigations are made and actions are taken and closing the loop with the complainant.- Cordaid staff, community and other stakeholders understand the Cordaid core principles on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse - Community engagement efforts complement and support protection strategies.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

All feedback, collected regularly throughout the project during FGDs, PDM, HH interviews, individual meeting and KIIs will be logged and analysed by Cordaid MEL/project team. Analysis will be shared with the Programme staff and community and discussions held to what extend there is a need, and how the assistance needs to be adapted. Any adaptations are done in consultation of Cordaid and community. Information collected after the distribution through the PDM is used to inform and improve future activities. In the project, the beneficiaries' feedback will be used to adapt project assistance through;Reflection meetings; Focus group discussions, Project management committee meetings.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

As part of Cordaid procedures, at proposal stage a project risk matrix will be completed identifying operational, political, programmatic, reputational, financial and organizational risks. At the start and during the implementation the risk matrix will be reviewed, and risks closely monitored. The below identified risks are part of Cordaid programmatic risks: 8.1 Risk of beneficiary’s exposure to violence during registration/ distribution: Likelihood is medium – Impact is high. Mitigation measures: Cordaid will ensure that all registrations and distribution sites are safe, nearby people’s dwellings and easy accessible for all. People will be hired for crowd control and when desirable, separate male and female distribution sites will be organised. Sites will be selection jointly with the community. In addition, Cordaid is using the PMWG checklist for safe distribution to identify potential risks. 8.2 Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence: Likelihood is low – Impact is high. Mitigation measures: Cordaid will inform communities about policies and procedures in place such as code of conduct and PSEA. All Cordaid staff are trained in these procedures and policies. This will explain expected behaviour of Cordaid staff and how to report misconduct. To prevent sexual violence by community members distribution sites will be safe and nearby people’s dwellings. Distribution times will also be convenient to make sure that people are home during day time. Community leaders and communities will be asked to protect their women, girls and boys as well. 8.3 Risk of insufficient internal mechanism to combat and prevent child safeguarding: Likelihood is low – Impact is high. Mitigation measures: Cordaid as part of the Caritas network has child safeguarding policies in place and staff are trained on these procedures. In addition, in the event children are on their own, Cordaid will make sure they are connected with local leaders and make sure communities look after them. 8.4 Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral service: Likelihood is medium – Impact is high. Mitigation measures: Cordaid will map other and protection actors in the area and establish a referral with these organisations. This information will also be shared with communities and community leaders, raising awareness on available services and referrals. 8.5 Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and Older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project: Likelihood is low – Impact is high. Mitigation measures: As part of the assessments, Cordaid organized FGDs about and with people with specific needs but also different age groups, to ensure that their needs were raised and incorporated in the assistance. Cordaid will continue to do this during the project preparations, implementation and also PDM 8.6 Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms: Likelihood is low – Impact is medium. Mitigation measures: Through the project starting at the first community visits and consultations until the PDM, Cordaid will share its complaints and feedback mechanism and how to access it with communities. Information on the CRM and its importance will be shared verbally and in writing and is based on communities’ preferred ways of communication (informed by assessments). 8.7 Risk of unsafe and unethical management of data, especially disclosure of sensitive personal data on beneficiaries: Likelihood is low – Impact is high. 8.8 Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict t over access to resources: Likelihood is medium – Impact is high. 8.9 Risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity: Likelihood is low – Impact is medium. 8.10 Risk of explosive hazard: Likelihood is low, impact high
