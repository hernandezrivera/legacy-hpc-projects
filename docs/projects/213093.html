Project Summary

The 2024 Humanitarian Needs Report indicates a critical demand for primary and secondary health assistance in Syria, with 14.9 million people in need. This requirement, slightly lessened by approximately 400,000 from the prior year, highlights the challenges posed by disease outbreaks and the need for a proactive health service approach amidst the deteriorating socio-economic conditions. In Jableh, a city within the Latakia governorate, these health challenges are compounded by the aftermath of the February earthquake, socio-economic decline, and the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic. A notable cholera outbreak in September 2022 further escalated health emergencies, leading to over 217,000 suspected cases by October 2023.
The affected population includes 5.1 million internally displaced persons, 2.3 million children under five, and 4.1 million women of reproductive age, alongside the elderly and those with disabilities. Psychological distress, exacerbated by emergencies and ongoing crises, affects approximately one in ten Syrians, with a similar proportion of children requiring focused mental health care.
To address these issues, the project will focus on delivering integrated primary and secondary health services to the targeted populations in Jableh. This includes enhancing access to emergency care, maternal and reproductive health, child health services, mental health support, and specialized care for vulnerable groups. Ensuring a consistent supply of essential medicines, medical equipment, and functional devices is also a priority, aiming to mitigate the health impacts of the current crises on the city's residents
Activities :
- strengthen the delivery of primary health care services by three clinics: pediatric clinic, gynecology clinic, internal clinic in addition to a pharmacy.
- Secondary health services, including surgeries:
Signing a memorandum of understanding with private hospitals in jableh regarding the prices of operations and the transfer mechanism and Sign and seal the hospital on the medical report stating the value of the operation after the signature of the specialist doctor.
The project supports: Caesarean sections, natural births, general surgery, gynecological surgery, eye surgery, orthopedic surgery and all types of surgical operations, including magnetic resonance imaging. The development of the dish is pivotal.
provide MHPSS services to people suffering from psychological distress and encourage meaningful living despite adversity, and those who were subjective to direct violence especially women who have experienced partner violence using guidelines and tools and other evidence-based protocols
