Project Summary

Strengthening WASH and health services of Healthcare Facilities (HCFs) and underserved communities, are considered extremely urgent needs in Gaza Strip (GS) according to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2022. Moreover, investing in adequate WASH facilities supports resilience building and strengthens communities’ ability to face protracted threats and chronic vulnerabilities. This project is aligned with HRP 2023 Strategic Objectives 1, 2 and 3 and complements WASH Cluster Objectives 1, 2 and 3, and Health Cluster Objective 2, contributing to SDG6. Improved WASH services in HCFs are essential for achieving all global and national health goals, especially SDG3 (Universal Health Coverage), and specific targets 3.1, 3.2 (reducing maternal and neonatal mortality) and 3.9 (reducing disease burden from unsafe WASH). Moreover, effective WASH and infection prevention and control (IPC) measures are one of the five objectives of the World Health Organization (WHO) Global Action Plan on antimicrobial resistance. The proposed intervention complies with HRP 2023 eligible activities, targeting the most vulnerable communities and HCFs according to the WASH 2022 vulnerability mappings (WASH Cluster). Also, the project complements UNICEF’s Strategy for Health 2016-2030. Finally, the project complies with the Palestinian Water Authority (PWA) Strategic Plan (2017-22) and the Ministry of Health’s (MoH) one (2021-25), and the soon-to-be-published National WASH standards for HCFs (UNICEF/WW-GVC 2022). WeWorld-GVC (WW-GVC), in partnership with Qatar Red Crescent Society (QRCS), aims to enhance the resilience, protection, and dignity of the most vulnerable people in GS. The needs and interventions were coordinated with and endorsed by the MoH, WASH and Health Cluster, Coastal Municipalities Water Utility (CMWU), United Nations Relief and Works Agency (UNRWA) and PWA. The general objective of this proposal is to increase the availability and accessibility of WASH and health services in HCFs and communities including flood mitigation and prevention, contributing to mitigating the impact of COVID-19 and other infectious diseases by (A) improving the access to WASH and Health quality services for Al Nasser medical compound (Al Nasser Pediatric Hospital, Rantissi Specialized Hospital and Ophthalmic Hospital) in Gaza City by implementing sustainable improvement of WASH infrastructure through construction/rehabilitation of handwashing stations, gender-sensitive latrines and sewer system and improving the quality of health services through provision of hygiene material, medical and protective equipment supply, monitoring tools), benefitting annually an average 280,000 people served by the 3 hospitals (data: Health Cluster HeRAMS Dashboard); (B) ensuring access to adequate WASH services at community level in Deir Al Balah, through the installation/rehabilitation of water transmission pipelines and HH connections, and the installation/rehabilitation of sewer collection networks and HH connections, serving 1,535 vulnerable individuals of two unserved communities, Al’Ansar (Al Musaddarc locality) and Block 8 (Al Buriej locality); and in Khan Younis Governorate through the rehabilitation of four wells, increasing the availability of domestic water supply for 13 communities (168,150 beneficiaries); (C) promoting flood mitigation and prevention, through the construction/rehabilitation of stormwater networks and drainage systems in the community of An Nuseirat camp (5,000 beneficiaries) in Deir Al Balah and (D) advocating for the right of the most vulnerable Palestinians to receive adequate access to WASH services through the development of 5 awareness products on access restrictions to WASH, estimating to reach 400,000 beneficiaries. WW-GVC and QRCS will ensure that all proposed interventions trigger positive effects on reducing WASH vulnerabilities and increasing the resilience of communities living under protracted threats in Gaza Strip.

1 - Needs

Israel’s longstanding blockade, recurring escalations in hostilities, and the COVID-19 pandemic have weakened the already fragile healthcare and WASH services system in GS triggering a humanitarian need for WASH and health intervention in this area. According to the latest WASH Vulnerability Mapping of the WASH Cluster, 26 HCFs in GS suffer from inadequate WASH facilities. Consequences are overcrowding of existing facilities and undermining of hygiene practices raising concerns for disease outbreaks including COVID-19. Also in September 2022, WW-GVC and QRCS conducted key informant interviews (KIIs) with MoH personnel identifying and updating the WASH needs and Al Nasser medical compound was evaluated as one the most WASH vulnerable HCFs. This compound comprises three hospitals: Al Nasser Pediatric Hospital (NPH), Rantissi specialized hospital (RSH) and Ophthalmic hospital (OH). NPH and RSH are the main pediatric clinics of Gaza Governorate providing general and specialized services and are referral hospitals for North Gaza and the Middle area, serving an average of 108,000 patients annually including a Neonatal Intensive Care Unit (NICU), a Pediatric ICU, hemodialysis and the only pediatric oncology center in GS. The OH, a specialized hospital, serves an average of 172,000 cases annually (mainly chronic patients with non-communicable diseases). Adequate WASH services in these hospitals are fundamental to reducing preventable causes of morbidity and mortality by minimizing risks of healthcare-associated infections and improving staff satisfaction and patient care-seeking behavior. Overall, the priority needs of Al Nasser medical compound, considering the special needs of staff, patients, women, elderly, children, and PWDs are: (1) unsafe drinking and domestic water, due to poor quality, corroded old infrastructure, and damaged desalination plants, besides the lack of water quality monitoring. Moreover, the lack of water connections to the firefighting system in RSH jeopardizes the safety of staff and patients; (2) lack of separated and sufficient toilets for staff and patients, respecting PWD, gender and children sensitivity; (3) old sewer networks in NPH and OH causing frequent flooding and no proper drainage systems in the NPH yard; (4) poor hygiene and environmental cleaning infrastructure in OH and NPH with a lack of designated waiting areas; (5) lack of safe designated areas for medical waste collection and storage. Concerning access to WASH services at HH/community level in GS, major difficulties persist in upgrading and maintaining WASH facilities due to limited financial capacity and Israeli restrictions on importing materials (HNO 2022). Though the WASH Cluster reported no damage to WASH infrastructure during the latest escalation, the access restrictions and power shortages have severely affected WASH services provision. Additionally, 290 WASH infrastructures affected during the May 2021 escalation are not fully recovered and service providers are struggling to restore and maintain the optimal functioning of WASH facilities. Disregarding these escalations, more than 1,1 million Palestinians in Gaza, are not receiving adequate WASH services and more than 640,000 people are in severe WASH needs (HNO 2022) lacking access to water, access to sanitation and hygiene, and flood mitigation and prevention measures. Regarding access to water, the daily water consumption in 2021 was 89.2 l/c which is below the 100 l/c recommended by the WHO and more than 78,2 % of the water supplied does not meet WHO quality standards according to PWA 2021. The MSNA 2022 indicates that 82,6% of HHs in GS are reliant on unimproved sources of drinking water. Concerning access to sanitation services, about 31,000 HHs are not connected to the sewer network and the sewage of 9,335 HHs ultimately ends up in open areas, creating health and environmental hazards to downstream communities (WASH Cluster Vulnerability Mapping 2022). Firstly, in coordination with CMWU, UNRWA and PWA and based on this Vulnerability mapping 2022, WW-GVC identified two under-served communities in Deir al Balah governorate, Al’Ansar and Block 8, that have no access to sustainable piped water and sanitation services, being disconnected from water supply and sewage networks affecting 322 HHs, with especially a negative impact on children and women’s health. Consequently, these communities’ priority needs identified are (1) lack of sufficient water quantities for drinking and domestic use; (2) exposure to unprotected and unhealthy environment (waterborne diseases); (3) augmented risks due to poor personal hygiene and scarce environmental cleaning (4) higher risk of displacement; (5) delays and difficulties of movement especially for children due to wastewater flooding (6) increased burden for women in charge of fetching water. Secondly, as a result of the internal assessment and coordination with PWA and CMWU, it was identified as a prioritized need to ensure access to water for drinking and domestic use by increasing the water supplied in the localities of qizan 'abu rashwan and albatn Alsamin and 11 surrounding communities, supporting the wider and urgent action of improving access to water in Khan Younis Governorate. Finally, considering the lack of technical and financial capacities of service providers in GS, climatic and population trends and GS’s geography, improving flood mitigation and prevention measures is an urgent humanitarian need (HNO 2022). According to the 2022 Flooding Vulnerability mapping, more than 400,000 Gazans are severely vulnerable to flooding. WW-GVC in close coordination with PWA and CMWU, identified as a priority stormwater management intervention in the locality of An Nuseirat (Middle Area Governorate).

2 - Activities

Proper coordination was performed with PWA, MoH, CMWU, UNRWA, Local Authorities, the WASH and Health Clusters, and major stakeholders active in the WASH and Health sector in GS on the target communities to avoid overlapping. At the project’s start, WW-GVC and QRCS will carry out a detailed needs assessment, to be conducted in cooperation with MoH and CMWU to prioritize and confirm needs and specifications. The proposed intervention will be delivered through service delivery modality (100%) through following activities:Activity 1: Improving access to WASH and Health services in institutions.WW-GVC will be responsible for the implementation of Activity 1.1 and QRCS for the interventions addressing Health gaps of Al Nasser medical compound (Activity 1.2, 1.3,1.4).Activity 1.1-Construction/ rehabilitation of WASH facilities in 3 HCFs (water supply system, handwashing stations, latrines, sewer system)A1.1.1-Upgrading water systems services in NPH, OH, and RSH, including rehabilitation of 3 water wells (installation of super duplex vertical pumps); rehabilitation of main water networks with internal separation; installation of 1 new desalination plant with 60 m3/day capacity in NPH, considering international and national standards for brine management. Upgrading the existing hot water systems and reinstallation of water connections for the central firefighting system in RSH, and rehabilitation of the firewater pump and groundwater tank, and rehabilitation of the stormwater drainage system inside yards through slopes' readjustment and installation of galleys. A.1.1.2-Rehabilitation of sanitation services in 3 HCFs, including maintenance of 20 already existing toilets considering the needs of staff and patients, men and women (gender), caretakers and special needs of PWDs and children, decreasing GBV risk and ensuring safety and dignity for all.A1.1.3-Rehabilitation/construction of 30 new handwashing basins and/or alcohol-based hand rub wall-mounted dispensers. Activity 1.2-The provision of medical waste management and infection control supplies and equipment at the HCFs. A1.2.1-Construction of 3 safe medical waste collection and storage areas, 1 for each hospital.A1.2.2-The provision of 600 personal cleaning kits in HCFs, and supplying 630 cover mattresses and 630 pillows.A1.2.3-Provision of patient beds, companion´s resting chairs, essential supplies, and equipment such as safety box, detergents, and disinfectants to maintain the IPC standard. Activity 1.3-Provision of training for 45 healthcare workers (HCW) to build their capacity on medical waste management and infection control measures based on national standards and guidelines. Activity 1.4-Conducting health promotion activities at the HCF to enhance the medical waste management and infection control practices A.1.4.1 – conducting 6 awareness sessions for the patients inside the HCFs, in addition to distributing 1500 brochures to increase their knowledge regarding medical waste management and infection control and improve their practices. Activity 2: Improving access to WASH service at HHs and community level WW-GVC will be responsible for securing access to sustainable water and sanitation services for 322 unconnected HHs in Deir al Balah governorate, 165 HHs in Block 8, and 157 HHs in Al’Ansar communities, located respectively in Al Musaddarc and Al Buriej localities (Activity 2.1 and Activity 2.2); and increasing the access to water supply for domestic use in 13 communities (Qizan 'abu rashwan, albatn Alsamin and 11 neighboring communities) in Khan Younis Governate (168,150 consumers). Activity 2.1-Installation/ rehabilitation of water transmission pipelines, networks, and HH connections, through the supply and installation of (a) 850 meters of water pipes (UPVC 110mm) to extend the main collector to the existing network in Block 8 community, and (b) 2500 meters for Al ‘Ansar with the same dimension and purpose.Activity 2.2-Installation/extension/rehabilitation of sewer collection network and HH connections, through supply and installation of (a) 850 meters of sewage pipes (UPVC 355mm) to extend the main collector to the existing network in Block 8 community. Likewise, for Al ‘Ansar community but through UPVC 110m 2,500 meters of pipes. As coordinated with CMWU, the collected sewage of Block 8 will be discharged via Block 8 pumping station and the implementation will be closely coordinated with UNRWA. Likewise, the collected sewage for Al’Ansar will be conveyed to the sewer main carrier line of Salah Aldeen and the implementation will be in closely coordinated with CMWU.Activity 2.3-Rehabilitation of water wells and springs in the communities of qizan 'abu rashwan (3) and albatn alsamin (1). More specifically and respectively it concerns the rehabilitation of the water well equipment of Al Bereka, Al Kuwaiti, Al Sha'er and Al Sa'ada wells including the pumping systems maintenance, full new manifolds and cyclones (Menvolt 6"), 110 kva generators maintenance, and new flow meters (Electromagnetic Water Meter 6). Activity 3: Flood mitigation and prevention Activity 3.1 -The construction/rehabilitation of stormwater network, lakes, ponds, or rainwater drainage system in 1 target community in Deir Al-Balah Governate: in the locality of An Nuseirat Camp to serve Omar Ibn Al-Khattab Street from Abu Sarar Junction in Al Nusairat to Al-Mughraqa Bridge. Activity 4: WASH response to settler violence and demolition incidents (Advocacy).Activity 4.1-Development of 5 awareness products on access restrictions to WASH, specifically, WW-GVC and QRCS, will elaborate 1 sectorial analysis report, 2 videos, and 2 infographics, with a focus on WASH in Health and IPC control measures. The advocacy action will also illustrate the Israeli restrictions on equipment and materials designated as ‘dual-use items’, and the poor governance of the WASH sector due to the limited technical and financial capacities of WASH service providers.

WW-GVC and QRCS will develop an M&E plan at the beginning of the project to track performance against indicators (outputs and outcomes), timeframe encompassing key criteria for baseline/end-lines and other related studies. 9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Well-designed and well-managed mechanisms for handling beneficiary’s complaints, feedbacks and allegations are of utmost importance for an inclusive, reliable and properly informed decision process. All feedback and complaints coming from beneficiaries will be given high consideration. WW-GVC and QRSC are committed to guaranteeing transparency, fairness and equity towards all our beneficiaries and will jointly scrutinize the content of the complaints and/or feedback and-if necessary, will further investigate the matter to assess whether an adjustment to the programming or disciplinary actions are needed. Also, the two partners commit to addressing the complaints and feedback within a reasonable timeframe according to the severity of complaint / allegation.

9.1b - If 'Other', please specify :

WW-GVC and QRSC will arrange a dedicated hotline for complaints coming from beneficiaries throughout the whole duration and up to 6 months after the project. This will complement WW-GVC’s Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM), accountability and commitment to guarantee transparency and equity toward all beneficiaries. The system aims to give individuals and groups the possibility to report complaints, suggestions and inquiries in a protected way, and receive response timely according to complaint/allegation’s severity. Additionally, an online form accessible to all beneficiaries and community members can be established to actively collect feedback and complaint allowing beneficiaries to anonymously report, besides complaints, any cases of mistreatment and/or abuse, that violates institutional policies including on Safeguarding. All complaints are reported to the WW-GVC’s and QRSC Monitoring and Coordination manager by focal points who refer the case to relevant staff according to it sensitivity, guaranteeing confidentiality, anonymity and do no harm principle. Additionally, through the Safeguarding focal point and relative procedures, the system in place to collect feedback and complaints also incorporates elements for a safe disclosure and proper PSEA. Upon registration of the complaint on the database, the Admin officer analyses the complaints and classifies each of them according to the internal classification system. Cases classified as mistreatment and/or abuse follow a different line of investigation, in line with institutional policies, where a Safeguarding Focal Point in WW-GVC follows an investigation with a Supervisory Body from WW-GVC HQ, in support of the Global Compliance Officer, to resolve the received cases. If the complaint (not concerning PSEA) requires to follow-up and consent was provided, the complainant will receive an answer within 15 calendar days from WW-GVC’s and QRSC ’s Monitoring and Coordination manager.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

The entire project cycle including need assessments, designed strategies, proposed interventions, set indicators, and outcome measuring procedures, takes into account the need of the most affected groups including women, children, PWDs and elderly. By taking a age and gender sensitive approach, this project aims at ensuring adequate and equitable access and participation to humanitarian goods and services, tailor assistance to meet the material and protection needs of these vulnerable groups and preventing/mitigating potential negative effects of the intervention on these vulnerable groups. In all interviews, surveys, focus groups and field visits, beneficiaries of all population groups will be informed about the complaint mechanism in place and modalities of accessing it. Field staff is obliged to inform and explain to all direct and indirect beneficiaries the presence of a complaint-response mechanism (CRM), including the referral pathway for complaints and allegations and cases of SEAH and how to safely access the different channels. Also, the beneficiaries’ agreements will contain special parts about the complaint mechanisms including the dedicated number for SEAH concerns. The complaint mechanism will be both in-person, at any time during the field visits of the staff of the two organizations, through the complaint box, satisfaction survey or remotely through the dedicated hotline or online form ensuring that all population groups may access the mechanism. The online form and hotline allow to anonymously report, besides feedback and complaints, any cases of mistreatment and/or abuse, that violates institutional policies such as PSEAH, Child Safeguarding and/or Data Protection. Accessibility to the mechanism to all population groups is a priority and is maintained throughout the whole project life cycle.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The proposed project ensured community participation -promoting the equal participation of the different groups in the community such as people with disabilities, women or children – in a way that endeavors to minimize exposure to additional hazards and mitigate as many risks as possible. Meaningful community involvement takes place through the identification of the needs and of the most suitable and equal solutions, resulting in increased empowerment at the individual and community level. WW GVC and QRSC want to assure compliance with the Do No Harm principle and that the programming reflects preferences, needs and resources expressed by the members of the HHs in a participative way. This approach is built from a multisectoral perspective, which supports the communities in assessing their vulnerability and in mapping appropriate protection responses, aiming to increase the capacity of the community to make informed decisions about their safety. Response and activities are carefully designed to ensure that interventions will not impair beneficiaries’ safety or expose them to further risk, at the same time upholding their dignity throughout the process. Activities are based on the preferences and needs expressed by the members of the HHs, considering the different threats that affect girls, boys, women and men of all ages including PWDs. Particular attention is given to input/feedback provided by women, whose participation is strongly encouraged in any decision-making process; further, the intervention takes also into account the indications provided by WW-GVC “Gender and WASH toolkit for Palestine”, which provides applicable and feasible suggestions on how to mainstream gender in WASH projects in the Palestinian context. WW-GVC adopts a well-designed and established complaint response mechanism system (WW-GVC CRM) allowing beneficiaries and the affected population to provide feedback and complaints, which will in turn influence management decisions. Staff will include awareness activities on PSEAH, including through this active feedback and complaint mechanism, through which information will be collected, aiming at taking corrective action in improving some elements of the response. Awareness sessions will focus on strengthening accountability towards affected populations, ensuring that communities and individual members and families are meaningfully and continuously involved in decisions that directly affect their access to safe and dignified live. The main components of the mechanism will ensure the collection, acknowledgement, analysis and response to the feedback received (including how to report SEAH-related complaints). Whenever possible the implementation will be led by community members through a participatory approach, nevertheless, the project will also instruct subcontractors, suppliers, and service providers to implement all needed measures to meet “Do No Harm” requirements, especially in light of COVID- 19 ongoing outbreak, by using all the preventive measures and equipment to minimize the risk of contagion. Finally, all staff involved in the projects is aware of, and in adherence to the Do No Harm principle as outlined in WW-GVC Code of Conduct and related policies including Child-Protection; Anti-Fraud, Bribery & Corruption; Anti- Terrorism; and Policy Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. COVID-19 protection measures (face masks, gloves, hand sanitizers, physical distancing) will be followed by WW-GVC and QRSC teams during all project activities to prevent the disease spread in line with governmental guidelines.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The proposal is following the sustainable humanitarian approach, aiming to provide immediate relief, ensuring equity, sustainability, and impartial access to improved WASH and Health services for the most vulnerable communities and institutions of Gaza. The project aims to increase the efficiency, predictability, and availability of WASH and health services and has been designed considering the direct and indirect impact on women, girls, boys, and men with or without disabilities. The impartiality principle is carried throughout the action based on needs, exclusively, giving priority to the most urgent cases of distress and making no distinction. The impartiality principle will be recognized throughout the actions' entire phases: design, planning, implementation and monitoring. This is illustrated by the fact that assessments have been conducted through the same tools and methods in every household and facility targeted, collecting the data directly from the duty-bearers as a way of accountability and transparency. During the design phase, several HHs and HCFs have been assessed by WeWorld-GVC and QRCS staff in coordination with the Ministry of Health, the Palestinian Water Authority, CMWU, and the OCHA WASH and Health Clusters, to identify the different needs, potential activities, and beneficiaries. Multi-sectoral needs are identified and ranked based on the contextual WASH and Health risks of communities and individuals. The use of focus groups and informed interviews ensure the involvement of the population in terms of location, age, gender, ethnicity, diversity, profession, and power. According to the access to the population, the level of trust built and other do not harm considerations, multiple specific techniques will be applied to get deeper insight (bias reduction and/or data gap filling) of a particular group, a specific situation, a particular household. Throughout the assessment and planning phases, the needs of all community members will be taken into consideration. The proposed WASH and health intervention address the urgent need of the targeted communities, accounting for non-discriminatory practices and considering the needs of every age, gender, and level of ability. Providing a voice for each member of the community, and requesting their direct action in the project’s implementation, preserves the dignity of each beneficiary and fosters community empowerment. The dissemination of information about the Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) in the communities will ensure the accessibility to all beneficiaries to provide their feedback and complaints about the implemented project. Continuous coordination will be kept with these institutions and related stakeholders to update the collected data and to respond to the most urgent WASH and Health needs before designing and delivering of any response.
