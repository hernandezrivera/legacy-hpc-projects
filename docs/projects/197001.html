Project Summary

The WoS Food Security Cluster/Sector of Syria links the hub-level clusters/working groups (NES, NWS and Damascus) offering technical and operation support and provides analysis of needs, response and gaps at a WoS level. Mercy Corps currently hosts the FSS NGO co-Coordinator.
In 2023, the sector will build on its work of previous years and will focus on reviewing and updating needs periodically, response and gap analysis to operational planning and
developing contingency/preparedness plans. The sector will continue to harmonize all aspects of the response such as assessments, analysis, geographical targeting, selection
criteria (including targeting youth for livelihoods) and monitoring. The sector will lead cross-learning initiatives on outcome indicators monitoring, response modalities and
cross cutting issues. The sector will also deliver on the key outputs for the Humanitarian Programme Cycle as well as feed into strategic discussions and decisions. Inter-sector
work will focus on a variety of areas including (1) linkages and collaboration with WASH, NFI, Nutrition, Early Recovery and Protection sectors (2) deeper focus on protection
analysis with the inclusion of the protection matrix at sector and projects level and ongoing collaboration with the Protection sector (3) an integration strategy aimed at
linking people on food assistance with livelihoods support from other sectors such as the Early Recovery and Livelihoods sector (4) joint needs analysis and advocacy, common
messaging to communities and assessing convergence at household level with nutrition and other closely linked sectors (5) improved linkages and coordination with ongoing
cash-based transfer activities and to help guide new activities as well as learn from ongoing and past activities.

3 - Needs

Syria 2023 PIN figures estimated food insecure + those at risk of food insecurity increased from 14.6 to 15.3 million, with 12.1 million facing acute food insecurity, 1.8 million at risk of sliding into food insecurity, 2.1 million living in camps deemed to be 100% food insecure. This is alarming given wheat yield was 30% below the long-term average in Syria. General analysis on food consumption scores (FCS), reduced coping strategy (rCSI) and diet diversity, show food security has consistently worsened over the past two years. People consuming an inadequate diet increased from 21% in 2019 to as much as 50% in 2022. rCSI levels is an indicator monitoring resilience to face-off future shocks, where a higher score relates to lower levels of capacity of coping. Consumed diets (diet diversity) highlights a clear reduction of consumption of proteins, fruits, dairy and pulses while sugars, fats and vegetables remains largely unchanged - a significant worsening of the diversity of the diet, now largely composed of cheaper and non-nutrient rich foods. The WFP national average standard reference basket a household in Syria can afford with their monthly minimum wage shows erosion of purchasing power over the past couple of years, which steadily increased as the COVID-19 impact coupled with the intensified depreciation of SYP.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Mercy Corps Syria has rolled out a protection risks analysis tool for staff and partners since 2018, as part of the protection mainstreaming strategies. Noting that the MercyCorps submission is reflective of protection programming incorporated into several donor grants (often multi-sector), each project and key interventions will have a protection riskanalysis. The principle of risk analysis and protection assessment is a cornerstone to Mercy Corps protection mainstreaming. Mercy Corps staff are trained onkey principles, staff will be gender balanced and complaints followed up on. Mercy Corps acknowledges that there are a significant number of protection risks that individuals and communities can face as a result of poor programming or programming in a difficult environment. All coping mechanisms, with the possible exception of sending children to work or to beg in opposition-controlled and Self-Administration areas, increased at a steady rate since October 2019. In particular families subject their daughters to early or forcedmarriages in conflict situations as a means to relieve financial burden, and because a husband serves as additional protection from pervasive sexual violence. However, girls married atyounger ages are at high risk of complicated pregnancies and deliveries, the children have worse health outcomes, are at higher risk of intimate partner violence, and on average receiveless formal education than girls married as a consenting adult.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Participant safeguarding is central to Mercy Corps’ values, and we uphold a zero- tolerance tolerance approach towards any form of sexual exploitation and abuse. In order to prevent safeguarding violations of any kind, all staff, casual labor and external contractors receive orientation and training, and are required to sign the Code of Conduct. The safeguarding training is comprehensive and is delivered to all Mercy Corps and partner staff. Where Mercy Corps and partner staff have higher levels of contact with participants, they will receive tailored capacity strengthening on Protection Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and will participate in quarterly events, which address PSEA concerns in target communities, and facilitate the roll-out of PSEA tools and preventative actions. This process is led by our WoS Safeguarding Manager and is supported by a network of focal points within Mercy Corps. Partner staff will have the opportunity to risk assess their own areas in terms of safeguarding and create proactive action plans. Community participants will be informed on what safeguarding is and how they can report any concerns through sensitisation work across programmes. In addition, external contractors and staff such as suppliers or Third Party Monitors (TPMs) are regularly trained by department focal points who have completed tailored Training of Trainer (ToT) safeguarding training.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Mercy Corps closely coordinates our contingency planning alongside the Humanitarian Access Team (HAT) which is hosted by Mercy Corps. Along with INSO, OCHA and clusteranalysis, Mercy Corps operational planning strives to minimise risk to beneficiaries, partners and staff. 2021 is a challenging operational context in northern Syria.Administered largely by the Kurdish Self Administration (KSA), NES contains pockets under Islamic State in Iraq and ash-Sham (ISIS) control. The large coalition presence has been reducing, creating potential destabilizing event in the delicate equilibrium between conflict actors which has been an enabler of humanitarian access since 2017. Ongoing clashes in DEZ continue to cause periodic access problems. Tens of thousands of IDPs remain in camps and informal sites, unable to return to their homes. This has meant that there is questions ofoperational viability across northern Syria in 2021. Mercy Corps remains in close coordination with our donors and partners for appropriate contingency planning to enableand adaptable programming.
