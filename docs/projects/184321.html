Project Summary

According to FSNAU Mudug region has recently been affected by various shocks such as prolonged drought and locusts invasion including the effects of COVID-19 outbreak which have impacted on the livelihoods and general wellbeing of the IDPs and host community in the area, with the reference of FSNAU report in Mudug region more than 179,970 people face crisis (IPC Phase 3), more than 321,770 people are Stressed (IPC Phase 2), while 7,670 people are emergency (IPC phase 4) bringing the total number of people in Mudug region experiencing acute food insecurity to 509,410 household.
As per Galkacyo Food cluster 129,860 people are food insecure, 39,620 IDPs and 90,240 host community. According to the results of 35 separate nutrition surveys conducted by FSNAU and partners in June and July 2021, the overall median Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate in Somalia among IDPs is 11.2 per cent. The total number of IDPs who are in need of nutrition services in 2021 is estimated to be around 0.3 million including women and children, with particularly highest prevalence of GAM among displaced children under 5 found in Galkacyo (17.6 per cent).
1500 individual (250HH) from the drought affected IDPS in North Galkacyo and will be targeted through unconditional cash transfer. These target beneficiaries will enable to access nutritious food and other basic needs in their local markets. The cash transfer provides the beneficiaries with the robust ability to identify and prioritize their needs and as well purchase the most pressing food commodities and household needs.
The project will target, and identify, vulnerable HHs and host communities using needs based inclusive community based targeting approach with the support of village elders and local authority with a special focus on, household utilizes one meal per day, with no income source (salary or remittance), with no skilled person and no employment, and with large family members, single women headed households from vulnerable communities. Households whose bread winner is either sick or underage, Special consideration for Households with disabled members or members with permanent sickness, Poor health including TB/HIV patients with no cattle/livestock or farm owned, HHs with no food/cash crop harvest; animals not producing milk and HHs with Widows, widowers, orphans, aged and disabled.
The action will undertake to reach 80% of the beneficiaries’ households to be female headed. The selection will be done with full participation of village leaders and other stakeholders. Beneficiary Registration and verification for the beneficiary's name and mobile card name are matched and correct and approving and signing of the final list of the relief beneficiaries. The process will identify and Signing Agreement with Local Service Provider for instance Sahal service to transfer the cash to the beneficiaries. SDC will conduct Monthly top-up of cash and ensure that each family receives their entitlement. The implementing team will undertake awareness in the camps and urban areas to ensure that vulnerable families are informed about their rights and how to report any form of PSEA.
These beneficiaries will receive unconditional cash transfer over a period of 12 months value of $70 based on Somalia Cash and Market Quarterly Dashboard

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The Cash modality is the simplest way to help vulnerable people, because life is valued in cash, it is also a way to protect beneficiary's dignity and give one the freedom to manage beneficiary's need priority.Cash transfer programming is prefered according to community needs to help HH to get easy access to their immediate food security, also the modality of delivering cash relief is easier than an in-kind, in addition, the transfer mode is safer as we can use mobile money transfer
