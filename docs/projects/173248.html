Project Summary

The proposed project will work to contribute on achieving the JRP objectives 2021 along with BRAC’s strategic objectives 2021 i.e. Localization of humanitarian efforts, resilience building of the affected FDMN and host community and strengthening coordination among the humanitarian actors and stakeholders. BRAC will undertake a holistic humanitarian approach, in combination with other partners and stakeholders, to enhance livelihoods and resilience of vulnerable host community, promote self-reliance of Rohingya refugees and strengthen sustainable natural resource management for Rohingya and host communities. The response towards enhancing livelihoods and resilience building will include technical capacity building on livelihoods strategies such as homestead gardening, livestock, aquaculture with inputs and service supports and market linkage; skill development and job placement for ensuring self-reliance. To ensure sustainable natural resource management inside and outside of the camps. BRAC will consider community based mixed plantation and tree saplings distribution to ensure biological land stabilization through increasing forest coverage.
Focus on gender inclusion will come into a new dimension through equity and equality based on the needs and prospect of engaging in income generating activities. In addition, strategies such as consultation and participation with different age and sex group people and their experiences will be an integral dimension of the project cycle to ensure that women and men can benefit equally from development interventions and inequalities are not perpetuated. The project will ensure inclusion of protection principles in the general project management and as core principles in delivering humanitarian aid by incorporating protection principles into project cycle management through protection mainstreaming indicators for each component. The project will collect baseline data based on age and sex disaggregation though which it will be ensured participation of different age and sex oriented people with special emphasis on persons with disability, elderly people, female-headed households, and single mother.
Cross-cutting issues like empowerment, inclusivity, linkage to other critical services (such as protection and health), ensuring safe environments, as well as long-term well-being, linked to resilience to climate-induced vulnerability to natural disasters and portable skills will be the high priority areas in BRAC’s food security and self-reliance interventions. Consisting of Agriculture and Environment, Ultra-Poor Graduation Program, Skill Development Program and Ayesha-Abed Foundation, JRP 2021 will target 27,230 beneficiaries (female 23,380 and male 3850) and the total estimated budget is 7.29 million USD.

1 - Identified Needs

The food security of both refugee and host community is a growing concern. Under the existing conditions, the refugee population remains 100% reliant on food distribution to sustain the minimum required kilocalories per individual. The hard-hit host community is also struggling to ensure basic needs of their family due to the increase of commodity prices and cheaper refugee labor, indicating the need for response actors to increase their focus on host community food security. Needs of Rohingya Refugees: Bangladesh now hosts nearly a million displaced Rohingya from Myanmar, who reside in 34 heavily congested camps in Ukhiya and Teknaf sub-districts of Cox’s Bazar. Recent trends of food security indicators suggest that overall food security amongst the Rohingya refugee population has improved, which is likely due to the regular and effective general food assistance programme through in-kind and voucher programmes. Under existing conditions, the refugee population remains significantly reliant on food assistance to sustain the minimum daily-required kilocalories per individual and for diet diversification. Rohingya refugees remain highly vulnerable (REVA 2019, MSNA) and 100% dependent on humanitarian assistance. Sustaining regular and timely food assistance through either food production and/or direct supply to the Rohingya population will continue to be of paramount importance in 2021. Multiple factors continue to heighten vulnerabilities including limited livelihood opportunities, restrictions on movement to search for income opportunities, depleted savings and asset stripping. Increasingly, Rohingya refugees are adopting negative coping mechanisms, increasing their vulnerability. Allowing the refugee population to engage in small-scale agricultural and skill development activities will foster self-reliance, food security, dietary diversity, and access to fresh foods. Needs of Host Community: The influx of Rohingya, as well as the surge of humanitarian services, have had significant consequences for the host communities and local markets in Cox’s Bazar. Tensions between host communities and refugees are on the rise. Access to alternative income beside assistance is vital to reducing host community vulnerability. Continuation of scaling up economic and livelihoods support initiatives will assist in mitigating the impacts of the influx and COVID-19. According to a rapid assessment by BRAC, the price of almost all daily consumed food items including rice and vegetables has increased by 120%. REVA 3 report projected that 40% of population in Ukhiya and Teknaf are vulnerable and are highly susceptible to shocks. About 58% of population had Borderline to Poor food consumption score [WFP, May 2020]. Immediate support is needed in the host community, especially for the people whose life have been affected due to this humanitarian crisis. At least, 93% of households reported diminished income as an impact of COVID-19 [MSNA, August 2020] and almost 7 out of 10 host community households relied on less preferred food [WFP, August 2020], which has increased from 4 out of 10 households in December 2019. This high level of economic vulnerability among host communities, which has further been accentuated by this unprecedented influx, needs to be appropriately and critically addressed.Ecosystems restoration: It has been revealed by a study that nearly 21,000 acres of dense vegetation and more than 1700 acres of moderate vegetation have been reduced within the period of one year in-between 2017 and 2018. With approximately 6000 acres of vegetation have been deforested by the expansion of refugee settlement. This has negatively impacted ecosystems with the threat to recurring disaster such as landslides to the people living both in the host and camp environment. To reduce disaster risk, it is highly important to ensure biological land stabilization through planting mixed vegetation within camp and host environment.

2 - Activities or outputs

• Technical training and skills development for income generation based on agriculture-based livelihoods (vegetables gardening, aquaculture and livestock etc.) both in the host and FDMN.• Livelihoods support (agro-inputs and cash/asset support) for both host and Rohingya communities for ensuring food security and building resilience of the affected community.• Enterprise-based skill Development Training to improve technical knowledge of both Rohingya and host community on Mobile Phone Servicing, Welding, Automobile, Wood furniture, Wood Furniture Design , Beauty Saloon Male- Female, Refrigeration & IT Support Technician trades.• Establish market linkage between producer groups and traders to ensure fair prices of the products. • Biological land stabilization and ecosystems restoration through planting mixed and cover grass both in the FDMN camps.• Tree saplings distribution both in the host and in the FDMN to increase forest coverage and ensure nutrition and alternative sources of income from fruit trees.• Resilience building through community based self-help group development.• Capacity building and partnership development with local institutions, CBOs, Youth Clubs for undertaking initiatives to ensure environment conservation like day observation, campaign, and plantation.• Capacity building through hands on coaching and building partnerships with local institutions, NGOs to ensure health care service.• Enhance social cohesion through engaging village solidarity committee and ensure social protection• Engage CBOs, local NGOs, youth clubs etc. in project activities and build capacity to localize the humanitarian response efforts.• Build strong partnership with local government and government officers and enhance their capacity in delivering humanitarian response related interventions.
