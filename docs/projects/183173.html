Project Summary

The compounded crisis that have hit Lebanon in the last year including the COVID19 pandemic, the economic crisis, the Beirut blast, and the recent electricity cut off have all put strains on an already struggling education system. Students are at risk of losing a school year if a systematic approach is not put in place to provide the adequate support. Anera which has an extensive experience in 1) outreach and access to local communities, 2) identification of students’ needs, 3) procurement of supplies in an efficient manner, 4) providing cash assistance, and 5) having an effective monitoring and evaluation mechanism, is keen to respond to the current emergency which requires a 2-level support at the community/households level and at the public schools’ level.
Anera, which has always put in place the appropriate means to ensure access of students to high-quality education is well-positioned in responding to the identified educational emergency needs. Anera’s annual back to school campaigns provide thousands of students with the essential educational supplies. Throughout the COVID-19 pandemic Anera has been one of the leading NGOs in moderating an online educational platform to out of school youths under a project funded by UNICEF, and it has always set the efficient procurement, capacity building, and monitoring procedures as a priority to support its implemented education programs. Anera has also been selected by UNICEF to develop a Youth Functional Skills (YFS) curriculum which is adopted by UNICEF partners implementing the YFS program.
In response to the education emergency appeal, Anera will target Lebanese and migrant students from Beirut, Mount Lebanon, Akkar, Bekaa, and South Lebanon with its planned activities. Anera will contribute to the conducting the needs assessment in coordination with the education sector, to identify the needs of 4,000 students in all areas in Lebanon. Anera will refer 2,000 of the students to available services provided by NGOs through RIMS and the interagency referrals. Anera’s experience with providing awareness sessions to school-aged students will allow it to provide 1,000 out of school students who have dropped out from school due to the socioeconomic conditions with awareness sessions on the importance of education, drug and substance abuse , child labor, and early marriage, and access to the job market with the aim of encouraging students to transition back to education. Additionally, out of the 4,000 identified students, Anera will support 750 families of students who are enrolled in schools but are facing hardships in covering the education needs, and 500 families of out of school children with cash for education over an 8 months’ period. The 750 families receiving cash for education will also be provided with low cost solutions for education including power banks, internet cards, and lessons printouts. Anera will also provide 3,360 families having children in learning support programs with food parcels., and 5,000 students with stationary. At the schools’ level, Anera will cover the costs of the recruitment of 500 teachers.
Anera will coordinate with MEHE, ILO, the interagency education sector, municipalities, and identified schools who received transfer of students to ensure providing a comprehensive response that aligns with the emergency response objectives. Anera will provide 20 vulnerable youth with cash for work opportunities to support with the outreach and identification of families, and awareness sessions. The youth and project staff will be trained on the PSEA principles, sign Anera’s PSEA policy, and code of conduct to ensure minimal protection risks.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

[SO1] Direct Community Support Measures to most vulnerable Lebanese and migrant children: 1. Anera will identify 4,000 students through following an effective outreach mechanism in coordination with municipalities, schools, and local partners. Anera will mobilize 20 trained youth from all areas to identify the students from all areas. 2. Out of the 4,000 identified students, Anera will refer 2,000 students to services provided by other sectors using the interagency referrals and RIMs. A referrals and data management officer will be coordinating with the project manager, and the assessment team to refer students. 1,000 students will also be targeted with awareness sessions which encourage students to transition back to education. 4. Anera will provide 750 enrolled students, and 500 out of school students with cash for education5.4 750 households receiving cash for education will receive low cost solutions (internet cards, power banks, and printouts) to support the education of their children. 5.5 3,360 families out of the identified 4,000 will receive food packages. To support identified schools, Anera will provide 5,000 students with stationary, 200 with referral to catchup, and will support the recruitment of 500 teachers.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Anera has well-established policies regarding protection of vulnerable populations including PSEA, child protection, and data protection policies. During the implementation of activities, if GBV is detected or beneficiaries request assistance in this matter, they will be referred through pre-existing referral pathways, to the Anera Program Team (which will investigate further), and to other specialized inter-agency partners. All project staff members will also be required to sign a Code of Conduct for protection measures, especially on Child Protection and Safeguarding and on of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). Anera will ensure equity with service provision through the inclusion of 50% males and 50% female beneficiaries, and Lebanese and migrants from all areas will be targeted.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

The assessment will allow identifying the exact needs of the communities and students. The food packages, and stationary will prepared based on the exact identified needs of students. The involvement of local youth, and schools in assembling the food and stationary kits will be another way of involving stakeholders from the local community in the project. Beneficiaries will also be able to provide feedback through follow-up phone calls and through client satisfaction surveys that will be conducted mid-term and at the end of the project. Anera has also established a feedback and complaint mechanism in place for all beneficiaries and stakeholders. to submit their feedback through a WhatsApp hotline. Beneficiaries and stakeholders can offer feedback or make complaints confidentially and anonymously, all of which will be investigated by Anera’s program team.
