Project Summary

The project will secure the food need of part of newly displaced HHs in north of Syria who fled from the last escalation through providing RTE rations for 2 rounds as a lifesaving rapid response, in addition to provide food baskets for newly displaced HHs and most vulnerable HHs of the host communities for 8 months throughout two modalities in-Kind and vouchers, aids modality will be determined after a communities market assessment to check market access and availability of essential food items, QRCS also will support bakeries managed by local councils with 960 MT of flour to produce bread to around 5,500 HHs with subsidized price for 8 months in daily basis, targeted beneficiaries by bread will not be the same targeted beneficiaries of GBF.
45,500 of the most vulnerable crisis affected people will benefit from the project including women, men, girls and boys in all age groups and disabled people in the targeted communities.
Beneficiaries will be involved in project designing and implementation through a close coordination with local council and community representatives, also beneficiaries will be engaged in evaluation through satisfaction survey and complaint mechanism.

4 - Needs

Humanitarian situation across northwest Syria, particularly in Idleb and Aleppo governorates continue to deteriorate at an alarming rate due to ongoing violence, which has a devastating effect on the four million people in northwest Syria. From 1 December 2019 to 2 February 2020, some 586,000 people fled from their homes in northwest Syria as a result of ongoing hostilities. Many of them have been displaced many times throughout the Syria crisis, eroding the resilience of families and communities. This latest emergency compounds an already dire humanitarian situation for people in northwest Syria who have been made vulnerable by years of crisis, violence, economic downturn and multiple displacements. (UNOCH, 2020)According to HNO 2020 draft version: The number of people estimated to be food insecure is 7.9 million or 39 percent of the total population of Syria. Of this at least 569,477 (6%) are estimated to be severely food insecure. The majority of those are located in Aleppo, Idleb, Rural Damascus and Ar-Raqqa governates. the increase in the number of people in need particularly those who are moderately food insecure is largely due to households depleting their savings and assets as well as struggling to find livelihood opportunities to meet their food needs, as the crisis continues into its ninth year. Some 47 percent of all sub-districts in the country are thought to be either severely or moderately food insecure. Aleppo and Idleb governorates are estimated to be of the highest levels of severity At least 37 percent of the food insecure population are in North West Syria. Of the total PiN, the ratio of total male to female is 51 to 49, with a substantial number of children (at least 42%) while adults and elders represent 54 and 4 percent of the PiN respectively.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project intervention will be throughout:- Providing in-kind RTE rations for 4,000 newly displaced HHs for two rounds, - Providing in-kind food baskets for 2,000 internal displaced HHs and host communities for 8 months.in kind RTE rations and food baskets will be prepared as Food Assistance recommendation in Humanitarian Response Plan 2019- Providing food assistance via cash/voucher modality to 2,000 internal displaced HHs and host communities who lives in communities with full access to active markets, each HHs will receive 60 USD/month for 8 months, transportation reimbursement is included.- Providing 720 MT of flour (4 MT per day for 6 months) to bakeries managed by local councils to produce 5,500 bread bundles with subsidized price in daily basis, the produced bread will be distributed to ID and host communities HHs in communities with high severity.Project implementation cycle will start from coordination with the communities representatives and local councils, performing market assessments to evaluate market access, food availability, diversity, and price.Beneficiaries selection will be built according to their vulnerability and need prioritizing displaced families affected by last escalation with the focus on female/children headed HHs and disabled persons, the project has been designed to mitigate the conflict consequences on adolescents as well by included them in the targeted beneficiaries.Trained M&E team will be assigned/hired locally on this project who will becharged with collecting information regarding beneficiaries and data analysis and will alsoclosely monitor and verify the services and renovations rendered to beneficiaries toensure correctness, timeliness and efficiency. An end impact phase will also beundertaken to obtain beneficiary feedback on quality, quantity and further needs for theservices/goods received.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Low Capacity coupled with challenges in recruitment and retention of appropriatelyskilled staff at all levels Mitigation measure: - Bridging the staff gap - implementing a capacity building and training program for staff at various tiers and implementation responsibilities 2. Lack of coordination and synergies with representatives of target communities Mitigation measure: involve focal persons from the local council to coordinate with the beneficiary elders 3. Identification of beneficiaries and appreciation of project achievements will be weak because they do not hear, read or see enough of project activities and positive impacts Mitigation measure: - Encourage field visits by community elders with appointed focal persons to see the results and hear from beneficiaries directly - Develop a communications strategy to guide constituency building through internal and external communications with Project stakeholders - Consider Media communication and mobile applications 4. Grievances and complaints of expected direct beneficiaries are ignored by the implementing partner with all the negative consequences this will have Mitigation measure: - Establish a comprehensive GrievancesRedress Mechanism (GRM) with well-defined guidelines for complaint acknowledgementand redressal. The GRM to be publicized externally as part of each project communication's strategy - staff are provided with the necessary training to report unaddressed complaints 5. Insufficient supervision and poor-quality control will cause faults and deficiencies in project deliverables Mitigation measure: periodically carry out spot checks, certify quality and report back to QRC 6. Continuing risk of insecurity and potential escalation in insurgency related violence with direct impacts on project activitiesMitigation measure: - train field staff on MOSS compliance (Minimum Operational SafetyStandards) - Use of alternative measures for project supervision including Remote Asset
