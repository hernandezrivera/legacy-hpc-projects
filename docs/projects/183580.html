Project Summary

The project is responding to the cluster objective 2: Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence. The project aims at providing direct and immediate age and gender responsive child protection, psychosocial support services case management and referral pathways to specialized and multi sectoral services, including specialized Mental Health and Social Protection Services. The project will ensure protective environment for children to mitigate the long term impact of being exposed to traumatic incidents and support them to develop positive coping strategies in the targeted communities through provision of structured, inclusive and integrated child protection mental health and psychosocial support. This will be achieved through the case management and resilience-building intervention, structured PSS and individual and group counselling employing the best practices in community based child protection mechanisms. This project will build on ongoing Child Protection in Emergencies interventions and the capacitated Emergency Response Child Protection Committees in the West Bank and Gaza for delivering emergency CP interventions through structured PSS and case management and referral pathways services as well as national and international advocacy efforts. This project will also be addressing the impact of the grave violations on affected children their families and communities.

1 - Needs

The last bombardments on Gaza from 10 to 21 May had devastating effects that further exacerbated the situation of girls, boys, women and men living in the Strip and who were already dealing with the daily effects of a 14-year blockade, multiple and recurrent violent attacks, and further restrictions and vulnerabilities due to Covid-19. According to (OHCHR), during the escalation in Gaza in May, 260 Palestinians were killed, including 67 children. Of those, 129 were civilians while the status of 67 remain undetermined. Over 2,200 Palestinians were injured during the hostilities, including 685 children and 480 women, some of whom may suffer a long-term disability requiring rehabilitation and specialized medical treatment. After 11 days of violence, children in Gaza are suffering from fear and anxiety, lack of sleep, and are displaying signs of distress such as constant shaking and bedwetting. MSNA 2021 indicates that 50% of HHs report that the escalation had an impact on their livelihoods and assets. Moreover, 27% of HHs (38% Gaza, 20% WB) report that at least one member was showing signs of psychosocial distress in the 30 days before data collection, with 9% reporting at least one child had these sign. Findings from a RNA released by TDH suggest increasing levels of distress across all sections of society, often manifested through somatization, social withdrawal, and/or increased irritability and aggressiveness in both children and adults. SCI and partners’ own data confirm a marked increase in the volume of Helpline calls from people requesting support to address fear, anxiety, and panic attacks in themselves and their children. The (CP AoR) estimates that some 678,000 children (345,800 boys ad 332,200 girls) across the oPt are in need of child protection and MHPSS services. including 122,000 in need of focused non-specialized support or specialized services. The full impact of the renewed violence on mental health and wellbeing, and children’s developmental trajectories, will only appear over time, as exposure to prolonged and unrelenting threat to life and limbs, as well as violent loss of loved ones, all carry a high risk of traumatization. Immediate reactivation of social support networks and scale-up of MHPSS interventions are required to support psychological stabilization and recovery. Findings from TdH’s RNA confirm that a gendered approach to supporting MHPSS needs is also required. Women and children tend also to be disproportionally affected by the increase in aggression and interpersonal violence that has been documented both within the domestic sphere and in the broader community. According to a survey conducted by the PCBS in 2019, Gaza sees higher rates of violence against women by their partner than West Bank (38% in Gaza vs 24% in WB). TDH’s RNA partially links the high rates of intimate partner violence with the severity of the humanitarian situation, hardship, financial stress, and displacement. Frustrations for not being able to fulfill the role of provider of protection and provider for their families can lead men to use violence in an attempt to regain control. TDH’s RNA highlights concerns about worsening of sexual violence during the emergency and the risk of increased child marriage.MICS 2019-2020 indicated that 20.1% of children (age 1-14) face sever physical violence from their caregivers, with a rate of 28.5% in Gaza and 13.7% in the WB. Also, 90% of children in oPt face some form of violent discipline; while 7.3% of children between 5-17 years of age are engaged in child labor with 5.1% are working under hazardous conditions.Within three months after the ceasefire, three ERW accidents had already been reported causing the death of a boy and the injuries of six boys. Protection risks including child protection vulnerabilities gender-based violence (GBV) and mental health and psychosocial problems are exacerbated in the Gaza strip due to the recurring military operations. The ongoing deterioration of security and humanitarian situation in Gaza has left children with a deep sense of insecurity for their future, hopelessness and despair. The psycho-social consequences of the ongoing blockade and flare-ups of violence has resulted in an estimated 678,000 children in need of Child Protection and MHPSS. More than half (53%) of all children in Gaza are in need, and 12% of all children in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem. Additionally, 185,000 caregivers overall, including 137,000 caregivers in Gaza, are also in need of various MHPSS services.Across the West Bank, protests and tensions continue and as the verdict on the Sheikh Jarrah evictions approaches, East Jerusalem families, including in Sheikh Jarrah, Batn il Hawa and Silwan, are still threatened with home demolitions eviction and forced displacement. Clashes between Israeli settlers and Palestinians also continue with injuries and damage to Palestinian properties by the settlers. Palestinian families and communities are exposed to a wide range of severe socio-economic shocks and protection risks associated with the Occupation, defined as a situation of armed conflict, and periods of escalated conflict in Gaza. In the West Bank, the population with the highest vulnerability is found in Area C, East Jerusalem, H2, and the Children and their families living in the West Bank, in particular in Area C including H2 area of Hebron, East Jerusalem, the Jordan Valley, and refugee camps suffer from ongoing military violence, settler violence, access restrictions and the constant threat of displacement, combined with other associated violence such as continuous harassment. In West Bank, an estimated 350,000 Palestinians living in close proximity of the settlements are at risk of settler violence, including physical assault and attacks on lands and houses. Area H2 area of Hebron suffering on daily bases from the settlers’ attacks, harassment on checkpoints, attacks on schools and restriction on movements.

Clear and transparent selection criteria will be put and shared with target communities to benefit from the following activities: Conduct Emergency CP/MHPSS Home Based Sessions.Provide Psychosocial group counseling sessions to affected and at risk boys and girls including CWDs.Provide Individual counselling sessions for the affected boys and girls including CWDs based on the assessment of their PSS needs.Provide affected boys and girls including CWDs with PSS Support sessions using the Child Resilience Approach. Reach out to boys and girls including CWDs with low intense PSS sessions using the TeamUp and Healing and Education Through Arts approach. Conduct interactive recreational Days for boys and girls including CWDs and their families to socialize in an accommodating and fun atmosphere.Referral of boys and girls including CWDs in need for specialized services based on the assessed needs. Provide ERW awareness raising sessions using child friendly materialsProvide Sensitization Sessions and Psychosocial Support for male and female Caregivers/ Parents on Child Protection and PSS support and caring for their children. Engage male and female Parents in Resilience building sessions. Provide Individual counselling sessions for the male and female parents based on the assessment of their PSS issues.Interactive recreational days for boys and girls including CWDs and their families to socialize in an accommodating and fun atmosphere with a participation of caregiver.Awareness Sessions for male and female Parents in the targeted districts on the child protection and MHPSS needs, support and caring of boys and girls including CWDs in emergencies. Referral of female and male caregivers in need for specialized services.Helping the Helpers support (stress management). Train, coach and supervise a group of 40 boys and girls on the Child Led Advocacy, Innovative Advocacy, Campaign Design, Community Mobilization Building Networks and connections, Building Allies, Implement the Campaigns and Advocacy Initiatives, Evaluate and incorporate lessons learnt in the other planned initiatives. All partners will be trained on PSEAH and Child and adult safeguarding policies and reporting mechanism, specifically, staff who are working directly with children and parents will receive the training before delivering any services to target beneficiaries including but not limited to (social workers, counsellors, case managers, GPFPs). Child friendly awareness raising materials will be printed and disseminated among children and adults to ensure they are aware of their PSEA and safeguarding rights, as well as reporting lines through which they can raise any concerns. Moreover, activities will ensure equal opportunities for all children to benefit with prioritization of children with disabilities and child who are most vulnerable and at risk of any type of abuse, exploitation and violence including boys and girls at risk of SGBV. Do No Harm principle will be well imbedded in the design of project activities as well as the throughout implementation timeline. where inequalities and barriers to boys', girls', CWDs, men and women participation will be identified and eliminated. Additionally, Risk assessment of all project activities will be conducted to identify risk factors, this will also help assess power dynamics and pre-existing vulnerabilities that may affect implementation of project activities; accordingly mitigation measures will be put in place to prevent and protect the most vulnerable and affected groups. And how to best address it to the best interest of targeted groups.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

SCI will follow a conflict-sensitive approach to ensure timely identification andmitigation of potential negative risks and consequences. SCI will conduct strongcommunity engagement to ensure regular exchange of information, consultation onpriorities and preferences and transparency for preventing tensions. Beneficiaryselection will be triangulated by field visits, cluster consultation, and local stakeholderinformation. SC will maintain its independence and impartiality. Staff will not engagein discussions on sensitive political and community issues while working withbeneficiaries. SCI will continuously monitor the development of the COVID-19 fragilecontext and risks. It will analyze the potential interaction between the planned activity,the changing context and potential implications for the relations between stakeholdersand communities. SCI`s Social Tensions Tracking and Mapping system will locategeographic areas with communities vulnerable to social tensions. SCI`s strong MEALapproach will identify any emerging harmful trends and activities and introduce coursecorrection to programming as required. SCI will strictly enforce its Child Safeguardingpolicies and processes including support to partners as needed.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

To ensure the participation of the targeted people and the equitable access to theservices provided, a series of discussion groups and consultation meetings will beconducted at all project stages with girls, boys, women, men, elders and people withdisabilities. This will enable communities to raise their concerns and together come upwith solutions to ensure that all the interventions do not exacerbate risks or create newrisks. SCI considers it imperative that the project is inclusive of individuals living withphysical and mental disabilities, especially given that current services outreach of bothmale and female people with disabilities tends to be limited. The project will prioritizefor example PwDs with means to enhance their access to project services. The projectgender sensitivity will be integrated and considered as a minimum standard. Theproject will be open to boys, girls, women and men according to the vulnerabilitycriteria, with the option to create women only working groups to facilitate theirparticipation. SCI, together with project partners, will check during the project visits theproblems that women are facing and that the project activities are designed throughgender and child-safeguarding (CSG) lens to prevent any harm by project activitiesand/or abuse by the family members. Both Men and women will be included insessions with different interests and fields related mainly to enhancing their protectionand building positive resilience and coping strategies. The Gender Advisor will ensuregender mainstreaming in the interventions. SCI’s Field Officer will supervise theimplementation in cooperation with the partners to ensure equal access to the projectservices. Inclusion and equity are dealt with by SCI at two levels; the first throughstreamlining in existing activities and indicators as part of SCI policy to reach the mostmarginalized, and the other through promoting specific interventions to serve thechildren with disabilities in communities.
