Project Summary

Relief International (RI) will provide quality emergency lifesaving and recovery services to conflict- and natural disaster-affected IDPs and host-communities in 5 localities in localities in North Darfur (3 localities of Kebkebiya, Dar As salam, Tawila) and Blue Nile (2 localities in Roseries, Damazine ) states.: RI will provide improved management, treatment, and prevention of essential primary health care services and emergency response to infectious disease outbreaks. RI will add a new integrated comprehensive community-based Protection and GBV response component, using a survivor centered approach. To reduce and mitigate GBV risks, the project will engage with community protection groups and strengthen referral platforms. The GBV response will include specialized services such as Clinical Care for Sexual Assault survivors (CCSAS),.
The health services will be delivered through 21 already-established health facilities and their surrounding catchment populations, building on RI existing work in the target locations. RI will use needs assessments, report analysis, routine monitoring and end line assessments to measure the impact of this project on vulnerable populations including women and girls, severely and moderately malnourished children under five, and pregnant and lactating women (PLW)

1 - Needs

RI conducted a needs assessment in one of the planned states, North Darfur, in June 2021. This was to understand the needs of some of the localities that were at severity level 5 then. Al Malha and Saraf Umra localities were targeted because of their high severity level and they continue to face ongoing social political impacts as a result of conflict for limited resources and spill-over effects from the unstable political situation. The findings indicated gaps in nutrition, health, WASH, and protection services with acute needs in Saraf especially where there are either no facilities and its empty space, or existing temporary shelters of one room. Out of the 10 facilities assessed, three in Saraf Umra have no service provision at all nor structures to provide services in, whilst one (Kartoum Jaded) only provides nutrition through a one-roomed temporary shelter. Thus, explains the escalated humanitarian needs where the causes of morbidity and mortality are compounded by the ongoing economic crisis. This has been exacerbated by the global COVID-19 pandemic which has further deteriorated economic conditions and created more emerging protection needs for women and girls, and increased cases of malnutrition in children and women lest not forgetting the Health and WASH situation with low coverage/existing poor infrastructure (from limited new investment in facilities). The recent emergencies and poor community management coupled with the high non-functionality rate have further worsened the provision of WASH and Health services. Women and girls face increased protection risks and incidences of GBV in the form of rape, harassment and assault due to delays while tracking water for domestic use and as a result of additional layers of risk associated with COVID-19 due to lockdown and decreasing household food security. This highlights the need for immediate attention to this previously underfunded sector. A considerable number of households (HH) in Sudan (21%) are female-headed according to the REACH MSNA assessment in target locations (December 2020). Women continue to take major responsibility for fetching water and a number of HHs in the communities have no access to latrines, some use shared latrines and others are forced to practice open defecation (29.2%), bringing further risk of contamination. About 72% of women and girls face cultural pressure to share their views due to fear of retribution from male counterparts in their community. With both men and women embracing the stereotypical view that women are supposed to be polite and submissive to men as justified in both Qur’anic and Biblical teaching, women and girls fear to report GBV perpetrated by the men. 88% of GBV cases reported are understood to be resolved through local traditional justice systems (jouda), which have patriarchal biases that have harmful psychological effects for female survivors. GBV is prominent in Sudan, with the most notable form of GBV being: domestic violence-19%, Sexual violence-19%, Psychological violence-16%, forced marriages-16%, denial of economic opportunities and education-14%, and female genital mutilation (FGM)-16% - findings from Voices of Sudan, a qualitative assessment conducted by UNFPA and yet to be published. Thus, the legacy and consequences of violent conflict can increase intra-family violence due to a number of factors: individual and social trauma; changes in gender roles and in notions of masculinity; generalized impunity and breakdown of the rule of law; and increased tensions and frustration caused by economic stress. RI takes these gender dynamics into account during the design of its programs and ensures that all interventions are aligned with applicable international standards.

2 - Activities/Outputs

RI will support twenty one health facilities in both North Darfur (5 HFs in Kebkebiya, 3 HFs in Dar As salam and 3HFs in Tawila and Blue Nile states (4 HFs in Roseries and 6 HFs in Damazine . These clinics will provide timely life-saving basic primary health care services in line with the HRP 2022, Strategic objective and the health cluster strategic objective 1. These services include general consultations, reproductive health, immunization and EWARS, while mainstreaming GBV interventions through CMR and referrals of GBV survivors and PSEA. In addition, RI will support staff capacity building and community outreach, integrating health, hygiene promotion and GBV messaging. In coordination with UNFPA, RI will train the local community volunteers in both localities on GBV prevention/PSEA protection messaging and survivor identification and referral for both clinical and protection services.Procurement, storage and distribution of drugs and medical supplies. Expand and maintain disease surveillance system with early warning component. Conduct awareness / orientation sessions at the health facility for the community

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The scenario accounts for health and protection concerns similar to those in previouswaves of arrivals, namely persons with special needs (PWSN), sexual gender-basedviolence (SGBV), malnutrition, and possibly tuberculosis (TB) and HIV. Large numbersof unaccompanied or separated children (UASC) are also anticipated, along withpersons in need of psycho-social support. The very limited number of latrines, withshowers and washing facilities, is of particular concern, and ensuring genderseparation will be critical as well as the provision of security lighting for use at nightand for protection of women and girls.RI recognizes that women are disproportionatelyaffected by shocks and stressors during displacement and at the same time are oftenexpected to contribute disproportionately towards coping strategies and recovery.Gender considerations will be mainstreamed throughout project planning andimplementation with a focus on women’s roles. Promoting equitable access to healthservices will ensure that the project reaches the most vulnerable members of thetargeted communities. Nearly all health indicators are gender-disaggregated, and it isexpected that over 50% of beneficiaries will be women. Special attention will be paid tomothers and PLWs through reproductive health services. Privacy and confidentiality ofpatients will be respected and ensured at all times of service delivery. RI will deployhealth workers of both genders who will be able to attend to the unique needs of bothwomen and men in a comfortable, private, and confidential way. Examination bedareas will be covered with medical screens to maintain privacy while the examinationis being conducted by the health workers (male and female). Also, language can be abarrier, so RI will focus on finding bilingual (Tigray/Arabic) people to help with thecommunication needed to provide the services. COVID-19 precautionary measures willbe taken into consideration when defining the response plans, particularly in highconcentration zones like reception centers. RI will work with other service providers toestablish referral pathways for S/GBV and CP survivors as necessary and will ensurefollow up of all cases.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Relief International has a standard operating procedure (SOP) that guides the properimplementation and management of the accountability to community principlesincluding safeguarding issues such as PSEA and child abuse. RI is working tostrengthen the accountability system to be able to capture the needs and voices of theprogram recipients at each stage of the project cycle management: need assessment,designing, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation. RI has also rolled out its ownChild Safeguarding Policy across the operation areas in order to reinforce existingpolicies such as Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) within RI andtherefore increase and/or maintain a protective environment for beneficiaries includingchildren attending all RI-supported services. All staff receives mandatory training onthis policy. Adding to existing feedback and complaint mechanisms, RI also aims tostrengthen its accountability system putting a mechanism which enables coordinationwith the inter-agency to facilitate learning on best practices, complementarity incomplaint responses such as referrals of GBV cases; and strengthening capacity ofstaff on management of complaints and feedbacks to inform project designing,improve service delivery, and, enhance program recipients participation in each state ofthe project management cycle, etc. As part of the existing accountability system, RIalso documents community satisfaction on services which enable informed decisionson program design and adjustment, budget allocations, and future programming.
