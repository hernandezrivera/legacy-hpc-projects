Project Summary

This project aims to improve the lives of people in Syria who have been affected by the crisis and living in poor conditions as a result of damages on their houses and apartments posing protection risks. A total of 6.8 million people are in need of Shelter in 2024 while 6.2 million people are in need of NFIs (HNO Summary 2024). 38% shelters were damaged and 49% of people are living in inadequate shelter in need of repair and rehabilitation. 69% of the host community and 82% of returnees are in severe to extreme severity level of need for shelter (Shelter HNO 2024). The IDPs rely on the host community who are also straining and this puts a lot of pressure on the host community and increases humanitarian need. Shelter and NFI sector needs have increased from 5.7 million people to 6.8 million people while people in need of NFI increased from 5.7 million to 6.2 million people. The need is spread across the whole of Syria with Aleppo and Rural Damascus having the highest need with severity level ranging from 3-4 an estimate of 2.7 million people, which is a 28.7% increase from 2023 (HNO Summary 2024).
ADRA scope of work will be under the following activities
1. Rehabilitation of damaged or unfinished houses
2. Provision of winterization NFIs
3. Rehabilitation of damaged or unfinished houses communal repair
4. Provision of shelter grants for minor repairs and rehabilitation
5. Distribution/ installation of emergency shelter kits/ materials
In 2024, ADRA is planning to reach 3,075 individuals including men, women, girls and boys from the most vulnerable families including IDPS, returnees and host community. ADRA previously has implemented projects in shelter rehabilitation, shelter kit distribution, winterization voucher distribution and installation of HH level head solar systems in Deir-ez-Zour, Aleppo, Latakia, Rural Damascus and Homs governorates. A total of 180 apartments will be targeted and implementation will be done as follows;
Aleppo:
Winterization assistance through a voucher modality (1,850 beneficiaries)
Deir-ez-Zour:
Rehabilitation of 180 damaged or unfinished housing (180 apartments 900 beneficiaries)
Rehabilitation of damaged or unfinished houses communal repair (40 common areas 200 beneficiaries)
Provision of cash grants for minor repairs and rehabilitation (25 units 125 beneficiaries)
Latakia:
Provision of cash grants for minor repairs and rehabilitation
Homs:
Provision of shelter packages
Distribution/ installation of emergency shelter kits/ materials
ADRA Syria will prioritize most vulnerable people with the aim of achieving SO1. The potential household list will be obtained from SARC / local authorities and ADRA will do market research to identify the potential vendors and distribute cash through service providers where beneficiaries can access with ease. ADRA will provide staff and volunteers who will ensure smooth operations of the project. They will also be available for feedback and provide complaint mechanism for the beneficiaries.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

ADRA will coordinate with the relevant authorities including the governorates, local municipalities and Ministry of Local Administration and Environment (MoLAE) to obtain the relevant approvals for implementation of the projects as well as technical assessments. The selection criteria will target the most vulnerable families who will be identified through social assessments. The potential areas of implementation will be identified and selected through technical and social evaluation. ADRA will prepare and share the BoQs with the relevant stakeholders, advertise tender and select the most successful bidder who will be evaluated by the committee based on the submitted samples, experience in similar projects, technical and financial offers. ADRA ensures that “Do No Harm” principle is mainstreamed to ensure protection of the beneficiaries. To prevent exploitation of the beneficiaries, ADRA will work closely with the local authorities and municipalities to explain the implementation procedure and selection criteria of shelter and NFI projects. ADRA will coordinate with the other partners implementing projects within the implementation areas to avoid conflicts between the community members as there is a risk of inter-community tension. A number will be provided for the beneficiaries and other partners who wants to raise any complaints or concerns about the project to call. ADRA will ensure the rehabilitated buildings have adequate lighting, lockable doors and windows, and general shelter adequacy. ADRA will ensure both male and female staff are available throughout the project implementation period. The MEAL team will conduct field visits to issue complaint cards which will provide complaint numbers to contact in case of any complaint or concerns. All complaints will be followed up in reference to ADRA protection policy. NFI project might experience protection risks with access to the distribution point for women, elderly and people with disabilities. ADRA will ensure distribution points are in close proximity with the beneficiaries’ settlements and provide rest areas for the vulnerable during the distribution. ADRA has a code of conduct that includes PSEA and all the employees strictly adhere to the code. Some of the PSEA activities include; quality control, reporting, analysis, policy setting and overseeing and supporting the collective PSEA activities. Employees will be trained and offered support on PSEA and safe complaint mechanism will be offered to ensure confidentiality of the beneficiaries. Field visits will be done by both male and female employees. In the case of vouchers distribution, ADRA’s staff will be present to ensure PSEA and safety for the beneficiaries. In case of unclear selection criteria or duplication of the beneficiaries, ADRA will work with NFI and relevant actors to avoid duplication, as well as local Gender Based Violence organizations.
