Project Summary

The project will implement activities as per the following description to achieve the objective of people in need having access to water and sanitation. Here below the three subsectors of intervention are presented: Water Supply: In accordance with the WASH Sector’s Water Plan, the IOM WASH Unit has identified key locations to prioritize the construction of new solar-powered piped water networks (Camp 20, 20 Ext, and 23), in order to provide safe, chlorinated water to the populations living there. Operation and maintenance of existing tubewells will continue, and new Tube Wells (TWs) will be drilled where needed to meet gaps. Operation, maintenance and monitoring of existing large-scale water networks (Camp 12/19 and Camp 24), as well as existing tube wells, will continue to ensure critical water supplies, and emergency stocks will be procured to prepare for cyclones or other natural disasters. To address potential long-term impacts, technical studies will be conducted on groundwater resources for both refugee and host communities, as well as on water supplies to address chronic shortages in the Teknaf camps, with a view to finding durable solutions.
Sanitation: Construction of durable latrines that minimize desludging and health risks will be undertaken in both refugee and host communities. These include provisions for gender-inclusivity, as well as improved access and usability for Persons with Disabilities (PwD). This approach will also be followed to upgrade existing latrines. New Decentralized Wastewater Treatment Systems (DEWATS) will be constructed to address coverage gaps, and operation of existing systems will continue. Older wastewater treatment systems and communal latrines will be upgraded to improve treatment and disposal, and to minimize exposure risks for community members and sanitation workers. Additional bathing facilities will be constructed, and drainage of existing facilities will be improved. The approach to solid waste management will be strengthened by focusing on reduction and recycling of waste produced – promoting household waste separation, large-scale composting, and recycling of plastics.
Hygiene Promotion and Epidemic Control: Critical supplies of hygiene items (soap, water containers, Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) materials, etc.) will be provided, together with community education and mobilization activities targeting all age and gender groups at the household and community level. Mass cleanup campaigns and camp-wide celebrations for Global WASH days will be conducted to improve engagement and ownership of camp hygiene conditions. Strong monitoring and community feedback mechanisms will be implemented to ensure immediate identification and response for any WASH-related health risks, and emergency response stocks will be maintained in case of any Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) or other outbreaks.

Objectives

SO 1: To ensure regular, sufficient, equitable and dignified access for all refugees living in camps and affected Host Communities to sufficient and safe water for drinking and domestic needs. SO 2: To ensure all refugees living in camps and affected Host Communities have adequate, appropriate and acceptable sanitation facilities to allow rapid, safe and secure access at all times.SO 3: To ensure the change of potentially dangerous behaviours through access and exposure to innovative hygiene promotion (including hygiene items) for all refugees living in camps and affected host communities.

Needs

Need for maintaining and improving water supply: TWs comprise the majority of water supply, so it is extremely important to ensure the ongoing maintenance and functionality of the tube wells throughout the camps. Also, while most of the population in the megacamp is able to receive water from tube wells, there are still some gaps in coverage, and relocation areas that will require additional deep tube wells. Furthermore, only about 30 per cent of the total refugee population is receiving chlorinated water from piped water supply networks (WASH household Monsoon season follow-up assessment, REACH 2019), and studies have shown a high level of secondary contamination of water at household level. Water supply in Teknaf is especially challenging as there is very little groundwater available, resulting in chronic water scarcity where refugees receive less than 15L of water per day during dry season. A sharp increase in AWD incidence in the C26, 27 and HC areas in October 2019 (AWD situation report No.8, WASH and Health Sectors in Cox's Bazar, 2019) shows the continuing need for emergency response capacity to address any potential disease outbreaks. Need for sanitation services and infrastructure: Thirty-four per cent of households (HH) report at least one member facing problems accessing latrines (ibidem, REACH 2019) , and the total number of latrines remains below the SPHERE standard (20 persons/latrine) for the post-emergency response phase in some of the most congested camps. While latrine construction is severely constrained by lack of space, there is still a need for additional latrines. The WASH Sector FSM mapping exercise has also shown gaps in geographical coverage for fecal sludge management systems to meet regular desludging needs. With no permanent dumping site, solid waste accumulation is still an issue. Thirty-six per cent of HH report disposing of solid waste in an undesignated open area, and 19 per cent of HH report that solid waste is always or often present . Need for hygiene promotion and epidemic control: 26% of HH reported diarrhea occurrence within the previous 2 weeks, and 10% reported the presence of feces always or often occurred. This is largely due to open defecation from children, with 38% doing so. The potential for AWD outbreaks remains high, so there is an ongoing need of rapid response capability.
