Project Summary

The project focuses on the delivery of basic lifesaving and protection/restoration of livelihoods of the target population as well as community resilience building activities in Gedo Bay and Togdeer regions with a special focus on all locations with high prevalence of malnutrition (GAM/SAM), including IDP camps and host communities that are in IPC 3 & 4. This will be done through unconditional cash and fresh food vouchers transfer to improve immediate access to food and reduce risk of malnutrition among vulnerable girls, pregnant and lactating women, boys and men as well as the provision of livelihood specific and seasonally appropriate inputs to protect/restore livelihoods and strengthen their resilience to current and future shocks.

Needs

The humanitarian situation in Southern Somalia especially Gedo and Bay regions has been deteriorating as cumulative effects of severe drought due to consecutive seasons of poor rainfall resulted in significant increase in food insecurity and related health & nutrition challenges. The 2018 GU season has tremendously performed better, however this led to floods that affected farms as wells as human beings. A multi cluster assessment in Dollow district in May 2018 found out that a total 22,000 people were affected by the floods, with 7500 temporarily displaced. Among these are 448 farmers whose crops and irrigation infrastructure was damaged. An estimated 2.4 million people are in Crisis (IPC 3) or Emergency (IPC 4) through December. Prices of essential food items and cereal prices increased thereby making the very vulnerable HHs unable to afford food. Depletion of livestock assets due to distress sales and mortality has contributed to increased indebtedness and destitution among many pastoralists. The frequency of HHs’ food intake was observed to be very poor. Families are faced with increased challenges of poor access to food, and forced to take up negative coping strategies to survive. On average most vulnerable HHs reported to consume one meal per day to none. The report shows 73% of HHs in Gedo and 76% in Bay, have poor FCS as sources and access to food continues to diminish. 93% of the respondents reported cases of malnutrition with 90% stating that the health status of children and general populations had deteriorated with the continued drought situation. In this regard HH are forced to sell their assets and those with little or none, borrow food and money to survive. It is worthwhile to mention, the loss of livelihoods assets has reduced access to loans, a common coping mechanism among Somali communities.

Activities

OBJECTIVE 1 ACT1: Provide unconditional cash transfer to 1500 HHs that are faced with severe levels of food insecurity (crisis and emergency) with the aim of increasing immediate access to food for 4 months. These groups will be consisting of vulnerable IDPs and drought/flood affected rural communities with a special focus on marginalized and minority groups ACT 2: Provision of fresh food vouchers (FFV) to 500 IDP HHs with malnourished children below 5 years, pregnant and lactating women who are at risk of malnutrition for 3 months. Selection of beneficiaries will be done in collaboration with nutrition actors, OTPs & MCH and caregivers especially women. OBJECTIVE 2 ACT1: Provision of locally preferred high quality seeds kits (localy available and nutritionally dense vegetables), trainings and fuel for irrigation for 500 female and male headed vulnerable riverine and agro pastoral farmers in Gedo region. ACT 2: Provision of locally preferred high quality seeds kits (drought tolerant) and trainings for 1000 female and male headed vulnerable agro pastoral farmers in Bay region • ACT 3: Emergency livestock treatment (Vaccination and deworming) campaigns for animals belonging to 500 HHs will be dewormed and vaccinated against endemic &prevalent diseases. OBJECTIVE 3 ACT 1: Rehabilitation of community productive assets through CFW targeting 300 vulnerable HHs. Local community assets (farmland, irrigation canals, and feeder roads) destroyed by the floods will be selected and rehabilitated through cash for work approach prioritizing vulnerable HH and minority groups. Women will be involved in selection & design. HHs that cannot work or are labour deficit will be provided with unconditional cash grants.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Contextual analysis is important in the implementation of this project considering the various potential sources of conflicts in the target locations both at leadership and community level. Regular assessments and community engagements will be done to learn if the project activities, approaches and methodologies are sensitive to the local context.Vulnerable community members generally refer to those disadvantaged with compromised access to resources to meet their daily basic needs. The project staff will ensure that during community mobilization and sensitization, all community members are given equal opportunity to participate.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Gender disparities are visible in the operational communities, where women usually take a back seat in issues of community development. The project, through community mobilization and awareness, will ensure women are empowered and represented to influence decisions and outcomes.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

COOPI will apply a conflict sensitive lens throughout project and implementation. As part of the baseline assessment, COOPI will undertake a conflict sensitivity analysis that will assess issues of conflict and existing community structures that can contribute to peace-building and conflict resolution. The results of the analyses will serve as a guide for ensuring that project stakeholders understand the complex dynamics within each beneficiary community. COOPI will also ensure staff; partners, and communities involved in the project are aware of and take into consideration actions that have the potential to cause conflicts within and between community and social groups.
