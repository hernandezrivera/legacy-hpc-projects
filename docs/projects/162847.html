Project Summary

Mercy Corps (MC) will deliver nutrition programming to conflict affected people in South Darfur State. Furthermore, Mercy Corps deliberately designs our humanitarian programs to do better than meet needs on a stop-gap basis. While our priority aim is to save lives, minimize suffering, and protect social and economic assets, we will also tackles the proximate and underlying causes of the emergency in order to interrupt repeated cycles of crisis and vulnerability by mitigating suffering and not just postponing it to a later date.
Building on Mercy Corps (MC) current nutrition project in East Jebel Marra South Darfur, MC propose to implement a community based nutrition program that aims to improve the infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices of communities, using a mother-to-mother support (MtMSG) model targeting 16,696 in Sunta , El Salam and Katyala localities within South Darfur state. IYCF practices, especially exclusive breastfeeding and complementary feeding, has been identified as the single most important intervention in a package of essential child survival interventions recommended for emergency contexts characterized by high levels of child undernutrition and mortality rates. Exclusive breast feeding is essential from 0-6 month given that the colostrum found in the mother’s breast helps build the child immune system during the first fragile 6 months of the child’s existence. Given the urgent nutrition needs and inadequate community based support as evidenced by 2020 Refugee Response Plan (RRP) Needs Overview, MC will use a community-based model to promote healthy behaviours that improve IYCF practices, with a primary emphasis on the first 1,000 days of life (from conception up to age two). This program will build on existing community groups wherever possible and will be based on the MtMSG model This is a peer-based nutrition promotion program that aims to create individual and community behaviour change in nutrition behaviours among at the community through dissemination of key nutrition messages. This model has proven to be extremely effective and cost-efficient in reducing child malnutrition, mortality and morbidity in emergency and development settings. Furthermore, it facilitates a broad coverage of beneficiaries by messaging through a cascade structure. Given the high prevalence of malnutrition and limited options for the treatment of severe and moderate acute malnutrition in the target areas, MC proposes to support the Ministry of Health (MoH) to provide treatment services to malnourished children and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) by implementing a Community-Based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) program in the target locations. CMAM is an an approach that enables community volunteers to identify and initiate treatment for children with acute malnutrition before they become seriously ill. This approach has been endorsed by UNICEF, the WHO and the MoH in Sudan
MC will work with the relevant stakeholders to develop a referral mechanism to enable children experiencing acute malnutrition to access necessary treatment and support. A screening system will be put in place with a local partner organization with mobile capability, to ensure children identified as malnourished are referred for appropriate treatment. In addition, six nutrition centres in both administrative units will be rehabilitated. MC will complement these activities with targeted distribution of restricted multipurpose cash assistance vouchers to 400 mothers of children with SAM cases and extremely vulnerable households. The vouchers will be exchanged for locally available nutritious foods at predetermined vendors.

1 - Needs

In 2020, a staggering 425,876 (Sunta 165,020, Katyala 144,148 and El Salam 116,708Persons in Need (PiN) have been identified in South Darfur State as vulnerable among other localities. Among this population, 93,181 are children under fivers years (U5s) and 43,876 are pregnant and lactating women (PLW) across the three targeted localities. Furthermore, South Darfur is among the states classified as IPC phase 3 with 45,253 (Sunta 24,753, Katyala 14,665 and El Salami 5,835) vulnerable individuals recorded as facing the highest severity of acute food insecurity as at December 2018. According to 2020 Refugee Response Plan (RRP) Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) Sunta has some of the highest levels of global acute malnutrition (GAM) in the country, with an estimated 16,039 children under five years with GAM prevalence rate of 19.14 percent above 15 percent threshold and pregnant and lactating women at 7.24 percent based on medium upper arm circumference (MUAC).The situation is not better in other targeted localities with U5 GAM rates in Katyala at 15.4 percent and ElSalami recording 12.9 percent. Overall across these 3 localities, 12,192 children U5 face moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) in Sunta, 10,712 in Katyala and 8,174 El Salam based on MUAC. Of the target population 7,427 U5s (Sunta 3,846, Katyala 2,464 and El Salam 1,116) cases of severe acute malnutrition in need of care and treatment.Chronic food insecurity is one of the facts that contributes to these high levels of malnutrition.Acute malnutrition is a life-threatening condition, which may result in increased morbidity and mortality among affected children. If left untreated, acute malnutrition could lead to death and cognitive disability. Protracted nutrition crises contribute to stunting which if not prevented may have debilitating consequences such as impaired physical growth and cognitive development which impact national economy and development. Mercy Corps will ensure increased access to life-saving services for acutely malnourished U5 children and PLW and prevention activities.Community-based care for the treatment and prevention of malnutrition is an important component of providing a continuum of care for low health resourced communities. Inadequate community-based health services to identify, refer and follow-up on acute malnutrition and/or provide nutritional education and counselling is also a gap and MC proposes to undertake a community-based nutrition intervention which aims to prevent acute malnutrition by improving the IYCF feeding practices of targeted households in Sunta, Katyala and El Salami. This has been further worsened since in the past three months no MUAC screening has been conducted in Sunta.Furthermore, public transport is limited or non-existent, forcing people to undertake this journey by foot or by donkey. The referral mechanism for children requiring treatment for acute malnutrition is weak, with onward referrals ad-hoc and treatment not easily accessible. The unavailability of transportation leads to delays on referrals of malnourished children to health clinics. To address this gap in service provision, MC will work with the relevant stakeholders including the MoH, the nutrition and health sectors, UNICEF and local communities to develop an appropriate mechanism to refer children identified as experiencing acute malnutrition for appropriate treatment. A mobile malnutrition screening service will be implemented with a local partner.

2 - Activities / Outputs

MC has established access in South Darfur and proposes to address the identified needs of these extremely affected communities through the following activities.1.1 Establishment and training of 60community volunteersThese volunteers will be trained on the identification of acute malnutrition within the community using MUAC tapes and referral forms. This method of active case finding will also include home visits, defaulter tracing and the collection of information on reasons for absenteeism and default from nutrition treatment programs.1.2 Referral and care for SAM with complicationChildren identified with SAM will be referred for treatment to the rehabilitated nutrition centres. At the centres, children presented with SAM will go through an initial assessment for medical complications or illness. 1.3 Quarterly MASS MUAC Screening MC will conduct quarterly MUAC screening for early identification of malnourished children through active case finding and behaviour change communication messages at the community.1.4 Formation and training of mother-to-mother support groups (MtMSG) MC will form six MtMSG that will focus on supporting, protecting and promoting IYCF practices that will include exclusive breastfeeding and empowering women to make correct food choices. A strong focus will be on the preparation, preservation and storage of the locally available foods to improve dietary diversification for complementary foods done through cooking demonstrations.1.5 Provision of care for children with MAM and SAM cases. After identification of cases during MUAC screening, children with SAM cases will receive treatment at OTPs and those with SAM complication will be referred to stabilization centres for treatment and care. The MAM will be targeted through distribution of food supplements.1.6 Promotion of optimal complementary feeding starting at six monthsMC will sensitize and encourage mothers to practice optimal complementary feeding once their children attains 6 months. MC will recommend high nutrient feeds to complement those in breast milk to ensure that children receive all the necessary nutrients such as (Iron, Zinc, and Vitamin A) that many children in emergency situations lack. 1.7 Establishment and training of husband clubsMC will establish one ‘husband club’ comprised of 10-15 leader fathers nominated by the community. These leaders will be trained in ICYF, food safety, and awareness raising, who will then facilitate monthly community sessions to educate the fathers in and promote optimal IYCF practices. 1.10 Facilitate monthly community meetings between MtMSGs and the 'husband clubs'MC will also hold the monthly community dialogues and bazaars at the nutrition centres where various issues and concerns will be raised and addressed including information on IYCF practice.1.11 Provision of Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) to vulnerable mothersMC will also provide MPCA to mothers with children with SAM complications who are referred to stabilization centres. The MPCA enable mother to provide basic needs to children while admitted at SC and help children integrate after they are discharged to ensure that they remain healthy and never fall back to MAM status.1.12 Construction/Rehabilitation of 6 Nutrition centresMC will rehabilitate six nutrition centres to provide easier access to nutrition services and meet other emergency primary health needs. The identified local partner will share the space for screenings with the MoH. These newly rehabilitated nutrition centres will then serve as an Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) for children with SAM, and children with MAM and PLW respectively.1.13 Conduct SMART surveyMC will engage with the nutrition and health sectors, and the relevant stakeholders to complete a SMART survey in the target locations to explore the nutritional status of children U5 and the mortality rate of the population.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

CARM Process: MC South Sudan’s team has established and standardized CARMS, based on our commitment to accountability to the affected population. MC will use CARM to enhance accountability and transparency to the target beneficiaries and stakeholders. CARM allows beneficiaries to raise complains through letter and phone. Community will designate an area to for letters. The received complaints will be documented and evaluated and referred to i.e. those which should be addressed at the field level and those that can be addressed at the national level. Investigated complaints are finalized and feedback will be given to the target population at the field. MC program members will sensitize community members on the available feedback mechanisms and ensure it is included in the activity work plan. This will ensure that context-appropriate communication and feedback channels that are safe, accessible, transparent and confidential for clients are considered. The CARMs will also establish feedback and complaints on MC services as well as seeking information and receiving timely, appropriate responses about MC’s actions and decisions. Complaints received will be analyzed by age, gender and diversity and a dashboard will generate summarized data and information for the field and senior management. Further, MC will engage community leader’s representatives to give feedback on complaints raised and communicate any change in strategy and or implementation. Interactive Participatory Implementation (IPI) – the IPI approach is focused on gaining the optimum involvement of the project’s target stakeholders and target groups. This IPI approach, combined with ‘community empowerment methodology’ will generate the needed involvement of all the segments of the community ensuring the Accountability to the Affected Population (APP). It will also give a sense of accomplishment and create ownership among the target groups as the project activities progresses and achieves its tangible results. MC will involve the target population in the whole project cycle, from design to implementation to close out. • Program Design: MC will engage refugee camp leaders to conduct feasibility assessments and map water sources.• Implementation: MC will continue to engage the community volunteers in program implementation. The program will further ensure that women are also included as part of the water committees. This will further deepen program ownership, participation and eventually enhance sustainability of deployed interventions.• Monitoring & Evaluation: MC will engage water committees to give feedback on water pumps functionality and hygiene promoters in monthly basis. This will be held through focus group discussions. • Close Out: MC will engage the beneficiaries in the final evaluation and handover of the water points to the community.
