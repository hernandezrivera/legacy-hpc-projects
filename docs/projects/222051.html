Project Summary

The project will identify and enroll out of school children into formal education through gender sensitive and disability inclusive safe back to school campaigns, support recovery of missed learning through accelerated education initiatives, such as catch-up clubs and ensure school retention of children at risk of dropout through remedial classes. In addition, it will establish temporary learning spaces and accessible WASH facilities (including hygiene promotion activities) and provide cash support and education kits distribution. Teachers and community facilitators trained on Teachers in Crisis Context package and on Catch up Clubs delivery, as well as psychosocial support and social emotional learning. Gender sensitive and disability inclusive approaches will be mainstreamed in every step of the intervention, with a particular focus on accessibility of spaces and training of teachers. Lastly, it will provide support to coordination of the Education sector, through the education cluster to increase educational actors' coordination, strategic direction and harmonized use of resources. This component will also contribute to the development of a plan of action for the implementation of the Safe Schools Declaration at national level.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The education sector in northern Mozambique remains severely impacted by ongoing conflict, particularly in Cabo Delgado, where violence and displacement have disrupted educational access — and thus the future — of approximately 278,000 school-aged children, adolescents, and teachers. Forced displacement, school closures, and infrastructure damage and destruction have left children without access to safe learning opportunities, exposing them to risks of exploitation, early marriage, and child labour. As of September 2024, 166 schools in Cabo Delgado had remained closed, depriving 48,408 children of access to education. Urgent needs include addressing learning gaps, access to essential learning supplies and learning spaces and psychosocial support integrated into the education system. The crisis is compounded by a shortage of adequately trained teachers, who are essential to creating safe and supportive learning environments for children affected by the crisis. Teachers themselves face difficult conditions, including overcrowded classrooms, limited resources, and insufficient training in critical areas such as supporting children with disabilities, providing psychosocial support (PSS), and implementing conflict sensitive and gender inclusive teaching and learning strategies. Poor infrastructure compounds these challenges, hindering efforts to establish safe, inclusive learning environments. Structural factors, such as deeply embedded socio-cultural gendered norms and a lack of dignified options for menstrual hygiene management, are further exacerbated by displacement. The situation is also particularly challenging for children with disabilities, who require tailored support, such as assistive devices, to engage in educational activities. The project will provide first-line and second-line emergency responses, supporting conflict and disaster-affected children to access safe, inclusive, and quality education services and ensure the most vulnerable out-of-school girls and boys, including children with disabilities, catch up on their learning and are enrolled/retained in formal schooling. Quality of education will be improved through Teacher Professional Development adapted to needs of teachers in the crisis contexts. Schools with the highest severity ranking, identified in the cluster’s EiE Intervention Triggers methodology, will be supported to create safe, inclusive, and hygienic learning environments. School enrollment and retention will be supported through kits and cash distribution. The target groups are displaced, host communities and returnee children and their families, as well as teachers and community facilitators. Targets: Children aged 6-17: 52,265 (25,087male; 27,178 female) Adults aged 18-59: 1090 (523 male; 567 female)

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Save the Children will implement priority education in emergencies activities across Macomia, Meluco, Mocimboa da Praia, Muidumbe, Nangade and Quissanga districts in Cabo Delgado: These include: - Conducting back to school campaigns and sensitization campaigns on the importance of education - Distributing student and teachers kits - Providing cash to families to support education related expenses - Provision of assistive devices to school age children with disabilities in schools and communities - Delivery of catch up clubs for children who lost up to one year of schooling - Delivery of remedial classes for students at risk of dropout - Provision of training to non-formal education community facilitators who will run the catch-up sessions;- Provision of training on remedial education for teachers - Training teachers on Teachers in Crisis Contexts (TiCC) package, including child protection, teachers' well-being, pedagogy and learning assessment. - Provision of Temporary learning spaces and accessible WASH facilities - Increasing emergency preparedness of local authorities

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The project will monitor and analyse risks such as conflict development and its patterns on a regular basis- ensuring safe delivery and relevance of our assistance. A feasibility assessment for cash distribution and a child protection risk assessment will also be conducted. Community engagement, (particularly consultations with children and most vulnerable groups) and conflict dynamics have influenced the project adaptations leading to ensure children’s safety, wellbeing and non-discrimination during their participation in specific activities. The community dialogues and consultations also promote community social cohesion to identify difficulties and define practical solutions to protect children. The project targets IDPs, host communities and returnee populations to minimize tensions and promote social cohesion. Centrality of protection has also been embedded in all the project activities to ensure no harm.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The project will ensure access to services is guided and based on a vulnerability assessment that is inclusive, and gender-sensitive considering age and ability. Assessments and inclusion criteria will be guided by the Washington Group Questions ensuring vulnerable groups including women, girls and persons with disabilities are consulted and included in the project. Save the Children will also collaborate with Humanity & Inclusion for strategic planning and technical harmonization and implementation of activities. The project aims at reaching a set proportion of children with disabilities (at least 6% of the total target), improving accessibility and responding to needs.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project will ensure access to services is guided and based on a vulnerability assessment that is inclusive, and gender-sensitive considering age and ability. Assessments, registration and inclusion criteria will be guided by the Washington Group Questions ensuring persons with disability are consulted and included in the project. Save the Children will collaborate with Humanity and Inclusion for strategic planning, technical harmonization, needs assessments and implementation of activities.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The project is not expected to have major impacts on the environment. To address any unintended impacts of the project on the environment, project staff will be encouraged to prioritize the use of nature-based solutions. The project will also apply a climate change lens to the choice and design of all activities, mitigating the impact of activities through environmental risk screenings. Save the Children's environment and social sustainability screening tool will be adopted by the project.
