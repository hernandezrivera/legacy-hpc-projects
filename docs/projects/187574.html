Project Summary

In order to provide life-saving nutritional support to PLWs, CU5 through the qualitative implementation of CMAM and IYCF guidelines supported with awareness-raising, referral, and COVID-19 prevention measures, targeting people in need as IDPs and Host Communities in 7 different sub-districts in NWS, and to support the cluster coordination activities, facilitate the nutrition actors access to supplies, and follow-up the malnutrition trend in NWs. This project aims to implement the following activities to support NWS nutrition response and provide life-saving quality nutrition services for children and PLWs in northwest Syria.
1. Supporting Nutrition supply chain management:
To ensure no break in the supply pipeline, PAC will receive the supplies from UNICEF based on the annual and quarterly forecast, and according to the estimated needs among mothers and children PAC outlined in the HNO. PAC will be responsible for:
• Receiving the supplies from UNICEF as In-kind donations.
• Store the supplies according to appropriate storage conditions.
• Receive the distribution plan from the cluster to the partners and cluster members.
• Report monthly on supplies availability and gaps.
2. Strengthening nutrition cluster coordination mechanisms through co-lead arrangement:
PAC is hosting the co-lead position in the cluster and information management to support the cluster achieve its core functions and mandate.
3. Support Nutrition surveillance in all NWS districts:
In 2022 PAC with UNICEF support will implement the surveillance activities according to community-based, not facility-based. PAC will reduce the number of workers from 100 to 27. The new modality tends to select one team in each district to complete community outreach-based surveillance, and the team workers will do all the anthropometric measurements (MUAC, length or High, and weight, and other CMAM and IYCF indicators) not only MUAC as previous.
SMART Survey
PAC will survey to update the information about nutritional status among children and mothers in the second quarter of 2022.
4. Supporting 12RRT teams targeting crowded Camps and communities and providing the following services: These 12 RRTs will provide multisectoral services through integrating the CMAM program and infant and Young Child Feeding in Emergency (IYCF-E) Program that directly works for with;
A- Health services through Screen, referral CU5 and PLW with medical problems to the nearby health facility disseminate awareness of self-hygiene messages, child protection, and COVID19.
B- WASH awareness: Support hygiene promotion by raising WASH awareness by providing Key messages (Hand Washing, Food Processing, and access to clean water). COVID-19 hygiene, Risk Communication, and Community Engagement (RCCE) related messages.
C- Child Protection: Identify visual signs of abuse/violence against childU5/PLWs during Nutrition screening
5. Support 3 Outpatient therapeutic feeding programs (OTP) and Mother-Baby Areas (MBAs) through Static OTPs: PAC will maintain 3 OTP center/ Mother and Baby areas MBAs from areas where high GAM rate reported (Darkosh, Armanaz, and Maaret Masrin). The OTP part of the centers will provide outpatient CMAM services by screening all the 6–59 months children, PLWs, treatment of SAM, and MAM children and PLW. In contrast, the MBAs part of the centers will provide IYCF services for Mothers of children U2 years and PLWs.
All these facilities have access to the nutrition supplies needed for MAM and SAM cases treatment. PAC through a partnership with UNICEF and WFP will ensure to provided qualitative services for people in need in both Aleppo and Idlib Governorate.
The target beneficiaries of this program are190500 children from both genders ( 96900 Male and 93600 Females), and 166667 Pregnant and lactating women. Female BNFs are ( Girls and women) 74%and26% are boys,10% of BNFs with a disability, Men's participation in this project is limited in the level of awareness-raising components.

4 - Needs

The overall nutrition situation in NWS is deteriorating. Children under 5, pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) are the most susceptible to malnutrition since their nutritional requirements are relatively high. According to the SMART survey conducted by PAC in June 2021, the prevalence of chronic malnutrition among children aged 6-59 months increased from 19 percent to 24.5 percent over the same periodThe prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition GAM increased from 0.9 percent to 4.7 percent between May 2019 and June 2021 in Northwest Syria in Aleppo and Idlib governoratesAmong PLW, the prevalence of acute malnutrition across northwest Syria is 12.4 percent. There are also significant differences across northwest Syria, Idleb more than Aleppo.According to the same survey, Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices were found to be sub-optimal. The high burden of stunting in children is linked to sub-optimal uptake of appropriate IYCF practices which are crucial for healthy child development and survival. Only 53.4% of children under 6 months are exclusively breastfed whilst only 10.8% consume the Minimal Acceptable Diet (MAD) in NWS. Micronutrient deficiency is another major problem. NWS 2021 SMART survey showed that the anemia rate is 53.8 % among targeted women and 46% among children U5.Because not all NC partners are UNICEF partners, PAC is responsible to coordinate and distribute all nutrition supplies to partners in NWS in coordination with UNICEF.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

PAC safety&security officer will manage the protection plan of this project. The field team will be trained on how they best deal with serious and life-threatening situations. They will be provided with regular updates and recommendations to assure their safety and security. Coordination with Syrian Civil Defense in areas of implementation will be followed up to make sure their prompt access and response whenever needed. Other coordination with the health facilities, camp management, and emergency response focal points will be enhanced. In the case of extremely severe insecurity, the work will be suspended based on safety and security focal point instructions with immediate coordination with the donor. Risk management plan summary considers the following: 1. Attacks on the beneficiaries and/or project personnel due to a deteriorated security situation.' Likelihood: low Mitigation: Immediate cessation of activities and waiting for a change in the situation.2. Destruction of fixed facility structures, plundering of furnishings due to a deteriorated security situation. Likelihood: low Mitigation: Suspension of activities; analysis of the situation, rehabilitation and/or replacement of missing equipment. Regarding child protection, and PSEA, PAC will ensure well implementation of nutrition cluster protection matrix recommendations by integrating child protection and PSEA in all the activities levels and ensuring a safe and sound environment for children and mothers.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

PAC is an active member in the NWS PSEA network and maintains close monitoring activities over the PSEA in its ongoing and planned projects. For this project, the PSEA strategy can be summarized as follows; Risk #1; Beneficiaries and community members have no access to the PSEA complaint and reporting system:Mitigation: PAC has clear signs and a feedback system in its fixed facilities that show how the reports can be provided through email, phone, social media, and feedback boxes. Also, the mobile team will distribute IEC matriels to encourage the BNFs to share their feedback Risk#2: PAC newly contracted staff have a poor experience in PSEA. Mitigation: PAC has a clear policy presented to every staff that shows the PSEA mitigation mesuears. Also, the PAC PSEA code of conduct is well written and all the staff signed on it. In addition to that, PAC has mandatory online PSEA training that should be completed by every newly contracted staff training link: " https://agora.unicef.org/course/info.php?id=18168", and all PAC staff should do annual refreshment training. furthermore, PAC has 4 PSEA focal points in the field to support the field workers in full adherence with PSEA guidelines. Risk #3: The investigation process in the reported PSEA is not well managedMitigation: PAC has well-trained PSEA officers. Also, any PSEA case reported to PAC team will be processed according to PAC's PSEA guidelines, and will be directly reported to the PSEA network to have their support
