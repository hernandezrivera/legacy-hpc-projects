Project Summary

The project aims to provide a community based approach to programming in Protection GAH proposes to use an integrated interlinked multi sectorial methodology that harnesses all possible outcomes to achieve desired goals working in coordination and collaboration with other sectors related directly or indirectly to Protection and livlihood, Justice Systems, Water Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) Nutrition and Health.
To achieve protection outcomes, GAH proposes to use a comprehensive community based approach that enhances local capacities of communities to care for the most vulnerable of their communities in 2019 GAH already made a foundation in the grass roots to enable communities have capacity to cater for their vulnerable and to give them tools and skills for resilience for sustained results. The project intends to have robust community based activities that not only target the support to children but also aims at building community capacity to identify respond to and refer EVI, PSNs , WAR, elderlies, CAR including SUAC)and their foster families. GAH would like to work with communities and partners to strengthen and enhance community based systems for support of Children at Risk (CAR) and build processes for resilience and sustainability. Community based protection mechanisms will be strengthened and more roles and responsibilities given to the CBPNs in coordination with MoSW
Project aims to conduct dissemination and sensitization forums separate with men, boys, women and girls and campaigns to end harmful practices and attitude and behavior change, in so doing communities have information vital for their support of eradicating vices and promotion of values.
Efforts will be made to ensure that persons with disabilities have access to services; in this project we target them to receive assistive adaptive devices, training and livelihood opportunities and Disability friendly infrastructure in service centers
This project would like to tackle the issue of GBV and SGBV through community based structures that are bound to offer results as in most cases cultural barriers have prevented women from realizing their full potential and accepted abuse and discrimination as a way of life. We shall target more girls than boys in all activities for children to start mentoring girls into positions of leadership and to develop a pool of future enlightened and educated girls. The project also aims at supporting GBV survivors (more women and girls) and offering them livelihood opportunities to revitalize their lives.
The Livelihood intervention will target CAR in South Darfur, and Refugees in khartoum Urban to increase their capacity to produce their own food for consumption and for the market. Many households in the Country have significant unmet food and nonfood needs and have lost productive crop agriculture or inputs due to ongoing displacement. The harsh economic conditions have resulted in reduced purchasing power and incomes of vulnerable households and thus hindering food security. There is the urgent need to support these vulnerable Refugees and vulnerable households to begin restoring lost crop agriculture or their livelihoods. The livelihood support will enable the farmers and vulnerable households increase food availability through own production, as well as earn income from surplus production
GAH plans to target households (HH) in targets localities through the proposed interventions. About The target for tools, vegetables and crop seeds distribution.The project will distribute livelihood kits composed vegetable kits, crop kits and agricultural inputs. The project staff will train on the vegetable and crop seeds recipients to improved vegetables and crop production practices and in agri-business.. In addition, the project staff will undertake post distribution monitoring and conduct post planting monitoring and provide periodic technical advice to to farmers and fishers as necessary.

1 - Needs

1. The project is based on needs and gaps identified by GAH UNHCR, UNICEF, other partners and line ministries. Moreover, this project puts those assessments plus information gathered in 2018-2019 through GAH running operations in targeted localities into perspective, in addition to the information from the Inter-agency AGDM Participatory Assessment, conducted by UNHCR and partners that indicate more than half SSR in Sudan are children, high number of UASC, The affected include but are not limited to children at risk (CAR), SUAC Women at Risk (WAR) and other vulnerable women and children, Persons with Specific Needs (PSN) Extremely Vulnerable Individuals (EVI), PWD, vulnerable refugees and their host communities. the protections mechanisms to which SSR have access to address their specific needs are occurring at the level of identification of PSNs, creation of community-based mechanisms, albeit weak and registration mechanisms. The problem is that services for persons of concern with PSNs such as at-risk children, PWD, older persons, and women headed households, and are insufficient in camps and settlements. Moreover, there is a limited State social support service to be able to provide the required services to these persons with specific needs. Initial consultation with persons with disability for instance has shown that they require specific assistance because of their disability. women and girls face specific challenges which pre-dispose them to various forms of SGBV. At the same time GBV continues to be underfunded especially given its sensitivity and limited data, GBV is a behavior change project that required time especially given the program design and contextual dynamics in the area. The absence of close technical monitoring also creates quality gaps. Sudan is hosting 15,474 CAR refugees , new arrivals face language barriers , cultural differences leading to difficulties integrating or access services, access to local job markets and agricultural extension opportunities through land/crop sharing. A lack of livelihood opportunities raise protection concerns, links to school dropout, child labor, early marriage and other protection issues for families, including vulnerability to exploitative labor situations. CAR refugee new arrivals are farmers and pastorals arrived without their livestock and productive assets and so are now dependent on food assistance, Dimsu, El Radom and Um Dafug are very remote areas and there are not a lot of opportunities for non-agricultural livelihoods CAR refugees in Dimsu, El Radom and Um Dafug will require targeted livelihoods assistance with protection initiatives integrated throughout to address the unique barriers they face access local markets, Vocational training and small business support is needed, especially for refugee youth. 293,894 refugees in khartoum Urban areas are considered in need of protection and livelihood assistance, Physical protection needs are very high and many refugees face challenges access legal aid and other protection assistance, Lack of documentation places refugees at risk of arrest, detention and deportation,High proportion of PSNs), including women-headed households, UASC, PWD, survivors of SGBV, underlies and victims of trafficking, t physical protection issues linked with host community tensions and challenges with equitable access to public services,Community-based protection interventions are needed to support community outreach, information dissemination, SGBV awareness .Early marriage remains a major protection concern, many refugees able to access only informal or temporary labor opportunities. estimated 30% of urban refugees in are living in poverty, Scale-up of targeted refugee livelihoods programming is needed to i reduce household need for financial assistance and other service, Livelihoods opportunities available to refugees largely informal and temporary or high risk.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Through protection mainstreaming, GAH interventions aim to ensure ease of access by the targeted population to community based structures. This approach will be implemented throughout the project in a fair and just manner devoid of discrimination and will take into account challenges of mobility and access by intended target beneficiaries. Additionally, the enhancement of safe and reliable referral mechanisms and more specifically for persons with special needs will be given top priority. In the same breath, to tackle the challenging social cultural barriers affecting GBV survivors from accessing services and assistance, GAH intends to adopt intense and inclusive awareness raising sessions with the involvement of all stakeholders so as to bring to fore and sustain much needed advocacy necessary to addressing GBV related concerns within the communities. through involvement of local partners. By focusing on activities including risk reduction, it is hoped that targeted communities’ level of resilience, awareness and capacity when faced with humanitarian hardships will be enhanced. As a strategy for sustainability, the inclusion of and focus on strengthening local partnerships and existing national systems also aims to boost mainstreaming of protection and GBV into essential services proposed for a more comprehensive, focused and sustainable result.The project will deliver humanitarian assistance in a manner that does not expose people to further harm, reduces risk exposure, and ensures the safety and dignity of individuals. To be effective, community engagement must be based on two-way dialogue and ensure that accurate and timely information is shared with affected communities and that their feedback and participation are integrated into the humanitarian response. the project will enhance gender and gender mainstreaming in Food Security and Livelihoods interventions. Agricultural interventions and will focus on ensuring zero impact on the environment, while at the same time promoting environmentally friendly, water and energy initiatives.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

As an organization that places protection at its forefront to wards program interventions, GAH ensures that all its implemented interventions across all projects applying the principles of offering services with dignity and conflict sensitivity. Through the use of technical skills, and experience to support this project, a complaint and feedback mechanisms will be established to ensure the satisfaction level of the beneficiaries. GAH MEAL team will work alongside program staff through conducting focus group discussions and community meetings aimed at sharing project information with direct and indirect target groups and collecting their views. All feedback and complaints will be logged into a Complaints and Response system, which will then be analyzed and utilized for project management and planning. In addition, GAH continues to apply the PSEA and child protection policy across all of its interventions.This is aimed at ensuring the needs and concerns for affected persons are addressed in a dignified and accountable manner.
