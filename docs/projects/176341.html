Project Summary

DRC proposing to implement a rapid WASH response that aims at providing WASH/GBV services to vulnerable in need populations of South Sudan through a time sensitive integrated WASH assessments and responses that aim to mitigate against WASH related protection concerns including but not limited to Gender Based Violence (GBV).
DRC aims to increase household’s access to lifesaving WASH services that increase households’ access to safe water, adequate sanitation facilities, and improve their knowledge of best hygiene practices and COVID-19 prevention/mitigation measures. Using its mobile response approach with competent and reputable mobile emergency WASH team operational out of Juba, DRC will be rapidly responding to cover various hard to reach and newly affected areas across South Sudan. DRC will respond through regular coordination with Emergency preparedness and response (EP&R) group, WASH cluster, and the Needs Analysis Working Group (NAWG) priority locations.
Throughout this intervention, DRC will focus on the centrality of protection, ensuring the safety and dignity of persons in communities affected by conflict/disaster and displacement. In addition, a do no harm approach will be maintained throughout the project cycle and DRC will ensure accountability to affected populations through equal participation of all the members of the community.

1 - Needs

South Sudan becomes the world’s newest country and the 193rd country recognized by the United Nations where years of prolonged conflicts, chronic vulnerabilities and inadequate access to essential services continue to impact negatively on the lives of the civil population in South Sudan. According to the 2021 HNO out of 12.1M total population in South Sudan 8.3 million people in South Sudan are estimated to need humanitarian assistance in 2021. A total of 72 counties face extreme needs while five are in severe need. This situation doesn’t seem to have changed or improved since the outbreak of Covid-19 which has had far reaching effects globally with South Sudan unexceptional given her dependency on Humanitarian support for most basic services. Due to COVID-19 outbreak, the non-oil revenue growth declined by 14 per cent from 2019 to 2020. COVID-19, sub-national violence, and flooding continue to strain access to the very limited basic services. COVID-19 mitigating measures, limited primary data collection delayed the usual assessment of needs during the lean season, when people’s needs are highest. Remote methods, including key informant interviews were used to safely collect information on humanitarian needs, where according to HNO 2021, 1.6M people are internally displaced. An additional 2.2 million South Sudanese are refugees in neighboring countries.Flooding along the Nile affected over one million people in 43 counties, and damaged homes, crops and infrastructure. According to OCHA in her South Sudan Humanitarian snapshot of Nov 2020, an estimated 1 Million persons have been affected by floods since July with about 480,000 people displaced. Access to the people in flood-affected locations, particularly to those in hard-to-reach areas, remained challenging due to impassable roads, flooded airstrips and insecurity. With a combination of both manmade and natural disasters as well as global pandemic of COVID-19 effect, the population is expected to experience a dire humanitarian situation. Living standard and wellbeing of an estimated 5.6 million people will be negatively impacted by lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices in 2021. According to 2021 HNO , an estimated 5.9 million people are in need WASH services. Lack of access to clean water increases the likelihood of waterborne diseases and malnutrition. Poor living conditions, especially for the displaced, weaken people’s health and security and affect their dignity where only 35% people having access to safe water. Some 70 per cent of households’ members reported being affected by a water or vector-borne disease. Lack of access to water has led to tension and conflict between communities over competition of already scarce resources. 41% of HH access their water from an unimproved water source. The highest proportion of HHs relying on surface water are in Upper Nile, followed by Jonglei and Central Equatoria. Every fifth person is unable to collect enough water for drinking, and every third reports insufficient access to water for hand washing. New health-related WASH needs arose in 2020 as a result of COVID-19 and will persist into 2021 as a vaccination campaign is not expected to reach a sufficient number of people soon, implying a greater need for functional hand washing facilities in areas of high density such as the urban areas and PoC/IDP settlements.77% of HHs have no access to latrines. Only 17% of HHs have a latrine in their compound and 4% have a communal or shared latrine while in 34 counties less than 10% of HHs reported having access to latrines. Four in five IDPs and returnees live in settlements with evidence of open defecation. At the country level, open defecation is practiced by 73% of the population. 15% of HH has at least one disabled family member.

2 - Project activities

DRC will implement WASH activities using emergency rapid WASH response strategy to ensure safe, equitable and dignified access to WASH services. DRC will align project activities in line with the cluster objectives 2.1 and 2.2. Through the mobile response approach, DRC will conduct protection integrated WASH needs assessments and analysis in affected areas which will in turn determine a needs-based response in those affected locations. Assessments will be conducted using the WASH cluster designed assessment tools while employing techniques including Focus group discussion (FGDs), key informant interview (KIIs), direct observations and service mapping to gather information. The deployment locations will be selected in consultation with the WASH EP&R team.DRC will increase access to safe drinking water by repairing non functional water point including water quality testing and disinfection of boreholes as required. To ensure sustainability, DRC will provide hand pump repair tool kits and as well conduct refresher training to water user committees (WUCs) of the repaired bores in order to build their capacity on basic maintenance and management of their water points. In locations affected/prone to seasonal floods or using unprotected water source, DRC will provide point of use water treatment products like PUR and Aqua tabs in order to ensure HHs disinfect their drinking water as sources of water are presumed contaminated during floods or other contamination. DRC will distribute WASH NFIs comprising of jerrycans, buckets and soap and menstrual hygiene management kit to women and girls of reproductive age.Depending on the outcome of the assessment, DRC will construct gender segregated communal semi-permanent latrines equipped with handwashing stations. The number of stances per response location being determined by the magnitude of the need. DRC will ensure that any latrines constructed are culturally appropriate and equipped with doors, sturdy internal locks, privacy fencing and other safety measures to mitigate against GBV risks.In an effort to increase community awareness on best hygiene practices and COVID 19 prevention measure, DRC will conduct a gender sensitive community wide hygiene promotion in the affected areas using a house to house hygiene promotion approach. DRC will conduct gender-sensitive hygiene promotion and risk information dissemination of key messaging and IEC materials to mitigate the risk of COVID-19 infection. Community will be encouraged to adopt COVID related risk mitigation measures including social distancing, hand washing and NO touch policy as well as covid-19 related fact messages such as signs and symptoms and WHO preventive measures. Additionally, jerry-can cleaning campaigns will be conducted at water points to ensure community members practice routine cleaning of their water collection and storage containers in order to promote a safe water chain. In an effort to alleviate the challenges created by loss of property exacerbated by lost livelihoods during crises, DRC will distribute WASH NFIs to support HHs hygiene practices. Also considering the challenges of menstrual hygiene management during emergencies, the project will provide MHM kit to women and girls of reproductive age with high priority given to school going girls.In mitigating GBV, MRT WaSH and protection team will conduct GBV service mapping in target locations and in consultation with GBV SC, to map out available services and enhance referrals. In providing case management services DRC will also ensure that GBV referral systems are established and/or strengthened and that GBV survivors are linked to available services and support. DRC will also train all frontline staff on safe, dignified quality referrals to promote referral of survivors of protection incidents. As per DRC PSEA policy, PSEA trainings will be done to all staff and information provided to POCs about staff’s behavior and reporting mechanisms for PSEA.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Being a protection centered organization, DRC will ensure that awareness is created through information dissemination using community mobilisers and display of banners communicating messages against PSEA. For instance, messages like ″no sex for humanitarian support″. DRC will ensure protection mainstreaming across its programming and seek to reduce/mitigate protection risks that may be incurred during the course of the project. Examples, of steps that will be taken by DRC include engagement of beneficiary members around selection of the locations for latrine construction and screening of locations with a GBV risk assessment

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

DRC has several entry points for lodging complaints comprising of in-person office visits, Let us know box (suggestion box) and through community visits (either orally at or formal written complaints, at both the FCRM desks or to other DRC staff), and through intermediaries working with DRC e.g. implementing partners and community representatives.. Apart from the aforementioned channels, DRC will also gather feedback from the vulnerable groups through community meetings and surveys. For the illiterate – oral methods like meetings, FGDs to collect complaints and give feedback, complaints desk, community leaders DRC will sensitize leaders or communities to bring forward and identify them as beneficiaries, including them in surveys/PDM/Assessments and getting their views.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

• Community will be involved during needs assessments to provide the appropriate space to communicate information on what the project intends to do and how they can provide complains and feedback to in regards to the project.• DRC will conduct post implementation monitoring and in so doing, questions regarding initial communication of project information are incorporated to check whether beneficiaries are aware of the project that took place in their community.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

• DRC has a functional Feedback, Complaints, Response Mechanism (FCRM) that allows beneficiaries to give feedback and complaints regarding the activities being implemented in their respective locations. The DRC F-CRM desks are set up to receive complaints and questions from beneficiaries and follow-up with feedback, including referrals to external service-providers or to the DRC protection desks when appropriate, either immediately or within a reasonable timeframe, depending on the nature of the complaint. DRC has a standardized FCRM form where all feedback and complaints are captured. All data captured on the FCRM forms is then uploaded onto a password protected database where all feedback and complaints are tracked until they are closed. Furthermore, beneficiary complaints and feedback will also be captured when conducting post distribution monitoring (PDM). Beneficiaries will be able to provide their opinions on how DRC interventions can be improved and they will be given the opportunity to provide feedback or make a complaint.
