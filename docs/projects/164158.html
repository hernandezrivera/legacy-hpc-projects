Project Summary

According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2020, 10,7 million people in Syria are in need of WASH assistance, with 3 million in acute need . This represents the largest need by sector in the Whole of Syria, with the most severe needs concentrated in areas of ongoing conflict or areas with large numbers of IDPs. As a result, to address the needs of the most vulnerable households, this intervention will focus on providing targeted and effective WASH support in Northwest and Northeast Syria, areas that witnessed a large influx of IDPs, combining elements of emergency response with the establishment of durable solutions. Indeed, immediate interventions will be provided through the distribution of WASH NFI kits and the establishment of emergency water treatment unit, aiming at meeting the most pressuring needs of the most vulnerable. These interventions will be paired with an improved access to sustainable safe water sources, with a particular effort to offer sustainable alternatives to water trucking through the rehabilitation of WASH assets, such as water stations, and boreholes. An effective exit strategy will also be ensured by working closely with local stakeholders, building their capacity to operate and maintain rehabilitated infrastructure. Furthermore, water quantity and quality will be monitored to meet SPHERE standards, enhancing the access to safe water. Lastly, community sensitization on water conservation, usage, and hygiene will be conducted, contributing to decrease Water Born Diseases (WBD) concerns. This intervention will benefit a total of approximately 373,760 individuals, 93,418 in Northeast and 280,252 in Northwest Syria respectively.

4 - Needs

In Northwest Syria, in-camps, as of November 2019, there are over 135,326 IDPs residing in the Northern and 35,484 IDPs residing in the western camps cluster. An assessment conducted by ACTED, highlighted significant needs regarding WASH inside the settlements, as all the camp residents rely entirely on humanitarian assistance, including water trucking and the operation/maintenance of pre-existing water distribution points, sanitation services and waste management services, to meet their basic WASH needs and having dignified living conditions. With regards to the situation off-camps, it has been estimated that almost 50% of the households are at high risks of using water from untrusted sources and encounter challenges in accessing safe water.In Northeast Syria, in-camps, after October 2019, the number of IDPS in Ar-Raqqa, Deir-ez-Zor and Al-Hasake governorates rapidly increased, with 70,590 individuals who remained displaced by December 2019. In the latter is located Areesheh camp where ACTED is acting as the Camp Management entity and where, in October 2019, 5,033 IDPs were relocated from Mabrooka camp, causing an additional strain on already limited services and facilities. Overall, across the three governorates, individuals are experiencing minimal functional WASH infrastructures, as reported by REACH assessments conducted in 2019. In fact, a critical high percentage of respondent households suffered from lack of access to safe water, and poor sanitation and waste management conditions.This is particularly true for women and girls; most affected by the negative physical and mental well-being impact of disruptions of services in IDP sites. Indeed, substandard conditions of WASH facilities are particularly concerning for women and girls, due to greater risks of sexual harassment, sexual violence, humiliation, physical violence and exploitation they face, and adoption of negative menstrual hygiene behavior, as well as for people with disabilities and older people

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Non-inclusion of highly vulnerable groups and minorities needs during project design/implementationLikelihood: Low – Impact: HighMitigation measure: The proposed intervention will be implemented in accordance with ACTED’s gender approach, which follows the Gender Handbook for Humanitarian Action reviewed in 2017 by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC). ACTED incorporates gender analysis and considerations into the full project management cycle, from project design to implementation and monitoring. During the community sensitisation, ACTED encourages the identification of special needs of most vulnerable groups including women, children, elderly, or people living with disabilities and conducts FGDs and/or community meetings prior to implementation. ACTED addresses where possible gender and protection concerns. In areas out of the scope of programming or expertise, ACTED relies on referral procedures to specialized agencies.Risk 2: Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuseLikelihood: Low – Impact: HighMitigation measure: All ACTED staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct which directly refers to the Protection of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and to Gender Based Violence (GBV). As part of on-going efforts to improve accountability, ACTED is committed to implement a beneficiary Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its projects to allow beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on ACTED work, as well as the work of the contractors, and to relay any arising issue, including potential PSEA or GBV issue.
