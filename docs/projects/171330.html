Project Summary

Unreliable access to a temporary and/or inadequate WASH facility remains an important feature of the humanitarian landscape in Somalia. In 2020, over 4.6 million people (1.16 million IDPs and 3.36 million host communities) don’t have sufficient quantity of water and adequate sanitation (HNO 2020). This dire situation is even worsened by the COVID19 pandemic which require more water for frequent handwashing for prevention and containment. Schools in Somalia are reopening and due to COVID19, a number of schools are requesting urgent water supply for both students consumption and to support frequent handwashing.
The water supply coverage in Somalia is strongly dependent on rainfall performance. In the last two rainy seasons namely 2019 Dayr and 2020 Gu, the performance of rains has improved slightly (SWALIM/FAO). From 2017 onwards, the water coverage has shown slight improvement due to improved rain performance. The National WASH Cluster estimates 59% as having sufficient drinking water with 66% of those with access walk less than 15 minutes for a water point and 20% having challenges to access the minimum quantity of safe water. (JMCNA ). 48% access their own improved latrines, while 52% use either unimproved latrines or practicing open defecation (22%) posing a huge public health risk. Furthermore, 2% used soap with water at latrines, 52% use soap at household level while 40% use other substitute for handwashing. 45% of women/girls access MHM materials.
On the other hand, Oxfam needs assessment conducted late 2019 in Sool, Sanaag and Togdheer regions indicates that over 40% of both IDP and host communities rely on purchasing water from commercial trucks. There is no well-organized and reliable government data that shows the coverage of water, sanitation, and hygiene in Somalia. In December 2019 and January 2020, Oxfam and FAO/SWALIM conducted a WASH inventory in all regions of Somaliland. The inventory focused on sustainable and strategic water sources in the country including boreholes, springs, and protected shallow wells. The functionality of these sources is critical. However, the current functionality rate is estimated to be much below 70% and decreases as the stress level goes up during the drought period. The analysis of the final report is not yet finalized but expected to inform the sector on ways to increase functionality and reduce interruption of services. The COVID19 outbreak has increased the need for water to support frequent handwashing practices and this will further stress the few existing water sources.
People living in the riverine areas in Somalia and other areas adjusted to seasonal rivers are affected by floods. The worst most recent flash-floods were reported from the Shabelle river basin particularly in Beletwayn town, Hiraan Region, and Qardho town of Puntland State. Until the end of May 2020, over 412,000 people are displaced by the floods (UNOCHA, Flood Bulletin). The capacity of regional states and the federal government is limited only to response instead of constructing large scale flood protection structures.
Somalia is one of the worst hit Horn of African countries by COVID19. So far, over to 3,864 cases and 99 deaths are recorded. However, the figures are believed to be much higher since testing capacity is limited to only few major towns. The situation in Somalia is different compared to other countries with a strong governance system in the horn of Africa. Only 31% of people in Somalia reported having a functional handwashing facility with soap (WASH Cluster, March 2020). This estimate is based on an assessment conducted before the Covid19 lockdown. The federal government and humanitarian agencies confirmed the increased market price of soap and other detergents and 11% contraction of national GDP in the country. Covid19 prevention requires frequent handwashing and social distancing which both are difficult in Somalia context.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Oxfam is one of the global partners leading the CASH and WASH innovation and advocacy. The JIMMI report findings indicate that the demand side of the curve is affected by the triple threat facing Somalia which also has an impact on the supply of essential items in the market. CVA enables communities to have a purchasing power of materials from the existing market instead of getting items from other markets which hugely affect the performance and reliability of local markets.Since 2018, Oxfam has been implementing cash for water approach which was evaluated several times. The PDM report indicates that over 60% of beneficiaries preferring this option and the remaining 40% preferred the same approach provided that the supply side of the market is supported. Thus, Oxfam started working, organizing and training women group to create safe water distribution points with all necessary storage and a business plan put in place. This approach helps communities to have a sustained market based supply of water even after the operation is seized, gives ownership of different items deployed for the operations and continue the services by empowering women. Oxfam will continue to scale CVA in 2021 by piloting cash in hygiene kits, MHM kits, water and sanitation. Each trail will be done carefully and lessons will be documented well for learning purposes.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

Providing people affected by emergency with cash enables them to fulfill their needs in a dignified manner and contributes to the local economy (UNHCR). Market is the critical player enabling the effectiveness of cash and voucher assistance. The needs of people affected by the emergencies are enormous and different in nature. Providing in kind assistants alone can’t suffice their needs. In the situation where cash and voucher systems are selected as the best approach, oxfam will conduct a detailed market assessment considering availability, effect and risk of opting VCA on the local markets.. A strategy will be developed to address the outcomes of the market assessment and addressing critical market supports in order to enhance its performance.

Mitigation Measures

Exclusion and marginalization: Oxfam has been working in Somalia for more than 40 years and has solid experience on how to avoid exclusion. We have developed strong community engagement tools and Oxfam teams are well trained on use of this tools. Community engagement will enable communities to understand the importance of not living anyone behind including marginalized groups, women and girls and will trigger communities to conduct detail community profiling. During the registrations, Oxfam will ensure to use the community profiling outcome, set criteria for selection and monitor the use of those criteria at all level. Interference of external stakeholders in impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries: Somalia communities have their own traditional means of sharing resources based on clans. It requires a careful analysis of the structures and norms of sharing resources within the communities. Oxfam community engagement tools helps to understand such local experience and to identify the best scenario of allocation between the IDPs and host communities. Oxfam will use the findings of this CE exercise and use in the advantage of ensuring equitable distribution of resources between the IDP/host communities.Protection mainstreaming: If distribution of resources are not handled well, communities are not aware of the selection criteria and reason for selection of beneficiaries, it highly possible to create tension within communities and trigger violence. Oxfam has guideline and set of procedures that gives a clear road map in community consultation, community led selection criteria, engages with community structures including religious and clan leaders and support communities based registration process. The verification process, again, independently confirms the process followed and suitability of the beneficiaries using the selection criteria’s. The outcome is dealt with strong engagement of the communities and distribution dates and place is agreed. In case distribution involves gender specific items like MHM – a separate women specific distribution with training is organized and monitored. This helps to reduce the risk and increase ownership at community level.
