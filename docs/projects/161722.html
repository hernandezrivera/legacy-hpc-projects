Project Summary

According to the 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview, An estimated 5.2 million persons are in critical need of protection. This includes 1.81 million IDPs (80 % of whom are women and children); 1.64 million returnees (IDPs and refugees, of whom 54 % are female); 823,000 civilians in inaccessible areas; and 0.9 million individuals from host communities. Female-headed households and child headed households, unaccompanied and separated children and adolescent boys and girls face heightened protection risks, including GBV, child recruitment and SEA (Sexual Exploitation and Abuse). Groups with specific needs include the elderly; the chronically ill; persons with physical and mental disabilities; and pregnant and lactating women.
Children under 18 years of age constitute 61 per cent of the IDPs, returnee and other affected populations; one out of two affected children are girls (55 %). Out of 3.2 million people in need.
In the WASH Sector, 3.6 million People need support to meet their basic water, sanitation and hygiene needs across the BAY States. The main drivers of vulnerabilities related to WASH are closely linked to the conflict, including recurrent, unpredictable and continuous population displacements. Historically low levels of access to WASH services in the areas because of chronic underdevelopment have also contributed to the current situation.
In the BAY States. Over 80% of internally displaced people are in Borno State and it’s the most affected state. Maiduguri Metropolitan Council host the largest numbers of IDPs as most of the people living in other LGAs have moved to Maiduguri to take refuge due to the high level of security in the City.
SOS Children’s Villages Nigeria will be working to support these people based on the needs identified in the Humanitarian Needs overview for protection and WASH sector. This will ensure that we align our intervention and be able to contribute to the overall strategic objective and the targets of the 2019 to 2021 HRP plan. In the Child Protection Sector, SOSCVN will setup a child friendly space and support 600 Children with Psychosocial Support and Case Management, build capacity of 100 care givers (including Men and Women) on Positive Parenting skill and set up a community structure know as key implementing partner/ Child protection committee and build their capacity to protect and care for children in the community.
For the WASH Sector, SOSCVN will support the community with sanitation materials, which includes rake, brooms, shovel, wheelbarrows, etc. for regular cleaning exercise in the community. Refurbished two existing nor functional manual water borehole and construct two new ones in the Community, support One hundred household (600 Persons) with hygiene kits and replenished kits quarterly, train 30 Persons, including men, women, Boys and Girls as Hygiene promoters and support them to carry out house to house hygiene sensitization within the community. and build capacity of the Key Implementing partner on good hygiene practices which include, proper waste disposal, methods, hand washing etc. We will work with relevant partners and stakeholders, especially Government, Child Protection and WASH Sector and other actors on referrers of cases that required further interventions.

1 - Needs

According to the 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview, Family separation (affecting 52 per cent girls), abduction and recruitment by armed groups (affecting 57 per cent girls), among other grave child rights violations and sexual violence have directly affected at least 49,500 girls and boys (51 per cent girls)75. Additionally, girls and boys affected are increasingly at risk of being engaged in hazardous forms of labour. More than 2 million girls, boys and caregivers need psychosocial support services due to severe distress, hardship and displacement. Girls are facing heightened risk of child marriage as parents have increasingly indicated using child marriage specifically as a coping mechanism to protect a girl’s honor against the widespread sexual violence perpetrated by armed groups, while girls have also indicated the fear of sexual assault or early pregnancy as a reason to get married early. At least 150,000 caregivers of vulnerable children, particularly female-headed households, foster carers and single caregivers with several children, require psychosocial or socio-economic support to assist them to better cater for children in their care. In the WASH Sector, Historically low levels of access to WASH services as a result of chronic underdevelopment have contributed to the current situation. Ongoing and protracted displacement puts an additional stress on existing and already limited WASH services and facilities, and with congested populations in displacement sites, waterborne diseases spread rapidly. Cholera outbreaks have struck each year: in 2018, the cholera outbreak has affected 18 LGAs with a total of 4,250 cases104 and 1.5 million people due to poor sanitation and good hygiene practices including access to portable water. Flooding, fierce winds and sand storms have resulted in substantial damage of WASH infrastructure. In 2018, at least 20 per cent and 28 per cent of IDPs living in camps in Borno and Adamawa states respectively have been seriously affected as many latrines, showers, handwashing points and solar panels were partially or totally destroyed, affecting access to WASH facilities and services.
