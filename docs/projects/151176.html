Project Summary

The proposed intervention will focus on the three food security cluster objectives that is to improve immediate access to food for vulnerable IDP households through provision of conditional and unconditional assistance depending on the severity of food insecurity phases, vulnerability and seasonality of the livelihoods. The project will also protect and restore livelihoods, related food and income sources, through provision of seasonally appropriate livelihood inputs and technical support to host communities in rural and peri-urban settings. And lastly the proposed project will Support household and community resilience to shocks, through rehabilitation and/or restoration of productive assets, enhance livelihood strategies and disaster preparedness.
The proposed intervention will be implemented in Bari, Gedo, Nugaal and Sool Region. the community members will participate effectively in the implementation of the project as they had been part of the gap identification process. Communities will be briefed regarding unconditional cash and Cash for work interventions and use of vouchers to redeem food. They will also help in the selection of cash for work and food vouchers committees that will develop the criteria for beneficiary selection.
The Red rose digital platform will be used to register beneficiaries and confirm project location using GPRS especially inaccessible areas ,in cash for work intervention the platform will be used to monitor the working hours of beneficiaries to ensure that they are paid according to the working hours and also confirm construction/rehabilitation was implemented in the target locations .on cash transfer the platform will be used to register beneficiaries, and distribute food stuff to beneficiaries using registered vendors. all cash transfer beneficiaries will be required to have a mobile phone that has mobile money transfer application to receive money
The Action has been designed based on various needs assessments by the partners to ensure that the most needed form of support reaches the most vulnerable people. The Action will be implemented in coordination with other humanitarian actors, as well as local and national authorities

Needs

The 2018 humanitarian situation caused by drought, displacement, conflict and seasonal floods in Somalia remains fragile even with the collaborative efforts of aid agencies , government authorities and support from donors .According to FEWSNET even with the recent climatic improvement and large-scale humanitarian assistance, acute food insecurity is prevalent among Somali communities who lost most of their animals and became destitute during the 2016-2017 severe drought, those affected by flooding in April and May 2018 and others who were affected by large-scale and protracted displacement due to a combination of conflict and natural hazards. These population groups are expected to face large food consumption gaps and need humanitarian assistance to prevent further deterioration of their food security and nutrition situation. In the Northern Inland Pastoral of Sool and Sanaag regions in the absence of humanitarian response over 1.5 million people will face Crisis or worse (IPC Phases 3 or higher) between now and December 2018 . According to HADMA report, an estimated 53,000 destitute pastoral households in Northern inland pastoral zones (Sool and Nugaal regions ) are in need of humanitarian assistance and most of these destitute pastoralist have moved to IDP camps across the region in Puntland. In Riverine Pump Irrigation livelihood zone in Hiiran and Gedo. More than 1.5 million people will face Crisis or worse (IPC Phase 3 or higher) through December 2018. An additional 3.1 million people are classified as Stressed (IPC Phase 2), which brings the total number of people in Somalia facing acute food insecurity through December 2018 to 4.6 million. Those facing acute food security Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse urgently need humanitarian assistance and livelihood support through December 2018 to prevent food security deterioration. Livelihood support is also required for people categorized as Stressed (IPC Phase 2).

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The proposed plan will Build the resilience and livelihood of the displaced households this will lead to protection of environment, displaced persons are already engaged in different forms of income generating activities some of which may not be sustainable, for example charcoal burning, firewood collection etc. in order to ensure protection of environment, the displaced households must be supported to develop diversified and sustainable positive livelihoods that will contribute to improved resilience. the project will also construct bunds and terraces to conserve water and soil in the target areas. 2- Is the project linked to government/community systems, or to longer-term interventions? the project will work closely with the government and community structures. The proposed project will educate the stakeholders and community on DRR and how to integrate disaster risk reduction into livelihoods activities hence a step towards enhancing the capacity of both community and government representative. The training will also strengthen the capacities of the local community to mainstream community managed disaster risk reduction concepts, principles and practices in the planning and implementation of community development efforts The rehabilitated community assets after completion will be managed by the community and supervised/ rehabilitated by the local government To ensure sustainability, the proposed plan will establish early warning and action centers in the community, the centers will be managed by community DRR committee. the role and responsibility of the community DRR committee will be to sensitize community of possible disaster and help community prepare on how to cope with the disaster. The rehabilitated/constructed communal assets will be handed back to community management committee with supervision of local government to ensure that the assets are accessed by all community groups (women, children and youths). the cash for work beneficiaries will be linked to the private sectors and CSO’s in the regions to access new construction job opportunities. NCA will engage rights holders and stakeholders at all levels and stages of project implementation to ensure their participation. During community mobilization and sensitization, NCA will provide information to the targeted communities about their entitlement /benefits. NCA will conduct consultative meetings with the relevant government officials and work closely with existing community leaders and representative bodies (Village Committees) especially in providing the criteria for beneficiary selection which will be translated into the local language

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Drought, conflict and floods are the main recurring risks facing the target population, when these calamities occur they trigger massive displacement of people and further exposes the community to serious protection threats and negative coping mechanism. This leads to deterioration of communities’ resilience and social fragmentation. In order to address this the proposed plan will continue to involve target beneficiaries in the project cycle where project planning, implementation and monitoring of activities and results will be done with concerted efforts of the target beneficiaries, NCA and local authorities in target locations. Through community mobilization and awareness sessions, Target communities will be given the platform to influence decisions about the services and the interventions they get through community consultations, gender sensitivity and though community participation and involvement. NCA will work closely with the existing local administration and community structures such as religious leaders, clan and community elders, minorities’ women group and youth networks, men, women, girls and boys from IDPs community to accommodate the most vulnerable communities and will ensure inclusiveness, accountability and transparency. NCA will adhere to the internal control procedures that ensure value for money is achieved through out the procurement processes. NCA will be responsible for back donor communication including reporting. NCA and partners will actively participate and report on project activities during the regional cluster meetings as well as the Nairobi meetings. Through the NCA complains handling mechanisms. Beneficiaries will at will be able to share any concerns or provide feedback on areas of improvement to the organization for improved project management and coordination.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Women and children are the worst affected by displacement caused by natural and man-made disasters. the Somalia customary system is based on a clear gender and age division, women and youth have unequal access to resources ,opportunities ,power and decision. During emergencies women and youth are not involved. The project will make deliberate effort to maintain a protective environment at community level so that girls and women in particular are not exposed to harm such as sexual and gender based violence while undertaking their daily chores. Further, gender equity will be observed while implementing all project activities. Men, women, Boys and Girls will be treated equally with their different needs being targeted and support provided accordingly. Women will be fronted to participate in decision making structures like the community selection community (CSC ) and be involved in project monitoring efforts.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The impact of armed conflict and violence will be in minimal, the project intervention will focus on areas where there is no security threat, currently the regions security is relative calm but in case of worst scenario NCA has a security plan in place so that its staff are not exposed to exaggerated risks and can quickly be moved to safer areas. Security focal points at field level and proper communication strategy have been established. Also, NCA regularly follows the security updates provided by the NGO Security Program (NSP
