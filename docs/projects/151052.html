Project Summary

In Somalia a huge percentage of the population still relies on humanitarian health
assistance with the number increasing. 41% of Somalia’s population need life saving
humanitarian assistance (OCHA ). Approximately 3.3 million women, girls, boys and men
need access to emergency health care.Neonatal, infant and child mortality remain high
with communicable diseases as leading causes. AWD/cholera is prevalent enhanced by
poor WASH. High maternal mortality rate remains contributed by limited antenatal care
(1/4 of women), the low proportion of births attended by skilled medical personnel (1/3)
and the inadequate facilities for emergency maternal care. Insecurity, poor health seeking
practices, lack of functioning referral systems, limited access to health services and
underlying malnutrition pose major challenges for control and prevention of disease
outbreaks.The in􀂢ux of IDPs continues to increase their vulnerably and reduces access to
health services decreasing their resilience as majority prefer urban centers.
SAF UK intends to provide lifesaving assistance and strengthen community level resilience. The organization will intervene by providing basic and emergency health services through 5 camp based and 4 mobile clinics in IDP camps in kismayo and Kahda, and OPD in all areas. The organization also seeks to establish a CTC in rural areas of Hobyo and Jariban where AWD cases are on the increase and maintain the one in Dalxiska Kismayo.The organization will also step up EPI in all areas, through health facilities and mobile teams. We also intend to improve birth assisted by skilled birth attendants through our MCH facilities and enhance ANC and PNC integrating with EPI and Nutrition services. Strong social mobilization for use of health services will continue.We will also focus of improving capacity of both health and community based staff (Men and women)in provision of quality services.

Needs

In Somalia a huge percentage of the population still relies on humanitarian health assistance with the number increasing. 41% of Somalia’s population needs life saving humanitarian assistance (OCHA ). Majority of women, girls,boys and men in target area need access to emergency health care. Neonatal, infant and child mortality remain high with communicable diseases as leading cause. AWD/cholera is prevalent enhanced by poor WASH. High maternal mortality rate contributed by limited antenatal care (1/4 of women), low proportion of births attended by skilled attendants (1/3), limited facilities for emergency maternal care. Insecurity, poor health seeking practices, poor functioning referral systems, limited access to health services and underlying malnutrition pose major challenges to control and prevention of diseases.The influx of IDPs continues to increase their vulnerably, reduces access to health services, decreasing their resilience and increasing need for protection interventions. Kismayo has a rapidly growing population due to immigration in search of aid . Therefore Health services, especially in IDP,are limited. AWD/cholera is prevalent with poor WASH at its back drop, few women deliver with skilled assistance due to cost, ignorance and culture and morbidity and malnutrition rates , especially among under 5,remain high.Dhankenley(Kahda) hosts the most vulnerable and protracted IDP in Mogadishu, most facing multiple displacement. IDP are increasing due to in-migration straining limited service. Health seeking behavior is poor though services are available,vaccination coverage is low and SAM rates are highest due disease Hobyo and Jariban have poor coverage of functional health services, and high morbidity rates. Nutrition situation is critical, vaccination coverage low, WASH status very poor,rural areas are hard to reach with some communities marginalized.SAF UK intends to provide lifesaving assistance and strengthen community level resilience.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

For IDP the main risk is displacement. for this the organisation hasmaintained a database for all bene􀂡ciaries with telephone contacts and that ofrelatives. For those in rural Mudug the risk is spontenious clan clashes. While it isdi􀂣cult to deal with this, we have ensured that services are provided for the differentclans in their areas equitably. Interclan clashes therefore have minimal effect ourprograms. in all location the organisation will seek to provide safety nets byimplementing BSFP . Victims of GBV will also be provided with an organisational GBVfocal point who will link survivors to appropriate assistance. Programs will also ensureIDp have access to basic services in an equitable and digni􀂡ed manner with equalityalso observed in job allocation in projects serving IDP population. Among IDP the riskof exclusion based on tribe is real. Most IDPs in Kismayo are from a certaincommunity who found it hard to access services in the recent past based on their tribeand skin color. The organisations' policy does not allow exclusion and actuallyreaches out to all community members to receive services. Recruitment of staff is notbased on tribe but professional competence. This has produced staff from a hybrid ofcultures who have learned to work together well. Some positions are also reserved tothe target communities and community groups that are marginalized like IDPs andwomen so that equity is achieved

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

All gender will be awarded opportunity to benefit in equitably. Women and youth whoare mostly left out in recruitment will be considered in all projects and positions will beawarded equitably. Needs assessment will include all community groups so that an allconclusive, agreeable and fair assessment is reached. both men and women leaderswith an additional youth representative will be part of community health board as perorganisational policy. This will ensure decisions made are all inclusive. SAF UKpolicies have sought to put men at the helm of needs affecting their wives, daughtersand female relatives. The organisation's APP framework seeks to ensure menparticipate through a􀂣rmative action for men ensuring that men are not excludedfrom interventions targeting women beneficiaries. Women , especially in IDP face multiple protection related risks, some whose effects are hard to reverse. The organisation has recently ammednded relevant policies to put protection at the center of our interventions. The organisation proposed to bring integrated services closer to beneficiaries, especially women and children, so that they do not have to walk far to access services exposeing them to multiple hazards. A clear referral pathway for referring cases of GBV has been established in out APP framework, with a GBV focal point set in every program to facilitate this. Children who are often left out will not only be seen as beneficiaries but as part of the community. Their views on service delivery ad need will occasionally be sought by a professional child specialist so that the whole community participates in program design and implementation. Community diagnosis,community dialogues, evaluations and assessment will seek to include all community groups to ensure all are reached.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Both in Mogadisho and Kismayo security risk is minimized by ensuring all staff gothrough the internal security vetting every quarter and present a clearance letter fromministry of internal security. They must also get reference from a local leader like anelder or imam who is accountable. These must also be known by local elders fromtheir tribe who pledge accountability. This ensures that our staff are not related to anynon state actors planning violence. For those in rural Mudug, the risk is spontaneousclan clashes. While it is di􀂣cult to deal with this, we have ensured that services areprovided for the different clans in their geographical areas equitably. Inter clan clashestherefore have minimal effect to our programs. However after conversations withelders, it was agreed that all clans are served by the same staff regardless of their clanbackground and without discrimination and threats to ones life and property. This isslowly but surely contributing to cohesion among the various clans and a realizationof each others strengths.
