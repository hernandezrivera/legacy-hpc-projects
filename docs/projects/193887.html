Project Summary

The Overall Project Objective is ", Improving resilience and protection capacities of women/girls/men/boys at risk of GBV, including GBV survivors in northern West Bank". The project specific objective seeks to attain in recognition of the multi-dimensional challenges in protecting and improving livelihood of the women/girls/men/boys at risk of GBV, including GBV survivors in four governorates in northern West Bank Northern West Bank.
The project will help to, on one hand, enhance the financial capacities of the target group through cash assistance and supporting income generation activities to create licit sources of livelihood opportunities, in order to achieve economic empowerment, and break the cycle of economic dependence upon perpetrators who may otherwise continue to exploit the vulnerabilities of victims and at-risk groups. On the other hand, the project will build the capacities of the specialized and non-specialized GBV service providers GBV prevention and/or risk mitigation and/or response and PESA related aspects and raise public awareness to prevent gender-based violence or to publicize response mechanisms and PESA complaint mechanisms while play active role on GBV referral process. This will be Implemented through conducting trainings for service providers, CSOs and CBOs on PSEA mechanisms, in relation to mitigating GBV risks, as well as case management/ referrals capacity building. This shall mainstream PSEA concepts and response mechanisms in WB northern CBOs and CSOs, especially those with PWDs experience.
MSS works in 9 governorates through its coalition organizations, amongst which are the 4 targeted governorates in this proposed action. As for Nablus, MSS has several offices in the city along with askar and balata refugee camps, and several areas in south of Nablus, which are all classified as areas C such as, Huwara, beit furik, beita, and more. Working in the cities and surrounding villages will in coordination with partnering organizations and local councils, moreover, working in the refugee camps will also be through partnering organizations as well as UNRWA offices;

1 - Needs

According to the latest available Palestine Central Bureau of Statistics survey on violence, which predates the COVID-19 pandemic, 29.4 per cent of (married or previously married) women between the ages of 18 and 64 experienced violence in the 12 months preceding the survey – 37.5 per cent in Gaza and 24.3 per cent in the WB. Of those, 56.6 per cent experienced psychological violence, 17.8 per cent physical violence, 8.8 per cent sexual violence, 32.5 per cent social violence, 41.1 per cent economic violence, while eight per cent experienced the emerging issue of cyber violenceThe context in occupied Palestinian territory (oPt) has a detrimental impact on employment, income, and educational opportunities, forcing women and girls, including those with disabilities, to adopt negative coping strategies that expose them to further exploitation and abuse, including exposure to higher risks of sexual exploitation and abuse. According to MSNA Key Sectoral Findings - WB, July 2022, % of households employing crisis or emergency livelihood coping strategies in the 30 days prior to data collection was as follow: Reduced or ceased payments on utilities 21.8%, Used savings 15.7%, Reduced expenses on health 13.3%, Borrowed money to cover food needs 12.3%, Buying food/non-food items on credit (incurring debt) 8.9%, Sold household assets 2.6%, Children (under 15 years) worked 1.5%, Sold productive livestock 1.4%, Sold productive assets/means of transport 1.1%, Begged (for money or food) 0.9%, Moved to less expensive accommodation 0.6%, Reduced spending on disability-specific hygiene item 0.6%, and Sold house or land 0.4%. The prevalence of child marriage within the oPt is at 24 per cent. Although, in general, child early forced marriage seems to be declining, an increase has been perceived in certain areas, particularly parts of Gaza and isolated parts of Area C and East Jerusalem. Women and girls with disabilities, Bedouin women, internally displaced women and women and girls living in refugee camps are particularly at risk of violence. Being physically cut off from employment prospects and financially dependent on partners leaves them with few options for supporting themselves financially, and therefore they are more likely to marry, and not to report intimate partner violence if it means losing their only source of financial support.Women and girls in communities impacted by conflict and displacement, i.e. Gaza, Area C and East Jerusalem, tend to suffer from higher incidence of GBV, including more frequent incidents of intimate partner violence, sexual abuse and exploitation, and forced marriage. These communities also tend to be characterized by limited availability of and access to multi-sectoral protection services. The areas with the lowest percentage of households reporting availability of psycho-social services in case of GBV were East Jerusalem (8.9 per cent), Gaza (31.6 per cent) and Area C (38.8%).According to MSNA Key Cash and Markets Findings - WB, July 2022, across the WB, 21.2% of households reported a member of their household being unemployed and looking for work at the time of the data collection. The most frequently reported barriers to employment were structural - related to a lack of suitable livelihood opportunities. The impact of barriers related to employment and the lack of sufficient economic opportunities is perhaps echoed in the fact that taking on debt was observed to be a widespread practice, with 43.1% of households having any outstanding existing debt and 37.0% having taken on new debt in the 3 months prior to data collection. Male-headed households were more likely than female-headed households to report taking on debt for reasons related to major purchases, and livelihood or income-generating activities. For context, self-employment was reported as a primary income source by 30.0% of male-headed households compared to 10.4% of female-headed households. The above analysis has been confirmed during a continuous consultation processes run by Mothers’ School Society in different occasions, such as meetings with Communities Protection Committees in the targeted communities, training workshops and other occasions.

2 - Activities

A1: GBV-Cash for GBV: Providing cash assistance for 60 Girls and Women to mitigate potential risks of GBV including risks of being economically exposed, entering the circle of poverty. The project will provide mainly one-off payment for the targeted beneficiaries with possibility of repeating payment for one more time depending on the case. The amount will be tailored for each case; on average, it will be around 700 ILS per payment per beneficiary. The project will apply unrestricted cash assistance - unless the case requires the application of restricted modality - to address protection-related needs, the same applies in terms of conditionality of the cash assistance. MSS will coordinate its cash assistance activities with the CWG Chairs in order to get their further guidance on cash assistance. At the beginning of the project, MSS will develop specific Vulnerability criteria for delivering cash assistance, the delivery mechanism and other related aspects in consultation with the CWG Chairs and local committees. The cash assistance for 20 Girls and Women to cover medical bills and other immediate needs of GBV survivors such as shelter, NFI items, etc. A2: GBV-Support for income-generating opportunities: Providing in-kind and material support for 40 women - who are at risk of GBV and GBV survivors - to create start their own small-scale income generation project in order to achieve economic empowerment and break the cycle of economic dependence upon perpetrators who may otherwise continue to exploit the vulnerabilities of victims and at-risk groups. 10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Mothers School Society will utilize and update the existing “Do-No-Harm” analysis of the targeted communities. The analysis provides an in-depth understanding of gender dynamics in the targeted communities and how the planned project activities can be implemented without causing harm for beneficiaries. The current situation in the targeted communities is characterized by the lack of men engagement on female’s issues and weak understanding about the fact that VAWG is not a female issue but a social one in addition to application of dominant norms within masculinity practices.MSS believes that every person, regardless of age, religion, disability or origin, has the right to be protected from all forms of harm, abuse, bullying, neglect and exploitation. The organization has zero tolerance for abuse, harassment or exploitation, whether by or against our employees or individuals associated with it.MSS addresses the issue of protection through three pillars: prevention, reporting, and accountability.Therefore, the project aims to increase men involvement on female’s issues and mobilizers of other men and boys. Reduced resistance and facilitation of the safe participation and strengthened agency of women and girls. Feeling valuable actors as catalyzers of positive changes for women and girls, benefiting and contributing to the welfare of the communities. Increased understanding of harmful cultural norms practiced by men and disadvantages of existing rigid gender roles in access to justice related to VAWG and GBV cases as a starting point for them to reflect and change negative attitudes and behaviors against women and girls. The Society will put in place specific safeguards and protocols to protect the safety (both physical and psychological) of beneficiaries during collecting data ensure that the project does not result in further violation of their rights. This shall include, among other things, securing privacy and confidentiality of information - such as referral information – to ensure that the names of individuals consulted will not be made public; for those project activities that involves children (under 18 years old*) the project team shall consider the additional risks and will obtain parental consent before activities. Project Team are well-trained and will be further trained PSEA, collecting and handling sensitive information and specifically data relating to violence against women with non –judgmental/ non bias language Data collection tool must be designed in a way so as to be sensitive and aware of the sequence of questions in a manner that may lead to re- traumatization in incidents of violence and data collection tool does not ask direct questions about incidence of violence immediately and insensitively. RATHER framing questions in a manner that leave room that reinforces women’s own coping strategies The society designs and implements activities in a way that is culturally appropriate and does not create distress for beneficiaries; and considers t the appropriate time and place of activities to minimize risk to them. The project team are equipped with necessary information and able to provide information on how individuals in situations of risk can seek support (referrals to organizations that can provided counseling support and the complaint procedures).Moreover, the collected data are well preserved and secured in an electronic archive system protected with a username and a password, only used by the archive employee. The do no harm policy extends to cover the service providers, i.e. the organization’s staff, especially psycho-social expert. When dealing with GBV survivors or victims, the worker is also protected and made well aware of GBV related risks in dealing with case managements, referral and debriefing sessions. Moreover, in case of an altercation between the beneficiary’s family for example and the worker, there is a self care set system for our work to help relieve the stress they have
