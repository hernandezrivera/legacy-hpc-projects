Project Summary

An estimated 2.1 million children aged 6-59 months suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025, including 650,000 cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM).
Acute malnutrition projection October 2024 to march 2025 puts Kapoeta East County in IPC AMN phase 4 (Critical) The Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate is critically high, exceeding the WHO emergency threshold of 15% indicative of severe nutritional crises driven by factors such as food insecurity, poor water and sanitation conditions, draught and limited access to healthcare services. (Nutrition Cluster strategy 2024).
Terekeka County has been categorized under IPC AMN Phase 3 (IPC projection October 2024 to march 2025) with GAM rate of 17.2% (Nutrition data from SMART survey) indicating a serious public health concern. This high prevalence is attributed to food insecurity, poor access to health services, economic instability, suboptimal child practices, floods and WASH challenges.
In response to the high nutritional needs in the targeted locations, this project will increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative and curative nutrition-specific services for children, and women in targeted Counties, hence contributing to the overarching strategic objective of the humanitarian community.
In order to encounter the emergency Nutrition needs of 80903 individuals ( 29,350 girls, 27,402 boys , 19,651 Women, 4500 Men) 40% Terekeka County and 60% Kapoeta East County, Impact Health Organization proposed project will focus on meeting the nutrition cluster’s strategic plan and response objectives by; Prevention of malnutrition through provision of specialized food, Prevention of malnutrition through behavior change communication, Prevention of micronutrient deficiencies; Early detection and treatment of girls and boys under 5 years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition, Monitoring visit, Promotion of community participation for a responsive nutrition service delivery, Prevention, testing and treating for malaria among children under five years with acute malnutrition; Provision of WASH kits to children with SAM in nutrition sites; Establishing or maintaining demonstration kitchen garden at nutrition sites for or in the vicinity; Integration of child stimulation and ECD in the provision of care for children with SAM and MAM; Mainstreaming of child protection and GBV risk mitigation at nutrition sites. Capacity building and training of health workers, mother to mother support groups and nutrition volunteers. The activities will be implemented through both static and mobile/outreach interventions. IHO will work closely with communities to identify the problem affecting them and the community will greatly participate development and implementation of activities and community shall provide feedback on the progress of the interventions. The project will build capacities of the communities through training and awareness campaigns designed to halt the spread of communicable disease. IHO will address unique protection needs of women and girls, particularly respond to GBV, WASH and protection by working closely with the line clusters.
IHO being the lead partner for the health sector transformation project (HSTP) in Terekeka County and having an active project in Kapoeta East ,as well as being a nutrition partner in Eastern equatorial state ,will leverage this presence to ensure quality implementation of the project. The estimated cost to deliver the proposed nutrition interventions is USD 4045150.

1. Needs

South Sudan faces a grave nutrition situation, affecting its most vulnerable children and women. The 2023 FSNMS and SMART survey indicate that national GAM levels surpass the WHO's 15 per cent emergency threshold. This places 2.5 million individuals at risk of acute malnutrition in 2024, with an estimated 5.8 million people facing acute food insecurity; inadequate access to safe water and sanitation, affecting over 50 per cent of the population; and elevated child morbidity rates as a result of diseases such as diarrhoea, malaria or upper respiratory infections (HNRP 2024)An estimated 2.1 million children aged 6-59 months suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025, including 650,000 cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). About 1.11 million pregnant or breastfeeding women (PBW) are also suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition in the same period. IPC phase classification (December 2024- March 2025) puts Kapoeta East County in IPC 4 (Emergency), while Terekeka County in IPC 3 (Crisis) highlighting severe food insecurity and a critical humanitarian need.Acute malnutrition projection October 2024 to march 2025 puts Kapoeta East County in IPC AMN phase 4 (Critical). (IPC-Published on 18 November, 2024). The Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate is critically high, exceeding the WHO emergency threshold of 15% indicative of severe nutritional crises, driven by factors such as food insecurity, poor water and sanitation conditions, draught and limited access to healthcare services. (Nutrition Cluster strategy 2024). The significant proportion of the population particularly children under five and pregnant or lactating women are experiencing high level of acute malnutrition requiring urgent action. Furthermore, numerous cattle raiding and social cultural barriers have greatly disrupted nutritional service delivery and limit uptake of available health services.Terekeka County has been categorized under IPC AMN Phase 3 (IPC projection October 2024 to march 2025) with GAM rate of 17.2% (Nutrition data from SMART surveyy indicating a serious public health concern. This high prevalence is attributed to food insecurity, poor access to health services, economic instability, suboptimal child practices, floods and WASH challenges. Terekeka County faces additional challenges, including recurrent flooding and intercommunal conflicts which severely hinder access to available services. In September 2024, a rapid needs assessment ( IRNA) conducted by RRC and humanitarian partners reported that 44912 individuals were displaced by flooding, the most affected payams included Mangalla North, Bari- Arie, Nantiko, Buko, Wonki, Tukor, Lengi and Korshoba of Gemeiza . Flooding disrupted access to 4 out 0f 20 health facilities resulting to shortage of nutrition supplies in the cut off locations.

2. Project activities

IHO will align the project Activities in line to the nutrition cluster 2024 and these will be as follows: 1. Prevent malnutrition through provision of specialized food. The project will provide under-five girls, boys and pregnant and breastfeeding women provided with specialized nutritious foods though the OTP services and Outreaches. 2. Prevent of malnutrition through behaviour change communication The project will target primary caregivers of children aged 0-23 months and provide them with Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition counselling, during the OTP days, community outreach, through mother support groups and nutrition volunteers. The project will also promote cooking demonstrations sessions and cooking demonstrations sessions. 3. Prevention of micronutrient deficiencies; The project will provide girls and boys aged 6-59 months with vitamin A supplement, Albendazole tablets and iron supplements to adolescent girls by working closely with WHO, UNICEF and MOH. 4. Ensure early detection and treatment of girls and boys under 5 years of age and pregnant and breastfeeding women affected by acute malnutrition. This will include; admission of girls and boys aged 6-59 months with SAM for treatment; admission of boys, girls aged 6-59 months and Pregnant and breastfeeding women with MAM for treatment. 6. Promote of community participation for a responsive nutrition service delivery. The project conduct community meeting which includes community leaders and as well conduct interviews using feedback questionnaires to ascertain the quality of services provide. The project will also provide suggestion boxes at service delivery points. The project will orient mothers on family MUAC. 7. Prevent, test and treat for malaria among children under five years with acute malnutrition: The activities will be integrated in OTP, TSFP, Stabilization Centre and outreaches. 8. Provide WASH kits to children with SAM in nutrition sites and well conduct assessment to establish WASH needs linked to NFI. The project will also educate mothers and caregivers on proper sanitation and hygiene. 9. Establish kitchen garden at nutrition sites where they don’t exist and maintain existing one to work as demonstration to promote dietary diversity by using locally available foods. 10. Integrate child stimulation and ECD in the provision of care for children with SAM and MAM, in this regard the project will, undertake outreach sessions in schools on ECD, Establish safe spaces at Nutrition site for children 0-5 years, their primary caregivers and pregnant/lactating women, Train nutrition/health staff on Care for Child Development and similar types of packages, Provide developmentally appropriate play materials for health/nutrition spaces (i.e. ECD kit or similar) and relevant health/nutrition materials and Integrate early stimulation and play in nutrition/health spaces where children and parents can use developmentally appropriate toys. 11. Mainstream of child protection and GBV risk mitigation at nutrition sites. In this regard, the project will undertake safety Audits, Assess community perceptions, norms and practices linked to nutrition that may contribute to GBV, Prepare and provide training for nutrition staff and community nutrition groups on the safe design and implementation of nutrition programmes that mitigate the risk of GBV, Implement strategies that increase the safety, availability and accessibility of nutrition services for women, girls and other at-risk groups (e.g. locate services in safe areas; establish supplemental feeding schedules in collaboration with women, girls and other at-risk groups; consider the need to bring feeding supplements to GBV survivors and their children in safe shelters; etc.) and undertake GBV awareness-raising. Carry out capacity building initiatives, training of mother to mother support groups, boma health workers, health workers and nutritional workers.
