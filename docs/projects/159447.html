Project Summary


Somalia is a country whose public health system has significantly underperformed for many years because of longstanding conflict and instability and is further overburdened by the impact of climate shocks such as drought and flooding. Somalia has the world’s highest child mortality rate. The risk of childhood mortality is highest between birth and one month, accounting for approximately 45 per cent of death among children under age five. Nearly 54 per cent of health facilities do not provide an ‘Expanded Programme on Immunization’ services; driving coverage below 50 per cent, leading to a rapid accumulation of children susceptible to vaccine preventable illnesses and exposing communities to the risk of communicable diseases. Surveys conducted by FSNAU and partners in June and July 2019 indicate the persistence of widespread acute malnutrition in Somalia due to a combination of factors, including food insecurity, high morbidity, low immunization and vitamin-A supplementation, and poor care practices.

As a way of working towards provision of Integrated life-saving primary health care services, we will improve access to health care systems by providing health interventions to the residence within the target population to the nearest proximity. Health care services through consultation and management of childhood illnesses with an aim f reducing maternal and neonatal mortality will be given focus. ANC and BEmONC with referrals of the CEmONC cervices will be provided to the pregnant mothers.
Staff and the community will be equipped with adequate information to manage emergencies and outbreaks that may arise from the dynamic natural and conflict related concerns. Reports shall be shared with the in line MOH and the cluster including proper coordination so as to respond to emergencies like AWD/cholera, measles, and some haemorrhagic fever outbreaks.
ADA staff will be equipped with trainings on ANC and BEmONC, IMCI, Emergency response preparedness and CHWs equipped with skilled on community management and targeting. Community dialogue will be key in adherence with the AAP framework.
The project staff per health facility will be comprising of 1 clinical officer, 2 qualified nurses, 2 mid-wives, 2 auxiliary nurses, 2 vaccinators and 2 Laboratory assistants, in the main central pharmacy we will have 1 pharmacy assistant with 2 separate store.
ADA will ensure that services are provided in safe and protective environment adhering to a do no harm standards. Mainstreaming of cross cutting issues like protection, accountability to the targeted population as well as avoidance of discrimination of the less fortunate or those with terminal illnesses. ADA will give equitable services to both the HIV and TB cases without biasness. Mental health services will be introduced to the health facilities with none existing services but for complicated cases we shall refer for comprehensive support.
ADA will therefore ensure equitable service delivery is offered to the beneficiaries within their reach to reduce on the morbidity and mortality rates in the area.
Proper coordination with partners, the respective focal point representatives as well as the zonal and the national team will be adhered to avoid overlap.
