Project Summary

Handover of Tel Hajar Primary Health Care Center: In 2021, SP elevated the clinic to the comprehensive primary healthcare level. Until the end of June 2022, SP will continue working in the Tel Hajar clinic directly with the CoH to support the Tel Hajar clinic. Financial inputs will be provided to cover the salary incentives of the clinic staff, medications, and consumables, per the current posture, and continue to provide technical oversight and capacity building at both the clinic operation and CoH level. The CoH will provide ambulance services to the clinic. In addition, SP will build the capacity of the health committee, particularly in regard to human resources, administration, and clinical service delivery standards. By supporting the Tel Hajar clinic, SP continues to provide life-saving services to the Hassakah urban community at the same, or better, quality of service level as is currently being offered and built into the long-term sustainable quality of the clinic.
Capacity Building of CoH Staff: The core team of five clinic staff will work alongside equivalent positions hired in 2022 by the CoH and provided with salary incentives by SP. During a six-month period of shadowing and handover, these CoH staff will receive a thorough education in HR and Administrative clinic management through our SP program coordinator, data entry, and M&E management through our dedicated M&E officer, warehouse management, pharmacy supply management, and ordering strategy from our Warehouse Manager, logistics, procurement, and supply chain management from our Logistics Officer, and finally the election of an existing CHW to receive intentional leadership and public health topic education from our CHW Team Lead. Through a scheduled, mutually agreed upon timeline, the administrative staff will transition to increasing responsibility, with CoH staff taking on the primary responsibility of the clinic functioning and SP staff playing a supportive role during the last two months of SP clinic support. Administrative staff will stay on as CoH staff at the end of SP's support of the clinic so that the clinic will continue to operate with the same systems in place after SP support ends.

4 - Needs

Samaritan’s Purse (SP) in Northeast Syria (NES) supports a primary health care clinic in Hassakah city to provide a comprehensive package of free primary healthcare services to vulnerable populations. The clinic addresses non-emergent and urgent cases at the outpatient level, which improves health outcomes in the local community by ensuring early access to free quality primary healthcare. The project will provide supportive referral and coordination structures alongside critical inputs, capacity building support and clinical staffing models to improve quality of care and adherence to Sphere minimum standards. Tel Hajar clinic currently services approximately 150-175 beneficiaries per day. Of these, approx. 90% are unique every month. In 2021, SP elevated the clinic to the comprehensive primary healthcare level. Financial inputs will be provided to cover the salary incentives of the clinic staff, medications, and consumables, per the current posture, and continue to provide technical oversight and capacity building at both the clinic operation and Committee if Health(CoH) level. In addition, the CoH will provide ambulance services to the clinic. In addition, SP will build the capacity of the CoH, particularly human resources, administration, and clinical service delivery standards. Samaritan's Purse will continue the support in Tel Hajar clinic until the end of June 2022.

5 - Implementation of activities

Tel Hajar Comprehensive Primary Healthcare Center, with a population catchment of 350,000 people, is the only free primary health care facility available in Hassakah, providing life-saving services to both the host community and IDPs. Since April 2020, SP has funded and built the capacity of 67 staff members to manage the clinic and consult 160-190 patients per day. In a phased approach contingent on the staff's capacity, SP has introduced increasingly complex services to the clinic's offering, the most recent being Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care. While the clinic's capacity to operate independently is increasing, additional training, oversight, and mentorship are needed to ensure service quality and sustainability. Key areas of growth needed to ensure a successful handover to the CoH by June 2022 include administrative processes (e.g., HR, data collection, information management); clinical service delivery; referral systems streamlining; and broadening the CHW's scope to include infant and young child feeding and nutrition education.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

a)Patients assaulted en route to/from clinic;b)Risk to beneficiaries related to the receipt of ready-to-use therapeutic food (nutrition program) and/or assistive devices and/or medication, the perceived economic value of which may make the beneficiaries or their families targets within the community. c)Risk to beneficiaries around inequitable access to programming for females and children, due to cultural context which gives preference to adult males as recipients of programming and/or programming commodities. d)Risk to female beneficiaries of forced pregnancy in order to have them qualify for nutrition programming benefiting pregnant and lactating women. All staff involved in the project are trained in managing and/or referring specific gender and protection concerns and have a operational awareness of protection risks/issues. A safeguarding focal point is assigned, and the complaints/feedback mechanism means that feedback is captured from remote/off-site locations and acted upon. SP will assess the contextualized assistance needs of other vulnerable groups, including PWDs and ensure their equitable participation and inclusion in the project. Across NES, the numbers of disabled people are quite high – HNAP's 2021 report puts the number at 37% of the population.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risks identified•Contracted doctors violating PSEA policy;•Beneficiaries exploited by SP staff members, partners, and/or vendors. Samaritan's Purse mitigates PSEA risks with the following measures:•Background checks on all personnel, safeguarding training for all staff, protection messaging displayed in the clinic, clear reporting system for violations. There is an HR and complaints processing department to support this risk mitigation.•Building PSEA capacity of partners. •A safeguarding focal point is assigned, and the complaints/feedback mechanism means that feedback is captured from remote/off-site locations and acted upon.•Reporting mechanisms for each project.•Hotline.•Email hotline.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

•Security situation or border restrictions cause the team to lose access to NES for a period of time. This is mitigated through the CoH staff and SP staff being able to continue activities in the interim period. •COVID-19 cases amongst health worker staff, SP or partner. Continued training and enforcement of infection prevention and control protocols. •Pockets of higher risk area due to tribal tension and ISIS sleeper cell activity. Mitigated through security assessment of the area to identify processes and protocols and coordination with local community actors and members. •Consistent provision of medication and consumables. The supply chain has remained relatively stable as SP procures all medications and consumables locally within NES. Samaritan's Purse has been able to successfully mitigate medication and supply shortages resulting from supply chain instability by setting up contracted agreements for the supply of 6-9 months' worth of medication from several local vendors. This medication supply is delivered to the clinic monthly or bi-monthly, which allows vendors time to plan well in advance, taking advantage of times the roads are open, to have adequate stock in their warehouse to continue to supply the clinic.
