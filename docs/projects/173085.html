Project Summary

The project aims to(1) support disaster affected children to improve access quality basic education (2) to support having safe, protective and responsive learning environments to the needs of vulnerable children including children with disabilities. This includes strict adherence to COVID19 safety measures and fostering children’s resilience during emergencies.
The project will target a total of 216498 in four states and 12 localities. Four localities in Central Darfur state; west, north and central Jebel Jebel and Zalingi. Three in Blue Nile; Tadamon, Wad Elmahi and Damazine. The third state is Khartoum, in Karri and Jebel Awlia and in South Kordofan, localities of Abujibiha, Elirrie and Kadogli.
Save the Children has opted to work on these location to build on its interventions already established there as well as to counite responding to serious education gaps. As an example, in Nertitie West Jabal Marra-where an EiE intervention funded by ECHO is implementing, it has been communicated that at least 19 schools in this locality were damaged because of conflict, and this is why students number per a class in functioning schools could reach as much as 150 students. Add to this, the seating is a challenge as many schools face shortage in seating, in an emergency context this becomes worst with many students sitting on the ground again Jebel Marra localities are good examples of this. Also serious shortage in basic learning and teaching materials were recorded for more than a third of the schools in Sudan. for instance, in some areas of Sudan, in particularly with the emergency context, such as Central Darfur, Blue Nile and South Kordofan, most of the students’ books are not available, when are available the student book ratio could have reached 10:1, ironically in some areas of Jebel Marra some communities thought the books are for teachers only! Also the other supplies i.e. note books, pens, pencils have put a huge burden on families, enforcing many children to withdraw from the schools, with girls being more affected. Add to this, parents and caregivers have to contribute to school operations in term of chalks and the related school supplies, through some fee paid monthly. Also teachers’ guidance books are not enough, and sometime no available at all. In addition, teachers have to buy the notes for the lessons preparation. Again this might put more burden on schools and might also be transferred to the parents in terms of using some of parents’ contribution to buy such items. Add to this, the teachers training has been a challenge all the way through. For instance, findings from South Kordofan-EQUIP Project EUD funded- has shown that 50-60% have received training in subjects, but very few have received training on code of conduct and child protection issues.
To this effect, this HRP activities were designed to support access include rehabilitation and construction of classrooms, and TLS, latrines, hand washing facilities, as well as seating provision and all learning, teaching, and recreational materials, add to this, uniforms and PPE will be be also availed. In addition, the the teachers are going to be trained in child centered approach, positive discipline as well as the protection issues include the PFA and schools as zone of peace(Save School Declaration). Also in all targeted schools referral path will be established, and also grants to support COVID 19 are considered. Considering the necessity of community engagement, PTAs are going to be trained as well as Mother Groups are going to be established in some schools where the education was absent for many years such as Rokero in Central Darfur. The implementation of all activities are going to be guided by the INEE, Sphere and Save the Children Quality Learning Framework (QLF) that put the children wellbeing at the heart of the education intended outcomes, and this why CP is well integrated and mainstreamed.

1 - Needs

In Darfur Region, according to the records at locality level, out of nearly 48,000 school-age children only 20% are enrolled in schools. Amongst the remaining 80% of out of school children, the majority are girls. The assessments done by Save the Children reveal that in 30 IDPs camps, 56% of school-age children lack access to education. 64% of IDPs schools are not connected to water sources and 45% of the school latrines, where in place, are not functional. Poor water and sanitation facilities in schools contribute to the spread of diseases, including Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD), and with COVID 19 pandemic serious impact is expected, and this poor water facilities have a negative impact on education, contributing to higher drop-out and lower completion rates for girls. There is also high prevalence of child labor, potential risks of recruitment of boys into armed groups, SGBV and other reproductive health issues. There is also lack of teaching and learning materials, desks, chairs and uniforms in all schools and particularly where IDPs are enrolled.Blue Nile: Specific challenges are facing these localities of El-Damazine, Wad El-Mahi, and El-Tadamon; displacement, for instance in El-Damazine More than 30% of the displaced from other localities, poverty and low families income, customs and traditions are affecting especially the education of girls, i.e. more than 70% of girls marry at school age at North of El-Damazine, more than 10% of pupils are homeless or orphans, lack of in-service training for teachers, Overcrowding of some schools, high number of nomadic students (in Wad Almahi: 1,235 male and 1,319 females; Al Tadamon: 1,625 males 1,184 females). lack of awareness of education importance by the society (especially in relation to girls), recruiting Children in the army in Wad Elmahi. And Considerable number of refugees in Al-Tadamon (estimated at 56,000).South Kordofan State: These three localities of South Kordofan, Kadogli, Elirrie and Abujibiha, have many challenges that affecting the education. For instance, Kadugli hosts IDPs from the three close localities (Heban- Al-Bram - Um Doreen)-under control of SPLM/N, also in all these localities families suffer from poverty and very low income. Elirrie specifically, hosts a considerable number of South Sudanese refugees, while Abujibiha does for IDPs. Add to this, number of students who lost their parents due to the war. Quality of learning is poor in theses localities because of, lack of trained for teachers, overcrowding of some schools as well as lack of enough learning apace as many students are with no classroom siting under trees, and more are with no seating as well as no learning materials. Moreover the inter-tribe conflict happened recently in Kadogli resultant in a set up of a new camp in Taffari which is located in western end of the town, is accommodating nearly 800 families. the SK MoE requested the INGO and UN support to set up a school for the children in Taffari camp because there is no school in that neighborhood and younger children cannon be sent to schools in the neighboring areas because they are far away. it has been found that the number of children who left their schools in the other sides of the town and came to Taffari is high.COVID 19 has seriously interrupted the education and and also records has shown absenteeism among students who sat for exams. an example, in South Kordofan more than 600 have missed the exams, moreover with severe impact on grade 8 exams. In addition, Save the Children has conducted FGDs in Abujibiha and found that grade 8 pass rate has dropped significantly from 80-90 to 10% and less in some schools. same impacts was also reported from Central Darfur and Blue Nile, and this ring alarming bells regarding violation of child rights, as many girl will be pushed towards early marriage and many of boys to the child labor.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The implementation of the project activities will be fully guided by Save the Children Quality Learning framework(QLF) that developed in consideration of INEE and Sphere.The first set of the activities are towards improving the accessibility though: 1. rehabilitation and constructions of more learning spaces and facilities- 2. provision of all supplies that include learning material and uniforms as well as MEAL- this set is going to be implanted directly by Save the children building on its legacy and replicating ,many of its best practices in this regards. the second set include: 1. Enrolment campaigns to increase the demand for education in particular for girls, and this will be implemented through local partners whom have the advantages of better communities engagement. The thirds set is the training of teachers, headmasters and PTAs.the conflict; 92% suffered from family disputes and 86% suffered from lack of self-confidence.Support is needed to ensure the provision of access to inclusive and protective life-saving education for girls and boys children affected by the crisis (IDPs, refugees, returnees or host communities). This includes safe learning spaces; psychosocial interventions; life-skills; school feeding; health/hygiene; protection; peace education; awareness sessions on child marriages, child labour including recruitment in armed groups, SGBV and reproductive health; and learning and recreational opportunity. High school fees are not affordable by both the host population and the IDPs. More classes are needed in schools to accommodate the IDPs as well as food in schools to retain children. There is also lack of teaching and learning materials, desks, chairs and uniforms in all schools and particularly where IDPs are enrolled.Girls and boys also need psychosocial support to better cope with shocks and stresses. To ensure reintegration of a significant number of children into the school system, an AEP will be a key component of this project. In Rokero, there is need to construct additional semi-permanent classrooms to accommodate the newly displaced children and establish a temporary school in Gemiza return village. There are inadequate teachers in all the 3 localities and this would require deployment of additional teachers. There are issues to do with out of school children where enrolment campaigns are required to bring boys and girls out of school back into school.
