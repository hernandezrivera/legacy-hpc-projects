Project Summary

Following the Beirut Port Explosion, which claimed 180 lives and caused more than 6000 injuries, there exists an urgent need for specialized services, including physical rehabilitation and MHPSS. HI’s outreach-based comprehensive approach is to 1. directly provide a community-based comprehensive package of physical rehabilitation and PSS services 2. provide assistive devices and mobility aids; and 3. refer individuals and/or households to humanitarian actors providing other services.
Vulnerable areas are prioritized based on a combination of factors including level of damage sustained from the explosion, pre-existing socio-economic vulnerabilities, and areas under-served by other humanitarian actors. In collaboration with health and physical rehabilitation actors, the intervention is set to take place where injured individuals are located after their discharge from hospital. Indeed, continuous coordination with the international actors (Emergency Medical teams (EMT), ICRC, MSF and WHO) as well as local organizations and networks (ArcEnCiel, Musawat, the Order pf Physiotherapists in Lebanon and others) provides a clear beneficiary pathway to ensure all types of cases requiring physical rehabilitation is taken care of. While the general longer-term pathway is not yet clear given the level of uncertainty around the extent of damage and disruption experienced by pre-existing physical rehabilitation services in Beirut, HI is working with local partners to develop a longer-term physical rehabilitation care capacity in the area. Through constant coordination, HI is involved with PHCs following the efforts made to ensure their timely rehabilitation, with other rehabilitation service providers and national health structures.
The intervention targets individuals who cannot access to rehabilitation services for several reasons. Whether due to the prohibitive cost of private services, especially in a difficult economic situation, or to the disruption of the health system and rehabilitation services affected by the explosion, HI intervention aims at ensuring continuity of care and preventing long-term impairment or disability. In addition, the provision of psychosocial support to injured individuals is promoted as a comprehensive package of care. Indeed, with such a brutal change of environment, potential loss of family members or friend and the personal physical consequences on their body, such support is paramount to foster a full recovery.
Given the imminent protection risks that exist following disasters, HI’s intervention will systematically consider the intersectionality of factors - including gender, age, disability, legal status, nationality - which can heighten an individuals’ vulnerability. Links will then be made with HI’s protection intervention and the individuals’ needs addressed through a strong cross-sectorial referral mechanism. This approach ensures populations most at-risk of being excluded are consistently identified and included in the response, regardless of nationality, gender, age, disability, or legal status.

1 - Needs

Along with causing destruction of buildings and infrastructures, the explosions at the Beirut Port have also resulted in direct physical injuries, loss of life and psychological trauma. While these direct impacts need to be addressed immediately, longer-term secondary impacts such as protracted mental health issues and protection risks have to be taken into consideration at the same time. With an estimated 6.000 injured persons, the number of individuals at risk of acquiring long-term disabilities could be quite high if access to specialized physical rehabilitation services is not guaranteed. After undergoing surgery and other primary health care treatment, individuals with traumatic injuries such as fractures, brain injuries, amputations, peripheral nerve injuries, tendons injuries or burns will require immediate support to ensure they recover their functional independence and prevent long-term impairment. The powerful blast devastated an urban and crowded area where glass, metal and cement have caused a whole range of injuries, overburdening an already fragile health system. Indeed, ICUs already had limited number of available beds due to the COVID-19 epidemic. In addition, several hospitals and PHCs among other health facilities have been directly damaged during the explosion and health staff wounded or killed. While physical rehabilitation service exist in the country, they are not able to absorb the surge in the number of injured and ensure a timely rehabilitation care. This situation led to generalized quick discharges from health structures and premature return of injured persons to inadequate living environments for a healthy recovery. Indeed, if not going back to their damaged houses or apartments, the most vulnerable of them end up living in crowded collective shelters, at a relative place where family members are crammed or even worse places such as streets. A community-based intervention ensures such persons have access to rehabilitation services and guarantees their continuity of care. In addition, the social and psychological impacts of the explosion are undeniably high on these injured individuals. On top of the general trauma linked to the event and destruction that is felt and shared by all persons affected by the explosion, they also face a drastic change in their functional independence. Depending on the gravity of the injury and the duration and intensity of care required, psychosocial support throughout this recovery process is more or less important, yet always needed. Indeed, feelings of uselessness, anger, sadness and depression linked to their physical state can hinder the recovery process and lead to longer-term impairments and mental health issues.

2 - Activities

The project aligns with FA Health Response Priority 2.4, through:• Provision of community-based physical rehabilitation for persons with injuries, newly acquired disability or pre-existing disabilities;• Provision of assistive devices and mobility aids for persons with injuries or newly acquired disabilities• Provision of community-based psychosocial support for persons with injuries and newly-acquired disabilities;• Cross-sectorial referral including to other health services, functional PHCs and mental health specialized services;HI outreach physical rehabilitation team uses different modalities of identification of injured person. Through advertising its prioritization criteria and areas of intervention to other actors (especially health facilities and service providers), referrals are received. Through direct field-based identification from any community member or other actor on the ground. Also, HI protection teams carrying out an active door-to-door PFA activity, refer identified injured persons to the physiotherapists. This ensures a more efficient identification of injured persons as well as synergies between HI’s health and protection interventions through which injured persons can benefit from a holistic access to services.Trained physiotherapists whose technical capacities in regards emergency rehabilitation are reinforced will provide physical rehabilitation sessions, including the provision of assistive devices. The activities will take place at community level, either at the individual’s house when conditions allow or within an adequate space to identify.In addition, when needs for psychosocial support are identified, HI team of Psychosocial Support Workers provides direct support at community-level. Based on the level and suitable modality for each individual, sessions can be either individual or group. Such groups will be defined alongside common characteristics of individuals identified such as gender, age and of course needs. While HI is providing non-specialized PSS services to reduce the levels of anxiety, distress and trauma, some individuals are in need of further specialized mental health services. Such services are meant to meet the needs of persons who, despite community and family support or non-specialized support, have trouble with basic daily functioning. Based on its strong cross-sectorial referral mechanism and its long lasting involvement within the National Mental Health Task Force (NMHTF), HI will ensure these individuals are linked up with appropriate services. Following the NMHTF Action Plan for the explosions, referral will be made to the Embrace Lifeline and synergies with longer-term service provision will be built. Also, within longer-term initiatives, HI works closely with mental health specialized service providers with the capacity in Beirut and other areas of the country. Specific cases such as peripheral nerve injuries and tendons injuries require longer rehabilitation care, as a higher number of sessions are necessary to recover a maximum of mobility and range of motion. A tailored pathway is designed between HI and other actors and services to ensure each type of injury receives the most appropriate care and the rehabilitation process is followed through. In that regards, and when possible, HI will link with locally implanted rehabilitation actors as well as the Lebanese health structures to ensure this follow-up care. While the geographic targeting of the intervention is rather dependent of where injured persons will be located, HI has identified most-vulnerable areas such as Quarentina and Basta. However, HI remains open to implement its early physical rehabilitation and psychosocial support comprehensive package in any blast-affected area. Cases are prioritized according pre-defined criteria as well as based on the overall vulnerability of the individuals, including vulnerability linked to gender, age, social status, economic situation and other protection risks.
