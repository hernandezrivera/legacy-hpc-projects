Project Summary

The proposed action seeks to increase the access and quality of education and PSS services in target areas by implementing a combination of formal (non-accredited) and non-formal education activities that complement each other where possible. PIN takes an integrated and holistic approach to address the Education in Emergencies (EiE), and Child Protection needs. PIN will employ a dual approach to supporting education, including through establishing temporary learning centres (TLCs) for provision of PSS and non-formal education using the cluster-approved Self-Learning Programme (SLP) curriculum in areas with acute concentrations of OOSC – including in IDP camps and informal settlements; and assistance to non-accredited schools where education has been disrupted through providing light repairs, equipment, teaching and learning materials, support for running costs, and provision of after-school catch-up and remedial classes to support pathways into education and reduce drop-out. Specific consideration will be given to particularly vulnerable households with Out of School Children or children at risk of education disruption, who will be provided with psychosocial support and pathways for (re)integration into education. PIN takes an evidence-based approach to programming based on rigorous feedback loops to promote accountability and enhance impact. All activities will be implemented in line with relevant Education cluster tools, materials, and approaches, in addition to core INEE and Sphere standards, with a focus upon promoting safe learning spaces and essential WASH facilities and maximizing accessibility for those at risk of education disruption, including girls and children living with disabilities. Teaching and student kits will be provided to facilitate quality learning opportunities and minimise education barriers for children from low-income HH. PIN also provides ECCD classes for adult TVET students’ children from 3 to 5 years old to help caregivers overcome barriers in attending the TVET courses while providing essential support during the developmental phase of pre-school-aged children. Geographical targeting will occur through further in-depth needs assessments in areas pre-identified in cooperation with the Education Cluster. These will determine schools and communities most in need of educational and child protection support. The process will ensure the participation of relevant stakeholders to consider local priorities while also ensuring that transparency is at the forefront and that PIN is accountable to the populations in its areas of intervention. Assessments will be conducted by PIN staff and engineers in collaboration with local authorities, where relevant. PIN will target communities where education services have been disrupted due to conflict, where there is no, or limited, access to education. Experience highlights that in such areas, significant gaps also exist to meeting teachers’ and students’ needs for safe and quality learning. Throughout the entire cycle, there will be a strong focus on community outreach and engagement, including caregivers. All activities have the final aim of reintegrating children into education through non-formal accelerated learning programmes and the creation of child protection safety nets.The organization plays an active role within several clusters and working groups across Northern Syria, including the Cash Working Group, the Protection working group, and the Education cluster, of which PIN has played a vital role as a member of the Strategic Advisory Group in Gaziantep.

4 - Needs

PIN’s needs assessment conducted in November 2020 in NWS found an estimated 12,443 school-aged children live across the 33 targeted IDP camps, of which an estimated 43.8% are considered to be vulnerable, and 36.1% to be working instead of going to school. An estimated 46.1% girls and 40.8% boys are out of school, often for more than a year. Concerning age, two relevant patterns emerged from the results. First, key informants (KIs) believe that children aged 6 to 11 are often out of school. Second, almost 40% of the KIs believe all children are out of school, which suggests age may not be a key factor, but rather that larger systemic or structural conditions are preventing children from attending school. The majority of children reported by KIs, are out of school for one year or longer. In NES, per the SSWG overview in January 2021, there are 46 sites, settlements and collective centres in Aleppo Governorate and 116 in Ar-Raqqa, 96 Al-Hasakeh and 118 Deir-ez-Zor Governorates. A REACH assessment in June 2020 shows 53% of education services were reportedly accessible for assessed settlements in Ar-Raqqa compared to only 11% in Menbij. REACH assessment in November 2020 revealed that 99% of the informal settlements, education barriers were reported; the most common was no education for children of a certain age and lack of learning spaces. COVID-19 and school closures decreased children’s interest in learning, increased child labour and no alternative learning opportunities. Response Snapshot by NES Forum (September 2020) reported no education intervention in Aleppo Governorate while intervention in Al-Hasakeh, Ar-Raqqa and Deir-ez-Zor reached 2,064; 1,475; 499 beneficiaries respectively, this shows underserving in Aleppo.

5 - Implementation of activities

PIN proposes an integrated, holistic intervention to support the most vulnerable conflict-affected children in Northern Syria, increasing their pathways to education, PSS and protection services in light of such acute Education in Emergencies and Child Protection needs. Activities are informed by detailed needs and protection risk assessments, engagement with relevant Clusters, and targeting based on IDP camps and informal settlements with the highest numbers of out-of-school children (OOSC). Schools and Temporary Learning Centres (TLCs) will be supported and established, providing non-formal and formal education with integrated PSS activities. All classes, will be taught by trained staff, who will also use this platform to identify children with Child Protection concerns or additional vulnerabilities such as physical disabilities. Upon consent, they will be referred to specialized service providers. PIN will support teachers and PSS specialists with professional development training and enhance resilience and safe learning environments by training staff and children on emergency preparedness. Throughout the entire year, there will be a focus on community outreach and engagement, including caregivers. The activities foreseen include setting up non-formal learning centers, light rehabilitation of schools and classrooms, provision of monthly operational costs, and provision of furniture and school supplies for formal and non-formal settings. PIN will contribute to the Education Cluster-specific objectives by providing quality offline and online classes, distribution of essential items for high-quality learning (operational items, stationery, textbooks, etc.) and ensuring safe access integrated PSS activities.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

PIN’s protection mainstreaming strategy carries out regular protection risk assessments. These protection risk assessments feed into the development of protection risk and mitigation matrices. The following major risks (R) and respective mitigation measures (MM) were identified:1/R: Specific vulnerable groups are excluded from accessing education;1/MM: accessibility for all genders and children with disabilities; CFRM mechanism, conducting consultations and FGDs to understand possible barriers related to access and safety (such as distance), organizing community and awareness-raising events, referral of children with mobility and non-mobility related disabilities to specialized actors; Conflict Sensitive teacher training (disability sensitive classroom for smooth integration of children with disabilities with their peers and reduction of the negative social effects of their injuries, such as stigma and bullying.2/R: Additional threats (child labor, early marriage, recruitment into armed groups)2/MM: targeting communities with different types of assistance; integrating FSL programming carried out in the same target focusing on the most vulnerable HHs to minimize barriers to education, PIN’s referral system and case management, community awareness sessions.3/R: Behavior of parents and education personnel threatening children’s wellbeing. 3/MM: signature of Code of Conduct (CoC); Child-friendly CFRM is available in all schools and children to know how to access it; training to all staff PSEA policy; any breach of the CoC is investigated and results in disciplinary actions and as necessary, termination; referral system is in place for child protection related cases, engagement of parents and awareness-raising.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

(A1/R1): The security situation in all target areas remains conducive to implementation; (MM1): Suspend/relocate activities to areas where access is upheld; and/or switch to remote management, potentially through pre-identified local partners. (A2/R2) PIN’s staff, offices and warehouses in Syria are not subjected to targeting/abductions/raiding by armed actors. (MM3): rigorous security monitoring and management mechanism. If specified risks occurred, PIN would temporarily suspend activities; carry out internal/external investigations; advocate for dialogue with relevant actors, inform relevant counter-terrorism agencies/authorities/partners of the incident; and seek to relocate the intervention;(A3/R3): LCs/others continue to collaborate in line with principles. (MM3): Temporarily suspend activities; engage in dialogue to re-sensitize on principles of engagement. If such action fails, PIN would relocate resources to other target areas.(A4/R4): Armed actors present in areas of operation facilitate safe and principled humanitarian access; (MM4): Suspend operations, engage in advocacy with local authorities and armed actors (jointly with others, including UNOCHA, NGO forums, and/or Access Working Groups) in an effort to restore free and unhindered access. If such efforts were unsuccessful, relocate activities to other target areas facing acute needs; (A5/R5): Local markets remain functional across target areas; (MM5): If border crossings to Syria were closed/restricted or PIN was prevented from transferring goods, PIN would suspend activities; advocate with UNOCHA/local authorities to facilitate access; purchase locally if possible; and shift funds to Cash-based programming.
