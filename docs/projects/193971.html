Project Summary

On 15 April 2023, clashes erupted between the Sudanese Armed Forces and the Rapid Support Forces, with devastating consequences for civilians. Due to violence and attacks on health, disruptions in the supply chain, and poor access by the populace to facilities that can provide needed services, health service provision across the nation and particularly in the country's capital Khartoum has been severely impacted. Several maternity clinics are out of service, including Omdurman maternity hospital. Power supply interruptions and blackouts continue to impact hospitals and blood banks, posing a serious impact on lifesaving interventions, including blood transfusion and critical care.
Furthermore, all nationwide supplies are managed and controlled in Khartoum with is significantly impacted. Access to lifesaving medicines and consumables as well as infection prevention and control for hospitals and community midwives is nearly absent, pushing several facilities out of services, and increasing the risks for infection and sepsis.
The total number of people in need of humanitarian assistance in Sudan rose by 57% to 24.7 million. 11
million people are in urgent need of health assistance, including 2.4 million women and girls of reproductive age among whom 262,880 are pregnant, and over 60,000 are expecting to give birth in the next three months. All of them are in need of access to essential and lifesaving reproductive health services.
During humanitarian crises including during conflict as the situation in Sudan, sexual and reproductive health services are disrupted, and social support systems are broken down, leading to an increased risk of unintended pregnancies, rape, STIs including HIV, and complications which often imply mortality and/or long-term serious health problems from pregnancy and childbirth for millions of women and girls. UNFPA will consider set of priority SRH activities to be implemented at the onset of an emergency as well as during protracted crises using the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) for SRH, hereafter referred to as the MISP. MISP aims at reducing maternal and newborn mortality and morbidity, prevention and response to sexual violence, prevention of HIV transmission, reduction of HIV morbidity and prevention of unintended pregnancies, and sets the platform for comprehensive SRH services integrated into primary health as soon as possible. All intervention of the MISP will be implemented simultaneously through coordinated actions with all relevant partners.
The intervention primarily targets women, girls in reproductive age, youth and adolescents, and men and boys. UNFPA will target 1.8 MILLION individuals.
All SRHR intervention will be implemented simultaneously through coordinated actions with all relevant partners. The planned interventions prioritize and aim reducing SRH mortalities and morbidities through: timely provision of Emergency Obstetric care and save delivery services, timely referral to lifesaving services, support the availability of Family Planning services, improving Clinical management of rape for survivors of SGBV, availing HIV and STIs prevention and response, improving Antenatal and postnatal care, Post abortion care, and other SRH services. These will be achieved through strengthening the capacity of SRH care providers with emphases of midwifery services, timely availability of lifesaving SRHR supplies, timely deployment of temporary clinics and mobile clinics, supporting the referral system, and demand creation through awareness raising and educational activities. Maternal Morbidities such as Obstetric fistula will begiven especial attention by UNFPA.
The implementation of activities will be conducted through NGOs and Government partners with the strong technical support from UNFPA, including maintaining the field presence.

1 - Needs

The total number of people in need of humanitarian assistance in Sudan rose by 57% to 24.7 million. 11 million people are in urgent need of health assistance, including 2.64 million women and girls of reproductive age among whom 262,880 are pregnant, and over 60,000 are expecting to give birth in the next three months. All of them are in need of access to essential and lifesaving reproductive health services. Around +192,000 pregnant women are estimated to either experience obstetric complication, need for post abortion care, or require a caesarean section. It is also estimated that total number of + 53,000 cases of sexual violence who will seek care during for the remining of 2023 after 15 of April incident. Prior to 15th of April, coverage was low due to limited supplies, infrastructure and capacities, coverage with EMONC was around40%, and CMR services were less than 20%. The situation had catastrophically impacted by the armed clashes, resulting in causalities including amongst pregnant women attending to care, and significant displacement from Khartoum, West Darfur and other affected states. protection risks have emerged as well including increased risks of sexual based violence. Limited safe pathways and access by both people in need and health care providers to seek care, the direct attacks on hospitals and Health care delivery points, Power interruption and water shortages including amongst hospitals, depletion of supplies and unsafe access to stocks, and direct attacks on health care providers; all these factors had serious impact on capacity of health care to deliver and operate. Sudan is characterized with a high maternal mortality rate (270 per 100,000 live births as per2019 UN estimates). In reference to the 2021 Maternal death surveillance report, the most prevalent cause was Obstetric hemorrhage, followed by hypertensive disorders, then sepsis. Moreover, it confirmed that of maternal deaths had no ANC before, and were attributed to limited accessibility (delay at home, or delay in reaching health facility). Coverage with lifesaving emergency obstetric and neonatal care remain very low in Sudan with around 40% by 2022. According to the 2022 multi sectorial needs assessment MSNA, only 12% of women had their deliveries in health facilities during 2022, 12% of deliveries were attended at home with no trained birth attendants. Gaps in provision of CMR services are identified nationwide through the regular CMR mapping, at both supplies, and trained cadres, the latest mapping remained less than 20% coverage. Midwifery cadres as well were not classified as skilled cadres in view of the duration of the pre service training. Sudan remained at high risk of disease outbreaks that have higher impact on pregnant women including Malaria, dengue Fever and Hepatitis E Virus cases. Moreover, the country remains prone to various risks and hazards such as floods, localized armed clashes and inter communal violence, displacement, climatic shocks and hazards, disease outbreaks, food security, malnutrition and protection risks. The deteriorating economic status worsened the situation. The protracted humanitarian crisis in Sudan continues to impact the already fragile health system and is reducing the capacity to provide basic health services and respond to the multiple emergencies affecting the country. The annual cycles of floods, disease outbreaks, border conflicts, and ongoing economic crisis have further impacted the accessibility to health services.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The planned activities include: 1. Support the SRH coordination mechanism at states and national levels to ensure the coordinated provision and expansion of MSP for RH 2. Strengthening national/ states contingency and preparedness planning focusing on Minimum Initial Service Package on RH in Crisis (MISP) and scaling up of MISP. 3. support immediate deployment of mobile and temporary clinics, and roving health care providers, with attention to lifesaving sexual and reproductive health services along with other emergencies. 4. Support referral system through enhancement of locally initiated system to all emergency obstetric cases. 5. improve the functionality of health facilities with emphases on delivery rooms, maternity wards, blood transfusion unit and theaters to support timely provision of safe and clean deliveries including caesarian sections, blood transfusion, neonatal resuscitation, management of complications of abortion, and other obstetric and maternal complications. 6. Support the establishment of Family Planning model centers within selected health facilities inside the camps. 7. Capacity building of health care providers through formal training, supportive supervision, on-the-job training and mentorship on relevant SRH topics including EMONC, FP, STI management, Clinical Management of Rape survivors and psycho-social support, etc.8. Procurement and delivery of emergency reproductive health kits, micronutrients, equipment and supplies. 9. Support Obstetric Fistula treatment in states with high humanitarian burden. 10. Support demand creation and awareness raising on reproductive health seeking behavior. 11. Address Youth/ adolescent RH needs focusing on their specific needs and vulnerabilities in humanitarian settings.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

UNFPA aims to make a positive contribution in the area of environmental impact mitigation. It’s work on sexual and reproductive health helps promote resilience among vulnerable populations. Access to voluntary family planning, maternal health care and quality education are key ways to empower vulnerable women and young people, making them better equipped to prepare for and respond to environmental -related crises. At the procurement, level the organization works with suppliers (International and national levels) to implement sound environmental impact provisions. Manufacturers working with UNFPA have active and documented waste-water programmes and have agreed to reduce raw material usage. UNFPA also produced and disseminates a guide for safe disposal and management of unused unwanted commodities. Throughout the project, planning and implementation process UNFPA closely monitors and set the mitigation measures that needed to avoid, reduce or compensate for environmental impacts to enhance the environmental and community benefits of the proposal.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Through all project steps, starting from planning, implementation, and monitoring and evaluation, the following principles will be adhered to 1. Avoid causing harm: UNFPA and actors will ensure that service and assistance delivery preserves the physical integrity of individuals and communities, their dignity. These packages have already been adapted to the context of Sudan, and are culturally appropriate and minimizes any harmful and unintended negative consequences. 2. Equality: UNFPA and the actors will ensure that assistance and services will be provided according to needs and not any other determinants such as ethnicity or nationality, in good quantity, within safe and easy to reach locations, are known by the affected individuals and are accessible by all various groups including disabled individuals. 3. Accountability to beneficiaries: UNFPA and the actors will ensure that the affected individuals and communities are engaged in the measurement of the quality of interventions serving them, including mechanisms for suggestions and complaints from the population. 4. Participation and Empowerment: Foster inclusive participation to decision-making processes, support the development of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights and empower themselves

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

UNFPA has zero tolerance for all forms of sexual wrongdoing, whether perpetrated against a recipient of assistance or coworker.
