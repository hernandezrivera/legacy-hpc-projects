Project Summary

ZOA will support 1630 individuals from 5 communities namely Julari host community,Gumsuri, waterboard, Marina Bololo and veterinary IDP camp in Monguno LGA with 8 months of cash transfer at the rate of NGN 4,010 per person per month to meet basic needs including but not limited to food, shelter, and health. The multipurpose cash grants have IYCF and diet diversification training targeting the caregivers among the beneficiaries. Basic hygiene messages will be mainstreamed through appropriate mechanisms such as home visits , IEC materials and community activities.

1 - Needs

Multi sectorial needs assessment(MSNA) conducted by REACH and published in September 2019 shows that about 44% of households interviewed in Monguno LGA have poor Food Consumption Score (FCS), a proxy indicator of food insecurity among interviewed households. Baseline data collected among 669 households selected for the ongoing ZOA MPCG project in Monguno shows that 87.7% of beneficiaries were in poor FCS category before the start of the intervention. 90.4% of these beneficiaries also reported high coping strategy (CSI≥10). Coping Strategy Index (CSI) is a proxy indicator of household food insecurity. A joint multisector needs assessment conducted by NJR partners in four LGAs including Monguno shows that 65.8% of households interviewed across three camps and three host communities are heavily dependent on humanitarian assistance. 52% said they do not have food in stock. With heavy reliance of humanitarian aid coupled with the lack of food in stock makes the future very bleak should there be deterioration of security situation including prolong closure of the only accessible Maiduguri-Monguno road which has clear risk of attacks by OAGs. The transfer value will be based on 100% of sector agreed Minimum expenditure basket estimated at 6808 NGN per capita.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Beneficiary targeting, selection and registration exercises - ZOA utilizes a criteria that allows of targeting and selection of the most in need or vulnerable, The vulnerability criteria includes variables such as female and child headed households, vulnerable members such as pregnant and lactating women, elderly, PLW and chronically ill, large household members and dependency and in cases such as food security, access to land and productive assets. A socio-economic score is determined and a cut-off to determine the number. This is followed by biometric registration using fingerprint scanners and tablet phones. ZOA is part of the Nigeria joint response funded by the Dutch Ministry of Foreign Affairs through the Dutch Relief Alliance. As such, NJR members coordinate targeting through regular planning and operational meetings, joint assessments and the establishment of communities of practice according to sectors for harmonization of targeting and selection. ZOA is also an active participant of the Food Security Sector Cluster and Cash working groups and regularly submits 5Ws and refers to the dashboard of partner presence. Activity 2: Community entry meetings and monthly feedback meetings. These meetings explain to the community the project and develops champions from within the communityActivity 3: Cash distribution events-5 monthly cash distribution with beneficiaries separated in smaller groups. ZOA distribution system through the use of color coded cards and different distribution dates by batch of beneficiaries to avoid drawing crowds and reduce security risks as part of ZOAs commitment in protection mainstreaming by ensuring the safety of beneficiaries at distribution sites. The integrity lay-out of distribution sites also ensures appropriate sections and priorities for vulnerable groups such as elderly and pregnant and lactating women. Activity 4: Sensitization on Protection issues and gender based GBV . These are sessions where women and girls join focused groups discussions that identify risks , threats, vulnerabilities and align these with community capacities to mitigate or external sources for reporting. ZOA has already conducted gender analysis training for staff and has a gender analysis framework ready for implementation for its year 2020 project. This is also how ZOA intends to integrate PSEA issues and prevention in its project. The gender analysis, coupled with sessions on identifying community mechanisms of prevention PSEA. ZOA has also established presence in the PSEA network task force to establish connections with the referral mechanisms where present.Activity 5: IYCF/HDD sessions- women beneficiaries will be selected and grouped. Bi-monthly sessions will be conducted in groups to enhance the nutritious preparation and feeding of food bought from multi-[purpose grants. Community nutrition mobilizers and lead mothers together with ZOA staff come together for regular sessions designed to increase the knowledge of mother and child pairs in proper feeding practices and preparation of various foods that diversify the household diet. ZOA does not do nutrition and considers this activity as a form of labelling to strengthen the use of cash for food. Activity 6: Post distribution monitoring , community feedback sessions and operationalizing CRM- The ZOA CRM system is operationalized on field through help desks during distribution, face to face conversations, suggestion boxes and hot lines
