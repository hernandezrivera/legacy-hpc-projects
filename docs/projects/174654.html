Project Summary

WHO 2020 report, indicates that Violence against women, girls and children, are recognized global health and human rights concerns. In humanitarian crises, it is widely acknowledged that disasters affect women, men boys and girls differently. Approximately 35% of women worldwide have experienced physical or sexual violence, and more than 70% of women experience GBV in conflict settings (OCHA, 2016). Due to existing socio-economic conditions, traditional practices and cultural beliefs, women, girls, children and persons with disabilities (PwDs) are often affected disproportionately as reflected in global data on disaster induced death, injury, loss of livelihood and increase risk of sexual abuse, and other forms of GBV heightens. The presence of armed actors, displacement, broken social and protective networks and limited services create an environment where these groups are at acute risk. In this case violence leaves significant short- and long-term physical, sexual, reproductive and mental health consequences, personal and social well-being challenges. WHO estimates globally, also reveal that nearly one-third (30%) of ever-partnered women have experienced physical and/or sexual violence by a partner, and about 7% of women aged 15 and older have experienced sexual violence by a non-partner, with wide variations by region.
Humanitarian context analysis
Cameroon is one of the countries around the world experiencing an increase level of GBV especially among the most vulnerable groups. Since 2016 a sociopolitical crisis broke out in the Northwest and Southwest regions that has affected individuals, families, communities and institutions severely with worsening effects on vulnerable groups. This crisis later aggravated into an armed conflict leading to loss of lives, displacement of people and families, loss of properties including relevant documents making the population in the affected areas more vulnerable to diverse challenges. This situation has exposed more women, girls, boys and older people especially those with disabilities, minority groups and person living with terminal illnesses to increase level of insecurity and violence characterized by physical abuse, rape, maltreatment, exploitation etc. OCHA 2020 reports that, the number of reported GBV cases keep increasing in Cameroon with 676 cases reported in September compared to 567 cases the previous month due to the ongoing anglophone crisis in the Northwest and Southwest Regions. The Covid-19 pandemic which began in the early months of 2020 has compounded the situation and increased the rate of violence among these persons due movement restriction and economic hardship. According to OCHA Situation Report No.3 as of 31 January 2019, the insecurity has forced more than 430,000 people to flee their homes. Many people displaced who were living semi nomadic lives have lost not only their herds but also other livelihood opportunities and are now found in neighboring regions that present as host communities with more than 380,000 needing shelter, and some 418,000 needing Non-Food Item (NFI) assistance. Meanwhile, access to basic health care has been difficult as most health personnel have vacated the facilities due to the high level of insecurity with a negative toll on pregnant women and lactating mothers. Economically, women, girls and children have lost or abandoned their regular sources of livelihood – and thus have become unable to access essential needs – are hence more vulnerable to all sorts of abuse. The impact of crisis on the sexual and reproductive health of women and girls is huge. Per our program data, an increasing number of adolescent girls are reported pregnant, find it difficult to access timely ANC services resulting in increased preventable complications and/or maternal and neonatal deaths. The conflict has resulted in increase in trauma cases from rape, physical abuse, domestic violence, denial of resources, harmful traditional practices, witness killings.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Needs assessment Due to limited GBV actors and services in NW, SW, West and Littoral regions of Cameroon as well as lack of awareness on available medical services, survivors are unable to access quality, comprehensive and inclusive life-saving GBV services. Inadequate startup capital for GBV survivors to start economic ventures as a means of generating income for livelihood sustenance increases their vulnerability to more severe GBV as many individuals especially women and girls now engage in sexual activities (e.g. sex workers for food rations, safe shelter, and access to basic goods) that can expose them to a higher risk of HIV and other STIs and mental illnesses with no prospect of accessing healthcare in case of need. Intervention strategies and Key Activities The quality of GBV services will be improved via targeted training, support to the health and community structures and mechanisms. The evidenced based effective model of service delivery, the Community of Practice, which the CBCHS adopted and successfully used in the NW will be strengthened and scaled up to other communities hosting IDPs to improve timely case identification and comprehensive case management. The project will strengthen the referral pathway for GBV survivors between implementing partners and stakeholders. Key activities will include;• Provide holistic support to people at risk of GBV including GBV survivors• Strengthen legal, medical, mental and psychosocial assistance for GBV survivors in conflict affected areas• Conduct safety audits and risk and vulnerability mapping for GBV• Strengthen the capacity of operational organizations and relevant line ministries and local authorities including security forces and the police on the holistic response to GBV • Strengthen GBV prevention activities at community-level including Sensitization of conflict-affected communities, local authorities, traditional leaders and religious leaders• Provide interventions for the prevention and mitigation of risks and response to GBV affecting children and women• Provide basic vocational training and economic support to women and girls including dedicated livelihood activities to women and girls victims of sexual exploitation. Expected outcomes• 50,000 community members of the targeted regions are sensitized to available GBV services in an inclusive manner• At least 2,800 People at risk of GBV including GBV survivors especially the most vulnerable (IDP women, girls, children, PwDs and elderly persons) have access to GBV services and receive adequate assistance within the targeted communities• 100 survivors receive legal services, 250 survivors receive medical assistance including post-rape kits and 200 hundred receive mental car 500 receive PSS• 200 CBOs and local authorities receive adequate knowledge and support are carrying out GBV prevention and management services in target communities• 2,500 women and children receive GBV risk mitigation and prevention interventions• 100 IDP women and girls with disabilities trained and involved in sustainable income-generating activities and participating in village saving schemes• 20 Health facilities are adequately supported to clinically manage GBV cases.• 200 Community Sensitizers trained on sensitization on GBV mainstreaming• 8 Sign Language Interpreters trained and engaged• 100 traditional leaders, 200 religious leaders and other local authorities are trained and engaged in GBV prevention strategies within communities• 220 local security forces including the police are trained on how to identify, respond to GBV issues • 40 GBV services providers trained and dealing with male survivors • 8 clinical and GBV case managers are engaged capacitated to facilitate the management of women and children victims within the target regions• 2 One-stop-shops are established and two existing ones strengthened to provide GBV essential and lifesaving services• 650 indigene GBV survivors care and treatment subsidized
