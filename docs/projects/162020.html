Project Summary

Action Against Hunger operationalizes prevention and mitigation of sexual and gender-based violence through its integrated Nutrition, Mental Health, WASH and Livelihoods programming basis to the comprehensive gender analysis and key sectorial assessments. Mental Health & Psychosocial Support to vulnerable women, men, boys and girls across age groups has been proven non-intrusive entry door to identify survivors of violence, provide appropriate case management including referral and follow-up services. ACF MHCPGP sector organizes regular capacity-building trainings for internal staff and volunteers on Types of GBV, Humanitarian Protection Principles, Core Humanitarian Standards, Inter-Agency Referral Mechanism/timely update on referral pathways for operations field team, and Case Management. Psychologists and Psychosocial Workers engaged in GBV case management are provided technical trauma-focused, evidence-based counselling training with adequate monitoring and supportive supervision by Programme Managers with Clinical Psychology background. In order to improve community's adaptation to refugee situation and camp living, various awareness and sensitization sessions on conflict management, mitigation of protection risks, and emergency preparedness measures are taken up to improve resilience, social cohesion, and peaceful coexistence through various support groups, key stakeholder groups, and men forums. ACF operated Baby Friendly Spaces, Safe Spaces for Women & Girls, OTP, TSFP and Stabilization Centers are consistently utilized for equitable, easily accessible GBV prevention and protection services by qualified and trained Psychosocial Workers and Psychologists. ACF programs strongly adhere to Gender and PSEA policies, follow complaints and feedback mechanism open to internal staff and community stakeholders, and have established systems and processes to manage grievance redressal through internal complaints committees involving senior management from head quarters. Gender and protection considerations are optimally mainstreamed across organizational technical sectors, implementation programs and response actions.

Needs

Sexual and gender-based violence remains a pervasive issue and has had negative consequences for the humanitarian response management for the most vulnerable women and girls amongst the Rohingya refugee settled in camps in Cox's Bazar sub-district. Due to resource constraints and lack of qualified service providers, the insufficient outreach to persons at heightened risk, especially persons with disabilities, elderly persons without support, adolescent girls and young single women, and persons in need of psychosocial support is a priority challenge that remains to be addressed. Only 35% of GBV survivors in need are reported to receive combined psychosocial support and legal assistance, while 61% of case workers are overburdened and unable to provide adequate support. Majority 66% of refugees have reported feeling unsafe and insecure in camp environment (ISCG Joint MSNA, June 2019).

Activities or outputs

Action Against Hunger (ACF) Mental Health, Care Practices, Gender and Protection (MHCPGP) program implements an array of comprehensive, community-based mental health and psychosocial support services for the crisis affected Forcibly Displaced Myanmar Nationals (FDMNs - Rohingya refugees) in order to build long-term resilience and holistic well-being with special attention to intersectionality of gender and ensuing protection needs of the population. Project activities are channelled through established in-patient and out-patient treatment facilities, and Safe Spaces for Women & Girls (SSWGs), as well through referral and follow-up coordination with partner agencies. Project-specific activities include: • Comprehensive community-assessment to identify barriers and boosters to access and utilization of combined MHPSS, Gender, and Protection services for FDMNs and host populations• Provision of quality, dignified, and accessible GBV prevention and mitigation services by establishing and regularizing Safe Spaces for Women & Girls (SSWGs) in 3 refugee camps (*existing centers in Camp 14 and 16 since 2018, with proposition to open new SSWG in Camp 26)• Provision of qualified case management services to survivors of SGBV including family-based psychosocial supports, individualized counselling, referral and follow-up• Formalize protection support groups of vulnerable women and girls, survivors of SGBV with provision of trauma-focused structured PSS, group psychotherapies, essential life-skills and SRH education to improve resilience and self-esteem• Establishing community-based women and child protection forums of key community stakeholders and volunteers proactively leading sensitization regarding GBV concerns, local grievance redressal, social cohesion and peaceful coexistence, and improving care-seeking and referral to appropriate GBV and protection services • Involving men and boys towards gender equality programming through men forums addressing GBV and protection concerns, prevention and mitigation of existing risks in the camp settings• Organize capacity-building trainings, consistent monitoring and supportive supervision for ACF internal staff and partner agencies engaged in GBV protection services (sectors and working group staffs from Nutirtion, WaSH, Food Security and CwC) to improve frontline response for SGBV incidents through structured PSS and case management. Capacity-building training topics include Types of GBV, Humanitarian Protection Principles, Case Management, Inter-Agency Referral Mechanism - with systematic monitoring and supportive supervision embedded in the training curricula
