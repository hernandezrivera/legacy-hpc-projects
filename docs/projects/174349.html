Project Summary

The goal is to provide 150,000 crisis-affected host Bangladeshi and Rohingya children and adolescents with life-saving information aimed at building an understanding of their rights and responsibilities, as well as strengthening disaster awareness, and other survival skills, to better manage their personal water safety and risk related to flooding and to promote globally recognised water safety practices. The project would use the "Aquatic Survival Programme", which is an open-source programme developed and used by a wide range of international water safety organisations. It has been robustly tested for cultural context and alignment to "Do No Harm" principles. CIPRB (SeaSafe) have run this programme in government schools in Cox’s Bazar town for four years. Helvetas & MOAS wish to expand across Teknaf and Ukhia host communities, and refugee populations. The core of the inclusive "level one" programme is a "flip book" or "flash card" resource. This flip book has ten key safety messages that the children are taught with the instructor leading them through the conversation. The children then are tasked with a creative activity based around the water safety messages to demonstrate their understanding and to share the messages widely with their friends & family. Helvetas & MOAS will provide ToT training (and a copy of the flip book) to the adults who are in leadership/instructor roles in the community who can then deliver the water safety messages as part of their usual activities. The ToT includes education around awareness of PSEA, equity and inclusion issues, particularly children with disabilities who are disproportionately vulnerable to the risk of drowning. The ToT workshops will be delivered at a variety of existing sites, and as such doesn't require any space that is not already in use as an educational facility. Additionally, as part of this project there are plans to pilot an adolescent lead peer-educator modality for the water safety messages. Globally, this project has an excellent track record of buidling social cohesion as drowning doesn't discriminate, it is a hazard faced by both Host community and refugees, by mixing groups and broadening access opportunities can be created to build social cohesion in specific target areas. MOAS has good operational links with CIPRB who have been tasked by the GoB DGHS to produce the National Plan for Drowning Prevention. MOAS & Helvetas have a track record of good levels of sector coordination across Site Management, FSS, Health and other sectors, should this project be approved the team commit to contributing to CPSS to the same high level. The team have much experience with effective camp level coordination, evidenced by successful projects in 2019 and 2020. The targeted areas have been selected based on several criteria including: serious risk of flooding; recorded drowning incidents; existing working relationship in the selected camps.

1 - Identified Needs

"This project directly addresses the points raised in the Education Sector Multi-Year Response Plan Needs Overview: ""Education in emergency / post emergency recovery provide children with life-saving information aimed at building an understanding of their rights and responsibilities, as well as strengthening disaster awareness, hygiene, and other survival skills."" Additionally, an ACAPS Thematic Report from December 2019 states that parents were concerned about the drowning risk to children in monsoon season. This is supported by SMSD sector data on drowning fatalities, and specific concerns about drowning fatalities raised directly to MOAS by CPSS.In 2014, the World Health Organisation published the Global Report on Drowning, which highlighted the need to address the global issue of deaths by drowning through a multi-faceted multi-sectoral approach. The report describes drowning as “a neglected public health issue” and identifies drowning as the single largest killer of children between one and five years old in Bangladesh. With annual monsoon rains and high risk of cyclones, low lying, riverine Bangladesh experiences devastating flooding on a regular basis. Given the high number of ponds and traditional water sources in the country, the daily risk of drowning is extremely high, without considering the additional risks posed by disasters such as monsoon rains and cyclones. Whilst drowning is a risk for the general population, children between one and five and young male adolescents are at particular risk. Preventative actions to improve community and family safety are necessary to reduce and reverse this trend. The WHO report identifies that most deaths of under-fives occurs in the morning, most often between the hours of 10.00am and 1.00pm. This period coincides with when the mother or carer is active around the home and children are more likely to be left mostly unsupervised. The number of open water bodies in Bangladesh means that tragically, over 75% of child deaths by drowning occur within 20 metres from the child’s home. In the densely populated refugee camps of Cox’s Bazar, the annual monsoon and cyclone season increases the risk of drowning because of the rising levels of existing water bodies, flooding, and storm surges from the Bay of Bengal. Additionally, given the hot and humid conditions during monsoon season, rivers and ponds become irresistible, refreshing places for children to play. Whilst we have this clear evidence from the host community of Bangladesh, there is little evidence from any official data sources to track the number of lives lost within the refugee settlements. However, through Site Management data collected, MOAS has identified that a second group with high rates of drowning are young people – predominantly male - between the ages of twelve and fifteen, with most incidents happening between 1.00pm and 3.00pm, when young people are coming back from school, learning centres or Madrassas. Previous schemes run in rural Bangladesh, providing day care and swimming lessons to young children have proven effective in reducing deaths by drowning. However, MOAS believes that by training trainers in water safety, first aid and risk reduction we can achieve significant and sustainable results. No national program for water safety has been rolled out for children or adolescents in Bangladesh.A number of incidents of ‘double drownings’ have been recorded in the refugee camps, where it is believed one young person attempted rescue of the another and both were injured or killed. The need for young people to be more aware of water safety and better able manage risks associated with flooding is imperative."
