Project Summary

In line with the UNHCR GBV policy, the Inter-Agency minimum standards for GBV in emergency, the IASC guidelines, the GBV strategic objectives of the HRP as well as the “Call to Action” road map 2021-2025, the project is designed with the three areas of Prevention, Risk Mitigation and Response to address gender-based violence (GBV). The project aims at strengthening protection of women, men, boys and girls internally displaced and returnees including those with specific needs against exposure to risks of GBV and ensuring that all survivors have adequate and timely access to quality services in Borno and Adamawa states.
Additionally, the project will address specific issues related to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) involving humanitarian workers, Security actors and leaders in the camps and host communities.
In prevention, the project will work to engage all women, men, boys and girls IDPs and returnees to address root causes of GBV such as gender inequality and unequal power dynamics in the community applying community-based approach which entails active participation of community-based structures and innovative approach such as Engaging Men in Accountable Practices” (EMAP), a one year primary GBV prevention intervention created by the International Rescue Committee. The framework for EMAP is centered around Accountable Practice which provides a method and structure for honoring women’s leadership and developing male engagement in a way that improves the lives of women and girls. Various capacity building will target IDPs and returnees leaders (religious and traditional), peer to peer groups, community volunteers. Raising awareness through mass sensitization campaigns will be conducted in and out of camps on GBV, effective behavior and social changes, sexual exploitation and abuse.
Risk mitigation is a key component to reduce GBV risks of women, men, boys and girls and promote their safety. The project will promote economic empowerment of women through life skills acquisition as a way to reduce vulnerability and mitigate risks of GBV and Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Targeted assistance of dignity kits and alternative energy sources (efficient stoves, briquette) will be supported as recommended by the joint NE SAFE assessment. UNHCR priorities the most vulnerable including GBV survivors identified through protection monitoring and GBV intervention.
For the integrated Response, the project will ensure maintaining quality case management and referral pathways to ensure GBV survivors have safe and meaningful access to safe, timely, confidential and adequate services to immediately address survivors’ needs such as physical safety, health, psychosocial support, legal assistance, material support and economic empowerment in line with the survivor-centred approach. UNHCR manages Women Girls Safe Spaces where women and girls feel physically and emotionally safe, comfortable and able to express themselves and discuss sensitive issues and opportunities for social networking, solidarity-building, confidence-nurturing among fellow community members. This project will be complemented by the UNHCR “access to justice” project which fight impunity by facilitating survivors’ access to justice for via legal representation, counseling and awareness.

1 - Needs

Since the beginning of the ongoing crisis in 2009, more than 2.1 million men, women and children have been forced to flee their homes in North-East Nigeria. The crisis coupled with soaring inflation and climate change, has compounded the vulnerabilities of the displaced men, women, girls, and boys resulting in massive unmet needs. According to UNHCR, 363,000 people have sought refuge in neighbouring Cameroon (121,000), Chad (21,000), and Niger (221,000) as of 30 September 2023. In the BAY states, a total of 8.3 million people are in need of protection and humanitarian assistance according to the Northeast 2023 Nigeria Humanitarian Needs Overview. This includes 2.0 million IDPs (in and out of camp), 1.5 million returnees, and 4.8 million host community members. According to the Protection Sector / GBV Sub-Sector’s analysis of GBV in the HNO 2022 for Northeast Nigeria, GBV continues to be a major protection concern facing IDP women and girls. They often find themselves trapped in a vicious cycle of violence, including sexual and gender-based violence, exacerbated by pre-existing gender inequalities. GBV incidents, especially intimate-partner violence (IPV) including sexual violence, continue to be reported across the BAY states. The vast majority affected are women or girls, though a significant number of boys are also victims of sexual violence. An estimated 1.3 million individuals need GBV prevention and response services across the BAY states in 2022 (of whom 46% are IDPs, 23% returnees, and 31% host communities; 82% are women or girls). The majority (63%) of those in need are in Borno State, 22% in Adamawa and 15% in Yobe. The GBV sub-sector uses the Protection Sector severity rankings and the severity analysis for the GBV Area of Responsibility (AoR) to calculate the number of people targeted in each state and LGA. Based on these analyses, the GBV sub-sector and its partners plan to target 1,030,000 people (46% IDPs, 23% returnees, 31% host communities; overall 82% are women or girls) with GBV prevention and response interventions in 51 LGAs (25 in Borno, 15 in Yobe and 12 in Adamawa) with the severest needs, as informed by the JIAF and other analyses from the GBV Information Management System. The majority (63%) of those to be targeted are in Borno State, 22% are in Adamawa and 15% in Yobe. As highlighted by the GBV Information Management System data, GBV continues to be a major protection concern for women and girls in north-east Nigeria and thus the reason why they remain the main target. Men and boys and other minority groups will also be targeted.

2 - Activities

This project aims to improve the protection and well-being of affected populations through effective GBV prevention and mitigation activities, and response to survivor’s needs through quality case management, psychosocial support and life skills activities. In turn, this project aims to restore human dignity and reduce suffering in the targeted LGAs. Through this project, UNHCR will: • Strengthen capacity of community-based GBV structures for awareness raising, prevention and referral for specialized services (women & girls support network, male engagement and support network, advisory group, and community volunteers) • Identify key male leaders in the community (religious, traditional) and engage them in prevention of GBV and SEA and peaceful dialogue • Implement innovative approach - Engaging Men in Accountable Practices” (EMAP), a one year primary GBV prevention intervention centered around Accountable Practice which provides a method and structure for honoring women’s leadership and developing male engagement in a way that improves the lives of women and girls. • Implement the SAFE approach to ensure IDP’s and returnees have safe access to fuel and energy by exploring distribution of alternative sources of energy • Life skills acquisition activities will be used to promote economic empowerment of women to reduce economic vulnerability of women and girls • Advocacy for protection of women and children. • Provide psychosocial support, case management and GBV critical material support to GBV survivors • Manage Women and Girls Safe Space (WGSS) in the camps and host community to provide information, educational and recreational activities. • Strengthen Case Management and Referral System • Provide psychosocial support including life skill acquisition • Dignity kits support for vulnerable girls and women including survivors living in the camps. • Contribute to the GBV Information Management System.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

Adhering to the UN triple nexus of humanitarian, development, and peace considerations, UNHCR is committed to collaborating with partners to provide durable solutions to internal displacement. This involves addressing immediate needs while empowering affected populations to enhance their capacity to tackle emerging challenges. The focus is on managing existing and potential tensions within the local context, ensuring more sustainable and effective outcomes. The strategy emphasizes context-based programming, actively engaging local communities in the development and implementation of projects to yield tangible and lasting results aligned with the specific needs of the affected populations. The approach is rooted in the centrality of protection, with a key focus on long-term durable solutions, including livelihoods. UNHCR's involvement in joint risk analysis incorporates conflict sensitivity, disability inclusion, and gender-sensitivity, fostering a shared understanding of the risks, needs, and vulnerabilities of the affected population for informed planning and programming.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The key protection principles underpinning the provision of humanitarian assistance informed the design of this project and as such will be observed during the project implementation. UNHCR will adopt a rights-based approach in the planning, implementation and evaluation during the entirety of the project. To ensure comprehensive protection mainstreaming across this project, the following measures will be taken for each of the protection principles: • Prioritize safety and dignity and avoid doing harm during the project implementation, UNHCR will closely monitor the security situation of the area of intervention, and mobile teams will use safe and authorized roads to access people in need. • To ensure meaningful and equal access, in the identification of beneficiaries, men, women, boys and girls will be prioritized based on need alone. Particularly vulnerable groups such as female- and child-headed households, but also elderly and persons with disability will be prioritized. The composition of the assessment teams will reflect gender balance, to facilitate contact with women and girls. • If necessary, additional measures will be taken to reach persons with disabilities and others extremely vulnerable. This can be the case of disabled persons unable to reach the protection desks. • In order to promote accountability, participation and empowerment of beneficiaries, beneficiaries’ participation to the project will be promoted by involving, to the widest extent possible, men, women, boys and girls of different ages in the community-based structures. • Establish measures aimed at preventing and responding to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, bearing in mind the UN zero tolerance policy. • All along project implementation, UNHCR will monitor protection, age, and gender aspects and will address any arising issues. To ensure a comprehensive response, all the findings will be shared with the Protection Sector as well as other relevant Sectors, with whom UNHCR will maintain close coordination.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

UNHCR will strengthen community-based complaint/feedback mechanisms, which will allow men, women, boys and girls to express any complaint; beneficiaries will be informed of the existence of such mechanisms, and it will be ensured that they are comfortable in accessing it. UNHCR will leverage on existing complaints and feedback mechanisms including toll-free/hotline, confidential email, complaint boxes, and reports to UNHCR or partner staff or community volunteers. Focus group discussions with people from different age, gender and diverse origin will be conducted regularly to collect feedback from affected population on available services and as well as protection risks. UNHCR will ensure that women and girls support network, male engagement and support network, advisory group, and community volunteers contribute to strengthening community-based complaint/feedback mechanisms. Through these, UNHCR will ensure that issues of concern to individuals, as well as, to the community are brought to its attention as well as to the attention of other partners, for intervention. UNHCR will also leverage on its active membership of the Taskforce on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and its Age Gender Diversity mainstreaming policy too. UNHCR and partners will also ensure that staff involved in the project comply with its code of conduct and the SG’s special measures for protection from sexual exploitation and abuse.
