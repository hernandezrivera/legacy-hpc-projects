Project Summary

Addressing the gaps in services to one of the most marginalised and vulnerable groups in Gaza, and factoring in differences along age, marital status, geographical location and health, and drawing on a gendered-responsive, holistic, multi-sectoral, GBV-centred, human-rights and needs-tailored approaches, the project will provide services to women and girls with physical and learning disabilities in the Gaza Strip, especially in the northern and southern parts (North: Al Qaraya al Badawiya al Maslakh, Beit Hanun, Beit Lahiya. South: Khan Younis, Mawasi Khan Younis, and Rafah). With the help of experienced operating local partners in the field, Women’s Health Centre (WHC) – Jabalya and Culture and Free Thought Association (CFTA), the project will provide GBV case management, psychosocial support, legal support, health and reproductive health services, referral, and cash for work opportunities.

1 - Needs

According to the 2017 statistics from the Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS)1 released in July 2020, the number of persons with disabilities was 255,228, constituting 5.8% of the total population in Palestine; 127,266 in the West Bank ( 5.1%) and 127,962 in the Gaza Strip (6.8%). The disability rate among males was 6.3% and 5.4% among females, and mobility difficulties are the most common type of difficulties, which reaching 32.2% with a prevalence of 2.9% among the population. It has been noted that in the oPt an increasing proportion of women and girls with disabilities experience a variety of intersecting forms of psychological, social, physical and sexual violence. Between 2007 and 2017, the disability rate in the oPt increased for males and females from 4.7 per cent to 5.8 per cent. The Gaza Strip experiences a disability rate of 6.8 per cent, compared to 5.1 per cent in the West Bank. (Gender Alert, UNW 2020) According to the Ministry of Health data until 24 September, the total number of positive cases (Covid-19) reached 2613, out of which 1746 are active cases. According to the Ministry of Social Development data, the number of quarantined persons in the Gaza Strip reached 1649 of whom 34% were women, 24% were children, and the number of quarantined persons with disabilities was 10, including 4 women and 6 men. In the Gaza Strip, there are 15 quarantine centers, most of which do not provide the necessary facilities for persons with disabilities including women. Impacted by entrenched social norms and lack of resources at both household and state levels, women and girls with disabilities in Palestine “are denied the opportunity to make decisions, participate in society, find employment or enjoy their right to inheritance”(UNFPA, 2019). Women who are able to find work encounter exploitation, underpayment, and relegation to menial functions that do not match their ability, while those who attempt to contest their discrimination under the law may find themselves denied because of antedated definitions of legal capacity. Across the oPt, voices of women with disabilities are routinely ignored, even in times of crisis. A survey of women and girls with disabilities in the oPt, undertaken in March and April 2020, discovered that “no official body, whether at the level of a ministry or a department, has contacted any woman or girl with a disability in order to examine their needs, or to check on their conditions in light of the emergency, whether in the West Bank or Gaza Strip”. (Gender Alert, UNW 2020). Violence against women and girls with disabilities is more serious and common. The PCBS Violence Survey in 2019 indicated that 37% of women with disabilities who are currently married or who have been married experienced violence at least once by their husbands, in the 12 months preceding the survey: of which 30 per cent were in the West Bank, while 42 per cent in the Gaza Strip. Meanwhile, 50% of these women who experienced violence were subjected to psychological violence, while 20% of the same group subjected to physical violence at least once by their husbands. According to UNFPA study (2019), an increasing proportion of women and girls with disabilities experience various forms of psychological, social, physical and sexual violence. They often exist under the authority of a guardian, are deprived of the right to make their own decisions, coerced to adopt certain behaviours and experience humiliation, degradation and exploitation at home and in society, in addition to different forms of discrimination, exclusion, exploitation, violence, abuse, isolation, confinement and lack of inclusion in family or public sphere, inevitably leading to increased risk and higher vulnerability to violence, compared to other social groups. Though limited data exists, according to a UNFPA study, the high prevalence of violence affecting women and girls with disabilities is most commonly against those with learning disabilities, followed by visual, hearing and physical impairments. Women and girls with disabilities have difficulty accessing sexual and reproductive health services due to several factors. Factors include limited awareness of the sexual and reproductive health rights of this particular group, inadequate training and poor interventions seeking to provide them with such services, and physical and cultural barriers, such as stigma, poverty, and isolation within institutions and homes. While protection mechanisms are weak in general, Gaza faces more significant challenges. These include the higher disability rate due to the recurrent rounds of conflict and the deteriorating political and economic conditions, which in turn increase the risk of violence for women and girls with disabilities in Gaza. When it comes to this group access to services, significant obstacles and challenges remain in place, primarily in the form of the weak protection system; limited service delivery to survivors of GBV; failure to respond to the special needs of women with disabilities survivors of GBV. There is also an apparent lack of ability on part on women with disabilities who are survivors of violence to access information and services in relation to prevention of violence, protection, recovery and others. It is important to note that in the absence of adequate services and income opportunities people living with disabilities are often dependent on their relatives to satisfy their needs, which creates additional responsibilities for caregivers – who are mostly women (UN Women, 2018). WWDs indicated that CFW promotes their self-reliance by assisting them to begin, rebuild or expand their livelihoods capacity, thereby improving chances of recovery and resilience. In testimony of this, four of the participating women with disabilities explained that their relationship at household level was impacted as they were previously perceived as a burden for their families. (CFW Impact Report: UN 2020)

2 - Activities

Project Localities: (Al Qaraya al Badawiya al Maslakh, Beit Hanun, Beit Lahiya) and Southern area of the Gaza Strip (Khan Younis, Mawasi Khan Younis, and Rafah). This will include addressing the needs of women and girls with disabilities affected Covid-19 and GVB. With regards to COVID_19 mitigation measures, the project partners will of course put in place online services in order to ensure that services are provided under a situation of a lockdown or in case there is difficulty facing WGWDs to access the services. The partners will also map online capacities/infrastructure of beneficiaries and will consider providing internet connection/ICT support to needy WGWDs based on thorough assessments of the needs of beneficiaries and within allocated resources of the project.UN Women’s and partners’ interventions will be carefully designed following holistic/multisectoral and survivor-centred approaches that will take into account individual differences among women and girls with disabilities such as area of residence, age, marital status, and economic status with an understanding that such differences (age, location, health, economic and marital status) can all be drivers of vulnerability and need to be addresses with tailored approaches. UN Women will ensure throughout project implementation that multisectoral GBV services are provided according to the GBV case management Standard Operating Procedures (SOP) as approved by the Ministry of Women’s Affairs and that project interventions are well-coordinated among the partners and well-consulted with the GBV sub-cluster, the Protection cluster, the child protection working group, the disability working group, and the Food Security cluster, Health Cluster, cash working group. UN Women will also make use of tools and guidelines that have been developed by humanitarian partners such as GBV case management and referral SoPs, CFW assessments, different tools on services for persons with disabilities, child protection protocols, and the Database and guidance of the Cash for Work working group. UN Women will lead on developing the analysis in full coordination with the Protection cluster, the GBV sub-cluster and the Child Protection working group.1-2 Conduct two training sessions for key humanitarian actors and women organizations including CFTA and WHC-Jabalya on protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Output 2: Women and girls with disabilities have improved access to multi-sectoral services (GBV counselling, Legal assistance, structured psychosocial support, health services, sexual and reproductive health services, referral, and awareness raising) • Activity 2-1: Provide legal counselling,court representation and legal awareness to women with disabilities in target communities. Currently Hawwa Zahra is being developed to provide online SGBV counselling services and referral to women and girls survivors of GBV • Activity 2-3: Organize 100 awareness sessions on GBV, disability and sexual and reproductive health rights and services in identified localities in the North and South of the Gaza Strip (Target 2500) Activity 2-4: Provide health and sexual and reproductive health services to women and girls with disabilities (Target: 150 WWD) Activity 2-5: Support two self-help groups for parents and caregivers of girls with learning disabilities (One in the South and one in the North) and organize 5 meetings with them to help them exchange best practices and provider peer help support. 3 - Indicators

Monitoring will be a continuous and consistent process occurring throughout the programme’s life span. Ongoing monitoring will be led by the UN Women in collaboration with implementing partners and other stakeholders including beneficiaries. The monitoring tools and system will be designed to focus on upward accountability (i.e. towards the donor, humanitarian partners) and downward accountability (i.e. towards programme beneficiaries and primary stakeholders). The programme workplan will be used to measure how well activities have been implemented and whether these contribute to the overall achievement of programme objectives. As part of continuous monitoring throughout programme implementation, UN Women and partners will ensure the collection of sex and age disaggregated data (SADD), which will be inclusive to the extent possible of data on women and girls with disabilities. SADD will be used to inform project direction towards being more inclusive and sensitive to addressing gender-based vulnerabilities. UN Women and partners will employ various M&E tools applying their internal oversight mechanisms including the internal monitoring and reporting mechanisms such as reporting to HQ, the regular monitoring activities by its monitoring/project officers, and the partners’ monitoring. UN Women and partners will regularly convene project teams’ meetings, field visits and discussions with relevant clusters to assess progress via set results. The partners will conduct a pre and post impact assessment and this will support the fine-tuning of baselines, conducting also project midterm FGDs to monitor progress. Overall, UN Women and partners will collect information on a quarterly basis. UN Women programme team will ensure full consultation with the humanitarian clusters including the Protection cluster, child protection cluster, the GBV sub-cluster and OCHA and also with project partners to decide on how the activities can be geared to supporting women and girls with disabilities affected by COVID-19. Depending on the situation, a team of women early responders will be composed out of service providers who can do outreach and services that are home based to WGWDs. Cash for work alternative schemes will be discussed with the cash working group. 9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Do No HarmAs a member of the Humanitarian Country Team in the oPt, UN Women operates under the SPHERE principles. Do No Harm is a key humanitarian principle under this framework, and guides UN Women’s programme development and implementation. Thus, UN Women ensures that assistance does no harm, or minimizes the harm that may invariantly be done. In this regard, UN Women will work with partners to ensure that the cash for work is coupled with protection-related interventions such as GBV case management and awareness raising of men and that to ensure a protective and enabling environment for women with disabilities, where income becomes an enabler to live a life that is free from abuse. On community sensitization, UN Women will support the partners to carefully develop their messages and pre-test them with the target groups and with victims/survivors to ensure that they will be effective to the target audience and will help prevent any unintended harm. As for psychosocial and legal counselling, UN Women will ensure it is provided as per IASC guidelines and that of the MHPSS WG and the legal task force.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UN Women and CSO partners will adopt a survivor-centred approach in supporting women and girls with disabilities including victims and survivors of GBV. This will ensure that survivors’ rights and needs are prioritized first and foremost. The survivor-centred approach aims to create a supportive environment in which the survivor’s rights are respected and in which women survivors are treated with dignity and respect. The approach helps to promote the survivor’s recovery and ability to identify and express needs and wishes, as well as to reinforce capacity to make decisions about possible interventions. Through this approach, women will be provided with options and will have access to information instead of being told what to do. -The project will include several activities that focus on GBV prevention such as cash for work; an intervention that was assessed by women beneficiaries as decreasing household tension . Providing economic opportunities to women also counters existing trends of negative coping mechanisms emerging from dire lack of resources such as substance abuse and school dropout. Coupling cash for work schemes with GBV services will empower women and girls with disabilities seek solutions out of abusive environment and become active actors in building their communities. Other activities include awareness raising that target women and men (also caregivers of WGWDs) which aims at addressing root causes and promoting behavioral changes that can limit GBV against women and girls with disabilities from occurring/reoccurring. Some of the interventions such as psychosocial support will address risk-taking behavior and conflict-preventive measures. UN Women will also support building the capacity of local actors in the area of GBV prevention and PSEA to ensure ownership and sustainability of the approach. -Given the multisectoral nature of the interventions, UN Women will ensure to coordinate the project implementation with the humanitarian clusters namely the Protection Cluster led by OHCHR, the GBV Sub-cluster led by UNFPA, the child protection WG (led by UNICEF), the Disability working group, and the Cash for Work working group led by Action against Hunger to avoid duplication, ensure proper protection mainstreaming, emphasize complementarity and maintain the spirit of delivering as One. UN Women will also ensure to team up with different civil society organizations as partners in the implementation of the project; capitalizing on their different expertise and knowledge of the affected communities and populations. Civil society partners will be responsible for coordinating the implementation of the activities with different community-based organizations and civil society coalitions.
