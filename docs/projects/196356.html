Project Summary

Over eleven years of crisis in Syria has drastically eroded the living conditions of Palestine refugees and Syrian nationals alike. The rapid depreciation of the Syrian pound accompanied by staggering inflation levels, the impact of Lebanon's financial and banking crisis, and the tightened trade restrictions imposed on the country since June 2020, have all contributed to a worsening of the socio-economic conditions. The COVID-19 pandemic has led to additional stress on basic services that has impacted the livelihoods of Palestine refugees and Syrians.
To support Palestine refugee youth access to employment opportunities, the UNRWA TVET programme will continue to provide short-term courses to 800 students in Damascus, Dera’a, Homs, Aleppo, Latakia, and Hama offering a variety of specializations that respond to the needs of the local market. Short-term courses (three months on average) are designed to strengthen technical skills, with minimal financial costs to the refugee students, to improve their prospects of accessing the labour market. In addition to the courses offered in the Damascus Training Centre, UNRWA will continue to expand its provision of long-term courses in its vocational training centres (VTCs) in Aleppo, Homs, Latakia and Dera’a and will introduce these in Hama in 2023. This is in response to the increasing demand of refugee youth in these areas for similar courses to those offered in Damascus and to promote equal access for all young Palestine refugees to livelihood opportunities to support their resilience.
UNRWA will also provide employability and soft skills sessions to TVET students to encourage their confidence and abilities in approaching companies and communicating with potential future employers during job interviews. The Agency will maintain the dormitory at the Damascus Training Centre to provide a limited number of students, who live in remote locations, with accommodation to support the continuation of their TVET training. Transportation of vulnerable students will also be subsidised to mitigate the impact of the fuel crisis and associated rising transportation costs on the ability of students to access VTCs.
The various components of UNRWA's interventions in 2022-2023 will also contribute to supporting refugees who have spontaneously returned to their places of residence either internally or from other countries. Where possible, UNRWA will aim at restarting services in camps that were heavily destroyed during the conflict (Dera’a, Yarmouk and Ein el Tal) through the rehabilitation of the Agency’s installations (including schools and clinics) in these camps.
Finally, depending on the availability of funds, UNRWA will explore the feasibility of conducting small-scale cash-for-work initiatives in 2023 mainly focusing on unskilled job placements supporting UNRWA works in camps and Palestine refugee gatherings.

3 - Needs

Out of the 438,000 Palestine refugees currently estimated to be inside Syria, two-thirds have been displaced at least once since the beginning of the conflict. Prolonged displacement further compounds existing instabilities, and entrenches unemployment, and loss of livelihoods opportunities within established Palestine refugees’ communities and camps. An UNRWA survey conducted in May 2021 found that 82 per cent of the refugees interviewed now live on less than US$ 1.9 a day and that 48 per cent of total household expenditure was spent on food, indicating severe distress.In 2020 and 2021, the already deteriorating economic situation has been further compounded by measures taken to limit the spread of COVID-19, which had a negative impact on the availability of employment opportunities. As such, an assessment conducted in August 2020 by UNRWA on the socioeconomic impact of COVID-19 indicated that Palestine refugees’ ability to secure their income has faced a devastating setback. Close to 30 per cent of Palestine refugee respondents said that the head of the household had lost her or his job over the past months, while 30 per cent also said that the wages of the head of household had reduced due to COVID-19. Close to half of Palestine refugee households mentioned that they currently have sufficient money to cover daily needs just for one day and only 21 per cent said the household has a stable monthly income. In such context, livelihood interventions are critical.

4 - Implementation of activities

In 2022-23, UNRWA will continue to fundraise for livelihood activities, which are critical to increase the resilience of Palestine refugees in Syria and to support their access to employment. To support youth employment and livelihood capacity, the UNRWA Technical and Vocational Education and Training Programme (TVET) will continue to provide short-term courses to 800 students (480 females, 320 males including 15 persons with disabilities). In order to support young adults in the areas outside of Damascus and Rural Damascus, UNRWA will also launch long-term TVET courses in Latakia, Daraa, Aleppo and Homs benefiting up to 260 students. As demand for TVET services grows, the Agency will continue its efforts to better link students with the job market in an effort to increase employability rates of graduates through developing their employability and soft skills. Stationaries and supplies will be procured to support delivery of courses. Premises will be maintained to ensure quality education/training. The various components of UNRWA's interventions in 2022-23 will also contribute to early recovery efforts through supporting refugees who have spontaneously returned by restarting services in camps that were heavily destroyed during the conflict (Dera’a, Yarmouk and Ein el Tal); this will through the rehabilitation of the Agency’s installations in these camps. UNRWA will seek to support 2500 of the must vulnerable families by giving them a job opportunity for 3 months through CfW.

Training to staff on the code of conduct and PSEA and regular reinforcement of the zero-tolerance policy as well as UNRWA staff standards of conduct, reporting obligations and channels for SEA. 3. 9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: 1. Humanitarian needs of of PRs in Syria remain relatively stable 2. Sustained commitment of donors to support UNRWA financially 3. Security situation and active violence do not deteriorate any further. 4. COVID-19 pandemic challenges remain within the scope of the Agency's capacity to respond financially and operationally. Risk UNRWA is unable to continue providing services and respond to PR needs because of lack of funding or due to increased humanitarian needs. Last year, UNRWA received only around 55% of its requirements for Syria. Mitigation: 1. continue to monitor the situation and to adjust plans prioritizing most pressing needs 2. Regularly engage with donors and partners to leverage funding opportunities 3. Ensure robust financial, planning and management systems 4. Maintain communication with stakeholders on status of funding. 5. Continue to monitor its interventions through a results-based monitoring framework. Risk: UNRWA staff and PR are exposed to violence and security threats. Mitigation: 1. Monitor security updates and conduct security assessments for facilities and missions 2. Train staff and beneficiaries on ERW, and personal safety. Risk: COVID-19 pandemic situation persists/worsens. Mitigation: 1. UNRWA will adapt its programme modality to ensure sustained delivery of services while prioritizing the safety of staff and beneficiaries 2. PPEs will be provided to frontline staff and sanitization material will be made available at work locations.
