Project Summary

The project aims to improve access to life-saving preventive and curative health services in Jezira, White Nile, Gedaref, Khartoum, South Kordofan and Blue Nile states in Sudan. This project will address the priority health needs of target communities leading to reduction in excess morbidity and mortality. The main beneficiaries are persons affected by recent crisis caused by armed conflicts between the Sudan Armed Forces (SAF) and Rapid Support Forces (RSF) including internally displaced communities (IDPs), and vulnerable host communities targeting children under five years of age, adolescent girls, women, boys and men. The project will hotspot areas affected by the current crisis as well as IDP receiving states and will support existing Ministry of Health (MoH) capacity to deliver lifesaving primary health care services, including management of communicable and non-communicable diseases, support reproductive; maternal and child health services including strengthening of expanded program for immunization; mental health and psychosocial support. The IRC will build the capacity of MoH staff, provide essential drugs and medical supplies, including COVID-19 personal protective equipment (PPE), as well as infrastructure improvement and surge teams in the form of mobile medical teams (MMTs) to quickly respond to health emergencies. This is well aligned with the IRC’ Sudan Response Strategy.
Protection services will also be integrated into the IRC’s health programming to achieve greater outcomes for women and girls, including prevention and response to Gender Based Violence (GBV). Through a community-based approach, the project will ensure equity by supporting community health volunteers to provide community health packages to hard to reach and underserved communities. The IRC WASH teams will closely support the HF teams to improve facility WASH infrastructures and practices including medical and non-medical waste management, hand washing stations and hygiene promotion.
The IRC has strong capacity to deliver health services in Sudan with current support including strengthening the MoH capacity to delivery integrated health services in Gedaref, Khartoum, Jazira and Blue Nile states, and currently expanding to White Nile state. Since 2019, the IRC has supported the delivery of integrated health, nutrition, protection, livelihoods and WASH services providing a one stop center to deliver life-saving interventions to beneficiaries in a safe way. The IRC health technical excellence unit at the headquarter in NY with regional representation in Nairobi provides technical oversight and guidance for adaptation of quality health standards, training and technical supervision including frequent field presence to support country teams. Even in the face of current crisis, the IRC has maintained in country capacity and regional support to continue to deliver essential health services.

1 - Needs

The ongoing armed conflict between the Sudan Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) since April 15 that has worsened the already precarious humanitarian situation in a country already facing influx of refugees from neighboring countries, putting additional pressure on the limited resources for social services already strained by a deteriorating economic situation and the COVID-19 pandemic. In addition to large-scale displacement, the conflict has shattered access to food, water, cash, fuel, health care and other basic services for millions of people. High insecurity and looting of humanitarian assets and offices has compromised humanitarian access to key locations. As of 13 May, the Federal Ministry of Health reports that at least 700 people have been killed and over 5,200 injured since the onset of thefighting. According to May 2023 revised HRP, the number of persons targeted for health assistance increased by 43% to 7.6 million following the onset of conflict in April 2023. This is against the backdrop of a fragile health system severely strained by economic decline, displacements, flooding, and multiple disease outbreaks including COVID-19, monkey pox, hepatitis E outbreak and the country on high alert for ebola viral hemorrhagic (EVD) fever following reported outbreak in Uganda in September 2022. In terms of economic wellbeing, inflation rate peaked at 423% in June 2021 and has remained high at 259% in January 2022 and is expected to remain in triple digits into 2023, eroding household purchasing power and pushing prices for food, fuel, healthcare, and other basic needs. Yet, the National Health Recovery and Reform Policy and Strategic Plan (NHRRP 2020-22) estimates that 69.3%of health expenditures are incurred by the private sector. The latest MSNA conducted in 2022 found out that 72% of respondents paid for health services. This highlights the declining contribution of public health systems in Sudan, and the increased impacts on access to health services.The MSNA 2022 also highlighted the declining availability of essential medicines and vaccine coverage (including COVID-19), lack of adequate healthcare workers, and recurring disease outbreaks. A survey to evaluate the availability of essential medications in Sudan in 2022 found out that on average, only 31% of critical medications were available in public health facilities and 30% in private health facilities. This is a marked decline from the 43% reported in 2021 for medicine availability in public health facilities. According to 2021 figures by the federal and state MOH, there are only 5,457 doctors, 12,601 nurses, and 17,343 midwives working in the public health system (0.76 doctors + nurses + midwives per 1,000 population, against the 4.45 per one thousand population WHO target).

2 - Activities/Outputs

Under this action, the IRC will support the following activities:• Improve primary healthcare facility staffing to provide basic integrated services• Provide basic IPC supplies and training to ensure continuity of essential PHC services• Conduct OPD consultations for communicable and non-communicable disease• Support mental health consultations, management of mental health cases as well as referral to secondary facilities• Provide reproductive health services including antenatal care, delivery, postnatal care, family planning and post abortion care• Provide clinical care of sexual assault survivors and linkage to protection services for psychosocial support and case management• Support routine immunization activities (EPI) at both static facilities as well as outreach services• Conduct MHGAP training to PHC level staff• Procure and distribute essential drugs and medical supplies• Basic maintenance of health facility infrastructure• Conduct supportive supervision and mentorship to health facility/community health staff• Ambulance support for referral to secondary health care facilities

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The IRC will support safe disposal of non-medical wastes like papers, plastics and medical wastes like soiled swabs and organic materials using the colored bin systems to ensure proper waste segregation before final disposal and sharp containers for disposal of all needles, sutures and blades. Medical wastes will be stored away from reach of children in secured or fenced area and incinerated promptly, and the remnant ash disposed safely. Organic waste like placenta will be properly disposed in placenta pits. The project will prioritize installation of solar systems for health facility lighting to promote sustainable renewable energy sources and minimize emissions. The IRC health team will closely work with WASH colleagues to ensure appropriate IPC measures instituted at all service delivery points.

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

In designing its programs, the IRC makes every effort to address protection mainstreaming in the technical design. From design to implementation and monitoring, the core protection principles of ‘Do No Harm’, meaningful access, safety and dignity, and participation will be mainstreamed. The IRC health team works closely with protection colleagues to identify protection risks, vulnerabilities and referral pathways for services. The action will ensure availability of clinical care for sexual assault survivors (CCSAS) through the training of health facility staff and preposition of supplies for clinical management of rape (CMR). Providers will be trained on ensuring privacy and confidentiality including in the health information system (HIS). The IRC will also train key providers at the health facilities and in the communities on psychological first aid to offer mental health support and enhance rights of individuals with mental illness.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The health team will conduct regular client interviews to collect feedback on their experience during the visit to the health service point. This will cover waiting time, quality of services, medications received, their perception on how well they were treated, what can be improved at the facility, among others. The IRC also generates additional feedback through the protection work in the community with the feedback shared with the health team for action. Throughout implementation, the IRC will leverage its MEAL Team and feedback systems to ensure community involvement in the design and re-design of the action. The IRC protection team will conduct outreach and awareness to ensure that beneficiaries are aware of the IRC Code of Conduct, prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse policies, child safeguarding policies, process for providing feedback, and the principles of beneficiary feedback (including confidentiality, non-retaliation, and accountability). The IRC will provide multiple channels of feedback to beneficiaries including focus group discussions (FGDs), KIIs, feedback boxes, and a hotline. Overall, the IRC has zero tolerance for bullying, harassment and sexual exploitation and abuse. The IRC has the following policies in place: Child Safeguarding Policy; Beneficiary Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) Policy; Harassment Free Workplace Policy. All allegations and investigation information received is maintained in a confidential centralized case management system. Cases are evaluated and prioritized when received taking into account, among other things, whether the alleged conduct is ongoing or historical. The IRC will ensure that all new staff hired for IRC Sudan are trained on and understand the seriousness with which the IRC takes such issues. The IRC will also participate in the UN-led PSEA network, ensuring communities and staff understand PSEA and how to access reporting channels. The IRC also has a neutral department called the global Ethics & Compliance Unit - ECU, who is responsible for all major complaints, including SEA and abuse complaints.
