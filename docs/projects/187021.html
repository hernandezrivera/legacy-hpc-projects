Project Summary

OVERALL OBJECTIVE:
Strengthen access and improve inclusive, quality and equitable basic education for 1200 conflict-affected displaced, returnees, host communities, school-aged girls and boys aged 3 to 17 years in Adamawa and Borno states of North-East Nigeria.
SPECIFIC OBJECTIVE:
1. Strengthen basic education for 1,000 children (ensuring 50% girls) through the provision of essential teaching and scholastics materials
2. Improve the capacity of 200 teachers and volunteer teachers (at least 40% females) in psychosocial support, education in emergencies, inclusive education, classroom management and pedagogy (including Teaching at the Right Level/TaRL to improve learning outcomes).
3. Increase access to safe learning spaces with gender-sensitive WASH facilities and water for the benefit of 5000 children.
4. Provide access to life skills development for 500 youth (50% females; aged 15-24)
5. Enhance the response and protection capacity of education service providers in school governance in emergencies through establishing and/or activating Monitoring of School-based Management Committees (SBMC) in targeted LGAs and building the capacity of 100 SBMC (40% females; 4,665 members) on SBMC effectiveness and provision of education services to out-of-school children, education-in-crisis, and education for vulnerable children including girls and children with disabilities with a focus on hard-to-reach areas.

1 - Needs

More than 59 per cent of those affected are children, including many unaccompanied minors or children orphaned by the crisis. More than 1.2 million children need urgent education services. Institutional and human capacities are overstretched to respond to the educational needs of children and youth in Northeast Nigeria. An estimated 19,000 teachers are displaced, and 645 teachers have been killed since 2009. Approximately 1,200 schools have been damaged or destroyed or are used for military purposes. In the Northeast, education facilities are often explicitly targeted by non-state armed groups in Nigeria. The deliberate targeting, burning and looting of schools began in 2009, with some schools repeatedly attacked over the years. Human Rights Watch reports that in some cases, students recruited by non-state armed groups attacked their school and kill their classmates and teachers. From December 2013 to June 2015, the UN Secretary-General reported that all schools in the north-eastern states were closed. More than 5,202,000 children are out-of-school in Northeast Nigeria of whom more than 1,400,000 children are out-of-school in Borno state.

2 - Activities

In 2022, TBSF will focus on increasing access to quality and inclusive basic education for 1000 school-aged conflict-affected, displaced persons, returnees and host community children (50% girls) and an additional 500 youth (aged 15 - 24) in the targeted LGAs in three states (Adamawa and Borno). The specific activities include the following: Access to quality and safe education: 1. Construct and rehabilitate 10 gender-sensitive WASH facilities in schools and learning spaces with connecting to water sources. 3. Train 200 teachers and volunteer teachers (at least 40% females) in psychosocial support, education-in-emergencies, special needs education, and classroom management and pedagogy (including TaRL to improve learning outcome). 2. Provide essential teaching, learning and recreational materials to school-aged children: (a) 60 Recreational Kits, (b) 1200 school bags, (C) 1200 school uniforms, sandals and socks. Response Capacity of Education Service Providers: 1. Provide access to learning, training and skills development for 5000 youth (aged 15-24). 2. Train 100 SBMC members on SBMC effectiveness, protection of schools and provision of education services to out-of-school children, education-in-crisis, and education for vulnerable children including girls and disabled with focus on hard-to-reach areas. 8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

TBSF considers protection of all persons affected and at risk when planning humanitarian response and protection is central in TBSF’s preparedness and life-saving activities throughout the duration of project. The population at risk would be identified considering the specific vulnerabilities that underlie these risks, including those experienced by men, women, girls and boys, and groups such as internally displaced persons, older persons, persons with disabilities, and persons belonging to sexual and other minorities. Primary consideration will be given to TBSF’s accountability to affected populations, to identify, understand and support their own protection measures.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The proposed action is defined by daily interaction and collaboration with affected communities, including support to community-based structures and their participation in public awareness-raising, referral processes and collaboration in identifying and implementing initiatives which contribute to improving the protective environments. Furthermore, TBSF ensures independent consultations with specific groups, including women, girls and children, to ensure their voices are heard and ideas are incorporated into all activities. As part of the emergency response activities, beneficiaries will be consulted through regular monitoring visits and post-distribution monitoring exercises, during which individuals will be consulted on matters including the quality and relevance of assistance received. TBSF will engage community and stakeholders in parodic focus group discussions receives complaints, reports and feedback from communities, analyses the data and shares with relevant sections in TBSF as reports and complaints come in. Each case is followed up to provide feedback to the community members who laid complaints.
