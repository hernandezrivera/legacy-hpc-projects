Project Summary

The proposed project aims to provide shelter kits (SKs) and basic household non-food items (NFIs) to persons affected by conflict and other natural disasters including drought and floods in the Bay and Banadir region of Somalia. The proposed intervention will target 6,952 households for ESKs and NFI kits (41,712 people); 10428 Men,14599 women,7925 boys, 8760 girls displaced by conflict and natural disasters in Banadir and Bay Regions. This will include both IDPs and the non-displaced populations.
The JMCNA 2021 states that shelter is one of the top three priority needs of more than half of the entire population, and for 58 percent of IDPs. An estimated 4.5 million people need shelter and NFI assistance mainly due to poor shelter conditions, overcrowding and lack of space, poverty, and lack of security of tenure. Those in need of shelter and NFI assistance include displaced and non-displaced populations. IDPs, refugee returnees, refugees, and asylum seekers are particularly affected and lack any financial capacity. Based on this, the shelter and NFI needs in 2022 have increased in comparison to the needs in 2021, mainly driven by extensive flooding that damaged shelters and caused the loss of NFIs, displacement due to conflict, drought, and forced eviction, and the inability of households to repair and construct shelters or buy basic NFIs due to limited financial resources.
The humanitarian needs assessment also documented the need to ensure that refugees, asylum seekers, and refugee returnees have sufficient access to basic and domestic items and provision of shelter assistance. Overall, 30,800 refugees and asylum seekers and 90,583 refugee returnees need shelter and NFI support in 2022. UNHCR post-return monitoring (PRM) data suggests that a high proportion of refugee returnees live in housing they do not own, are squatting, or have no documentation, which leads to protection concerns, including housing insecurity and risk of eviction. The assessment also noted that women and girls living in sub-standard and overcrowded shelters are vulnerable; they don’t feel safe in makeshift shelters which do not offer privacy and sufficient protection from weather elements and increase the risk of GBV. Over a quarter, 27 percent, of IDPs reported that their bathing facilities are not safe as their main shelter concern. Marginalized groups and people with special needs also require additional support and priority.
According to the CCCM Cluster in 2021, 1.8 million IDPs (62 percent of IDPs) are at high risk of eviction, mainly due to the general lack of land tenure agreements for IDPs. The Eviction Information Portal, in 2021 (from January to August), estimated that 92,042 people were evicted, mostly in the Banadir region, where about 85 percent of all evictions occur. Eviction means the loss of a home or a shelter, loss of physical protection, livelihood assets, disruption of social support networks and coping strategies, and potentially physical violence during the eviction process.
Mercy Corps will prioritize the recently displaced households from Lascanood of Sool region (estimated 7,250 households), who remained in Banadir, and the non-displaced. UNHCR PRM data suggests that some refugee returnees are residing in the overcrowded IDP settlements due to lack of ability to pay rent. This has increased the size of the IDP camps putting the affected persons at high-risk of fire outbreaks, diseases including COVID-19, GBV and flooding, whilst they also lack sufficient water and access to health facilities. Many currently reside in rural, peri-urban, and urban informal crowded settlements, with insufficient access to basic services and poor shelter conditions that raise protection and health concerns. The shelter and NFIs support will improve their living conditions and chances at livelihoods and durable solutions. The poor host community households will also be targeted, to ensure peaceful coexistence, because many are equally vulnerable.

1. - Projects activities

Mercy Corps will base its project activities on the shelter cluster objectives of (CO1 and CO2) ensuring the persons affected by the conflict and natural disaster have protection from the bad weather elements as well as their privacy and dignity assured. Through the distribution of non-food items and emergence shelter kits. The project focuses on Newly displaced IDPs and recently evicted households in Bay and Banadir region. Under the shelter cluster objective 1 and 2 Mercy Corps will implements the following activities enabling the affected communities have access to Shelter Kits and Non-Food Items. CO#1: Ensure that persons affected by conflict and natural disasters have protection from the weather and privacy through provision of shelter and NFIs. Activity 1.1.1 a) conduct community/local authority engagement/consultation to inform the grassroot about the project expectation and the approaches of implementation Activity 1.1.1 b) Market assessment to determine the availability of standard SKs/NFI within the local market (Mogadishu and Baidoa). This will help Mercy Corps to design the intervention in terms which modality is appropriate to be used in reaching the community with the Non-Food Items and Shelter Kits. Activity 1.1.1 c) Site planning and decongestant assessment will be done together with the Shelter and CCM cluster to ensure suitable sites are identified and selected before implementation. the planning process will involve local authorities and community representatives to ensure the process is participatory, adequate and involves all stakeholders Activity 1.1.1 d) Mercy Corps will procure and distribute 2532 Emergence shelter kits to 2532HH, 200 transitional shelters to 200HH and 120 durable shelters to communities affected by conflict and natural disaster as IDPs, poor refugee returnees and vulnerable non-displaced local host community in Banadir and Bay regions. Mercy Corps will first carry out a vulnerability assessment of the targeted beneficiaries in the cluster/ local authorities and MC-identified project sites to identify the most vulnerable households and then carry out the final registration. Mercy Corps will set the vulnerability criteria in consultation with shelter cluster, other partners implementing similar activities in the region including those working on CCCM projects. Activity 1.1.1 e) Mercy Corps will procure and distribute 3,196(1,598HH for Banadir and 1,598 HH Bay region) non-food items to the conflict and natural disaster-affected IDPs, poor refugee returnees and vulnerable local host who are in Banadir and Bay region. Mercy Corps will first carry out a vulnerability assessment of the targeted beneficiaries in the cluster/ local authorities and MC-identified project sites to identify the most vulnerable households and then carry out the final registration. Mercy Corps will set the vulnerability criteria in consultation with shelter cluster, other partners implementing similar activities in the region including those working on CCCM projects. CO#2: Improve the quality of shelter and NFI assistance and ensure accountability through effective complaint and feedback mechanisms.Activity 2.1.1: In adherence to our standard PDM protocols, after four (4) weeks of the SKs distribution, Mercy Corps will conduct a one post-distribution monitoring (PDM) to cover 100% of the households that have received the ESKs to ensure accountability, appropriateness of materials, and effectiveness of distribution and use of the distributed kits. Data will be disaggregated by sex, age, and diversity to evaluate whether specific needs of women, girls, boys, men as well as PLDs were met. The results will be shared with the cluster and the donor for evaluation purposes and it will help inform future programming.- Mercy Corps will adhere to the Inter-Agency Standing Committee guidelines on inclusion of persons with disability in humanitarian action. MC will provide disability friendly shelter kits.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Mercy Corps will use cash-based approach in its intervention in Baidoa and Banadir region which it assumes the kits will be available in these markets. Mercy Corps believes that, the cash that are provided to individuals, households or communities as emergency relief contribute to meeting their basic needs and assisting them in protecting or re-establishing their livelihoods . However, Mercy Corps will undertake a market assessment to find out which modality is appropriate for the intervention while considering the availability of the materials/items for distribution. The items distribution will be done in line with the Shelter cluster minimum standards.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

Mercy Corps believe in Market systems development by supporting the existing markets in the target locations to ensure the community lives are impacted therefore MC will procure the non-food items and emergence shelter kits from the local markets ensuring the cash is injected into the local market improving the local economy and in turn impact the live of the common people in the respective target district.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

The proposed project will mainstream protection best practices, as per guidance from each sector cluster to ensure community members have access to safe, dignified, and inclusive humanitarian assistance. MC will avoid or minimize the unintended negative consequences/ impact of assistance and is committed to a ‘do no harm approach to programming. Mercy Corps has global and national systems to ensure the incorporation of key protection principles into all stages of implementation, these include equality, accountability to affected populations, participation, and empowerment of beneficiaries. The delivery of the proposed activities will be done in a non-discriminatory way that promote the safety, dignity, and integrity of the people receiving assistance. The design of the project will ensure the distribution of the ESKs and NFIs to the most vulnerable participants identified and selected through a rigorously and transparent vulnerability survey, verified and then registered. The modality of the distribution will depend on the market survey outcomes either been through in-kind or voucher systems. Mercy Corps' staff have all been trained on SPHERE protection standards, humanitarian principles and will deliver assistance in accordance with global best practices and guidelines provided by the Somalia Shelter/protection and CCCM Working Groups to ensure effective delivery of the desired emergencyresponse. Mercy Corps assesses the role of local clan structures in the area’s administration, whether they are distinct from any extremist elements, and whether they can guarantee non-interference by extremist elements and prohibited parties in the beneficiary selection, aid distribution, and related implementation The MC team will also work closely with other humanitarian actors, the clusters, local organizations, and groups to ensure programming is complementary, does no duplication, and responds to the needs of affected populations. As part of programming start-up in all locations, Mercy Corps holds discussions and consultations with key stakeholders in the community to galvanize support for programming and ensure community participation and ownership of interventions.
