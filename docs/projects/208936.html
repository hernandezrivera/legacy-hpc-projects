Project Summary

As of 31 December 2023, almost nine months into the SAF-RSF conflict which started on 15 April 2023 between SAF and RSF, nearly 7.5 million individuals have been displaced due to the ongoing conflict, inclusive of about six million internally displaced and over 1.5 million mixed cross-border movements into neighboring countries. Those who are internally displaced are in need of Shelter and NFI assistance urgently, as many, including children, women, elderly, and others with specific needs, find themselves crammed into gathering sites, with imminent risks such as overcrowding, lack of privacy, limited to no WASH facilities, lighting and electricity, as well as no security set up to govern the access to such sites, increases protection risks, in particular GBV risks, as well as concerns related to theft and other criminality. In addition, the limited space, overcrowding and limited services have been a cause of tension and disputes among residents. Another number of internally displaced are forced to sleep outdoors exposed to severe weather and threats, while those hosted by local communities pose an additional burden to their host families with very limited resources to maintain or extend their shelters.
The priority NFIs need are plastic sheeting, solar lamps, jerrycans, kitchen sets, blankets, mosquito nets and sleeping mats which form a complete NFI kit. Provision of these essential household items will be crucial for restoring basic daily activities. Many of the settlements are not planned and lack physical infrastructure including roads, drainage, lighting and lack access to basic services such as sanitation facilities. Immediate provision of shelter assistance is critical to ensure privacy and safety, mitigate GBV risks, and safeguard displaced populations from harsh environmental conditions to preserve their health amid the upcoming rainy season.
UNHCR will provide life-saving emergency shelter and NFI assistance to the persons affected by the conflict, and to those impacted by recurrent natural disasters such as flooding that affects Sudan every year, as well as populations affected by fire incidents. Newly displaced populations, especially those affected by the recent conflict and populations affected by floodings and fire incidents will be prioritized. Where feasible, cash modality will be implemented especially for emergency shelter and NFI assistance.
Full NFI kits each consisting of 3 blankets, 3 sleeping mats, 2 plastic sheets, 2 jerry cans, 2 mosquito nets, 1 kitchen set, and 1 solar lamp will be distributed to all targeted populations, one kit per household. Emergency Shelter Kits consisting of wooden poles, bamboo sticks, Hazeer, Shargani (3.5x2m) for the roofing, locally Produced Rope (Jamica), Wood Saw, Shovel, 1 Meter Rod and Axe, will be distributed to targeted households to replace and/or reinforce tents considering the short life span of the latter, Sudanese hot climate, and higher cost of tents in comparison with ESK materials which are locally sourced. Additionally, UNHCR will deliver and install family tents in the IDP settlements as a last resort measure, in addition to construction of communal shelters, rehabilitation of collective centers and general site development.
Through this project, UNHCR plans to respond to shelter and NFI needs of 1.18 million people through implementing partners. The total financial requirement of this project is $67.4 million. Subject to the security situation, access and availability of resources, UNHCR will ensure that the assistance to affected populations is provided in a timely manner, leveraging on its robust global procurement and the existing central common pipeline for NFIs. UNHCR will build from the well-established cross-border operations in place in Frachana, Chad and possibly from other countries. UNHCR and implementing partners will inform and consult affected population at each stage of the project and will establish complaint and feedback mechanism.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

UNHCR aims to strengthen protection, gender mainstreaming and accountability to affected populations as they link with the principle of “do no harm”. The particular needs of such individuals will be reflected in the assessments, programme design, implementation and monitoring and evaluation. In these areas, UNHCR will ensure the following elements are considered: (1) safety, security, and dignity; (2) equitable access; (3) accountability through community-based feedback mechanisms, with information collected based on on-site and post-distribution monitoring that will feed into the sector’s program cycle; and (4) sharing information, consultation, participation, and community empowerment. UNHCR will strengthen the implementation of the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse through awareness-raising for both partners and suppliers. UNHCR within its capacity as Shelter/NFI Sector lead will advocate to draw attention to shelter issues such as housing, land, and property rights, particularly for returnees and/or integrated IDPs. HLP due diligence tools will be set in place with cooperation of the HLP WG to harmonize the shelter responses among partners ensuring that before building durable shelter partners will ensure in cooperation with the communities that beneficiaries’ land is free of claim and refer those conflictive cases to protection partners providing ICLA support through a referral pathway. This is to reduce the risk of further harm and mainstream protection on the shelter construction programs.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

In our Shelter and NFI Cluster project, we firmly believe that the success and sustainability of humanitarian interventions are deeply rooted in active community engagement. Understanding and addressing the unique needs of those we serve is paramount. Hence, our project is designed around a community-centric approach, ensuring that feedback from those directly impacted by our actions is not just heard but is integral to our decision-making process.Feedback is collected continuously, with designated team members responsible for gathering and documenting input from all sources. This information undergoes a thorough analysis process, identifying key themes, concerns, and suggestions. Our team is trained to discern the nuanced needs and cultural sensitivities of the community, ensuring a respectful and accurate interpretation of the feedback.We incorporate this feedback into every stage of our project. From initial planning to implementation, monitoring, and evaluation, community input is the guiding force. This iterative process allows us to adapt our strategies and activities in real-time, ensuring our response remains relevant and effective.Our project includes initiatives to enhance the community's ability to provide meaningful feedback. This involves educational workshops, informative sessions, and materials that explain the importance of feedback and how it can be effectively communicated. Empowering the community to articulate their needs fosters a sense of ownership and investment in the project's outcomes.We commit to full transparency with the community regarding the utilization of their feedback. Regular reports are shared, detailing how input has influenced project decisions. This transparency is coupled with accountability measures, ensuring that community suggestions lead to real, tangible changes.We maintain an open line of communication with the community, providing updates on how their feedback is shaping the project. This is done through community meetings, newsletters, and local media partnerships. Keeping the community informed and involved is key to building trust and ensuring continued engagement.Periodic assessments and Post-distribution monitoring (PDM) are conducted to evaluate the effectiveness of the feedback integration. These assessments help us understand the impact of changes made and guide further modifications. They serve as an opportunity to reflect and continuously improve our feedback mechanisms.Recognizing potential challenges such as language barriers, cultural misunderstandings, or logistical issues, we have developed comprehensive strategies to address these. These include employing multilingual staff, cultural sensitivity training for team members, and leveraging local networks to facilitate logistics.In conclusion, our project is deeply committed to embedding community feedback into its core. We believe that the most impactful and sustainable humanitarian work is achieved not just for the community but with the community. Our dedication to this participatory approach will be evident in every aspect of our project, as we continuously strive to meet and exceed the community's expectations and needs.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

UNHCR will continue to improve PSEA across the operation. This includes strengthening the culture of accountability through the integration of PSEA actions within protection functions and broader accountability to affected populations (AAP) strategy. This will be achieved through regular PSEA training for UNHCR staff, partner staff, and contractors; regular SEA risk assessment and development and implementation of annual PSEA action plans for field offices and collaboration with operational partners to establish inter-agency and community-based complaint mechanisms accessible to IDPs and returnees. The operation will continue to maintain its active participation in the inter-agency PSEA fora, including the RC/HC’s PSEA Network through which UNHCR will mainstream. A joint WFP-UNHCR call centre is functional with a tollfree helpline where affected population can reach out to provide feedback or to complain. UNHCR acts on received complaints and feedback as required.
