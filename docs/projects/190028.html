Project Summary

The project aims at ensuring that quality GBV services are available to IDP and host populations.
Case management will be strengthened to integrate mental health and psycho-social support services (MHPSS) and legal services using a mobile approach.
Prevention will include community engagement through outreach volunteers, will support the economic empowerment of women and will offer adolescent girls life-skills programs.
Tailored GBV risk reduction activities will be conducted in all sectors to ensure that GBV risk mitigation is mainstreamed across clusters.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project will set up crucial GBV responses, promote GBV risk mitigation and prevention targeting IDP sites, transit, urban and hard to reach contexts according to gaps. The project will target women and girls, but also the following groups identified as at risk by recent UNHCR GBV assessment: adolescent girls, women and girl headed households, sex workers, LGBTIQ+ persons, persons with disabilities and men and boy survivors. GBV services will take a mobile approach to be flexible and enhance coverage. Safety audits will assess GBV risks and the need for responses adapted to each community. Simplified response mechanisms will be developed to improve GBV response and risk reduction in hard-to-reach locations.The project will produce learnings including through safety audits, the assessment of norms and perceptions, and GBV case trends analysis. All will be shared with the GBV sub-cluster in charge of coordination to inform wider responses. Capacity building will be woven throughout using existing models and curricula will be adapted to the context. Government and local women’s organizations will be supported in their GBV response capacity. Women and girls will be engaged in the design, implementation, and monitoring of the project.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The project will provide quality and accessible GBV services, strengthen GBV mainstreaming, and prevent and reduce GBV. Target will be groups at heightened protection risk and hard to reach areas. Direct activities and capacity building will follow a survivor centered approach, ‘do no harm’ principles and mainstream GBV risk mitigation across the humanitarian response. The activities will include:1. Mobile GBV services including case management, MHPSS (psychological counselling, group therapy, community-based PSS), and legal counselling and advice. Mobile services will be in IDP sites in Chiure, Metuge, Montepuez and Ancuabe, and Pemba, they are flexible to change location according to needs/gaps.2. Community engagement through trained and experienced GBV activistas (community volunteers) who can conduct GBV awareness using tested messaging and refer survivors (Chiure, Metuge, Montepuez, Ancuabe, Pemba, Mecufi, Balma, Mueda, 3. Women’s economic empowerment through life skills sessions that link to livelihoods programming. Pemba, Metuge, Chiure, Montepuez4. “Girl Shine” –a program model and resource package that seeks to support, protect, and empower adolescent girls in humanitarian settings will be used Chiure, Montepuez and Metuge. 5. Coordinated GBV risk reduction through safety audits, training of Clusters, mainstreaming GBV risks in plans (Pemba, Chiure, Montepuez, Metuge, Ancuabe, Ibo, Mueda)6. Development of a model for GBV responses - including rapid GBV assessment and simplified referral pathways for hard to reach areas. Community mapping; identification and orientation for GBV community volunteers (activistas), Contextualized key messages on GBV and SEA. 7. Engagement of Clusters in risk mitigation and strengthen staff’s capacity to provide immediate support to survivors in emergency situations when technical actors are not available. Locations will depend on emergency needs identified with the AoR . 8. Capacity building in GBV core concepts and on PSEA for community members and humanitarian actors. 9. Capacity building on Case management using a survivor centred approach of government, UN, and NGO service providers. (All locations below).

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

UNHCR as the protection mandated agency ensures the centrality of protection throughout all its work. As this a GBV-focused project persons at heightened protection risk will be identified of all genders, age and background. Protection from violence and abuse, and protection of children and their best interest will be considered at all times. UNHCR will promote the survivor centered approach.Mainstreaming risk mitigation is also a key principle that will be applied as par new UNHCR GBV Policy aligned with interagency approaches, which confirm GBV services as life-saving.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

UNHCR takes the ‘do no harm’ and age, gender, and diversity approach to ensure meaningful access to project activities very seriously. UNHCR has the capacity to identify and respond to risks through protection and specifically here GBV case management. UNHCR will leverage its current work in protection coordination in Cabo Delgado to mainstream GBV all across Clusters, using the IASC GBV guidelines. UNHCR has developed GBV referral pathways for food assistance and trained the food security and livelihoods cluster on GBV core concepts. UNHCR has supported the inclusion of GBV risks in the design of temporary shelter models. UNHCR has trained midwives and other health roles on GBV core concepts and referral and mapped health response. These are some examples of UNHCR’s protection technical support across the response.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project will work with affected communities to actively engage persons with disabilities in the program design and implementation. UNHCR is the co-chair of the persons with disabilities working group and thus is coordinating integration across responses in Cabo Delgado. Safe spaces will be designed to be accessible and GBV referral pathways will include persons with disabilities specialized services to improve access. UNHCR trains staff and community volunteers on supporting persons with disabilities to actively engage them and enhance safe community led support. UNHCR is currently providing services to survivors with disabilities and has provided tailored support to improve their access and respond to needs (e.g., distribution of mobility devices and engagement of a sign language interpreter for GBV service provision). The proposed project will include training for case workers on supporting GBV survivors with disabilities, adapting established training packages.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

UNHCR ensures environmental considerations across the project. The project is not material assistance or infrastructure based so risks are less significant. However, materials used for safe spaces are local, natural, and sustainable, the work on the spaces is conducted directly within the community. Also, the project tries to ensure sustainability and localization of all inputs including furnishings for safe spaces and re-usable material masks for project participants.
