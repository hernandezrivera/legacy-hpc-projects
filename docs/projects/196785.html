Project Summary

In the North-West, South-West, Littoral and West regions, following the Humanitarian-Development-Peace Nexus (HDPN) approach that IOM strongly supports and actively promotes in Cameroon, IOM plans interventions for 2023 to respond to the most urgent needs of displaced populations through a multisectoral approach focusing on coordination, shelter and NFI, mental health support (health), psychosocial support (protection) and WASH interventions.
The last Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) exercise conducted in the North-West and South-West regions of Cameroon, undertaken in August 2022, showed needs in most of the sectors, including access to water, food, health, education, etc. In the West and Littoral regions, the last Mobility Tracking assessment showed that the North-West (NW)-South-West (SW) crisis impact continue to spill over to these neighbouring regions with the number of IDPs coming from NW-SW increasing from the previous year, while the last MSNA, undertaken at the same time, showed again severe needs in most of the sectors. In view of such needs identified, IOM plans to expand its programmes accordingly and in line with each mandate and capacity, with a multisectoral response aiming at contributing to reinforce life-saving assistance and to improve well-being of displaced and the most vulnerable populations affected by the crisis. IOM will mainstream protection and recovery through the development of local capacities, in all its proposed activities to support displaced populations in preparing and achieving durable solutions.
IOM will support the work of the Coordination sector through the collection and production of critical data on displacement situation, alerts as well as humanitarian Multi-sectoral humanitarian needs following its DTM methodology, that will be provided to the humanitarian community to inform its humanitarian programming. In the frame of the Shelter Sector response, IOM will distribute emergency shelter and NFI kits, both in-kind and using cash-modality solutions when possible (bearing in mind that lack of civil documentation and concerns of diversion of cash to non-licit uses poses very important challenges) and help the construction for most vulnerable people. In the WASH response, IOM will drill and rehabilitate boreholes, construct water points, latrines and other infrastructures and conduct hygiene sensitization and promotion activities combined with distribution of WASH kits. Finally, IOM multisectoral assistance will also include mental health activities including direct MHPSS assistance using PMTs, reinforcing referral mechanism and capacity building of health actors (Health sector), while also directly providing psychosocial support to populations impacted by crisis by reinforcing safety and social network as well as strengthening community capacity to provide psychosocial support to victims of GBVs (Protection sector).
Through activities, direct feedback from beneficiaries and community members will be gained via IPs and also directly by IOM staff during field visits and visits of the M&E/AAP independent team in accordance with the wider IOM AAP emergencies workplan. AAP focal points will be assigned in Buea and Yaounde respectively, with clear SOPs to be in place to capture complaints from beneficiaries and to take any appropriate action. Members of IP will be trained on topics such as the proper handling of disclosures of gender-based violence (GBV) incidents, aspects of confidentiality, and respectful communication in high-stress environments. Targeting of beneficiaries will be done according to a vulnerability criterion including persons living with disabilities assessed through RRM household level assessments conducted by IOM. Also, IOM will integrate measures to ensure gender-specific needs and to mitigate risks of GBV in all activities (e.g., including through training of IPs on GBV core concepts, guiding principles as well as on how to respond to disclosures of GBV).

1 - Besoins / Needs

Humanitarian needs in Cameroon remain high. In the North-West, South-West regions, the socio-political crisis that erupted in 2017 after peaceful protests by lawyers and teachers against a perceived encroachment of the regions’ cultural practices and autonomy, and which turned into an armed conflict between the government defense forces and Non-State Armed Groups (NSAGs), continues to trigger very dynamic and massive internal displacements and related humanitarian needs. Throughout the year 2022 the landscape of NSAGs continued to be fragmented following a further breakdown between the diaspora and the NSAGs on the ground, blurring command and control structures and making humanitarian access more complex. This and the sustained rise in the use of improvised explosive devices points towards the increasingly protracted nature of the conflict, with unaddressed grievances of local populations in NWSW and low amounts of socioeconomic opportunities for youth who are also vulnerable to exploitation and recruitment by armed groups. In August 2022, IOM’s Mobility Tracking counted 368,742 IDPs and 376,707 internal returnees within the NWSW.In the neighboring regions of West and Littoral, an estimated 194,065 displaced populations are presently displaced dominantly due to the spillover from the NW-SW region conflict into these regions (99.3%), with 62% of IDP individuals assessed were displaced in 2020 and before (Mobility Tracking August 2022). Currently, all humanitarian response actors are struggling to meet the large humanitarian needs due to a low level of humanitarian funding and an overall low visibility of the NWSW crisis at an international level. Low funding has meant that there is a greater need in the NW, SW, Littoral and West for the reinforcement of response capacity, including a Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM). The last multi-sectoral needs assessment conducted by IOM in the North-West, South-West regions (August 2022) showed a variety of needs relative to individual sectors, especially WASH, Health, Protection, Shelter and NFI. Assessments in the NWSW region, such as previous MSNA findings also recognize the rising number of urban and peri-urban displaced populations and the identification of cash as the preferred modality of assistance, as these persons have more access to functional markets than in rural areas. For the Shelter/NFI sector for example, 57 per cent of internally displaced persons (IDPs) reported living with host families, followed by 27 per cent in rented accommodation, 8 per cent squatting and 8 per cent in makeshift shelters, with most makeshift shelters being constructed using fewer durable materials such as bamboo (70%), wood (55%) and tarpaulin (33%). Key difficulties reported in terms of access to NFIs included them being too expensive (90%) or difficult to access in the local market (44%). For the health sector, 76 per cent of respondents noted that they had difficulty accessing health services due to health services being too far away. As a result, displaced populations are to a large extent currently relying on alternative sources for healthcare, including traditional healers (63%), community health workers (55%), pharmacies (42%) and mobile clinics (13%). In the Littoral and West regions, various needs have also been observed including WASH, Health, Protection, Shelter and NFI. In terms of NFI, most of key items are available in the assessed villages though facing difficulties such as items being too expensive (94%) and markets being difficult to access (39%), in addition to unavailability of certain items including Chlorine, Tarpaulin, Cement. The lack of drinking water has been identified to be the biggest challenge (34%) in West and Littoral regions, with only one percent of assessed locations reported that everyone has access to water.
