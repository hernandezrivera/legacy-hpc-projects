Project Summary

The project addresses the main problem "rise in SGBV and discrimination against acutely vulnerable
women/girls exacerbated by COVID-19 and May 2021 escalation and their limited access to safe/dignified
multi-sectoral SGBV protection/prevention services." It responds to immediate critical needs identified in specific needs assessment conducted on 10- 11/10/2021: MHPSS services like emergency helpline, group/individual PSS, couple therapy, specialized mental health; legal counseling & legal documents; SRH; diagnosis/treatment for SGBV survivors with cancer; rehabilitation & assistive aids for WWDs/GWDs; case management; dignity kits; cash assistance; awareness & information materials; self-care & capacity
building for frontline staff of SGBV actors; community engagement & advocacy to raise women's voices on their humanitarian needs/gaps. Its overall objective is "Acutely vulnerable women>18-59 and >59 years and girls 14-17 years SGBV survivors, have meaningful access to safe, dignified, gender responsive& inclusive multi-sectoral protection/prevention services based on GBV Minimum Standards” and outcomes are: 1. Acutely vulnerable women/girls SGBV survivors manifested psychological well-being, good health, met basic needs, safety/dignity, knowledge, changed attitudes/behaviors toward reporting SGBV, resilience & positive coping; 2. Front-line staff of SGBV actors are better able to detect, refer/provide safe/ dignified multi-sectoral protection/prevention services; and 3. Local/national duty bearers sensitized and act to protect acutely vulnerable women/girls SGBV survivors via community engagement & advocacy. The project targets directly 9810 beneficiaries (5790 women, 640 men, 3220 girls, 160 boys, out of them 489 PwDs) identified based on vulnerability criteria: with disabilities, cancer, elderly, IDPs, refugees, pregnant/lactating women, FHHs, SGBV survivors & below poverty line according to their location, status, protection risks & needs severity. Beneficiaries are selected from Jabalya (Tal Za'tar/ Twam) and Beit Lahya (A'tatra/Salatin) ; Gaza City (Shijaia, Sheikh Radwan); north Nuseirat camp; KhanYounis- Mawasi& Qarara; Rafah- Mawasi & ARAs in coordination with GBV SWG.
The project links to GBV RNA, MSNA, HNO 2022, HRP SO2, PC Objectives 1 & 2, activities & indicators.
Partners consolidate their experience in geographical& thematic areas and coordinate with CBOs to address critical needs of women/girls SGBV survivors.
They implement blend of in-person & remote activities: A) Provide PSS/ legal counseling via partners’ helplines to 2340 women/540 girls & refer 780 women/180 girls to structured individual PSS & group PSS to 150 women/60 girls; couple therapy to 150 women; specialized mental health to 54 women/12 girls; recreational days for 600 women/girls; SRH to 210 women/60 girls; diagnosis/treatment to 51 women with breast cancer; rehabilitation/assistive aids to 39WWDs/9GWDs; dignity kits to 360 women; cash assistance to 300 women/30 early married girls; economic enablement/ support to 33 women; case management to 72 women/9 girls; 1 SGBV service directory; awareness sessions to 150 women/60 girls/150 men/60 boys; B) self care for 54 partners and training to 120 frontline staff of SGBV actors; C) community engagement& advocacy: 1 radio ad, 6 press releases, 9 community meetings, online pamphlet, 1 fact sheet, 3 position papers, feedback/complaints mechanism, PSEA & advocacy initiative.
Partners ensure changes in lives of women/girls; survivor-centered & rights-based approaches; humanitarian principles; beneficiary participation/satisfaction/empowerment; gender equality; sound MEAL framework; information sharing; gender/age difference; mainstreaming disability/protection/GBV via prioritizing safety/dignity; do no harm; equal/ meaningful access to services; AAP; PSEA; respect informed consent, privacy/confidentiality; LEAVING NO ONE BEHIND, cost effectiveness & adhere to COVID-19 safety rules.

1 - Needs

Partners conducted specific needs assessment (NA) on 10-11/10/2021 and concluded participants differentiated timely and critical SGBV needs in context of COVID-19 and aftermath of May escalation in alignment with protection priorities of HNO 2022. NA included: 1) phone calls to 35 women18-59 and>59 years and 15 girls<14- 17 years SGBV survivors; out of them 11 WWDs/ 5 GWDs, and 4 women with cancer in Jabalia- Tal Al zaatar/ Twam , Beit Lahya- Atatra/ Salateen, Gaza City- Shijaia/ Sheikh Radwan, Deir ALBalah- north Nusairat camp, Khan Younis-Qarara/ Mawasi, Rafah- Mawasi, Restricted Areas; 2) calls of women/girls SGBV survivors via partners' emergency helplines; and (3) In-person meetings for 12 SGBV professionals like case managers, psychologists, lawyers and psychiatrists; 4) 6 women members of Local Community Committees.All participants expressed their grave concern and high suffering from almost 14 years of an Israeli blockade, internal Palestinian political divisions, COVID-19 pandemic and May 2021 escalation, while households face difficulties in meeting their basic needs. Specifically, women who were internally displaced or staying at host families expressed their sense on lacking safety and security and being at high risk of SGBV.SGBV professionals explained that they were affected by working with SGBV survivors which can be traumatic and can result in vicarious or secondary trauma. They expressed that they need training on self-care and safety to manage stress and support better job performance and overall well-being; while partners’ staff need monthly support and psychological support by a professional psychiatrist and training on PSEA and feedback, complaint and response mechanisms.Acutely vulnerable Women and girls SGBV survivors explained that they were subjected to psychological, physical, sexual, and economic violence; lack awareness on their legal rights, SGBV risks and protection mechanisms; loss of legal documents during escalation; lack of essential material items that enhance their safety, facilitate basic hygiene, enable access to humanitarian services, and promote their mobility and presence in public spaces; and their increased risk to COVID-19 infection. They expressed their main problem as “rise of SGBV and discrimination against them compounded with impact of COVID-19 spread and May 2021 escalation and their limited access to safe, well-designed SGBV multi-sectoral and gender responsive specialized services”. They requested partners to meet their common timely and critical needs, in the context of COVID-19 and May 2021 escalation including: 1) MHPSS services like individual psychological counseling, couple therapy, specialized mental health treatment and recreational open days; 2) individual legal counseling and producing official legal documentation; 3) emergency SRH and family planning; 4) emergency hotline to provide legal and psychological counselling and referral services to reach left behind women/girls in remote/inaccessible communities; 5) integrated case management including shelter services to address sophisticated women cases with multiple problems; 6) unconditional cash assistance to support women displaced persons and meet immediate needs of their families; 7) economic enablement and support; 8) dignity kits to provide basic items to interact comfortably in public and maintain personal menstrual hygiene toward preserving dignity, protection and coping in current stressful humanitarian situation; 9) awareness raising on SGBV protection mechanisms and information materials on available SGBV services, including where and how to access those services; 10) COVID-19 personal protective measures; and 10) advocacy campaign to raise their voices on humanitarian protection critical needs in the aftermath of May 2021 escalation. WWDs/ GWDs SGBV survivors have additional needs including home- health care visits, medical care (physio- therapy, nursing care), counseling to family care givers, materials (dressing, diapers, gloves, medicines), assistive aids (Wheel chairs, walkers, toilet wheel chair, air bed mattress, air chair mattress). Women with breast cancer GBV survivors have also additional needs including diagnosis using mammography and ultrasound, pathological lab testing, medication, home visits, rehabilitation and reintegration (physiotherapy, artificial silicon breast in addition to medical breast bra, artificial hair, and primary health care kits. Chronically ill women SGBV survivors need drugs for diabetes & hypertension.GBV RNA (2021) presented that women GBV survivors need access to multi-sectoral services including MHPSS (PFA, individual/ group PSS, family therapy & specialized mental health; self-care of women/ girls and their families), emergency SRH services, cancer treatment/rehabilitation, disability health care, legal aid , unconditional cash assistance, dignity/hygiene kits, shelter, case management & legal awareness.MSNA (2021) reflects that 53.08% of people in Gaza are in severe need for humanitarian assistance; PIN for physical and mental wellbeing are 19%; women and girls who suffer from GBV, including child, early, and forced marriage are 641,000 in Gaza.HNO (2022) indicates that due to May 2021 escalation, women/girls suffer from higher incidents of GBV. 37.5% of women 18-64 years experienced violence. Of those, 56.6% experienced psychological violence, 17.8% physical violence, 8.8% sexual violence, 32.5% social violence, 41.1% economic violence, 8% cyber violence & 24% early marriage. 19% and 17% of households are concerned about safety/ security of girls & women respectively. 8,250 persons were internally displaced need legal aid for GBV survivors and widowed women relating to denial of resources, custody of children and loss of identity documents; girls< 18 yeas who are still displaced need PSS; 58% of WWDs lack information on GBV services; socio-cultural patriarchal norms; limited access to multi-sectoral GBV services.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

- Partners chose the service delivery modality to protect and respect the rights of acutely vulnerable women and girls SGBV survivors via increasing their access to safe, gender responsive & inclusive multi-sectoral GBV services based on GBV Minimum Standards towards advancing their psychological well-being, good health, met basic needs, safety/dignity, knowledge, changed attitudes/behaviors toward reporting SGBV, resilience & positive coping. - Partners chose the cash modality to meet women's basic needs as follow:- Women including early married receive a transfer value of 100$/woman and 6.5$ (equals 20 ILS) cover bank fee/woman. Women will receive multi-sectoral services including MHPSS (PFA, individual/ group PSS, couple therapy & specialized mental health), legal aid (counseling, representation), emergency SRH, breast cancer, disability rehabilitation, dignity kits & case management. Transfer value of 100$/woman supports ONLY most essential and survival goods (not covered by project/referral) to meet women's basic needs like food/ water during crisis. It is estimated based on our experience from OCHA-funded projects taking into consideration the high demand for acutely vulnerable and poorer women for immediate cash during the emergencies, funding constraint and gap between own resources including income/ production, and other assistance received and SMEB/MEB.-Partners monitors cash disbursement over counter in bank Of Palestine. Partners MEAL officers and accountants attend cash disbursement in banks to ensure that cash is disbursed ONLY to targeted women and they receive correct amount. - Partners use UVAT to target beneficiaries of unconditional cash assistance.- Protection concerns are mitigated here via: i) Tailored household financial management visits to ensure that cash intervention does not cause harm; ii) Financial management coaching for the family; iii) A communication channel in case harm resulted from the cash intervention.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

- Partners analyze protection context in target communities in terms of identifying the threats women/ girls are exposed to, their vulnerabilities and ability to protect themselves, particularly from SGBV. - Partners train SGBV front-line staff (aid workers) on protection implications and risks, protection potential of humanitarian projects and applying PSEA policy to avoid exposing target women/girls to further harm and to enhance their safety. - Partners identify the protection risks to women’s/girls' safety and wellbeing that the project might inadvertently exacerbate and propose mitigation actions.- Partners ensure that the project will not be used to fuel further conflict, or to disadvantage a particular social group or to lead to increased human rights violations.- The location of partners’ facilities and the routes to them are safe and distant from the risk or threat of SGBV. - Partners have infrastructure adaptations including fitting ramps and railings to facilities and provide WWDs/GWDs with assistive mobile devices and hearing devices so that they can access and use facilities and services in safety and with dignity. - Partners will respect informed consent, confidentiality and privacy in all forms of consultation, counseling and personal information sharing. - Partners’ SGBV specialists will maintain the highest levels of professionalism in receiving and counselling SGBV survivors. - Partners ensure that their facilities and office arrangements are safe for the beneficiaries and staff, and they adhere to COVID-19 minimum precautionary safety measures, as defined by MoH.- Protection mitigation measures for cash modality, such as: i) Tailored household financial management visits to ensure that cash intervention does not cause harm; ii) Financial management coaching for the family; iii) A communication channel in case harm resulted from the cash intervention.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

- Partners ensure that all beneficiaries (gender and age: women, girls, men and boys) can equitably access and use SGBV services provided under the project.- Partners provide a blend of adapted in-person and remote activities to overcome any barriers that beneficiaries may encounter. - Partners provide SGBV services at an adequate time when all targeted women and girls can realistically access them.- Partners facilitate access to SGBV services for persons with reduced mobility (e.g. persons with physical disabilities, the elderly, bed-ridden individuals) via making access paths smooth and fit ramps for wheelchair access. - Partners facilitate access to SGBV services for persons with non-mobility related disabilities (e.g. the blind, hard of hearing, intellectually disabled) via providing information about services in both verbal and written forms and developing pictorial representations and explanations. - Partners raise awareness of direct beneficiaries about their rights and SGBV services and service providers via disseminating service directory. - Partners monitor access and discrimination through disaggregated indicators (# of individuals accessing services) by age, gender, and location or specific group (e.g. persons with disabilities). - Partners use their helplines to facilitate access of women and girls in remote and inaccessible communities (e.g. ARAs) to SGBV services towards implementing UN 2030 agenda of “LEAVING NO ONE BEHIND” and to “endeavor to reach the furthest behind first”. - Partners involve women CBOs and local communities in these localities to keep up implementation of project activities during all times including times of crisis. - Partners’ access approach is consistent with the humanitarian principles (impartiality, neutrality, independence and humanity) in accordance with IHL. Partners apply: 1) Humanity in addressing the human suffering of most vulnerable women and girls; 2) Neutrality so that humanitarian aid must not favor any political side; 3) Impartiality in providing services to women and girls solely on the basis of need and without discrimination; and 4) Independence in advancing the autonomy of humanitarian project objectives from political, economic or other objectives.
