Project Summary

Right from the start of the program in 2017, Malteser International succeeded in building up its own structures and closely coordinating with established local actors in such a way that the measures could be seamlessly integrated into the existing overall structure and gaps could be closed. While some INGO's focused only on the construction of new infrastructure, Malteser International was able to close supply gaps efficiently and effectively by rehabilitating existing but dysfunctional hand pumps. Malteser International provides Water facilities through repairing broken hand pumps and solar powered pumps, drilling new boreholes were required or upgrading hand pumps to solar powered systems. Sanitation facilities are being rehabilitated and maintained or constructed new, depending on the demand. Hygiene Promotion activities are being carried out in an participatory approach through training community volunteers and the provided infrastructure is handed over to Community WASH Committees, whom are also receiving training on Operation and Maintenance of the infrastructure. Furthermore Hygiene, Dignity and Non Food Item Kits are being distributed to the beneficiaries. This is mainly implemented in the IDP camps identified through closing supply gaps with the WASH sector (UNCIEF and MoWR), leading to MI working side by side with other actors in different IDP camps. The assumption of such rehabilitation work, the provision of infrastructure as well as so-called soft components such as the establishment and training of water committees, or the implementation of hygiene promotion activities is very much welcomed by other actors and partly proactively requested. MI is able to react quickly to the extremely dynamic and volatile situation in Borno in the coming planning and strategic orientation. Cross-sectoral coordination with relevant organisations in Borno State should lead to implementation in regions where MI can close gaps in the WASH sector in a multi-sectoral context. A regional needs assessment has been carried out with the aim of identifying locations for the next project phase (Pulka in Gwoza LGA) where there is a lack of resources in the WASH sector, i.e. where there is a need, and where no actors are already working on the supply of structures and the staunching of demand.

Needs

Lack of rainfall, refugee flows and instability reduce the agricultural yields of the population. In the three states most affected by the conflict, Adamawa, Borno and Yobe, around 8.5 million people are urgently in need of humanitarian aid. The number of internally displaced people in need amounts to 2.5 million, 54% of whom are women and children. Security problems affect activities in agriculture, fishing and animal husbandry. This leads to dependence on expensive food on local markets. The need for food is high, but access to clean water, adequate sanitation and health care is also urgently needed. In Nigeria alone more than 8.5 million people urgently need food support, almost half a million children are extremely malnourished (HRP 2019). The security situation is precarious in the region. Even in Maiduguri, the capital of the state of Borno in Nigeria, suicide bombings occur repeatedly. Although the military offensive of the Nigerian army has led to land gains in the northeast and thus to a temporary expansion of humanitarian aid, BH is still present and many people remain inaccessible. Front lines are constantly shifting, and access to those in need in many districts is never assured for long periods of time. The situation in the areas of nutrition and food security as well as health and WASH is still alarming in the region. Many IDPs are afraid to return to their homes as long as Boko Haram remains a real threat. Suddenly returning refugees from Cameroon pose additional challenges to the humanitarian aid system. At the same time, access to the people who need support is still not easy. In addition to the security aspects, the rainy season also makes access to certain regions more difficult. Some 9 million people in northeastern Nigeria are in need of humanitarian aid, and almost 1.9 million people are displaced within the country. More than 5 million people are malnourished and almost four million depend on water supplies. Due to the threat situation in northeastern Nigeria the security situation in these areas remains volatile and dynamic. Access to humanitarian aid is repeatedly influenced by this. The United Nations estimates that approximately two million people in northeastern Nigeria have not yet received humanitarian assistance. Suicide attacks and explosions by unconventional explosive and incendiary devices have increased in recent months. However, well-planned civil-military cooperation can reach the people here with humanitarian aid. As a result of the conflict in the region, both the quality and quantity of food have declined sharply. Lack of clean drinking water and malnutrition has become a widespread problem as access to water supplies in Nigeria has deteriorated in recent years. This is particularly true in the Northeast region, especially Borno State. The affected households have a limited income level over several years; assets and livelihoods have been destroyed and access to food is limited. Trade in food and livestock between countries has almost come to a standstill due to the security situation. This has led to increased market prices in neighboring regions. The lack of crop yields as a result of the conflict and climate change is causing prices to rise further. Moreover, due to the unstable security situation in north-eastern Nigeria, the health system is characterized by a lack of personnel, equipment, medicines and poor infrastructure, which leads to inadequate to non-existent supplies.

Activities

Activities are:- Provision of safe drinking water by rehabilitation of 150 boreholes with hand pumpbased on the multi-barrier approach.- Provision of safe drinking water by drilling of boreholes and rehabilitation of 95 boreholes (15+30+50) with solarization, storage and distribution taps based on the multi-barrier approach.- Provision of safe drinking water by establishing or strengthening 150 water-user committees in the communities including training's, equipment, and support for maintenance based on the multi-barrier approach.- Provision of safe drinking water by providing 14,500 water storage container and hygiene awareness for households, based on the multi-barrier approach.- Participative construction or rehabilitation of 300 (200+100) gender segregated Sanitation Facilities at schools, community centers and health centers (needs based).- Hygiene promotion activities by training's of 100 hygiene promoters, based on local needs of health centers, schools, storage facilities and focusing on most critical hygiene behaviors that could have adverse impact on health.- Systematic hygiene awareness activities for the local population by hygiene promoters, based on local needs of health centers, schools, storage facilities and focusing on most critical hygiene behaviors.- Supply and Distribution of NFI Kits (hygiene products) for 14,500 households.- Provision of dignified and culturally appropriate Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) items and sensitization of the target group.- Risk analysis, design and implementation of WASH preparedness and mitigation actions in disaster prone areas.
