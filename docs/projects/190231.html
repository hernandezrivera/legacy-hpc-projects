Project Summary

East Bale is one of the Zones found in Oromia Region that newly established a year ago. Out of seven Rural woredas five woredas are in lowland, desert, semi-desert weather conditions. In these woredas More than 80% of population are pastoralist that depends on livestock as main economic source and livelihoods. East Bale Zone experiences two rainy seasons, Gena and Arfasa. Gena lasts from fourth week of August to November while the Arfasa season extends from March to May. Dawe Sarar and Lagahidha are some of the lowland Woredas extremely hard hit by the drought. Following the extreme drought in the Zone, IRC has approached a donor and secured fund from Ireland Government to intervene this drought through deploying relevant and experienced staff. The expected output of this project is Reduced mortality and morbidity and increased recovery and resilience of drought affected people in Dawe Serer and Lagahidha Woreda of East Bale Zone, This will be achieved through provision of multi-sectoral humanitarian response, such as disbursement of unconditional and unrestricted cash transfer which enables the community to purchase basic need as per their choose and provision of safe water supply to targeted drought affected community in order that reduce school dropout rate cased due to drought. The IRC will implement the proposed intervention through close coordination with government and non-government partners in the region to effectively implement the lifesaving interventions across the Two targeted woredas where drought highly affect the community.
The duration of the project is for six months (March 01, 2022, to Aug 31, 2022) and 28140 (Male 13849& Female 14291) individuals will be benefited.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

The MEAL team reviewing the report will share this with the concerned department or concerned body (internal or external) for response, which will be provided to the clients through the client’s response channels like cleint feedback committee, FGD, call to dedicated IRC staff for response like what has been done in year one. In the previous response, the IRC learned that advocating of client feedback channel will promote the clients voice on our service which worked well, and IRC will need to continue building up on it. We have captured lesson from the current RRM project that the most preferable channel used by most of the client was Contact phone number, In-person conversation and FGD to give the feedback to project team including Field coordinators. A banner with means on how the clients can present their feedback and complaints will be posted at places visible and accessible for clients and shows through which phone number feedback and complaints can be made and displays the e-mail of field coordinator and MEAL team from Adama and Addis. At MPC distribution, kits, or in-kind a client’s feedback and complaint committee will be selected from the clients by the clients to collect and handle any feedback and complaints that may arise during the distribution, including about the quality of items, the impartiality of the response and others. The collected feedback and complaints will be reviewed together with the committee, government representatives and IRC staff immediately after the distribution before departing the distribution site. And the collected feedback, if it is relevant, can be responded to onsite by the committee and IRC staff. The Safe Programming team is also part of the MEAL Unit and supports in ensuring protection of clients and gender mainstreaming. The team conducts safety audits, protection risks assessment and gender assessments.
