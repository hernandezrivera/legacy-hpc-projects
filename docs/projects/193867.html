Project Summary

Through this emergency project, and in compliance with the Cluster Objective 1, ACTED and the Arab Centre for Agricultural Development (ACAD) aim to improve the resilience capacity and the livelihood of the most vulnerable 320 farmers, herders, and their households, representing 1,504 individuals. The intervention will target the 8 most vulnerable communities located across 2 governorates, in need of livelihood support. The targeted communities will be the following:
Bethlehem: Za’tara el Kurshan, Kissan, Wadi Muhammad, Ar Rawa'in, Arab ar Rashayida, Al Walayda
Jericho: Fasayel Al-Wusta, Fasayel Al-Fuqa.
In order to provide the most complementary assistance to these households, ACTED and ACAD will conduct the following activities, in close coordination with the Ministry of Agriculture (MoA) and the FSS:
- Restricted unconditional cash assistance for land rehabilitation and provision of inputs for the most vulnerable 200 farmers and their households;
- Restricted unconditional cash assistance for animal shelter rehabilitation/winterization and provision of inputs for the most vulnerable 120 herders and their households;
- Strengthening of 8 CBOs in targeted areas, to ensure protection mainstreaming within communities, communications with communities, and accountability to affected populations throughout the project duration.
The project will be supported by the mainstreaming of an Integrated Protection Approach (IPA), with a specific focus on the most vulnerable groups, including women, girls, boys, PwDs and older persons. Gender principles will be mainstreamed throughout the entire action and CBOs will be trained on referral mechanisms of protection cases such as GBV with a particular focus given to women and children. Finally, AAP will be core to the proposed Action by giving account to, taking account of and being held to account by affected populations.
Across all activities, beneficiaries will be made aware of ACTED’s complaint response mechanism (CRM) and PSEA awareness leaflets will be distributed. At last, any outstanding needs detected all along the different phases of the project, especially protection-related ones (PSEA, MHPSS, GBV) will be referred to sectoral partners.

1 - Needs

In the WB, Israel’s prolonged military occupation, illegal settlement expansion and settler violence continue, as well as the demolition of residential, livelihood and service infrastructure (Protection cluster, August 2022). Administrative measures including Israeli plans for the settlement of land title in Area C, the Seam Zone and EJ, place more communities at risk of dispossession, displacement, loss of shelter, loss of livelihood, poverty and food insecurity. Overall, the current challenge of limited economic access is further exacerbated by the limitations/challenges linked to the volatility of global commodity prices due to the Ukrainian crisis. WFP’s monthly market dashboard for July 2022 noted that the onset of the conflict in Ukraine and the economic sanctions imposed on Russia resulted in a global shortage of essential products, disruption of supply chains, and a dramatic rise in food prices. Palestine was affected due to its high import dependency ratio of 32%: Palestine's agricultural production was severely affected, leading to dramatic price increases and shortages of many inputs. Under its emergency response, as a member of the WBPC, ACTED has registered multiple requests for livelihood assistance and more specifically of cash assistance to purchase fodder, as the price of this commodity increased in the past months as a consequence of the Ukrainian crisis. Whilst there has been a reduction in inflationary pressures in August, prices for staple commodities remain elevated in Palestine. Furthermore, disruption to fertilizer supply and poor agricultural output are expected to continue through late 2022 and early 2023 which is anticipated to further exacerbate the supply deficits, particularly of grain and other staples (WFP, August 2022). There is thus a need to support small-scale farmers and herders considering the current economic pressure the sector is facing. In addition, ACTED conducted in 2021 a multi-sectoral vulnerability assessment in the WB which covered 802 HH (4,789 individuals) across WB governorates (including Hebron and Jerusalem) who were nominated by either local partners or Community leaders/representatives. The findings of the assessment showed that 353 (44%) of the HH depended on agriculture (including livestock production) as their main source of income. 479 (59.7%) HH had livestock and/or cultivated land as a source of income, among which 213 (26.6%) reported the needs to rehabilitate fully or partially their livestock shelters. 337 (42%) HH reported that they had lost some of their livestock due to lack of money to buy medicine, fodder, water or due to poor animal shelter in the last year. The majority of assessed HH (74.5%) that cultivated land as a source of income reported that their ability in cultivating land had been decreasing. The main reason for these changes were related to the lack of water, insufficient money to prepare the land (ploughing, cleaning, etc.) and lack of agriculture inputs such as seeds, fertilizers, and pesticides. The vast majority of the assessed HH (93.7%) depended on negative coping mechanism to meet their HH's needs. Due to limited funding these needs remain unaddressed and assistance is still required. In 2022, ACTED carried out consultations with key informants (MoA and community members) to complement MSNA data and provide the necessary granularity. This was complemented by data collected as part of ACTED’s ongoing humanitarian interventions in the WB which highlighted the need for livelihood assistance, including the need for cash assistance to purchase fodder.Agriculture is a key sector in the Palestinian economy, contributing around 7% of its GDP. However, in the agricultural sector, women’s employment is mainly only through their unpaid work on family farms. Although 84% of adult family agricultural workers are women in Palestine, they are usually deprived of any financial returns for their work, despite that work having helped to increase profits for the family (FAO, 2022). Given that women’s role in agriculture is in addition to their roles in housekeeping, caregiving and community involvement, often with negative health consequences, there is an urgent need to support women in agriculture, including opportunities to reduce their manual labour burden, increase their wages and enhance their livelihoods and the livelihoods for their families (FAO, 2021). Moreover, during the last PCBS census in 2010, there were only 6.90% of female agricultural holdings in the WB (FAO,2021). In addition, the inequity in water distribution should be highlighted as it is on two levels in Palestine. Firstly, between poor water resourced small-scale family plots and large productive farms with wells, and secondly, the gender discrimination by water suppliers in terms of the time slots (FAO,2022). As such, this proposed action will attempt to support small-scale female farmers, which are often discriminated in the current system. Following coordination with the MoA, in charge of with the DoA in the targeted governorates in October 2022 and based on requests for livelihood assistance through its CRM under the WBPC program in 2022 (notably in the Jericho governorate; particularly Fasayil al Fauqa and Fasayil al Wusta communities), ACTED and its partner ACAD identified and confirmed outstanding livelihood needs in 8 communities across 2 governorates which will be targeted through this proposed action. This action responds to the needs identified in the 2023 HNO and considers FSS priorities; it will address the needs of most vulnerable small-scale farmers and herders that are suffering from shortages of feed, inadequate production and farming facilities. It will target the most marginalized and less resilient people losing their livelihoods due to man-made and natural shocks, both at HH and community level, to face the negative effects of demolition and confiscation, economic restrictions and impediments, general lack of employment.

2 - Activities

A1. BNF selection: BNF will be selected through a transparent process including open calls for applications, information sessions in target communities, and socio-economic and technical assessments with eligible HHs. ACTED and ACAD’s project team, along with the support of local CBOs, will ensure the largest inclusion of vulnerable categories, such as women, PwD, older people, with special attention to protection related issues. ACTED and ACAD will for instance organise participatory information sessions exclusively for women to ensure they have the opportunity to voice their concerns and needs. During the selection process, communities will be made aware of ACTED’s CRM and key PSEA messages through the distribution of ACTED’s CRM card and a PSEA leaflet designed based on recommendations from the oPt PSEA network. In total, the 320 most vulnerable HHs located in the target communities will be supported, with 200 farmers under A2 and 120 herders under A3 benefitting from the project. Once the 320 HHs selected, based on verification with the Directorates of Agriculture (DoA), ACTED and ACAD will develop Bills of Quantities (BoQs) and workplans for each BNF under A2 and 3, to identify all needs and establish a timeframe for rehabilitation works. The sessions to design BoQs and workplans will be individual to ensure these documents are tailored to each BNF specific needs and their protection concerns, notably for more vulnerable groups like women, PwDs and older people. These documents will be included in the grant agreement signed with each BNF which will also include a technical manual to conduct the rehabilitation works, and an environmental awareness leaflet. A2. ACTED and ACAD will provide 200 small-scale farmers (between 2 and 3 donums) with restricted unconditional cash assistance in local currency (ILS) to facilitate the rehabilitation of 500 donums of agricultural land in total. This cash assistance modality will be used to allow farmers to purchase the needed inputs to cultivate their land and to secure needed labour to successfully rehabilitate their lands. Specific activities will be determined at the onset of the project, while designing a tailored BoQ and workplan with each BNF based on their assessed needs. The cash assistance will be disbursed in three instalments (40%, 40%, 20%), for a total average amount of 2000 ILS per HH. The first two instalments will be provided following the agricultural season needs timeframe. As the BNF will advance the 20% for the inputs, the third and final instalment will be a reimbursement granted based on the purchase of all agricultural inputs. In exceptional cases where the BNF would not be able to advance 20% of the needed funds, the project team could decide to provide all the needed cash in only 2 instalments (40%, 60%), after validation by the Project supervisor and in coordination with the DoA. The workplan will be monitored through visits to each HH by ACTED and ACAD staff to review progress and decide whether to trigger, postpone or cancel subsequent instalments. The visits will also help identify technical challenges and provide corrective actions. To confirm use of funds, assess BNF satisfaction and formulate recommendations, ACTED’s AMEU will conduct a Post-Rehabilitation and Post-Distribution Monitoring of this activity. A3. ACTED and ACAD will support the 120 most vulnerable herders through restricted unconditional cash for rehabilitation and winterization of their animal shelters and provision of cash for inputs. Specifically, a self-repair approach will be used for the rehabilitation of shelters through the provision of cash-for-rehabilitation. The cash assistance will be disbursed in three instalments (40%, 40%, 20%), for a total amount of 2,700 ILS on average per HH. The workplan will be monitored through visits to each HH by the project team to review progress and decide whether to trigger, postpone or cancel subsequent instalments. The visits will also help identify technical challenges and provide corrective actions. To confirm use of funds, assess BNF satisfaction and formulate recommendations, ACTED’s AMEU will conduct a Post-Rehabilitation Monitoring of this activity. A4. Strengthening of CBOs: A community-based protection approach, established in 2016 by ACTED under previous projects, will be followed throughout the whole project to ensure solid engagement and ownership at community level and to guarantee better respect of the safety and privacy of each individual, especially the most vulnerable groups (female-headed HH, PwD, older people). ACTED and ACAD will identify and strengthen the capacity of 8 CBOs in the targeted areas, which should be comprised of at least 5 to 7 members each (of which at least 30% should be women). Should target communities not be represented by any CBOs, ACTED and ACAD will establish CBOs (5 to 7 members, at least 30% female members). The capacity of CBOs members will be strengthened, based on project needs, notably on protection-related topics (protection, PSEA, case referrals), climate change awareness, disaster-risk reduction, legal framework of the intervention, and project-related topics. The contents and the pedagogy will be adapted to ensure a better understanding and the relevancy to the context specificities. CBOs will also be used to identify unmet needs which ACAD and ACTED staff will then refer to external parties. At each step of the implementation, ACTED and ACAD will ensure to prioritize the most vulnerable categories, such as PwD, older people, and girls/women, with a particular focus on BNF affected by protection-related concerns. Finally, ACTED will ensure that a standard close on PSEA is included in all agreements in accordance with the IASC (Inter-Agency Standing Committee) principles and guidelines.

3 - Indicators

Project implementation will be supported by a comprehensive monitoring system to gather all relevant data to steer the action, maximize its impact, measure its effects, anticipate and mitigate any challenge. Specifically, ACTED’s Appraisal, Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU) is independent from the Program Department and will be in charge of reinforcing the projects’ accountability throughout the project cycle management by regularly assessing project implementation. AMEU is an essential part of ACTED’s project management structure, providing a mechanism to crosscheck and give recommendations on the work of the project team and partners. AMEU leads assessment of baseline situations of beneficiaries through data collection, monitors ongoing implementation through regular field visits and consultations with beneficiaries, and evaluates the outcome of the activities. This methodology ensures the AMEU uses an outcome monitoring approach to analyse the effect of the action on target groups. ACTED will measure the following indicators: # of farmers, herders, breeders, fishers receiving inputs and services. Target: 320; # of dunums made productive or improved production capacity – target: 500; # of Community-based organisations (CBOs) reinforced throughout a refreshing training – target: 8; % of CBOs' members who demonstrate improvement in their knowledge after attending the refreshing training – target: 80% % of HH reporting that the humanitarian assistance delivered is appropriate and satisfying – target: 85%. ACTED’s project management team will use the Project Management Framework (PMF) tool to plan and monitor implementation to assess progress and highlight blocking points. The action will promote the use of best practices in accountability and transparency, in line with Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) principles, by using ACTED’s Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM). The CRM will enable beneficiaries and other stakeholders to provide feedback and suggestions directly to ACTED staff through a phone line, post, email, and complaint boxes in relevant locations, as well as directly to partner’s staff members. The CRM should ensure that beneficiaries can easily and without fear of repercussion communicate cases of abuse of power by financial institutions, community leaders and/or other key actors involved in the cash-transfer program and/or aspects of program and/or transfer modality that inadvertently have negative implications. ACTED’s AMEU is in charge of handling this mechanism to provide timely response or adequate action to address any concerns.Financial monitoring will be undertaken by all project partners and then consolidated by ACTED, with external audits of the action taking place on an annual basis. ACTED staff will request from ACAD monthly reporting on activities on top of contractual obligations to ensure all indicators are on track and register the beneficiaries’ satisfaction and any difficulties encountered. This monthly reporting will minimise the risk of delays in the activities’ implementation, that any change in the implementation of the project is notified and finally that all indicators are on track and if needed corrective measures are taken to reach the target.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

Cash assistance ensures multisectoral coverage, while keeping beneficiaries involved all along the activities, as they can choose the more adapted items to cover their multiple needs. As noted in the 2022 MSNA findings, cash assistance is beneficiaries’ preferred method, as 64.2% of households surveyed in the WB reported that they would like to receive aid in the form of cash and voucher assistance. This is particularly accurate for women and PwD, as cash provides them with more choice and flexibility, while safeguarding their dignity. In addition to being a cost-efficient method for humanitarian actors, the multiplier effect associated with injecting cash within the local economy will contribute towards restoring the Palestinian economy.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

The project will be underpinned by ACTED’s programmatic approach of meaningful community engagement and participation of the population, in line with the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Five Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP), namely (1) leadership/governance; (2) transparency; (3) feedback and complaints; (4) participation; (5) design, monitoring and evaluation. More specifically, AAP will be ensured throughout the intervention by taking account of, giving account to, and being held to account by the affected population as follows. ACTED and ACAD will be held to account by the beneficiaries in line with Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) principles, by using ACTED’s Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM). The CRM is overseen by ACTED’s AME Unit, which is independent from the delivery of activities. Beneficiaries can access ACTED’s CRM through a dedicated phone line, WhatsApp, email address and complaints boxes as well as directly to ACTED staff during field visits. Through this CRM, beneficiaries can raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on ACTED and co-applicants’ work in a secure and confidential way, and receive a response in a timely manner. ACTED’s AMEU is in charge of handling this mechanism to provide timely response or adequate action to address any concerns. Having a well-designed and well-managed mechanism for handling beneficiary complaints will improve the quality of the action, enhance the trust and confidence of beneficiaries and identify areas of the project which need to be improved, and ensure that co-applicants learn from the feedback provided through this process.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

Beneficiaries can access ACTED’s CRM through a dedicated phone line, WhatsApp, email address, complaints boxes and directly through ACTED staff during field visits. This plurality of means aims to ensure CRM accessibility to all, including vulnerable groups with specific needs. At the first stage of the project, during the assessment, field officers will provide a CRM card to each BNF, including all communication channels mentioned above, and explanations on the CRM, to ensure that all BNF feel comfortable and in measure to reach it if needed. Female staff, part of the team to which BNF will be able to refer to, will notably ensure female BNF feel safe to complain. AME staff will dedicate specific time to vulnerable groups during field visits to re-explain the CRM and emphasise that BNF can directly complain to them during visits - if one person would not have the capacity to use/access a phone/email address or the complaint box (notably children, PwD, and the elderly). ACTED’s commitment to accountability to affected populations is fully integrated into its recruitment process, staff inductions, trainings and performance management and partnership agreements. All ACTED staff working for the ACTED oPt mission are trained on the Code of Conduct, on the Policy Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Child Protection Policies. Trainings on PSEA aim to ensure that all staff understand the Code of Conduct and the PSEA policy, their obligation to report any allegation or suspicion and the sanction measures taken for any breach of the Code of Conduct. In early March 2022, all ACTED staff also participated in a refresher session on PSEA organized by the PSEA network, to strengthen their capacities to recognize exploitation and abuse situations, as well as adopt a good behaviour to report. This session also provided an overview of the Interagency Community Complaint Mechanism and its SOPs.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

In all programming, ACTED ensures respect and promotion of the rights of all individuals and communities involved, in accordance with the relevant bodies of law (international human rights law and international humanitarian law). In order to take account of affected populations, the action will also take strong consideration of the needs and interests of beneficiary communities, particularly in regard to designing activities with their safety and dignity in mind. As part of protection mainstreaming, ACTED analyzes protection risks; anticipates possible consequences that may arise; and ensures that issues can be reported/referrals can be made to appropriate agencies/host government structures if required. In particular, ACTED and ACAD will undertake regular visits to the communities and beneficiary households to ensure that protection concerns are incorporated throughout the intervention, thus enhancing safety, security and privacy for women, children and other vulnerable groups. ACTED is firmly committed to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality, and strives to ensure the ‘Do-No-Harm’ approach by making sure that its activities do not inadvertently worsen tensions among different groups due to its mere presence and/or provision of assistance. To this end, all staff will be made aware of and in adherence to the Do-No-Harm principle as outlined in ACTED’S Code of Conduct and related policies including Child Protection; Anti-Fraud, Bribery & Corruption; Anti- Terrorism; and Policy Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Under this framework, ACTED has developed mechanisms that can contribute to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse such as: signing of the code of conduct by all staff in order to clarify unacceptable behavior and in particular sexual exploitation and abuse; information to the beneficiaries of existing remedies (Complaints and Response Mechanisms); implementation of training and awareness sessions in order to train and empower the teams and identify risk factors on an ongoing basis.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In order to maximize the reach of the intervention, the project will adopt a deeply integrated protection approach (IPA) throughout all stages of the project cycle. The action fully acknowledges that different groups and individuals have specific needs and assistance will therefore be adapted accordingly. To this end, ACTED’s project team will ensure that information is provided through accessible channels, formats, languages and family and community support networks to ensure that the beneficiary community are kept fully informed of the assistance available under the project.ACTED’s AMEU will also ensure equal and impartial access to aid by overseeing the CRM, as well as by conducting various verification exercises. For example, ACTED’s AMEU will conduct a verification exercise to authenticate the fairness of the selection process and all applicants will be notified of the final decision and provided with the means to raise concerns regarding the selection process through ACTED’s CRM. Similarly, to ensure the agency of beneficiaries and further local ownership of project outcomes, the affected population will be actively consulted during the assessments to ensure BoQs and workplans are responsive to their self-identified most pressing needs. Where applicable, at least one male and one female per household will be consulted to ensure gender-specific needs and concerns are considered, as well as those of boys, girls, PwDs, older people and other marginalised persons to ensure assistance reflects their unique vulnerabilities. In addition, CBO members and ACTED staff will be present to ensure the integration of household concerns into BoQ and workplan design. In the event beneficiaries are dissatisfied with their respective BoQ and/or workplan an appeal mechanism will be offered through the CBOs and ACTED’s CRM. If the CBOs or ACTED/ACAD staff consider the request justified, a new BoQ and/or workplan will be produced by a different agronomist. To mitigate this, all staff involved in the assessment process will undergo extensive refresher training in protection standards prior to the assessments to ensure equal and impartial access to assistance. Finally, the project will deliberately target the most vulnerable groups for inclusion into the program. For instance, women involved in farming activities in the targeted areas will be approached and highly encouraged to participate in the CBOs to ensure that membership remains at 30% female at a minimum. After all, women represent a significant but underserved segment of the agricultural workforce in the target areas. Similar efforts will be made to ensure that vulnerabilities do not act as a barrier to accessing aid. For instance, CBOs will conduct household visits to assist in the completion and collection of applications for the vulnerable (including elderly, people with disabilities) that might be unable to reach the registration site.
