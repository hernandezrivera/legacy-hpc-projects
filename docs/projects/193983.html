Project Summary

Aimed at delivering emergency life-saving basic services and at preventing/reducing excess mortality and morbidity, the Action embeds ACTED experience in Blue Nile, and is informed by the multi-sectoral assessment to bridge the IDPs, returnees, and residents' major needs driven by prolonged conflict, displacements, hazards, disease outbreaks, and the economic crisis' effects in Bau, Geissan and Kurmuk.
In the framework of an integrated Action, ACTED will identify hot spots of water contamination and diseases through health centers to be prioritized by increasing access to safe, clean drinking water through rehabilitation of water assets, public latrines and handwashing points, provision of water quality control and treatment, as well as an awareness component including delivery of hygiene and dignity kits.

1 - Needs

As highlighted by IPC Acute Food Insecurity Analysis published in June 2022, up to 11.7M people faced acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above) in Sudan in 2022, compared to 9.8M in 2021. Blue Nile state, and particularly Baw and Kurmuk counties, ranks among the five Sudanese states with most food insecure people, with over 20% of the population classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). This is the result of a series of factors, including increases in food and other commodity prices due to rampant inflation; a reduced harvest due to climatic shocks such as flash rain, floods and droughts; and continued conflict impacting the normal conduction of income-generating activities and movements of workers and commodities. Projections for early 2023 are even more alarming considering that the first months of the year correspond to the harvesting season, showing a percentage of food insecure (IPC Phase 3 or above) population in Blue Nile over 11% higher than early 2022. The WASH situation in Sudan has further deteriorated in 2022 as a result of the exacerbating economic crisis and protracted political instability, leading to a lack of infrastructure investment or maintenance. The most vulnerable under this perspective are displaced and extremely poor populations who cannot afford WASH services and practices and are therefore more exposed to WASH-related diseases. In Blue Nile, high prevalence of poor hygiene, including open defecation (OD) and inadequate treatment/storage of drinking water contributes to high incidence of cholera, diarrhoea, dysentery, typhoid. Communal assets such as health facilities, schools and markets often have inadequate or completely lack sanitation services. Water sources also appear scarce, thus far away to be easily reached by foot from certain locations, and often in poor condition or not functioning.With the outbreak of fighting on 15 April 2023, the WASH situation in Sudan will only grow more dire. Economic instability and disruptions in supply chain will couple with the influx of formerly out-of-camp refugees to the camps in Blue Nile, creating an increased burden on the available water sources in Blue Nile, as well as the existing santiation systems. These factors will compound the already poor WASH situation in Blue Nile, creating even greater need for affected populations.

2 - Activities/Outputs

ACTED will support target communities through MPCA, as per major needs and vulnerabilities identified. 3 monthly rounds of MPCA will be reserved to 1,500 vulnerable conflict, flood, drought affected and displaced HHs in Damazine for them to make autonomous decisions on how to fulfil their priority needs, while more sustainable solutions to income-generation are identified. Within the supported sites, ACTED will focus on an integrated WASH response, including ensuring an adequate water supply, improving access to sanitation facilities, and providing access to hygiene items for camp residents. Under this project, ACTED is providing daily water trucking for 8,819 camp residents in Village 6, Wad al Mahi, Blue Nile, while constructing and rehabilitating existing water points for camp residents. Alongside water point construction and rehabilitation, ACTED is conducting water monitoring through a water committee in Wad al Mahi. In addition, ACTED is rehabilitating hafirs in Ulu, Blue Nile, ensuring access to water for 6,000 individuals. 3 water committees are established, conducting water monitoring at these sites. In addition to water supply activities, ACTED is constructing 40 latrines in Wad al Mahi and Ulu, and rehabilitating 50 latrines in Wad al Mahi. 80 waste pins will be constructed and 10 rehabilitated in Wad al Mahi, serving 2,610 individuals. In addition, ACTED is rehabilitating the sanitation facilities of 4 health centers and 6 schools in Ulu, serving 5,550 individuals. A total of 6,150 individuals will be reached by awareness raising activities and hygiene promotion at schools in Ulu. CLTS activities in Ulu will reach 17,750 individuals in total. In addition to the awareness activities around hygiene, ACTED will distribute hygiene and dignity kits on a quarterly basis to the 8,819 residents of Village 6. 6,150 additional individuals will receive hygiene kits in Ulu.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The Program envisages specific needs faced by different socio-demographic groups direct and encourages gender-balanced participation of target communities– with a zero-tolerance policy towards discrimination– throughout the Action, which is embedding the “Do No Harm Principle” and follows a conflict-sensitive approach. Preparation Phase: - Before each intervention, ACTED conducts an assessment of security context and protection risks, followed by regular monitoring of situation throughout project’s lifespan. - The whole community will be sensitised on project’s scope and activities and fully informed on selection criteria, which were designed to address protection risks and cover sectoral needs of most vulnerable (single women, PLWS, children, elders, PwDs, HHs with no source of income, HHs with PwDs, HHs with more children under 5 years old). Program Implementation Phase: - ACTED mainstreams gender-adapted and key protection messages during community sensitization and trainings. - MHM component is included. - Distances to fetch water, waiting time, safety during traveling will be addressed through rehabilitations and ad hoc protection measures. - First aid kits and protective wear are also provided to ACTED’s staff, beneficiaries and daily workers, in line with the type of activities. Accountability: - ACTED has an independent Appraisal, Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU) with experience in setting up Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRMs). A dedicated staff will be responsible to manage the CRM at Area office and Field office levels, where beneficiaries can report complaints/feedback in a safe and confidential way. Beside its staff permanently deployed in the field, ACTED will also conduct regular spot checks. ACTED will evaluate if the complaint was triggered due to flaws, shortcomings or gaps in procedures or implementation strategy. In addition to caseby case handling, anonymized complaints reports will be shared internally to possibly reshape the ongoing Action and inform the future projects’ design. A more detailed description of the CRM is provided under the AAP section of this proposal Human Resources - ACTED has a Code of Conduct that is consistent with the U.N. Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Task Force on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse in Humanitarian Crises. ACTED also complies with The Code of Conduct for The International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement and the UNHCR Code of Conduct. This CoC is implemented in each program location and is under the direct supervision of the Program Manager. Any incident would be immediately reported to the coordination through a standardized communication pattern and actions will be taken by the coordination. - ACTED will set-up a gender-balanced Program team to ensure a safer environment, improve protection mainstreaming and favour inclusionary mechanisms. In all activities, ACTED will promote an equal gender participation.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

As part of its accountability strategy, ACTED will mainstream protection and PSEA elements during all activities, particularly trough safety audit tools, CRM and inclusion of PSEA training modules into capacity development action plans for community governance structures. ACTED has appropriate and proportionate procedures and controls in place to prevent, report, detect, respond and report on safeguarding issues & allegations. Recruitment - ACTED prevents employment/deployment of unsuitable individuals through rigorous screening. People employed by ACTED already have obligation to read and sign the code of conduct and internal policies. Administration and employee cycle: - Signature of Code of Conduct for staff; - Monitoring of trial periods for cases detected, - Disciplinary measures follow-up on cases detected in conjunction with country coordination. Training program related to safeguarding issues. - ACTED has an in-house training related to safeguarding issues: i) Annual training on the Code of Conduct and Policies in all countries and at HQ for 7000 staff; ii) Pre-departure training for international employees. Iii) Specific training on SEA & Child Protection in intervention countries. ACTED Complaints and response mechanisms. - ACTED will establish Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) for beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback in a secure way, and receive response in a timely manner. A dedicated staff will be responsible to manage CRM permanently installed in Area Office as well as in all target locations throughout the project. At the beginning and during implementation, target communities will be sensitized on the mechanism, and on how to submit complaints. FGDs shall be organised. Complaints will be confidentially recorded and reported each month at country-level. CRM will ensure accountability to beneficiaries’ and build trust between ACTED and community, pay specific attention to gender-related concerns, and improve programs quality, moving from intention to prevent/mitigate any adverse social/environmental/health risks. Complaint’s investigation & disciplinary measures - ACTED has capacity to investigate safeguarding issues in an independent and confidential way, while protecting both the alleged victim and the alleged perpetrator (ACTED SEA Policy, Articles 19 to 21; ACTED Child protection policy, Articles 20 to 22). ACTED must always ensure that the author of a report does not suffer from any harm as a result. ACTED also endeavours to protect victims of prohibited acts (ACTED SEA policy, article 23). ACTED has psychologists available to support individuals who would have been impacted as a result of a failure to comply with ACTED's safeguarding policies. The HQ HR department will assess allegations to design an investigation plan and select an investigation team. Depending on investigation conclusions, different measures can be taken including disciplinary actions or reporting to prosecuting authority.
