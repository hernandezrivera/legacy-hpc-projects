Project Summary

PCDCR targets affected children by conflict related violence & violations& child protection workers, namely the community-based organizations (CBOs)working on children protection in Jordan Valley (JV) in areaC of West Bank(WB). This target is due to urgent needs & bad psychosocial status of children in JV because of school drop-out, working in Israeli settlements, facing sexual harassments, suffering from health diseases, negative coping mechanism & so on, as stated in HNO2020, HRP2020 & others. The need has become even more urgent due to COVID-19 outbreak. JV in areaC is targeted since it’s one of most critical areas in WB where people particularly children face many protection challenges from occupation at different levels. This coercive environment & situation deterioration grows due to Israeli annexation plans. The project targets the most affected 6areas in JV: Ein Shibli-Nablus governorate, AlAqaba, Kardala, Bardala, & Ein Elbeida-Tubas governorate, & AlAuja-Jericho governorate. PCDCR, member in Global Partnership to End Violence against Children, is located in Nablus & access all targeted locations & populations to provide its services. PCDCR’s previous experiences in targeting all WB particularly for children projects
As children in JV suffer from conflict related violations & violence & JV is one of the vulnerable areas for Palestinians, PCDCR targets children both males & females & CBOs in order to achieve complementary work to meet children’s need
Through this project, PCDCR participates in meeting the need 498.7k of cluster obj.2. Thus, project goal is:Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent & mitigate the effects of the occupation & conflict related violence on children in JV. The goal is achieved by following strategic objectives:
1.The wellbeing of children who have been affected by conflict related violence & violations is improved through PSS services
2.Child protection workers are empowered in MHPSS for children affected by conflict related violence & violations. Thus, PCDCR contributes to & align with HRP2020 objectives as well as protection cluster objectives related to children protection especially in areaC. To that end, the following interventions will be conducted:
1-Provision of emergency child protection responses, including case management, psychosocial support & programs aimed at supporting vulnerable children & families who have been affected by conflict related violence & violations & violence in households. It includes individual & group counselling for 134 children(males & females) in need
2-Child Protection workers are trained on the guidelines on mental health & psychosocial support(MHPSS):6 CBOs (24male & female representatives)working on child protection in JV will be trained & empowered on guidelines on MHPSS. The guideline on MHPSS will be conducted & distributed to the participants
Protection & gender are mainstreamed under the project. PCDCR also has solid relevant experiences & good partnerships. PCDCR accesses targeted areas & communities through:
1-CBOs: PCDCR has long experience with them as Ein Elbeida Women Center, Bardala Women Center,etc
2-Municipal & village councils: PCDCR’s cooperated with Ein Shibli village council in conducting plans, trainings, meetings with different beneficiaries, & other activities related to women economic empowerment. PCDCR cooperated with AlAuja municipality on accountability & municipalities’ development
3-Schools: PCDCR’s cooperated with many schools in targeted locations so we can coordinate with schools-if needed- only to get lists of dropped out children or most affected children in targeted locations.No activities'll be inside schools so no coordination with education cluster is required; but they'll be in CBOs.
PCDCR contacted Chief Child Protection during the proposal phase to ensure alignment with HRP2020. PCDCR is an active partner in protection cluster (CPWG & GBV)
PCDCR’ll resort to virtual means in case of COVID19 curfew

1 - Needs

HNO(2020) confirms that 44,6% of people in need for protection are children living in marginalized areas of the West Bank (WB), as area C including Jordan Valley(JV). The Palestinian children suffer from conflict-related violations particularly children living in area C including JV. In area C, the children are exposed to all elements of coercive environment, conflict-related, IHRL, & education-related violations. They are also vulnerable to violence, violations by ISF, & at risk of resorting to negative coping mechanisms (HRP 2020). Particularly, children in JV have faced an increase in overall grave violations, house demolition, & settler violence. The settler violence & the climate of fear & intimidation created by repeated violence & harassment have a serious psychological impact on children affecting their psychosocial well-being (HNO2020). Children also are at risk of resorting to negative coping mechanisms, dropping out of school, labor in settlements exposing to severe forms of violence. The school dropout out in (WB) is 0.99% (1.27% boys & .71% girls); particularly 1.4% (2% males & 0.8% females in Tubas) (The Educational Statistical Yearbook 2018/2019). Children in high schools (15-17 years) demonstrate high levels of school dropout especially among males three times the females. Around 7.8% of males (15-17 years) dropped of school versus 2.6% in Tubas. In Jericho, 5.2% males (15-17 years) dropped out versus 1.1% females. About 4.9% of children (10-15 years old) are out of school; 7.6% of them are boys and 2.2% are girls; according to UNICEF Palestine Report on Out-Of-School Children2018. The labor of children (10-17 years) reaches 4% in WB (6% males & 0.8% females); PCBS2019. The deputy director of Directorate of Education in Tubas ensured children in JV face severe risks, consume long time going to school due to occupation, study in camps as occupation destroyed their schools, & drop out of schools to work in Israeli settlement where they face abuse & harassment. Human Rights Watch2015 indicated that33 children out of38 (total sample for their report) stated following violations in JV: lower wages than other workers in WB, exposure to poisonous chemical materials without the required safety procedures or tools, no legal contract resulting in informal labor conditions; thus, no labor insurance. The sample suffered from forced school drop-out because of poor life conditions caused by Israeli control over the land & water. The Directorate of Labor office in JV-Jericho indicated that children living in JV are forced to leave school & work in Israeli settlements where they face violence as: school drop-out, sexual harassments, drugs, & health diseases due to unhealthy work environment with unjust conditions. Percentage of children labor in Israeli settlement increases causing negative impact on their psychological & social wellbeing due to harsh living conditions.In addition to the above, the deterioration of the situation in JV grows lately due to annexation plans in the area. Where Israeli occupation plans to annex around 68% of JV, the livelihood of children & their family become even more difficult. In addition, COVID-19 lockdown also makes the environment even much worse & pressing on the children in JV. The movement limit of workers to home in WB has resulted that many workers stayed in Israel. This has created additional burdens on women and children (Findings of COVID-19 Analysis, 2020). The displacement during the COVID-19 pandemic would increase protection vulnerabilities greatly in JV for both males & females.Accordingly, the environment of JV causes devastating psychosocial effect on children both males & females. The children are the most vulnerable group in JV. As a result of that, children living in JV suffer from lack of receiving protection response interventions to prevent & mitigate the effects of occupation & conflict related violence. This problem is due to two reasons:1-lack of psychosocial support(PSS) services for the affected children which let their psychosocial status getting worse;2-lack of capabilities of children protection workers on providing MHPSS in these areas. To meet these needs, PCDCR will participate in providing protection response mechanisms for affected children in JV through providing them with PSS services (group counselling) and specialized individual case management. PCDCR will also empower the CBOs working on/for children in JV in providing MHPSS for the children. PCDCR targets male & female children with reasonable % as they both suffer from the same violence. The % of school dropout & labor among male children are higher about 3 times than females. The project tends to bridge this gap through targeting more male children than females. PCDCR considers the gender alert analysis 2020 in addition to other needs. For instance, both boys & girls in area C in JV do not have access to technology for distance learning practices during COVID-19, this leads to higher percentage of school dropout & labor, work in settlement, & other forms of violence. The school drop out increases after COVID-19 pandemic that creates the worst economic situation for their families. PCDCR focuses on children due to annual increase in percentages of school drop-out, child labor, & violations against them. Protection concerns are considered in all stages to ensure safety for children, engaged parties & staff. PCDCR is member in Global Partnership to End Violence against Children, protection cluster (GBV SC, CPWG), Child Protection Committees, Higher Council of Childhood & many networks at different levels. PCDCR has in place children safeguard policy & code of conduct

2 - Activities

As PCDCR a local NGO capable of covering all West Bank to access targeted locations &population easily, the following activities will be implemented successfully. Hence, PCDCR will achieve project goal, contribute to the strategic objectives of HRP2020 & protection cluster objectives, under which our project is proposed, & meet HNO2020 needs. The following activities consider gender & age needs, & protection for all engaged entities: Noting that all activities will be face-to-face unless the COVID-19 curfew is imposed we will move to remote interventions. In cases of partial COVID-19 lockdown, PCDCR considers health protocols & COIVD-19 prevention measures. 1.Provision of emergency child protection responses, including case management, psychosocial support & programmes aimed at supporting vulnerable children & families who have been affected by conflict related violence & violations & violence in households. To that end, the following will be done:1.1.Training 10graduates of psychology (5 males & 5 females; both gain equal opportunities) to qualify them as educators run the structured psychosocial support(PSS) group sessions(group counselling) for children in need. The educators are youths graduates above 23 years. The educators’ training lasts for20 hours1.2.Holding 6preparatory meetings for targeted community-based organizations(CBOs) working on child protection to initiate the project & explain its goals along with their roles. It’ll be one meeting in each targeted CBO & 15participants (5males &10 females) per meeting. The participants are adults above 19 years.1.3.Holding 4focus group discussions to assess the needs of children affected by occupation & conflict related violence & violations in JV. Thus, based on these discussions the specialized PSS interventions (both individual & group counselling) will be arranged appropriately. The breakdown of 4focus group discussions is:2 focus group discussions for children (15-17 years since the highest % of school dropout occur during this period). One group for each of male &female children.2 focus group discussions for parents (one for each of women & men). Each focus group discussion includes 7participants. Persons with disability are highly encouraged to participate in focus group discussions to identify the appropriate needs for them.1.4.Providing 84 children (10-17 years), who have been affected by conflict related violence & violations & dropped out of school or involved in labor, with specialized PSS services (group counselling). This will be done through groups. Hence, 12 groups of children (8 groups for males & 4 for females) will be targeted as follows: 2 groups in each location & 7 children in each group will receive four sessions of PSS. The PSS services (group counselling) will be run by the trained specialized educators for both female & male children. Children with physical disability are highly encouraged to benefit from the group counselling. PCDCR ensures appropriate places and transportation for them as needed.1.5.Providing 50 children aged 10-17 years (33males;17females), who have been affected by conflict related violence & violations & dropped out of school or involved in labour, with specialized PSS (individual counselling).This will be done by psychosocial specialist at PCDCR premise. Children with physical disability are highly encouraged to benefit from the individual counselling. PCDCR ensures appropriate places and transportation for them as needed. PCDCR also provides in-home individual counselling as needed for children with physical disability.1.6.Preparing & publishing 1000 copies of an educational brochure to be distributed on male & female children (10-17 years), who are affected by conflict violence & occupation. The brochure will reinforce the knowledge & services they receive in the PSS sessions (individual & group counselling).In case of COVID-19 lockdown & curfew, the individual PSS will be provided via telephone & group PSS in addition to other meetings (1.1., 1.2, 1.3) will be done via virtual means as Zoom. PCDCR not only considers the ethics of work in physical activities but also in remote activities. Confidentiality, safety, parents consent, etc are adopted by PCDCR. Finally, the brochure will be distributed online & other educational material could be produced & disseminated virtually.2.Child Protection workers are trained on the guidelines on mental health & psychosocial support through the following sub-activities:2.1.Holding 6meetings for CBOs working on child protection to prepare them for the training;1 meeting for each CBO2.2.Holding 2central trainings for 24 representatives from 6 cooperated CBOs working on child protection on the guidelines on MHPSS. The 24 participants (16 females & 8males) are adults above 21 years. Each central training includes 3 CBOs working on child protection & lasts for 20hours. Each CBO will have 4 representatives in the training (both males & females). 2.3.Preparing, conducting, & printing 500copies of a guideline to be distributed on CBOs’ representatives who work on child protection & attend the training.During COVID-19 lockdown, (2.1, 2.2) will be done virtually. The guideline copies will be distributed both electronic & in paper version to be accessible for them anytime & anywhere.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The project is designed to achieve strategic objective #1 of oPt HRP2020 responding to 2020HNO which considers the violations of IHL & IHRL in oPt as the core of severe protection threats against Palestinians. Thus, the proposed interventions will contribute to enhance protection by respecting human rights in accordance with IHL & IHRL, demand accountability, & mitigate violations impact. Thus, the proposed project aims at protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent & mitigate the effects of the occupation & conflict related violence on children inJV. PCDCR looks for responding to children’s needs through providing PSS services & empowering CBOs working on children issues by training them on guidelines of MHPSS. Hence, children realize their full potential & abilities to live with dignity, &safety. The project promotes protection through PSS-one of self-protection mechanisms by project activities focusing on structured PSS for male & female children (individual & group counseling). The interventions considers gender, age, & disability disaggregation of affected children. The project management prioritize safety & dignity of beneficiaries by providing services to vulnerable affected children in JV confidently & privately even in case of COVID-19 lockdown. So, the targeted groups are encouraged to receive our support without being stigmatized. All project staff are committed to PCDCR child protection policies besides signing children code of conduct. All 6 CBOs in in JV will sign code of conduct to promote safety, dignity, & confidentiality of beneficiaries’ data. In addition, PCDCR commits to implement its proposed interventions without any harm for all beneficiaries & staff as PCDCR considers Do No Harm principles & has a separate program for children protection rights & one for democracy & human rights under which protection must be maintained. PCDCR considers the minimum standards for child protection in humanitarian action especially standard (10) MHPSS & ensures the adoption of standardized operating procedures (SOP) for emergency response & IASC Guidelineson MHPSS in Emergency Settings. Even in some cases where there is a need to move the beneficiaries to safer place, PCDCR’ll take the required procedures to do so & maintain safe places. PCDCR also considers the child protection standards as follows:1- Ensuring quality of child protection response through:1.1Coordination with CBOs,NGOs, OCHA, municipal & village councils & others1.2Human resources. PCDCR’ll hire a qualified staff for the project to invest their capacities & capabilities. They’ll sign & commit to the PCDCR’s child safeguarding policy & respect international child protection laws & agreements1.3Project’s cycle management will be directed towards mainly protecting all targeted groups particularly children & ensures quality of service provision. It’ll also tackle any challenges or violations against child rights at national or international laws1.4Information management for information exchange with partners & enriching PCDCR database. Thus, PCDCR’ll provide its further services depending on the needs of collected & managed information, measure quality of service provision, & develop them as needed. PCDCR’ll also maintain the privacy of children as it has internal procedures to prevent any abuse for children related information as names, photos,.etc1.5PCDCR’ll monitor child protection through designing the project, monitoring the planning, & implementing of project activities2-PCDCR develops adequate child protection strategies through community based mechanisms. 9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

PCDCR adopts the principles of humanitarian action including equality and impartiality. Hence, PCDCR will ensure & enable equal and impartial access to assistance and services by the targeted vulnerable groups (children including persons with disabilities) & CBOs working on/ for children. This target depends mainly on the needs and priority to the most urgent cases of distress. The children will be reached through coordinating with local and village councils and CBOs in order to target the most conflict-affected beneficiaries in need without any discrimination on the basis of nationality, race, gender, religious belief, class or political opinions. Accordingly, PCDCR will coordinate with the local entities in the targeted locations to identify the most affected children who are in need. Additionally, PCDCR has a MoU with the Ministry of Education and Ministry of Social Development; thus, the PCDCR could cooperate with the ministries to reach the children in need referring to their documents and records. Furtherly, PCDCR will provide individual PSS (individual counselling) as needed for children and referring only to their priorities & needs regardless of their backgrounds. As persons with disability (PwD) are at higher risks of vulnerability & face more difficult challenges than persons without disabilities, PCDCR adapts IASC guidelines on inclusion of persons with disability in humanitarian action. Thus, PCDCR integrates children with disability at different levels:1-Analysis: Both children with disabilities & parents with disabilities are involved in the focus group discussions to identify their necessities and tailor the PSS interventions based on their needs.2-Resource: There’s a budget allocation for private transportation for PwD to allow them to participate & benefit from our interventions.3- Implementation: The data is disaggregated not only by gender & age but also by disability. PCDCR selects physically accessible locations for PwD to engage in the activities. Also, we provide in-home individual PSS for children in need.4- Monitoring & Evaluation: PCDCR will consider the feedback & satisfaction of children with disabilities about provided services. We may conduct sub-plans based on their feedback to improve the provided services. Such sub-plans will be approved by OCHA.
