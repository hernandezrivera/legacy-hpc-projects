Project Summary

Tdh will continue child protection services for the well-being of children and adolescents of Rohingya and host community with the objective is to strengthening protective factors and reducing risk factors. Tdh will operate an Integrated and Holistic Child protection program in camp 27 at every level of the socio-ecological framework. Besides this, Tdh Child Protection (CP) sector has been implementing child protection interventions since 2017 in refugee camps and host communities across Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazilas of Cox’s Bazar.
Tdh's Child Protection Sector wants to strengthen services and systems for protecting children throughout the camps and host communities by 2023. The program will maintain sectoral coordination with a priority on integrated services for children with disabilities while ensuring the continuation of existing services such as, unstructured psychosocial support (PSS), structured PSS, case management services, adolescent engagement, and community-based child protection mechanisms, such as the Community-based child protection committee (CBCPC). Children with disabilities will get the services listed above in every area of the Child Protection program. To provide prompt assistance, referral support for kids and teens, especially kids with impairments will be boosted. The program will take the effort to make sure that every facility is secure from calamity. For better understanding on the part of the emergency response team, PSS programming will be tailored to the specific needs of girls and boys with a particular focus on adolescents and with the objective of increasing resilience and well-being From the MPCC/home-based one and one session and Child Play Area (CPA), children from both refugee and host communities will be received support. Tdh will complement these one-off, low-impact structured play activities at the CPA with more holistic, longer-term structured PSS in the Multi-Purpose Community Centre (MPCC) at camp 27. The objective will be to strengthen children’s resilience and well-being through weekly sessions for two months, targeting core competencies outlined in Tdh’s MHPSS Framework.
By attending the meetings, workshops and offering technical help as needed, Tdh will continue to maintain excellent collaboration with the Child Protection Sub-Sector (CPSS), Case Management Technical Working Group (CMTWG), and CPSS localization working group. Through active participation and contribution, Tdh CP activities will be carried out in coordination with protection working group (PWG), the mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) working group, the age and disability working group, and the youth working group. The status report, the 5W report, and the need-based report would all get sectoral contributions from Tdh.
Child protection data will be gender-, age- and disability- disaggregated. In line with the IASC Guidelines on Inclusion of Persons with Disabilities in Humanitarian Action, the Washington Group Short Set of Questions will be used to ensure Tdh identifies the risks, barriers and needs faced by persons with disabilities.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Among 945,953 Rohingya people living in Cox’s Bazar and Bhasan Char, 52% are children who are mostly dependent on humanitarian support (Ref: UNHCR-Govt. Population factsheet September 2022). Children’s concerns about their safety in the camps have been affecting their access to basic services and encouraged unhealthy coping techniques since the beginning of the influx. Children living in Cox’s Bazar (camps and host communities) are still facing diversified protection risks and challenges that reflect their different vulnerabilities, needs, strengths, and capacities depending on a number of intersecting factors, such as age, gender, disability, and other relevant diversity factors. According to the Child Protection Sub Sector (CPSS) assessment from October 2021, the most reported child protection concerns perceived as existing currently in the camps were Child Labour (64%), neglect (59%), Child Marriage (57%) and risks of trafficking (32%). These were followed closely by depression (27%), separation of children (25%) and physical abuse of the child (23%). Mental health and psychosocial distress is also important child protection concern in this setting. Unaccompanied and separated children need special attention as they are more prone to different forms of protection concerns Rohingya households reported that at least one child is engaged in child labour. This is also a great concern in the host community as a significant number of working children are involved in hazardous work. To reduce various types of conflict between the host society and the Rohingya people, social cohesion must be ensured. As a result, the program will take a comprehensive approach, providing services to people over the age of 18 (including boys and girls in various camps and host towns) as well as children and adolescents aged 0 to 18. Services such as case management, structured psycho-social support, early childhood development, life skills, pre-vocational skills, and service provision for children with disabilities will all be provided using a holistic approach. In order to increase the system's overall child safety measures, It will also involve the local community and other significant stakeholders.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The development of self protection capacities is central to Tdh’s Child Protection approach which operates on a socio-ecological level, empowering individuals (Case Management, MHPSS), families (Positive Parenting, PM+), and the wider community (CBCPC), to respond to child protection risks and increase their wellbeing. This will also be achieved through outreach activities which will equip targeted communities with the knowledge to improve hygiene practices, identification of child protection risks, and health seeking behaviour as well as knowledge of where to access services. Tdh will continue to build the capacity of health staff, WASH staff, and community health workers, including on child protection mainstreaming, as well as volunteers supporting the Case Management process. This will enable communities to engage meaningfully and substantially in all aspects of Tdh programs that affect them, strengthening the community’s leading role as driving force of change, and their capacity to respond.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Prioritizing safety & dignity, and avoiding causing harm: Unintended negative effects of the intervention will be mitigated. Communities will be sensitized on the participation of women and girls for successful implementation of the activities. Registration areas at PHCs will be gender-segregated to ensure individuals can disclose sensitive information without fear or impact on their dignity as well as to ensure that they receive suitable treatment. Protection desks are established in the PHCs to ensure that protection cases are addressed and referred to confidentially to avoid further harm. Meaningful Access: Special attention is given to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services. The barriers that prevent adolescent girls, adolescent boys, and children with disabilities from accessing services will be assessed and acted upon accordingly.

3.2 - Gender

To address gender-based power imbalances and associated risks and threats, Tdh’s activities are gender-sensitive and disability-inclusive. By advocating at the community-level through FGD and outreach for the inclusion of marginalized groups like adolescent girls, LGBTQI+ community, female-headed households and people with disabilities in decision-making processes, Tdh will contribute to social norms change and gender equality. Project has in-built mechanism for surveys including beneficiaries from all age and gender groups including PWDs. Findings and recommendations from the surveys will be incorporated in adjusting the project activities. Moreover, project has Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism and dedicated hotline in receiving feedback from beneficiaries that includes their say in project decisions. All the staff members including the outreach volunteers will be oriented to cover beneficiaries from all categories including LGBTQ and people with disabilities.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

The Child Safeguarding Policy (CSP) shall apply to any cases relating to a child. Tdh’s Global Code of Conduct (GCC) refers to IASC’s six principles on PSEA in its foreword and our safeguarding policies further relay these standards. All our staff, volunteers, partners and any visitors or persons representing Tdh must read, sign, and be briefed on our GCC and must abide by it during and after working hours. The same stakeholders must read and sign our Child Safeguarding Policy which also refers to a specific Code of Conduct, and they must be provided with our Policy on the Protection of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). Inappropriate behavior and abuse in the workplace are taken very seriously within Tdh and the organisation actively promotes a culture of respect and equality both at headquarters and in the delegations. Conduct awareness session quarterly with staff.

3.4 - Diversity

Tdh will place children and communities at the centre of development and recovery, regardless of their age, gender, role, religion, race as well as Tdh will enable all children and adults regardless of their age, gender or diversity to engage, participate in and influence our projects, and to benefit in an equitable way from what the project will influence and change. The Gender and Diversity Policy outlines Tdh’s commitments and guiding principles for gender and diversity across its operations and the inner functioning of the organization. It provides an overall framework for the organization to bring about a more inclusive working environment, and to mainstream and monitor gender and diversity in Tdh operations.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

In compliance with AAP framework, Tdh will involve beneficiaries at every Project Cycle’s steps, from needs assessment and activities design to M&E process. Tdh will ensure compliance and transparency with the AAP framework through the implementation and management of the child friendly Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) that appropriately includes girls and boys, men and women, parents, teachers, community leaders, religious leaders, people with disabilities, minorities and other groups who may identify themselves as excluded and vulnerable. The complaint forms capture gender and age of each responder before feedback/complaint is recorded. Complaints are also handled through Tdh hotline and through a single email address (concern@tdh.ch) at Tdh headquarters. ‘Do No Harm’ and CHS principles will be applied throughout the project.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

In order to respond to emergencies collaboratively, Tdh already has an emergency shelter(MPCC) and ERT (Emergency Response Team) structure. The project will contribute to disaster risk reduction during floods/flash floods, and different outbreaks. During such emergency situation, project team will meet the target community needs through community-based approaches. protection and referral mechanisms. Tdh will be part of carrying out evaluation by collaborating with all the sectors working in the impacted region during environmental emergencies and man-made or natural disasters. Tdh program employees, including community volunteers, get crucial Disaster Risk Management and Emergency Response training from the DRR sector in order to respond to an emergency situation successfully. As a result, the response team will provide support to the Child Protection program and Tdh is part of PERU team.
