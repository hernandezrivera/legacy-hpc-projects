Project Summary

The project aims to strengthen the capacity of the public health system in delivering quality primary and secondary health care, with integrated GBV and MHPSS services. In close collaboration with the County Health Departments (CHDs) and State Ministry of Health (SMoH), MdM will support 41 health facilities across Bor South, Duk, and Twic East counties by providing regular supervision, monitoring, and training to the health and MHPSS/GBV personnel and supplying the necessary drugs, equipment and materials. Through Sudan Medical Care (SMC), an experienced local NGO, mobile medical units (MMUs) will be deployed to hard-to-reach locations and displaced populations. MHPSS and GBV services will be integrated through counselling services at PHCC and hospital level with awareness-raising and referral activities at community level, complemented by a remote MHPSS hotline. This intervention design which combines static with mobile health facilities, as well as remote support, and offers integrated protection services, provides an inclusive, accessible, and holistic approach to health care. The community-based component of the Boma Health Initiative (BHI) and MHPSS/GBV community facilitators makes the project embedded in local structures and contributes to long-lasting impact.
The project is targeted towards meeting the basic health, MHPSS and GBV needs of the population in Bor South, Duk, and Twic East counties, with a particular focus on vulnerable persons, such as women of reproductive age, pregnant and lactating women, at-risk pregnancies, children under 5, and people with particular needs and vulnerabilities across physical and mental wellbeing, living standards and coping mechanisms, such as displaced people, returnees, and host community members.

1 - Needs

Bor South, Duk, and Twic East counties in Jonglei State have been severely affected by conflict and flooding for the past years. Regular incidents of ambushes, cattle raiding, and intercommunal violence make the region a volatile environment with high numbers of internal displacement and obstructed humanitarian access. Seasonal flooding has damaged much of the public infrastructure and leaves communities cut off from basic services. Health needs assessments of the last two years show that the region lacks qualified health personnel, sufficient supply of essential medication, and effective referral pathways. The majority of health facilities are in need of rehabilitation and have poor WASH infrastructures. Due to limited funding, a large number of health facilities are closed, reducing the overall access to health care for the entire population. The Health Cluster has identified 149.100 people in Bor South, 77.673 people in Duk, and 60.545 people in Twic East as in need of health assistance. MdM has determined the following areas as priorities:- Neonatal and child health services: since South Sudan has one of the highest under-five mortality rates in the world (90.7 deaths per 1,000 live births) and 75% of deaths related to preventable diseases (HNO 2021), routine immunization needs to be strengthened as well as screening and referral of malnourished children.- Sexual and reproductive health: a high maternal mortality rate (789 per 100,000 – HNO 2021) and little awareness of SRH demonstrates the limited access to gender-sensitive information provision, low number of ANC and PNC coverage, low number of births attended by skilled health personnel, lack of sensitization on family planning, STIs and menstrual hygiene in South Sudan.- Communicable and non-communicable diseases: while experiencing a ‘second wave’ of COVID-19 infections country-wide, concerns at state and county level evolve around limited knowledge and sensitization on infection prevention and control and isolation ward capacity, limited access to treatment for CDs (e.g. HIV and Tuberculosis), lack of diagnosis of NCDs (e.g. hypertension and diabetes) and appropriate treatment. - CMR and GBV services: while needs assessments and security reports show a high rate of GBV incidences, the low reporting on CMR demonstrates the limited knowledge of both communities and health staff on accessible medical treatment for GBV survivors, including PEP kits. - MHPSS: a large number of persons in Jonglei are exposed to stressors such as conflict, domestic violence, substance abuse, and GBV. The HNO 2021 states that 59% of South Sudanese show signs of distress and that the overall mental well-being will deteriorate in 2021 as a result of continued violence, economic instability, and environmental hazards. The response will target the low awareness of mental health and psychological distress, lack of specialized personnel in mental health care, limited access to psychotropic medication, and low numbers of service providers trained in basic psychological support.- Emergency preparedness and response: Bor South, Duk and Twic East counties are prone to (waterborne) disease outbreaks due to flooding and poor WASH infrastructure, therefore stricter surveillance of and timely response to potential disease outbreaks is needed. Bor South, Duk, and Twic East are IPC-4 level counties, hence the referral pathway for nutrition as well as health, GBV, and MHPSS cases is essential. Stronger coordination between humanitarian and governmental service providers is needed to address the malnutrition levels and the psychological effects and risks of GBV related to food insecurity.

2 - Project activities

Guided by the National Health Policy for South Sudan (2016–2026), the IASC Guidelines on MHPSS in Emergency Settings and the WHO Pyramid Framework, MdM will ensure implementation of National Policy for Health in supported health facilities to address priority health needs and enhance provision of basic essential health services while ensuring protection mainstreaming in Bor South, Duk and Twic East counties of Jonglei state. The intervention focusses on strengthening the capacity of CHD/MoH and local implementing partners for essential health services including child health, SRH, communicable and non-communicable diseases, immunization and MHPSS by enhancing quality standards on diagnosis, treatment and timely referrals. Technical support supervision and on-the-job training for health facility staff, financial support for human resources and running costs, emergency repair of health and WASH facilities, provision of drugs, consumables and medical equipment, referrals and linkages with relevant actors for non-medical services such as protection will be provided for at least 41 targeted health facilities in the 3 counties and Boma Health Initiatives (BHIs); encompassing 14 Primary Health Care Centers (PHCCs), 22 Primary Health Care Units (PHCUs), 3 Mobile Medical Units (MMUs) and 2 secondary/ Tertiary Healthcare facilities including the state referral Hospital. At PHCU level, the intervention will improve quality standards for diagnosis and treatment of minor acute ailments, SRH and routine immunization; at PHCC level, outpatient and short-term inpatient diagnosis and treatment of communicable and non-communicable diseases including, IMNCI, BEMoNC and referral to secondary health care will be provided. The project will support in delivery of secondary and specialized services: surgical services, obstetrics/gynecology and hospitalized care. SRH services will be enhanced through support for CEMoNC and CMR. Through WHO/MoH EWARs and BHI, real-time detection and response to communicable diseases will be improved through community-based surveillance and early warning systems. COVID-19 surveillance will be integrated in all supported health facilities through enhanced IPC protocols and screening for early detection, monitoring and referral through the RRT for case investigation and contact tracing. MDM will provide support to 3 isolation centres for case management of moderate to severe cases of COVID-19. MHPSS services in the 3 counties will be integrated through the health response. Community Facilitators at PHCUs will disseminate SGBV awareness and prevention messaging at local gathering areas, while linking with referrals to counselling and legal services. In addition to wide audience community members, activities will also target vulnerable groups and men and boys to be advocates and allies in the prevention of SGBV. Referral mechanisms within the health facilities (PHCU to PHCC to secondary) will be strengthened, with particular focus on reporting and referring SGBV cases. MHPSS counselling will take place in 9 different health facilities in Bor South and 2 health facilities in Duk and Twic East. MHPSS individual counselling sessions are available for any person presenting emotional distress, including linked to medical disorders, malnutrition, trauma, SGBV and other stressors. At Bor State Hospital, the psychosocial counselling will be in junction with the trained mhGAP provisioners: 3 counsellors and 2 clinical officers. The Counsellors at Bor State Hospital will operate the tollfree MHPSS hotline offering counselling services to expand the reach for MHPSS services to remote areas of the county, and support in PSS assistance in COVID-19 social distancing restrictions. Counsellors will also work in the Isolation Ward in all 3 counties to support COVID-19 patients in isolation as well as their families with coping mechanisms and PSS.

3 - Project indicators

The objective of the project is to increase the capacity of the health sector to offer quality services to the population of Bor South, Duk and Twic East counties, including MHPSS and GBV services and referrals of nutrition cases. The impact of the project corresponds to the Health Cluster’s planned priorities and achievements, with several results related to the Protection Cluster’s strategic objectives and outcomes.Firstly, the project will contribute to reducing excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic-prone diseases and health insecurities by supporting 41 health facilities (of which 3 mobile) in human resources, medical supplies, training, and technical supervision. Disease surveillance and response, including diagnosis and treatment, by this network of health facilities will be monitored through all data submission in the DHIS tool and measured by percentage of alerts raised through national EWARs investigated within 48 hours. The project is also committed to improving access and utilization of integrated life-saving quality health care services to crisis-affected and vulnerable populations through static, mobile and community-based health service delivery systems in Bor South, Duk, and Twic East counties. To measure the quality and life-saving aspect of the health care services, all 41 health facilities will receive regular supervision visits, documented by supervision checklists and the health facilities’ supervision records. To measure the integration and vulnerability aspect, MHPSS/GBV counsellors will be providing psychosocial support services in 11 health facilities while keeping records on the different diagnoses and the number of consultations carried out. To measure the access, utilization and life-saving aspects in child health services, the coverage of PENTA3 in under-one-year-olds will be recorded through the EPI teams’ records. And to enhance the AAP and measure the performance of the intervention, at least 33 out of the 41 health facilities will have a community feedback mechanism in place. Through complaint boxes, satisfaction surveys, and stakeholder meetings the feedback will be assembled, analyzed and adopted. MdM also uses this project to increase access to services for survivors of GBV, disabled, mental health disorders and the elderly. Health care staff will receive refresher training sessions on the CMR as well as Psychological First-Aid. The MHPSS/GBV community facilitators and counsellors will be trained on the referral pathways and inter-sectoral SOPs to strengthen the referrals from community and PHCU level to PHCC and hospital level where CMR is available. The success of the capacity building on CMR and integrated MHPSS among health care personnel will be measured by recording the number of GBV survivors receiving CMR services. While following the strategic objectives of the Health cluster, the project is also committed to achieve better critical protection related assistance and specialized services through the integrated approach of the project to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas. This is to be achieved through training a team of counsellors and community facilitators in offering (remote) one-on-one and group-based GBV and MHPSS services and referrals to other (protection) partners. To measure the reach of the specialized services, the number of MHPSS consultations will be recorded and reported. Additionally, MdM will make prevention and mitigation of (GBV) protection risks a priority through enhanced preparedness and resilience activities. The reach of community-based sensitization activities will be measured by recording the number of persons reached through the awareness sessions conducted by the MHPSS/GBV community facilitators on topics such as stress management, suicide prevention, hopelessness, and coping mechanisms for STIs, GBV and COVID-19 affected people.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

MdM has already trained focal points at health facilities, Womens Champions, and Boma Health Initiative workers on the AAP procedures in health facilities currently supported. Regarding PSEA, all allegations and subsequent follow-up will be documented in a secure and confidential database to ensure accountability. The report will be officially acknowledged within 24 hours, and a protection team will convene a case conference to assess immediate risks and next steps within 72 hours.An immediate investigation team will be assigned by the higher position in the organization internally or externally based on the level of the incidence. The PSEA responsible will provide assistance in communicating with the survivor, will ensure the follow-up and informing of any update and will provide further assistance if needed. The first feedback should be delivered to the survivor within the first-two weeks following the first communication with the survivor informing about the receipt of the case. The full investigation period should not exceed more than 4-5 weeks. The PSEA responsible will provide support in coordination and contacting the personnel. Upon the conclusion of the investigation report, the Human Resources department will decide on the appropriate course of action. This will be without prejudice to criminal proceedings at the national level. This procedure is documented in detail in MdM’s PSEA protocol on which all staff have been trained in 2020. A refresher session in scheduled for April 2021.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

MdM has carried out several need assessments as well as regular meetings and participation in key clusters with different stakeholders, community leaders and local and international organizations. In addition, MdM works in collaboration with local partners such as SMC (Sudan Medical Care), with an extensive knowledge on community sensitization, giving us the overview and tools to adapt the interventions to the specific needs of the targeted population, particularly those from the most vulnerable members. Therefore, the messages will be adapted to the socio-cultural context, as well as to the ages and genders of the right holders. The language and means of transmission of these will also be adapted and culturally appropriate. For example, counsellors and Community Facilitators will be trained on recording oral feedback so those who are most vulnerable, cannot access the health facilities, or are unable to write have an opportunity to give confidential feedback.All participation and consultation tools will be age and disability inclusive and will have a gender and anthropological approach.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

MdM commits to the inclusion of the targeted communities into the project decision making and embed community engagement, public communication, and management of project information into its projects. MdM incorporates Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) in its interventions through communication, transparency, learning, and adaptation. During the first months of the project, the M&E Officer will collect data from the affected population on their preferred way to receive information, give feedback or complaints and the channels will be adjusted as necessary. Throughout the project, beneficiaries and other stakeholders will receive information about the project activities. This will be channeled through different ways including verbal communications and others based on beneficiary preferences. A Complaints Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) ensuring confidentiality and accessible to all beneficiaries, community members and stakeholders will be implemented through: - Satisfaction Surveys will be conducted throughout to measure quality of services, giving patients opportunity to share experiences, criticism, and highlight strengths & weakness of staff & resources.- Comment boxes will be installed in supported health facilities for patients’ recommendations and share experiences. Only MdM staff will have access and responses will be collected and reported on weekly. - IEC material validation workshops with relevant and key members. - Satisfaction surveys after awareness raising sessions and training sessions.- Appointment of AAP focal persons in each health facility and each community who receive training on how to receive and report complaints in a confidential manner. The use of plural communication channels increases the accessibility and diversity in responses, thus allowing for an improved monitoring of project performance and effectiveness.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback from beneficiaries and staff is essential to ensure accountable, safe, inclusive, and meaningful service provision. Feedback from beneficiaries will be recorded, analyzed, and presented in a systematic way. Emphasis will be placed on closing the feedback loop, meaning that the person who raised a complaint will always receive feedback on their case and that as far as possible, beneficiary data will be used to inform programmatic decision-making. Every three months the M&E Responsible will produce an accountability report and a workshop will take place to discuss main findings and decide on action points.Moreover, MdM’s CFRM ensures follow-up of the feedback/complaints from the entry points through: - Complaints or Feedback receipt and categorization: Complaints or feedback are sent to coordination committee that categorizes the information into 1. Feedback, 2. MdM complaint, 3. External complaint, 4. Complaint out of the scope of the mechanism- Complaints and Feedback handling category: 1. Feedback/suggestions are shared in a confidential manner with team leaders and supervisors as input for programs, 2. MdM coordination committee decides how to assess and address the complaint about quality of services or staff conduct; Country Director is involved in serious complaints, 3. Complaints about other services are shared with the relevant organizations as appropriate; Country Director is involved in serious complaints, 4. Complaints out of the scope of the mechanism do not require follow-up- Response and organizational learning: Complainants should receive a response, preferably within 1 month; Complaints and actions taken are recorded in an internal MdM recording keeping tool; Monthly reporting of key complaints and feedback is sent to coordination. All collected feedback will be reviewed by the MdM team and, if appropriate, partner organizations. Then appropriate changes, communications to health staff, policies or process will be done, with information shared to the community through one of the already established communication channels.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Special efforts will be undertaken to address the protection risks associated with the project. The affected population will be in the program design through an assessment and participatory discussions that include questions on their basic needs and preferred ways to address them, access to channels of information, as well as protection risks associated. A beneficiary feedback mechanism emphasizing on two-way communication will be in place. The priority will be to offer a variety of channels to raise feedback or complaints, so that affected populations with different needs will be able to access and use the mechanism. Beneficiaries will be consulted on their preferred mechanisms to give feedback, ensuring that people with different vulnerabilities and characteristics are part of this assessment. Furthermore, to ensure staff neutrality, the mechanism will be handled by the M&E team, who will receive on-the-job training, including training on protection, to ensure that feedback and complaints, especially any complaint on any case violence and PSEA will be handled in an appropriate, confidential and time efficient manner. At the same time, clear SOPs and referral pathways will be developed, including handling critical or sensitive cases that will be registered on a separate database with limited access and will be handled within 24 hours. Other practical measures will be taken in the activity design to minimize risk of exposure to violence as a result of distance to medical services. Most notably, mobile clinics to remote areas will be prioritized as a modality that reduces the need for women to travel to reach high quality maternal and child health, SRH and SGBV/CMR services. The health workers running the mobile clinics will receive training on SGBV and mental health topics to better address protection needs that are identified in the rural communities.Regarding data protection, MdM counts on a reference guide on the management of data related to health and life stories of persons in the field, “For Ethics in the Field”, (https://www.medecinsdumonde.org/en/actualites/publications/2012/10/08/ethics-field), which details several tools and guidelines based on Humanitarian and Do No Harm Principles. Program users must understand what we are going to do (consultation, examination, treatment, interview, film, photographs...) and must give their full and free consent. Specific guidance is provided in case of child mistreatment, acts of torture or cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatments and victims of any kind of violence. MdM has the duty to inform, to protect and to attest following a request from a victim of violence.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

MdM will intervene to strengthen the national health care system both at primary and secondary level, with a specific focus on the integration of MHPSS and SGBV components. The objective is to ensure the coverage of humanitarian needs in health and protection of the most vulnerable populations in the area and respond to any emergencies and critical events. Access to and quality of primary/secondary health care services, including SRH services, MHPSS, PFA and referrals will be reinforced both in static health facilities and at community level. MdM will organize training of health workers on SGBV prevention and management including PFA to reinforce understanding, ensure appropriate response and support as well as enhance linkage between MHPSS and clinical management of rape in supported PHCCs/PHCUs and BSH. Refresher trainings will also be provided for staff of BSH. Community mobilization will be done with the support of community structures, existing women’s groups, community health workers (CHW) and community volunteers using participatory processes to engage women and girls. MdM maintains a community-based component for prevention of SGBV through trained Community Facilitators that perform awareness raising sessions and referral of identified persons in need of assistance (medical, legal, MHPSS, other protection). The Community Facilitators are trained on specific SGBV and protection topics to build their capacity in knowledge, communication methods, relevant actors/stakeholders operating in the implementation area, referral pathways, confidentiality, and reporting. Mobile Medical Units (MMUs) will be operational in partnership with Sudan Medical Care (SMC) for the provision of health services for vulnerable populations in isolated areas with special focus on emergency consultation services, disease surveillance and response, SRH, nutrition, MHPSS and SGBV services, and PFA. During previous MMU activities and projects, the teams have been trained on the basic principles of the gender approach. A refresher and follow-up training will be held to strengthen the access to health care, SGBV and MHPSS services for girls and women. To mainstream MHPSS services in the MMUs, a MHPSS/SGBV counsellor will be included in the MMU staff and all health care staff will be trained in PFA. Awareness on health and psychosocial well-being, as well as COVID-19, will be raised both at the health facilities and the communities. To enhance community awareness, MdM will support elaboration and dissemination of IEC materials, appropriate and respectful to cultural and religious beliefs. Protection, prevention, and comprehensive response mechanisms to SGBV will be mainstreamed through all supported health facilities as well as at community level, to increase awareness and to ensure the provision of inclusive, accessible, quality and coordinated health and social protection services.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

Equitable and impartial access to health services for every person is a core value of MdM, regardless of race, religion, ethnicity, or gender. Need for health services is the only criteria required to access free-of-charge services in the supported facilities, whether under direct implementation or through partners’ support. MdM recognizes the specific needs of certain groups who might have special limited access to health and other basic services. In line with South Sudan HNO 2021 and based on MdM previous assessments, particularly vulnerable population constituting our main target in the intervention area are identified as: women of reproductive age, pregnant and lactating women, at-risk pregnancies, children under 5, and people with particular needs and vulnerabilities across physical and mental wellbeing, living standards and coping mechanisms such us persons with disability and older persons, displaced people, returnees from internal displacement, spontaneous refugee returnees, and host community members. Contact with the affected populations is through existing health community structures in place. Through activities in the project, local connections will be reinforced to better address the needs of the population targeted. A key element to ensure equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations is to have a good coordination between organizations implementing in the same intervention areas. MdM is active in the national and Jonglei state Health Cluster, Protection Cluster, GBV sub-Cluster, and MHPSS Technical Working Group. Additionally, in Bor MdM liaises closely with the MoH, Ministry of Gender, County Health Department (CHD) and local government to implement activities, discuss issues, and share information. In the field, MdM shares communication with organizations that are not direct partners as well. Coordination has been ongoing with Save the Children (SCI) for joint supervision to sites that share both MdM health projects and SCI nutrition programming, as well as for strengthening referral pathways for nutrition-health activities. Additionally, the MdM MHPSS program plans to work together in promotion of Gender and SGBV prevention and awareness in joint training and awareness sessions for staff from organizations working in the same implementation areas. Other actors working in Bor South have been essential to MdM’s work and response in the flooding, including ACTED and Jonglei State Women’s Association. Contacts at both national and international organizations have been critical to the implementation and understanding of the context. Our close partnership with SMC has enriched the health services offered in those facilities due to their close communication and understanding of the communities in which they work. A cross-cutting element of the proposed action is to enhance capacity and empowerment of health staff and local partners and to ensure quality of care according to the WHO quality standards, South Sudan National Health Policy and the IASC Guidelines on MHPSS. This action considers Do No Harm, identifying, addressing protection concerns and needs, mainstreamed in all activities. This will include training of relevant staff to identify cases susceptible of protection and non-medical referrals, based on regularly updated service mappings and coordination with other service providers.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

As a health actor, MdM integrates nutrition screening and awareness in its service delivery. Health care staff will be sensitized on diagnosis and treatment of malnutrition and all personnel responsible for triage will be trained on MUAC. For referral to the nutrition partner in the areas where MdM works, MOUs have been signed to streamline collaboration and coordination. For example, these collaborative agreements organise that the MMUs that MdM is deploying to hard-to-reach areas are joined by a nutrition team to offer integrated services. In Bor South, MdM has an MOU with Save The Children. In Twic East, MdM works closely together with Tearfund. And in Duk county, MdM coordinates health and nutrition outreach activities with JDF. MHPSS services are also available for caretakers and families of malnourished children to assure that psychosocial support is offered when and where needed.
