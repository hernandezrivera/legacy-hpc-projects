Project Summary

In 2021 HEKS/EPER has been the camp focal point in camps 14, 15 and 16.
Proposed project is amid to continue the shelter support to the Rohingya community to make their shelter safe during monsoon and ensure dignity of them. However, HEKS/EPER will only work in the camps 15 and 16 in 2022 and will target 15331 HHs from those camps.
TSA 3 is planned for 60% (9199) of HHs including support with labor and porter assistance for most vulnerable households (100% of EVI to receive assistance).
08 Community building will be Repaired and maintained.
DRR training will be provided to at least 9199 individuals.
HH level site improvement will address 3680 HHs (including accessibility for people with disabilities)
Treated bamboo will be provided under TSA 3 or emergency activities.
6132 HHs will received NFI through vouchers modality, through in-kind distribution (40% of population) including support to EVI with porter assistance.
Provision of emergency shelter support -RDV and distribution of shelter material (including EVI support with porters and labour) -(assumption for target: 20% of the population).
Provision of TDK, awareness, and technical support (Target HHs 100%)
Provision of emergency NFIs according to RDV (including support to EVI with pottering) assumption for target: 15% of the population.
Provision of Catchment/Camp level FP (Target 02 camps-FPs) for camp level coordination and to address referrals (100% referrals should be addressed).
Provision of shelter activities involving women, older people, people with disabilities and other forms of gender diverse population (estimated target 3% of cfw)
The cost of all activities only includes operational costs because materials will be coming from IOM through common pipeline.
Beneficiaries will have the opportunity to choose shelter and nonfood items through voucher approach according to their need.
Regular shelter maintenance and upgradation support will be provided following the sector approved approach.
Capacity building initiative will be included in this project. HEKS/EPER staff will receive technical training and beneficiaries will receive training focused on the DRR aspects and the key features of the shelter.
HHs with special needs will receive porter and construction labor support according to their needs.
In all steps of the proposed project HEKS/EPER will ensure the safety and security of PwSN, older people and PLW. Separate sitting arrangement, separate rest room, breast feeding corner will be ensured at distribution point level. As COVID-19 measures need to be taken into account - according to RRRC/DGHS protocols - HEKS/EPER will ensure security measures in all shelter activities and PSEA measures will be taken.
HEKS/EPER will follow S/NFI Sector guidance and will ensure appropriate coordination with other partners who are working in the same area.
A mixed approach will be used to ensure CFRM. Real-time feedback will be collected by the volunteers during household visits. During distributions one help desk will be installed to support beneficiaries. PDM will allow beneficiaries to raise their complaints. Activities will be monitored by M&E team. Findings will be used to improve the quality of activities.

1 - Identified Needs

TSA 2 was implemented in the targeted camps from early 2021. More than one year have past and those households didn’t receive any shelter materials for improving the shelter structure. According to the last JMSNA (October 17,2021), need of shelter materials and NFI is the 1st priority out of seven. 74 % of households reporting top 3 priority needs for 2022 (in comparison with 2019 and 2020 J-MSNA results on top 3 priority needs for 2020 and 2021). HHs need shelter materials for repair and maintenance of their shelter.For shelter 66% HHs reported roof leaks during rain, 23% have limited ventilation, 13% lack of insulation from cold, 5% have severe structural damage. 59% HH reported top three priority as household/cooking, Mosquito net and blankets there is also need of solar fans, batteries, bedding items, mattress/sleeping mats, and steel truck. The JMSNA report indicates an ongoing need for kitchen items, clothing, bedding and lighting for HHs. And HHs with special needs need specials care during materials delivery and implementation.The Focal Point system is intended to improve emergency coordination and response including RDV. CFP will support the CiC/SMS agency with the development of emergency response plans for moderate and major events. CFP will also have a role during extreme events specifically at cyclone, in accordance with the 72h Cyclone Plan. CFP will refer protection and gender, the monsoon response will be coordinated at camp level by FP/CFP.
