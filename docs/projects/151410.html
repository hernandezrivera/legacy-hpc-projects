Project Summary

Although Gu rains which was average generally in south central of Somalia has improved pasture and water availability, and normal livestock migration patterns have resumed across most of the country, the Post Gu 2018 assessment of the FSNAU 2018 indicated that 369,000 people are acutely food insecure in Bay region which includes in Burhakaba and Dinsor districts.
The dire humanitarian situation affecting the agro-pastoral community in Bay region is best captured by FSNAU Post Gu 2018 Technical Report, which shows that 261,000 people stressed, 101,000 people in crisis and 7000 people in urgently need of assistance. Nutrition situation deteriorated from Serious to Critical mainly due to reduced households’ access to food and income in those affected by the food insecurity.
This project is going to stabilize food security and nutrition situation of the target affected communities through the provision of seasonal appropriate and certified quality seeds and farm tools in order agro-pastoral farmers cultivate their farms in Gu 2019 and produce enough food for household consumption as well as for sale in local markets. Provision of sustainable animal health services through training and equipping of community based animal health workers (CBAHW) and animal health sensitisation, vaccination and treatment. As shown by its objectives the project will address food security and nutrition problems facing the target agro-pastoral households.
The project is going to distribute 40 Metric Tons of Cowpea seeds to 4000 agro-pastoral farmer households and 60MT of white sorghum to 5000 agro-pastoral farmer households in Burhakaba and Dinsor at the rate of 10 Kgs and 12 kg per household respectively. In Addition the project will sensitise 50,000 pastoralists on livestock health and better livestock rearing practices, vaccinate and treat 150,000 heads of animals and train and equip 50 CBAHWs. It is also anticipated that after harvest the crop production will increase and animal health and productivity will improve; thus improving the food security of the poor target households and the local community at large. Also fodder from the leaves of the Cowpea and sorghum will be used to feed livestock; thus improving the body condition, animal productivity and marketability of livestock.

Needs

Although Gu rains which was average generally in south central of Somalia has improved pasture and water availability, and normal livestock migration patterns have resumed across most of the country, the Post Gu 2018 assessment of the FSNAU 2018 indicated that 369,000 people are acutely food insecure in Bay region which includes in Burhakaba and Dinsor districts.The dire humanitarian situation affecting the agro-pastoral community in the Cowpea Belt is best captured by FSNAU Post Gu 2018, Technical Report, which shows that 261,000 people stressed, 101,000 people in crisis and 7000 people in urgently need of assistance. Nutrition situation deteriorated from Serious to Critical mainly due to reduced households’ access to food and income in those affected by the food insecurity.In recent meetings and discussions we had with the communities in the two districts identified opportunities and threats. The community representatives emphasized opportunities of livestock rearing, hides and skin business and crop productions in the target areas. However, those opportunities are under continuous threat of evershortening cycle of drought, lack of security due to the long lasting conflict in the country and economic shock due to loss of crops and livestock and infestation of human and animal diseases.The beneficiaries of the project will be inclusive for the both genders and ages. Especial attention will be given to female and older farmers for the seed distributions, similarly especial qouta will be assigned for women for the CBAHWs. in animal vaccination and sensitization women in all ages will be prioritized.

Activities

To address those problems the project is going to distribute 40 Metric Tons (20MT each district) of Cowpea seeds to 4000 agro-pastoral farmer households (2000 FHHs in each district) and 60 MT of white sorghum (30MT for each District) to 5000 agro-pastoral farmer households (2500 FHHs in each district) in Burhakaba and Dinsor at the rate of 10 Kgs and 12 kg per household respectively. The project will conduct sensitization of 50,000 pastoralists (25000 in each district) on livestock health and better livestock rearing practices, vaccinate and treat 150,000 heads of animals(75000 in each district) and train and equip 50 CBAHWs (25 in each district).

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

- Drought and conflict-based displacements: Somalia continues to be characterized by a multi-faceted armed conflict. It is also compounded by the persistent drought, governance challenges, weakened resilience and economic vulnerability of families and communities. We assume that the current situation interms of security and accessibility to be the same or improve, so the project activities to be implemented smoothly and without interruptions. Similarly, we assume that the Gu and Deyr rains in 2019 will be above average, so there will not more displacements. - Insecurity: The situation in Somalia in terms of security is improving, though incidents can happen every time and everywhere. There are military operations in different parts in the country involved by government forces and rebellions. Although the specific areas are in full control of the South Western State and the federal government, if the power balance shifts in favour of the militias – which is unlikely - it can affect the project areas. However, ADT will monitor the situation very closely and will update Food Security Cluster and donors if any change that can affect the project implementation immediately. In addition, contingency plans for relocation will be in place in case of necessary.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Women are always at the back of the queue in Somali community when it comes to participation activities such projects. ADT will assign gender officers for the project to make sure that gender and age based exclusions are minimized. The gender officers will work with women groups and activists in the areas to avoid gender and age exclusions.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

based on ADT Do not Harm policy, the safety of the beneficiaries is the first priority in all our interventions. We will work with community parts such us youth, women, elders, imams, and local authorities to make sure that safety of the beneficiaries is compromised. Before conducting any activity security situation assessment and updates will be conducted with the youth, elders, women, imams and local authorities and mitigation measures will be put in place in case something happens. We will work with local authorities and police to mitigate any armed conflict that my happen.
