Project Summary

The project is designed to contribute to a reduction in WASH-related public health risks through improved access for 366,693 people living in 14 UNHCR managed camps and 76,835 affected host communities.
The WASH response will be based on three sector strategic objectives to; 1) Ensure regular, sufficient, and equitable access to safe water for drinking and domestic needs, 2) Ensure adequate and appropriate sanitation facilities allowing safe management and disposal of excreta and solid waste and 3) Ensure the change of potentially health-compromising behaviours through participatory hygiene promotion and distribution of hygiene items, with particular focus on contagious diseases
In 2023, UNHCR and partners will ensure continuity of essential WASH services through a combination of service provision activities involving the operation and maintenance of existing 54 water supply networks and the development of a large-scale, unified urban-based water systems.
In addition, UNHCR will continue to support the operation and maintenance of sanitation facilities develop urban scale sustainable sanitation infrastructure. UNHCR will seek approval for integrated household latrines and bathing areas to be adopted as the standard in all new transitional shelter designs covering 3,500 households. Shared household latrine coverage and bathing area, especially for women and children, will be increased and improve coverage rates to UNHCR standards. Faecal sludge treatment capacity will be increased with a mix of large off-site units and increased use of small-bore sewerage and appropriate cost-effective on-site disposal. UNHCR will ensure more effective solid waste management systems and infrastructure and further enhance hygiene promotion activities with community engagement and reduce partner support in favour of refugee ownership.
A particular focus will be given to emergency preparedness and response to disease outbreaks (Acute Watery Diarrhea/Cholera) as well as climate induced disasters such as monsoon and cyclone. UNHCR and partners will explore innovative approaches for community mobilization and engagement for hygiene behaviours. Other priority interventions will include water quality monitoring, provision of adequate WASH services in learning centers, health and nutrition facilities, child friendly spaces and other public spaces within the camps. In addition, UNHCR will advocate for more durable solutions to be considered and funded for the refugees and hosting communities. In all cases, WASH response will consider gender, environmental, age and disability inclusion and will ensure equal access for persons with special needs

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Despite significant efforts made since 2022 by UNHCR and sector partners, water and sanitation access in the camps sites does not yet reach the required standards. The main source of domestic water for Rohingya refugees varies between the two main camp areas: Kutupalong and Teknaf. For the camps in Teknaf, the main water source is surface water collected in small reservoirs, treated, and distributed through a network with tap-stands. Seasonal water stress / shortage remains a serious issue in Teknaf and has a gendered impact on surrounding host communities in Ukhiya. The Teknaf water situation requires large investments in water infrastructures. In Kutupalong, the main water source is groundwater accessed by hand pumps and by a system of solar-powered chlorinated water networks developed throughout 2019-2021. A total of approximately 3,522 functional tube wells exist in the 10 UNHCR managed camps in Kutupalong serving a population of 253,500 people, resulting in 98 persons per pump—significantly below the UNHCR standard of 250 persons/pump. UNHCR’s Knowledge and Practice (KAP) survey carried out in June 2022 indicates that all camps receive above the post-emergency levels of 20 l/c/d. Four years following their construction, the solar-powered chlorinated water networks in Ukhiya need to be upgraded and optimized to ensure the sustainability of their operation and maintenance. To date, sanitation coverage remains an issue within the camps. UNHCR’s Knowledge and Practice (KAP) survey carried out in June 2022 indicates high rates of open defecation for children aged under five years. The WASH needs of People with Disabilities have been only partially addressed in 2022 and gaps remain. Communal or shared -households latrine usage averages 20 individuals/latrine in the 14 UNHCR managed camps (ranging from 13 in Camp 4X to 39 in Camp 2W), just at the post-emergency target of 20. Functional bathing area usage averages 31 individuals/facility, well above the post-emergency target of 20. Lack of full coverage places particular strain on women and girls, who report being unable to use bathing spaces and latrines and leading to an increase in construction of ad hoc bathing areas and latrines inside shelters, causing contamination of surface drainage with faecal coliform. The large and increasing stock of latrines requires frequent desludging, posing health risks and increased costs, as sludge must be pumped, transported, and treated. Despite major developments in 2022, treatment capacity remains below necessary rates and must be improved. The management of the solid waste management (SWM) chain needs to be rationalized and improved and will require stronger community engagement, infrastructure development and coordination between contributing sectors and authorities. Seasonal and localized acute watery diarrhoea (AWD) and other WASH related disease outbreaks remain a reality and causal factors need to be mitigated. Testing of greywater shows heavy contamination with faecal coliform, often running into the open environment. The high refugee population density and lack of sufficient land are contributing factors to the above, resulting in a lack of space for the construction of facilities/access for desludging, as well as challenging environmental conditions in hilly terrain and flood/ landslide-prone areas. Moving forward in 2023, the WASH response will require extending the interventions with more durable solutions that increase the coverage for both refugees as well as for host communities. Some WASH facilities built as part of the emergency response and are not compliant with WASH sector standards need to be decommissioned and replaced. All refugees in the camps require sustained WASH service provision, including sufficient quantity and quality water, desludging of wastewater, solid waste management, access to WASH/NFI and hygiene behavior change and operation and maintenance of WASH facilities.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

As part of the programme, UNHCR conducts regular needs assessments to identify assistance required and prepare for providing support considering AGD and prioritization of persons with specific needs. It also ensures regular risk assessments and adjusts the implementation as deemed feasible to obtain the desired outcome of the programme. UNHCR has a precise idea of who is doing what in the 14 managed camps and will continue to strengthen Inter sectoral camp level coordination mechanisms, through camp focal points and continued engagement of camps-in-charge (CiCs) and other government authorities, coordination at inter-agency and inter sectoral fora. With information sharing, mapping of partners roles and responsibilities within among organizations operating in the camps, camp level coordination the overall response will be more predictable with increased accountability helping to rationalize and provide better services to refugees and host communities. Projects will be implemented in complementarity with other operational partners working in the same areas and will ensure than no duplication and overlaps will occur. The UNHCR WASH programme will continue to explore more innovative and integrated multi sectoral approach with other UNHCR units such as Protection, Education, Child Protection AoR, Gender-Based Violence (GBV) AoR, Health, Nutrition, Shelter/NFI, Energy and Environment and SMSD. UNHCR is considering rationalizing its partnership agreements for the Shelter and WASH partners aligning with the implementation of both programmes in the same camps. This will contribute to effective coordination at the camp level, efficient and integrated service delivery as well as strengthening strategic relationships with different stakeholders including the CiCs and refugees been developed over the course of time. Through this integrated approach, there is joint planning and partners review, field supportive supervision through existing multi-functional teams.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Protection mainstreaming will be at the core of this intervention ensuring that the proposed response contributes to saving lives, ensuring safety and security, alleviating suffering providing dignity for women, girls, boys and men, particularly marginalized groups such as older people, people living with disabilities, and minority groups. UNHCR and its partners will use gender-sensitive quantitative data analysis to track beneficiaries’ satisfaction, participation, acceptance, knowledge and practices. UNHCR will ensure maximum protection mainstreaming in the WASH response by prioritizing the most vulnerable groups affected by protection issues. As in previous years, WASH and partner staff will complete the annual prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) full day training by the UNHCR protection team. A PSEA assessment has already been conducted for all UNHCR partners with satisfactory results.

3.2 - Gender

The provision of safe and dignified WASH services for differentiated needs of refugees is one of the most fundamental principles of protection and gender-based violence (GBV) risk mitigation. Assessment of the needs of the various communities will focus on gendered needs to determine their specific requirements for WASH facilities. Focus on persons with special needs and risks associated with gender, age and diversity will be assessed through participative and inclusive risk mapping focused on the provision of basic services, as well as safety audits. The project will also include a Menstrual Hygiene Management improvement component for adolescent girls. UNHCR will ensure ongoing information sharing between WASH and GBV teams in the targeted areas, and ensure appropriate training of the WASH team on gender and age equality and GBV mainstreaming.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

Children have the same right to clean water, sanitation and hygiene as everyone else. At the same time, UNHCR recognizes that children may have specific needs. Improperly located and poorly maintained WASH facilities such as latrines and water points, especially in crowded refugee camps, may expose children to risks of sexual exploitation and abuse. During repair and maintenance activities, appropriate health and safety measures will be taken to ensure children are safe by ensuring that children are not engaged in labor and porter activities. The project will also consider youth engagement and training to support WASH facilities maintenance activities within their communities. Specifically, elderly person will receive WASH support- mobilizing hhs to support repair, maintenance and construction works including the collection of WASH NFIs from distribution points.

3.4 - Diversity

UNHCR's service delivery is based on a diversity analysis to ensure that all persons of concern have equitable access to the support required. To improve participation and ownership of critical infrastructure and other community assets, UNHCR will continue working with refugees including all ages, genders, and diversity (AGD), LGBTQ+, adolescent and Partners to implement community-led projects under care and maintenance where communities identify and implement projects and are part of the implementation through Cash for Work (CFW) with material support from UNHCR. In addition, UNHCR plans to expand community maintenance to enable communities to maintain WASH facilities as well.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Systematic and meaningful engagement of the affected population in the design of the projects are key steps in ensuring successful programmes and achieving quality output and outcome results. UNHCR will continue to promote compliance with AAP commitments on participation and partnership and information, feedback and action. Community consultations will be conducted during the planning, implementation and post-project phases through house visits, Focus group discussion (FGD) and key informants’ interview (KII). Trainings will be provided to beneficiaries for all programmes to explain the programme so that eligibility and process are clarified, and beneficiaries are trained to utilize NFIs provided properly. HH level post distribution monitoring (PDM) will be conducted. Through Common Feedback and Response Mechanism, the project will promote information sharing and community feedback mechanism among the community

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The strategy for DRR/DRM and climate resilient WASH services is a topic that UNHCR will discuss in more depth in the coming months with the WASH Sector, and WASH facilities will be designed and constructed in consideration of existing climatic and environmental risks and vulnerabilities. The following measures continue to support the objective of ensuring safe and sustainable WASH services: preliminary topographic studies prevent locating WASH infrastructures in heavily flooded areas; water points have a concrete apron to limit erosion, stagnating water and destruction by floods; water supply and sanitation pipes are buried deep enough to limit erosion due to water runoff; productive boreholes are subject to a long duration pumping test to ensure that the aquifer tapped not dries up during the drought period; the system is designed to use the aquifer sustainably; pump stations are solarized to increase profitability and reduce vulnerability to fuel price variation and carbon print
