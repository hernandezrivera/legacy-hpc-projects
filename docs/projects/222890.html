Project Summary

In 2025, UNICEF will scale up inclusive WASH service coverage from eight camps to 15 camps, reaching nearly 50 per cent of the Rohingya refugees. This project will strategically focus on improving the quality, efficiency and functional WASH infrastructure and services and support the implementation of WASH sector Water and FSM strategies as well as approved standards/designs including harmonized WASH NFIs. The project will continue to support improving water networks to meet the water needs of the refugees and reduce drudgery of water collection for children and women. New water networks and tap stands will be constructed to cover gaps. Facilities will be assessed for climate proofing. The project will support operations, maintenance and improvement of existing latrine and bathing spaces to ensure functionality, quality, safety, and privacy and address the needs of women, girls, Persons with Disabilities (PwD), older adults and other vulnerable people in the camps.
Improving the efficiency and quality of Faecal sludge management facilities and solid waste management will be supported. New latrines, solid waste, and Fecal Sludge Management (FSM) sites will be constructed to fill critical gaps, while efforts will be made to improve FSM effluent quality in all camps. In line with the approved FSM strategy, UNICEF will explore the construction of centralised FSTP in few camps to minimize the operational cost. UNICEF will explore technologies for improving desludging and management of sludge especially the accumulated solids. The operation and maintenance of Solid waste management sites will be supported, while the project will strengthen integration with nutrition and livelihood for the use of compost.
The project will prioritize community engagement for promotion of hygiene behaviours and better management of WASH services. UNICEF WASH project will continue to collaborate with other sectors, such as CP/GBV, to address gender-related inequalities in WASH that contribute to violence against girls and women. This approach includes engaging with women and girls directly while also supporting men and boys in efforts to shift attitudes, biases, and behaviors that negatively affect the WASH experience, outcomes, and involvement of women and girls, ultimately reducing their exposure to GBV. UNICEF will be responsible for provision of hygiene items including soap and locally produced MHM kits as per approved WASH sector standard. The project will support new technologies and strategies to improve efficiency. Real time monitoring of WASH services across the camps is key. The project will address environmental issues, gender, age, and disability inclusion, and ensure preparedness and response for natural disasters, fire, and diseases outbreaks.
In host communities, UNICEF will continue to support the paradigm shift to do more in host communities to strengthen Humanitarian Development nexus, address grievances, tensions/conflict, and disparities in WASH services. The project will support sanitation promotion through community approach for total sanitation (CATS) and Sanitation Marketing (SanMark) and informal/ private sector engagement. The project will explore alternative mechanisms particularly surface water treatment in water scarce areas. The promotion of water safety planning and hygiene promotion for improving personal behaviour and environmental health is an integral action in the project. WASH in Institutions (school and health facilities) will be supported to ensure access to adequate WASH services. As part of system strengthening, the project will support government counterparts and local government on evidence generation, WASH planning, coordination, demonstration of good practices, monitoring, reporting and capacity building. UNICEF will support other areas of Teknaf including Saint Martin due to its vulnerability.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

WASH services remain a priority for around one million Rohingya refugees. In 2025, UNICEF will be responsible for providing daily WASH services to half of this population. Despite the humanitarian community’s commitment to providing basic services, significant challenges persist, including limited livelihood opportunities, climate risks, disease outbreaks, malnutrition, and increasing insecurity in the camps. The escalating conflict in Myanmar’s Northern Rakhine State is leading to new arrivals in the camps, further increasing pressure on WASH services. Water security for households remains a challenge. The average domestic water collection rate in many camps is below the WASH sector standard of 20 liters per person per day. Many households still use tubewells (deep/shallow) as their primary drinking water source, which are prone to contamination, while others use private water pipe connections from unimproved sources in adjacent host communities. This indicates a need to improve water access and availability. Public health risks have increased, with 33% and 44% of Rohingya households reporting cases of diarrhoeal diseases (AWD/cholera/dysentery) and skin infections, respectively, in 2024. Additionally, water scarcity remains a challenge in Teknaf camps and host communities. Despite the substantial number of latrines and bathing facilities in the Cox’s Bazar camps, 32% of women and girls do not feel safe using them at night. Around 49% of people with disabilities do not have sufficient access to latrines. As per the 2024 Protection ISNA, 46% respondents felt that there is an increased safety concern for women and girls in the community. For women and girls, safety concerns predominantly revolve around gender-based violence (GBV), including risks of sexual assault, harassment, domestic violence, and exploitation. These threats are often systemic and gender-specific, occurring in public spaces such as toilets and other WASH facilities, pathways, and markets as well as their respective homes. Maintaining the quality and functionality of sanitation facilities, including faecal sludge management systems, remains a significant challenge due to the ageing and semi-permanent nature of the infrastructure. The most critical need is the reconstruction or replacement of dysfunctional and ageing faecal sludge treatment plants to meet acceptable standards, alongside the decommissioning or protection of WASH facilities exposed to disaster risks. Climate risks cause significant damage to WASH facilities in camps and host communities every year. Solid waste management also poses challenges, with approximately 48% of households reporting visible waste in their surroundings. Conflict and tension between the host community and refugees remain a concern due to the strain the Rohingya response has placed on host community resources, such as water and agricultural land. The disparity between the living conditions of refugees and the host community generates significant grievances and tensions. According to the 2023 Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (J-MSNA), 58% of the host community still relies on unimproved water sources for drinking, in stark contrast to the situation in the camps. Host communities also lack proper faecal sludge and solid waste management systems, and WASH facilities are continuously impacted by climate-related disasters. UNICEF’s support for WASH in schools and health facilities in Cox’s Bazar District is crucial to ensure a better learning environment for host community children and adequate WASH services in healthcare facilities. However, significant gaps remain in WASH services within schools and health facilities. Menstrual hygiene management facilities are available in only 4% of schools, with 10% absenteeism among girls during menstruation. There is a growing need to support and improve the WASH situation in host communities to reduce grievances and conflict between the host community and refugees.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

In line with protection principles, the project will ensure that services are delivered without barriers and that vulnerable populations are engaged to plan, monitor and evaluate the response. The project will prioritize and ensure the protection, safety, privacy, and dignity, adhering to the principles of “Do No Harm”. The project will ensure measures to prevent and minimize any unintended negative effects that could increase physical and psychosocial risks for affected populations, including children, women, adolescent girls, and persons with disabilities. Empowering and involving affected communities and establishing mechanisms to ensure accountability for addressing concerns and complaints is key for the project. Women and girls will be supported to address gender and protection needs, empowering them to actively participate in decision-making processes for safe and sustainable WASH services. Appropriate measures will be established to allow affected populations to provide their feedback/satisfaction level of the interventions. Community Feedback and Response System will be strengthened, and findings reviewed regularly to ensure concerns and feedback from affected populations are addressed. The project will support ensuring sufficient access to WASH services by persons with disabilities through their engagement and improvement in the sanitation facilities to meet their needs. Gender-specific topics will be incorporated into WASH awareness messaging, as well as in the training and orientation of partner staff and front-line workers. Both partner staff and community members will be oriented on gender-based violence (GBV) in WASH programs to ensure protection issues are appropriately addressed. UNICEF and its partners will work closely with the GBV sub-sector.

2.3 - Age & Disability

UNICEF and partners have been working towards overcoming barriers to easy access to WASH facilities for children, the elderly, and persons with disabilities. It is mandatory for all UNICEF partners to have their Child Safeguarding Policies, and to ensure their staff sign to adhere to the provisions therein. UNICEF and its partners will take measures to reduce barriers and stress on meaningful participation and empowerment towards decision-making processes and accessibility initiatives. The children of all ages and those who are at risk of discrimination are participating in the planning, implementation, and monitoring of the WASH interventions. Partners, contractors, and vendors will be trained to take appropriate actions related to age and inclusion. The project is focused to ensuring inclusive access to WASH services, with a focus on all age individuals, while also integrating child protection measures. The program adopts a twin-track approach to disability inclusion following the guidelines set by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC). Disbility friendly Feedback Mechanism has been established and community engagement interventions are ongoing to ensure the participation of persons with disabilties in feedback provision, decision making and designing programmes. The project also be given emphasis on data disaggregation for establish a system for monitoring.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

UNICEF continues to promote compliance with AAP commitments on participation, partnership, information, feedback, and action. The UNICEF WASH programme has a strong AAP focus through community engagement, where the user is at the center and activities are adapted to maximize outputs of the Project. Community consultations will be prioritized at stages of WASH programme with the active participation of men, women, girls, boys, and persons with disability. UNICEF will ensure that an AAP feedback and response mechanism is established following the AAP principles of participation, inclusion, communication, and transparency. UNICEF and its partners will take measures to reduce barriers so that children of all ages and those who are at risk of discrimination are participating in the planning, implementation and monitoring process while implementing the projects by the partners. UNICEF will support partners to strengthen organizational Child Safeguarding policies and increase staff capacity to take appropriate measures. Moreover, UNICEF will ensure that feedback and complaints are received from communities and addressed. These include Information and Feedback Centers (IFC), complaints/feedback /comments box, focus group discussions and house visits by the CBVs. Feedback analysis and reports, which reflect critical complaints and frequently asked questions raised by Rohingya about unmet needs and/or services delivered by sectors and implementing partners, are shared monthly to ensure effective AAP and it is fundamental pillar of this response.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

UNICEF has a zero-tolerance policy towards prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). UNICEF will continue to support partners to strengthen their institutional PSEA policies, procedures, and its proper application. UNICEF and partners will continue to ensure mandatory PSEA orientation is conducted for all staff, volunteers, consultants, vendors, and contractors. Communication materials have been developed and disseminated to create awareness among the affected population, on SEA including prevention, reporting, and victim assistance. All the partners have an awareness-raising plan to strengthen community-based protection mechanisms. Complaints mechanisms such as the UNICEF and partners-supported Information and Feedback Centre (IFC) mechanisms will be more strengthened, to facilitate confidential reporting and referral of victims for the required and appropriate services. UNICEF has completed a PSEA risk assessment for all the partners and currently, has no partner with high risk. All the partners have PSEA action plans to prevent and mitigate the risk of SEA. It is mandatory for all UNICEF partners to have their Child Protection Safeguarding Policies and to ensure their staff sign to adhere to the provisions therein. From 2024 UNICEF started the effective application of ESS (Environment and Social safeguarding) with high priority. UNICEF will not tolerate its employees, volunteers, consultants, partners, or any other representative associated with the delivery of its work carrying out any form of sexual harassment, sexual exploitation, or sexual abuse. UNICEF commits to supporting survivors, improving safeguarding capacity, reporting, investigating, responding. UNICEF will closely monitor partner organizations' implementation of approved PSEA action plans through field visits and reporting.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes - UNICEF WASH projects in Cox’s Bazar camps and host communities incorporate climate resilience adaptation and mitigation through several key actions including i) ensuring use of renewable energy for operation of water sources, use of surface water treatment and ground water monitoring. (ii) Climate-Resilient Infrastructure: The construction of WASH facilities is designed to withstand climate impacts. For example, latrines and water points are built to be flood-resistant, ensuring they remain functional during heavy rains and floods. (iii) Community Engagement and Education: UNICEF works closely with the community to raise awareness about climate change and its impacts. This includes training on water conservation, hygiene practices, and the importance of maintaining WASH facilities. (iv) Waste Management: Effective waste management systems are implemented to prevent environmental contamination. This includes the safe disposal of waste and the promotion of recycling practices. (v) Implementing ESS to minimize environmental, climatic and social risks. (vi) Monitoring and Evaluation: Continuous monitoring and evaluation help to assess the effectiveness of WASH interventions and make necessary adjustments to improve resilience against climate change. Anticipatory action is an integral component of the WASH programme

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, The WASH project aims to enhance emergency preparedness and response (EPR) capabilities to ensure the continuity and resilience of essential water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) services during crises. UNICEF and its partners have developed emergency preparedness and response, as well as Business Continuity Plan (BCP) for each camp. Each contract and partnership agreement has a contingency budget line for emergence response such as cyclones, landslides, flooding, new arrivals, monsoon impacts, and fires—as well as health emergencies like cholera and dengue outbreaks. WASH supplies are prepositioned in critical areas of the camps. Standard protocols are in place to strengthen the WASH preparedness and response. ERTs and JART teams are available for immediate response. UNICEF responses to emergencies will follow a conflict-sensitivity approach, with contingency planning for new arrivals of Rohingya refugees and catering for new displacements.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

UNICEF Project engaged youth aged between 15 to 24 years not only as beneficiaries of WASH services but also as a key stakeholder in WASH. The WASH services are inclusive for all including youths and adolescents. Adolescent girls are engaged in leading and facilitating menstrual hygiene activities to overcome barriers for adolescent girls in accessing MHM services. Boys are also engaged to ensure taboos and misconceptions about MHM are improved. Youth are engaged to take part in decision-making process for the overall WASH programme improvement. UNICEF believes that youth's contribution towards SDG 6 is crucial and empowering them as WASH champions remains key. Youth are supporting monitoring and improvement of WASH services in communities. They are also represented in WASH committees. Their knowledge and skills will continue to support communities even after the project period as part of sustainability measures.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes - apart from supporting WASH infrastructure, the Project ensures community engagement and refugee participation in the management of WASH facilities, thus enhancing self reliant. Volunteers have been trained and equipped with skills to serve as pump operators and technicians for repair and maintenance of WASH facilities, thus ensuring refugees are self-reliant. The project will ensure access to quality WASH services to reduce WASH related diseases which will enable to the refugees to rebuild their lives rather than delaing with diseases. The project will also contribute to livelihood opportunities through working with livelihood sector for the local production of MHM kits in the camps. This creates economic opportunities for the refugees especially women.
