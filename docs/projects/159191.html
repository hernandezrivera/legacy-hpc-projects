Project Summary

Despite substantial investment in the treatment of acute malnutrition, seasonal FSNAU assessments show that for many years the prevalence of acute malnutrition has remained above serious. The post Gu 2019 nutrition assessment findings showed marginal decrease in the median prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) of 13.8% down from 14% of post Gu 2018 and 17.4% of post Gu 2017. The corresponding median SAM prevalence are: 2.3% (Gu 2019), 2.2% (Gu 2018) and 3.2% (Gu 2017). Current estimates indicate that 1 in 10 children under the age of five is acutely malnourished resulting in an estimate 1 million boys and girls of which 178,000 are projected to be affected by life threatening severe acute malnutrition while 830,000 are moderately malnourished. Further, 832,000 pregnant and lactating women are estimated to need nutrition assistance.
Malnutrition appears worse in rural areas compared to urban areas: across rural areas assessed, the average GAM rate was 17.7%, compared to 12.1% in urban areas (non-displaced households). Similarly, the average SAM rate was 3.0% in rural areas compared to 2.1% in urban areas. IDP households are more likely to suffer from malnutrition than non-displaced populations. The average GAM rate for IDPs is 18.0%, whilst the average SAM rate is 3.5 percent. IDP camps affected by critical GAM are; Galkayo 20% and Doloow 18.6%, Banadir 16 percent. Across regions, GAM and SAM rates varied: lower rates were observed in the relatively stable north-west of the country such as Woqooyi Galbeed, Awdal and Togdheer regions (8.7% GAM / 1.3% SAM;10.7% GAM / 1.9% SAM and 11.8% GAM / 1.9% SAM respectively), with highest rates in Hiraan region (22.9% GAM / 5.0% SAM), followed by Bay (21.5% GAM / 4.2% SAM), Bakool (20.9% GAM / 4.4% SAM) and Gedo regions (20.4% GAM / 3.2% SAM). GAM rates also varied significantly at the district level, with average rates of above 22% in Belet Wayne, Jalalaqsi, Bulo Burto and Baydhaba districts, whilst average rates were less than 10% in Berbera, Gebiley, Xarardheere and Hargeisa districts
UNICEF recognizes that girls, boys, men, women and people with disabilities have different nutritional needs at different life stages with different capacities and risks in accessing nutrition services. Gender inequality exacerbates food insecurity, malnutrition and poverty in humanitarian crises. Although data from SAMRT survey conducted by FSNAU and other partners shows that malnutrition rate is higher in boys than girls, the latter have a higher admission rate than boys for reasons yet to be identified. Overall, children affected by severe acute malnutrition are nine times more at risk of death compared to those who are well-nourished. Therefore, this response plan is aligned to UNICEF’s new global narrative on wasting that seeks to focus both on protecting children from becoming wasted as well as caring for those who need treatment. Key interventions of this HRP will aim to deliver promotive and preventive services (Micronutrient supplementation, deworming, IYCF, PLW) and early detection and referral for treatment of SAM targeting in priority rural and IDP communities. In addition, UNICEF in collaboration with WFP will continue to provide care and treatment to children affected by MAM in hard to reach areas using the simplified protocol. Out of the 178,000 children with SAM, UNICEF will target an estimated 133,500 (68, 085 girls, 65,415 boys) and 43,000 children with moderate acute malnutrition under the combined protocol for the management of acute malnutrition in close consultation with WFP. Through this HRP, UNICEF will procure nutrition supplies to IPs(e.g SHF,SHINE) with 25% buffer. Meanwhile, UNICEF OTP sites will provide a platform for the delivery of integrated multi stakeholder and multi sector services particularly WASH, C4D, Health and safety net. Drawing on the successes and lessons of previous years, UNICEF will continue to lead the cluster and the delivery of this response.

Mitigation Measures

To prevent sexual violence and sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance , UNICEF organized training and capacity building of implementing partners on prevention and response to sexual exploitation. UNICEF continues to stand firm on zero tolerance for (sexual) misconduct. Therefore, all UNICEF implementing partners, are obliged to create and maintain an environment that prevents sexual exploitation and sexual abuse. Managers at all levels have a responsibility to support and develop systems that maintain this environment, are committed to the protection of vulnerable populations in humanitarian crisis, including from sexual exploitation and abuse. By entering into an agreement with UNICEF and implementing partner undertakes to adhere to:(i) the standards set out in the Secretary-General’s Bulletin “Special measures for protection from sexual exploitation and sexual abuse” (ST/SGB/2003/13);(ii) any minimum operating standards adopted because of the Statement of Commitment on Eliminating Sexual Exploitation and Abuse.(iii) any other Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policy or guideline. Accountability to affected population : UNICEF and implementing partner will ensure affected populations to play an active role in the decision-making processes that affect them through the establishment of clear guidelines and practices to engage them appropriately and ensure that the most marginalised and affected are represented and have influence. UNICEF and partners will adhere the principles of "do no Harm" through not creating any partial, nepotism, conflict oriented and sensitive issues within the project effaced population and beneficiaries. women, men, girls and boys will be engaged in decision-making and enhanced their resilience / capacity to prevent and response to severe acute malnutrition.
