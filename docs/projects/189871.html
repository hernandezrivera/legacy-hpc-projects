Project Summary

Through comprehensive CCCM response and services, improve living standards, strengthen accountable life-saving service delivery and provide a path for durable solutions for conflict affected IDPs in sites, site-like settings and in host communities in the Northern provinces of Cabo Delgado, Nampula and Niassa. In response to the ongoing, complex response in the region, CCCM must be scaled up through on-site and mobile approaches, supporting IDPs and partners through service monitoring, community engagement and sustainable site-planning and development to ensure minimum standards are met and displaced populations are living in dignified conditions as the context evolves.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

IOM CCCM plans to target 195,351 people in need through a full CCCM response including site planning and improvement, community engagement, information management, coordination of services, and capacity building. With the ongoing displacement in the northern regions of Mozambique and the evolving context, displaced populations remain in dire needs due to heavy congestion in the temporary sites exposing Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) to further risks. Displaced persons living outside of sites are equally vulnerable as host communities areoverwhelmed and traditional social protection nets are not able to meet the needs of this many displaced people. With its expertise in site planning and development, IOM has led several site assessments across government-identified priority areas to establish service areas with improved living conditions for displaced communities. IOM will continue to expand its capacity to develop sites that meet improved standards while also capacitating local governance structures to ensure a principled response. In 2021, through community engagement and community participation, governance structures in sites and site-like settings have increased in their inclusivity through women and disability inclusion committees engaging with leadership, equipping these structures to support the community and service providers while ensuring protection and social cohesion. Complaint and Feedback mechanisms set up in 2021 in line with protection principles and regular capacity building of governance structures, partners, and government counterparts will be expanded to cover new sites. In hard-to-reach areas or in areas of new displacement, mobile teams will provide coordination support and advocacy for improved services as well as support reception management of new arrivals. IOM CCCM will activate the Urban Displacement Out of Camps (UDOC) approach in specific districts and areas where there are concentrations of IDPs living in the host community.As CCCM Cluster lead agency, IOM will also take on the responsibility of CCCM provider of last resort. IOM will engage in regular CCCM partner and service provider meetings to ensure response consistency across sites and between actors, bringing these meetings to district and site level as appropriate.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

In order to ensure none of the CCCM project activities and modalities contribute to further discrimination of marginalized groups, an assessment will be conducted to determine the dynamics that exist in the community including factors exacerbating marginalization. CCCM team will promote representation of marginalized communities in decision-making and community governance structures (i.e. Community Disabled Committee; Proportional Representation among Block Leaders; bilateral consultation with leaders of newly arrived communities). Through CwC, Camp Management will ensure marginalized groups are reached with all public awareness messages, information on project activities and concerns regarding access to services. With the accessible complaints and feedback mechanism most marginalized groups will be able to address issues of exclusion/lack of information, and lack of representation. Prior to any CCCM intervention in new sites, Camp Management teams will regularly consult with different actors including community leaders, state and local authorities, OCHA and partners regarding camp administration and land usage to ensure that the displaced population will not be forcibly evicted from the site. Where returns are affected by housing, land and property (HLP) issues, Camp Management will link them with HLP actors for assistance. CCCM will participate in inter-agency safety audits to identify gender-based violence (GBV) risks and address related issues. In addition, Camp Management will collaborate with protection and security actors for the development of community based protection measures that enhance the safety of women and girls reducing their exposure to sexual violence including proper site planning, infrastructure, and site care and maintenance interventions to reduce GBV risk (i.e. Solar lighting, elimination of dead-end corridors, better designed public spaces) and engaging with women’s governance structures, representatives, and IDPs directly to identify and address safety concerns. Through the GBV referral pathways Camp Management team will be able to refer GBV cases to GBV actors for assistance.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

IOM will use CCCM Cluster tools to conduct rapid assessments engaging with the target population to ensure that the project is in line with their needs. Through the project implementation, Complaint and Feedback Mechnanism and CBCMs in sites and site-like areas, and the reinforcement of the national Linha Verde contact number, will be used to ensure that feedback is considered as the project progresses. In addition to this, the creation of community committees and strengthening of pre-existing leadership structures will provide an additional means for the affected population to hold IOM accountable. Camp Management will promote the representation and participation of vulnerable members including persons with disabilities, women and girls, older persons and persons with chronic illnesses in community structures and CCCM forums to raise issues of exclusion, stigmatization and access constraints to services. Messages and meetings minutes will be context-specific and tailored for the targeted audience respecting age, gender, and diversity translated and shared via notice boards keeping in mind cultural context and target beneficiaries. The project contains a PSEA training element to ensure that IOM and committees receive PSEA training and are aware of reporting structures, including Linha Verde. SEA information will be provided through posters in public spaces and engagement through the community committees and townhall meetings as required.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Baseline data for the project is disaggregated to show disability. IOM will work within the project to ensure that people living with disabilities are represented in community structures, such as through a Disability Inclusion Committee. The project will use assessments to understand the main barriers to inclusion of PWDs and understand how or support can help to overcome these. In site design, IOM CCCM team account for structural elements to support accessibility such as bridges, ramps and advocate for adaptations in water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) facilities.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Site planning is crucial to reduce the risk of creating a negative impact on the environment. Through thorough assessment of the area prior to implementation, the site planner will be able to adjust for environmental degradation of resources such as water sources and trees. In addition, the interventions will use locally sourced materials wherever possible to reduce the transportation requirements and to ensure that materials are appropriate for the context.Labour based interventions will be prioritized reducing use of fuel and increasing community participation in the sites.Community Engagement throughout the project will ensure community buy-in to environmentally friendly practices through community committees.
