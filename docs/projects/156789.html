Project Summary

The project will address the needs of both IDP and Host Communities in the countryside of Idleb and Western and Northern countryside of Aleppo with an emphasis on continuity of current projects. SRD will provide integrated protection activities with health facilities, prevention and risk mitigation activities through awareness raising sessions, in addition to response activities through case management approach (case disclosure, specialized GBV PSS, case management, referral, case follow up and close cases) and solid referral pathway. Psychosocial Support Services (PSS) will be provided in the currently functioning safe spaces to enhance resilience of survivors and vulnerable women and girls.
SRD will provide protection and GBV services through women and girls safe space in addition to community-based outreach services in rural Idleb (Almaara, Kafr Nobol, and Ehsem commuities) and Northern Aleppo (Albab, Jarablus, and Azaz). While SRD provide integrated GBV and PSS services in currently functioning SRD health facilities located in 10 different communities (Rakaya, Maar Tahroma, Saraqab, Azmarin; Idleb Governorate) and (Hur, Abin, Afrin, Jenderis, Albab, Azaz; Aleppo Governorate). SRD provide center based services and outreach services in the affiliated areas including community based protection and GBV structured awareness sessions through outdoor activities.
Integrating the two and streamlining outreach activities will increase access and reduce barriers for women and girls. Health centers proved to be a safe and confidential access point to access GBV services and this successful model is therefore replicated and continued under this project. SRH services will include a focus on adolescents and family planning (in response to child marriage) raising awareness of relevant services available which include psycho social support, clinical management of rape cases and providing safe spaces for women and girls.
The objectives of this project are aligned with the most crucial protection needs as indicated by the Protection Cluster, as it will increase and ensure continual access to GBV services and alleviate suffering of those requiring critical care in countryside Idleb, Western and Northern countryside Aleppo. The safe spaces will provide quality and integrated GBV services with a focus on community-based approaches, including awareness raising, targeted support to persons with specific protection needs and other community initiatives through community centers and outreach mechanisms.
Qualified and trained psychosocial workers will be present in the safe spaces and PSS rooms to manage the activities and provide private sessions for GBV survivors, referred cases, or other individuals seeking focused specialized PSS services. The work will include instruction on integrated case management, communication skills, community protection practices, behavior change communication, maternal and child health, hygiene promotion, nutrition, GBV survivor support, reporting techniques, cultural sensitivity, and referral pathways. Protection awareness messages will be delivered to beneficiaries through daily household visits, prioritizing messages of child labor, child marriage and GBV. Referrals will be made for those with protection needs or other forms of support to facilities and services providers within the referral pathway to minimize the risks of vulnerability.

Needs

Protection concerns such as trauma, domestic violence, sexual harassment and early marriage permeate lives of especially women and girls (W&Gs) and leaves them exposed to grave psychosocial risks and results in SRH consequences. (HNO 2019). Focus group discussions in Aleppo and Idleb reported an increase in domestic violence because of the war, linked as well to issues of child marriage, with survivors rarely seeking medical services. The number of W&Gs reported GBV incidents increased during 2019 compared to the previous year. More than 50 female GBV survivors were assisted with GBV case management services. Yet, the current crisis increased the risks of GBV, especially to displaced or separated, and to those who are now in crowded displacement settlements without segregated facilities. The needs of ensuring immediate access to clinical care for survivors of sexual violence and provide case management and psychosocial support through skilled service providers and referral services are still huge. In Idleb, women reported on well-functioning safe spaces and mobile teams with psychosocial support. The spaces are for women the only place to spend time besides their home. W&Gs’ access to GBV services is restricted because of cultural and social norms as well as movement restrictions linked to violence and harassment. (Voices HNO 2019). Aligning with the HRP Objectives, the focus of this project aims to reduce protection risks and strengthen adaptation and resilience factors for W&Gs to ensure physical, emotional, and social well-being. Through this comprehensive intervention, the protection of children and women will be prioritized and the effects of IDP status in northern Syria will be mitigated. Sexual harassment was reported to affect girls and women. The threat of sexual harassment was associated with movement in/out camp settings (especially communal bathrooms) and on roads. There were reports of parents keeping girls from school due to threat of sexual harassment.) Whole of Syria GBV Area of Responsibility 2018.SRD conducted Health and Protection Needs Assessment (late 2018) in order to capture the needs. The data was collected through Surveys, Key Informant Interviews (KII), Clinic Assessments and Focus Group Discussions (FGD) with Adolescent girls in different Northern sub-districts. The analysis of information showed that the GBV concerns highlighted by the discussion group were lack of access of those affected married adolescent girls due to movement restrictions. The assessment demonstrated gap in protection awareness at the community level. This point emphasizes the need to amplify community-level interventions regarding GBV promotion through outreach teams. SRD plans to improve the accessibility to services by operating at preexisting integrated facilities, in order to continue providing services, and to improve the quality of the services utilizing the expertise of SRD personnel in managing many integrated protection and health care facilities, as well as to link those health facilities to the concrete referral in Idleb and Aleppo.According to the 2019 HNO, the ongoing or recurrent conflict environment experienced by many boys and girls the past seven years has profoundly affected their psychosocial well-being – weakening their sense of security, purpose, control and significance, or exercising social agency, and causing profound distress.It is estimated that more than 2.6 million children remain displaced and separation from caregivers is a reported child protection issue in all 14 governorates of Syria putting children at risk of exploitation, abuse or neglect. Events leading to family separation indicate how related protection and child protection issues increase vulnerability of affected boys and girls, with death or disappearance of caregivers, disappearance of children, separation due to displacement or conflict, child marriage and child recruitment among cited reasons for family separations.

Implementation of activities

The six WGSSs will contniue offering the following services: Case management and confidential referral to relevant service providers, vocational training and empowerment activities, SRH and GBV awareness sessions for all women and girls (W&Gs), and specific sessions for GBV survivors. Daycare is available for children of the women and adolescent girls attending the activities in the center. In order to remove physical and psychological barriers for W&Gs with disabilities, in coordination with the local council, SRD will offer transportation service to W&Gs focusing on adolescent with disabilities. WGSS facility will be audited for physical access purposes. An additional caseworker will be recruited with specialized services disabilities. Transportation fees and services will cover beneficiaries in need.Consultation with local councils, parents and adolescents will be organized at the WGSS to decide on the modalities of the services, and activities offered, and timetable in order to ensure child safeguarding measures are in place. Coordination with Humanity and inclusion will be established to ensure WGSS staff are properly trained on how to identify adolescents with disability in need of treatment for a timely referral to relevant services.The WGSS with trained Case workers will provide an entry point for women and girls to report GBV incidents (including SEA and child abuse) and access referrals to other safe and non-stigmatizing GBV response services. Case management will have fund to cover needs of vulnerable beneficiaries whom cannot be referred to other services. Fund can be used in-kind material support and cash transfer. Guidance will be shared with Cluster with vulnerability criteria; documentation requirements.The integration of GBV PSS services with reproductive health care proves to be an excellent entry point to initiate care for GBV survivors, given that W&Gs can more discretely access GBV services as part of visits to for SRH needs or other related concerns. GBV caseworkers ensure survivor-centered approaches, safe and confidential reporting and referrals and provide GBV awareness and mitigation measures sessions for community members to reduce barriers to access services. Each of 10 facilities will have private GBV consultation space, private waiting room for women and men and separated bathrooms; necessary maintenance will be completed within first quarter.SRD will increasingly focus on providing emergency response services to be able to adapt quickly in displacement and increasing and different needs. All MC staff will be trained on emergency contingency plan and therefore ready to expand coverage in order to conduct rapid protection assessment to identify risks face by W&Gs, providing life-saving services including distribution of dignity kits, PFA and urgent referral. SRD has strong relationships with local authorities, contributing to the overall sustainability of GBV services.SRD will allocate spaces for children in in Maart An Numan and Ihsem WGSS and a Maart Horma PHC. In these spaces, essential Early Child Development (ECD) services and structured PSS activities using Save the Children’ Child Resilience Program will be available. Only children with emotional distress will be selected for the activities and this will be communicated with the community, outreach teams and schools in the area. SRD will build the capacity of ECD and PSS facilitators to work with vulnerable children and their caregivers. SRD will distribute ECD materials, promoting safe motherhood and ensuring psychosocial well-being of children. The facilitators will also be trained to identify signs of maternal depression in mothers, severe distress and neglect in children neglect in children and they will provide referrals. SRD will also work with the community and other service providers to ensure all vulnerable accessing its centers are provided with appropriate assistance through direct service provision, referral, and advocacy

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Resistance or backlash from the community for a woman only space. Mitigation 1: Community engagement from the onset of project, from assessment phase and throughout all project cycle. It includes meetings with religious and community leadersRisk 2. Facility targeted by airstrikes- especially when PSS services are integrated in clinics in certain areas. Mitigation 2: Safety measures for low visibility and underground centers in some areas, i.e. Aleppo.Risk 3. Livelihood opportunities may increase risks of GBV. Mitigation 3. Consult women and men, boys and girls in the design of the program.Risk 4. Expectations of income may be unrealistic and might create dependencyMitigation 4. Ensure beneficiaries know services where to disclose if violence occursRisk 5. Exploitation by service provider or by other actor in the communityMitigation 5. Raise awareness on PSEA and other types of exploitation and where to reportRisk 6. Pressure on the service provider from the local authorities on who should or should not receive the service. Mitigation 6. Establish good relation with local authorities, clarify humanitarian principles and stick to them. Risk 7. Community and family stigmatization for participating in programming, family resistance to girls’ mobility. Mitigation 8. Targeting adolescent girls with socially accepted activities like life skills as safe entry point Risk 9. Low participation of girls because of family and community resistance. Mitigation 9. Engage with local authorities and clear communication on project objectivesRisk 10. Lack of quality services and referral expose girls survivors to harm, enhancing risk of stigmatization, reprisal and further trauma. Mitigation 10. Service mapping including only those that can provide girls' centred services. Risk 11. Lack of quality services also leads to fewer girls' disclosures and lack of trust in services. Mitigation 11. Promotion of Adolescent girl strategy, SOPs and awareness toolkit rollout, for all staff of organizations involved in girls' programming. Risks 12. Focus on quantity of service providers trained instead of on quality of training, relevance and impact on knowledge and improved service provision. High turnover and loss of capacity. Mitigation 12. Use of SOPs and other guidelines and resources from GBV AoR in line with global standards.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The population displaced and specifically IDPs women and girls might face challenges in accessing GBV services due to multiple barriers on where/why to access. Referrals from and to other services might be disrupted due to the security situation. Overlapping with other GBV services might occur. Security situation and continuous air strikes can hinder the work of the WGSS, PHCs and MCs. Partners’ staff working in a highly insecure environment might not be able to access counseling services. Adolescents with disabilities might face challenges in accessing the WGSS to attend girls’ club sessions. Women might face challenges accessing the WGSS due to child-rearing responsibilities. Community members might have high expectations of receiving material assistance, not only awareness discussions. Confidentiality/privacy of mobile clinics might not always be guaranteed in certain locations and this might not be a conductive environment for women and girls to share their concerns GBV. Challenges in measuring the impact of capacity development and compromised quality of trainings due to contextual constraints. Privacy concerns along with confidentiality and safety might prevent women & girls to access GBV services. Program and field team ensures confidentiality principles are in line with global standards; data protection protocols are in place and physical space are designed to meet criteria. Mobilization activities will take place to share information with the community focusing on IDPs women and girls about the GBV services in the WGSS. Regular meetings are conducted with other services providers to facilitate referral pathways and project staff is regularly updated on the up-to-date service providers of the surrounding communities. Regular communication with the community members and service providers will reduce the risk of duplication. One of the GBV mobile outreach clinic trained on GBV emergency response to respond rapidly to new displacements with PFA and dignity kits and clean delivery kits. Specific budget is allocated for transportation costs for the most at-risk individuals. Self-care training is available for SRD staff in Gaziantep but currently not inside Syria. SRD organizes social selfcare days for their staff. Designated car will be provided to facilitate the access of adolescents with disabilities to the girls’ club at the WGSS. Daycare will be available at the WGSS to ensure women’s participation at the vocational training initiatives. MC that run GBV awareness session also distribute dignity kits based on immediate identified needs within a short timeframe. In consultation with female. Ensuring that the MC/WGSS will mitigate the risks of violating confidentiality and privacy. SRD clinic staff are from the local community to reduce turnover. Protection/GBV officer in charge of case management supervision. Staff is sensitized around attitude in terms of sensitive service provision.
