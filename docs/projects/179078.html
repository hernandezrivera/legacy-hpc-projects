Project Summary

As part of Medair FLER interventions to vulnerable HHs in areas that are Hard to Reach, Newly Accessible, or with recent or sudden population movement to meet HHs in urgent need for basic shelter-related assistance, Medair intends to reach around 13,750 beneficiaries (excluding overlap) with this SHE/NFI intervention through:
i. Rehabilitating HH-level shelter units in damaged/unfinished buildings via tailored rehabilitation to improve physical safety and security, privacy, and provide more dignified living conditions to a total of up to 250 HHs, targeting the most vulnerable households according to Medair’s vulnerability criteria. It is expected that 10% of HHs are in need of disability adjustments, which will be provided under the next activity.
ii. Providing additional adaptation for elderly or disabled people, such as hand rails and adjusted toilets, Medair will follow accessibility and universal design principles to complete adaptations for up to 25 households with members who live with mobility impairment, with disabilities, or elderly people wherever this is possible within the constraints of the building structure.
iii. Distributing NFI, and Winterization kits, and Cash for NFI to up to 12,500 vulnerable HHs. NFI kits aim to address the emergency and ongoing shelter needs in selected areas, Medair will provide NFIs to vulnerable HHs to improve climatic protection and privacy, and provide more dignied living conditions, the most vulnerable HHs or communities will be prioritized. Winterization kits are distributed to help target families cope with winter weather, which will improve resilience in the upcoming and subsequent winters. MPCA will be a pilot for Medair this year, aiming to address the emergency and ongoing needs, after the economic deterioration that has taken place all over Syria.
Medair teams have been able to conduct independent house-to-house assessments jointly with SARC, using Medair’s own vulnerability and needs assessment criteria to identify the “most vulnerable” HHs. As Medair uses a multi-sectoral approach, these needs assessments will cover both Shelter/NFI needs, as well as WASH needs. Distribution activities for NFI/Shelter will happen in parallel with distribution activities for WASH. Details on WASH interventions can be found in Medair’s WASH submission. Interventions are customized for each HH according to specific technical needs or HH size. Medair will conduct FGDs wherever possible prior to distribution to obtain beneficiary feedback in order to customize interventions perbeneciary needs and prioritize their involvement for the particular community.
It is assumed that there will be a 100% overlap between the NFI and winterization kits.
Therefore, these numbers have not been included in the total direct beneficiary count.
While Shelter rehabilitation activities focus more on the longer term shelter needs, the distribution activities are part of Medair’s FLER. Medair does not plan to have an overlap between the beneficiaries for shelter rehabilitation and distribution activities.

4 - Needs

Syria’s Central Bureau of Statistics reported an average inflation rate of 200% in 2020 compared to 2019, with goods inflation reaching 300% (WFP 2021). The unaffordability of basic goods resulted in soaring needs for NFI, incl shelter items. The deterioration of socio-economic conditions remains the primary concern for large swathes of the population, with rapid price hikes and lack of availability of basic NFIs in local markets. National prices of standard reference food baskets are rising rapidly; between Nov. and Dec. 2020, the rise was 13% and reached the highest average since WFP monitoring began in 2013. Families’ heating fuel entitlement decreased across 9 governorates, causing a 20% price increase of heating diesel during that period, while the SYP depreciated a further 6% (WFP 2021). Shelter needs are concentrated in ALP, IDL, Raq, RD governorates, and it is estimated that over 31% (incl +/- 50% of returnees and 26% of IDPs) of the overall population lives in inadequate shelter(HNO 2021). Needs are further intensified by new displacement in NWS, critical infra gaps, protr. displacement in NES, and limited housing options. DEZ and rural ALP have been underserved with limited humanitarian presence. DEZ city suffered from signifant shelter and infrastructure damage because of the protracted offensive, forcing returnees and IDPs to live in overcrowded spaces. Unfinished or damaged buildings pose protection concerns. In ALP, it is reported that around 18% of the city’s apartments were damaged in addition to limited access to safe shelter solutions. In the WoS, 25% of people have some sort of disability, significantly higher than the 15% globally, with 15% of the PiN of shelter assistance living with a disability.

5 - Implementation of activities

SHE: Medair will carry out up to 250 (owner-occupied) shelter rehabs. Technical assessments are done to define basic required rehabilitation in each shelter, after which necessary approvals from MOLA and security clearance is requested. Rehabs focus on basic house repairs, addressing protection, security, and WASH needs according to Sphere standards. Additional works will be completed to accommodate special needs of elderly or PLWMI (+/- 10%). Medair will target vulnerable HHs within the significantly damaged areas in DEZ city, RD, and ALP. HHs will be selected following individual HH assessments. After rehab and checking the works, shelters will be handed back over to the HHs. A PPM will take place in which beneficiaries can give their feedback as well as to the focal point present during the works. NFI: Medair will provide NFIs, WK, and MPCA assistance for up to 2,500 HHs as FLER to areas that are newly accessible or with recent or sudden population movement. Beneficiaries are identified through an assessment conducted directly by Medair where possible, or through SARC/local partners. Eligibility is based firstly on vulnerability, then need, incl. HH size. Items in the kits are selected based on sector recommendations and previous beneficiary feedback. Medair distributions are in coordination with SARC and local municipalities. All Kits are in accordance with Sector Standards and delivered directly to HHs. Quality assurance teams examine kits prior to distribution, monitoring teams verify contents with beneficiaries during distribution. During distributions, an AAP focal point will be present for beneficiary feedback, and PDMs will be undertaken at least one month after the distribution.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1.Confidentiality of sensitive beneficiary data is breached, risking stigmatization. Data collected using ODK is encrypted; awareness raising among staff of data protection. 2. Tension between and within host communities, IDPs and returnees. Ensure distribution is needs-based. Clear vulnerability criteria for HH selection; independent and targeted Medair assessment or verification by Medair of any 3rd party assessment; explanation in advance to communities; including contact info for feedback. 3. Distributed materials do not benefit women or elderly within HHs. Clear messaging about purpose of interventions and need for protection of particularly vulnerable groups. FGDs prior to intervention will identify the most relevant items to be distributed. 4.Children at risk from accidents during building works. Explanation in advance to beneficiary communities; Contractual requirements for safe work sites. Medair employed site engineer to oversee contractors and suppliers day by day. Spot checks by Medair project staff. 5. Inequity of access by PwD. Customized adaptations of shelters and distributions directly to HHs that consider physical access for the disabled and elderly. 6. Staff gets caught by an IED. Medair monitors and analyzes security context and provides clear movement protocols. Shelter assistance is only done in places cleared of IEDs, staff is trained and briefed on safety and security. 7. CVA causing household- or community level tension. Clear vulnerability criteria for HH selection, ensure distribution is needs based. 8. Market volatility making cash useless for beneficiaries. Medair analyses the market before any interventions, and only uses cash if appropriate.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

ASSUMPTIONS: Political and Security situation remains stable enough to allow project and support functions to resume. Market systems remain relatively stable. Any inflation of the SYP will be able to be offset against hard currency (USD). Acceptance and participation of national and local authorities. Continued cooperation and coordination among relevant authorities and partners. No major deterioration in logistical conditions, supply chain for essential supplies and equipment remains reliable, and necessary goods are approved for transport RISKS: not securing timely approvals. Harm to team members within Syria due to security incidents or COVID-19 exposure. CBS restrictions stop or constrains adequate cashflow. Fraud or non-compliance with procurement guidelines. General Aid Diversion. Chaos/violence ensuing at distributions. Fuel crisis exacerbating further. MITIGATIONS: Continue regular security planning and monitoring, context analyses, incl. close liaison with local stakeholders on security issues and clear briefing to all staff. Flexibility in project locations if access is unavailable. Maintain good contact with SARC, Ministries, local authorities, communities, UN agencies and other stakeholders. Train new staff on procurement guidelines, conduct internal and external audits to assess adherence to financial and logistic guidelines and spot check for fraudulent activity. COVID prevention policies, training, and PPE for eld staff. Ensure all stakeholders mapped. Establish strong links with authorities; joint planning and written agreements on intended beneficiaries. Distributing directly to HHs, making distribution plans in cooperation with community leaders. Discuss alternative items to stoves with shelter sector.
