Project Summary

After 12 years of hostilities and persistent economic crisis, people Armanaz and Salqin saw their situation worsen when a series of earthquakes hit northern Syria and Türkiye on 6 and 20 February 2023. The earthquakes uprooted hundreds of thousands of families and severely damaged infrastructures already weakened by the conflict. Many families lost their main breadwinner due to death or injury at a time when the economic situation was already dire, increasing the vulnerability of millions of people previously unable to meet their basic needs with an extreme severity rate (SUMMARY HUMANITARIAN NEEDS OVERVIEW, 2024). This disaster compounded an already dire humanitarian situation in the target location, where populations had relied on humanitarian assistance to meet their basic needs due to the compounded factors, including rapidly deteriorating economic conditions (HNO,2023). The assessments and analysis carried out across the country continue to show a general deterioration in access to livelihoods, the ability to meet basic needs, and the availability and affordability of infrastructure and services. These factors threaten the resilience and social cohesion of households. After a life-saving and life-sustaining response to the population affected by the earthquake, people became urgently in need of more sustainable mid-term and long-term interventions to improve their resilience and adaptation to the conditions that prevailed after the earthquake. Currently, the target location (Salqin and Armanaz sub-districts remains relatively stable. This level of stability allows for high numbers of IDPs to find refuge in the area, which increases the pressure on the available services and resources.
The project will support the start-up and recovery of micro, small, and medium-sized non-agricultural businesses (including those complementing and interdependent with agricultural value chains) with a small business grant worth 800 USD. 50 HHs will be selected based on specific selection criteria, including referrals from protection sectors. IhsanRD will prioritize these businesses based on market demand, which IhsanRD already identified during the designing phase of this intervention. The targeted BNF will be supported with a training program focusing on business planning, financial management, marketing, and respecting environmental principles throughout business design and business management.
In addition, infrastructure rehabilitation involves a multi-faceted approach:
Firstly, by improving road infrastructure, the project aims to enhance accessibility, connectivity, and transportation efficiency within the community. The total BNFs will be 89565 (64% of them IDPS).
Secondly, school infrastructure development focuses on creating conducive learning environments and ensuring gender-responsive facilities. The total BNFs will be 580 (460 girls, 120 boys).
Lastly, park rehabilitation aims to provide safe and inclusive recreational spaces, fostering community engagement, physical well-being, and environmental sustainability. The total BNFs will be 23107 (39% of them IDPS).
