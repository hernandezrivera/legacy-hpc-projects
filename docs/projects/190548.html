Project Summary

According to the draft HRP 2022, ESNFI cluster people in need (PIN) cases-loads there are a total of 4,375,110 peoples at national level, of these 67.9% and 29.6% are residing under extreme and severe situation conditions, respectively. In the same light, 1,309,412 (360,258 Afar and 949,154 Amhara) people in need are living in Afar and Amhara regions and of these 500,455 (38.2%) peoples are residing under extreme poor living conditions.
In Amhara, the situation along areas bordering Tigray, namely in Wag Hamra, North Wello and North Gondar zones, remained unpredictable but relatively calm during the reporting period. Several locations remained hard to reach for partners’ humanitarian operations due to security concerns, including Abergele and Tsagibji kebeles, parts of Zequala kebeles in Wag Hamra Zone, large sections of Kobo in North Wello Zone, and in Addi Arekay in the North Gondar Zone. Also in Amhara, the regional government authorities continued with the relocation of IDPs in Kobo and Sekota. To date, and since 14 March, they have relocated more than 20,000 people out of an estimated 58,000 registered IDPs planned for relocation from Kobo in North Wollo Zone to Jara, near Weldiya, the zonal capital. In Wag Hamra, more than 3,000 IDPs have been relocated to Weleh IDP site so far, out of an estimated 12,000 IDPs in the zone planned for relocation to the site. The construction of basic facilities and installation of tents and rub halls at the relocation sites continued, but this is still insufficient to accommodate and meet the needs of the relocated IDPs.
In Afar, the humanitarian situation remains dire with amounting needs bypassing the current response and increased number of IDPs and vulnerabilities. According to regional government authorities, the fighting in Kilbati/Zone 2 has displaced more than 294,000 people in Berhale, Koneba, Magale, Erabti, and Dallol woredas since the beginning of the year. These figures are not yet verified by humanitarian partners due to access constraints. It is estimated that the population of Zone 2 is around 400,000 people. The majority of the IDPs in the region are residing with host communities and need humanitarian assistance which has so far been limited. The conflict has also seriously impacted the delivery of social services to the population due to damage to civilian infrastructure including water schemes, electricity, health posts, hospitals, schools, and government offices. In Afar, 245 schools in conflict affected areas remain closed, out of the 1,195 schools in the region, forcing more than 53,000 students, of which 42 per cent are girls, out of school. In addition, five schools are currently occupied by internally displaced people (IDPs) in Afar. In Semera, there are about the 8,000 internally displaced people (IDPs) of which their freedom of movement is restricted and therefore are unable to seek referral services unavailable at the site. Partners continue to improve services at the site including health, food, shelter, water, and sanitation. There are plans to relocate some of the IDPs to new locations to decrease the crowded conditions and improve the overall living environment. According to the ESNFI cluster, peoples in need in the proposed locations such as Gulina woreda (2,424 HH), Antsokiya woreda (5,094 HH), Delanta woreda (3,798 HH), and Tehuledere woreda (1,034 HH) are residing in extremely poor conditions who need urgent emergency shelter and NFI assistance. Gulina and Delanata woredas have IDPs whereas returnees are residing across the three operation woredas (Gulina, Antsokiya, and Tehuledere).
The ESNFI proposed interventions for this HPC will be.
Emergency shelter repair kits distribution for 1,500 HHs returnees (1000 Antsokiya woreda and 500 Tehuledere woreda)
Emergency Non-Food Items Kits (NFI V2 lowland) distribution for 2060 HHs IDPs in Gulina woreda.
Emergency Non-Food Items Kits (NFI V2 disable lowland) distribution for 364 HHs IDPs in Gulina wored

1. - Projects activities

Beneficiary Targeting and Identification: Jointly with the local authorities at Zonal or Woreda level GOAL will conduct beneficiary identification and registration for the emergency shelter repair kits and Non-Food Items Kits activities as relevant in accordance with the Guidelines for Needs Based Targeting of Humanitarian Response in Displacement Areas and Shelter Cluster guidance. Then, GE will conduct independent beneficiary verification process based on revised SOPs considering national/regional cluster standards. Finally, the registration process will be conducted together with representatives of the Woreda DRMO, kebele officials, female representatives and a committee representing the affected community (returnees and IDPs). Procurement, transportation, and distribution of emergency shelter repair kits and Non-Food Items Kits package for returnee and IDPs respectively. In-kind distribution is identified as the appropriate modality due to the market functionality, availability and quality of the items; therefore GOAL will procure emergency shelter repair kits for 1,500 HH and Non-Food Items Kits package for 4,824 HHs (of these, 664 Disable Highlands HH) through a competitive bidding procedure, transport them to the returnees location and IDP camps, and then distribute them to targeted households with the support of the local authorities, IDP camp representatives, respected local leader, and daily laborers who will act as distribution agents. GOAL will support targeted households to meet their shelter repair needs as per the cluster standard, Shelter damage prioritization draft guideline for Amhara/Afar BOQ and supported by community sensitization, carpenter training and HLP training/support.Demonstration sessions will be conducted where necessary. The demonstration shelters will be handed over to the most vulnerable people from the targeted households who cannot construct their own shelter. In addition, GOAL will organize and fund Carpenter Training for the target households. GE will also provide HLP training based on the IDMC and NRC training manual on Housing, Land and Property. This is factored into the BoQ per household for shelter repair kits. This will be organized with Woreda experts.Information sharing with community before receipt of emergency shelter repair kits and Non-Food Items Kits package. In conjunction with the relevant local authorities and IDP camp representatives, GOAL will conduct information sharing sessions in advance of the distributions, to inform the stakeholders about the project, GOAL, our Code of Conduct, targeting criteria, entitlements, information about the distributions (time, date, location), modality and feedback mechanisms. Protection mainstreaming: GOAL will make sure that people with specific needs or vulnerabilities are prioritized and supported. The project will put in place mechanisms to ensure safe and equal access to inclusive and non-discriminatory provision of services.Feedback mechanism will be established; the project is designed to respond to identified needs and will priorities safety and dignity of communities. The activities will ensure meaningful access and participation of the targeted communities. GOAL has strong accountability mechanisms in place.HLP assessment will be conducted before the shelter repair kits distribution to avoid the potential risk of eviction of beneficiaries from their shelter after construction and the documents will be one of the vital documents to guarantee their right to use the land. GOAL will discuss with the Cluster and relevant Woreda/Kebele representative where any issues arise to identify a solution.Regular joint and independent monitoring of the project activity with local government: GOAL will work closely with the local government starting from targeting/verification up to the process of distribution and jointly evaluate the effectiveness of the intervention with local government and the beneficiary’s representation.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

The project is designed in consultation with the local government and regional cluster need and priorities. Government agreement will be reviewed by relevant stakeholders and ensure that the proposed interventions are in line with the actual needs of the affected communities. GOAL Ethiopia (GE) has institutionalized accountability matrix across all levels, ensuring accountability towards our project participants and partners through participation, feedback, transparency, information sharing, appropriate activities that respond to the needs, and appropriate use of resources. GE believes and uses community networks, linkages, partnerships, and coordination which enables effective activities, service delivery and advocacy, maximizing resources and impacts, and coordinated, collaborative working. GE will ensure the proposed interventions are accessible to all who need them, evidence‐informed and based on community assessment of resources and needs. Therefore, GE ensures that affected populations are engaged at all levels of the project cycle and that consultation is ongoing. GE considers it of paramount importance that beneficiaries have access to information about their entitlements, have access to GOAL’s Code of Conduct and can provide feedback on programs. During any community activities discussions are held in advance with the affected community, project participants, community leaders and authorities. Information is provided about the project, the Donor, targeting criteria and entitlements, GOAL, our Code of Conduct, and ways to contact the organisation with feedback, requests for information, and grievances. Moreover, joint supervision and monitoring activities are conducting on regular base which involves the local government and the community representatives from all categories like gender, age, ethnic and health status. A Community Response Mechanism will be established for all locations for stakeholders, including beneficiaries, to be able to provide complaints or feedback. Beneficiaries will be informed of this mechanism during activities, entitlement posters will be printed and displayed at relevant locations. The CRM is designed for direct and indirect project participants, community members, local and national government, partners, and GOAL staff. Core principles of GOAL’s CRM are confidentiality, accessibility, safety, and transparency. GOAL has dedicated third party (independent from operations) in-country resources in place to manage the CRM. All staff are trained on accountability measures, including the CRM, and comprehensive guidelines and SOPs are in place. Regular joint and independent monitoring of the project activity with local government: GOAL will work closely with the local government starting from targeting/verification up to the process of distribution and jointly evaluate the effectiveness of the intervention with local government and the beneficiary’s representatives.Post-distribution monitoring will be conducted after NFI distribution and shelter rehabilitation in targeted locations to understand the impact of the project and assess the appropriateness of the response, how it met the identified needs, organizational accountability to the targeted communities, and beneficiary satisfaction with the response. It will strive to identify any risks or perceived negative consequences, as well as lessons learned and recommendations for future projects.The findings will be shared with the national and regional cluster (where relevant). Monitoring visits will also address accountability questions to establish the level of knowledge of the beneficiaries of the selection criteria, their entitlements, and GOAL’s Code of Conduct. GOAL is committed to a having a culture of evidence-based learning and accountability.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

In conjunction with the relevant local authorities and IDP camp representatives, GOAL will conduct information sharing sessions in advance of the distributions, to inform the stakeholders about the project, GOAL, our Code of Conduct, targeting criteria, entitlements, and feedback mechanisms. GOAL will prepare the targeted community members to receive shelter repair kits and NFI, by providing information about the distributions (time, date, location), and modality. GE will ensure that households targeted to receive support fully understand how and where they will be able to collect their in-kind materials. Protection messages will be provided during the information sessions to ensure that targeted communities are not at risk when carrying cash or in-kind items, and do not share financial or identification details with others, and that all risks to the beneficiaries are mitigated. Targeted beneficiaries will be provided with information on what gap/need the cash (where this modality is employed) is expected to cover (shelter repair, essential NFIs). For shelter repair, GOAL will use a phased approach based on incremental payments to ensure that cash has been used for the intended shelter repair activities. GOAL will establish a complaints and feedback response mechanism (CRM) for community feedback on Shelter repair kits and NFI distributions. All targeted households will be provided with information on the project, GOAL, their entitlements, and modalities for collection of Shelter repair kits and NFI, as well as ways to contact GOAL and provide feedback through the CRM or by other means. Male and female representatives from the targeted community will be selected to take part on a community feedback committee during the distributions. More details are provided in the Accountability to Affected Populations Section. GE is using integrated complaints mechanism which is more participatory not just consultation. Before establishing GE ensures the mechanism is age and gender appropriate, safe, and confidential channels and conduct effective community awareness raising campaigns and create multiple complaints channels which more confidential and user-friendly. Project staffs are well training how to empower the community to use the channels, and how to employee appropriate complaint handling mechanism.
