Project Summary

This project proposal is informed by the FSL Sudan IPC 2021-2022, People in need and severity of needs data for Sudan HNO 2022, HNO 2021 Food Security & Livelihoods chapter, Sudan Periodic Monitoring Report, and WFP Sudan Market Monitor. The proposed interventions are designed to improve the livelihoods of the most vulnerable communities shocked by crises and natural disasters and support communities to build resilience and mitigate negative coping mechanisms through access to basic goods and services especially livelihoods and assets ownership. The target populations are resident groups of Baw and Al Kurmuk Localities of Blue Nile State, which are classified in IPC Phase 3.
The proposed interventions are targeting (4,000 households) in Baw and Al Kurmuk Localities in a 12-month duration. The proposed interventions are composed of the following activities:
1. Provision of Agricultural Support (seeds and agric tools) for 3,000 people, 1,500 in Baw and 1,500 in Al Kurmuk localities to support crop production and agricultural activities at the households level.
2. Provision of Livestock Support (distribution and treatment) for 600 people, 300 in Baw and 300 in Al Kurmuk localities to support livestock production and support supply chain at the households level.
3. Livelihood Kits Support for 200 people with special needs, 100 in Baw and 100 in Al Kurmuk localities to support and build their resilience as a marginalized group.
4. provision of Vocational Training for 200 people (females), 100 females in Baw, and another 100 females in Al Kurmuk localities to support livelihoods and build their resilience.

1 - Needs

According to Sudan IPC 2021 Acute Food Insecurity Situation April - May 2021 and Projections for June - September 2021 and October 2021 - February 2022, the latest data showed that an estimated 7.3 million people in Sudan (16% of the population analyzed) are in high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above) between April and May (current period) and require urgent action. During the first projection period (June–September 2021) which coincided with the peak lean season, it is estimated that around 9.7 million people (21% of the population analyzed) will likely be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse. During the first quarter of 2022, Prices are anticipated to decrease seasonally during the harvest between October 2021 and February 2022. The stabilization of the economy will continue, further facilitated by the possibility of receiving funds from WB and IMF. Inflation might remain high and prices still above the five-year average, though with progressive stabilization and seasonal decrease of prices, especially for sorghum and millet. Prices of imported goods and fuel might also remain above the five-year average. The government is likely to expand the Family Support Program (FSP), where 80% of the total population (vulnerable households) are planned to receive cash transfers of 5 7 USD per person on a monthly basis. Conflict and displacement are expected, with security situation fluctuations.Al Kurmuk Locality hosts 185,529 people, 40% of the population are classified under IPC Phase 3 Or Higher. While Baw locality hosts 180,593 people which 40% of the population are classified under IPC Phase 3 Or Higher as well. Baw locality hosts mixed groups of host-community, IDPs, recent returnees, and IOM permanent groups. There is a dire need to subsidize agriculture inputs such as seeds and fuel, and improve investments in extension services and support to protect productive assets, especially areas impacted by flooding, conflict, or other shocks. Scale-up diversified livelihood programs for improved self-reliance, resilience building, and social protection for all vulnerable communities classified in IPC Phases 2, 3, and 4, with special support for reconstructing and restoring livelihood assets in areas affected by flooding.
