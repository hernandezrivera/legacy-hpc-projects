Project Summary

In South Sudan around 1.4 million children under the age of five years; 483,000 pregnant and lactating women are expected to be acutely malnourished in 2021. This is the highest number in three years and related to increased food insecurity, decrease in coverage and uptake of nutrition services due to conflict, flooding and impacts of COVID-19 (HNO 2021). According to South Sudan, IPC Acute Food Insecurity and Acute Malnutrition Analysis October 2020 - July 2021; there is deterioration nutrition situation and in the year 2020, 47 counties out of 78 have a proxy prevalence of GAM above the emergency threshold of 15%.
Jonglei, Warrap, Upper Nile and other states have been severely impacted with the complex emergencies of both natural disaster and man-made of severe flooding, inter communal violence respectively; 85,000 people were affected in Bor South, 60,000 in Pibor and 30,824 in Twic East Counties respectively (UN OCHA 2020). The, Multiple shock, have caused food insecurity interruption of service delivery, increase need of nutrition intervention and the population are within Severity need level 4 and 5 (HNO 2021); Catastrophic (IPC Phase) 5 and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) (November IPC Reports). In Pibor, the Famine Review Committees confirmed the extremely critical levels of acute malnutrition; estimating Pibor and Verteth payams likely to pass the IPC Famine thresholds of 30% for GAM based on WHZ for children aged 6-59 months; while the Cluster Proxy GAM level in Bor South, Twic East and Twic are 28.37%; 13.83% and 22.2% respectively (≥ 15% WHO threshold).
In this Project, JAM will contribute to decrease prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under the age of 5 years and PLW in in prioritized locations by the end of 2021 as follow: In Strategic Objective 1: Reduce morbidity and mortality, as well as suffering from protection threats and incidents, of the most vulnerable populations in severity levels 4 and 5. This project is embedded to nutrition Sector Objective 1, 2 and 3 - JAM will support increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific service delivery for children, adolescents and women in prioritized locations; through Blanket Supplementary Feeding Program (BSFP) to under 5 of age children (33,908 Bor South; Twic 7,600; Twic East 10,430 ); Pregnant and Lactating Women (14,277 Bor South; 4390 Twic East and 3,200 Twic). JAM will promote behavior change communication, and 25,397 (Male 1270; Women 24,127) primary caregivers of children aged 0-23 months has been target for MIYCN counselling and Family MUAC. The plan is to reach 30,634 (51% female) aged 6-59 months who receive Vitamin A, and a total of 27,570(Males: 13,234; Females: 14,336) aged 12-59 months whom will be dewormed to prevent micronutrient deficiencies.
JAM will establish up to 30 static site and 8 outreach sites in Pibor county; 10 sites with OTP and TSFP Component and one SC in Jebel Boma; 22 sites in 4 Payam (Pibor, Verthet, Gumuruk, Lekuangole) will have OTP to support the screening, treatment and rehabilitation of 7976 AM children between age 6-59 months i.e 5960 SAM cases and 2016 MAM cases. It is expected that 2664 PLW and 33 HIV/Care takers will be reached through targeted supplementary feeding program (TSFP) and at least 73 children with SAM with medical complication. JAM will provide Monthly NIS report, participate in FSNM and SMART survey to monitor the nutrition situation in Pibor County.
In Strategic Objective 2. Anchored to Nutrition Sectoral Objective 4 and nutrition sensitive interventions; JAM will support individuals including children with HIV through in Patient Feeding (IFP) in the Boma hospital, Malaria testing to 5960 SAM children, distribution of Long Lasting Treated Mosquito Nets (LLITNs) ; WASH basic supplies; ECD kits to AM children, creation of child friendly spaces in nutrition sites ; and lastly linking the caregivers to FSL, Health and Protection services.

1 - Needs

The protracted conflict, lack of durable peace and limited investment in basic services are holding people back from stability and sustainable development in South Sudan. In 2020, communities were hit hard by the triple shock of intensified conflict/ sub-national violence, a second consecutive year of major flooding, and the impacts of COVID-19 including movement restriction that led to a shrinking economy resulting in high inflation for food products (inflation rose to 40% in 2020) 82% of the population live on < 1 US$ a day while 60% of South Sudan’s population (7.24 million) face severe acute food insecurity. There is general deterioration of the nutritional status of children, around 1.4 million children under the age of 5 years are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition in 2021 (an increase from the 1.3 million children in the year 2020). This is the highest caseload of acute malnutrition (AM) in South Sudan since 2013.The number of children suffering from severe and moderate acute malnutrition (SAM & MAM) is expected to increase from more than 292,000 and 1008,696 children in 2020 to more than 313,000 and 1,078,867 children in 2021 respectively.In Pibor County situation is alarming as it is classified in IPC 5 (famine likely) by IPC Technical Working Group and External Famine Reviews. The nutrition status for children aged 6-59 months is critical and the County GAM level by MUAC is ≥ 15% (above WHO threshold) with averages of 24% in Pibor Centre while Murlil and Kireka in Verteth Payam having a GAM rate of 27% and 32% respectively. A total of 5,960 and 10,380 children between age 6-59 months are expected to suffer from SAM and MAM respectively and this is equivalent 11% increase on SAM caseloads from 5368 in 2020.In November to December 2020, JAM conducted a mass Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) screening exercise alongside the measles and vaccination campaigns in order to further understand the nutrition situation in Pibor County. The assessment found that the nutrition status for children aged 6-59 months was at a critical level ≥ 15% (above WHO threshold) with averages of 22% in Pibor County. The FRC report further, confirms the extremely critical levels of acute malnutrition from the exhaustive mass screening conducted; estimating Pibor and Verteth payams likely to pass the IPC Famine thresholds of 30% for GAM based on WHZ. The areas found with high GAM rates were primarily the areas where nutrition services were suspended due to accessibility and security challenges presented by conflict and flooding. Lack of food/ hunger and access to basic health and WASH which are the major underlying causes for malnutrition as well as the inadequate nutrition services to meet the growing needs of the most vulnerable people in the displacement locations remains a key concern.Levels of acute malnutrition are expected to further deteriorate in the year 2021 due to the early onset of the lean season, in adequate food in the households and lack of health and WASH services. There is need to provide nutrition interventions, by both nutrition specific intervention and nutrition sensitive interventions. There is need to provide screening, treatment and rehabilitation of children between age 6-59 months with Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) through Out-patient Therapeutic Program (OTP); treatment of SAM with medical complications as in patient services through Stabilization Centre (SC); screening, treatment and rehabilitation of children’s, Pregnant and Lactating Women(PLW) suffering from Moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) through Targeted Supplementary feeding (TSFP).Need to provide/ Support Maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) through maternal individual counseling; hygiene promotion and breastfeeding promotion.There is need to prevent children from vitamin A deficiency and helminthic infection which tend to cause iron deficiency anemia and malnutrition contribute to addressing the underlying cause of AM.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

JAM has strong safeguarding policies and procedures in place to maintain the safety of the staff as well as the beneficiaries. The sector leads will be superintending the protection of the target groups and ensure do no harm is correctly integrated. JAM has a gender policy that includes GBV and safeguarding that is adopted within the country. Project staff and each sector lead will also be trained on protection, gender equality (mainstreaming), with a focus on the PSEA, child protection, and GBV. The project applies a strong community based approach from the formation of the project to the monitoring and evaluation stage. The integration of protection and gender staff in the project team will help the implementation of key activities to apply proper safe programming and Do No Harm approaches. In the nutrition component, community consultations with women, men, girls, and boys of all ages and abilities will take place to determine the service safety. Nutrition services will include confidential entry points and the facility staff will be trained to faster detect indicators of violence and to be able to manage SGBV cases. All the involved staff like CMAM Nurses, Nutrition Assistance, Nutrition officer; community workers such as the CNV, Lead Mothers, MIYCN will be trained to provide beneficiaries information; nutrition services, will help to manage SGBV cases and provide support to GBV victims through utilization of Pibor County Referral Pathway. The participation (consultation) of community members, especially women and girls, will ensure a gender-sensitive and culturally appropriate project approach. Sex, age, and disability disaggregated (SADDD) data will be collected by accessing facility registers such as mobility registers, program admission registers and referral slips. The raw data is then shared and uploaded on the nutrition management information system—standard systems within the respective clusters. These standard tools support the collection of disaggregated data which allows the team to monitor gender equality.While in the PSEA the staff will share the information using the organization Policy to the field focal person who will use the stipulated referral pathway and referral cards/ slips and other modalities to provide information in a confidential way; it is expected that the shared information through the referral pathway and communication mechanism will trigger support to the survivor and handling the perpetrator in a confidential manner to ensure do no harm.

Through JAM nutrition intervention, it is expected that cure rate and death rate will be maintained within the sphere standards and the program data or surveys will provide data on the level of decrease of the prevalence of global acute malnutrition.
