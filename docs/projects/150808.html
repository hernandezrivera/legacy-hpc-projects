Project Summary

In this project, QRCS will improve the access and utilization of Emergency integrated health/WASH and Nutrition services for women and children living in the most affected areas in all the states of Somalia using both static and outreach facilities. The static health centres will be strengthen by adding WASH and Nutrition Services, provision of medical and nutrition supplies and the improvement staff capacity. Remote villages will be reached through mobile clinics; community mobilizers and CHWs will be recruited and trained. Among the activities that QRCS will be providing include: Immunization to under 5 children and Women of child bearing age (WCBA); Provision of antenatal and post-natal care to pregnant and lactating mothers, Treatment and control of communicable diseases targeting; Provision of basic lifesaving medical services to sick patients; Treatment of uncomplicated malnourished cases i.e SAM. Identify and refer patients with severe medical cases that requires admission at secondary health facilities after providing first aid services; Referral of complicated malnourished children; Promote the adoption of good hygiene and sanitation practices to the affected communities; Breast feeding promotion and support of infant and young child feeding; Identify and assist in the train of community volunteers on health promotion and education; MUAC screening and identification of malnourished children; Organize community sensitization and mobilization sessions at facility and outreach level of the affected areas; Distribute standard IEC materials for social mobilization; Support distribution of hygiene kits; provision of adapted sanitation infrastructures and related hygiene promotion; MUAC screening for all children 6-59 months and pregnant and lactating women (PLW); Treatment of SAM cases without medical complications; Referral of SAM cases with medical complications and failed appetite test. Under this project, QRCS will scale up its intervention through two fixed integrated health and Nutrition facilities and mobile outreach services targeting 81338 drought affected people IDPs and Host communities. the health services to be offered include outpatient treatment, management of common child illness like Pneumonia, diarrhea, ear infections, fever, provision of immunization services, antenatal care services as well as skilled deliveries .Through this project, the capacity of 45 staff will be improved through training while the 60 community members will also access health education and awareness session on services utilization and communicable disease prevention and treatment

Needs

Climatic shocks, continued insecurity and armed conflict, recurrent humanitarian right violation, political instability and lack of basic services contribute to high level of protracted humanitarian needs in Somalia. Drought remain the climatic drivers of humanitarian crisis in Somalia with devastating impact on the health and access to WASH services for communities. The overall humanitarian situation in Somalia remains fragile due to residual impact of drought, ongoing displacement, conflict, critical gaps in health and WASH services. These largely pastoralist communities that already lost up to 70 per cent of their livestock due to four consecutive seasons of below normal rains and are at risk of slipping to crisis (IPC 3) if humanitarian assistance is not provided or sustained. Despite recent improvement, an estimated 1.5 million people in Somalia are expected to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse through December according to findings from the post-Gu seasonal assessment conducted in June and July 2018. According to data analysis from UNICEF, The Under-Five and Maternal Mortality rates in Somalia are amongst the highest in the world; one out of every seven Somali children dies before seeing their fifth birthday. This highlights the need for improved access to life-saving health, Nutrition, FS, Shelter and WASH interventions in Somalia. The fate of pregnant women and mothers is particularly precarious in Somalia, one out of every 12 women dies due to pregnancy related causes. The average fertility rate is 6.6 children per woman. Somalia is also drought prone and faces food insecurity, which is exacerbated by poor healthcare, lack of access to safe drinking water and safe sanitation facilities. Access to maternal health services and ANC coverage remain low. Only about 26% of Somalis have antenatal care coverage, and the number of necessary emergency care facilities for obstetrics is 0.8 per 500,000 people.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

QRCS will work the Government in order to build a resilient and responsive health system infrastructure. QRCS will focus both on the everyday routine challenges that front line staff face on a regular basis as well as focus on sudden shocks such as disease outbreaks. QRCS will work to build the leadership capacity of district and regional state health systems and in this regard will target Government key health managers, health facility staff, community members as well as patients accessing services so as to empower them to solve problems at their level and build relationships and social networks. QRCS will focus on strengthening existing social networks to provide access to multiple resources available in the community and forms of value such as community knowledge and reputation that can build and restore resilience to health systems. QRCS will use the model of everyday resilience building in the health system of Government through a combination of absorptive, adaptive and transformative strategies that health authorities, staff and community members can use to adopt the health system in responding to strains and shocks. This will help in producing a strengthened and more resourceful resilient health system infrastructure. QRCS will train the Health Authorities, health facility staffs, patients and community members on how to deal with sudden shocks to the health system such as outbreak of diseases like Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD), Cholera, and Measles and so on. All the different strata of the health system will be taught how to manage such disease outbreaks at different levels starting from the community level to the primary health unit to the health center to the referral health facility level so as not to overwhelm only one level of the health system but to build a resilient and responsive health infrastructure into every level. QRCS will use the funding from donors as well as its own funds to ensure adequate resources and strong health system infrastructure is built to contribute to generating a resilient and responsive health system in all the states of Somalia.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

QRCS is an independent, neutral organization ensuring humanitarian protection and assistance for victims of armed conflict and other situations of violence, natural disasters such as drought and famine. It takes action in response to emergencies and at the same time promotes respect for international humanitarian law and its implementation in national law. QRCS as part of the movement of the International Red Cross and Red Crescent Society it is guided by a code of conduct to ensure that displaced people are treated with the humanitarian principles of humanity, neutrality, impartiality and independence. As such the highest regard for the protection of human life and health will be provided to all people needing and seeking help with clear policies and procedures in place for QRCS staff and partners to endure that not protection risk is incurred. In the event of any exposure to protection risk, QRCS will follow its laid down procedures to mitigate those risks to ensure protection is provided at all times to all in need. Displaced populations, where by natural or human-made disasters are at a high risk to be excluded from accessing the humanitarian support they need the most due to multifactorial issues some of which include segregation of minority groups, stigma, exclusion due to lack of knowledge or information sharing. Taking cognizance of these risks of exclusion that displaced populations face, QRCS will actively seek an all-inclusive policy in order to reach all those in need especially in the areas QRCS is working. QRCS will recruit some of its project staff from displaced populations especially for positions such as community health workers in order to ensure IDPs get inclusion in accessing and benefiting from all our projects and will also ask for their participation in project design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

QRCS is an equal opportunity employer and does not discriminate against anyone on the basis of sex, religion, race, or any other factor. QRCS as a member of the International Convention of the Red Cross and Red Crescent Society follow its code of conduct at all levels of its operation whether at HQ, regional office, or facility staffs, to ensure that displaced people are treated with the humanitarian principles of humanity, neutrality, impartiality and independence. In this regard, QRCS will ensure no discrimination of whatever form is allowed on any basis including on gender and age. QRCS believes all members of the society of all gender and age have a role to play in alleviating human need and suffering. In that vein, QRCS will engage men, women, boys and girls, young and old alike, in the delivery of services either as staff or as beneficiaries.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Populations displaced by conflict and violence face a lot of problems especially humanitarian need and lack of life-saving health, nutrition, WASH, Shelter and FSL services. QRCS will work to ensure the provision of access to basic life-saving health, nutrition, WASH, Shelter and FSL to all populations in need in our areas of interventions without discrimination. In this regard, QRCS will also work within the humanitarian context and coordinate with other actors to ensure the severe impacts of armed conflict and violence is reduced. QRCS works very closely with communities and local authorities, ensuring that local laws, customs and culture are adhered to, and avoiding action and advocacy that may be perceived as offensive.
