Project Summary

THE PROJECT SCOPE:
Bearing the strategic objective of the Cluster, this plan intends to achieve the following Outcomes, Outputs, Assumptions and Indicators:
OUTCOME:
Populations affected by the conflict in Lainya and Morobo target areas have timely access to appropriate life-saving Shelter and NFI.
OUTPUT:
Individuals are provided with life-saving shelter and/or non-food items on the basis of assessed and verified felt needs.
ASSUMPTIONS & RISKS:
 Assuming need of materials, access to populations, safety and security to enable provision of assistance is one of the risks.
 Breaking of conflicts and limited access due to roadblocks and ambushes and climate shocks or whether, poor road conditions.

PLAN MANAGEMENT:
This project shall be managed by competent staff including Project Manager (ProM) who is responsible for the overall program implementation in the target areas, including oversight of finance and administration of all activities and is the main contact person for government officials, donors and partners in the Region, Field Project Coordinator (FPC) will supervise and coach team leaders, ensure all logistics, finance and administration are properly reported, according to laid system, to ARD-Africa HQs; initiate workshops with the stakeholders and state government; ensure coordination of information and data collection as well as compiling quarterly reports/annual reports. At the local (county and Payam) level, Specialized SNFI Officers (Specialized Project Officers- SPOs) are responsible for training, supervision and coaching of facilitators; support to local-level; continual monitoring/reporting of unforeseen impact, security risks, etc. They will facilitate quarterly planning with the community and administration; prepare monthly reports. At the Community (Boma) level, Liaison officers (Community Liaison Officers- CLOs) are responsible for the implementation of Community Workshops, Hygiene Promotion, Mobilization of target communities, distribution of supplies, weekly and or monthly monitoring, and facilitation at the community level; reporting to Project Officers (SPOs). From the HQs, the ProM will report to the Secretary General (SG) and receive strategic direction, management and fiscal oversight for all Project activities/initiatives for the target areas. The ProM provides expert advice in the design, development and execution of all relief and recovery related programming including S/NFIs. The S/NFI Coordinator will provide technical support to planning, IEC processing, local partner identification, programmatic planning and management, and technical directive and support on related matters to processing, delivery and distribution of S/NFI and PDM coordination with donors/cluster.
Further, ARD-Africa will consider receiving support from Volunteer Support Team(VST) to provide strategic direction on the program, as well as expertise advice regarding best practices, innovations, and lessons learned from partners and globally and causal support for the project activities.

Needs

Due to the back and forth of IDPs and Returnees moving to re-settlements and refuge in Uganda and DRC. They keep coming back bearing all risks to collect food for their families in refuge or hideouts. Others decided to move back to their original villages due to limited services and access to basic needs including dignified shelters, NFIs, and food at their hideouts and refugee camps. Children keeps disturbing their parents about the uncomfortable lifestyle they are put in due to displacement and forceful emigration to refugee camps. Most of them who decided to come back got their HH either burnt, destroyed, lost properties/NFIs and could hardly make it again back to refugee camps. These people have decided, no matter what, not to move out of their places due to experiences they faced in IDP and Refugee settlements, yet, they could not afford to help themselves or afford to acquire basic shelter and NFIs including clothing especially to children, kitchen sets, sleeping mats, bedsheets, seats, carpets, shelter building tools and materials, sanitary provisions, mosquito nets even laundry soaps, water treatment tabs etc for their families, though could access minimal food for their families from what they have cultivated while moving back and forth to camps. atleast more than 5 individuals sleep in one tukul, yet, some of these tukuls are damaged. for instance, in Mugwo, most of the IDPs sleep under trees without proper covers. access to light at night become additional problem to them. Beneficiaries served under this project are those directly affected by the conflict that erupted in the state since July 2016. The primary locations will be Wuji and Kupera of Lainya county, Gulumbi and Lujulo of Morobo county and surrounding areas, but ARD-Africa will maintain flexibility to adapt to a changing security environment, in the instance that IDPs/Returnees from these areas and surrounding areas move to neighboring locations. It has already been reported that IDPs from Kupera and other parts or Lainya and Morobo have been seen in Yei and Juba. Beneficiaries targeted are estimated at approximately 7,200 individuals. this particular project seek to:- ensure that women, girls, boys, men and all age groups that are vulnerable access lifesaving Shelter and NFI assistance based on needs and registration. it seek to serve 7,200 vulnerable population in the target areas ensure women, men, boys and girls equally participate in all steps of the project from design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation.  Mitigate sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) experienced by women and girls with regard to distribution of the items as well as other distribution challenges. contribute to the repair of damaged houses in return and Host communities by providing shelter materials and tools.

Activities

o Meet local and community authorities and Conduct entry (baseline) assessment to determine specific needs of the various groups, registration and verification of identified primary beneficiaries.o Conduct focus group discussion on shelter construction and allocation among boys, girls, men, women of diverse backgrounds and outcome fed into programming accordingly.o Design and disseminate communication campaign targeting women and men about the project and ensure that communities understand why majorly women are registered as primary beneficiaries or recipients of the assistance.o Develop, in written, verbal and illustrative formats and display code of conduct on sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) and where and how people may make reports confidentially.o Develop a participatory system to monitor the safety and accessibility of distribution sites taking in to account the needs of women, men, unaccompanied girls and boys, the elderly, sick and disabled.o Conduct focus group discussion to consult with women to determine culturally appropriate hygiene materials for supply to the needy.o Assist the community to identify women, girls, boys and men with specific needs by sex, age and disability with shelter construction needs and ensure these needs are prioritized and met accordingly.o Consulting with women on what arrangements regarding child care, transport, lodgings, etc would need to be in place for them to work on distribution campaigns.o Monitor women and girls participation in decision making on site layout and shelter design and ensure their needs are shared and net appropriately.o Before distribution, initiation of communication on distribution entitlement in forms of written, verbal and illustrative and disseminate widely to communities in target locations.o Establish distribution committee and Train distribution volunteers from the community and or staff.o Request and Distribute the received (released) S/NFIs from pipeline to the primary, registered beneficiaries in the target locations.o Support most vulnerable HH especially PSN with establishment and or repair of damaged Shelters beneficiaries shall also be trained on efficient cooking energy material (briquette) and local stoves development and management. During entry assessment, Vulnerable households are identified and ARD-Africa will coordinates with the CCCM, WASH and Protection partners on ground in order to ensure that these households are prioritized for reception of NFIs. Furthermore, vulnerable households as identified through assessments by WASH, and Protection partners shall be targeted to receive fully completed Robust Emergency Shelters and shelter supplies as they meet certain criteria which deem them to be unable to complete their shelters structures using the shelter material kits provided without additional support. These vulnerable households are also placed in locations in close proximity to camp services. ARD-Africa shall coordinate and complement S/NFI distribution activities with line Agencies doing S/NFI on ground, this is to avoid duplication of services to the same beneficiaries. we shall engage or collaborate with line partners including TITI Foundation Africa, Concern, etc in covering up what we did not cover and are not covering in terms of S/NFI assistance while on ground.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

 The project will create visible public notices in writing and with pictures stating the distribution is free and with no return favors. The distributors will wear nametags and reflector jackets for identity incase beneficiaries have complaints, notices, problem that require attention. There shall be suggestion and complain boxes and response mechanisms in place Staff behaviors shall be monitored and issues reported against them are followed up and feedback communicated to reporters appropriately. On Protection and Sexual Exploitation Awareness (PSEA), ARD-Africa will get all involved staff trained about PSEA and participate in taking concrete steps towards prevention, such as discussing PSEA action plans with stakeholders. A PSEA Focal Point shall be appointed and have Focal Points in sites to conduct awareness raising sessions with women, girls, boys, and men in communities or centers of concern and with partners; the project will assess and map out potential risks areas and discuss protective actions to combat them; the PSEA Focal Points will design feedback mechanisms with communities and partners and coordinate with partners for receiving complaints (action plans for who will do what, where, and how); Community members and participants are encouraged to report any suspicion of sexual exploitation and abuse of persons of concern immediately to our Protection Sexual Exploitation Awareness (PSEA) or local authorities for handling. The project will ensure that an effective anonymous and confidential complaint mechanism exists. The complaint mechanism shall be easily accessible to complainants and they shall be treated with dignity to feel that it is easy to use and does not put them in any risk. Approach Ethics Office or PSEA Focal Person for guidance if in doubt of procedures.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Community leaders including local male and female chiefs, women, both school going and dropout girls and boys were consulted in our initial assessment process in Yei in which 36 individuals were reached through FGD, KI and questionnaires. Their views were captured and contributed to this informed plan. We further intend to conduct inclusive entry assessment to gather timely information from the community members in the targeted locations including women, girls, boys, elderly, community leaders, ex-combatants, and PSN.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Relaying info back to the community, we rely on traditional methods at settings, such as involving the community and elders as well as in participating in community ceremonies and activities. This reliance and considerations on traditional and customary methods throughout the project’s design will give it more ability to attract participants and more credibility for them to follow introduced conflict resolution and healing options as well as share grievances freely and boost their livelihoods. The project takes a multi-stakeholder approach in the implementation of all feedback activities. Following careful and logical stakeholder mapping in the targeted communities, the project will engage a range of stakeholders such as religious leaders, local government, local influential persons such as leaders and elders in communities, groups, committees in disseminating messages and life guidance. Where stakeholders and leaders who may have influence and support to conflict resolution will be encouraged to do so in structured manner. The structured and caring actions of the local partners will motivate communities to take concrete actions to raise and resolve conflicts especially on disputes among returnee, Host, IDPs, ex-combatants’ integration tension, etc. In addition, ARD-Africa will utilize other feedback mechanisms including group discussions, media leaflets, awareness campaigns, cluster feeds and progress reports to inform the community and respective stakeholders.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

 Through protection referral systems we will remain abreast of developments in field locations and where special needs may exist, provide assistance on an ad hoc basis as necessary. We will continue to work with the Protection Cluster to identify ways in which the Shelter-NFI team can better serve protection needs of our beneficiaries. During this project and as per usual practice, ARD-Africa will coordinate with both the State and national levels Shelter-NFI Cluster Coordinators to ensure that Security Risk Assessments facilitated in CES and Juba with coordination from the ICWG, UNDSS, etc., are conducted and a security analysis is done prior to deploying to response sites, and will take in to account the unique risks faced by our national staff of certain tribal groups when traveling to specific sites. Particular risks during transport of stock and field staff will be mitigated through effective coordination with other humanitarian actors to travel in convoys where necessary, and with forced protection escort as a last resort when serious need arise. Women, Children and PSN will be at front check of their protection and safety when intervening in these communities. Prior understanding of them and the condition or environment in reflection to their protection risks. Given the specific security dynamics in the communities and surrounding areas, ARD-Africa will continue to ensure protection of beneficiaries including women and girls, humanitarian staff and assets are at the forefront of planning and implementation throughout the project period. ARD-Africa will continue linking with protection actors both at national and CES levels.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

 Road blocks and lack of freedom of movement of beneficiaries including aid workers is another impediment to community access of the services. Placing distribution sites away or at unsafe locations may pose access threat to beneficiaries, some of them may fear crossing to other sites due to fear of insecurity. Stigmatization where community may socially exclude individuals who are associated with certain issues connected to taboos such as HIV/AIDS, or Rape. This act may increase stigmatization on both male and female beneficiaries making them shy away and hinders access. Creation of tensions within communities as provisions are targeted to specific individuals or groups with specific needs and those who did not get them pay create access problem to the target beneficiaries by causing tensions, fights or roadblocks and confiscations of items from vulnerable beneficiaries. Exposing supplies and beneficiaries to the risk of theft and violence including rape, robbery and injury which subsequently pose threat to access. Inter HH and Community e.g. Host and IDP and Returnee tensions especially those that are not among the target groups and missed the supplies may be jealousy and cause havoc to beneficiaries hindering access to services by other beneficiaries

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

 Involving relevant stakeholders in defining the selection criteria for beneficiaries to reduce conflicts during and after delivery and distribution of the items. We shall actively device appropriate communication modality with the communities on the selection criteria and the rationale for targeting Our field team will study the issues that represent social taboos and that has potential of causing an access barrier to certain beneficiaries and avoid associating individuals with taboos by offering mixed assistance. Confidentiality shall be maintaining for any consultations by offering secluded rooms and refraining from labelling rooms or spaces for consultation purposes as HIV/SGBV, etc consultation spaces/rooms to protect beneficiaries’ dignity. Locating distribution points close to the homes or shelters of the beneficiaries targeted and reducing visibility of supplies from crowd and only brought to site on certain quantity while distributing. By continuing to operate on a life-saving needs basis, ARD-Africa intends to demonstrate to all actors in the conflict affected areas that its assistance is impartial and neutral, thus limiting the opportunities for parties to the conflict to deny access to the team providing life-saving materials. Our team on the ground has been and will continue to coordinate daily with Partners including OCHA, Cluster coordinators, and security focal point in the State and in Juba in order to understand the latest developments related to safe movement to field locations, conflict dynamics, and assurances that humanitarian activities will be allowed to continue independently without interference by parties to the conflict

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

 Population informed on the dates and procedures of distribution as per agreed with the community Beneficiaries have unhindered access to the S/NFIs and make use of them. Delivery of items unhindered with chaos neither roadblocks and services provided free of charge without asking favors from beneficiaries. People with special needs, lactating and pregnant mother, elderly are given unhindered primary services. Crowding during distribution are well controlled Ensuring the awareness campaigns as well as capacity building and WASH supplies are indiscriminately easily accessible to all target groups including IDP/Returnee/women, youth PSN and children. Ensure beneficiaries to the intervention are free from discrimination based on their political affiliation, religious, cultural biases, geographic locations, etc.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

 Elderly and people with special needs are provided the necessary safety services including supporting them with reach of items to their homes or ensuring their items reach them at home without hindrance or charges. Women and girls reach with their items homes without threats of rape, confiscation, or robbery. Items are placed where it does not put beneficiaries in to exposure to violence eruption, random picks and injuries during distribution. Items distributed does not put families in to conflict within themselves Target groups including IDPs, women, shop keepers, PSN, etc are protected, rehabilitated and integrated within the communities without fear or stigma or harm. Both formal and traditional structures shall be utilized to this effect and agreed safety monitoring measures shall be initiated together with the members in the target areas including local authorities’ participation.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Beneficiaries are engaged throughout the intervention process. Actions are taken based on their various felt needs and recommendations. They are allowed to make choices freely without dictation on the use of the support provided to them. Views of each individuals and groups are valued and the communities are encouraged to participate in planning, implementation and evaluation processes. Women and girls, victims of traditional norms, rape, etc are allowed to express their feelings freely without interference at safe places.
