Project Summary

Samaritan’s Purse has been implementing multisectoral interventions in nutrition, health, and Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) in Mayendit, Mayom, and Maiwut counties in Unity and Upper Nile States, South Sudan. With over six years of operational presence in these areas, the organization is well-positioned to continue addressing the critical needs of conflict-affected populations. In alignment with the 2025 Humanitarian Response Plan, Samaritan’s Purse will scale up these interventions to meet the growing demand for nutrition services and improve resilience and nutrition outcomes among vulnerable populations.
The program aims to reach 52,710 conflict- and famine-affected individuals ([M: 25,828 F:26,882] [Host community: 34,262; Returnees; 14,813; IDPs: 3,635]) in Mayendit, Mayom, and Maiwut counties, including 27,510 children under five (U5s) and 27,510 primary caregivers, such as Pregnant and Breastfeeding Women and Girls (PBWG). Samaritan’s Purse will collaborate with the County Health Department (CHD), County Water Directorate (CWD), and other implementing partners in nutrition, health, and WASH to improve nutrition and build resilience in these populations. Additionally, the project will strengthen the capacity of 340 Care Group members, 70 Boma Healthcare Workers, and 44 skilled healthcare workers to implement high-impact nutrition interventions effectively.
The program will employ the Care Group (CG) model to deliver Social and Behavior Change Communication (SBCC) and promote optimal Maternal, Infant, and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) practices. Family-based Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) screenings will be conducted, alongside mass MUAC screenings and referrals for malnourished children under five and PBWG. Nutrition-sensitive activities will be integrated with internally funded agricultural programs to promote the availability of nutrient-rich foods. The program will also raise awareness of locally available food recipes and methods for nutritious food preparation.
In areas without nutrition treatment facilities, Samaritan’s Purse will implement the Tom Brown Supplementary Feeding Program to treat moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) in children under two. This program involves an eight-week feeding plan featuring porridge made from roasted grains, soybeans, and groundnuts. Routine malnutrition screenings and caregiver counseling will complement the intervention.
To ensure comprehensive support, the nutrition program will be implemented alongside health and WASH interventions in the same locations. Health services will be delivered through Mobile Medical Units (MMUs), Temporary Health Posts (THPs) where static facilities are unavailable, and community-based health initiatives. These services will include malnutrition screening and referrals, and healthcare workers will be trained in MIYCN and the management of acute malnutrition.
The WASH component will focus on improving access to clean water at nutrition sites and within communities. Sanitation facilities such will be constructed, and safe health and hygiene practices will be promoted to reduce the risk of disease. Additionally, children under five identified with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) will be provided with WASH Kits. Together, these integrated efforts will provide holistic support to the targeted populations, addressing their multifaceted needs effectively.

1. Needs

The nutrition situation in South Sudan is worsening, with an estimated 2.1 million children aged 6–59 months expected to experience elevated levels of acute malnutrition (AMN) between July 2024 and June 2025, including 650,000 cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). Additionally, approximately 1.11 million pregnant or breastfeeding women are anticipated to suffer from AMN during the same period. About 67% of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in five states, including Upper Nile and Unity, which together account for 26% of the total cases. Key drivers of malnutrition include high disease prevalence, restricted healthcare access, unsafe water, and poor sanitation, inadequate food consumption and poor infant and young child feeding practices. Despite exclusive breastfeeding rates exceeding 70% for children under two in both states, other indicators are critically low. According to the most recent IPC AMN classification, Unity and Upper Nile States are categorized as Critical and Severe AMN level respectively. In Upper Nile, Minimum Dietary Diversity (MDD) is 20.7%, Minimum Meal Frequency (MMF) 9.1%, and Minimum Acceptable Diet (MAD) just 3.9%. Similarly, in Unity, MDD is 20.3%, MMF 16%, and MAD 4.9%. Mayendit and Mayom Counties in Unity State, along with Maiwut County in Upper Nile State, currently have Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates of 22.2%, 22.0%, and 22.8%, respectively. Without urgent intervention, these areas are at risk of reaching critical acute malnutrition levels during the April–June 2025 lean season. To address this, sustained investments are essential in lifesaving nutrition support and preventive measures. These efforts should focus on improving complementary feeding practices and dietary intake for children and women of reproductive age (WRAs). Key components include social and behavior change communication (SBCC) combined with improved access to healthcare services, and water and sanitation facilities to enhance maternal and child nutrition.

2. Project activities

Samaritan’s Purse plans to implement targeted nutrition sector interventions to improve the nutritional well-being of populations in Mayendit, Mayom, and Maiwut Counties. The program will focus on increasing access to diverse diets and promoting better maternal, infant, and young child nutrition and care practices (MIYCN). These efforts will be integrated with health and WASH services to address the root causes of malnutrition, ensuring coordinated delivery of services to enhance health outcomes and resilience in the targeted areas.The nutrition intervention will leverage the Care Group (CG) model using Care Group (CG) members to facilitate Social and Behavior Change Communication (SBCC) for improved MIYCN-E practices. The CG Lead Mothers (LMs) and Lead Fathers (LFs) will receive biweekly training on exclusive breastfeeding (EBF), dietary diversity, food preparation, and mid-upper arm circumference (MUAC) screening for malnutrition. These trained individuals will conduct household visits, establish peer learning platforms, support family and mass MUAC screenings, and refer cases of malnutrition for treatment. They will also lead nutrition-sensitive activities such as kitchen gardening and cooking demonstrations. Additionally, participation in village savings and loans associations (VSLAs) will empower families to access nutritious food ingredients.Healthcare workers, CG members and Community Nutrition Volunteers or Boma Health Workers (BHW) will be trained in Community-Based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) and MIYCN practices. A quality improvement and verification checklist will be used to supervise CG members. For treating acute malnutrition, Samaritan’s Purse will identify and refer malnourished cases to existing nutrition partners such as UNIDOR, CARE International and RI, who provide these services in the three counties. In areas without nutrition treatment partners or facilities, Samaritan’s Purse will implement the Tom Brown Supplementary Feeding Program to treat moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) in children under five. This program includes an eight-week feeding plan using nutrient-rich porridge made from roasted grains, soybeans, and groundnuts. Routine MUAC monitoring and caregiver counseling will be conducted, along the feeding program.Additionally, Samaritan’s Purse will support mass MUAC screenings and collaborate with the Ministry of Health to implement biannual Vitamin A supplementation and deworming campaigns. These combined efforts aim to address both the immediate and underlying causes of malnutrition in the targeted counties.
