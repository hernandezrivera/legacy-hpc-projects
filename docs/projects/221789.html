Project Summary

This project proposes emergency humanitarian interventions that aim to save lives for conflict-affected populations in the districts of Muedumbe and Mueda. The focus of the project will be to support newly displaced populations (IDPs and Returnees) with essential services to access WASH, and when necessary, support to non-displaced populations.
It will also include rapid response activities, including distribution of hygiene supplies, transportation of emergency water, and installation of emergency latrines for displaced populations. WASH partners will coordinate with Shelter clusters to provide support through mechanisms such as the Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) and the Joint Response Plan (JRP). Each partner’s designated intervention zones will ensure a rapid response, while contingency funds in the work plans will prepare partners to meet emerging needs in different areas.
Hygiene and sanitation promotion will incorporate risk communication and community engagement (RCCE) activities to help prevent disease outbreaks, with a focus on gender-sensitive messaging aligned with the Gender, Protection Cluster and GBV Working Group guidelines.
Interventions will include support and provision of essential WASH supplies including hygiene kits (with menstrual hygiene management items), water treatment chemicals, handwashing stations and latrine signs in partnership with the government (INGD).

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

In the proposed districts (Mueda and Muedumbe), approximately 150,902 individuals in the areas affected by the armed conflict require emergency services for access to water, sanitation and hygiene, with a total of 100,751 people in Mueda district (51,023 returnees and 49,728 IDPs) and a total of 50,150 returnees in Muedumbe district. In these two districts, ASMOG aims to reach a total of 12,000 beneficiaries in the two districts. In Mueda the project will reach a total of 7,000 beneficiaries (2,000 IDPs and 5,000 returnees) and in Muedumbe district 5,000 beneficiaries, all returnees. When implementing the project, ASMOG will take special consideration of the most vulnerable groups, mostly women, girls, people with disabilities and the elderly. ASMOG proposes that interventions will include contributing to the achievement of the following results:Outcome 1: Increase access to drinking water in adequate quantity and quality (in accordance with WASH group standards). Outcome 2: Increase access to safe and inclusive sanitation facilities (in line with WASH standards) to save lives during the emergency Outcome3: Improve hygiene access to the affected population, through hygiene promotion, gender-sensitive emergency critical supplies and adequate handwashing facilities for personal hygiene.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Access to safe drinking water in adequate quantity and quality to save lives • Rehabilitation and improvement of existing water wells/boreholes by equipping them with hand pumps, cleaning, disinfestation, etc. • Construction of water holes equipped with hand pumps in areas where internally displaced families and returnees are concentrated • Distribution of water treatment products for domestic use, including training for households beneficiaries. Access to emergency sanitation to save lives • Promotion and construction of emergency community latrines for displaced populations/health facilities,• Promotion of SANTOLIC adapted to emergencies.• Urgent support to communities in the construction/rehabilitation of family latrines and temporary portable latrines, etc. Promoting hygiene to save lives in emergencies • Distribution of gender-sensitive emergency critical hygiene kits;• Construction of hand washing systems in public places (market, schools, hospitals, etc.);• Door-to-door awareness campaigns and public demonstrations on good hygiene practices;• Issuing public messages, radio listening sessions on good practices and personal hygiene standards

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Protection is essential to an inclusive WASH humanitarian response. In priority districts, between 2% and 11% of women report feeling unsafe when accessing WASH services, with this figure rising to 45% in Nangade. Women also face challenges in managing menstrual hygiene, with 30% resorting to unhygienic materials. WASH partners have integrated menstrual hygiene supplies into hygiene kits.In order to reduce the risk of protection for vulnerable people (women, elderly), the project will not only ensure privacy through the separation of male and female latrines and the inclusion of locks on doors but will also strive to ensure that the site does not increase risk. Specific needs will be addressed through consultations with these groups and the results of the discussions will inform the specific needs of project interventions to reduce or eliminate risk. Provision of menstrual hygiene items to vulnerable women and girls will be considered in the project implementation.In the intervention areas, ASMOG will ensure a rapid response to meet emerging needs in different affected areas, aiming to prevent conflict and gender-based violence by providing inclusive and gender-sensitive water, sanitation and hygiene services. Hygiene and sanitation promotion will incorporate risk communication and community engagement (RCCE) activities to help prevent disease outbreaks, with a focus on gender-sensitive messaging aligned with the Gender Working Group, Protection Cluster and GBV guidelines. ASMOG will also use the WASH Integration Checklist that was developed by the WASH and Protection Clusters.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Women and elderly empowerment activities, promoting a community-involved approach to preventing and mitigating Gender-Based Violence (GBV) and strengthening community knowledge. ASMOG will regularly make consultation sessions in all phase of the project development, with affected communities and key stakeholders to ensure that the programme is designed in a way that responds to the needs and preferences of the affected population, including during the project implementation and phase out to ensure project owenship and community empowerment. The programme design is intended to meet immediate life-saving needs. Post-distribution monitoring (PDM) and on-site distribution monitoring tools will be used to track beneficiary satisfaction with different aspects of the intervention. Monitoring data will be consolidated and analysed, improving ongoing programming and informing future response plans. ASMOG will adopt a participatory approach to ensure ownership of interventions at the target community level. Latrines will be constructed with wheelchair-accessible plastic latrines. with disabilities and will be separated by gender.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

In its interventions, ASMOG considers the inclusion of people with disabilities to be an absolute priority. In all interventions of this project, great attention will be given to the inclusion of means to eliminate barriers to access to water, hygiene and sanitation services for people with disabilities. In the process of mapping and assessing needs, together with the communities, standardized tools will be developed for the implementation of interventions dedicated to people with disabilities, in accordance with the WASH cluster standards.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

ASMOG recognizes that, to a minimum, the activities to be implemented may generate residual impacts on the environment in the following terms: a) Opening of new areas for the construction of sanitary latrines, water sources, etc;b) Extraction of flarestal products for the construction of family latrines based on local material;c) Introduction of non-degradable solid waste that can contribute to environmental pollution if disposed of inappropriately. Example: waste from bottles of water, menstrual hygiene kits, plastic packaging, etc. As part of the mitigation of these potential impacts, ASMOG will integrate pro-environmental initiatives at community level into its actions, aiming to raise awareness of good practices for the prevention and mitigation of these potential impacts. These initiatives include community cleaning campaigns, tree planting and reforestation, recycling and reusing damaged containers, promoting the final decomposition of organic material, among others.
