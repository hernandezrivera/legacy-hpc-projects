Project Summary

COVID-19 poses considerable risks to already vulnerable populations living in countries with severe development deficits, limited government capacity and, importantly, poor healthcare infrastructure. Communities dealing with malnutrition are now doubly vulnerable because of the pandemic. Since malnutrition can weaken the immune system, these populations may be more vulnerable to acquiring COVID-19.
Therefore, Plan International Nigeria proposing to implement 9-months project focusing on Improving the nutrition status of the children under-5, pregnant and lactating women and vulnerable group (PLWHA and elder people) amid COVID-19 pandemic & impact in the North East through increasing access to quality preventive & curative nutrition services and strengthening capacity government structure for the management of acute malnutrition in both Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) & Stabilization Centers (SC), community mobilization, maternal Infant and young child nutrition including MNP and Cash for nutrition distribution to most vulnerable group in selected LGAs in Yobe and Borno state. This will be done through screening & referral of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) children cases from the communities and IDP camps to OTP for treatment of uncomplicated SAM cases and referral of Complicated SAM cases form OTP to SC, promoting Optimal MIYCN(Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition), Micro-nutrient Powder (MNP) distribution, COVID-19-risk communication, SBCC on COVID-19, and distribution of COVID-19 Nutrition social palliative (conditional 4-months Cash Transfer) to the mothers/caregivers of SAM, PLWHA, and elders.

1 - Needs

According to the Nigeria 2019-2021 Humanitarian Response Strategy, a total of 7.1 million people including 2.3 million girls, 1.9 million boys, 1.6 million women and 1.3 million men are in need of humanitarian assistance in north-east Nigeria as an effect of the crisis in the past ten years. The on-going conflict in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states continues to undermine social stability and economic development due to the scale of displacement, disrupted livelihoods and diminished agricultural production, and has an especially severe impact on the food security and nutrition situation of women, children and vulnerable groups. The October 2019 Joint Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance (JNAFSS, 2019) conducted in BAY States revealed that the overall GAM and SAM rates in Borno state were at 8.1 % and 0.9% respectivelyThe COVID-19 crisis in Borno state have further increase the impact of food insecurity on the households and met with a fractured healthcare system including disrupted nutrition and health system in many inaccessible LGAs, non-functional health facilities, lack of health care staff, overwhelmed response capacity of nutrition partners and lack of resources. A tightening of pre-existing movement restrictions, economic deterioration coupled with strained public services will further increase nutrition needs of children under five years and that of the entire households compound existing operational challenges.Before the emergence of COVID-19, 35 % of health facilities in the affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe were damaged as a result of conflict. There has also been significant disruption of OTP& SC services, vaccination campaigns and other essential health services for children and other vulnerable groups in inaccessible areas. In addition, dwindling funding has been a major challenge, (WHO,2020). To break the cycle of malnutrition in the Yobe and Borno states, this proposed project will support the delivery of COVID-19 integrated and comprehensive package of preventive and curative nutrition services, (Nut Sector COVID-19 RP, 2020). Interventions for treatment of severely malnourished children (SAM) will be implemented in the three-LGAs across the two states, treatment and management of moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) for children under five with MNPs, COVID-19 nutrition–social palliative (unconditional cash transfer of 3-months) for mothers/caregivers of SAM, pregnant and breastfeeding women and promotion of appropriate Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) and COVID-19 risk communication will be prioritized for IDPs in the camps and host communities, returnees and the most affected portion of host communities.

2 - Activities

- Community mobilization on COVID-19 - capacity building of health facilities and health care workers on Covid-19 prevention and response through training, equipping with IPC and PPE - distribution of cash to the most venerable people like female headed house hold, household with SAM children, elders, people with underlying medical condition and etc- Maternal, infant young child nutrition focusing on PLW and children under five.- complicated and uncomplicated SAM children treatment - Management of moderate acute malnutrition - nutrition for elder people. - distribution of hygiene kit for care taker of SAM children
