Project Summary

The proposed project aims at improving access to quality, affordable and comprehensive primary health services for vulnerable populations in Lebanon. Given the current deteriorating economic situation in Lebanon – vulnerable populations have become more at risk of being unable to access healthcare in Lebanon. These populations include Lebanese, Palestinian refugees residing in Lebanon, and migrants that are becoming increasingly unable to afford or access healthcare in Lebanon.
For health activities, IMC will work with 4 PHCCs: Al Moasat PHCC (South), Quabit Medical center (Akkar), Nahda Bedawi (Tripoli) and Nafelaa PHCC (Bekaa). These centers have been selected due to the high density of vulnerable Lebanese as well as a considerable size of migrant populations in the areas. The support to these four PHCCs will include: increasing the hours of operations for PHCCs by four hours a day, five days a week by providing salary support for physicians, nurses, data entry clerks, and other personnel and running costs. Secondly, donating essential acute medications as per the Ministry of Public health (MoPH) essential medication list, and lastly, IMC will be providing PPEs for the healthcare providers at the PHCCs in order to be able to provide high-quality services in a safe manner for both the health staff and the beneficiary. IMC will be recruiting and training Lebanese Health Outreach Volunteers (HOVs) from the target population in order to disseminate and provide health awareness on IMC services to vulnerable Lebanese communities and migrants - as well as identify cases requiring primary healthcare and referring them back to the PHCC. The outreach targeting migrant workers will use specific translated IEC materials and coordination with the migrant sector to identify migrants and reach them.
Lebanon’s multiple crises have contributed to fracturing its already fragile health care system. The medication shortage has exacerbated the situation and health care has become financially inaccessible for most. Vulnerable populations face multiple socio-economic challenges, including unemployment, poor living conditions, and the inability to cover the cost of both transportation and MHPSS services required to treat their mental health conditions. Shortages in skilled health care providers of MHPSS, as well as lack of awareness about where and how to seek services further exacerbate access challenges. This is compounded by the multiple crises, which have seen an increase in people seeking MHPSS services. IMC MH case management teams constituting of case managers, psychotherapists and psychiatrists are based in PHCCs to support provision of MHPSS consultations through an integrated approach and in line with the MH LPSP packages to be finalized following the piloting phase. IMC will work in two PHCCs, Cedars Medical Association – Cedars Medical Center in Mount Lebanon and Deir El Ahmar PHCC in Bekaa to support the increase in the number of vulnerable Lebanese and migrants reaching out for MHPSS interventions.
IMC is planning to procure acute and psychotropic medication which will be donated to the PHCCs. IMC will coordinate with MoPH to complement quantities and try to plug gaps in the MOPH supply chain. IMC will run a detailed assessment, quantities will be shared with MoPH for coordination of numbers and specifying the number of PHCCs to be covered as well as the duration of coverage and timeframe. At the PHCC level, medications will be dispensed free of charge and only upon medical prescriptions.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

IMC will procure acute medication (3,240) and psychotropic medications (1,640) from the local and international markets to be donated to PHCCs. - Salary support for physicians, nurses, and running cost to allow the 4 PHCCs to operate for additional hours beyond their existing schedule. - Patients will benefit from subsidized consultation and will receive medication free of charge. If the patient requires diagnostics (Lab/Imagery) then he/she will be covered under other projects. - Two Health Outreach Volunteers will be recruited from each targeted area (eight in total). HOVs will be trained on key health messages and will provide health awareness in the community and refer cases back to PHCCs. - The community health club aims to raise awareness around positive health and wellbeing through a series of awareness sessions and activities conducted at the field level to 2,880 individuals among Lebanese, refugees and migrant workers within PHCCs catchment areas. The outreach for migrant workers will use specialized translated IEC materials and coordination with the migrant workers sector. – Support to PHCCs to implement MH integration model in line with the MH LPSP approach while focusing on adequate management (including prescription of psychotropic medications) and referrals between trained General Practitioners (GPs) and MH consultants.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

IMC pays attention to ensure that all groups can access to health and mental health services at all of its program sites. At health facilities, IMC circulated new guidelines in 2018 to support protection of beneficiaries’ privacy and rights, particularly for women. As part of its safeguarding policy, IMC developed a safeguarding/protection risk assessment and mitigation measures in order to ensure that all its facilities and programs ensure meaningful access, accountability, participation, and safety of the community members, as well as respect of the do no harm principle. Staff are prone to experience burnout among other stressors as they are sharing the same challenges with the people they are providing services to. IMC aims on extending the already established staff care project, by covering a percentage of the staff care consultant providing individual and group support to IMC staff. Allowing the organization to better respond to the needs of the staff for a longer duration.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

IMC Lebanon employs mechanisms and approaches in order to ensure the participation of the affected communities throughout the program cycle. To ensure beneficiaries’ feedback is received on a regular basis, the MEAL department conducts regular patient satisfaction surveys and post-distribution monitoring. Program teams conduct FGDs throughout program implementation in order to collect beneficiaries’ feedback concerning project. IMC Lebanon is currently implementing a Community Based Feedback and Response Mechanism (CBFRM), which is a set of clear, transparent procedures that provide affected communities with access to safe, timely and confidential means of voicing complaints related to IMC-supported services and activities. A series of focus group discussions were conducted with male and female beneficiaries of different nationalities across all areas in Lebanon. Moreover, FGDs were conducted separately for male and female beneficiaries to design the CBFRM based on different needs.
