Project Summary

The project is aiming to provide formal and non-formal education, to IDPs and orphans in NWS, through operating 5 schools in Idleb and the Northern Rural of Aleppo.
The project will 100% targeted subdistricts with high severity, 1 subdistrict with severity 4, and 2 with severity 5.
The first four schools will be in non-formal camps to provide non-formal education and will use UNICEF curriculum type B.
While the 5th schools will be specified for orphans and it will be located in Albab city. the 5ht schools will provide both non-formal and formal education according to the student's situation.
The school will receive both girls and boys equally, and will also encourage families to send their girls and students with disabilities to school, the estimated number of BNGs with disabilities is around 10%.
The orphan school will be part of a bigger orphan project for Turkish NGOs, called "the new step", and we will accept orphans out of schools, and provide them with NFE (ALP), and those who were already part of the FE, and will provide them with FE. We will also provide STEM education, programming, robotics, and applied science in Orphans school. MAPs have 8 years of experience in providing this type of education to the most vulnerable people, and we gain multiple international achievements that reflects in a very high self-confident and self-esteem in students. we would like to pilot this with Orphan school now, in addition it will a window for gifted students to advance their knowladge. we are using MAPs curriculum that been developed during 8 years with collabiration with many NGO/INGO and private spicliaist companies.
The 4 non-formal education school is already funded, while the 5th school for the orphan is not yet.
All teachers were undergo on a weekly training session through out the year, inlcuding teaching methodilies, teaching skills, traing based on evaulation reports.
The project will also provide a set of training that will help the resilience of youth and will be as followed:
1- Robotic Coding training.
2- Programming languages training.
3- Media content & design.
4- Digital marketing.
5- Freelance jobs.
This training will target no less than 1500 BNFs, we are targeting youth in 15-24 of age, in the same previously mentioned regions, in NWS. This training will be followed by an internship with either NGOs offices inside Syria, as volunteers and/or private sector if available, all the training and the internship will be paid with small incentives.
All this activties are in line with 1st education sector activties in sacling up safe and equitable access to formal and non-formal education aged (3-17) years.
Throught the implemntation a full M&E plan will be implemented, through, annyynmous tests for studnets, FGDs with parents and staff as well, complaints and suggestion boxes, on-site visits, daily reports for absence, homeworks done, and learned objectives. pre-and post training evaluation. etc.
MAPs has an etesnive expreince in Lebanon, to provide high quatility education including innovative education and gained 35 international awards in robotic for Syrian refugees, including the world champoinship title in Dubai 2019. and has it's evidence based experince of integeration of some of these acitvites in NFE, and would like t start this year to reaplicate the expeince for NWS.
MAPs till now secured half of the requested costs and working to secure the rest.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2021, it's estimated that in 2020, 2.45 million children were out of school and 1.6 million children were at risk of dropping out due to the cumulative effect of the conflict, the closure of learning facilities, and/or reduced learning hours due to COVID-19, worsening economic conditions, increased population numbers and displacement, the numbers of children out of school, and children who are at risk of dropping out, almost certain to increase in 2021.94% of school-aged children are living in areas with severe, extreme or catastrophic education conditionsmainly concentrated in Aleppo, Idleb and Rural Damascus governorates, an increase to last year’s 86%. Through the project we will targeted areas with high severity 4 and 5 to improve the situation. According to our data, there are no less than 25 camps in areas where we are operating they needs access to schools, and there are about 2900 children's out of schools, we targeted part of the camps and are aiming to teach 1000 students. in addition there're around 2600 orphan families in Albab city and around 9,600 orphans. Children with disabilities are less likely to access learning. Insufficient investment in learning facilities, specialized teachers and materials to provide adequate support to children. Around 10% of the children are CwD, the percentage is higher in Idleb than Northern Aleppo. MAPs will make sure to facilitate the access of CwD to the schools, including their WASH needs. students and school personnel with psychological distress need access to appropriate services within their schools or through referrals to specialized service. while there is a sharp drop in attendance for adolescents

5 - Implementation of activities

The project is aiming to operate 4 non-formal schools and one combined formal and non-formal school specified for orphans, as they considered as one of the most vulnerable groups. the NFE provided is mainly ALP 4 out of the 5 schools are already operating in non-formal camps in idle and another Aleppo. while the The 4 schools started with the beginning of 2021 to provide a full academic year, with some summer activities, while the 5th for orphans are in establishing phase in Albab city and it should start in July 2021. All the schools will provide non-formal education, while the orphan schools will provide also formal education according to ASERassessment that will be done to check the educational level of the students, part of the orphans were out of school while the rest were not. an innovative education will also be teaches besides the normal curriculum, some students will have afterschool classes for attending innovative learning in laboratories for applied science, and robotics, as part of STEM education curriculum, MAPs through the last 8 years with the help of NGOs/INGOs and private sector established a STEM curriculum and was teaches through years to refugees children in Lebanon, the dropping out ratio was much lesser when provided this education. we are targeting children from 9-15 years of age. All teachers will attend weekly training to improve their capacities in order to provide higher quality education. according to the regulation MAPs will provide either distance learning or physical learning due to Covid, all measures were be taken to reduce the pandemic according to the cluster recommendations.An M&E plan also will be implemented including on-site visits, anonymous tests, suggestion boxes.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The risk is Violence against education facilities and education, likelihood: Medium, MM: reduce the risk of an attack learning facilities can be deconflicted using the deconfliction mechanism, Learning facilities have contingency safety plans and safety protocols in place, Contingency safety plans, and safety protocols, regularly practiced by students, teachers, and education personnel. The risk is Violence, physical punishment, or bullying in schools, likelihood: High, MM: All learning facilities have an enforced code of conduct, and all teachers signed the code of conduct. and teachers and school personnel will be trained on the prevention and reporting of violence, and students and their families are sensitized to the code of conduct and related prevention. and the system is in place for supervision during the teaching classes. Risk of SEA or GBV can be noticed during the implementation of the teaching process, likelihood: medium, Mitigation measures: ensure that all the staff is oriented and attended MAPs policies for PSEA, and all the staff signed a code of conduct containing a clause for PSEA, and this is well explained as part of the recruitment process, and make sure that the PSEA compliant system is in a place and are effective and rapidly respond to any abuse.

7 - Does the project include a PSEA strategy ?

Yes

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

The risk of PSEA is existed mainly between the teachers and students, without mentioning the risk between the project administration with teachers and school administration themselves, the likelihood is medium. the mitigation is the presence of PSEA policy and its mainstreaming in all other policies and administrative procedures. the training of the MEAL staff to deal with this kind of issues in the field, the orientation sessions, and the refreshment training that conducted during the project lifetime, in addition to the strong compliant and feedback mechanism that MAPs will include to be notified ASAP when any PSEA accident happened, including engaging senior-level management to react. and finally the singing of all staff on the code of conduct that has a PSEA clause included and explained for each staff. in addition to making awareness sessions to the students' parents, and with additional focus on children and orients them to complaint if they faced any abnormal movement.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The risk of threats on safety and security from bombing or airstrike, likelihood: low, MM: 1)MAPs will sure to do a security risk assessment before starting the project activities and will make sure to implement the activities in a safe and secure area. 2) in case of any threats part of the activities will be conducted as virtually as possible, while the rest will be postponed till the situation is clear and secure. the risk of stopping the activities due to new Covid-19 regulations, likelihood: high, MM: MAPs will also have a plan converting for distance learning if it was required in the new regulations, in addition, to take care of all safety procedures like masks, social distancing, place capacity, etc.
