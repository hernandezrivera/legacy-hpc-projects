Project Summary

NGO will deliver Integrated Protection services through NGO’s Women’s Protection and
Empowerment, Child Protection and Protection Rule of Law programs and through
partnerships with LNGOs, reaching 50,000 vulnerable conflict-affected individuals in
Raqqa, Hasakeh and Deir ez Zor governorates.
By working through an integrated approach, NGO aims to provide a comprehensive
response to the protection needs of all community members, including children, adults
and persons with specific vulnerabilities such as the elderly and PwD. Through this
project, to provide people who have been harmed or otherwise exposed to violence with
the opportunity to recover, the NGO and partners will provide and/or connect individuals to
services that protect them (case management services, legal awareness, appropriate care
services), can promote their healing (case management services, psychosocial services
and referral to health services), and help them to become less vulnerable to harm in the
future (eg. appropriate care services, civil documentation information services, economic
services). By establishing Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSS) and Safe Healing and
Learning Spaces (SHLS) combined with outreach teams, the NGO and partners will provide
specialized case management services for GBV survivors and children who have been
harmed by abuse, neglect and exploitation, including supporting CAAFAG children with
CPCM and reintegration in the community, provide cash assistance to meet immediate
needs, provide structured and unstructured PSS services, parenting skills training for
caregivers, and life skills education for boys and girls.
Throughout the implementation of this project, NGO will regularly monitor the violation of
rights and the protection situation in NES to understand the changes overtime, to screen
and improve access to primary and specialized services for those that wouldn’t otherwise
seek services, and advocate on their behalf to improve the protection situation in their
communities and inform response planning. Regular assessments and legal analysis on
civil housing and property rights will inform legal support, programming and advocacy.
Protection mainstreaming will be within protection activities and other NGO sectors’ activities to ensure safety, access, participation, and accountability. Particular attention will be given to accessibility and full inclusion for people with disabilities. People with disability are specifically targeted through specific activities (case management, PSS and distributions) and included in all other activities (GBV and CP). NGO spaces are accessible to them and the services are provided in an inclusive way, for example with the support of a sign language interpreter or having consent forms and other materials in Braille.
This integrated approach sits within a multi-sector response provided with NGO’s Health
and Economic Recovery and Development programs, with all three sectors – where NGO Integrated Protection activities are delivered through multiple modalities, including
through a center-based approach (e.g. WGSS or SHLS), through mobile teams (e.g. the
Protection Rapid Response mechanism) and through community based interventions
(such as CP committees and women led-committees).
NGO has established strong MEAL systems that combine regular collection, analysis and reporting of data with quality control and accountability components. Data on outcomes for individual case management and psychological support will be tracked using survivor satisfaction surveys, while data on outcomes for psychosocial support and life-skills activities will be collected via pre/post tests and semi-structured feedback sessions. Furthermore, NGO will employ its Client Responsiveness (CR) Mechanisms for both clients and the broader community to communicate needs, concerns or feedback regarding the program. All data gathered will be triangulated to maximize knowledge about implementation and to achieve confidence in evidence.

4 - Needs

In its tenth year of protracted emergency, with 13.13 million people affected and 11million people in immediate need of humanitarian assistance, protection remains one oflargest sectoral needs across in Syria. According to 2021 HNO, different forms of GBVcontinues to disproportionally affect the lives of women and girls in Syria, in their homesand communities. In addition to the armed conflict and the displacement, the COVID-19outbreak has negatively impacted on the safety and the wellbeing of women and girls,increasing their economic vulnerability, their exposure to exploitation, increase in theirpsychological distress and exposure to the risk of Intimate Partner Violence. Adolescentgirls, widowed/divorced women, women and girls with disabilities and older women aremost often taking the exposed to a multitude of GBV risks, resulting in few places wherethey feel safe and supported. Children are subject to grave violations of their rights.According to the HNO 2021, separation from caregivers and recruitment and use ofchildren by parties to the conflict continues to be described as a persistent CP issue inSyria and psychosocial distress among children is reported in 23 per cent of surveyedhouseholds demonstrating the cumulative toll on mental well-being with immediate and ifnot addressed, lifelong consequences. The lack of civil status documentation (CSD) isanother serious protection concern affecting displaced population in NES and hinderingtheir access to rights. It has disproportionate effects on women and girls because of legaland social practices that relate many rights to their documented relationship to malefamily members.

5 - Implementation of activities

NGO’s Integrated Protection program will deliver activities across all subsectors through WPE, Child Protection and Protection Rule of Law teams in Raqqa, Hasakeh and Deir ez Zor governorates and through partnerships with LNGOs. Through an integrated approach, NGO responds to the protection needs of all community members, including children, adults and persons with specific vulnerabilities such as the elderly and PwD. Child protection activities will include: case management (including for CAAFAG), referrals to specialised services, PSS, Social and Emotional learning, parental training, awareness sessions, CP Committees. GBV activities will include: case management, referrals to specialised services, individual support sessions, structured and unstructured PSS, information and awareness sessions, support to women-led committees. Protection activities will include: case management for PWSN, referrals to other specialised services, info desk on available services, PSS for men, boys and caregivers of PWDs, protection monitoring and production of protection monitoring report, legal awareness and legal consultations. Cash for protection will be integrated to provide cash support to cases identified through case management to address protection needs.NGO activities are delivered through multiple modalities: static programming allows for sustained delivery of protection services, key for those with complex protection needs who require individual services delivered over a longer period. Mobile teams permit NGO to flexibly respond to different contexts and operating environments and adapt to emerging needs. Community-based interventions support in the sustainability of NGO's protection programs and self-protection.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Risk: Risk of sexual exploitation and abuse and sexual harassment of women and girls clients by staff and volunteers. Likelihood: Medium/Low Impact: Very HighMitigation: IEC materials for beneficiaries explaining reporting channels forbeneficiaries to report PSEA and ASH. NGO adheres to a strong internal code ofconduct related to prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) andsafeguarding beneficiaries, especially children, as well as a safe environment forreporting that allows for anonymity. Each year, all NGO staff attend a full day NGOWay Code of Conduct Training that includes discussions on NGO’s commitments tobeneficiaries and NGO’s zero tolerance for sexual misconduct. NGO will work withpartners utilizing their existing beneficiary mechanisms to establish commonstandards for beneficiary feedback and ensure protection from sexual exploitationand abuse and accountability to beneficiaries and other stakeholders. This willinclude capacity building activities with partners to reach these standards asnecessary.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Change of context and limited access to the supported sites. Likelihood: MediumImpact: High Mitigation: External risk. Access situation, maintaining link with localstakeholders and facilitate negotiations through the NGO’s Humanitarian Access team. TheNGO is recruiting local staff from targeted communities who will be able to run facilitiesand provide the essential live saving services. Risk: Security conditions and COVID-19disrupt activities and limit client access to sites. Likelihood: Medium Impact: HighMitigation: Project activities allow for adaptive management in line with internalcontingency plans related to each operational area. Ongoing security planning during theproject will ensure the activities can continue to adapt to the security environment andaccess context, with Humanitarian Access Teams providing regular security assessments. During periodic access constraints, NGO will strengthen implementation through community based focal points. Remote service modalities will also be used as needed,building from NGO’s experience in 2020.
