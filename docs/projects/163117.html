Project Summary


In the midst of changing crisis environment in the NW/SW regions, the provision of basic education services to an estimate of 3,000 children affected by the Anglophone crisis in Mawoh and Makoka communities in Limbe is mandatory. More than 50% out of school IDP aged 3-17 years (boys and girls) in the Mawoh/Makoka communities cannot access quality formal or non-formal education. Although CAMHELP in collaboration with individual members of the Mawoh / Makoka community have contributed to erect a semi-unfinished wooden infrastructure for children aged 3-17 years to study with severe need for such education services during the crisis, most of the children do not have access to formal and non-formal education in the upper part of the community called upper Mawoh. Both out of school IDPs and children of the host communities lack critical access to educational facilities which could permit the children study in an appropriate environment. More than 25% out of school host and IDP children sit on the ground to write and study in an exposed environment. The lone wooden unfinished Government Primary School built located in this environment has no benches for children to sit, School teachers lack tables and chairs for teaching and there are little or no prospects for the out of school IDPs and host communities aged 3 – 17 years to access quality formal or non-formal education. The humanitarian consequences are effects of stresses and shocks on the lives and livelihoods of the affected population who have stayed for 3 years out of school due to the crisis in the Anglophone regions. Moreover, the Humanitarian consequences cuts across other sectors such as very poor access to water and prevalence of Gender-Based violence (GBV).
From the pictures collected during field operations by CAMHELP, we discovered that’over 10,000 inhabitants living in Mawoh and Makoka communities lack access to clean water, sanitation, and hygiene, and are exposed highly to GBV within their localities. Although education is a priority for the Cameroon government, there is a very high percentage (25% - 50%) of children (3-17 years) in the Mawoh and Makoka communities who lack access to basic educational facilities, clean water, sanitation, and hygiene. These Mawoh / Makoka communities drink polluted and untreated water from running streams filled with bacteria and water-borne diseases. Both education, and GBV fall under the Humanitarian Consequences called – LIVING STANDARDS.
More than 25% households in these localities have no access to clean water source. Access rate to latrines is also very poor leading to open defecation in the stream where children aged 3- 17 years drink during and after school periods.
At the time this project was written, these communities are highly exposed to epidemic outbreak of diseases such as cholera, typhoid, and water borne-diseases in their communities. Field work (see appendices) shows that many cases of cholera, typhoid, hook worm, tape worms, diarrhea and dysentery have been registered, with dead, occurring from these water borne diseases within these hard-to-reach areas in Limbe, South West region.
The outbreak of protracted crisis in the NW/SW regions has lead to high influx of IDPs in these Mawoh and Makoka communities where Camwater has not yet provided clean pipe borne water to these populations in Limbe. Unfortunately, majority of these IDPs are women (pregnant women), children under 5 years, youths and disabled persons.
The purpose of this project is to improve educational infrastructures for basic education (nursery and primary schools), and intervene to reduce GBV/SGBV cases in Mawoh/Makoka communities of Limbe.
The essential of basic education for children during emergency is a fundamental human right. Children are able to receive lifesaving messages in health, protection, and nutrition.

Besoins / Needs

Needs Assessment;-improve access to quality basic formal and non-formal educational opportunities for the host and crisis affected girls and boys aged 3-17 years within a safe environment in the locality. -increase access to clean water supply, sanitation, and hygiene to the IDPs and the host communities (young girls; women; boys, and adolescence). - strengthen community awareness on the 8th category of GBV types. -increase psychosocial support for GBV/SGBV cases. -provide 3000 learners and teachers with learning and teaching materials. -strengthen and protect classrooms with adequate basic WASH facilities for both formal and non-formal education, targeting 3000 children. -empower communities through school management committees to protect education and assist in the establishment and maintenance of child-friendly school environments; -reinforce GBV vocational centres with different training skills.
