Project Summary


SKUS worked as the operational partner of KNH-Germany’s partner AMURT in the Rohingya response in the field of Child Protection in the Cox Bazar district since January 2018. In September 2019 SKUS became a partner of KNH-Germany by the model Multi-service hub in camps 16 and 17. In these three years, SKUS developed significant capabilities in child protection, psycho-social support, non-formal education, and community mobilization & outreach activities.
As a partner organization of CPSS, SKUS has been coordinating with CPSS from the beginning of its intervention. SKUS is the child protection focal point (CPFP) of camp-17 and assigned a full-time staff to play the CPFP role. SKUS is the first-ever organization among CPSS partners who implemented the child protection quality benchmark exercise in camp-17 with other CP partners. SKUS is the organization to successfully complete a 90% scoring of the child protection quality benchmark in camp-17 among 34 camps. SKUS is also the first organization to organize an Interactive Popular Theater (IPT) show with Rohingya children as part of the children's lead child protection campaign. This campaign was appreciated by the Rohingya community people.
Over the last one year of PSS services, the community in the catchment area of our three multi-service centers are more aware and concerned about positive parenting and the PSS needs of their children and themselves.
SKUS has developed a facilitators guidebook for PSS, Child Rights Module (CRM) for 8-12 years’ age children and conduct a pilot exercise and submit a report to the education sector for sector endorsement.
Considering previous experience, SKUS is proposing a child protection project both in Rohingya camps and in the host community. The project is prepared based on the protection objectives of JRP. The project aims to address the community-based protection approach, capacity development of adolescents, and multi-sectoral integration.
The project will develop the capacity of CBCPC members on protection issues that members could take their action plan to prevent and protect children in the community. The project will also support to organize awareness campaign.
The project will work to develop the capacity of adolescents’ boys and girls on child rights, child protection, SRH that they can urge their rights. The project will also support to adolescents to organize campaign on child marriage, child labor, child abuse. The project will also provide pre-vocational training for adolescents to develop their skills.
The project will conduct positive parenting sessions with caregivers that they can do positive parenting for their children's wellbeing. The project will provide MHPSS service to the community.
The project aims to provide positive discipline and non-violent teaching training to different organization’s teachers that they could able to establish a corporal-punishment free teaching environment in learning centers and children become protected.
SKUS will emphasize the inclusion of children with disabilities. SKUS has trained staff in disability inclusion.
Younger children are more vulnerable in every emergency. So SKUS has the contingency plan to help the young children in emergency.
As COVID-19 pandemic still continuing over the Bangladesh, the project will address the issue through community led approach. According to the SKUS own survey findings, it was found that Rohingya community people more rely on their community leaders (educated and intellectual persons among Rohingya community). So, the project aims to cover Rohingya community people through this types of community leaders.
Rohingya camp-17 and 8W are mostly hilly are and vulnerable regarding land slide, flood and cyclone. So, the project will address DRR issues in community led approach.
SKUS aims to implement the project under the CPSS mechanism. SKUS will maintain PSEA principles along with own gender policy to create gender friendly environment in the office.

1 - Identified Needs

Since August 2017, over 710,000 Rohingya refugees have arrived in Bangladesh’s Cox’s Bazar District fleeing military operations characterized by widespread reports of human rights violations in Myanmar. Over 860,000 Rohingya refugees are living in 34 camp settlements in Ukhiya and Teknaf and, of these, 55% are children.Humanitarian crises, including natural disasters and complex emergencies, compromise children’s rights to survival, development, and protection. According to COVID 19, Child Protection Situation Monitoring Survey the top child protection concern in the Rohingya camps are emotional abuse, followed by psychosocial distress, child labor and physical abuse.Socio-cultural practices, such as purdah, have restricted the mobility of women and adolescent girls and their access to assistance, information, services and opportunities for community representation and self-reliance in the campsSKUS own surveys from April - July 20204 found that a. Vulnerability of children and youth [in camps]: From both education and protection perspectives, children and youth continue to remain vulnerable regarding safety, security and access to education. Overall, the majority of the respondents agree that risks to children’s safety have increased in recent months [after the start of the pandemic].b. Mainstreaming psychosocial support in local [host community] communities is crucial, due to the COVID-19 situation, helping the community with culturally acceptable methods of dealing with personal mental health.c. There is a need to Increase inclusive and participatory interventions for people living with disabilities.Due to the social and cultural as well as religious conservativeness and habituate unhealthy practice the children are at risk of safety and protection. The restriction of movement, camp boundary with wire, and restriction of protection work due to COVID-19, the vulnerability of children, women, and adolescent girls and make their safety and dignity badly affected. The project will identify the vulnerable people and take initiative accordingly especially for the special needs children and their rights. SKUS will sensitize the community about the rights of the special needs children, adolescent girls rights, access to education in learning center by the awareness session and campaign, individual and small session, counseling, etc. Early marriage, child marriage, forced marriage, child abuse and neglect, and child labor are com-mon phenomena which can be traced back to poverty and socio-cultural practices. The UN's multisector needs assessment (MSNA 2019) reports that child trafficking has increased in the population's perception. The hopeless situation, insecurity, and lack of prospects make people restless, frustrated, and lead to anti-social behavior and escape attempts.Adolescent girls are extremely difficult to reach and have no rights of self-determination due to socio-cultural stigmatization. Girls in adolescence are expected to stay at home. The sexual and gender-specific violence that Rohingya women and girls have experienced as a result of conflicts and mass expulsions is well documented. While the humanitarian emergency exacerbates violence against women and girls, the underlying causes are attitudes, beliefs, norms, and structures that promote or tolerate gender discrimination even in times of stability. Several studies show that violence through intimate partners and child marriage within the Rohingya community is deeply rooted and normalized in Cox's bazaar. Economic pressure, cramped living conditions, men's frustration with the lack of employment opportunities, and the lack of a legal structure in the camps have led to an increase in gender-specific violence.
