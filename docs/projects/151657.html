Needs

The protection crisis in Somalia continues to be characterized by a multi faceted armed conflict and is compounded by the persistent drought, constrained effective governance, eroded resilience and economic vulnerability of families and communities, and new as well as protracted displacement often resulting in undignified living circumstances with severe protection implications for affected men, women, and children. Highly localized conflict dynamics, consisting of inter communal rivalries and competition, including regarding access to land and political power, further compound the conflict, displacement, and overall protection situation.Gender based violence affecting women and girls remains underreported but widespread, with IDPs remaining extra vulnerable (96 per cent of GBV survivors who accessed services in 2016 were female; 76 per cent IDPs). Data indicates that GBV occurs while in transit to IDP sites, while exposure is especially high in IDP sites, for example during water collection after dark or in shelters that offer little physical protection. IDPs from minority clans are reported to be more frequently affected, allegedly due to outsiders intruding into IDP sites. GBV cases related to intimate partner violence, especially domestic violence and deprivation of resources, continue to be the most reported type of GBV. Gaps exist in basic service provision for survivors, including post rape care, psychosocial support, and comprehensive survivor centered care, including case management.The crisis has serious consequences for children and the protection of their rights, leading to abuse, violence, neglect, and exploitation. Key risks for children are family separation and child recruitment, arbitrary arrest, an elevated exposure to GBV including early marriage, trafficking, psychosocial distress, a lack of access to education, and hazardous child labour. Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Most IDPs protection will be improved and the safety access of their location of and their access routes to child friendly spaces, women spaces and social centres pose no safety risks to children and social workers,SOVA will identify safe residential locations to its beneficiaries and avoid placing facilities near military installations and conflict front lines and will Keep in mind the distances beneficiaries have to travel and their mode of transport. Consider ways to reduce threats – introduce a buddy system, advocate for community watch, arrange for appropriate policing in all target locations.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

SOVA will Collect and use data disaggregated by age, gender and diversity to effectively inform programming and will ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable - Identify and prioritise the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalised groups,SOVA will ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable - Identify and prioritise the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalised groups,Vulnerable persons are exposed to a combination of risks due to their vulnerability and typically have less capacity to withstand the impact of a crisis. Vulnerable persons therefore experience a cycle of vulnerability and poverty, which limits their access to assistance and services and exposes them to protection concerns, such as isolation, exploitation and abuse. Prioritising the most vulnerable groups in SOVA protection programming will reinforce the protection element of providing assistance based on need and without discrimination and ensures vulnerable persons are not neglected or deprived from humanitarian assistance during and after its implemention of protection project in Gedo and middle Juba .

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

SOVA will identify safe residential locations and avoid placing facilities near military installations and conflict front lines,wil consider safety risks associated with landmines, explosive remnants of war (ERW), busy roads, exposed wires and dangerous materials on the ground,freelance millias,looters and where potentiality of force eviction may occur.
