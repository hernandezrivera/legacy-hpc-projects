Project Summary

The proposed response plan seeks to contribute to provision of quality health care to crisis-affected people, prevent and respond to health threats, and strengthen the health system in need in Uror, and Nyirol (Jonglei state) with intersectoral priorities 1 and 2 respectivesly and cluster severity of 4 for both counties , through the implementation of CO1#: Improve equitable access to life-saving essential quality health care services including maternal, child, and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment of common illnesses, disability, MHPSS, and GBV health services; CO#2: Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases, and CO#3: Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms. YAH’s 2025 HRP aligns with its strategic goal to contribute to reduction of mortality and morbidity rate through quality health care services to communities. This also aligns well with 2025 cluster HRP objectives 1. Improve equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations. 2 Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases and 3. Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms.
In response to the arising Health needs, YAH will focus on the following services including maternal, child, adolescent, sexual and reproductive health, common and chronic diseases, neglected tropical diseases, MHPSS (Mental Health and Psychosocial support) and GBV (Gender Based Violence). The response will also improve sexual reproductive health services of EmONC (emergency newborn and obstetric care), family planning, PMTCT (prevention of mother to child transmission), Clinical Management of Rape and supplies for vulnerable women and girls. The response will use referral mechanism to enhance efficiency, integration, and quality of care. The response will follow Universal Health Coverage principles and prioritize prevention, preparedness, response, and mitigation for vulnerable groups. YAH will also continue to respond to multiple and concurrent disease outbreaks, including measles, yellow fever, poliovirus, meningitis, hepatitis E and cholera prevention, preparedness, and readiness activities, as South Sudan faces the threat of all these outbreaks. The response will support health facilities and community-based systems to serve people in need. The response will involve strengthening coordination at national and subnational levels through joining Technical Working Groups and a multi-sectoral approach, integrating efforts with other clusters like Nutrition, WASH, Protection, Education and FSL. YAH will also strengthen its technical, operational, and field coordination with other partners and local authorities.
The response will also support disease surveillance and reporting system and preposition medical supplies and equipment in Uror and Nyirol conties in Payams hosting Returnees and IDPs affected by Sudan Humanitarian Crisis, inter comminual conflicts and other natural disasters inlcuding floods.
YAH’s response will be aligned with the National, State and County MOH strategy. YAH’s HRP plan considers the humanitarian-development-peace nexus framework. An approach that aims to build a resilient health system capable of meeting both immediate and long-term health needs in line with South Sudan National health policy 2016-2026 and 2023-2027 health sector strategic plan. Essential health services will be delivered in a complementarity manner across 3 static facilities, 6 mobile teams, and community health outreach. 1. Needs

Uror and Nyirol: IPC 2024 indicates both counites with 75% population being intersectorial prioritization 1 and 2 and at risk of deteriorating further which might see up to 80% of the population experiencing worst acute food insecurity. Health indicators in the counties remain low with the 2023 HMIS data revealing low consultation utilization rates for all ages and coverage of antenatal care 1st and 4th visit and deliveries by skilled birth attendants amongst the lowest in Jonglei (ANC 1 –15.7%; ANC 4 –7.8%; SBA –2.3%). Immunisation coverage remains poor. DPT Dropout rate remains high (17%). FSNMS R-29 report shows Uror and Nyirol Counties GAM rate of 13.6%, SAM prevalence of 2.7% and a MAM prevalence of 12.1%. The counties also have one of the worst maternal nutrition status in the country with 43.2% of women of reproductive age are malnourished. The key drivers of high GAM rate is attributable to interrelated causes within the Health, Nutrition, Food Security and Livelihoods, and WASH sectors; majorly prevalent childhood illness, sub-optimal feeding practices, high prevalence of disease coupled with inability to get early treatment. Frequent stock outs and extensive delays in restocking make treatment difficult. Recent floods spot checks in Uror confirm risk of snake bites and potential outbreak and upsurge in preventable diseases; diarrhoea, pneumonia, and malaria cases. Conflict, flooding and unequal power relations affects women, people with intellectual and mental disabilities (PWDs) and the elderly’s access to health and increases their vulnerability to gender-based violence. In addition to poor or lack of access to health services, young and adolescent girls face abuse, neglect, early marriage, poor access to education and are forced into early child-bearing. The elderly and people with disabilities face neglect, abuse and violence. Gender-based violence places women and girls at higher health risks of contracting STIs and HIV transmission and maternal risk due to childbirth-related complications during delivery and post-traumatic stress and depression. Location or distance to the health facilities limit access for people living in the affected areas and protracted conflict and outbreaks of violence and limit delivery of medical supplies and related services. Restrictions on women and girl’s mobility, lack of autonomy regarding their reproductive rights in terms of marriage and child bearing coupled with low literacy rates, social cultural beliefs and discriminatory attitudes put women and girls in Nyirol and Uror Counties at greater risks than men in accessing information hence at greater risk of unintended pregnancies, STIs/HIV and cervical cancer. In addition, lack of training and awareness amongst healthcare providers on specific health needs and challenges faced by women and girls limits the appropriateness and quality of assistance provided.

2. Project activities

Provide routine and national immunisation campaign services against common preventable childhood illness through static and mobile outreaches, Conduct timely disease surveillance and outbreaks detecting, reporting and response within 72 hours of occurrence Conduct OPD curative consultations for common morbidities including management of non- communicable diseases Support laboratory capacity to undertake testing, counseling and referral for communicable and Neglected Tropical diseases including inpatient case management in the health facilities. Provide Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) services, referral and necessary counselling to the SGBV survivors in 2 counties. Support Preventative measures against the spread of Cholera, malaria and other vaccine preventable diseases through vaccination, risk communication and community engagement and control by triaging, screening, case detection and isolation at the health facilities Provide ANC, delivery by Skilled Birth Attendants and Post-natal services at the health facilities including BEmONC and CEmONC centers to pregnant and lactating women. To enhance safe delivery for mothers, this project fronts gift for antenatal visit scheme for women who attend up to 4 antenatal visits, proposed to complement the existing vehicle hiring for referrals besides LLITNs distribution for prevention of Malaria. Provide basic psychosocial support and mental health services including referrals at the health facilities, Provide OPD curative and other essential health care services to IDPs, returneed and Host communities including persons with disabilities Support WASH in health facilities through rehabilitation/construction of latrines and handwashing facilities and Waste Management Areas, Conduct MUAC screening and referral of malnourished children and Pregnant & Lactating Women to the nutrition centers, Support the installation of suggestion boxes for feedback mechanism in the health facilities and the organization’s offices, Enhance the capacity and knowledge of health workers and the County Department (CHD) officials through formal and in-services trainings, Procurement of drugs, medical supplies, laboratory reagents, other buffer stocks and other consumables, Support sub-national humanitarian coordination and reporting activities in the DHIS2 ans IDSR. Installing Mobile health clinics in safe locations close to the population settlements. Consultation with Women and men, PWDs, PLW/HIV, the elderly and people from different socio-economic and ethnic groups to understand their needs and vulnerabilities. Established Mobile health and outreach facilities at the geographical boundaries between different groups and consider mobility issues for PWDs and reduce protection risks related to GBV. Stakeholders engagement, especially vulnerable and minority groups, to support dialogue initiatives in the health committees. Feedback and complaints desks will be established within the mobile health facility with dedicated staff members who are representatives from the beneficiary groups. Clinical care for rape survivors will be managed by YAH and other health partners in the target locations. As a multi-sectoral team, YAH will establish and document clear referral networks and pathways, communication systems and follow-up strategies. Staff and volunteers will be trained on PSEA and sign the personal conduct policy
