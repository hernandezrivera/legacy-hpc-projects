Project Summary

UNRWA plans to provide up to 418,000 Palestine refugees with cash assistance to meet their basic needs, including food, non-food items (NFIs) and shelter. The Agency will continue to provide this assistance based on a targeted approach introduced in 2019, prioritizing refugees assessed as most vulnerable with a higher cash transfer. In 2021, it is expected that around 140,000 Palestine refugees will be assessed as most vulnerable based on the four vulnerability categories used by UNRWA (female headed households, families with persons with disabilities, families headed by an older person and unaccompanied minors/orphans).
Our cash transfer values were calculated while designing our Emergency Appeal 2021 intervening during the last quarter of 2020. The values were mainly based on a poverty line assessment for Syria, undertaken by the Relief and Social Service department at UNRWA HQ, and were also discussed with the CWG at the time. Based on this assessment, which adjusted the abject poverty line to USD 32, our cash transfer values were set to USD 27 per person per month for tier 1 (most vulnerable categories) and USD 16 tier 2 (remaining caseload of vulnerable Palestine refugees). These values may be considered as ceilings for our cash transfer based on our poverty line assessment. In reality and based on funding patterns and forecast, we expect to provide an average of USD 14 per and USD 9 per person per month for tier 1 and tier 2 respectively. This would amount to SYP 35,000 and SYP 22,500 per person per month respectively or SYP 175,000 and SYP 112,500 per month for a family of five, which is within the range of the CWG recommendation. 3) We plan to cover twelve months of assistance in 2021. We deliver our assistance in rounds of cash distribution depending on fund availability and taking into account cash flow considerations and donor requirements for the different grants. The cash distribution round is usually launched once the needed funds are secured for the full round based on the adjusted amounts (USD 14 and USD 9 per person per month). We are about to complete the first round of 2021, which will cover the needs for three months. We are planning to launch two other rounds in July and October 2021, which shall cover the remaining 9 months (5 months for one round and 4 months for the other).
M&E plan:
UNRWA plans to conduct two post-distribution monitoring exercise in June and December 2021 after the first and the third (final) round of cash distribution. UNRWA does not have the leverage to hold PDM surveys after every single round due to limited resources and in view of the long approval process from authorities. In addition to PDM, UNRWA also has a result based monitoring (RBM) system which tracks a number of indicators for all programmes and interventions including cash assistance. Quarterly management review (QMR) meetings involving relevant staff, including senior management, are held to track and assess the indicators. Mid-year result review (MYRR) and annual result review (ARR) are also conducted with departments at HQ to add another layer of scrutiny and to provide an overall overview of achievements and challenges. Project associates are deployed to cash distribution points to monitor the process on the ground and address any issues that may emerge on location. Humanitarian Officer for cash assistance provides overall support and supervision and UNRWA, with the help of the Emergency Operational Support Team with UNRWA Syria. Monitoring visits are conducted by EOST staff and by senior management on a regular basis. Area offices in Damascus, Aleppo, Homs and Dara’a are also closely engaged in the overall process of cash assistance. Finally third party monitoring exercises are often commissioned by prominent donors.
UNRWA has been providing cash assistance through multiple FSPs in the same area since 2013 and we didn’t face problem as such. UNRWA send the SMS to each family mentioning the name of the specific FSP.

4 - Needs

The protracted conflict in Syria has left 91% of the 438,000 Palestine refugees estimated to remainin the country in absolute poverty (UNRWA Vulnerability Assessment 2017) and 40% displaced. Following nearly a decade of extreme hardship, living conditions deteriorated further as a result of an economic crisis that has seen a sharp fall in the value of the Syrian Pound, leading to an increase in prices of the most basic commodities.The national average food basket price in February 2021 was 241 percent higher compared to February 2020 (WFP Market Price Watch Bulletin). The value of the Syrian Pound against the US Dollar has continued to dwindle, reaching a historic low of SYP 4,000 by early 2021. In the third quarter of 2020, wheat flour and fuel shortages led to scarcity of bread in bakeries in several areas of the country. Severe fuel shortage is still observed as of March and April 2021. The COVID-19 pandemic has placed increased hardship on the entire population in Syria, including Palestine refugees. According to an assessment conducted by UNRWA in July 2020 on the socio-economic impact of COVID-19 on Palestine refugees in Syria, close to 80 per cent of the surveyed persons stated that the number of meals or quantity of food consumed had reduced since the beginning of the pandemic in March 2020. According to PDM conducted by UNRWA following the 3rd cash round in December 2020:• 83.5% of respondent agree or strongly agree that cash transfers helped families in prioritizing their needs in terms of purchasing what they most needed.• Around 49% indicated that their living standards have improved because of the cash transfers, while around 72% find the cash transfers helpful in terms of not accumulating debt.

5 - Implementation of activities

Multipurpose cash assistance remains the most critical form of UNRWA emergency support to 418,000 conflict-affected and vulnerable Palestine refugees in Syria. Currently, UNRWA distributes cash through Al Baraka Bank, BBSF Bank and Al Haram Company in addition to its own cash distribution centre in Mazzeh, Damascus. UNRWA generates a list of eligible Palestine refugees and provides the list of beneficiaries to the partners ahead of the starting date. Once the local currency is availed in the outlets and following coordination with the partners, UNRWA sends SMS to each beneficiary communicating the amount, location and time. UNRWA assigns dedicated technical UNRWA staff to each of the cash outlets, allowing the Agency to ensure strict compliance with financial rules and regulations for the distribution of cash and also for verification. In 2021, UNRWA also plans to extend the use of ATMs in its cash programmes. Some 50,000 ATM cards will be issued, distributed and credited to selected beneficiaries, on top of the 10,000 cards already issued in 2019. Kindly note that the targeting criteria was based on our vulnerability assessment released in 2018, which found 91% of Palestine refugees in Syria to be under the poverty line. Based on this assessment and in order to respond to funding pressures, UNRWA introduced a targeted approach to cash assistance in the second half of 2019, which gives priority to the most vulnerable Palestine refugees as per the findings of the assessment. Based on this two-tier system, female households headed by older persons and unaccompanied minors (orphans) receive a higher social transfer value, while the remainder of the vulnerable caseload receives a reduced amount of cash assistance.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: 1. Humanitarian needs of of PRs in Syria remain relatively stable 2. Sustained commitment of donors to support UNRWA financially 3. Security situation and active violence do not deteriorate any further. 4. COVID-19 pandemic challenges remain within the scope of the Agency's capacity to respond financially and operationally. Risks linked to CVA implementation e.g. further inflation, liquidity issues, economic deterioration, impact on the market, etc. Mitigation: UNRWA is engaged very closely with the UN Country Team in its negotiations with the Central Bank of Syria to address the spread between the informal and formal exchange rate. UNRWA continuously assesses the socioeconomic context (for example, the Agency has conducted an assessment on the socioeconomic impact of COVID-19 in summer 2020) and remains open to considering alternative options and modalities, including in-kind assistance, to address the volatile conditions and the increasing inflation. With regards to liquidity, UNRWA provides cash assistance in local currency. Liquidity crisis for the local currency will be solve in discussion with Risk UNRWA staff and PR are exposed to violence and security threats. Mitigation: 1. Monitor security updates and conduct security assessments for facilities and missions 2. Train staff and beneficiaries on ERW, and personal safety. Risk: COVID-19 pandemic situation persists/worsens, leading to further operational challenges. Mitigation: 1. UNRWA will adapt its programme modality to ensure sustained delivery of services while prioritizing the safety of staff and beneficiaries 2. PPEs will be provided to frontline staff and sanitization materials will be made available at offices and distribution centres.
