Project Summary

Since the conflict crisis in 2013, and the consequent aggression coupled with drought, locusts, floods and the army warm infestation, the factors of food production for families over the years has been greatly affected to the present state. Household livelihood systems and patterns has been on the shift with many households only counting the cost. The cost of foods prices has remained every year high which has also affected the food production capacity of many households. Furthermore, family household incomes and dietary intakes has been greatly affected and to larger extent deteriorated and very wanting. As the hard economic times continue to press and bite the communities, many households have succumbed to survival mode as their livelihoods has been diminished or destroyed. Many household dietary intakes have significantly deteriorated and many can hardly afford two meals per day.
Conflict in Sudan has also greatly affected the food security situation in South Sudan with over 260000 returnees pushing the already worse food security and livelihood situation to precarious levels. The returnees are currently competing for very limited food staffs in the country that is recovering from its own conflict and three years of flooding that have destroyed farm lands.
The objective of the project is to provide food and livelihood protection to population affected by conflict and flood related displacements, hunger and diseases in Abiemnhom, Kapoeta North and Rubkona Counties. In line with FSL Cluster priorities and HRP 2024 strategy, WART will provide complementary and integrated agricultural crop and vegetable seeds, farming tools, fishing kits and agricultural extension support including training on improved agronomy practices and fish handling/preservation techniques to the communities in Abiemnhom, Kapoeta North and Rubkona Counties where indicators show a continuously degrading situation. The project will target 13,000HHs most vulnerable IDP, returnee and host populations in in these counties and provide Agricultural to support and increase their Agricultural production and productivity hence strengthening their capacity to produce enough food for household consumption and help them evade future shocks and builds their resilience.
The project will endeavour to support integration of activities through nutrition and protection to offer the vulnerable and affected populations with the best integrated services as well as putting the protection needs on the communities in the implementation of the project.
This project will further ensure protection mainstreaming; acknowledging that women, girls, men and boys with diverse ages, disabilities and backgrounds have very different needs, risk and coping strategies. This will be achieved by paying particular attention to protection and inclusion of vulnerable groups based and on gender and diversity analysis in addition to ensuring prevention of incidences of Gender Based Violence, sexual exploitation and abuse and other protection concerns and ensuring activities adheres to Do No Harm approach, and environmental protection.

1. Needs

The Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO 2024) estimates 9.4 million people almost 76% of the total population are in need of humanitarian assistance including returnees refugees and are expected to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the 2024 lean season (May – July). This is comparable with the situation described in the FSL sectoral analysis (December 2022) and IPC analysis (March 2023) where food security needs remained large and severe. Food insecurity is highest in Jonglei where 79% of the population will likely face severe acute food insecurity, followed by Unity (75%), Warrap (70%), Northern Bahr El Ghazal (66%), Lakes (61%) and Upper Nile (60%) with the highest levels of severe acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or worse) at the peak of the lean season 2023. Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) analysis (October 2022 –July 2023) shows that in the post-harvest projection period of December 2022 to March 2023, an estimated 6.31 million people (51.0% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 33,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity and an estimated 1.97 million people likely to face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Whereas in the lean season projection period of April to July 2023, an estimated 7.76 million people (62.7% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 43,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity and an estimated 2.90 million people are likely to face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Approximately 345,893 will need SAM treatment while another 1,057,703 will need MAM treatment.Food insecurity is exacerbated by shocks such as including the macro-economic crisis caused by the depreciation of the local currency and the global impacts of the conflict in Ukraine, the conflict in Sudan, climatic shocks (floods and dry spells), conflict and insecurity, climate- and conflict-induced population displacements, persistent low agricultural production levels, and the cumulative effects of prolonged years of asset depletion that continue to erode households’ coping capacities, and the loss of livelihoods. Acute food insecurity has also exuberated other cross cutting issues like conflict and gender based violence. Land ownership especially for women is also a challenge calling for efforts to address these issues to improve household food security.The impacts of conflict and weather shocks has not only affected household capacity to produce food, but also reduced the household capacity to purchase food by disrupting trade, diminishing market functioning and integration, and suppressing income-generating activities. Markets in the areas severely affected by floods or conflict are either being rebuilt or remain significantly disrupted. In Abiemnhom, Kapoeta East and Rubkona Counties, pockets of conflicts and violence, floods and inflation of food prices have not only destroyed household’s productive assets but also disrupted their livelihoods. These shocks have had a disproportionate effect on children, persons with disability, older persons and women headed households. Through this project, WART targets 9,000 most vulnerable households in these counties with interventions including; distribution of emergency livelihood kits; vegetable, crop, fishing kits and farming tools intended to stimulate food production to save lives while building resilience. Households will be trained on environmentally friendly agronomic techniques as well as good fishing practices aimed at maximizing yield for both household consumption and for sale. WART will as well improve main season crop production that is key in improving food security in all the three target counties. Through distribution of farm tools and agricultural inputs and capacity building of farmers for increased agricultural production.

2. Project activities

1. Conduct community consultations meetings with beneficiaries and key stakeholders for needs analysis and prioritization and also building consensus on Implementation 2. Conduct beneficiary mobilization and sensitization campaigns in Abiemnhom, Kapoeta North and Rubkona Counties. 3. Identification, verification, registration and distribution of crop and vegetable seeds and farming tools to 9,000HH in Abiemnhom, Kapoeta North and Rubkona 4. Identification, verification, registration and distribution of fishing kits to 4,000 HH in Abiemnhom and Rubkona Counties 5. Mobilization and training of 3,000 beneficiary household heads on agronomy, utlilization of distributed crop, vegetable and fishing inputs and post-harvest handling and preservation 6. Post Distribution Monitoring after distribution of seeds, farm tools and fishing kits for purpose accountability, effectiveness and satisfaction 7. Capacity development of 30 Agriculture extension Officers through refresher training 8. Establishment and maintenance of 12 demonstration plots. 9. Formation of agriculture clubs in 30 schools and training them on good farming techniques, preservation and storage. 10. Awareness creation and Sensitization on proper nutrition and protection, especially for lactating / pregnant women and for children 11. Train community animal health workers (CAHWs) on extension services, disease surveillance, control methods, equipped with skills and deployed in Abiemnhom, Kapoeta North and Rubkona 12. Conduct mass vaccination campaigns targeting 30,000 livestock (cows, goats, and sheep) in Abiemnhom, Kapoeta North and Rubkona counties. 13. support farmers during the main season in improved agricultural production through distribution of farm inputs and capacity building of farmers.
