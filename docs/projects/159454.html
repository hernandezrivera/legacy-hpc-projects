Project Summary

FSD proposes to provide potentially life saving mine risk awareness sessions to the elderly and people with disabilities by specifically focusing on providing these sessions where the elderly and people with disabilities are most likely to be - in their homes, and for the elderly that live in NGCAs, at the EECPs and at Pension Fund Offices close to the EECPs . FSD also proposes to add to the knowledge of the locations of where hazard areas are (and where they are not) by leveraging the increased awareness of mine and UXO in the communities selected for risk education by conducting survey activities soon after.
FSD propose to provide Mine Risk Education in approximately 34 isolated communities within 5 km of the contact line in the GCA of eastern Ukraine. MRE will be conducted using the 'house to house' approach with a view to ensuring the elderly and disabled residents of these communities are engaged. FSD will also provide MRE sessions at various times at the EECPs to engage elderly pensioners crossing to maintain residency requirements and to attend the Pension Fund offices. Sessions will also be conducted at the Pension Fund offices at the towns closest to the EECP. FSD also propose to add value to the MRE sessions by providing a Non-Technical Survey team to conduct surveys of potential explosive ordnance contamination in the vicinity of these isolated communities.
FSD will provide two Mine Risk Education Teams, one based in Sloviansk and the other in Mariupol to ensure wide coverage and access to the EECP, and one Non-Technical Survey Team in Sloviansk for a period of 12 months during 2020. The project will reach in excess of 146,000 beneficiaries and provide mine action survey data on approximately 27 to 32 of the isolated communities.
Accountability Mechanisms: FSD understands that the elderly are a particularly vulnerable sector of the community and have taken a number of steps to ensure accountability:
- all staff sign the FSD Code of Conduct which addresses Sexual Exploitation and Abuse.
- FSD has a 'whistleblower procedure', which is explained to all staff, that allows anonymous reporting of reporting of concerns.
- new MRE materials are field tested with beneficiaries prior to full release (and require government approval). Materials and sessions are provided in both languages.
- FSD staff wear branded 'uniforms' and all materials for distribution include donor and FSD logos.
- FSD will conduct pre and post session assessments with beneficiaries to allow feedback on the appropriateness of the style of presentation, the message and to allow them to raise any issues. MRE materials have the contact details for the Project Coordinator for any complaints.
- Survey activities strive for maximum community involvement (men, women, boys and girls).
- information on beneficiary numbers is disaggregated by location, sex and age.

1 - Needs

The impact of mines and UXO on communities close to the contact line is continuously highlighted as a major Protection issue. Mine Risk Education and the identification of hazardous areas (via Non-Technical Survey) are specifically mentioned in the 2019 HNA and HRP as high priority Protection issues and are key life saving interventions. Needs Assessments contributing to the design of this project included the 2019 HNO, the REACH Analysis of Humanitarian Trends in the GCAs of Donetsk and Luhansk Oblasts, dated June 2018; and the HelpAge International Baseline Report ' Emergency Protection Based Support to Conflict Affected Older Women and Men in the GCAs of Donetsk and Luhansk Oblasts, dated July 2018. Information related to EECP crossings was drawn from the Eastern Ukraine Checkpoint Monitoring Dashboard. No specific Mine Action related Needs Assessment exists for this project.The Swiss Foundation for Mine Action (FSD) is an international NGO that focuses on the operational aspects of mine action. FSD has been active in Eastern Ukraine since 2015, primarily in the provision of Mine Risk Education (MRE) to children, the conduct of Non-Technical Survey to identify hazardous areas and in mine and UXO clearance. The focus by major donors on the provision of MRE to children in educational facilities and summer camps has led to the situation where smaller, isolated communities located close to the contact line have been somewhat neglected, especially with regard to MRE. Risk Education that is conducted outside of education facilities is typically linked to taking advantage of community events or to visiting individual households (known as House to House sessions). House to House sessions are time consuming and do not generate the numbers of beneficiaries as group sessions in schools can do. Experience has demonstrated the best way to access the elderly, the chronically ill and persons with disabilities is by visiting them in their homes.There are even greater challenges in accessing the elderly located in the NGCAs of eastern Ukraine, in that mine action NGOs are generally not permitted to conduct mine action activities in the NGCAs. The fact that large number of elderly people are required to regularly cross the contact line at the EECPs in order to maintain residency requirements or to attend the Pension Fund Offices provides an ideal opportunity to briefly interact with these people to deliver MRE information and materials - both at the EECPs and at the Pension Offices.Experience has shown that a Non-Technical Survey that is linked to recent MRE sessions is a very efficient method of gathering the information required to define hazardous areas in and around communities. Non-Technical Survey activities are subject to the approval of the MoD, however FSD is confident that approval will be provided in approximately 32 of the 53 isolated communities identified by the Protection Cluster.Gender Analysis: FSD has considered the following when developing MRE sessions:- Identified the behaviours that put at risk people of both genders.- Identified how people of both genders react to potential mine and UXO threats.- Determined the best 'message' for both genders.- Conduct MRE sessions at the most convenient times and places for recipients.- Ensure that the language used is not too technical. - Included a mix of male and female practitioners.With the project focus being primarily on the elderly located in a number of the isolated settlements within 5km of the Contact Line (GCA) and on the elderly from the NGCA crossing at EECP to attend to pension and/or residency matters, FSD has conducted an analysis of the target beneficiaries and determined: - elderly women form the majority of heads of households in the isolated settlements.- women form the majority of people aged 60+ in the isolated settlements.- 40% of people crossing the EECP are women aged 60+ MRE sessions have been designed to cater for the target audience.

2 - Activities & outcomes

Outcome 1: Knowledge of the risks associated with mines and UXO is increased.Output 1.1: Increase of awareness of risk associated with mines and UXO via direct MRE sessions and distribution of MRE materials.Activity 1.1.1: Conduct of 'House to House' MRE sessions at households in selected isolated settlements.Activity 1.1.2: Conduct of 'Face to Face' MRE sessions, focussing on the elderly, at Entry/Exit Crossing Points (EECP) and at Social Security Offices close to the EECP. Sessions in the Social Security Office will include video presentations and displays.Activity 1.1.3 Distribution of MRE materials (awareness raising). Materials include posters, leaflets and pocket guides.Outcome 2: Contribution to prioritisation and planning of the national mine action response - specifically mine and UXO clearance. Output 2.1: Increase to the area, particularly in the 'isolated settlements', that have been subjected to the Non-Technical Survey (NTS) process. Activity 2.1.1: Conduct of NTS activities in the 'isolated settlements'. In the same settlements that FSD will conduct MRE activities. The majority to be conducted during reasonable weather conditions of late Spring, Summer and early Autumn. NTS are subject to Ministry of Defence tasking approval. Activity 2.1.2: Emergency marking of roads and track leading into and out of Confirmed Hazardous Areas.
