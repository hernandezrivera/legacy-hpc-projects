Project Summary

The project is well in line with the Protection cluster’s objective 2: “Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence,” by: 1) providing child protection case management services and psychological counselling for individual children and/ or families, in addition to referral for legal aid, specialized mental health services, health and rehabilitation as needed 2) providing structured group psycho-social sessions targeting girls and boys, utilizing evidence-based methodologies 3) providing individual MHPSS counselling and structured group psycho-social support and awareness sessions targeting women and men, 4) providing Staff Support/ Helping the Helpers interventions targeting CBOs, facilitators, volunteers, and other relevant partners staff members. This project targets most vulnerable Palestinian children and caregivers affected by conflict-related violence living in marginalized communities of the Gaza Strip and subject to stress resulting from the deteriorating socioeconomic conditions, blockade and instability. Targeted areas by this project are the following communities in 5 governorates:
-Gaza City: Beach Camp, Zaitoun, Shoujaieh
-North Gaza: Jabalia Camp, Beit Lahiya, Beit Hanoun
-Middle Area: Al Burej camp, Nusseirat Camp
-South Gaza: Tal as Sultan (Rafah), AlAmal/Khan Younis Camp, Fukhari (Khan Younis);
This project aims at improving protection mechanisms and ensure access to specialized case management, MHPSS and protection services across most vulnerable communities in the Gaza Strip. War Child will use its child protection expertise to ensure building strong national capacity in provision of child protection case management services, in line with national referral system and SoPs and with global quality standards. Tamer Institute and Community Training Centre and Conflict Management (CTCCM), members of the national CPN, will provide case management services to children across the above-mentioned geographic areas. War Child will support CTCCM and Tamer in strengthening their humanitarian response capacity and sustainably cascade this at community level through coordination with CBOs and schools. War Child will train partners in community-based PSS methodologies, including preventive GBV messages through integration of the She Deals methodology. CTCCM and Tamer will provide structured PSS activities to increase resilience of children and support safe identification and referral of most in need to specialized services, including mental health, legal, health, and to case management when needed. CTCCM and Tamer will be trained on remote counselling and will be able to provide individual support online if required by Covid19 restrictions. Caregivers will be provided with psychosocial support and positive parenting skills using War Child’s Caregivers Support Intervention (CSI) and will be referred to specialized MHPSS for in depth therapy if needs are identified. Tamer and CTCCM will be complementing geographical coverage thus allowing to reach the majority of vulnerable communities as requested by this allocation, and filling a crucial gap in child protection and MHPSS service provision. Added value is the engagement and coordination with schools in targeted locations, increasing reach to out of school children and/or at risk of drop out and child labour-a group of children recognized as most vulnerable.

1 - Needs

This project has been designed based on findings of different needs assessments: War Child needs assessments (War Child Rapid Needs Assessment, March and September 2020, and WCH assessment on MHPSS needs of children 0-6 years old, December 2019); preliminary protection needs and identification analysis (NIAF) report by the CPAoR, September 2020; HNO 2020 and OCHA monthly reports; consultations with communities and partners. The Gaza Strip has seen a sharp deterioration in the humanitarian, human rights, security and political situation since 2018 and an enormous rise in Palestinian casualties. An increasingly destabilized economy and weakened social fabric, with more households resorting to negative coping mechanisms, have also generated complex protection threats with a disproportionate impact on youth and children, refugees, as well as women, particularly in terms of increased risk of GBV including intimate partner violence, sexual abuse and forced marriages .Preliminary findings from the HNO 2020 show that 26 % of children experienced severe physical violence; some 197,903 children are in need of MHPSS due to severe and moderate mental health concerns and a total of 35,815 children are in need of individual case management. According to Tamer and CTCCM case management information systems, most children receiving individual case management are between the ages of 7-17 years (approximately 43% girls and 57% boys), of which a majority are exhibiting psychological distress or mental health disorders (51% of CTCCM cases and 84% of Tamer’s cases). In addition, analysis of CTCCM’s case management information system shows that out of 72 children (56% males, 44% girls, between ages of 7-17) receiving individual case management in 2020, 44% were registered for emotional/psychological harassment (67% boys), 17% for physical harassment (50% girls and 50% boys), and 12% for child labour (67% boys). According to Tamer’s case management information system, out of 707 children (43% girls and 57% boys) in 2020, protection risks registered were 10% for physical violence towards children/corporal punishment (61% boys); and 5% for unaccompanied and separated children (53% girls). War Child’s assessments confirm the gap in MHPSS services in targeted areas as various segments of the community are exposed to stress. Women experience a high level of stress as a result of family conflicts which leads to disrupting family relationships. The surge in humanitarian needs in Gaza has also exhausted already overburdened service providers, who are unable to meet increasing needs. Front-liners, staff members and volunteers at local NGOs/ CBOs are subject to stress, as a result of both primary and secondary trauma. As the Covid19 pandemic escalates, it is further exacerbating an extreme fragile humanitarian crisis with increased strains on households due to unemployment, food insecurity, home isolation, school closures, continued occupation-related violence, and lack of access to services, leading not only to a greater risk of infection, but also increased domestic violence, child abuse, neglect, and exploitation, and exacerbation of mental health issues, which are already being reported. Updates from the NIAF preliminary report confirms child labour violence, neglect, exploitation and abuse, distress and need of MHPSS as top three child protection risks as result of Covid19. War Child’s rapid needs assessment in September 2020 showed gaps in health, child protection, MHPSS, and education services, coupled with very limited capacity of most families to provide basic needs for their children. The assessment showed that Gazan families and children are experiencing high stress and tension levels, an inability to cope with the current emergency, and the need for MHPSS and child protection services. 79% of the respondents reported increased violence and physical abuse directed from parents/caregivers toward their children and/or wives, and increased violence among older brothers and/ or cousins living in the same house. All 10 key informants reported knowing about child physical abuse incidents, highlighting that children are not well-protected as domestic violence increases. 97% of the caregivers said that caregivers and/ or their children (boys and girls) have had increased stress levels: 86% of caregivers said that their children have become unhappy due to conflict/ war, followed by Covid19 and then domestic violence. 70% of key informants said there is no effective government social protection system to address children’s protection needs, highlighting a gap in child protection/case management services. Frequent power outages have made the delivery of remote services more difficult and concerns are raised about the unavailability of recreational activities for vulnerable children and multidisciplinary service, such as counselling and MHPSS. Based on consultations and coordination with the MoSD and through War Child, CTCCM and Tamer’s regular field presence in targeted locations, the project will reach most vulnerable communities identified as most affected and at the same time less served by protection and MHPSS services contributing to filling in a critical gap and in response to the needs. War Child, Tamer and CTCCM have been providing CP/MHPSS services in Gaza through their networks of local CBOs and schools, targeting children (males and females from 7-17 years) and caregivers (males and females) in all governorates. This project will focus on geographical areas with gaps in service provision compared to population size including refugee camps (Jabalia, Nusseirat, Beach/ Shati camps), towns/ neighbourhoods of poor socioeconomic situation (TalAssultan, Beit Lahia, Shujaieh and Zaitoun in Gaza and AlAmal in Khanyounis) and in ARAs (Bureij, Beit Hanoun, Fukhary.) At the beginning of the project the partners will conduct a rapid mapping of services to avoid duplication.

2 - Activities

The Project will provide a combination of community-based and specialized child protection and MHPSS services to the most vulnerable communities with gaps in services. Children will be identified through project activities, self-referrals, and outreach via 11 CBOs and 10 schools in targeted locations, through volunteers and school-counsellors who will refer to CTCCM or Tamer case workers depending on locations. Case workers at the beginning of the project will conduct awareness sessions and meetings with service providers, child protection committees, caregivers and others on the services, and accept referrals.CTCCM and Tamer will provide child protection case management to boys and girls at high risk as per locally-set vulnerability criteria and agreed SoPs, including information management. This approach will create linkages between formal and informal protection structures, strengthening the national protection system while increasing access to services. WCH will provide technical supervision and build core competencies of Tamer and CTCCM on CM supervision (including information management) and structured PSS methodologies that will be cascaded to CBOs, contributing to improving localized responses. Additional value of this project is the availability of adapted versions of WCH’s PSS and child protection methodologies for remote modality due to C-19 movement restrictions. For families unable to afford remote modalities (internet or phone) the project will support with SIM cards as necessary for phone consultations, sessions, and information dissemination via text messages. Printed materials for all activities will also be distributed to those without access to remote technology. Children and caregivers will benefit from tailored structured psycho-social support, individual psychological counselling and awareness sessions that have been designed in a gender and ability-sensitive and age-appropriate manner. Activities are designed to ensure inclusion of CWD and PWDs, and facilitators will be trained on inclusion. For CWDs in case management services, the project will support needs for assistive devices either directly or through referrals. Risk mitigation measures for C-19 will follow WCH SOPs and the Protection Cluster guidance document including hygiene supplies on site, physical distancing, wearing masks, checking temperatures, and cleaning schedules. War Child has also adapted C-19 materials for PWD’s including videos in sign language for hearing impaired and materials in braille for visually impaired. This project will mainstream gender by engaging girls in structured PSS activities (She Deals) that provide context-appropriate life skills on sexual and reproductive health and gender awareness. Through capacity building, supportive supervision and coaching for case workers, supervisors, and facilitators, WCH and its partners will support staff to improve their own well-being. The provision of this integrated package of protection and MHPSS services combined with capacity building and coaching of CBOs and NGOs aims at strengthening the protective environment of children and families most affected by violence, thus fully responding to the needs and priorities identified by the HNO and Protection cluster’s objectives. *HRP Standard Activity: Provision of emergency CP responses: (1) Case management services for 450 boys and girls, provided by Tamer and CTCCM based on Gaza SoPs including vulnerability criteria for registration and intake of cases. Case workers will provide basic counselling and psychosocial support to children and their caregivers (including PWD), and identify and refer children with mental health or other issues to specialized services, in line with the national referral pathway, including to the CPN. Children and families in need of specialized mental health services will be referred to individual, group and family counselling and therapy provided by CTCCM under this project. Those in need of online specialized MH services will be referred to Gaza Community Mental Health (GCMH). (2)Tamer and CTCCM will provide 10 individual counselling services to 180 children and their parents. (3)Structured PSS sessions for 3000 children including the War Child Team Up methodology (12 sessions per group of boys and girls, 7-14 years; Little Fellow, 10 sessions per group of boys and girls, 6-10 years; and She Deals: 15 sessions per groups of female children,13-17 years. Each group will include 10-20 participants depending on modality (remote vs. in-person); *HRP Standard Activity: capacity building for CP service providers: WCH will train Tamer and CTCCM staff: (1) 25 caseworkers in remote case management services. War Child has adapted its case management response to ensure continuing services for children during C-19 and related restrictions. A guidance note has been developed and training material is available in Arabic and includes informative videos that explain how to provide remote CM services. (2) 12 CP supervisors on supervision and coaching. WCH has contributed to the design and regional piloting of the global supervision and coaching toolkit for case management and will train partners to improve case management practice in their organization. (3)Humanitarian response including CHS, CPMS, safeguarding including remote feedback, online safe communication with children, and (4) remote counselling/PFA *HRP Standard Activity: Provision of MHPSS for adults and youth: (1) Tamer and CTCCM will implement WCH’s Caregivers Support intervention for 930 caregivers (10 sessions per group of 12 caregivers (males and females) aged 18+ years). CSI has been adapted for remote modality should C-19 lockdown persist, and (2) CTCCM will provide 6 family counselling sessions to 350 families *HRP Activity - Self-care for service providers: 3 days of activities for CBO facilitators and frontliners to enable them to better cope with stress and maintain momentum to support project participants.

3 - Indicators

War Child has an established global monitoring and reporting framework and uses standardized and globally validated means of verification to assess program effects on the wellbeing of participating children and families, and participant satisfaction. Through WCH’s monitoring and evaluation methodology, quality implementation of programming and delivery of care will be ensured. Regular field visits to and supervision of all activity sites will be conducted. Activities and interventions will be further refined and adapted to meet the specific needs of the communities through engagement of CBOs and community facilitators who will regularly assess needs and participant satisfaction through quarterly focus group discussions and using WCH tools for measuring participant satisfaction and through the establishment of a complaints and feedback mechanism with support from WCH. An end-of-project evaluation with participants through focus group discussions willalso give children, caregivers, and community volunteers the opportunity to evaluate the various activities. This project includes the following benefit indicators: 1. % of children participating in targeted psychosocial interventions who show improved psychosocial well-being. Globally validated tools including the short Warwick Edinburgh Mental Wellbeing Scale are used to measure this indicator. The Short Warwick Edinburgh Mental Wellbeing Scale includes seven validated items and two additions from WCH. The items cover the feeling and functioning dimensions of mental wellbeing. 2. % of caregivers with improved mental wellbeing score.The short Warwick Edinburgh Mental Wellbeing Scale is used to measure this indicator 3. Participant satisfaction - Children, young people and adults including those with disability have a right to provide feedback about how satisfied they are with program activities. This indicator gathers feedback from participants about how they feel about a program activity, and how they think it can be improved. The group exercise gathers both qualitative and quantitative information. In addition, this project includes the following HRP Standard and customized Indicators: *# of children affected by conflict related violence and violence in households benefiting from structured psychosocial services and child protection interventions, Target: 2550 (1050 boys and 1550 girls including 130 CWD: 70 boys and 60 girls), MOV: attendance sheet, sessions' reports, project's reports, focus groups, pre/post questionnaires, assumptions/risks: consent of caregivers to participate; interest of children to participate. *# of children affected by conflict related violence benefiting from specialised individual case management, Target: 450 (225 girls, 225 boys including 30 CWD: 17 boys and 13 girls), MOV: case files', case management information system, project's reports, assumptions/risks: consent of caregivers for case management services. *# community facilitators and counsellors trained on PSS and CP methodologies, Target: 60 (20 men, 40 women), MOV: training reports, assumptions/risks: retention of community facilitators. *# of vulnerable men and women affected by conflict related violence and violence in households benefiting from structured psychosocial services, Target: 916 (600 women, 316 men; 30 PWD: 20 women and 10 men)

War Child, Tamer, and CTCCM will work with the communities and CBOs under the project to identify and develop tailored and child-friendly feedback and complaints mechanisms, to ensure that the mechanisms are user-friendly and relevant for the communities, also during C-19 and with movement restrictions. Community-based and child-friendly complaints and feedback mechanisms will be established for all services, and participant satisfaction assessments using WCH tools and through quarterly focus group discussions with women, men, girls and boys will regularly inform the project of what’s working and what needs improvement and to be further tailored. Should movement restrictions persist and focus group discussions are not possible, we will adapt to collecting information from a sample through phone or whatsapp. Feedback is also collected through services and activities as part of the M&E systems. War Child and partner team members will meet every month (in person or by Zoom) to analyse and reflect upon feedback and complaints received to ensure regular reflection on the issues raised, solutions proposed and who reported them which help us to address problems, and feed into strategic decisions and help to the program as needed. An end-of-project evaluation with participants through focus group discussions or surveys will also give children, caregivers, and community volunteers the opportunity to evaluate the various activities.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Protection is mainstreamed into all aspects of project implementation: 1 –Prioritize safety & dignity, and avoid causing harm: War Child considers the specific needs and vulnerabilities of boys and girls of varying ages, as well of men and women, recognizing their roles in the communities, while trying to empower those who tend to be left out of power. War Child aims at being accountable to the children and families of this project and has taken a systematic approach to ensure all its interventions do not harm. Practical applications of the Do No Harm in the proposed project will encompass: • Identifying vulnerable groups across communities to foster social stability, avoid exacerbating tensions within the community and promote the equitable distribution of services across identity groups;• Reaching out and including vulnerable children within War Child intervention as per locally contextualized vulnerability criteria;• Analyzing existing community power dynamics to ensure that the needs of individuals and groups at risk of marginalization are voiced (also through the feedback mechanism);• Do No Harm principles included as a component of child protection and child safeguarding competency development of partners and communities;• A strong focus and commitment on child safeguarding and confidentiality in all interventions• With regards to case management services, case workers consult with the child and caregiver as to where they feel most comfortable to receive the service – for some they prefer in their homes, for others they prefer in the case worker’s office• Timely referral to specialized MHPSS, CP, and GBV professionals

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

To ensure meaningful access and participation, vulnerable children and caregivers (including people with disabilities) will be identified through outreach (including home visits), awareness raising sessions in the community, structured PSS programs, then referred to case management for assessment. Access to external MHPSS services in the targeted communities are limited, and as the travel and transportation costs may at times be a barrier to access, War Child in these instances will provide transportation support and case workers will accompany the children (including children with disabilities) to the specialized services to ensure access to care. In addition, given the overall and high need of children and families affected by conflict- related violence, selection of participants in community-based PSS activities and case management services will be based on specific vulnerability criteria (such as being acutely affected by or at high-risk of conflict- related violence, family or community violence/abuse/neglect, disabilities, child labor, COVID-19,etc), which will also be well publicized through community meetings.Tamer and CTCCM will guarantee outreach to children with disability in their community based activities, recognizing that the participation of children with disabilities can be a challenge due to social-cultural norms. The project will actively identify person with disabilities through outreach to schools and CBOs as well as referring to services. A total of 70 people with disabilities will be engaged in our activities.Case workers and community facilitators will also receive an orientation training by War Child staff on inclusion principles in order to skillfully adapt PSS interventions to ensure inclusivity, including for children with disabilities.
