Project Summary

As part of its mandate to provide protection and solutions, UNHCR through the Protection Safety Nets Project will initiate measures through livelihoods programming activities that will provide opportunities for returnees, IDPs and affected populations to recover their livelihoods and, acquire skills and capacities that enable them to engage in profitable income generating activities. Vulnerable households and individuals are provided a pathway to economic empowerment, recovery and durable solutions. The project aims to ensure that all persons of concern are able to make a safe and sustainable living that meets their basic needs, contributes to their dignity, and provides for the full enjoyment of their rights. The project will promote their resilience, limit negative coping behaviours that heightens protection risk exposures, as well protect the affected population from exploitation and abuse.
UNHCR will implement activities in support of this project in the following locations:
• Borno (Ngala, Maiduguri, Jere and Bama)
• Adamawa (Yola North, Yola South, Mubi North, Mubi South, Michika and Madagali)
• Yobe (Damaturu and Gujba)
The Project will provide opportunities for Women and Men, Girls and Boys – using the Age Gender and Diversity approach. UNHCR will ensure the achievement of this objective through the following means
I: Enable Access to Agricultural / Livestock / Fisheries Production: Build capacities of vulnerable IDPs, Returnees, and affected populations - through training and support for activities in sustainable agriculture/livestock/fishery production to support economic self-reliance and reintegration.
II: Facilitate Access to Self-Employment / Businesses: Promote business-oriented community-based cooperative units. Beneficiaries in such cooperatives will be provided necessary training – vocational skills, business management, financial literacy as well as business startup kits to undertake collective production and marketing of the produce.
The Protection Safety-net measures implemented through livelihoods programming will reduce needs, vulnerabilities, and heightened protection risk exposures - including sexual and gender-based violence risk exposures. It will also contribute to conducive conditions for durable solutions for IDPs and returnees, enhance community resilience and boost the provision of (or access to) sustainable livelihoods. These actions align with the Strategic Objectives of the 2019 – 2021 Nigeria Humanitarian Response Strategy, the HCT Centrality of Protection Strategy (Nigeria, 2017), and the Strategy on Protection, Return and Recovery for Northeast Nigeria (2017).

1 - Needs

Now in its 12th year, the conflict in North-Eastern Nigeria continues to have a devastating impact on civilians in the affected communities. It is projected that the affected states and Nigeria as a whole have lost over nine years of human development progress since the crisis began. According to the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) Round 28, about 2 million peoples are internally displaced in the Northeast and the returnee figures as at August 2019, stand at over 1.6 million persons in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) States. In being forcibly displaced from their places of usual residence, the displaced populations have lost their livelihoods and livelihoods assets. The poor and most vulnerable members of affected communities – being the most affected by the loss of livelihoods. The continuing insurgency and counter-insurgency operations limits their ability to access their fields and farm lands due to the fragile security environment which exposes them to the risks of abductions, kidnappings, rape, killing and other forms of violence perpetrated by the insurgents. This further pauperizes the affected populations, exerting an extremely high toll on their sense of dignity and ability to attain self-reliance in meeting their basic needs. The loss of livelihood assets often results in dependence on Humanitarian aid as is currently being witnessed in most displacement sites within the Northeast. As a consequence of the severe economic hardship that has been caused by the insurgency, most of the affected households have limited or zero opportunities to support themselves and their families. The long-term loss of skills, livelihoods and economic independence is known to be a driving factor for negative coping mechanisms which impacts negatively on the protection environment, and exposes these vulnerable individuals to heightened protection risks. The UNHCR Protection Safety Nets project will ensure that vulnerable households and individuals are re-tooled with the capabilities, assets and strategies that they could use to make a living – as an integral part of solutions for the displaced population in the Northeast. In alignment with 2019 – 2021 Nigeria Humanitarian Response Strategy, the HCT Centrality of Protection Strategy (Nigeria, 2017), the Strategy on Protection, Return and Recovery for Northeast Nigeria (2017) and the Nigerian Governments development plans, UNHCR continues to lead and coordinate efforts to deliver Protection and life-saving assistance while remaining focused on the search for solutions. Improving livelihoods through economic inclusion is a key component of UNHCR’s strategy to achieve protection and solutions outcomes for displaced populations in the Northeast. UNHCR Protection Safety Net measures implemented through livelihoods programming – will empower vulnerable individuals and households to access employment opportunities, entrepreneurship and small-scale business opportunities, business financing, as well as agro-based livelihood opportunities. These actions promote self-reliance, restoration of dignity and the sustainability of returns as a durable solution. Increased returns have been witnessed across affected communities in the Northeast, including the voluntary repatriation of refugees (from Cameroon) and spontaneous returns. The increased returns creates an urgent need to support the returnee households with the skills and assets they require to meet their basic needs. There are existing gaps in structures and facilities for the provision of technical and vocational training in the return communities and LGAs. Limited number of actors implementing self-reliance activities in return communities creates a gap and limits access to opportunities for sustainable livelihoods engagement for a large percentage of the displaced population.

2 - Activities

In the project 2023, IDPs and returnees will be supported with livelihood interventions, capacity buildings and other assistance. Farming- Displaced affected individuals under this component will have access to land either provided by the state or host community or in the case of returnees, their own land. The Displaced affected individuals will receive training in sustainable agricultural practices, including FAO training on Good Agricultural Practice (GAP). AUN has simplified this training to educate and equip the Displaced affected individuals, so they adapt the methods to reduce pest, increase crop yield and protect harvest from insects. Extension workers will provide continuous support to the farmers throughout the period until harvest. • This component will benefit individual Displaced affected individuals. • Apprenticeship: Required• Empowerment Approach: Vouchers for starter kits including seeds, fertilizers and other farming inputs as per budget.Fishery – Displaced affected individuals under this component will be trained at fishery centres managed by state, AUN and other partners. This component will benefit Displaced affected individuals in cooperatives. AUN will train the cooperative leaders on management and group dynamics as well as governance of cooperatives. • This component will benefit individual Displaced affected individuals. • Apprenticeship: Required• Empowerment Approach: Vouchers for starter kits water tanks, nets, fish feed, fingerings and other fishery inputs as per budget. Livestock/Animal rearing – Displaced affected individuals under this component will be trained in animal husbandry basic skills. Training will be hands on and with a farmer who is already rearing goats, sheep and or cattle. Apprenticeship required.• This component will benefit individual Displaced affected individuals. • Apprenticeship: Required• Empowerment Approach: Vouchers for starter kits including seeds, fertilizers, and other farming inputs as per budget.Extension workers hired by the project will provide technical support to all Displaced affected individuals under this output. After the component training, all Displaced affected individuals will receive training on basic business management and financial literacy before they graduate.All Displaced affected individuals will be supported to open bank accounts both individual and as a cooperative.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The key protection principles underpinning the provision of humanitarian assistance informed the design of this project and as such will be observed during the project implementation. UNHCR will adopt a rights-based approach in the planning, implementation and evaluation during the entirety of the project. To ensure comprehensive protection mainstreaming across this project, the following measures will be taken for each of the protection principles: • Prioritize safety and dignity and avoid doing harm during the project implementation, UNHCR will closely monitor the security situation of the area of intervention, and mobile teams will use safe and authorized roads to access people in need. • To ensure meaningful and equal access, in the identification of beneficiaries, men, women, boys and girls will be prioritized based on need alone. Particularly vulnerable groups such as female- and child-headed households, but also elderly and persons with disability will be prioritized. The composition of the assessment teams will reflect gender balance, to facilitate contact with women and girls. • If necessary, additional measures will be taken to reach persons with disabilities and others extremely vulnerable. This can be the case of disabled persons unable to reach the protection desks. • In order to promote accountability, participation and empowerment of beneficiaries, beneficiaries’ participation to the project will be promoted by involving, to the widest extent possible, men, women, boys and girls of different ages in the community-based structures.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

UNHCR will strengthen community-based complaint/feedback mechanisms, which will allow men, women, boys and girls to express any complaint; beneficiaries will be informed of the existence of such mechanisms, and it will be ensured that they are comfortable in accessing it. Child friendly complaint boxes will be introduced in child friendly areas as well as in educational structures. The existing tollfree number and email will be vulgarized and peer to peer approach will be established to support reporting of complaint. Focus group discussions with people from different age, gender and diverse origin will be conducted regularly to collect feedback from affected population on available services and as well as protection risks. UNHCR will ensure that committees of women, men, boys and girls are in place in order to participate fully in the implementation of activities. Through these, UNHCR will ensure that issues of concern to individuals, as well as, to the community are brought to its attention as well as to the attention of other partners, for intervention. UNHCR will leverage on its active membership of the Taskforce on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and its Age Gender Diversity mainstreaming policy too. UNHCR and partners will also ensure that staff involved in the project comply with its code of conduct and the SG’s special measures for protection from sexual exploitation and abuse.
