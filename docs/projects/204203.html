Project Summary

On 15 April 2023, clashes erupted between the Sudanese Armed Forces and the Rapid
Support Forces, with devastating consequences for civilians. Due to violence and attacks
on health, disruptions in the supply chain, and poor access by the populace to facilities
that can provide needed services, health service provision across the nation and
particularly in the country's capital Khartoum has been severely impacted. Several
maternity clinics are out of service, including Omdurman maternity hospital. Power supply
interruptions and blackouts continue to impact hospitals and blood banks, posing a
serious impact on lifesaving interventions, including blood transfusion and critical care.
Furthermore, all nationwide supplies are managed and controlled in Khartoum with is
significantly impacted. Access to lifesaving medicines and consumables as well as
infection prevention and control for hospitals and community midwives is nearly absent,
pushing several facilities out of services, and increasing the risks for infection and sepsis.
During humanitarian crises including during conflict as the situation in Sudan, sexual and
reproductive health services are disrupted, and social support systems are broken down,
leading to an increased risk of unintended pregnancies, rape, STIs including HIV, and
complications which often imply mortality and/or long-term serious health problems from
pregnancy and childbirth for millions of women and girls. UNFPA will consider set of
priority SRH activities to be implemented at the onset of an emergency as well as during
protracted crises using the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) for SRH, hereafter
referred to as the MISP.
MISP aims at reducing maternal and newborn mortality and morbidity, prevention and
response to sexual violence, prevention of HIV transmission, reduction of HIV morbidity
and prevention of unintended pregnancies, and sets the platform for comprehensive SRH
services integrated into primary health as soon as possible. All intervention of the MISP
will be implemented simultaneously through coordinated actions with all relevant
partners.
The intervention primarily targets women, girls in reproductive age, youth and adolescents,
and men and boys. The latest updates to the HRP as result of the 15th of April conflict
eruption, the health cluster indicated 10,088,298 in need of health intervention, and targets
9 Million. This estimates include 3.1 women are estimated to be at reproductive age in
need of humanitarian assistant, including 241,377 pregnant women. UNFPA will target 1.8 million with more than 80% are women and girls in reproductive age. The geographicalcoverage follows the HNO identifi ed locations with severity ranking three and above.
All SRHR intervention will be implemented simultaneously through coordinated actionswith all relevant partners. The planned interventions prioritize and aim reducing SRHmortalities and morbidities through: timely provision of Emergency Obstetric care andsave delivery services, timely referral to lifesaving services, support the availability ofFamily Planning services, improving Clinical management of rape for survivors of SGBV,availing HIV and STIs prevention and response, improving Antenatal and postnatal care,Post abortion care, and other SRH services. These will be achieved through strengtheningthe capacity of SRH care providers with emphases of midwifery services, timelyavailability of lifesaving SRHR supplies, timely deployment of temporary clinics andmobile clinics, supporting the referral system, and demand creation through awarenessraising and educational activities. Maternal Morbidities such as Obstetric fi stula will begiven especial attention by UNFPA.
The implementation of activities will be conducted through NGOs and Government partners with the strong technical support from UNFPA, including maintaining the field presence

1 - Needs

The updated Health Sector Humanitarian response May 2023, indicates 10,088,298individuals expected to be in need of humanitarian assistance and basic health service inSudan which includes 2.4 million women and girls at reproductive age. Reference made toMISP calculator 2019; around + 321,000 pregnant women are estimated to be in need forobstetric care and access for safe vaginal delivery. Around +192,000 pregnant women areestimated to either experience obstetric complication, need for post abortion care, orrequire a caesarean section. It is also estimated that total number of + 53,000 cases ofsexual violence who will seek care during 2023. Prior to 15th of April, coverage was lowdue to limited supplies, infrastructure and capacities, coverage with EMONC was around40%, and CMR services were less than 20%. The situation had catastrophically impacted by the armed clashes, resulting in causalities including amongst pregnant women attending to care, and significant displacement from Khartoum, West Darfur and other affected states. protection risks have emerged as well including increased risks of sexual based violence. Limited safe pathways and access by both people in need and health careproviders to seek care, the direct attacks on hospitals and Health care delivery points,Power interruption and water shortages including amongst hospitals, depletion ofsupplies and unsafe access to stocks, and direct attacks on health care providers; allthese factors had serious impact on capacity of health care to deliver and operate. Sudanis characterized with a high maternal mortality rate (270 per 100,000 live births as per2019 UN estimates). In reference to the 2022 Maternal death surveillance report, the mostprevalent cause was Obstetric hemorrhage, followed by hypertensive disorders, then sepsis. Moreover, it confirmed that of maternal deaths had no ANC before, and were attributed to limited accessibility (delay at home, or delay in reaching health facility). Coverage with lifesaving emergency obstetric and neonatal care remain very low in Sudanwith around 40% by 2022. According to the 2022 multi sectorial needs assessment MSNA,only 12% of women had their deliveries in health facilities during 2022, 12% of deliveries were attended at home with no trained birth attendants. Gaps in provision of CMR services are identified nationwide through the regular CMR mapping, at both supplies, andtrained cadres, the latest mapping remained less than 20% coverage. Midwifery cadres aswell were not classifi ed as skilled cadres in view of the duration of the pre service training.Sudan remained at high risk of disease outbreaks that have higher impact on pregnantwomen including Malaria, dengue Fever and Hepatitis E Virus cases. Moreover, thecountry remains prone to various risks and hazards such as fl oods, localized armedclashes and inter communal violence, displacement, climatic shocks and hazards, diseaseoutbreaks, food security, malnutrition and protection risks. The deteriorating economic status worsened the situation. The protracted humanitarian crisis in Sudan continues to impact the already fragile health system and is reducing the capacity to provide basic health services and respond to the multiple emergencies affecting the country. The annual cycles of floods, disease outbreaks, border confl icts, and ongoing economic crisis havefurther impacted the accessibility to health services.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The planned activities include: 1. Support the SRH coordination mechanism at states andnational levels to ensure the coordinated provision and expansion of MSP for RH 2.Strengthening national/ states contingency and preparedness planning focusing onMinimum Initial Service Package on RH in Crisis (MISP) and scaling up of MISP. 3.support immediate deployment of mobile and temporary clinics, and roving health careproviders, with attention to lifesaving sexual and reproductive health services along withother emergencies. 4. Support referral system through enhancement of locally initiatedsystem to all emergency obstetric cases. 5. improve the functionality of health facilitieswith emphases on delivery rooms, maternity wards, blood transfusion unit and theaters tosupport timely provision of safe and clean deliveries including caesarian sections, bloodtransfusion, neonatal resuscitation, management of complications of abortion, and otherobstetric and maternal complications. 6. Support the establishment of Family Planningmodel centers within selected health facilities inside the camps. 7. Capacity building ofhealth care providers through formal training, supportive supervision, on-the-job trainingand mentorship on relevant SRH topics including EMONC, FP, STI management, ClinicalManagement of Rape survivors and psycho-social support, etc. 8. Procurement and delivery of emergency reproductive health kits, micronutrients, equipment and supplies. 9.Support Obstetric Fistula treatment in states with high humanitarian burden. 10. Support demand creation and awareness raising on reproductive health seeking behavior. 11.Address Youth/ adolescent RH needs focusing on their specifi c needs and vulnerabilitiesin humanitarian settings.

At the procurement, level the organization works with suppliers (Internation aland national levels) to implement sound environmental impact provisions. 6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Through all project steps, starting from planning, implementation, and monitoring andevaluation, the following principles will be adhered to 1. Avoid causing harm: UNFPAand actors will ensure that service and assistance delivery preserves the physicalintegrity of individuals and communities, their dignity. These packages have alreadybeen adapted to the context of Sudan, and are culturally appropriate and minimizesany harmful and unintended negative consequences. 2. Equality: UNFPA and theactors will ensure that assistance and services will be provided according to needs andnot any other determinants such as ethnicity or nationality, in good quantity, withinsafe and easy to reach locations, are known by the affected individuals and areaccessible by all various groups including disabled individuals. 3. Accountability tobenefi ciaries: UNFPA and the actors will ensure that the affected individuals andcommunities are engaged in the measurement of the quality of interventions servingthem, including mechanisms for suggestions and complaints from the population. 4.Participation and Empowerment: Foster inclusive participation to decision-makingprocesses, support the development of self-protection capacities and assist people toclaim their rights and empower themselves
