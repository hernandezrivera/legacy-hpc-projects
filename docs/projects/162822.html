Project Summary

This project will address critical food security and protection needs of vulnerable communities in Central Darfur and South Kordofan by providing agricultural and livestock inputs, food assistance, livelihoods support and capacity building opportunities. In addition, DRC will provide case management and referral services while strengthening community protective factors, targeting the most vulnerable people in areas of implementation. Throughout the project, DRC will develop linkages across the humanitarian-development-peace nexus to ensure effective exit strategies and promote durable solutions.

1 - Needs

For 17 years, Central Darfur has faced ongoing conflict, protracted and new displacement, and a complete disruption of community structures. Ongoing conflict has had a similar impact on South Kordofan. Through its community-based programmes, DRC has observed a number of protection risks facing, women, men, boys, girls, and older people. A loss of household assets has exposed many to negative coping strategies, including begging, reduced food consumption, and engaging in livelihoods activities that expose them to exploitation. Women and girls are often exposed to sexual exploitation and GBV due to their vulnerability. Children and Adolescents face unique protection risks, including GBV, removal from school, child labor, early marriage, malnutrition and abuse. A breakdown of community structures has left older persons and persons with disabilities without strong social support networks, increasing their vulnerability. Young and Middle-Aged Men often travel in search of livelihoods opportunities, increasing their exposure to exploitation. In addition, any people have experienced or witnessed traumatic events and have experienced psychological harm. Social stigma, weak services and referral networks, and cultural barriers often prevent individuals from seeking protection services. In particular, men and boys who have survived assault face stigma and taboos, preventing them from seeking assistance. Persons of alternative gender identities also face stigma and societal taboos, increasing their protection risks. Weak governance structures and a lack of trust between duty bearers and communities widens the gap between vulnerable populations and critical protection services. Many displaced people, especially children and adolescents, lack civil documentation, preventing access to services. Beyond this, there is a general lack of government services, including safe learning environments, medical facilities and safe roads and transportation infrastructure, which increases the vulnerability of communities and make IDP returns difficult. Returns are further complicated by a lack of documentation on Housing, Land and Property, which exposes displaced persons to additional protection risks, as land and critical resources, such as water points, can be triggers of conflict. This is especially the case between pastoralist and agrarian communities. Due to a breakdown in community structures, the traditional justice mechanisms that normally settle such disputes have been weakened. Without household and livelihood assets or access to land, many displaced populations face food insecurity, malnutrition, and a lack of sufficient income to meet household expenditure. This prevents vulnerable persons from being able to cope with shocks, increasing their vulnerability. Returns are also hindered by a lack of household assets. During focus group discussions with return communities, a need for shelter and NFIs was consistently mentioned, and a lack of shelter options prevented permanent returns in some communities.

2 - Activities / Outputs

To address these needs, DRC is placing protection activities at the center of its 2020 programming. Anticipating that 2020 could lead to increased access to new areas around Jebel Marra as well as increased returns throughout Central Darfur, DRC will strike a balance between a facility-based approach and an outreach approach to providing critical protection services. DRC is currently implementing general protection, SGBV and child protection activities in Jebel Marra, Um Dukhun and Zalingei through projects funded by Danida, ECHO, UNHCR and SHF. In 2020, DRC will continue to provide protection services through community-based protection networks, women friendly spaces and child friendly spaces, working closely with MoSA to ensure adherence to international standards and coordination with government initiatives. Through these facilities, DRC will conduct safety audits and protection monitoring, community mapping and mapping of referral services, community dialogues and awareness campaigns around critical SGBV and child protection issues. DRC will work closely with government ministries and other humanitarian actors to ensure that referral mechanisms are in place to allow vulnerable individuals to access life-saving services according to their individual needs. DRC plans to operate 5 WFS, reaching 5000 women and girls in Golo, Nertiti and Zalingei. DRC will also seek to improve the food security of IDPs, host communities and returnees throughout Central Darfur, through general food assistance – as is currently being provided through a WFP-funded project – and by providing critical agriculture and livelihoods assets. As agriculture and livestock remain the main source of income for most communities in Central Darfur, DRC will prioritize strengthening these livelihoods sectors by providing agricultural and livestock inputs, trainings on best farming practices and establishing support groups to allow for a sustainable transition to longer-term economic recovery activities. Recipients of agricultural inputs may also receive food vouchers to support them during the lean season, allowing them to focus on tending to their farms. As travelling to farmlands can expose some individuals to increased risks, DRC will also promote winter gardening and kitchen gardening within camps and villages. DRC also recognizes that many individuals cannot access these traditional livelihoods opportunities. Based on vulnerability and a sound risk analysis, DRC will identify at-risk individuals without access or ability to farm or raise livestock, and develop alternative livelihoods opportunities. these livelihoods opportunities, including vocational training groups, will target individuals based on their protection risks. DRC will conduct market assessments in partnership with communities in order to identify urban and peri-urban livelihoods opportunities, particularly for youth and those unable to access farmland. Finally, DRC will also provide shelter and NFIs to vulnerable individuals, prioritizing new IDPs and return communities. As new displacements or returns occur – and as new areas of operation become accessible, DRC will conduct rapid needs assessments and conflict assessments to determine the most critical needs and develop programming in accordance with the principle of do no harm. DRC will work with other humanitarian actors to develop a coordinated response based on these needs and vulnerabilities. DRC will aim to provide the same services in South Kordofan. PDMs will be conducted after all distributions.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Agricultural activities are resource intensive, requiring land alteration and water use. DRC will work with WASH partners to determine environmental best practices from a water-use standpoint. In addition, DRC will look at the ways it can offset the negative environmental of agricultural activities through crop rotation, intercropping, natural pest control and permaculture. For livestock production, this will include dialogues with communities on how to mitigate the impact of overgrazing. While shelter items, particularly plastic sheets, can have a negative environmental impact, DRC will look at local sourcing to prevent the carbon footprint of international shipping and will work with communities to discuss more environmentally friendly alternatives for shelter materials.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

In designing projects, DRC develops activities and selection criteria based on a context-specific risk analysis and continued engagement with local communities. All activities are designed in consideration of the risks and vulnerabilities of local communities and target beneficiaries, and include components aimed at mitigating risk factors and strengthening protective factors in the community. Throughout implementation, DRC will conduct ongoing protection monitoring in order to identify new risks and vulnerabilities and assess the impact of our programming on the protection environment. While DRC has these structures firmly in place, it will spend the beginning of 2020 providing training and capacity building opportunities for all staff around protection mainstreaming to ensure that there is not only a culture of protection mainstreaming but also the capacity to mainstream protection effectively.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

DRC has made AAP and PSEA a priority for 2020. DRC is in the process of conducting an accountability assessment in target communities to understand the preferred means of feedback. On a community-by-community basis, DRC will establish multiple methods to ensure triangulation of complaints, with dedicated staff responding to complaints as they arrive. DRC will share the results of this assessment and any trends in complaints and feedback with other agencies and actors.
