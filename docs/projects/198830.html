Project Summary

The project seeks to strengthen protection and emergency intervention and promote durable solutions through a coordinated multi-sectoral response by reinforcing CCCM activities delivery, taking into consideration new developments within the operation. The intervention will be multi-sectoral, focusing on the Out of Camp Area Based Approach (ABA) ,and within prioritized camps/camp-like settings in Borno and Adamawa states, while also maintaining the conventional approach in camps with CCCM needs. The proposed CCCM approaches have been successfully implemented in NE Nigeria, with ABA recently piloted by UNHCR and partners in Borno, and currently rolled out to Adamawa. Given the prevailing circumstances in NE, it is not practical to abruptly discontinue the implementation of conventional CCCM approaches in camps and camp-like structures, knowing that several camps will still be in existence for better part of 2023. Inclusion of the host communities and returnees, and the population that have relocated or those attempting to integrate in the community is a sure way to promote durable solutions and community cohesion through equitable targeting. The project will cover 4 LGAs in Borno (Ngala, Monguno, Banki (Bama) and Damasak (Mobbar)), 5 LGAs in Adamawa (Yola South, Yola North, Mubi, Madagali and Michika) and 2 LGAs in Yobe state (Damaturu and Gujba). The breakdown of the target for this CCCM project is 97,298 individuals (19,460HH) for IDPs and 22,702 individuals (4,540 HH) for returnees, distributed as follows: 46% (male), 54% (female), 60% (children), 36% (adult) and 4% (elders).
Overall, the response will focus on enhancing multi-sectoral coordination of humanitarian response, monitoring of protection and assistance, promotion of participatory approach, site facilitation, community organizing, capacity building for government authorities, partners and the community, strengthening information sharing, communication and coordination, promoting, and strengthening of accountability to the affected population, ensuring infrastructureal improvement and to mainstream protection, Child Protection, GBV, Prevention from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and other cross-cutting issues like COVID 19 into CCCM for improved living conditions, and to promote sustainable durable solutions. Variations in gender, ethnic origin, diversity, and age that may be wrongly applied to eliminate specific persons from accessing services and lead to vulnerability and risky coping strategies will be considered during every stage of the project life cycle.
ABA is a steppingstone to early recovery/stabilization, hence CCCM response will attempt to link areas under ABA with public service activities such as education, food security, infrastructure development/rehabilitation, health, and public works among others. The community leaders will be oriented to sensitize the community on peaceful coexistence and how they can benefit from public services, development work within the geographical locations, including how they can get employment, access markets and conduct business, including available livelihood opportunities as contributing factors to sustainable durable solutions.

1 - Needs

According to the 2022 Nigeria Humanitarian Response Plan, 7.9 Million people, including 2,012,660 IDPs needed urgent humanitarian assistance in the BAY States. IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM R42), states that 344,415 men, 432,062 women, 556,622 boys and 679561 girls were displaced as of August 2022 with an estimated 41% of IDPs living in 308 camps (898,389 people) and camp-like settings while 59% are living in host communities (1,292,804 people). An estimated 91% of the displacement was attributed to the ongoing insurgency and counter insurgency operations with over 75% of displaced persons living in Borno State. 60% were estimated to be below 18 years, 40% between 18 and 59 years and 4% above 60 years. Adamawa had the second highest number of IDPs with 209,322 followed by Yobe with 162,394 IDPs. Over 46% camps are not under CCCM, while some which were covered in the past years have been dropped by NGOs, either because of insecurity and continuous attacks by the NSAG on humanitarian facilities, abduction, and threats from NSAG, and limited resources to cover the increasing population in need. There are around 1 million people in hard-to-reach areas, plus some 1.6 returnees since 2015, and unknown number of spontaneous returnees, including those living within the host communities, and host families who are of concern to CCCM. ABA is a new approach that requires proper orientation, and in-depth understand by authorities, partners, and other interlocutors, including camp committees. Community-based activities with the camp closure, carry out mentorship and coaching to partners, NEMA and SEMA representatives who have been trained to ensure clear understanding of roles and responsibilities, and sustain protection and mainstreaming of GBV and other cross cutting issues in to CCCM, community orientation on infrastructure maintenance, and to connect the committees with leadership structures in returning communities. There will be a need to reinforce participation of women, girls, boys, and men of different ages and of diverse backgrounds, minority groups and persons who live with disability (PLWD) in decision making process, strengthen inter-camp, intra-camp coordination, and to integrate the out of camp coordination by improving communication through both remote, virtual, and physical meetings in full observance of COVID-19 protocols. The desperate approach and alarming rate at which new arrivals continue to access safer LGAs and camps/camp-like settings, and the ongoing ‘forced’ government-led camp closure has further contributed to housing, land, and property issues, over congestion, and constrain on humanitarian capacity to meet basic needs for IDPs and that of other affected populations. This means members of the community must be oriented to adopt community resilient coping mechanisms, and to espouse & create sustained awareness on co-existence. Poor site planning and high congestion in camps/camp-like setting leads to untimely response to natural hazard occurrences such as fires, flooding and storms that cause massive destructions & creating more gaps and needs among POCs. There are also unmaintained, abandoned, damaged or dilapidated camp structures and infrastructures that need urgent decommissioning, including in camps that have been declared closed. Maintenance of coordination structures, and to sustain synergy among service providers and the community, including the local authorities, and effective implementation of Complaints and Feedback Mechanism. The roles and responsibilities, particularly on shared responsibility of camp coordination and camp management activities between the local authorities and the supporting NGOs (under the conventional CCCM) still needs to be streamlined, and to further orient the authorities on their key responsibility of maintenance of law and order in their camp administration role.

2 - Activities

The CCCM project will ensure equitable and dignified access to services, assistance, and protection for IDPs through camp coordination and camp management (CCCM) and Out-of-Camp Area Based Approach through the following expected deliverables: • Support to community self-management, leadership, and decision-making initiatives • Roles and responsibilities for camp managers and service providers defined and agreed • Ensure and support the mainstreaming of protection in all CCCM activities • Monitoring and information management systems integrated across camps • Conduct site selection, site planning, and site monitoring/implementation. UNHCR through will prioritize mainstreaming of cross-cutting issues including protection mainstreaming, inclusion of persons living with disabilities, chronic illnesses, etc. into all CCCM activities, and will integrate a community participation approach to enhance Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) while ensuring Age Gender and Diversity (AGD) mainstreaming. , Site planning, Land advocacy, mapping of existing suitable lands, relocation, fire response and prevention, flood mitigation for Rainy season response through provision of mitigation tools and formation of water channels in all camp/camp-like sites and prioritized host community locations based on needs, and with involvement of the affected population (women, girls, boys, men of all ages and diverse background, including People Living with Disability (PLWD) at camp/camp-like sites and within the host community under the Out of Camp ABA. Establishment and maintenance of an all-inclusive community committees in camps/camp-like and in Out of Camp settings with membership of minority groups, youth, PLWD in full respect of AGDM approach. Community mobilization through sensitization and awareness creation on leadership and selection of leaders, joint community mobilization events that leads to peaceful coexistence and cohesion among leaders in camps/camp-like structures including leadership in Out of Camp settings. Training for community leaders, volunteers, religious leaders, youth, and women leaders on how to adequately discharge their responsibilities , the understanding of camp management, protection, GBV, trafficking, PSEA, referral system, community participation among other cross cutting issues, in full respect of the AGDM approach. . Management of information & Coordination centres (Madagali-Adamawa, Monguno, Ngala, and Damasak – Borno) – out-of-camp. area based approach. Management of information & Coordination centres (Damasak, Monguno, & Ngala) – out-of-camp to ensure continuity.. It will also cater for carrying out minor ICC center rehabilitation.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The key protection principles underpinning the provision of humanitarian assistance informed the design of this project and as such will be observed during the project implementation. UNHCR will adopt a rights-based approach in the planning, implementation and evaluation during the entirety of the project. To ensure comprehensive protection mainstreaming across this project, the following measures will be taken for each of the protection principles: • Prioritize safety and dignity and avoid doing harm during the project implementation, UNHCR will closely monitor the security situation of the area of intervention, and mobile teams will use safe and authorized roads to access people in need. • To ensure meaningful and equal access, in the identification of beneficiaries, men, women, boys and girls will be prioritized based on need alone. Particularly vulnerable groups such as female- and child-headed households, but also elderly and persons with disability will be prioritized. The composition of the assessment teams will reflect gender balance, to facilitate contact with women and girls. • If necessary, additional measures will be taken to reach persons with disabilities and others extremely vulnerable. This can be the case of disabled persons unable to reach the protection desks. • In order to promote accountability, participation and empowerment of beneficiaries, beneficiaries’ participation to the project will be promoted by involving, to the widest extent possible, men, women, boys and girls of different ages in the community-based structures. • Establish measures aimed at preventing and responding to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, bearing in mind the UN zero tolerance policy. • All along project implementation, UNHCR will monitor protection, age, and gender aspects and will address any arising issues. To ensure a comprehensive response, all the findings will be shared with the Protection Sector as well as other relevant Sectors, with whom UNHCR will maintain close coordination.

This action will fully adhere to UNHCR's Accountability to Affected People's strategy, centred on 4 pillars of participation and inclusion, communication and transparency, feedback mechanisms, and organizational learning. To ensure participation, UNHCR will employ participatory methodologies at each stage of the operations management cycle to incorporate capacities and priorities of people with disability into protection and assistance activities of this action by engaging with organizations of people with disabilities. Secondly, UNHCR and partners will use all available communication mechanisms to engage with affected populations, including through focus group discussions. Thirdly, UNHCR will establish and promote protection-sensitive feedback and response systems, which will include provisions for confidential complaints.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Accountability to affected populations will be strengthened through the development of a community-based feedback mechanism; supporting partners to the inclusion of functional and accessible complaints and reporting mechanisms, convening frequent meetings with the community leaders, and beneficiaries and focus group discussions. UNHCR is an active member of the Taskforce on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and has community-based structures known as Protection Action Groups (PAG) within Maiduguri and LGAs where UNHCR is present such as Bama, Banki, Damasak, Damboa, Damaturu, Dikwa, Gudjba, Gwoza, Jere, Gwoza, Madagali, Michika, MMC, Monguno, Ngala, Rann, Yola North, Yola South. Through these structures, UNHCR ensures that issues of concern to the community are brought to the attention of UNHCR and its partners for intervention. UNHCR shall also establish a feedback mechanism through its Protection Desk and Information Communication and Coordination Centers . In previous projects, some key concerns raised include beneficiary selection, the support provided, community representatives and payment process. All issues reported will be systematically investigated and followed up in a timely manner. Received complaints shall be categorized according to the level of sensitivity and severity and responded in accordance with protection principles.
