Project Summary

As a result of the clashes that erupted throughout the country between Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and Rapid Support Forces (RSF) on 15 April 2023, the already fragile protection environment in Sudan has significantly deteriorated and reduced access to services such as water, health care, electricity, and basic items and commodities such as food, cash, and fuel for millions of people. Several cities have faced heavy bombardments and clashes, and associated unexploded ordnance, causing hundreds of civilian deaths and casualties, as well as damage to critical infrastructure. The collapse of rule of law has led to an increasing in criminal activities including indiscriminate attacks on civilians. Furthermore, high insecurity and looting of humanitarian assets and offices have compromised humanitarian operations in key locations. As of January 2024, a total of approximately 10.7 million individuals have been displaced across the country making Sudan the largest internal displacement crisis in the world. Vulnerable groups are increasingly exposed to protection risks including but not limited to attack on civilians, gender-based violence, exploitation, abuse, forced displacement, family separation leading to unaccompanied and separated children, which has caused significant harm to the physical and mental wellbeing of affected persons. This project intends to contribute to promoting a favorable protection environment in communities particularly affected by the current crisis, while providing humanitarian assistance to vulnerable individuals and communities. The proposed intervention intends to provide general, child, and GBV related protection services in the areas of interventions, contributing to the cluster response which estimates that 20.6 million people are in need of these protection services. The project is in line with the Cluster and Sub-Cluster Objectives and will target localities identified as “hotspots” by the Protection Cluster.
IOM will contribute to the protection response by conducting specific protection assessments in areas where information is needed. IOM will collect data on protection needs, gaps, and trends in coordination with the IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) team, Protection Cluster and other clusters such as Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) and health, to leverage effective synergies and increase the impact of interventions in view of delivering the best protection outcomes for affected populations. In coordination with the Civil Registry, UNHCR, and NCR, IOM will support the local partners through the provision of trainings and office equipment to strengthen their capacity and resources to provide civil documents including birth certificates. IOM will support the existing protection actors through in kind assistance and technical capacity support including training to communities, non-specialized and specialized service providers, operational support to existing women and girl safe spaces and child friendly spaces, facilitating regular visits of staff to hard-to-reach areas to provide services in affected communities. IOM will also provide direct assistance at its Migrant Response and Resource Centres or deploy protection mobile teams to provide assistance to persons at risk such as distribution of dignity kits or referrals to specialized service providers. IOM will further enhance community-based protection by supporting existing community-based protection networks and organizations who are a lifeline to persons at risk in many locations across Sudan. IOM will prioritize working with the existing community committees, community leaders, and representatives including women and youth groups, persons with disabilities to ensure inclusion of all groups and that protection services are provided to the most vulnerable persons.

Implementing Partners

IOM intends to support both displacement sites and hard-to-reach areas in Khartoum, Northern, Eastern States, and Darfur region, whenever possible, and in coordination with international and national partners, including state authorities, specialized service providers, and community-based protection organizations and networks. The unpredictable security situation on the ground and the significant challenges in accessing and providing humanitarian assistance in target areas will require IOM to adopt a certain measure of flexibility in the provision of humanitarian assistance as well as to adapt its interventions to changes in an already volatile environment. The assistance will be provided using direct implementation, deployment of mobile teams, and localization approaches through service providers, community organizations, or other partners who may have better access to the beneficairies. Moreover, such flexibility will allow IOM to prioritize those locations where protection and humanitarian needs are most urgent, to tackle the above-mentioned challenges, and address humanitarian needs accordingly.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

IOM in coordination with partners and Protection Cluster will undertake protection assessments to identify the gaps in service provision, in target locations across Sudan and collect relevant information on protection needs, gaps, and trends for programmatic and ad hoc assistance purposes. Relevant findings will be shared regularly with the Protection Cluster and Protection Working Groups (PWGs). IOM will also use the available protection assessments, reports, analysis, as well as other relevant information products such as IOM DTM to inform its multi-sector response and mitigate protection risks. When designing and implementing all project activities, protection of the crisis-affected population, particularly women, children, the elderly, and persons with disabilities, will be prioritized. IOM will mainstream survivor-centred and trauma-informed approaches when creating and implementing activities directly impacting at-risk groups. The support provided will seek to strengthen access to basic services and items including dignity kits in displacement contexts. IOM will also coordinate with and provide support to service providers on life-saving responses including case management, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), and referrals to social workers and external specialists caring for child, adolescent, and adults. The proposed action will target the displaced and returnee population and the most vulnerable individuals and households from the host communities. Furthermore, IOM will keep local authorities/communities and other relevant partners involved and/or informed on the implementation of field activities and on the selection criteria to avoid tensions with local communities. Key actions will therefore include identifying local authorities, community leaders, community-based protection networks, organizations, service providers and strengthening their role where possible to support implementation of integrated protection programming and proposed responses. Other relevant actions will include raising awareness and providing information on protection from exploitation and abuse and additional protection-related concerns to communities. IOM will use a gender-balanced team that will provide culturally appropriate services to beneficiaries depending on their specific needs, vulnerability, comfortability to ensure they are not further traumatized. Keeping any beneficiary data collected confidential; and seeking informed consent and anonymizing the information before further data sharing and analysis. All data and feedback provided will be in line with the IOM Data Protection Principles.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

IOM will collect feedback from beneficiaries directly through interviews and questionnaires and take all measures to ensure confidentiality of individuals participating in the feedback mechanism. IOM will also establish partnerships with local community organizations, networks, and mechanisms to ensure protection-related assessments and programming can proceed in the target locations including direct assistance and protection mainstreaming amongst NGO personnel, authorities, community protection networks, and other relevant parties. These community-based mechanisms will form part of the Community-Based Complaints Mechanisms (CBCM) with the aim of facilitating reporting of protection needs and gaps and referral for at-risk and vulnerable people to access assistance and services. Confidentiality safeguards will be developed to ensure feedback and complaints on matters such as sexual exploitation and abuse are handled sensitively, and individuals feel and are safe when sharing information. In coordination with these community-based groups IOM will raise awareness on potential protection risks, available protection services, referral pathways, and community self-help activities at state and national level.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

IOM is part of the inter-agency coordination approaches, including the PSEA Working Group, and will ensure to follow the relevant guidelines on accountability to affected populations, to provide feedback and complaint mechanisms to all beneficiaries. For example. IOM has an internal PSEA focal point and is streamlining Protection and PSEA approaches across all programming. All IOM staff must complete the initial mandatory PSEA training and IOM will take measures to have a similar structure in each project location. As for the prevention of SEA, IOM has a zero-tolerance policy on SEA, which is in line with the UN policy, and has institutional guidelines on PSEA and reporting mechanism in case of suspected SEA, as well as guidelines from IOM's Institutional Framework for Addressing GBV in crises. IOM staff will also receive refresher training on how to report SEA and ethical and appropriate ways to handle a GBV disclosure and make safe referrals through existing referral pathways. IOM will also inform all beneficiaries of the available feedback and complaint mechanisms, including PSEA reporting. Similarly, IOM PSEA focal staff will take measures to train Implementing Partners on PSEA and establish an accountability structure to report incidents of SEA for support provisions, prevention, and mitigation of incidents, through the internal network of PSEA sub-focal and focal staff at the field, programmatic, and mission levels.
