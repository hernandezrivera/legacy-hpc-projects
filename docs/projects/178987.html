Project Summary

The main goal of this Action is to ensure targeted crisis-affected households, including those in underserved communities, have their core and the essential non-food item needs met, in accordance with the sector’s outcome indicator. The Action will provide mid-to-long-term sustainable resilience to those most in need for items assistance, to maintain their health, privacy and dignity.
WWGVC plans to distribute essential and supplementary NFIs kits to 15,000 highly vulnerable adults and children, including IDPs and returnees in Deir Ez Zor Sub-district: 2000 HHs (15,000 ind, 7,290 males and 7,710 females) will receive a core NFI kits (A.1.1), supported by the distribution of core winter NFI kits (A.1.2)
The distributed items comply with the Shelter / NFI specifications. Beneficiaries will be selected according to vulnerability criteria set in close consultation with the target population. Different stakeholders, including people in need, community leaders and other local actors will participate in setting these criteria, to ensure transparency, acceptance and accountability. Selected HHs will include female-headed HHs, HH including elderly people, and/or people with disabilities, and/or infants.
In order to respond to the latter, and to integrate the action with education and protection programs currently delivered by GVC in Deir-Ez-Zor, the project targets the most vulnerable HHs among GVC’s beneficiaries’ database. identified through WWGVC’s Education program’s database of out of school children or children at risk of dropping out. The intervention is also designed based on WWGVC’s needs assessment conducted on families of children targeted by GVC education programs. The analysis revealed that a high percentage of HHs do not have the ability to afford the basic necessary non-food items and clothing. GVC has a base in Deir-Ez-Zor able to implement and closely monitor the proposed activities. All contents of the kits are purchased from local vendors in Syria.
The Action will be conducted adopting safe measures to prevent the spread of COVID-19, in line with relevant Sectors’ standards, stated in the Recommendations for adjusting NFI & Emergency Shelter Distributions in the context of Covid-19 outbreak (May 2020).
GVC will coordinate with SARC, MoE, NFI and WASH Sectors as well as other present humanitarian actors (including UN Agencies, i.e. UNICEF and UNHCR) to ensure information sharing, and avoid any duplication and overlapping during implementation.

4 - Needs

Needs have been assessed through UNOCHA’s HNO and other reports/updates, along with an updated needs assessment targeting families whose children benefit from GVC’s education programs in Deir-Ez-Zor. With a 78% drop in the value of the Syrian currency since October 2019, and with the COVID-19 pandemic, people in need of humanitarian assistance has increased to reach 13.4 M according to Syria HNO 2021. 82% of households are facing decreased affordability to meet basic needs. 35% (4.69M) of the total people in need are in need of NFI support. Access and affordability of lifesaving NFI such as winter items is diminishing. Deir Ez Zour governorate is a chronically underserved area as the presence of the humanitarian community is still limited with a severity scale of 3 and total people in need of 26,437 ind.Based on 2021 HNO data, WW-GVC’s assessment confirmed the same. Families with pre-existing vulnerabilities and limited income are affected the most. The population at risk includes female head of Households, youth, and people with functional disabilities who’s access to income is reduced and therefore their affordability to basic non-food items. The majority of interviewees (99.5% of the total of 1000 HHs assessed) declared that they are not able to afford necessary items that would help their family have basic living conditions such as hygiene, core NFI, and most important winter items. Beneficiaries’ selection criterion will focus on the severity of life conditions in each household, taking into consideration the financial status, family composition and presence of vulnerable individuals, in accordance with The HNO of 2021 and standards accepted by the local communities and the UN.

5 - Implementation of activities

The activities planned to be implemented are the following: A1.1.1 Assessment of family needs of identified households in Deir Ez ZourPhone calls assessment to avoid any physical contact and eliminate the risks of C-19 transmission. Phone numbers are retrieved from WWGVC's Education database. A1.1.2 Market assessment of supplies and launching procurement procedureNational level market assessment identifying suppliers able to supply and preposition stock. A1.1.3 Distribution of core NFIs, and core winter items to 2000 HHs(i) Preparation and coordination with SARC to obtain relevant approvals.(ii) Selection and informing beneficiaries of eligibility and distribution plans via phone calls(iii) Creation of detailed distribution plan.(iv) Distribution of items, in cooperation and coordination with SARC in pre-identified schools close to the selected beneficiaries allowing for space for social distances and better control of the distribution process.(v) Monitoring. A1.1.3 Monitoring, evaluation, and reportingStandard monitoring tools will be used to collect information from the field and both qualitative and quantitative data will be collected. The data collection tools will include distribution reports; key informant interviews (KIIs) and observation checklists in order to be able to measure the process, progress, and impact on beneficiaries of the project. Monitoring will be conducted at each step of the project, from items purchase to distribution. GVC staff will be present at each step of the distribution process. GVC will be in charge of conducting and monitoring the distribution of kits in the identified schools in Deir-Ez-Zor city.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

RISK: Tension between host communities, IDPs and returnees, and between households receiving and not receiving the assistance. Likelihood: High, Impact: High, Mitigation: Ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs and not based on status. Selection criteria are transparent and communicated with the community in advance. Distribution partners engage with the communities to sensitize the population. RISK: Abuse of Authority by distribution actors (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion). Likelihood: Hight, Impact: Hight. Mitigation: Ensure the team is aware of the code of conduct. Internal procedure is developed and applied to identify perpetrators. Coordinate with local protection actors and involve them in the planning and conducting the distribution. RISK: Risk of physical harm at distribution points. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation: Well-organized and planned distribution in schools with enough safe space for the distribution. Consult with authorities and communities and agree on the location of distribution that is close and accessible to persons with disabilities. RISK: Lack of accepted documentation may unfairly exclude certain populations. Likelihood: low. Impact: low. Mitigation measures: Assessment is done irrespective of availability of documentation. Households are asked for available documents during the confirmation of eligible phase on the phone and informed and agreed on the methodology of verification.
