Project Summary

Through the Humanitarian Response Plan Project (HRP) 2024, the World Health Organization aims to increase access to quality lifesaving nutrition services for children with acute wasting and tackle the high prevalence of malnutrition in the country. To achieve this, the WHO strategy will focus on providing integrated nutrition, health and WASH services targeting 10% (39729) of children under five suffering from Severe Acute Malnutrition who are likely to develop medical complications, the project will also focus on malnutrition prevention in priority locations with a high burden of acute malnutrition and food insecurity.
WHO’s HRP project interventions will prioritize provision of PAEDS/SAM kits to nutrition partners supporting Stabilization centers and other treatment centers for management of medical complications, deworming campaigns, capacity building of nutrition partners on inpatient management for severe wasting with medical complications, and stabilization center data management as well as support to early childhood development and rehabilitation of children on admission using psychosocial simulation tools.
To ensure benefits of integration is realized, the project will also support improvement of water quality for safe preparation of F75 and F100 in the stabilization centers through provision of water candle filters. Finally, the project will facilitate integration of stabilization centers in health facility services delivery. Overall, the project will facilitate Early detection and treatment of girls and boys under 5 years of age and pregnant and lactating women with acute malnutrition contributing towards the NC sector objective 2 that seeks to increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of wasting for girls and boys under five years of age and PLW in prioritized locations by the end of 2024.

1. Needs

In South Sudan, children and women continue to suffer from acute malnutrition driven by high food insecurity, hyperinflation, and poor macro-economic conditions, along with poor water and hygiene conditions, poor child feeding and caring practices, high child morbidity associated with diarrhea, malaria, and upper respiratory infections, and the effects of gender-based violence on households. The September – November 2023 Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) report suggest that 46% of the country’s population are facing high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above) with 35,000 people in IPC 5 Catastrophe, only 4% of children access the minimum acceptable diet for proper growth and development. In addition, there is poor water and hygiene conditions that exacerbates household nutrition conditions as over 50% of the population lacks access to safe water or sanitation. The 2023 FSNMS and SMART survey findings recorded unacceptable levels of acute malnutrition in children and women with the Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels high above the WHO emergency threshold of 15% in 46 out of 79 counties in five states (Jonglei, Unity, Warrap, Upper Nile and Unity). Eighteen (18) counties including Aweil North, Abyei, Gogrial West, Cuebiet,Awerial Pibor, Nyirol, Canal/Pigi, Ayod, Fangak ,Panyikang, Fashoda,Manyo, mayendit, Rubkona, Koch, Guit and Mayom were categorized as priority one counties based on multiple deprivation. The report also estimates that up to 2.5 million (SAM= 480,000 MAM= 1,170,000) and 870,000 women will be at risk of acute malnutrition in 2024 requiring urgent treatment. The above shocks are in addition to the already very poor performance of the country's economy. South Sudan remains among the poorest countries in the world with 85% of its working population surviving on non-wage agriculture and livestock; four out of five people subsist below the poverty line ($1.90) a day. The government's ability to provide basic services is low and inconsistent across the country due to years of under-investment, climate variability, and conflict, resulting in high dependency on humanitarian aid. There is a general deficiency in service level availability across all sectors due to poor performance of the economy characterized by low production and limited access to essential goods and services, hyperinflation, and erratic market prices for essential goods and services contributing to high protection risk and low quality of life across the country (UNDP 2020) In 2024, the South Sudan Nutrition cluster estimates that 2.5m people will need assistance, however the cluster will target 1.8m among them 397,292 SAM cases, 817,144 MAM cases and 609,328 PLWs. Severe Acute Malnutrition in children is associated with risk of medical complications. It is estimated that up to 10% of SAM cases will likely develop medical complications. Consequently, WHOs Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) 2024 will conduct activities that will enhance delivery of quality lifesaving nutrition services for treatment and prevention of wasting, specifically ensuring Early detection and treatment of girls and boys under 5 years of age and pregnant and lactating women with acute malnutrition. The project will contribute to the sectoral objective 2 that seeks to increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of wasting for girls and boys under five years of age and PLW in prioritized locations by the end of 2024.

2. Project activities

Through the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) 2024, WHO will conduct activities that will enhance delivery of quality lifesaving nutrition services for treatment and prevention of wasting;1. Provision of albendazole to support the two rounds of Vitamin A and Deworming Campaigns 2. Procure and distribute PAEDS/SAM kits to support treatment of medical complications in severely wasted children admitted at stabilization centers 3. Conduct refresher trainings for health workers on inpatient management for severe wasting with medical complication 4. Conduct supportive supervision and on job trainings for health workers supporting the stabilization centers 5. Improve water quality for safe preparation of F75 and F100 in the stabilization centers through provision of water candle filters. 6. Print and distribute stabilization centers reporting tools and job aids to stabilization centers 7. Support integration of stabilization centers in health facility services delivery 8. Train data clerk and managers on stabilization centers data collection, reporting to improve nutrition information on DHIS2 8. Provision of Zinc tablet and ORS to treat diarrhea in children under five 9. Integration of child stimulation and early childhood development in the provision of care for children with SAM at the stabilization centers during rehabilitation through the provision of psycho-social simulation tools.
