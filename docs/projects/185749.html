Project Summary

UNHCR will strive to ensure that Rohingya refugee children aged 3-24 have access to equitable quality education opportunities through the continuation of existing programs by maintaining existing learning facilities, recruiting, training and deployment of teachers, procurement of teaching and learning materials and expansion of learning facilities by construction and maintenance of existing and additional single-story classrooms with sex segregated latrines and WASH facilities. Looking at implementation beyond the Myanmar Curriculum pilot, UNHCR supported the Education Sector with the development of a comprehensive scale up roadmap guiding Education Sector partners in MC scaleup approach. Furthermore, considering COVID-19 and its implications, UNHCR and its partners will implement EdTech solutions. UNHCR will continue ensuring community engagement in the overall project cycle ensuring Age Gender and Diversity (AGD) mainstreaming.
In addition, the transitioning of LCFA level I-II students to Myanmar curriculum (MC) will be a critical undertaking for the second half of the year. As a step forward to ensure the provision of quality education and with the introduction of MC, language and teacher professional development training is required to ensure educators and learners have the required language competencies and expected teaching qualifications, hence teacher professional development incorporating both in-service and ongoing teacher professional development activities, continuation and expansion of language acquisition program for Myanmar and English language as well as scaling up certified mentorship trainings for the education partners’ staff in collaboration with BARD college and BRAC University will be undertaken. Additionally, certified mentorship training for around 2,500 teachers will be executed to equip them with pedagogical knowledge and skills to deliver MC.
UNHCR will continue its host community support through maintaining and operationalizing the Community Technology Access (CTA) and the satellite teachers training facility, constructing, or renovating classrooms and WASH facilities and provide classroom furniture and supplies. UNHCR further will work with sector and partners to ensure a synchronized student data collection system using proGres data base to avoid duplication and ensure reliable education data and analysis. Activities will also enhance school community governance and participation mechanisms, including risk reduction activities; integrate school community-based participatory planning and community awareness, with an emphasis on targeted training on child rights and child safeguarding principles, the role of parents in school enrolment, participation and retention, activities promoting peaceful co-existence and social cohesion such as co-curricular activities and community events.
UNHCR engages with the refugees’ community on a regular basis through the Age-Gender-Diversity-Mainstreaming (AGDM) exercise. UNHCR also collects feedbacks through post distribution surveys and protection profiling exercises.
In addition, UNHCR has the following mechanisms in place to received feed-back from the refugees:
•Rohingya refugees are provided a helpline (+88 017 359 3300) to speak directly with a UNHCR protection staff on various thematic issues. The issues raised were channelled to various sections within UNHCR to seek appropriate solutions.
•Suggestion box are in place in each of learning centre where the parents and community members can share their complain and suggestion for further improvement.
•The community are being consulted periodically through FGDs and KIIs to identify their opening on the services provided as well as identify the area of improvement.
•UNHCR monitors and evaluates its intervention through Multi-functional team visit in which the parents/ LCMC members play a vital role as an important member of the team.

1 - Identified Needs

As per the joint multi sector needs assessment carried out in August 2021, 44% of households reported that children and adolescents aged 3 – 24 do not have regular access to home based learning. In addition, 40% of households with children and adolescents report not having been enrolled in any learning facility pre-COVID-19. An alarming figure reported is 35% of households reporting that children aged 3- 24 will not be sent back to learning facilities once they will reopen. Coupled with the above problems, retention rates are low, and school dropouts will have a considerable impact due to the extended school closures which have resulted in learning loss. Inadequate space to establish additional classrooms to accommodate students in a two shift system and reach out of school aged children, inadequate WASH facilities and lack of qualified and trained teachers remains one of the major barriers which will lead to further deteriorate the quality of education provided. LCFA will continue to be the modality of teaching for the first half of the year and LCFA level III throughout, which is a major obstacle to access to meaningful education for the refugees. While the implementation of the Myanmar Curriculum pilot and the transition of LCFA level I and II remains a positive development, retraction of approvals on the expected date of commencement/rollout remains an issue of concern to the community as well as the education stakeholders. In addition, the language barrier has been a persistent issue in the camp context where Rohingya is the only spoken language that all refugees understand and prefer. While all the teaching and learning materials are in a non-native language, English, and Burmese, teaching in a multilingual setting is very challenging especially for facilitators/teachers with no qualification, and professional certified training on pedagogy, language, and content matter.Learning facilities were reopened after 18 months of closure which had an overall impact on learning loss and resending children back to learning facilities. In addition, due to the closure, UNHCR has received reports of early marriage, teen pregnancy, child labor and children in general engaged in harmful practices. The consequences of the above could lead to student dropout and lack of focus in the classrooms. Following the reopening approval on 22 September 2021, education is provided only one day a week with three shifts a day which limits the number of days a student spends in the learning facility and contact hours. In addition, LCFA level 1, remained closed from the onset of 2020 leaving a large portion of students at home. Therefore, students are missing vital learning opportunities, partners cannot provide psychosocial support to both students and educators, limited scope to provide clear communication. Technology based education services cannot be fully implemented due to government restriction and resource constraints, poor internet connectivity, lack of knowledge among teachers/facilitators on use of technology. This has created barrier to introduce EdTech interventions to continue education services.Besides, the poor enrollment rate for adolescent and youth signals a concern from the community which has been emerging as to the adequacy and appropriateness of the available education services for the refugees. For instance, during FGDs, concerned parents have asked progression pathways and opportunities available and why their children were only allowed to study informal curriculum as well as adolescent and youth do not have access to appropriate skills training. Additionally, disengaged adolescents and youth are predisposed to significant protection risks including worst forms of child labor, forced marriages, sexual gender-based violence, the risk of human trafficking, negative coping such as drug abuse among other hordes of risks.
