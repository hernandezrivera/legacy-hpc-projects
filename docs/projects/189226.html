Project Summary

This project proposes intervention for (GP,GBV, HLP and CP AORs).
Repeated episodes of sub-national and regional conflict of Juba, Terekeka, Kajo Keji, Yei, Upper Nile State counties of Longechuk, Maiwut, Ulang, Nasir, Malakal, Baliet, Manyo, Fangak, Pibor, Pochalla, Twic East, Ayod, Akobo, Uror, Bor, Aweil West, Aweil East, Aweil Centre, Aweil North, Tonj North, Tonj East, Tonj South, Yirol, Rumbek North, Rumbek East, Rumbek Center and Wulu counties of South Sudan., coupled with general insecurity and adverse weather in South Sudan have led the country into a protection crisis for many years. According to UN-OCHA, an estimated 4 million South Sudanese remain displaced, with an estimated 2 million among them being internally displaced, up from 1.7 million the year before. Apart from displacement, killing of men, women and children, high levels of violence, rape, as well as abductions have been reported in multiple parts of the country, hence increasing cases of housing, land and property rights violations, gender-based violence, sexual exploitation and abuse, as well as other human rights abuses.
Cattle raiding conflicts, displacement by flooding, clashes over housing land and property, as well as the Covid-19 pandemic, have further worsened the humanitarian situation. According to the HNO 2022, 8.9 million people, are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 since 2021. At least 20% are in IPC4 and 1% in 1PC 5 bordering catastrophic proportions. A rising number of IDPs and returnees has worsened the protection situation, with UN-OCHA estimates putting the number of persons in need of protection services at 5.6 million.
Armed violence in Unity, Jonglei, Western Bahr el Ghazal, Central Equatoria and Western Equatoria throughout 2021 and in 2022 has further worsened the humanitarian situation and posed challenges in accessing basic services, resulting in a multifaceted protection crisis.
Women and children are sexually abused by family enemies during cattle raising and cases of revenge killing. Such cases are not reported due to shame and embarrassment. Abused women lack access to essential services in GBV case management, and children suffer unattended. Displaced communities lack access to basic needs, stay in congested areas without access to water and sanitation and have no access to HLP redress mechanisms. Congestion for those internally displaced does not provide privacy to women and girls. Children have invariably been affected by the repeated cycles of violence and are equally in need of protection services.
Other protection concerns that have increased include child labor, child marriage, human trafficking, grave violations such as killings, targeting of civilians, child abuse, restriction of freedom of movement, forced recruitment, abduction, constraints on safe and equitable access to services and HLP.
This project will target 100,000 Individuals for General Protection, GBV, HLP and Child Protection across 37 counties and one administrative areas segregated by gender in Juba, Terekeka, Kajo Keji, Yei, Panyijiar, Leer, Mayendit, Rubkona, Ruweng Administrative Area, Koch, Guit, Longechuk, Maiwut, Ulang, Nasir, Malakal, Baliet, Manyo, Fangak, Pibor, Pochalla, Twic East, Ayod, Akobo, Uror, Bor, Aweil West, Aweil East, Aweil Centre, Aweil North, Tonj North, Tonj East, Tonj South, Yirol, Rumbek North, Rumbek East, Rumbek Center and Wulu counties of South Sudan.
Coalition for Humanity proposed the following activities:
1. Protection monitoring, assessments and analysis of protection, HLP rights, and human rights violations
2. Provide capacity building trainings on HLP rights among displaced persons as well as alternative conflict resolution mechanisms
3. Provide capacity building trainings on PSEA and Psychological First Aid (PFA) and establish mechanisms to monitor and report PSEA
4. Provide legal counseling and case management to persons who have HLP issues as well as those with cases related to GBV, Early Child Marriage and SEA
5. Community awareness on GBV, ECM and HLP through outreach activities
6. Pursue case management, referral and individual protection assistance, including for persons with disability
7. Provision of Psychosocial Support (PSS)
8. Strengthening Community based structures / Networks and outreach activities.

1 - Needs

According to UN-OCHA, an estimated 4 million South Sudanese remain displaced, with 2 million among them being internally displaced, up from 1.7 million the year before. Apart from displacement, killing of men, women and children, high levels of violence, rape, as well as abductions have been reported in multiple parts of the country, hence increasing cases of housing, land and property rights violations, gender-based violence, sexual exploitation and abuse, as well as other human rights abuses. Cattle raiding conflicts, displacement by flooding, clashes over housing land and property, as well as the Covid-19 pandemic, have further worsened the humanitarian situation. According to the HNO 2022, 8.9 million people, are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 since 2021. At least 20% are in IPC4 and 1% in 1PC 5 bordering catastrophic proportions. Human rights violations, conflicts, natural hazards, limited access to livelihoods, and rising number of IDPs have worsened the protection situation, with UN-OCHA estimates putting the number pf persons in need of protection services at 5.6 million. Armed violence in Unity, Jonglei, Western Bahr el Ghazal, Central Equatoria and Western Equatoria throughout 2021 and in the early months of 2022 has further worsened the humanitarian situation of huge portions of the population and posed challenges in accessing food, drinking water and basic services, resulting in a multifaceted protection crisis. Covid-19 has complicated humanitarian response across the country, while close to a million persons have further been displaced by flooding. An estimated 52% of the affected population are children. Numerous cases of housing land and property, forceful evictions, and forceful occupancy remain unresolved, leading to tension. Most women and children are sexually abused or abducted by family enemies, during cattle raising and during cases or revenge killings. Such cases are normally not reported due to the shame and embarrassment. Most of the time women lack access to essential services in GBV case management. The displaced communities lack access to basic needs and stay in congested areas, without access to water and sanitation. The congestion does not provide privacy to women and girls. Other protection concerns that have increased include child labor, child marriage, human trafficking, grave violations such as killings, targeting of civilians, grave violations against children, restriction of freedom of movement, forced recruitment, abduction, constraints on safe and equitable access to services, housing, land, and property. IDPs, refugees and returnees are putting pressure on the limited resources by host communities. The conflict over limited resources has augmented the cases of GBV, sexual exploitation and abuse. During movement due to floods and conflicts, families have been separated, households are living in insecure places with women, children, people with disabilities and the elderly at risk. Communities depend on humanitarian aid for survival. During distribution of food and NFIs, women and girls are exposed to GBV, sexual exploitation and abuse, which calls for constant protection monitoring, GBV risk analysis, protection risk mitigation and reporting. The protection cluster has identified Ayod, Cueibet, Fangak, Gogrial East, Kapoeta East, Mayendit, Mayom, Pibor, Rubkona, Rumbek North, Terekeka, Tonj East, and Tonj North as being IPC phase 5 (catastrophic), while the rest are in IPC 4. Coalition for Humanity has a strong presence in Unity and Upper Nile States as well as Central Equatoria and Northern Bahr El Ghazal. This will enable it to address the above gaps. This project will target 100,000 Individuals for general protection, GBV, HLP and Child Protection across 37 counties and one administrative area segregated by gender: 20,000 men, 30,000 women, 30,000 girls and 20,000 boys.

2 - Project activities

Coalition for Humanity will deploy both static and mobile response to implement activities and achieve the proposed targets across different counties. Implementation will be based on context and feasibility:1. Protection monitoring, assessments and analysis of protection, housing, land and property rights, and human rights violations to inform response and advocacy – Coalition for Humanity will conduct monthly protection assessments, protection analysis and human right violations to inform response and advocacy and protection monitoring of the conducted activities to evaluate and measure the progress. Beneficiaries will be reached through KIIs and FGDs. CH team will also carry out direct observations, safety audits, meetings, community mobilization and home visits.2. Provide capacity building trainings on Housing, Land and Property rights among displaced persons as well as alternative conflict resolution mechanisms – Local authorities, women and youth leaders will be trained by CH legal officers on housing, land and property rights as well as the redress mechanisms available to persons displaced by either violence or flooding, and whose property and housing might have been taken over by invaders. 3. Provide capacity building trainings on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Psychological First Aid (PFA) and establish mechanisms to monitor and report PSEA in the school environment: Coalition for Humanity will advocate for PSEA code of conduct mainstreaming it in all work places, in schools and in the community. Will hold forums with employers and partners to ensure code of conduct is adhered to. CH will develop a PSEA pocket guide to be utilized by employers. We will establish referral pathways and reporting mechanisms for PSEA and offer a day training. 4. Provide legal counseling and case management to persons who have housing, land and property issues as well as those with cases related to GBV, Early Child Marriage, Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, among other human rights violations. Cases beyond the scope of CH legal officers will be referred to other humanitarian actors in areas of operation.5. Community awareness through outreach activities – Conducting awareness raising in the community to sensitize community on the available redress mechanisms and actions they can take on critical protection concerns, rights violations protection risks, referral pathways, redress mechanisms, and how to enhanced resilience in the community. Coalition for Humanity will reach people throw radio talk shows, door to door and community Public Address System6. Pursue case management, referral and individual protection assistance, including for persons with specific needs – Cases that need special attention will be referred to partners in the region who can handle them. CH GBV and protection officers will refer child protection issues, GBV cases that require specialized attention and eviction from housing or land that are beyond its scope.7. Provision of Psychosocial Support (PSS) - Coalition for Humanity will hold 2 PSS sessions per day, twice per week. The PSS sessions will hold a maximum of 10 participants per session, sitting 2M apart. Participants will be trained on how to stitch face masks from locally available material for their protection8. Strengthening Community based structures / Networks and outreach activities – Different leaders have different roles in the community. The project will target women leaders, youth leaders, opinion leaders from religious groups, teachers and local authority. The training will focus on their role in Protection/GBV, how to identify protection concerns, how to seek further assistance, the protection referral pathway, and how to resolve protection concerns.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project has designed a house-to-house monitoring form to be used by community leaders and hygiene promoters, the form will be used to follow upon households that are not able to use suggestion box, they will provide means in which they can be contacted by phone, or will give verbal feedback to leaders and hygiene promoters to document. Coalition for humanity has a PSEA code of conduct signed by all project staff. All humanitarian staff, community leaders will be taken through a 1-day training on PSEA and sensitize the community members are also sensitized on their right to access the humanitarian assistance without being exploited.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will hold inception meetings with community members and brief community about project objectives. The project will also hold community dialogue days and forums to give feedback to the community members. The project will establish a suggestion box and telephone contact for communication. The project will conduct end of project survey door to door to get feedback in beneficiary satisfaction.
