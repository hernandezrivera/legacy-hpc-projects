Project Summary

MedGlobal will provide essential primary healthcare services at the Health Post operated by MedGlobal in Camp 24 as per the Minimum Essential Service package (Health Post level) following clinical guidelines of Bangladesh and Sphere Standards. Besides, prevention and treatment of communicable and non-communicable diseases will be prioritized. Special health camps and telemedicine service sessions will be organized to cater to special health needs. MedGlobal will also engage CHWs in raising community-level awareness, promotion and increasing preparedness on health issues and emergency response.
From the MedGlobal Health Post in Camp 24, Leda, Teknaf -minimum essential primary healthcare services required as per health sector guideline will be provided that includes outpatient curative care, maternal and child health, adolescent health, family planning, gender-based violence, diseases, mental health and psychosocial services, capacity building for local health care professionals, and community health workers such as case-follow up and public health education keeping gender, disability and diversity inclusion considerations in programming. The activities and outputs will be monitored using the KOBO toolbox and internal clinic census, and reports will be made available to the Health Sector. The project also intends to provide crucial medical supplies and equipment to other camp facilities. All activities will be prepared by strategies of the Health Sector to ensure that they are consistent with the Health Sector's priorities and strategies in Camp 24. MedGlobal will prioritize global advocacy by showcasing outcomes and lessons learned from the activities. To ensure accountability to the affected population the following steps will be taken:
(1) A continuing logic model planning process based on needs assessments and baseline surveys to involve the community.
(2) Rigorous audits and budgets to ensure financial transparency; and
(3) Program monitoring and evaluation to assure program quality management.
(4) Protection, Age, Gender, and Diversity (AGD), disability inclusion, accountability to affected populations, PSEA, disaster risk management, and environment and ecosystem rehabilitation for addressing the impacts of climate change to be mainstreamed in the MedGlobal 2022 sustainable operation strategy. At the health post, MedGlobal's work concentrates on 51 percent of women and includes maternity care for young mothers, as well as family planning. Gender, sex, disability, diversity, and age are reported in the statistics. MedGlobal is already performing needs/baseline evaluations for 2024, with women, girls, disability, diversity, and other age groups in mind, and we will aim to incorporate the results into our next logical framework model and project management cycle. We already conducted multiple COVID-19 capacity projects in 2020 and 2021 including supporting Bangladesh government health facilities e.g., the Upazila Health Complex with Central Oxygen Systems and other medical equipment and supplies to health facilities in the FDMN camps, and we spent roughly $505,000 on the projects. MedGlobal is committed to building the capacity of the health sector partners. In this JRP MedGlobal with collaborate with the health sector and SRH working group in building local capacity for reducing maternal and neonatal mortality and morbidity.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

According to Existing Refugees + New Arrivals: Age and gender breakdown by Upazila , 2024 total 101,298 Rohingyas are currently sheltered within in 33 camps in Cox’s Bazar. Ensuring a minimum essential health service package remains a top priority for 2025. However, the periodic onset of communicable diseases in the camp setting continues to create additional challenges to health and other service provision. Managing vector-borne disease control, communicable diseases, and non-communicable diseases are some of the key priorities in the current response. As the crisis has reached its seventh year, people with vulnerability have faced the crisis differently. As per the Public Health Needs Assessment (PHNA) in Rohingya Refugee Camps 2024-2025 almost 90% reported that they needed health care in the prior 3 months of reporting. The general morbidity/disease distribution pattern has remained consistent where Acute Respiratory Infection, Dengue, skin diseases, Diabetes and Hypertension of the top 5 health conditions that have a high impact on the public health status along Rohingya camps. Rohingya community reportedly experienced challenges in accessing education, healthcare, food, shelter support, humanitarian assistance, employment, and social interactions due to their impairment. Most households reported barriers to accessing healthcare, by households with at least one member with a disability were long waiting times, unavailability of specific medicine, treatment, or service needed, not receiving correct medications, and health facilities being too far away. Thus, health service delivery is not reaching certain geographic areas within the camp and certain populations are not accessing needed health services when they need them.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) will be ensured during the different phases of the project. At the design and planning phase, the need assessment reports such as periodic situation reports published by ISCG, will be utilized in addressing the community need for support as the documents are readily available to save scarce resources. At the implementation level, consultations will be conducted with the beneficiaries. Special hot line will be set up for providing any feedback at the program implementation level. Besides, written feedback mechanisms will be installed such as complaint registers or complaint boxes will be maintained for any type of feedback. Special consideration will be given to maintaining confidentiality and neutrality in addressing the community feedback and complaints. MedGlobal will work with other organizations, advocacy groups for disability groups, and/or specific age groups, in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the responses as well as designing ways they may meaningfully participate in influencing or creating this response. Periodic coordination meetings will be held among various groups regarding the services available and mechanism of accessing the services. These feedbacks will be the basis for input from the community in terms of influencing the programming. The project will adopt all the structural and behavioral orientation required to tailor the needs of various age and gender groups. The results of these steps will be measured based on the data that will be generated.
