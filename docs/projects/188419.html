Project Summary

WVSR plans to continue provide life-saving comprehensive WASH package for IDPs living in displacement sites across NWS/NES. Water trucking, solid waste management and hygiene supplies/cash/voucher distribution will be provided in the subdistricts of Azaz, Daret Azza, and Atareb (8 IDP locations), Harim (3 Ikhaa Hamaa, Al Khan & Al Kurah camps), Maaret Tamsrin (Rabee Masrounah & Harbanoush), Ras Al Ain (Tal Halaf village), Tell Abıad (Amin village)and future sites. In some of these locations such as Harim, Dana and Azaz there are plans for new water networks in the settlements and towns through connection to existing and new water points/elevated storage tanks to allow for the phasing out of water trucking to a more sustained and durable solution, 8 water treatment stations will be supported in subdistrict in Azaz(4), Janudiyeh(2), and Tell Abıad(2), through the operation (staffing, chlorine and fuel supply), maintenance of generators and submersible pumps and electricity. Some of these sites have potential to provide a solarized alternative of energy to support using hibernate system decreasing the electricity costs of running the systems. In addition to O&M support, there will also be capacity building and feedback consultative meetings arranged to serve as a knowledge sharing with local council, water station management and community focal points on various topics such as cost recovery, maintenance and reporting. Desludging activities are proposed in IDP sites in Azaz (Al-Ryan, Talil & Al), and Atma village, Dana subdistrict. Sewer network rehabilitation and expansions are proposed in the subdistricts of Azaz (8 IDP locations), Dana, and Ras Al Ain. In 2018, WV notably piloted the construction of a wastewater treatment plant (first of its kind) in Sejjo camp with private funds where treated water it acceptable for agricultural purposes. From learnings and ongoing O&M WV also plans to repeat this piloted approach in 2 new locations in NWS this time powered through renewables. On the institutional setting, WV plans to rehabilitate and construct WASH facilities in 32 schools: Ras Al Ain and Tell Abiad (8), Ariha & Jisr-ash-shugur (24) in particular gaps identified for persons with disability. There will be 18 Healthcare facilities in Idleb with proposed improved WASH and IPC support along with HCF waste management for hygiene Promotion, WV will support various hygiene and climate smart promotion interventions and RCCE sessions in various formats (from school messaging, posters in streets, on water tanks, Solid Waste vehicles, social media) with tailored messaging to the appropriate setting and age groups. WV is focusing more on ‘nudges and cues’ such as painted steps to hand washing stations at the school setting to social distancing markers at communal water point to serve as reminders for healthy behaviors. For children, WV will use its existing partnership with Sesame Workshop, in 10 informal learning centers in IDP camps in Azaz, Ras Al Ain, Tell Abıad Sub-districts, with plans to expand to the formal school setting. The Sesame Workshop program educates and empowers children to healthy lives, through promotion of behavioral changes in sanitation and hygiene through children play-based learning materials, and multimedia content program developed through Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation. There are also social-emotional objectives, including coping with big feelings, building resilience, fostering hope, developing conflict resolution skills, and practicing kindness and mutual respect, along with climate adaptive water conservation messaging. In summary all of these interventions will improve access to essential WASH services, reduce harmful coping strategies; that have adverse cascading effects on individuals and communities in terms of public health, environment, inequality, poverty and provide a foundation for infection, protection and control of C19 and reduce transmission of water-bourn illnesses and ultimately save lives

4 - Needs

WASH gaps include: 1.4 million in need of water system rehabilitation/operational support; 632,000 IDPs in need of water trucking support; 1 mill. in need of sewerage rehabilitation or construction and 1.2 mill. people in need of solid waste management support. Water trucking are the only source of water for 73% of IDP site residents and only 24% HHs are connected to simple sewer networks, indicating a continued need for medium-term investment in IDP sites infrastructure to provide more sustainable, affordable, and safer way to supply water and dispose wastewater[1]. Shortage of electricity remains the root cause for water supply systems underperformance or cessation. Over 30% of garbage is inappropriately disposed or collected with low frequency, while the dumpsites often pose serious health, environmental and protection risks.[2] At least 70% of sewage is discharged untreated and at least half of the sewerage systems are nonfunctional [3] thus Investment in sanitation infrastructure is critical. WASH needs in schools remain very high. Out of the children that complained about WASH-related issues, 63% mentioned inadequate conditions for handwashing (no soap, water, or handwashing station itself), so critical for maintaining hygiene, especially during the COVID-19 pandemic. Most all WVSR proposed sites have the WASH severity score of 4. [1] HNO 2022 [2] Mid-2021 WASH Household Assessment (countrywide). [3] Ministry of Water Resources, 2021.

5 - Implementation of activities

This intervention will follow a mixed methodology (direct and indirect) of implementation where WVSR and its implementing partner will divide the roles and responsibilities to ensure the best support and maximum impact provided for the targeted beneficiaries. The implementation partner will be responsible for the camps/community's selection, announcement, registration and beneficiaries’ registration management. WV will be responsible for quality assurance, financial and data management, vendor contracting and regular market mentoring. For construction works this will be implementing through qualified only contractors whom are selected being lowest price, all design and specifications with be in accordance and coordinated with the local municipal departments water,sanitation,health which during implementation contractors will be monitored by engineers of WVSR and its implementing partners to ensure quality of construction and rehabilitation per designs from the bids tendered. For school settings, WV will ensure coordination with the Ministry of Education on targeted schools and ensure approvals for new curriculum such as the Sesame hygiene approach along with construction works in rehabilitation of WASH facilities in schools. WASH in Healthcare will also fall in accordance to ministry of health. WVSR will not do standalone WASH in HCF without guaranteeing that another partner, Ministry of health is also providing health specific support in that particular healthcare facility

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

The WASH Sector will ensure that the ’do no harm’ principle is systematically applied.Potential protection risks related to the implementation of the WASH related activities,along with a description of the relevant mitigation measures and resources requiredfor monitoring those risks have been identified.4 WASH partners will take adequatemeasures to minimize exposure of the most vulnerable groups, including women,adolescent girls, children, people with disabilities, female-headed households, togreater protection risks, particularly GBV. WASH staff will be trained on key GBVconcepts and referral pathways. Women, adolescent girls and people with disabilitieswill be consulted during project cycle steps to facilitate feedback. High level ofexplosive hazard contamination close to water and sanitation infrastructure, orcontamination of accumulated garbage/rubbles, which potentially put humanitarianstaff in danger during WASH assessments, rehabilitation works, andoperation/maintenance will be considered. Where relevant, WASH staff will be trainedon explosive hazard risk mitigation, while risk education sessions and awarenessraisingmaterials for beneficiaries will be integrated as much as possible into WASHprogramming. Risk of underage labour are mitigated through proper registrationsystem implementers; add zero tolerance disclosure on child safeguarding and labourin the contracts; check and register civil documentation.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

WVSR adopts a zero-tolerance approach towards SEA and it is committedresponsibility at all times to ensure that all people – women, girls, boys and men aretreated with dignity and respect, furthermore, uphold humanitarian principles andensure the integrity of the humanitarian operation. Support will be provided for thePrevention of Sexual Exploitation & Abuse mechanisms to protect beneficiaries andtheir caregivers which include the technical support related to the safeguarding andPSEA available policy review and development with partners, ToT and training to beprovided for first line workers, assigning field focal points with partners in additionWVSR field and national focal points, raising the awareness of the community inproper way including child friendly material, regular reporting on all related activitiesand incidents in coordination with other clusters in addition of PSEA network which isled by IOM. Child safety concerns are identified and monitored through regularchild/beneficiary checklists which are used by WVSR field staff which all is constantlymonitored through the project lifecycle. Furthermore, WVSR has its own incidentpreparedness plan which is understood by all staff and partners and is summarized bythe procedures and actions related to proper responding to safeguarding and PSEAincidents in multiple levels and categories, in addition WVSR uses a standard onlineplatform which is called Ethics Point that ensures the proper and timely response


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

WVSR will consider conflict patterns and contextual analysis as part of decisions forestablishing WASH service provision, ensure safety audit is conducted to ensure safeaccess, engage with local authorities on safety and security in the community, relatedto certain activities. The risks for water, solid waste and desludging trucks timing ofdelivery being not appropriate/reliable which can be mitigated through consultationwith targeted communities (separately women & girls) regarding location, deliveryroutes and its schedule. Also, service provision takes security and safety considerationsby avoiding narrow streets, congested areas; school areas as well as conducting safetytraining with truck drivers/operators. Health risk related to construction and supportof municipal water supply/sewage due to exposure to chemicals which will bemitigated by staff/partners/contractors involved in construction works or working withchemicals wear appropriate PPE and follow established safety regulations. This is inaddition to conducting and repeating safety training for staff/partners/contractors.Unequal distribution of WASH services inadvertently strengthen the position of armedgroups, thus WVSR will establish strong links with authorities and operators topromote equity and “do no harm principle” all the time; Include community andauthorities in targeting; Ensure sensitive information is handled with care.
