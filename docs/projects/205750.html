Project Summary

According to the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) projection, in the lean season projection period from April to July 2024, the food security situation will deteriorate and an estimated 7.1 million people (56.3% of the population) will be severely food insecure (IPC Phase 3 or above). 79,000 people are likely to be in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) while an estimated 2.34 million people are likely to be in Phase 4 (Emergency). The project aims at addressing the food security and livelihood of the most affected people with chronic vulnerabilities worsened by frequent climate-related shocks (severe flooding and dry spells, the macro-economic crisis, conflict and insecurity. Assistance will help affected people to meet their immediate, medium and long-term food security needs of the target communities of IPC level 3,4 and 5.
To ensure the target population recover from food insecurity and the disaster which occurred as a result of flood, conflict, insecurities and displacement, PAH will distribute agricultural inputs such as vegetables kits, staple seed kits and tools to the most vulnerable households as well as multi purpose cash assistance. In addition, PAH will conduct trainings and agricultural extension activities focusing on good agronomic practices, Integrated pest management, post-harvest management and market linkages (value-addition).
Extension workers will be trained as trainers for community farmers. This methodology ensures ownership and long-lasting effects, as the capacity of trainers stays within the community. PAH will introduce different irrigation methods including drip irrigation systems together with community training on their adequate and sustainable use to enhance the copying mechanism during the dry season. The extension workers will be trained on production of quality seed, seed testing, packaging, labelling, pricing, preservation of seeds from weather conditions like humidity and pests and proper storage. Special attention will be given to seed quality testing to ensure high quality of the seeds.
Additionally, to traditional plots, PAH will encourage setting up small-scale vegetable gardens, which can be established at the Nutrition/Education sites. This facilitates planting and irrigation allows higher diet diversity and minimize SAM/MAM cases and support enrolling children to schools.
Fishing has the potential to immediately address food security needs. It enables people to immediately harvest fish from the rivers and swamps in the areas.
In the communities with access to functional markets and availability of cash programming, will be considered. The decision will be based on initial market and community assessments, considering cash vouchers/MPCA or cash for work. Food for asset (FFA) will be conducted in the areas where there are needs and local markets can support the project targeting the establishment of community structures and facilities like schools and infrastructures like community road projects and communal farms establishment.
Food for education and school feeding programme will be considered in the project areas where PAH carries food security and livelihood activities with seeds distribution and vegetable and cereal crops production.
A holistic approach to addressing food security and livelihood, building resilience and long-lasting effects, PAH will integrate interventions with other sectors such as WASH, Nutrition, Education, Protection and S/NFI through combined hygiene and nutrition promotion in addition to screening and referral to nutrition partners on the ground. Education and health will be integrated through training of school hygiene clubs and school gardening and awareness at the schools, nutrition and health centers. Protection will be mainstreamed across all sectors and additional protection team will be supporting the communities.

1. Needs

According to the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) projection, in the lean season projection period from April to July 2024, the food security situation will deteriorate and an estimated 7.1 million people (56.3 percent of the population) will be severely food insecure (IPC Phase 3 or above). 79,000 people are likely to be in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) while an estimated 2.34 million people are likely to be in Phase 4 (Emergency).Given the high levels of severe acute food insecurity in South Sudan, there is a need for immediate scale-up of multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance to save lives and prevent the total collapse of livelihoods in the affected counties, particularly those with a high propoertions of populations in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) and Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity. The most food insecure populations are in locations that have been significantly affected by frequent climate-related shocks (flooding and dry spells), the economic crisis (currency depreciation and high food prices), conflict and insecurity – including the spillover effects of the conflict in Sudan – causing forced displacement, low agricultural production and a reduction in humanitarian assistance.According to PAH multisectoral assessment of July/August 2023, the overall food security and situation needs humanitarian assistance especially in Jonglei’s Canal Pigi where flooding resulted in destruction of farms resulting in General Food Distribution (GFD) to save lives in the crisis affected communities.

2. Project activities

1. Provision of fishing kits: Fishery as an immediate source of food and livelihood: a) Fishing kits' distribution and demonstration on proper use. In case of access to market and availability – cash or vouchers modality will be used. b) Training on fishing techniques, environment preservation, risks mitigation while fishing. Linking trainees with traders to encourage market linkages between fisherfolk and the supply fisher men. c) Training on smoking and other forms of fish preservation. 2. Provision of agricultural inputs: distribution of inputs for the main planting season including crops kits depending on FAO packaging and availability in the Core Pipeline) and vegetable seeds kits. Some of the distributed seeds are resilient to climate shocks like droughts. The beneficiaries will also receive basic tools like Maloda, hoe and watering cans depending on the availability from the core pipeline. Some new nutritive types of vegetables may be introduced to improve the nutritional status. Seasonal fruit trees will be part or the distributed seedlings (2 per HH) for reclaiming degraded lands and improved agroforestry practices. Cash or vouchers modality may be used. 3. Raising stability of crop & reducing post-harvest losses - PAH aims to introduce various technologies (e.g., silos, granary from local materials) and processing equipment training in rural areas. PAH will support identified Artisan groups of farmers through vocational skills training on the production and maintenance of appropriate farm implements and storages equipment. 4. Capacity building: a) farming practices that limit negative impact on the environment and climate, including avoiding cutting trees, burning trees and decomposition of the cut trees and stalks to use them as a source of nutrients, fertilizers and improve the soil fertility. This approach of drip irrigation using recycled materials and allows to limit the negative impact of the agriculture on the environment, avoiding procurement of new items as well as being water efficient and not requiring usage of fuel. b) Training for extension workers and setting up demonstration plots Schools: i) Farmers to farmer’s exchange visits ii) Support in capacity on HH level – training in groups or on the household level on preparing nutritious meals with fish, vegetable, crop, and preservation of food. C) Access to organic pesticides and production d) Trainings on production of organic pesticides from locally available materials such as neem tree, pepper and garlic. PAH will also conduct cooking classes as training on the cooking (preparation) of the different vegetables. During the cooking classes, the different veggies are introduced, as some vegetables are not indigenous to the community6. Food for Education: a) PAH will consider school feeding programme after assessments of needs and the possibility of sustainability and ownership by the community. b) PAH will target education and sustainable health improvement by training school hygiene clubs and school gardening.7. Cash programming will be based on the initial functional markets and community assessments, considering cash vouchers or cash for work. PAH will mainstream cross-cutting issues across all activities focusing on gender mainstreaming, GBV, protection, accountability to the affected population and committing to improving the effectiveness of cooperation with communities. PAH will have an integrated approach with FSL and WASH, NFI, protection and nutrition. PAH will pay attention to community hygiene and nutrition promotion on food hygiene and safe water management, to break the chain of contamination at the household level, nutrition screening and referral of SAM/MAM cases to nutrition centres, protection of most vulnerable and decrease the risks of GBV.
