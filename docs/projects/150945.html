Project Summary

The project will enhance the capabilities of the FGS and Federal Member States to promote human security among local communities. Activities support the Comprehensive Approach to Security, the Transition Plan from AMISOM to Somali Security Forces, Community Recovery and Extension of State Authority and Accountability, as well as wider efforts towards preventing violent extremism. Principle activities include: (i) Conduct clearance and risk education and victim assistance in areas contaminated with landmines and other explosive hazards, that pose a threat to local communities and hamper the delivery of humanitarian aid; (ii) Support SEMA’s capacity to coordinate and manage humanitarian mine action activities, and; (iii) Further enhance the explosive ordnance disposal (EOD) capabilities of national mine action organisations including the Somali Police Forces.

Needs

There is a clear requirement to empower local communities and SEMA to deliver explosive hazard management interventions to protect the lives of IDP’s, returnees and communities that are living in proximity of contaminated areas. Furthermore, the project will provide dignified employment opportunities for marginalised people and contribute positively to their recovery. With consideration to the critical lead-up to presidential elections in 2020-21, as well as with regards to the transition from AMISOM to Somali Security Forces, the enhancement and further development of EOD/IED-Disposal capacities within the Police forces and other national mine action related institutions also remains crucial.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Removing explosive hazards such as landmines, ERW, and remnant IEDs can significantly contribute into the the resilience of the affected communities. Displaced people are generally more vulnerable to the risk of explosive hazards. Making land and other infrastructures such as roads safe from the explosive hazards can be an enabling factor for durable solution. In addition mine action is a very good employment provision opportunity for youth.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Due to the ongoing conflicts and wars that have happened in the past since 1977 significant explosive hazards are left behind in Somalia. Landmine contamination is mainly affecting communities located in border areas between Somalia and Ethiopia while ERW contaminated communities can be found throughout Somalia wherever the armed clashes have happened. Lives and livelihoods of IDPs due to movement and poverty are prone to explosive hazards. Host communities also suffers because explosive hazards blocks their access to the land thus put their lives at risk of being killed or injured. The project will target IDPs that are located closed to the minefields and other ERW contamination by providing risk education and undertaking clearance and some aspects of survivors assistance. The project will ensure employment is provided appropriately to both IDPs and host communities and deliberately make efforts for facilitating inclusiveness of IDPs and other marginalized communities. This can be achieved by adopting the concept of community based approach to explosive hazards management.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The impact of explosive hazards on men, women, girls and boys can be different due to their mobility patterns and different roles and responsibilities in the society. Overlooking the specific gender and age requirements can be devestative. For example vital information could be lost if mine action during surveys misses to consult with youth. The project will make sure gender consideration are taken into account during the design and implementation of the project. Community liaison officers and mine risk education employees will be recruitment both from men and women. All the reporting formats will include gender and age related questions.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Clan based social set up in Somalia can be a significant source of conflict and violence. Mine action interventions can make things worse by overlooking this aspect. The risk of conflict and violence can be mitigated by adopting a community based explosive hazard management . The approach will involve careful analysis of the project location from the conflict and violence perspective. The project will provide specific training to the field personnel who will be working with communities in relation to the project implementation on how to interact with different clans. They will constantly monitor this risk and take appropriate action when needed.
