Project Summary

This proposed project focus in three localities in North Darfur: Malha, Mellit and Um Keddada and is addressing MYHS objectives 1 and 2, specifically:
- Restore and sustain food and livelihood security of vulnerable populations in Malha, Mellit and Um Keddada
- Supporting displaced populations and host communities in Malha, Mellit and Um Keddada in meeting their basic needs and foster access to essential services to improve livelihood and self-reliance.

Needs

In 2018, over 980,000 people in North Darfur are classified in IPC phase 3 and 4. While the 2018 seasonal rainfall across Sudan is reported to be on average (2018 Seasonal Monitor & JJAS Forecast), increasing prices of seeds and livelihood assets as well as long dry mid-season spells are exacerbating Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) food security outcomes, requiring urgent humanitarian interventions to protect livelihood assets and strengthen self-reliance of vulnerable HHs.Compared to the previous year, cereal crop productivity is significantly reduced across all North Darfur (2018 State Ministry of Agriculture’s post-harvest assessment report).The current economic crises in Sudan (limited foreign currency, fuel shortages, high inflation rate) is projected to persist and will continue to reduce household’s purchasing power and limit their access to food. WFP Monthly Market Updates (January - August 2018) reports that average prices of sorghum, goats and groundnuts are steadily increasing, all prices are over 130% compared to 2017. Livestock diseases including PPR, Sheep Pox and HS are affecting gravely the livelihood of the purely pastoral and agro-pastoral communities (COOPI - livestock vaccination campaign report). Reduction in the animal production contributes to the deterioration of food security. Water for production is scarce with few functional structures, often shared between humans and livestock.
