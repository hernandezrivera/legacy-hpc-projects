Project Summary

The project will target a total of 40,173 beneficiaries of which AVSI Foundation aims to reach 30,707children and women In Ikotos county with sustainable malnutrition preventive interventions and a total of 9,466children and women will be treatment/manage for all forms of acute malnutrition.
The project interventions and strategy will contribute to national nutrition cluster objectives through the following:
The project will emphasize on malnutrition prevention through responsive interventions which will address the key drivers of cause of malnutrition based on previous surveys and most recent IPC report. the key outputs will include: strengthening socio behavioral change (SBC) promotion communication through community based approach to improve on appropriate food handling and preparation, and optimal feeding practices among children under five and PLWs; Provision of micronutrient such as (Vitamin caps) supplements to community of Ikotos with poor diet diversity score as per the FSNMS reports, provision of helminth deworming tablets and also provision of blanket supplementary feeding especially during the lean seasons.
The second area of intervention areas of AVSI'S project will be to increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services through the following; robust early detection and referral for treatment of girls and boys under 5 years of age and pregnant and lactating women with acute malnutrition at the govern health units/centers by vetting and training of CNVs how to carry effective malnutrition screening and referral of malnutrition cases to nutrition centers and make home visit for follow up. To ensure quality nutrition services AVSI project will strengthen the capacity of existing CHD health workers through trainings, regular support supervision and onsite mentorship to CHD HWs by AVSI's nutrition project technical staffs (Nutrition officers) to be able to provide effective quality nutrition services. the project will ensure that all nutrition sites have a compliant feedback mechanism for accountability to the affected population.
The project will contribute towards strengthening the CHD capacity in nutrition situation, program monitoring, analysis, and utilization of nutrition information. AVSI's technical staffs will mentor CHD official on the use of DHIS/NIS software. The project staffs will actively support the national FSMNS surveys, conduct community MIYCN barrier analysis survey, and on monthly basis enter facility-based data from the standard M&E tools and share through NIS/DHIS screening, admission, MIYCN counselling etc. to nutrition cluster and other relevant stakeholders and sectors.
For cost effectiveness, AVSI's programing approach relies always on project synergies and integration. Therefore, this proposed project will ensure that testing and treatment for malaria among PLW and children under five years with acute malnutrition are conducted on routine basis in all nutrition sites and provision of ICTN to prevent malaria. WASH facilities will be provided to all nutrition sites, households in the community will be sensitize on hygiene practice and on water treatment using aqua. tab by CNVs. To increase access of nutritious food, the project will promote establishment of climate smart and nutrition sensitive vegetable kitchen gardens at household level. caregiver of children discharge malnutrition management program will be link to nearby social safety net such as GFD, cash vouchers services. The project will ensure all nutrition sites are integration with child stimulation and ECD activities for children with SAM and MAM. To mitigate the risk of GBV, the project will conduct GBV safety audit. Through close collaboration with CHD, children admitted in OTP/TSFP programs with diarrhea will be provided zinc and ORS.

1. Needs

In Eastern Equatoria State where Ikotos county is located, an estimated 364,000 people (37% of the state’s population) are facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity between September and November 2023 as per the IPC analysis, of which 119,000 people are facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, and 245,000 people are facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. Ikotos is among the worst affected 6 counties in Crisis (IPC Phase 3). As per the baseline survey conducted by AVSI for one of the FSL project findings from the farmers group indicates that the key drivers of food insecurity in Ikotos were due to intercommunal conflict that affected agriculture activities, dry spells (unreliable rainfall), and traditional methods of agricultural and limited tools don't allow farmers to open bigger land to produce adequate food that can last for year. Many households in Eastern Equatorial including Ikotos county are also facing unusually high staple food prices which is usually imported from Uganda. In term of prevalence of Malnutrition, Ikotos is among the three worst affected 3 counties of Eastern Equatoria states with counties in IPC AMN Phase 4 (Critical). According to the 2023 FSNMS and SMART survey’s findings, the national Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels remain above the WHO emergency threshold of 15%. Ikotos county with the GAM rate of 18.4% (SAM 5.2% and MAM 12.7%) is among the 46/79 counties whose GAM rate are above the threshold. As per IPC Analysis report (Sept 2023-April 2024) Ikotos county is expected to have a total of 13,090 people. (Children & Women) in need of lifesaving treatment for both severe and moderate cases of malnutrition. Therefore, there is need to strengthened malnutrition treatment in all the CHD health facilities to be able to reach this number 9,465 (72%) with timely quality lifesaving curative services in 2024 with this project. Up to 213,000 people are likely to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity in EES where Ikotos is among the worst affected counties in term of food insecurity. To prevent the vulnerable to becoming malnourished there is need to provide blanket supplementary feeding and general food distribution.Contributing factors of acute malnutrition include high prevalence of diseases with 55% of children reported to have been ill two weeks prior to the FSNMS Round 29 assessment and 50 out of 80 counties reported illness greater than 50% among children. Fever, cough and diarrhea were the leading symptoms reported at 42.4%, 25.4% and 18.7% respectively, and malaria prevalence is still at peak among children. Integrating health care/malaria preventive services such as provision of ORS and Zinc, routine malaria testing at Nutrition sites and distribution of ICTN would halt the prevalence of malnutrition among children under five.Poor access to improved drinking water and sanitation compromises hygiene practices and increases the risk of high incidences of illness leading to acute malnutrition. This therefore requires integrating wash activities such as provision of WASH kits at nutrition sites, water treatment safe for drinking) in nutrition programing to holistically address the causative factors of malnutrition and to realize optimum nutrition outcome. Practices of infants and young children is another high-risk contributing factor to acute malnutrition. All Infant and young child feeding (IYCF) indicators remain suboptimal at in lkotos county, making them another high-risk factor for acute malnutrition. Only 5 children out of 100 are getting the recommended quality and frequency of food required for optimal growth. 34.6% achieved the Minimum Meal frequency while only 21% achieved the Minimum dietary diversity. this therefore requires increased socio behavior change communication to promote appropriate food handling and optimal feeding practices among children under five and PLWs with provision of micronutrient supplements.
