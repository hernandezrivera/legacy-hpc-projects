Project Summary

Currently, AHC is constructing 6 TLS, teachers and SMCs training activities in Maiwut County in collaboration with UNICEF hence this proposed plan is to support the activities into 2024 and integrate the education intervention with our ongoing projects in Nasir. The project is aligned to Education Cluster objectives for 2024 HRP.
In order to achieve the intended goal, the program will implement interventions that will lead to increased access to dignified education for 4160 children; Nasir (M2000 F1160) Maiwut (M500 F500) and youth (aged 3-18) in the target counties. AHC will plan to support 50, 35 and 15 percent of the total beneficiaries (4254) being Host communities, Sudan/Ethiopian returnees and IDPs respectively.
The interventions will be implemented in accordance with Ministry of General Education and Instruction (MoGEI) and other quality standards as set in INEE minimum standards of Education in Emergencies. Community participatory mechanisms will be utilized to promote school enrolment, retention and acceptable transition of learners to next class/school at the end of the school year. The establishment of the 11 semi-permanent learning spaces (6 Nasir and 5 Maiwut) shall be based on the approved MoGEI’s establishment designs, specifications and all engineering requirements stipulated in the design.
AHC will also construct 15 gender sensitive WASH facilities (10 Nasir and 5 Maiwut) in the selected schools. Protective fences will be erected around semi-permanent structures and schools for added safety. The established infrastructure shall also be disability sensitive, gender friendly and in such a way to withstand adverse weather conditions. AHC will work with communities, local county officials, State Ministry of General Education and Instruction (SMOGEI) in the in project implementation through community participatory approaches.
In regard to protection mainstreaming, safety audits will be conducted in the three target locations to map existing and potential risks and possible impact on project activities. The findings will be embedded in project activities and used to advocate for protection of staff, beneficiaries, project supplies and assets. AHC will rely on protection cluster and UNOCHA for security updates. Local authorities will be informed of staff’s movement and project activities well in advance and requested to provide security. AHC will liaise with Community Social/Case Workers to refer any GBV case they come across to the referral pathway.
AHC will orient AAP committees and community leaders on the rights of PWDs and the project’s intention to incorporate CwD as participants and in supporting the project team to identify potential barriers not already considered to be incorporated into project activities. The project will provide equal opportunities to youth, women and girls and other marginalized or at-risk individual and groups with disabilities to participate in decision making in matters that affect them. AHC will also ensure safe programming accordingly to promote safe access to the project interventions in a dignified manner.

1. Needs

The education system in Upper Nile State has suffered immensely from the impact of decades of bitter civil conflicts, which has resulted in the widespread destruction of infrastructure, disruption of learning activities, the breakdown of education and other vital services with subsequent displacement of school-going children across counties and into the neighboring countries. The State is considered by both Government and the International community as a fragile State prone to complex emergencies like deep insecurities, cattle raiding, child abduction and ethnic conflicts (CSRF 2021). African Humanitarian Corps (AHC) education assessment in Maiwut indicated that most young children cannot access schools due to the sheer distances to the learning facilities and the threats imposed by active combatants and armed personnel between the villages. The communities thus recommended the construction of temporary learning space (TLS) nearest to the IDP sites and their host villages and communities as a priority among others. As schools were recovering from Covid-19 related challenges, intense flooding commenced in the country leading to massive displacement of parents, teachers, and pupils, destruction of education infrastructures (classrooms/Latrines/WASH Facilities) and learning and instruction supplies. As of 30th November 2022, 384 schools were either partially or completely damaged by flooding across the country, out of which 107 (27.9%) were from Upper Nile State. The lack of adequate learning spaces and teachers where pupils got displaced due to flooding meant that the majority of them either dropped out of school or could not enroll due to inadequate learning spaces and instructors. South Sudan has the highest rate of primary school-aged children out of school in the world, with an estimated 3.3 million children under the age of 18 facing education barriers in 2023, 53% of whom are girls (HNO 2023). Many barriers to accessing quality education in South Sudan exist and include a lack of schools within a reasonable distance from learners, school facilities such as classrooms in poor condition, inadequate WASH facilities and supplies, lack of female teachers (affects girls’ enrolment), lack of assistive devises for Children with Disabilities (CwD) and issues with teacher absenteeism, inadequate teaching and learning materials (HNO 2023). Education quality is further impacted by the insufficient retention of qualified teachers, who often face irregular and low wages. This project is aimed at decreasing the number of out-of-school boys and girls in selected Payams through the construction of Temporary Learning Spaces (TLS) with inclusive community participation at all levels. The strategy is to use community support in areas of mobilization, planning, participatory monitoring and evaluation to ensure, community ownership, continuity and sustainability of the inteventions in the selected schools. The proposal directly responds to the priorities of the General Education Sector Plan (GESP) 2017-2022 and seeks to address issues of access and equity, quality of education from early childhood development, primary, secondary and AES, and to contribute to the Dakar framework on education for all (EFA), the Sustainable development Goals number four on Ensuring inclusive and Equitable Primary Education.

Incentivized community mobilizers and facilitators will lead community dialogues. These mobilizers will undergo a comprehensive training on the Community Action Cycle (CAC) model.Activity 1.3: Rehabilitate 3 (Nasir 2, Maiwut 1) learning spaces in line with INEE's Minimum Standards; The intended outcome is that crisis-affected children and youth in the target counties/schools have access to education services that is appropriate for their age groups and level of learning.Activity 1.4: Rehabilitate 15 gender sensitive latrines (10 Nasir and 5 Maiwut) in line with INEE and sphere standards; Activity 1.5: Distribute dignity kits to 600 adolescent girlsSpecific focus will include Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) and comprehensive sexuality education. 600 MHM kits will be procured and distributed, accompanied by awareness campaigns on MHM. Each kit contains: re-usable Medium size sanitary Pack (each has 6 Pads); re-usable maxi-size sanitary pack (each has 6 Pads); 3 medium size pieces women underwear; 3 small size pieces women underwear; 800gms multi-purpose bar soap.AHC will also conduct awareness raising on key issues for the girl child and transition to post-secondary education; Activity1.6: Distribute assistive devices to 100 (33 girls and 67 boys) children with disabilities (CwD)AHC will conduct annual community awareness campaigns to promote inclusive education and enhance access for children with disabilities (physical and psychosocial disabilities, ensuring inclusion of children with learning difficulties/additional learning needs) and young mothersActivity 1.7: Establish and equip Child Help Desks within the selected schoolsThese will be trained on psychological first aid (PFA), identification of children needing CP support and safe referrals, to identify and provide immediate support to vulnerable and at-risk learners and refer them to dedicated case workers when necessary. Linked to Education HRP CO2: Children and youth are provided with quality teaching and learning services that support retention and progress through the education cycle. Activity 2.1: Conduct in service training for 48 teachers; Nasir (M5 F33), Maiwut (M2 F8) on child safeguarding, MPHSS and GBV risk mitigation, referral mechanism; Activity 2.2: Distribute ECD Kits to school children in 6 ECD Centres; distribute Recreation Kits to children in 6 primary schools; Activity 2.3: Support 48 Nasir (M5 F33), Maiwut (M2 F8) volunteer teachers with financial incentivesActivity2.4: Distribute teachers Kits to 48 teachers in 11 schools; Linked to Education HRP CO3: Coordination and crisis management capacity is strengthened to promote efficient, accountable, and timely education in emergencies response.Activity 3.1: Conduct training for 8; Nasir (M1 F6), Maiwut (F1) school management committees (SMC) members, Parent Teacher Associations (PTA), and education authorities trained on school management, governance, climate change and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR)
