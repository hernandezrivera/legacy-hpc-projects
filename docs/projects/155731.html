Project Summary

Under FATA transition plan, SIF aims at meeting the humanitarian Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WaSH) needs of 13,535 returnees in the Upper Orakzai tribal district and Sararogha sub district in South Waziristan tribal district by improving access to safe drinking water services through the rehabilitation of water supply schemes; rehabilitation of gender and culturally appropriate, secure and user friendly sanitation facilities for returnees through community participation approach; and hygiene awareness among returnees to improve health & hygiene practices and bring positive behavior change through community health clubs. Institutional WaSH will be supported through the rehabilitation of sanitation facilities and hand washing points at five schools and one health facility the targeted regions.

Needs

SIF has been working in Orakzai district since November 2017 to address WaSH, food security, livelihoods and agriculture needs of the returned families. Over the period, a local dynamics study and multi-sector assessment conducted in March and July 2018 respectively testified the hardships of the returned families. During the needs assessment, 7 villages of returning families were assessed. To date, 35,823 verified families have returned to the Orakzai tribal district. Around 29,000 families are yet to return in the different areas of tribal districts in 2019. Despite official return, the families are still unable to return due to the lack of basic services in the area, such as: - Currently, most of the community members defecate openly. In daylight, women use open compounds (within houses) for defecation. On average, there are between 80-150 houses in one village; and 60% of these houses are lacking toilet and hand washing facilities. The remaining 40% houses have toilet facilities but are not using them due to damaged water supply infrastructure interrupting water supply, and dysfunctional sewerage system.SIF aims at improving access to WASH facilities through rehabilitation of sanitation facilities at household, community, health facilities and temporary learning centers/ schools level, as well as ensure provision of hygiene kits and raise awareness on improved water, sanitation and hygiene practices for behavioral change. Moreover, SIF is planning to launch its Integrated Emergency Assistance (IDEA) project to support 1,060 vulnerable families in Upper Orakzai from January 2019. The project aims at improving access to WASH communal and domestic facilities and increased hygiene awareness. The revival of agriculture land is also aimed to improve food security and livelihood conditions. The SIF funded project will complement the proposed project and expand its response coverage to serve the communities at larger level. During 2017-18, SIF has supported 2,160 families in 11 villages of Upper Orakzai, Ismailzai and Mamouzai through its multi-sectoral project and ensured rehabilitation of 35 WaSH schemes, agriculture & livestock support to 118 and 209 vulnerable families respectively; plants nursery formation for ensuring environmental protection; and improved livelihood of 21 male and female entrepreneurs. These interventions are contributing in ensuring sustainable return of the displaced vulnerable population.Based on the requests received from provincial authorities to expand SIF response in other tribal districts of former FATA, SIF aims at providing emergency WaSH services to the returnees of South Waziristan tribal district, in Sararogha sub district. The area is selected based on recent return taking place there.

Activities

The proposed project objective is to improve access to safe drinking water, sanitation and hygiene services to 13,535 returned persons in Orakzai and South Waziristan Tribal Districts. Following activities will be carried out to meet the set objective:1. Government and communal water supply schemes will be prioritized for the rehabilitation to ensure water supply to 5,138 returnees in Upper Orakzai and 5,160 returnees in Sararogha sub district. Existing Village Development Organizations (VDOs) and Public Health Engineering Department (PHED) will be engaged for rehabilitating schemes in Upper Orakzai and South Waziristan. Construction supplier will be engaged for the rehabilitation of prioritized schemes. WaSH cluster guidelines will be adopted while conducting the proposed activity. 2. SIF will rehabilitate 2 inclusive sanitation facilities, one each in TLCs in Upper Orakzai and Sararogha sub districts. Hand washing points will be also rehabilitated to improve hand washing practices among teachers and students. Proposed TLCs will be prioritized in close coordination with district authorities, Education and PHED. Cluster guidelines, SPHERE standards and UNICEF’s WaSH in Schools strategic plan for FATA 2017-2022 will be adopted for ensuring inclusive sanitation services at TLCs. 3. Sanitation support will be provided to 3,853 returnees in Upper Orakzai and 4,268 in Sararogha sub district by rehabilitating sanitation facilities with the community participation approach. Female headed households, vulnerable families having pregnant & lactating women (PLW), persons with disabilities and elders will be prioritized for the rehabilitation of latrines. Beneficiary households will be engaged in the process by adopting community participation approach, and their contribution will be sought by utilizing manpower and ensuring land allocation. SIF engineer will provide technical assistance during the rehabilitation process. Cluster strategy will be adopted for implementing the activity.4. SIF will identify male/ female Community-based Facilitators (CBFs) and form Community Hygiene Clubs (CHCs) to raise hygiene awareness among 6,422 returnees in Upper Orakzai and 7,113 in Sararogha sub district. Their capacities will be built to work on an autonomous basis to contribute effectively in initiating the behavioral change process through 5-day Training of Trainers. 50 each clubs will be strengthened in both tribal districts. These clubs will be instrumental in building awareness and shaping resilient health and hygiene practices at household, health facility and schools level. Health and hygiene messaging geared towards the reduction of WASH-related diseases will include six critical components: hand washing, safe excreta disposal, safe drinking water practices, water borne disease identification and prevention, basic nutrition practices e.g. balanced diet, food groups; and sustainability of CHCs. Weekly or bi-weekly visits will be carried out by CBFs. Female members will be also targeted for improving awareness on menstrual health. WaSH Cluster reference material or standard guidelines will be adopted to develop culturally appropriate Information, Education and Communication (IEC) material. 5. 5,000 hygiene kits will be distributed among targeted CHC members strengthened in both tribal districts. Special attention will be given to female, PLW, persons with disabilities, and elders. These kits will help in improving hygiene practices. The hygiene kits will be finalized as per Cluster strategy to ensure provision of standardized hygiene package among targeted beneficiaries.
