Project Summary

CARE intends to provide emergency food assistance complemented with emergency food production and livelihoods support to reach an estimated 157,693 people including 49 percent men and 51 per cent women of different age groups with and without disabilities in the targeted locations. The intervention would contribute to the strategic objectives of the HRP and all the three FSL cluster objectives to save lives through food assistance, promote emergency food production and livelihood support and enhance and maintain agro pastoral community emergency coping capacity in South Sudan. CARE will deploy sets of proven approaches and outstanding experience focusing on linking emergency food security interventions with medium- and longer-term livelihoods and market-based approaches. CARE places women and girls at the front and centre of all our work to enhance impacts of all interventions conducted across humanitarian and development projects. CARE beliefs than when women and girls have equal access to resources, they lift their families and communities out of poverty. In this project CARE will focus assistance to those who need them most including child headed households, persons with disabilities, women headed households within the areas of displacements, vulnerable host communities under IPC 3+ food insecurity levels, returnees and refugee caseloads. Households and vulnerable populations within the severity food insecurity areas of severely flooded and drought and conflict affected areas would be the priority locations of our project targets. In the programme cycle, CARE would provide emergency food assistance through cash and voucher (CVA) system to populations falling in the IPC 3+ and areas of extreme concerns including Jonglei, and Unity states complemented nutrition sensitive programming and kitchen gardening around the Nutrition facilities to promote appropriate feeding best practices. While in the areas of very high and high concerns respectively, CARE would provide emergency food production and early recovery livelihoods support and resilience programming to build absorptive and adaptive capacities of crises affected population. CARE International is a global facilitator of village savings and loan associations (VSLA) scheme for recovery and resilience building for humanitarian affected populations. In this cycle, CARE would diversify and restore the livelihoods of affected women, men, girls and boys including people with and without disabilities to enable them to access an alternative source of capital investments and improve savings. In the Equatoria, particularly Eastern Equatoria (Greenbelt) and Western Bahr el Ghazala, CARE will promote the Farmer Field and Business School (FFBS) approaches to provide capacity building supports to smallholder farmers, herders and producer groups to increase their productivity, improve resilience, adapt to climate change, diversify diets, and boost nutrition. CARE’s FFBS system is a participatory, women-focused extension approach that helps women farmers build skills necessary to increase production; access markets and sell at competitive prices; collaborate with each other; and engage in beneficial and efficient decision-making processes. It also transforms the status and recognition of women by providing the support they require to be successful farmers, business-people, leaders, and agents of change. The VSLA saving model will be integrated across all the FBBS and producer group systems.

1 - Needs

In South Sudan, since 2019/2020, trends of severity and magnitude of food insecurity have worsened pushing many communities and households above IPC 3+ food insecurity thresholds. The protracted crisis since December 2013 and consecutive years of flooding, severe localized conflict events and continued indirect impact of COVID 19 pandemic had significantly limited commercial and humanitarian flows of food commodities and services, while current economic crisis (food commodity price hikes) is impacting people’s ability to access markets. Increased sub-national violence in many parts of the country, particularly in Jonglei, Unity, Western Equatoria (Tumbura) and Eastern Equatoria (Magwi) states have continued to disrupt economic livelihoods, market and agro-based farming opportunities. The ration cuts by WFP in 2021 from 70 to 50% and the reduction of duration of food rations from 12 to 9 months have affected IDPs the most due to funding gaps that have also disrupted people’s livelihoods and food security. According to the recent Food Security and Livelihood (FSL) sectoral analysis, an estimated 8.3 million (including the refugees) people are expected to experience severe food insecurity by the depth of the lean season (May – July) 2022. This indicates an increase of 7 per cent above the 7.7 million people in need in 2021. The counties with greatest severity in food insecurity amplified by compounded shocks and areas of extreme concerns in 2022 include: Canal/Pigi (10%), Fangak (35%), Pibor (10%) all in Jonglei, and Leer (5%), Mayendit (5%) in Unity state and Tumbura (15%) in Western Equatoria. Several counties in Upper Nile, Eastern Equatoria, Western Bahr el Ghazal, Central Equatoria, Lakes, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Warrap states are also in extreme concern, very high and high concern respectively according to the report. The main drivers of severity of food insecurity are floods and localized dry spells, inflation and high food prices, and the continued disruption of livelihoods due to violence or the fear of violence and household stressors (such as death of the bread winner, gender-based violence, and morbidity and mortality due to poor hygiene and lack of access to essential basic services), which are greatly amplified by the continuous effect of shocks. In South Sudan, 95 per cent of livelihoods depend on traditional rain-fed agriculture, crop farming, pastoralism and animal husbandry. Climate induced shocks (floods events, localized dry spells and conflicts for grazing areas and farming) have increased violent conflicts among agropastoral communities and have led to an increase in livestock mortality and a decline in the agricultural productivity. Among the most affected people are child headed households, persons with disabilities, older people and women headed households. South Sudan ranks amongst the top ten countries in the world most vulnerable to climate impact, including droughts and flooding. Flooding has been a major driver of displacement followed by conflicts. An estimated 835,000 people in 33 out of 78 counties in 8 states between May and December 2021 were reportedly displaced and more than 37,000 tons of crops were destroyed and nearly 800,000 livestock were killed. Households in the severely flooded areas of Unity (208,000), Jonglei (278,000) and Upper Nile (126,000) will require high levels of support in 2022. Similarly, households in the most drought affected pastoral and conflict affected farmers in Eastern Equatoria (Greater Kapoeta) and Pibor areas would also require high levels of support inform of emergency food assistance complemented with emergency livelihoods and livestock support across farming, livestock and fishing communities for medium and longer term to build both absorptive and adaptive capacity to mitigate such extreme levels of food insecurity. As of December 2021, there are 2 million displaced persons across South Sudan either due to flooding or conflicts.

3 - Project indicators

CARE has a robust monitoring and evaluation system that will be used to track project activities and measure performance. During project inception, the Country MEAL Manager and the M&E Officer assigned to the project will develop a monitoring framework. The monitoring framework will have detailed indicators to be tracked, detailed implementation plan, frequency of data collection, staff/community groups involved, how and where the data will be collected from. Description of indicators will be made user-friendly to ensure that the project beneficiaries, especially women, are able to participate effectively in both the targeting and monitoring process. CARE will adopt WFP and FAO mandated tools such as SCOPE registration model, distribution forms to register selected project beneficiaries and beneficiaries who received food assistance and the livelihood kits. Post distribution monitoring questionnaire will be used to interview project beneficiaries who received livelihood kits in order to gather information on satisfaction of beneficiaries in terms of timeliness of input distribution, relevance of the inputs distributed, quality and quantity distributed, overall organization and modalities of distribution including security and protection issues in collaboration with FSL cluster. The PDM will be conducted within a period of 2 weeks after delivery of assistance to follow up on beneficiary satisfaction with the quality of assistance provided, security and distance distribution sites, and time for distribution and collection particularly to women and child headed households and people with disabilities. Post-planting surveys will be conducted to get more information on field preparation, planting, germination rate, crop vigor, weeding and other early-stage agronomic practices, including challenges faced in the process. Data from the post-planting surveys are used to estimate percentage of farmers who prepare the land and planted on time, total acreage planted and percentage of farmers who weeded on time. The post-harvest surveys will be used to gather information on crop yields, challenges faced by farmers during the season and post-harvest handling and management. Findings from the surveys will be used to sensitize farmers on improved agronomic practices like good land preparation, proper weeding, integrated pest management and soil fertility maintenance practices, among others to increase production and minimize post-harvest losses. Findings from the assessments will be shared and presented in the FSL cluster meetings and other stakeholders. The planned monitoring and evaluation activities will be coordinated with FSL cluster, specific donors and other stakeholders. CARE will embed gender sensitive monitoring in all the different stages of intervention through conducting gender analysis, that aims to identify and analyze gender relations, identify gender specific needs and suggest specific measures to strengthen women rights in relation to access and control over resources during the different stages of intervention. CARE will continue to use the existing feedback and complaint mechanisms based on community consultations, to avoid marginalizing the vulnerable community members, more than one channel will be available, focusing primarily on verbal rather than written communication and allowing confidentiality and safety. Analysis and results of the feedback and complaints provided will be shared monthly and this will contribute to learning and provide information for evidence-based decision making in order to improve to programme performance.

5 - Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)
5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash and/or voucher assistance is the most flexible systems of assistance in South Sudan provided market functionality is ensured. Affected communities particularly women and people with disabilities find it more convenient and easier to assess assistance wherever they need because it often reduces the risks to travel to collect assistance from remote distribution sites that require movement with associated delays food collection time during the distribution processes. More importantly, it enables beneficiaries to purchase the most preferred food items and types that are not limited to the food baskets choices provided. Cash is more efficient in terms of value for money compared food delivery that require high levels logistics arrangement. And cash provides timely and rapidly response the people especially during the onset of the disaster. Current joint market assessments in the areas where CVA are being proposed indicates that the markets are either functional or have high likelihood of recovering within the project period. This applies for both basic needs and livelihood inputs. CARE is one of the key players and active participants in the routine joint assessment organized by REACH and WFP including other actors in South Sudan. The joint market information always indicate that project inputs are available and local procurement are possible and financial service providers are as well present in most parts of the locations proposed.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

CARE is an active member of the Joint market monitoring information led by REACH and WFP. The project analyzed this information from market assessment reports provided by both WFP and the joint market monitoring initiative (JMMI), where CARE participates in some of its project sites. This is mainly based on trend analysis of the market prices and availability of essential goods and services, based on seasonal changes.

Yes

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

The project will ensure to create awareness on timely manner in accessing services especially GBV services with clear messages on benefits of timely access to service and risk of delay in accessing services especially those lifesaving services on a timely manner. CARE integrates its programming with GBV activities included in FSL farmer field and business school (FBBS) curriculum to address, offer and manage some of the GBV services and referral system.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

The project will integrate some climate resilient practices in the food production system and female male participants of different age groups including people with and without disabilities shall be trained on natural resources management (NRM) for sustainable land and water resources management. Training on Agroforestry system and practices and promotion of and production of fuel efficient stoves shall be provided to all participants including the internally displaced persons, returnees and refugees who are the main consumers of natural resources products. More than 90 percent of the population in South Sudan primarily depend on forests for fuel and food products to sustain and enhance their livelihoods. Similarly wetlands encroachment for agricultural expansion and animal trampling are rampant and main sources of land and water resources degradation in South Sudan. Capacity building components around strategic FSL cluster objective 3 shall be centered around building local capacity on wetland restoration and management, alternative livelihoods, climate- resilient agricultural best practices, market system development, and natural resource and disaster risk reduction strategies, postharvest handling techniques and introduction of appropriate preservation technologies (e.g. hermetic storage bags), diversification & restoration of livelihoods; small grants for petty trade; village saving & loan schemes & micro finance and use of local biomass briquettes & fuel-efficient stoves.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

Targeted vulnerable populations (IPC phases 3, 4 and 5) will be identified, mobilized, assessed and verified based on a widely consulted agreed upon selection criteria for support through appropriate cash / food vouchers. People will also be supported to promote emergency food production through distribution of emergency livelihood kits including crops, livestock and fishing gears.
