Project Summary

During the conflict in East Ghouta, Harasta, among other communities was at the battle frontline and witnessed fierce confrontations lasted until March 2018. As a result, a network of underground tunnels of a total length of more than 15 Km and various depths (3 to 27 m) and dimensions and many of which had as purpose securing building-to-building movement for civilians.
Since the situation was stabilized, the city has witnessed a noticeable return of IDPs who lived mostly in neighboring communities (Al-Tal and Damascus). Moreover, an internal population movement has taken place where residents who relocated during the conflict to safer parts of the city started to return to their original residences whenever their houses looks structurally sound. As a result, a spontaneous light shelter rehabilitation has taken place since then. Being invisible, the tunnels, and the shallow ones in the first place, remain a threat to public safety because they jeopardize the stability of buildings that otherwise look sound and inviting for people to return. And with water resulting from the rainy seasons, Yazid river, and the city’s malfunctioning sewerage system, building collapses becomes more probable. In fact, the city witnessed few collapse incidents during the last 2 years. No casualties have been reported luckily, but with return taking place at higher pace, more and more buildings are expected to be occupied and the possibility of such incidents can’t be ruled out.
Objectives:
Based on the above, on the results of a detailed needs assessment conducted jointly by the municipality and groups of volunteered engineers, and on the joint action plan exercise led by the municipality with the contribution of the main public actors and service providers in the city under the UN-Habitat’s municipal support programme (MTOS), UN-Habitat is proposing an approach aiming at providing the returnees and residents of the selected community a secure access to their housings by minimizing life-threatening posed by the shallow tunnels extended beneath residential and public buildings.

4 - Needs

Harasta residents (displaced persons and returnees included) are in need to secure their houses from collapsing threats caused by damage to structural elements of the residential buildings and the spontaneous tunnel networks expanding beneath them. This need has been identified as a result of a detailed structural assessment that took place in 2018 directly after the city was retaken by the government and open to return and conducted jointly by the municipality and several teams of volunteered engineers from Harasta. As a result of this assessment Harasta residential buildings were categorized based on structural soundness into sound buildings (green, 240 buildings), partially damaged buildings (yellow, 540 buildings) requiring local interventions and destroyed buildings (red, 180 buildings). Later on, Harasta municipality carried out a detailed survey of the tunnel network classifying them based on their deepness. Intersecting these two information layers produced the buildings requiring intervention. These results were also confirmed by a joint action plan exercise led by the municipality under the UN-Habitat’s municipal support programduring which the number of beneficiaries was identified and compiled demographically and based on displacement status. As a result of these assessment, approx. 250 buildings were identified based on the following criteria: a candidate building must be green or yellow and located over a shallow tunnel (less than 10 m depth).The location targeted by this intervention has been selected based on the following criteria: • Being in line with the Shelter Sector and HRP 2020 strategic priorities and objectives, as well as severity of needs;• Complementarity with ongoing projects already through UN-HABITAT resources and donor commitments. The list below informs on targeted sub-district locations: Governorate: Rural Damascus, District: Duma, Sub-district: Harasta Severity: 4 PIN: 67,311 IDPs: 22,212 (33%) Status: Planned

5 - Implementation of activities

Implementation of the project will adopt a process sequence as follows: • Develop the Municipality’s action plan identifying the priority neighborhoods/areas of intervention through a stakeholder-inclusive consultative process (done). • Form a community committee to guide through project implementation, in coordination with the municipality and local authorities. • Detailed assessment for the activities in priority neighborhoods • Tendering process, awarding and contracting • Implementation • Completion of work and handover to the relevant stakeholders. in addition to regular reporting to the FTS, all activities and indicators will be reported to the sector through regular 4W mechanisms. UN HABITAT has developed monitoring mechanism including site visits reports, progress reports, inspection tools and financial tracking tools. All these tools and templates will be utilized during the implementation to enhance the monitoring process. The municipality will have key role in monitoring the implementation through the technical committees that shall be formed to supervise the work and issue the oﬃcial handover reports, also the implementing partners are required to provide regular reports showing the progress on the ground.Technically, the soil under the targeted buildings will be improved by injection under pressure of concrete of 200-250 kg/m3 Portland cement through boreholes drilled for that purpose. For tunnel sections to be backfilled, at least two boreholes will be drilled, each on one end of the section, while in special cases where long tunnels exist, other boreholes along the tunnel’s path will be drilled.The quantity of concrete required to treat the targeted tunnels (only the sections located directly beneath buildings) is 6,000 cubic meters.
