Project Summary

Somalia has been impacted by multiple shocks in 2020, including desert locust invasion, flash floods and COVID-19. This has negatively affected the ability of vulnerable households to meet their food security needs. The urban poor, including IDP households, have been disproportionally affected due to the socio-economic effects from COVID-19 due their heavy reliance on food purchases from the market and casual labor as the main sources of income. These households are likely to face food consumption gaps due to declines in employment and income-earning opportunities. The desert locust caused significant damage to cereal crops, fruits, vegetables and pasture in the northern and central regions. Control efforts of the government and partners in Somaliland and Puntland made progress, but an increasing number of adult locust groups was reported in central regions. In addition, in the northeast, remained swarms have matured, and some have moved towards central areas. As per FAO, the hatching and band formation is occurring now and more swarms are likely to move to other areas in central Somalia and at later stages are expected to move towards crop producing areas in the south; which can have some negative impact to the crops and pasture up to early next year.
The impact of localized flooding particularly in riverine areas destroyed farmland and crops and caused population displacement, leading to significant crop losses and the loss of income from agricultural employment.
Jointly with the Government and other humanitarian actors, WFP Somalia has supported communities and vulnerable houses impacted by the multiple shocks. The high levels of sustained humanitarian assistance in 2020 have played a critical role in stabilizing the situation. The immediate food security outlook for Somalia however looks bleak despite the efforts sustained in 2020. According to the latest Post-Gu Assessment, 2.1 million people are expected to be food insecure (IPC 3 and 4) across Somalia through December 2020. An additional 3 million people are expected to be Stressed (IPC 2), bringing the total number of people facing cute food insecurity to 5.1 million until the end of this year. Based on results of the 2020 Post Gu assessment, an estimated 849,900 children under the age of five years face acute malnutrition between September 2020 and August 2021, including 143,400 likely to be severely malnourished.
Humanitarian needs could increase further in 2021 due to the influence of a La Niña that is currently developing (75 percent chance through Feb 2021). Potential drought conditions will likely develop as a result of below average 2020 Deyr (Oct-Dec), a harsh 2021 dry Jilaal (Jan-Mar) season and a possible delay and/or poor performance of the 2021 Gu (Apr-Jun).
Continued emergency relief alongside a further scale up of support to increase resilience is required in order to avert the hardship situation. WFP is therefore seeking resources in 2021 to sustain the on-going efforts and enable households meet immediate food needs and help build resilience against shocks. WFP is planning to implement unconditional and conditional transfers to improve access to food under integrated multi sectoral assistance to meet needs of vulnerable girls and women, pregnant and lactating women, boys and men and leverage resources with other partners to meet the level of humanitarian assistance required. WFP will continue to participate in cluster, joint planning, assessments and regular meetings with other humanitarian actors in Somalia. WFP employs gender sensitive beneficiary targeting in all its projects and prioritizes female and single headed households. Community consultations are carried out with men and women to ensure that their voices are heard. Accountability to affected populations ensured through information sharing, surveys and beneficiary feedback mechanisms. Beneficiaries are targeted and registered through biometric SCOPE system that provides real time data.

Project Activities

WFP proposes to implement relief emergency and livelihood recovery projects and leverage resources with other partners. This will allow to sustain efforts and enable households meet immediate access to food, protect and restore livelihoods and support resilience against shocks for vulnerable women, men, boys and girls through unconditional food and cash-based transfers in targeted IPC 3, 4, 5 areas. WFP plans to reach 1.1 million people with relief and 280,000 people conditional interventions.1. Beneficiaries will be targeted geographically through gender analysis-informed food security and nutrition needs assessments and targeted at the household level using vulnerability criteria that reflect the particular needs of men, women, boys and girls.2. Biometric registration of beneficiaries through SCOPE, socio-demographic profiling and assistance will be monitored through the SCOPE platform.3. Provision of specifically targeted relief rations providing up to 80 per cent of energy requirements for households as required though in kind or cash unconditional transfers (maximum target will be 1,100,000 beneficiaries from January to June; and 735,000 from July to December). This is a conservative estimate given the projection of worst-case scenario is 3.1 million. WFP will closely monitor how the food security situation evolves and will adjust the target if needed.4. Rehabilitation of productive assets and skills training that will enhance resilience through food/cash transfers based on seasonality (target 280,000 beneficiaries for 6 months) 5. Monitoring progress of implementation of unconditional activities, provision /distribution of food commodities/cash/vouchers to targeted beneficiaries (target 30% of Food Distribution Points monitored monthly)6. Calls to beneficiaries for feedback on targeting, entitlement and to understand if they face any challenges to receive entitlements (cash or food). Beneficiaries receive messages on programmes and entitlements as well as information on the hotlines and Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms (CFMs).7. Monthly reporting to the food security cluster8. Development of early warning bulletins, participation in regular food security assessments, regular participation in FSC meetings.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

• WFP will use both modalities; i.e in-kind and cash based transfers (CBT) depending on the most appropriate modality based on context. WFP will prioritise CBT where markets are functional, and also consider any protection concerns such as the distance to the markets, and the beneficiary preference. The modality for CBT includes vouchers or mobile money depending on programmatic and operational appropriateness. In 2020, WFP introduced the mobile money modality which allows for contactless assistance to beneficiaries, hence reducing risk of COVID 19 transmission. Both modalities provide beneficiaries with greater choice and dignity and contribute to improve their diet diversity. In 2020, WFP also introduced a home service modality through its e-shop app whereby households can opt to get their food delivered to their homes, which also contributes to reduce the risk of transmission, and improves service options to beneficiaries.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

WFP conducts market assessments in the target areas to check the capacity of the potential service providers e.g banks, retailers and telecommunications service providers. The assessments also consider distances, and security issues, as well as possible mitigating measures that can be put on place beforehand. The programmes are implemented only after the assessments are completed and a recommendation is put forward on which will be the best modality for the specific context.

Mitigation Measures

WFP works closely with a large network of local partners throughout the country to provide assistance to vulnerable populations where they are, in a safe and dignified manner. The use of cash-based transfers and SCOPE cards allows beneficiaries to access their entitlement at locations most convenient to them. The introduction in July 2020 of mobile money transfers will expand the options available for beneficiaries to redeem their entitlements, prevent overcrowding and further reduce travel distances and associated security risks for people accessing assistance, especially women.At the beginning of each project, WFP carries out consultations with all stakeholders at the community level, discussing all aspects of the project design and implementation. This engagement serves to secure community buy-in and ownership of the project and avoid any backlash that could lead to violence against recipients of assistance. To mitigate against exclusion and marginalisation WFP employs community-based targeting principles and processes through which the most vulnerable and under-represented members of the communities including IDPs participate. Communities are consulted on the validation of vulnerability criteria and formation of all-inclusive selection committees for the identification of most vulnerable households at the village level, with specific attention paid to gender, age, minorities, disability and clan representation from across the community. Later on in the project cycle, the use of biometric identification through the use of SCOPE cards at the points of redemption ensures that only intended beneficiaries receive their assistance, and mitigates the risk of the transfers going to non-intended people.WFP has three standard ways of communicating with beneficiaries; through cooperating partners who are responsible for sensitizing beneficiaries throughout the assistance process, through field monitors who advise beneficiaries and record feedback during field visits, and through the WFP Call Centre that receives beneficiary calls through a Somalia-based toll-free hotline. Feedback collected by field monitors and received through the hotline together make up the WFP complaints and feedback mechanism (CFM). Information on WFP’s hotline numbers is available through posters at food and cash distributions points and is printed on SCOPE cards. WFP has a zero-tolerance policy for sexual exploitation and abuse which focuses on acts committed by WFP employees or others associated with its work against the people it serves. WFP’s CPs are required to abide by WFP’s regulations and to adhere to its commitments against harassment and abuse. WFP has a Somalia-specific GBV referral protocol and is continuously working with protection partners such as UNICEF and the GBV sub-cluster to update referral pathways to ensure coverage in locations with gaps in service provision. WFP has provided training on PSEA and GBV to its local partners and organises regular refreshers sessions for the CPs on the same.

Please provide further explanation

When selecting Cooperating partners WFP considers capacity to implement the described project and access. Access here is view in terms of the security situation on the ground that is the protection environment. This is to ensure that the lives of staff and the affected populations are not exposed to danger or any gross insecurity by virtue of implementing the project. WFP through its partners ensures that there is wide consultations with the different layers of Government including the local authority. This also includes leveraging on the existing local community structures, systems and networks. The Community leaders (Which includes the Imams) are consulted through a robust Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) process to ensure that the project becomes a building block for longer-term durable solutions to displacements. Protection mainstreaming principles of safety /dignity, access and not causing harm and also applied. The aims of consultations with Government is to ensure that the environment is sufficiently safe and stable enough to enable durable solutions. Cooperating partners also conduct protection risk analysis and mitigation measures to inform project implementation and durable solutions. This in some instances also include Do No Harm analysis of identifying dividers, connectors and local capacities for peace. To improve on targeting, the WFP/FSC developed community-based targeting guidelines after receiving complaints from the affected communities on targeting issues which in some instances led to conflicts. Community-based targeting method therefore proposes local community groups and households as the main drivers of the targeting process the focusses on vulnerabilities. The inclusive process led by a carefully selected representative community selection committee, ensures that the most vulnerable based on local understanding of vulnerability and on agreed upon selection criteria. This reduces fears, tensions, suspicions and promotes cohesion and stability. This process also includes working the Government and other partners such as the protection cluster, UNHCR and the CCCM cluster in protection the rights of the displaced. These rights also highlighted in the Humanitarian Principles is disseminated to staff through refresher training sessions and capacity building sessions.
