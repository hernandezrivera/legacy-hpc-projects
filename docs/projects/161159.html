Project Summary

The project aims at providing male and female children, adolescents and schoolaged youths with equitable and uninterrupted access to safe, protective and inclusive learning, improving cognitive skills of male and female children, adolescents and school aged youths through education in emergency, ECD, basic education, youth education, and ensuring girls and boys are protected through increased access to psychosocial support, life skills, and referral pathways. Justice for children (J4CO) will provide an integrated enabling environment so as to increase enrollment, retention and reduce vulnerability against hunger and malnutrition among school children through establishment of a gender-segregated block of 5 latrines each (2 for boys, 2 for girls and 1 for teachers) in 4 schools in Leer County in order to ensure a safe disposal of faeces for girls and boys, installation of hand-washing facilities and distribution of soap and chlorine in 10 schools to reduce the rate of water-borne diseases. The funds will help in establishing school stores and basic kitchen blocks for meal preparation to make them ready for school feeding programme including facilitating the training of cooks and store keepers on the enriched supplementary food preparation, handling, cooking and storage of food in 7 schools in Leer County. Kitchen sets, plates, cutlery for school feeding will be provided as well including provision OF emergency school meals in areas most affected by food insecurity in 7 schools (3,155 boys, 2,450 girls). As a crosscutting activity mental health and psychosocial support services to help youth deal with trauma and social pressures which are heightened by movement restriction, health and hygiene promotion, including Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) to 500 adolescent girls, andyouth training onleadership , peacebuilding and peaceful co-existence for 130 youths.

1 - Needs

More than 6.35 million people (54%) of the South Sudanese population were severely food insecure in August 2019, despite large scale humanitarian assistance, according to the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) analysis released jointly by three United Nations agencies and the Government on 11 September, it shows that between January-April 2020, 10.4% of the population (about 1.21 million people) will be in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency); 36.7% of the population (about 4.29 million people) will be in IPC Phase 3 (Crisis); 34.9% of the population (about 4.08 million) in IPC Phase 2 (Stressed); and 18.0% of the population (about 2.10 million) are in IPC Phase 1 (Minimal). Leer County will be under IPC level 3 between Januarys to April in 2020. Suggesting that slightly over 50% of the population will be severely food insecure and harvest from 2019 depleted. A total of 9,739 HHs returnees have been registered by ROSS in Southern area of Leer (Touchriak, Yang,Thonyor, Panhiam, Dindin and Pilieny). The returnees are mainly from Sudan, Uganda and Kenya in Eastern area of Leer. Food security is linked to school attendance as showed by Education Cluster Assessment South Sudan, October 2018. According to the report, lack of food at school is the number one reason for drop out. During this time of food shortage, children are more likely to be pulled out of school to help the family find food and will simply be kept home if they have no energy to attend. For girls in particular, earlier drop out and marriage has been an ongoing effect of the economic and food crisis, as families look at them as source of dowry which would be used for household survival. School children will also be able to access protective factors of school (psychosocial support, lifesaving messages and access to other services). The combined effects of displacement, low-income levels, weak productivity, inflation, loss of productive assets and climatic shocks are seriously jeopardizing the lives, livelihoods and dignity of vulnerable IDPs and host community households in Leer County. The county continues to face a humanitarian crisis, resulting from the inter-communal conflict and food access remains a big challenge, which has been further exacerbated by the harsh economic conditions as a result of conflict and high inflation currently being experienced in the country. In addition, the economic crisis, poor functioning markets and climatic shocks which includes prolonged dry spells and flooding have further worsened the household livelihood capacities, are impeding heavily on school attendance as most household are adopting negative coping strategies. Justice for Children Organization (J4CO) in a baseline assessment conducted prior to submission of this proposal, results generally showed a poor education system in all the participating schools in the County, with a marked evidence of several and different problems that are negatively affecting enrollment and reinforcing dropout rates. The majority of the schools assessed have unsuitable learning spaces and environments, lack kitchens and cooking utensils, and have low hygiene and sanitation levels. Thus, the most relevant needs identified concern these three main issues. First of all, the data concerning the targeted schools show an alarming lack of the most basic scholastic materials, such as blackboards, textbooks and other stationery, which are pivotal necessities for ensuring a proficient education for pupils, but also a decent teaching environment for teachers. The collected data show also a compelling need for training and activities for pupils, PTAs, teachers and parents on both GBV and inclusion topics. Out of 20 assessed schools in Leer County, 10 of them are highly recommended for onsite feeding activities, 8 schools are recommended for establishment of new semi-permanent sex-segregated latrines, 6 schools are recommended for medium-scale renovation including its sex-segregated latrines .
