Project Summary

This project proposed interventions are informed and designed in line with the RCF activities and indicators and the Sudan Country Refugee Response Plan 2021 appeal to address the needs in FSL sector in AlSalam and Aljabalain localities. The proposed interventions will target (780) households, approximately (2,220) individuals refugee. FSL sector intervention will contribute to enhancing resilience and self-reliance of refugees by improving access to food & livelihood activities targeting households and individuals through conditional seasonal programs linked to livelihoods and asset creation through improving self-employment and entrepreneurship skills. applicable vocational, technical, and skills training. Targeted household-headed females will be supported to diversify & increase domestic, food security, nutrition, income, and access to markets. (60) household-headed women & girls will be provided with livestock support through the provision of hybrid small ruminant females known for high productivity in order to significantly increase meat and milk production of the locally-bred goats and sheep. and will, later on, be supported by a small business grant and micro-credit management. (360) men and boys/youth will be targeted for applicable vocational training depends on the needs and in consultation with The National Council for Vocational Education and Training. Vocational and technical education in food-processing, general electricity, cell phone maintenance, construction, and soap making. (360) Women, men, boys, and girls will be provided and supported with a business grant in order to enable them to start small businesses. targeted women, men, boys, and girls will be subject to community-based micro-credit project management and group enterprise training to build their capacities in cash management, marketing, basic accounting, and will be linked to the existing VSLAs. Persons with disabilities and people with special needs will be included in each intervention.
For the Ethiopian refugee response, the pre-approved Ethiopia appeal will focus on (3) multi-sector interventions including; Health & Nutrition, WASH, and Shelter/NFI. These proposed activities will target approximately (31,000) refugees in Kassala/Gedaref state. the following activities will be conducted:
1. Health & Nutrition:
1.1. Conduct (60) COVID-19 awareness-raising sessions, risk communication, and sensitization.
2. WASH:
2.1. Distribution of (2000) personal hygiene kits.
2.2. Rehabilitation of (40) communal latrines.
2.3. Provision of (300) handwashing facility.
2.4. Provision of chlorination & disinfection services (6 sets).
3. ES/NFIs:
3.1. Distribution of (1000) dignity kits for women and girls.
3.2. Distribution of (500) standard ES/NFIs kits.

1 - Needs

The impacts of the economic situation on refugee communities is compounded by limited access to livelihoods for South Sudanese refugees across the response. Against this backdrop, the increase in the cost of living due to high inflation has affected the purchasing power of refugees and undermined their capacity to earn enough income to become self-reliant, heightening assistance needs and dependency across the response. This inability to cover basic needs is also accompanied by several knock-on effects, including: increased tensions with host communities, school drop-out and low enrollment, prevalence of child labor, early marriage and other child protection issues, and sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) risk.South Sudanese refugees interviewed during participatory assessments also cited poverty as a major challenge they face in White Nile state. This is driven by a lack of productive assets that can be utilized to support their livelihoods, insufficient technical and entrepreneur skills, lack of access to work permits and documentation, limited employment opportunities, and gaps in financial services to support entrepreneurship. For refugees living in rural areas, both in camps and out-of-camp settlements, key constraints include prohibitions on land ownership and/or limited access cultivation via crop-sharing, and limited market accessibility. Although In White Nile state, refugee resilience relies heavily on host community resilience, and many host communities lack access to the services and infrastructure that they need to overcome climatic and economic shocks and vulnerabilities. Refer to the HNO 2021 and Ethiopia Situation Updates by UNHCR.
