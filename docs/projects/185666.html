Project Summary

HI aims to introduce a project related with nutrition & disability. The aim of the project is “The resilience capacities of children recovering from SAM/MAM including children with disabilities (under 5) and their caregivers/family members are increased through early screening, early intervention, referral and mainstreaming activities”. Through this project HI is expected to work to ensure two specific results; i) Early recognition and prevention of childhood disability among children under age 5 including children recovering from SAM/MAM and ii) Improve access to health and nutrition service for children with disabilities. To achieve the expected results HI aims to implement following activities:
1. identification & screening of SAM/MAM children & children with disabilities,
2. Assessment and intervention plan for children with disabilities
3. capacity building of service providers of different types; nutrition, health, education and child protection actors,
4. caregiver/mothers’ knowledge enhancement,
5. provision of childhood development and stimulation therapy,
6. provision of different assistive devices,
7. appropriate referral etc.
HI intends to adopt twin-track approach through providing specific services to children with disabilities and orienting, training mainstream partners to ensure mainstreaming of disability inclusive interventions in health & nutrition sectors.
HI has wide experience of working on nutrition in many other contexts and developed a unique ‘early childhood stimulation therapy’ intervention which benefits SAM/MAM children in early identification and prevention of disability. HI also applies community-based approach by involving caregivers, community leaders in design and implementation of its activities to ensure accountability and sustainability. HI has CFRM SOP and mechanism at camp level to consult the beneficiaries and receive their feedback, complaints and address these in confidential manner. We collect feedback through multipole formats and create awareness on CFRM among the beneficiaries through various modes. HI will implement activities in 10 camps due to HI’s strong presence in those camps on health/SRH sector. In selected camps HI has volunteers & registered nurses to conduct health/SRH related activities as well as provision of rehabilitation services by Physiotherapist. This project is a combination of nutrition & rehabilitation. To ensure the holistic approach HI will implement the activities in selected 10 camps. In addition, HI is member of PSEA network in Cox’s Bazar & has own PSEA & Harassment policy in line with IASC. All staffs, volunteers, vendors, members of HI must follow the PSEAH policy to protect beneficiaries. HI maintains zero tolerance about the violation of PSEA policy. HI has PSEAH focal in site offices including in Cox’s Bazar, Ukhiya & Teknaf.

1 - Identified Needs

In 2020, the mean prevalence of malnutrition among children under five in the camps was estimated at 3.2 percent for Severely Acutely Malnourished children (SAM) and 17.4 percent for Moderately Acutely Malnourished children (MAM), highlighting the need for ongoing nutrition-focused activities. The Sector assesses that almost 65,500 SAM and MAM refugee children along with Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) in the camps are in need of urgent nutrition services. In April 2020, 37 percent of Rohingya families were found to be living in a situation of poor or borderline food security, contributing to sustained high rates of malnutrition. In July 2020, 30 percent of Rohingya households reported experiencing barriers on accessing integrated nutrition facilities during and after the COVID-19 lockdown. The Sector estimates that a total of 160,544 children under five will be in need of a range of nutrition care services in the camps. Close to 12 percent of children under five in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazilas are considered malnourished, with a total of 11,775 SAM and MAM children and MAM PLW in need of nutritional care. The Sector estimates that some 25,500 parents and caregivers of children under the age of five still require support to enhance their Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) skills to help prevent malnutrition in their children. Malnutrition among infants, young children and children with disabilities is directly linked with each other. Many research revealed that early development delay in infant and young children is direct consequence of deficiency of some essential micronutrient during the pregnancy and early childhood developmental stage. Most low-tone infants caused by bi-birth defect or deficiency of micronutrient have delayed developmental growth, but the length of delay can vary widely. Children with SAM have motor and cognitive developmental delays or deficit, which can consolidate over time and lead to irreversible disabilities if no management is in place. In addition to this, the poor health service, poor hygiene and sanitary practice among children are some other contributing factors of causing irreversible disabilities or development delay among new born and children under age 5.Despite having presence of many predisposing factors (led by micronutrient deficiency/malnutrition) which can cause birth defect in new born, delay in the neuro-development, cognitive, mental, sensory delay among children in early childhood; limited emphasis has been given to detect such problems within child health and nutrition programme in overall programming of Rohingya response. Hence, many SAM/MAM children are left undiagnosed and might suffer from lifelong disabilities and social isolation. Hence assessing the overall development of children and early screening of the potential risk of developing disability among the children recovering from SAM/MAM is very important to minimize the impact of impairment and prevent children from developing lifelong disabilities. Furthermore, due to existing impairment, poor and delayed neuro-muscular development among new-born and infants (under 5) who are born with impairment/birth defect, further cause of nutrition deficiency among them may develop. These children often have difficulty taking foods or have poor oral motor skills

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1: Early screening of children under five with SAM/MAM & disability. The information of SAM/MAM children will be taken from other nutrition related actors (if any) or HI nurses will screen children by measuring MUAC. Provision of Stimulative Physical Therapy for children recovering from SAM/MAM. as well as provision of early intervention services with focus to improve functional, cognitive and mental development of child through appropriate referral.Activity 2: Provision of adaptive devices such as early stimulation kits, positioning kits and mobility aids for children with developmental delay/disabilitiesActivity 3: Capacity building of caregivers/family members on basic home care advice, positioning and handling, activities of daily livings, feeding skills, oral motor skills training, etc.Activity 4: Capacity building of staffs of stabilization centers/OTP centers as well as health post staffs on early recognition of disability and its prevention among children under 5 recovering from SAM/MAM as well as provision of basic home-based therapeutic care.Activity 5: Promoting inclusion of children with disabilities into nutrition, health, food security services provision & ensure internal and external referral. Activity 6: Capacity building on disability mainstreaming in humanitarian and developmental action to humanitarian actorsActivity 7: Provision of reasonable accommodation in nutrition and food distribution site to improve access for person /children with mobility problem.
