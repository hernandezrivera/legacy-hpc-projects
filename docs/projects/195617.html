Project Summary

Over 216,000 most vulnerable population of Akobo and Pibor counties of Jonglei state are food insecure according to the emergency and famine status analysis and classification in 2022/2023 South Sudan IPC data. Recurrent communal conflicts, climate change effects like floods, insufficient rainfall in major parts of the year, macro-economic challenges and apathy amongst the population have greatly contributed to this worrying food insecurity and nutrition safety for the Jonglei population. The few farmers who have been cultivating have experienced crops and animal losses since 2020 to date. The overall food insecurity is projected to worsen throughout 2022/2023 in the two counties. The population will continue in lack and experience vulnerabilities due to limited physical, social and economic access to sufficient, safe and nutritious food that meets their dietary needs and food preferences for an active and healthy life. Recent protection analysis by UNMISS and actors in Jonglei revealed that lactating women, under 5 years children, persons with disabilities and elderly have borne the brunt of human suffering, violence, abuse and marginalization compared to other sects within the community. These vulnerable persons are often subjected to extreme levels of gender-based violence and psychological distresses in search for food locally such as wild fruits and leaves as coping mechanisms of survival. The youths; the most productive human resources have been used to fuel the conflicts over time; but could in peace building efforts be useful to address the multi-faceted challenges of food and nutrition insecurity.
The latest interagency needs assessment undertaken by partners and led by OCHA in the greater Jonglei, supported by IPC 2022/2023; data revealed that about 216,000 persons will be food insecure from December 2022 through to July 2023. Rural Action Against Hunger has further analysed that over 818 most vulnerable household (54,000 individuals) in Akobo (Alali and Buong payams) and Pibor (Gumuruk and Lekuangole) will be worst affected compared to other payams in the counties following the unique challenges they face. The key drivers found out in these payams included: revenge killings, cattle raid, humanitarian access impediments and more significant absence of active local partner to voice needs of the community and capacity build in the nexus programming aspects. It’s therefore against this background, that Rural Action Against Hunger (RAAH) is desirous to identify and work with this community in emergency food production and food/nutrition security resilience activities as per FSL cluster objectives CO2 and CO3 in this expression of interest. RAAH has a wider stakeholder engagement, partnerships and donor relations avenues to mobilize resources and thus contribute to the fulfillment of the need to make food and nutrition secure for the targeted population.

RAAH, a strong national partner dedicated in community resilience aspects with proven experience in Kapoeta region Eastern Equatoria and Akobo; Pibor; in Jonglei State; will employ climate smart agriculture and diversified livelihood practices not limited to: flood water usage for fish farming and vegetable crop production, agro-forestry fruit trees planting, valley dams development and maintenance for yearly safe water provision for animals, crops and humans and agronomic capacity building of the targeted population is desirous to turn the situation around in the two counties for resilient empowered, food and nutrition secure population in Jonglei state, South Sudan. Our approach would incorporate the most productive assets of the community the youth and women in food production (e.g. in farming; vegetable/crops production, vaccination and treatment of livestock and fish farming). Resilience aspects such as: financial literacy, negotiation and communication skills, conflict and peace nexus for communities and self-reliance based on handwork and loving work will be mainstreamed in all emergency FSL activities in this project. 2 - Project activities

Under the FSL strategic objectives of C02 and C03; RAAH proposes to undertake a. Emergency food production through:i) Main season agriculture seeds & tools, fishing kits, vegetable & crop kits distribution targeting 100% of the 54,000 each in Akobo and Pibor counties. ii) Training farmers on quick impact climate resilient agronomic practices, and distribute farm inputs such as maize, groundnuts, sesame, okra, tomatoes, eggplant, amaranths, hoes, rakes, and watering cans iii) Establishing vegetable gardens and practically train women on vegetable gardening and preparation of nutritious meals iv) Emergency livestock support through: Undertaking livestock treatment and vaccination; livestock disease surveillance, community animal health workers (CAHWs) training and inputs support, cold chain establishment and maintenance, prevention & treatment especially zoonosis' e.g., RVF. v) Train CAHWs in basic animal health, extension and business skills; equip and deploy them to deliver animal health services. The training will be conducted in line with the guidelines provided by the Ministry of Livestock and Fisheries while observing Ebola and Covid-19 prevention protocols of Ministry of Health and Centre for Diseases Control. Conduct disease alert investigations for all animal disease alerts that occur during the project implementation period- samples will be collected from animals during investigation of all disease alerts. vi) Vaccinate 120,000 animals against priority animal diseases. The vaccination coverage will include Hemorrhagic Septicemia (HS), Contagious Bovine Pleuropneumonia (CBPP), Black Quarter (BQ) and Anthrax diseases in cattle, Pest des Petits Ruminants (PPR), Contagious Caprine Pleuropneumonia (CCPP) and Sheep and Goat Pox (SGP) in sheep and goats, Rabies in dogs and donkeys and Newcastle Disease (NCD) in poultry. Each animal (cattle) will be vaccinated against at least three diseases during the project period, depending on the ranking of the diseases in each region, while goats will be vaccinated at least against the three diseases — PPR, CCPP and Sheep and Goat Pox, and at least two vaccines in sheep (PPR and Sheep and Goat Pox); Treat 50,000 animals against priority animal diseases and parasites. This will be clearly explained during the mobilization and sensitization phase of the intervention for livestock keepers and will be encouraged to report sick animals to the CAHWs as soon as clinical signs are detected so that treatment is commenced early. RAAH will endeavor to ensure that CAHWs carry out treatment of the number of animals against internal & external parasites, infectious diseases and blood parasites and record the data; Livelihood support to the vulnerable targeted householdsUndertake community livelihood enterprise mapping with priority actors such as most vulnerable women, girls, boys, youths, PWDs and elderly; and prioritize three most productive livelihood activities with a marketing and income generating potential based on Akobo and Pibor contexts vii. Train the selected farmers (theory and practical) on basic entrepreneurial skills, business management skills, assessments and prioritization of most valuable livelihoods in Akobo and Pibor counties; Support in seed financing (in kind) of prioritized livelihood activities after training and successful induction. Integration of Nutrition and WASH into FSL intervention; In close coordination with Nutrition and WASH partners in the Akobo and Pibor support the multi-sectoral linkage of FSL, nutrition and WASH assistance in line with IPC 4 & 5. viii) Peace building (conflict sensitivity) and Resilience programming; Increase peace building campaigns while undertaking integrated FSL activities in the communities for peaceful co-existence and Disaster risk reduction activities (dykes, field contours, fruit trees planting initiatives, renewable energy local stoves making by farmers and climate changes awareness messaging to the community

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Aware of the illiteracy and other impediments especially to persons with disabilities, women, and girls; RAAH will use multi-layered communication approaches not limited to: sign language, language interpretation, pictorial presentation, site scenes as core elements to ensure the most vulnerable are not left out and their contributions are inputted for project success

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

At the project design phase; all segments of the community namely (men, women, boys, girls, persons with disabilities and the elderly) will be consulted on the priority needs in the context of emergency and early recovery- the needs will be participatorily ranked by them and most significant themes of planned actions to address the needs while preserving good copying mechanisms and do not harm approaches will inform the project approach. At implementation select committee representing various group leaders will be involved in monitoring the outcomes of the project where various platforms such as face to face meetings, periodic project update reports, field visits and RAAH level project management contact sharing with the beneficiary communities to enable confidential and natural flow of information amongst the project stakeholders.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

RAAH staff consent to zero tolerance on SEA, the front project staff will endeavor to guide the participating communities on SEA and how it undermines aid delivery and her sole responsibility to safety and safeguarding the stakeholders. PSEA awareness sessions through meetings, Fliers, pictorial information will be used to provide information

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

As the project consultations and engagements happen at community level spearheaded by RAAH through meetings, project site visits, monitoring of the output and activity progress indicators, each feedback obtained will be used for adjusting and improving on the project deliverables/activities in line with gender needs, disability and safety needs of the participating communities

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

Climate smart awareness creation in areas of fruit trees plantation, responsible use of wood fuel and charcoal through locally made energy saving stoves, reforestation efforts are embed by RAAH in this climate change awareness inclusive relocation awareness from extreme flood prone areas to less flood prone locations. Local dykes and use of contours on the farm lands will be advocated for to minimize the destructive impact of floods on livestock and humans within the project

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

All emergency FSL activities designed have embedded 'do no harm approach' involving beneficiary selection (consult all gender groups within the community); form community level project monitoring team with all segments of gender and persons with disability representation; continous project information flow through feed back mechanisms namely meetings, phone calls, beneficiaries interactive visits and trouble shooting through protection tip sheet and case management will all contribute to the collective outcome of safeguarding stakeholders in the project delivery. 12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

In turn, livelihood diversification (vegetable crops, fish, and main season farming activities all combine to reduce high level external food dependency by the most vulnerable households
