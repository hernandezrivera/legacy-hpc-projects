Project Summary

The main objective of this proposed project is improving WASH services in 20 schools located in area C in Hebron, Bethlehem, Ramallah, Salfeet, Nablus, Tubas and Jenin governorates. This project is designed to be implemented by World Vision and in partnership with Juzoor organization. It’s composed of two main components; First, improving the critical WASH services at the 20 schools through decreasing the gap between available facilities based on MoE standards, while accommodating the needs of the Children with Disability (CWD). This will be achieved by rehabilitating and increasing the number of available sanitary units to cover the number and be sufficient to both genders and various age groups and CWD (especially for schools accommodating primary and secondary age students). Likewise, the number of drinking fountains will also be increased for the same reason. Meeting the needs of the Children with Disability (CWD) is an integral part of the project, taking into consideration their gender needs, where activities will be intentionally designed to accommodate their needs to increase their access to services and information and participation including infrastructural work (sanitary units and water fountains), awareness on WASH practices (hygiene practices) and the rest of the activities.
Secondly, in parallel with the infrastructural works, capacity building and awareness sessions for all target school students (the 3,302 girls and 2,171 boys students) as well as teachers in charge of school health committees will take place in a creative and tailored manner so that to uplift the current hygienic practices so that to ensure that children and schools are protected through improved hygienic situation in their daily lives. Protection measures will be integrated to the capacity building programs through considering the basic needs of girls, young boys and people with disability to have safe access to the WASH services in their respective schools The overall project will be monitored at different levels, at national level, the Ministry of Education-School Health department will be updated on quarterly basis about the project implementation and its impact. At Governorates levels, Directorates of Education will be kept at constant communications by the respective implementation staff. Locally, the village committee in each locality (as explored below) and Child Health Committee at schools will monitor and lead the implementation of activities so that to guarantee, relevance, compatibility and impact realized

1 - Needs

The needs assessment conducted by World Vision in September 2019 to the most vulnerable schools in Area C revealed an alarming gap in WASH services. Among the 55 assessed schools, 89%and 43% were found without water and sanitary units appropriated for students with disability, respectively. Among the 25 schools accommodate boy students with disability, eight of them have sanitary units that is not appropriated for those students with disability. The situation is almost similar for girls students were six schools were found misappropriated for those girls with disability Around 40% were found far beneath the Ministry of Education Standards when it comes to number of WASH facilities available compared with the number of students. Ten out of the target 20 schools suffer limitation in the number of sanitary units compared with number of students being served, these ten schools have more than 40 students per unit and four out of them are having 72 to 169 students per sanitary unit. Likewise, the water faucet were found at critical shortage level in 10 out of the targeted 20 schools, the number of students per drinking faucet ranged between 42 and 89 students. The highest range was found in the mixed schools and this was found limiting girls access to drinking because of cultural sensitivities related to share the faucets with boy students or drinking together with boys In addition, and in terms of cleanliness, the sanitary units in most of the schools were found unclean. However, this is realized only by 27% of the school principals who reported dissatisfaction from bathrooms cleanliness. Principals attributed this poor hygienic situation to poor practices and behaviour by the children. Such poor hygienic practices are reported to be same at home. 64% of the assessed schools are found accommodating both boy and girl students despite the cultural constraints due to resource limitation. 50% are using exactly the same units. This was found having severe access limitation to sanitary units for the girls and younger boys due to feeling insecure to share sanitary units with adolescent/ older boy students. 71% of the assessed schools were found have sanitary units serving all its students at primary and secondary stages (regardless age) despite the bullying problems reported. In addition to the infrastructural limitations, target communities especially the remote Bedouin communities were found suffering from poor inherited hygiene practices. Examples on this include self-hygiene and safe usage of sanitation. These poor practices are found as a hazard at school and house level that contributes negatively to some health issues. For example, the staff field visits to target schools revealed that the cleanliness of most of the sanitary units is at risky level. Most of the sanitary units were lacking cleaning materials. These conditions are expected to keep user’s health at risk. Moreover, these remote communities suffer from services limitations because of their sensitive location in area C away from key cities and localities. Moreover, most of target communities have low population size due to the tough living conditions historically. Such communities are still practicing old methods of upraising and deeply built socially based on patriarchal and male-centred culture (Source: Village Committees needs assessment, 2019. Therefore violence against children including gender-based violence were reported to be skyrocketing according to recent assessment conducted by World Vision to assess the main vulnerabilities faced by these communities. For example, in Nablus governorate, gender based violence including early marriage was identified as one of the top five vulnerabilities (ADAPT Assessment, 2018)

2 - Activities

Through this project, 20 schools in the targeted areas, accommodating more than 5,473 boys and girls students and 54 Male and Female children with disability, will experience improved compliance between the available WASH facilities and the Ministry of Education Standards. The selected schools were assessed thoroughly based on specific criteria related to WASH, occurrence of protection and GBV issues, and accommodation of the needs of CWD, and activities will be set to tackle these issues to ensure impact is achieved and improve safe and quality access to WASH services in these schools. For example, in the schools were sanitary units are shared between boys and girls as well as CWD (which leads to limitation of girls and CWD access) will have the intervention tailored to grant access to girls through increasing number of sanitary units and/or developing a culturally and gender-sensitive way to guarantee safe access for girls. The same apply for schools where issues of bullying are reported where older (secondary) and younger (primary) children and CWD share the same WASH facilities. Better access to healthy and safe sanitary units is a cross cutting issues across the four geographical locations, where the exact intervention in each schools will be carefully tailored with the village committees/ Student Health Committees and other stakeholders such as school administration. Students (boys, girls and CWD) will be taught about adequate hygienic practices in an age and gender-sensitive manner. Hygiene campaigns, hygiene promotion classes as well as demonstrations and visuals will also be utilized to optimize students’ grasping of information. The long experience of Juzoor and World Vision in School WASH will be utilized to implement a comprehensive and sustainable awareness raising campaign. Through close coordination with the Directorates of Educations, the School Health Supervisors will develop Training of Trainers (ToT) curriculum that will be used to train respective teachers in charge of school health in the 20 target schools. These supervisors will conduct the interactive ToT which will be concluded by health action plan for each school. Those teachers will be mobilizing and formulating school Health Committees represented by students (boys and girls and CWD where applicable). These committees will be trained by Teachers and will conduct needs assessment to identify key WASH priorities and solutions will be proposed accommodating the WASH needs of each and single targeted school. One of the first outcomes will be designing of awareness raising campaign that fits with the local capacities and appropriate to age and gender in each of the targeted school. In addition, the committee will also identify one or more demonstrations, which will provide visual accessible resource to remind boys, girls and CWD about main messages of each campaign. Girls will be consulted in a safe and culturally acceptable manner to design and implement awareness raising interventions specific for girls’ hygiene including girls with disability. Similar intervention will be conducted regarding the specific hygiene practices for the children with disability (CWD). In parallel with the above, health committees, supervisors and teachers alongside with World Vision and Juzoor in these locations will be trained on school WASH awareness campaigns. They will be responsible to conduct an average of ten classes to cover the specific awareness needs of each age group and each gender at schools. In order to make these classes more meaningful and appreciated, hygiene kits will be distributed at the end of each class so that to enable students apply what they learned during these classes.

3 - Indicators

The project is designed to measure 4 main indicators related to WASH sector targeting in total 5553 boys, girls, CWD and male and female teachers. Indicators set under this project includes: 1-# of people have benefited from improved access to safe drinking and domestic water services (target is 2,234 girls and 1,245 boys students including 54 CWD) 2- # of people have benefited from improved access to sanitation and hygiene services (target is 2,234 girls and 1,245 boys students including 54 CWD), 3- # of key institutions with improved access to WASH services ( 20 schools), 4- # of people in humanitarian situation who benefited from hygiene promotion activities (Target 5553 boys, girls, CWD and male and female teachers).Targets under each indicator will be segregated to the specific target group including, boys, girls, and children with disability, and that is to measure the change achieved throughout the life time of the project. A solid Detailed Monitoring and evaluation ( M&E) plan will be developed based on the set indicators and targets, and details all monitoring tools and time-frame. Pre- Post information will be collected to measure the level of impact and information will be inserted with the M&E plan. Monitoring tools will be developed taking into consideration age and gender and CWD aspects and will be child friendly, where tools will include pre-post tests to measure the level of knowledge, observation reports, focus groups, surveys, PRA, interviews with key stakeholders including children and children with disability, alongside with the monitoring reports that would be submitted from each of the targeted schools stating progress, impact and successes. The project M&E approach will follow key principals as follows: 1- Joint monitoring practices through collaboration with the MoE. WV has strong relationships with the MoE, thus creating a foundation for shared responsibilities and accountability in M&E tasks. 2- World Vision field team will be oriented on using the monitoring tools .3- Progress reports will be regularly collected on bi- weekly basis and shared with key stakeholders 4- End of project report and evaluation. The project team will ensure Community participation and following up on activities as part of the M&E.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

World Vision uses the "Do No Harm" Principle (also known as "Local Capacities for Peace" or LCP analysis tool) to integrate conflict-sensitivity wherever it is relevant. Do No Harm (DNH) is now used widely by many aid organizations, including governments and multilateral organizations. World Vision is an active organizations globally using the "Do No Harm" principle. Therefore, it is mainstreamed throughout the project design, and will continue to be used throughout the implementation with all relevant parties

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Ensuring equitable access and gender sensitivity was taken into consideration regarding the selected schools, as two schools are girls schools and two boys while the rest are co-ed. Moreover , impartiality was considered on prioritizing the schools with high need due to location and # of violations and activities are designed gender specific and attending also to CWDs. Each group will be exposed to set of activities that are fit to their specific needs. this is clearly shown in the gender specific activities that are part of the project, including but not limited to, the split of sanitary units for males and females specifically, and hygiene awareness information for males and females.
