Project Summary

Clashes between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) that erupted on 15 April 2023 are having devastating consequences for people across Sudan. Life-saving responses related to WASH, Food Security, nutrition and agriculture are urgently needed for conflict affected populations. Mercy Corps response plans aims to provide humanitarian assistance to new IDPs and CAPs people during the first 6 month of the crisis.
Goal:
“To provide humanitarian assistance to conflict affected people in Sudan to meet immediate needs for survival and work towards recovery”:
Objectives:
1. Save and protect lives
2. Improve capacity to withstand further shocks (e.g. flooding)
3. Prevent backsliding for vulnerable communities indirectly affected by the conflict

1 - Needs

Clashes between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) that erupted on 15 April 2023 are having devastating consequences for people across Sudan. Since the beginning of the conflict, IOM’s Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) estimates that approximately 843,130 individuals (168,354 households “HHs”) have been displaced. Notably, the current estimate for displacement during the last two weeks is larger than that recorded by DTM Sudan for all conflict-related displacement during 2022. Furthermore, an estimated 253,500 individuals have crossed into neighboring countries. The revised Sudan Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), launched May 17, 2023, indicates a 57 percent increase in the number of people in need (PiN) in comparison to the Dec 2022 launched Sudan HRP, equal to 24.7 million people in need of humanitarian assistance. Shortages of food, water, medicine, and fuel continue, especially in Khartoum and surrounding areas, while access to communications and electricity is limited in many parts of the country. The Red Sea State is projected by WFP to reach 56 percent prevalence of food insecurity in the coming months, amongst the highest in the country, and along with Khartoum and South Darfur is rated as the highest priority for WASH interventions according to the HRP. North and South Kordofan serve as important transit points for displaced Sudanese leaving Khartoum and/or seeking to enter into South Sudan, and Gedaref is an important transit point for IDPs heading to Ethiopia. The on-going crisis in Sudan has forced a stop or pause in previous humanitarian operations across the country, resulting in critical needs prior to the current crisis being unmet and further adding to the severity of needs for highly vulnerable and marginalized populations. Displacement of civilians continues to be reported in Khartoum, Northern, Blue Nile, North Kordofan, North Darfur, South Darfur and West Darfur states, as well as cross-border movements to surrounding countries, including Egypt, Chad, South Sudan, Central African Republic (CAR) and Ethiopia. Large numbers of residents, especially from Khartoum, have relocated to safer locations in the east of Sudan. In Khartoum, heavy clashes between RSF and SAF actors have remained ongoing across the city. Since the beginning of the clashes, IOM estimates that approximately 64,595 IDPs have left Khartoum State. Notable displacement has been occurring in response to severe water and electricity shortages in many locations. Shelling of residential areas in many neighborhoods across Khartoum is widespread. Large numbers of displaced households have arrived in Port Sudan from Khartoum State. Additionally, families from Khartoum are fleeing to the relatively safe town of Gedaref. IOM DTM field teams report that state authorities have set up three IDP centers to shelter those displaced from Khartoum. A recent NRC Rapid Assessment has identified priority needs for IDPs as access to 1) Food, 2) Shelter, 3) Health care, 4) Water. Mercy Corps conducted a Rapid Market Assessment in early May 2023 which found that many of the vendors / shops in the target areas continued to stock basic food items, but that the displaced communities were unable to access essential commodities due to a loss of income as a result of displacement and extremely high / rising food prices. Additionally, many of the IDPs had fled with few belongings and were in need of emergency shelter and NFIs.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Mercy Corps will distribute seeds through a restricted value voucher system to conflict households. Project participants will then be able to redeem their value voucher for their choice of seeds at selected certified seed suppliers. Mercy Corps will provide basic and refresher trainings on the CMAM guidelines and the Essential Nutrition Package to the existing MoH staff and community volunteers. The training sessions will focus on the four main components of CMAM, including community outreach, OTP, targeted supplementary feeding program (TSFP), and inpatient care by the MoH. To ensure a continuous high standard of care, Mercy Corps will provide biannual refresher training sessions for the nutrition center staff. Mercy Corps will also conduct SAM treatment for children 6-59m (without complication) in OTPs (first 6 months) as well as SAM treatment for children 6-59m (without complication) in OTPs (Second 6 months).Through the nutrition interventions about about 76% of the nutrition sector’s lifesaving response activities will contribute to nutrition resilience. Mercy Corps through implementing partners will provide GBV, Child protection training and as cross cutting activities across all other thematic areas. As part of the GBV strategy, Mercy Corps will promote prevention of gender-based violence and will provide support to survivors and their families as well as promoting responsive legal and justice systems. Mercy Corps will ensure that children protection and GBV is an integral part of the programs in order to ensure that women and girls are protected and given the opportunity to participate in the project implementation. In the target locations, water collection is typically the responsibility of women and girls. Limited access to water sources forces women and girls to walk long distances in search of water, often exposing them to additional protection risks. This intervention will help to reduce these risks through rehabilitating infrastructure which will increase the availability of water and reduce the distance women and girls are forced to walk to collect water. In addition, Mercy Corps will ensure that women and girls are represented in the community-level WASH committees – minimum 50 percent of WASH committee members are women. Mercy Corps also plans to encourage the involvement of women and girls in all elements of the community-based operation and maintenance of the water sources. In terms of child protection Mercy Corps will conduct awareness campaigns on children’s rights and protection concerns, plus establishing community-based child protection committees trained on child protection standards and referral mechanisms. Mercy Corps will address WASH needs through rehabilitation of water sources for both community and institutions, and formation and training of WASH committees to ensure improved access to clean and safe water is sustained.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

To ensure that gender and protection are mainstreamed in the program, Mercy Corps will abide by Do No Harm principles. MC will also strive for gender balance in the recruitment of staff at all levels and will train officers, responsible for delivering services and inputs to participants, on gender issues and gender-sensitive approaches. These efforts will be supported by a Gender and Safeguarding Coordinator who will be partially funded through this project. Mercy Corps’ in-house gender expertise will provide a solid foundation for the program.
