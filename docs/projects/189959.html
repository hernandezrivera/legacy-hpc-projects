Project Summary

This project aims to provide essential life-saving and life-sustaining Gender-based Violence (GBV) prevention, risk mitigation, and response services to displaced and vulnerable populations (women, girls, men and boys) in Cabo Delgado, Nampula and Niassa provinces

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

UNFPA commits to continue providing leadership to emergency GBV responses in the next humanitarian project cycle, in keeping with its mandate under the IASC. UNFPA therefore, working together with its implementing partners, and in coordination with the GBV AoR, will continue to provide and further expand quality life-saving GBV prevention, risk mitigation and response services in Northern Mozambique in coordination with the respective provincial governments and government institutions in the three provinces. The proposed interventions aim to increase availability and accessibility of quality life-saving GBV prevention/response in conflict-affected and IDP-hosting locations where women and girls have limited access to survivor-centered GBV multisectoral services. Additionally, lack of privacy, crowded living conditions, inadequate lighting in compounds and shared spaces in host and IDP communities contribute to increased GBV risks for women and girls. The project also aims to establish new and sustain existing Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSSs), offering comprehensive psychosocial support and GBV case management as well as GBV awareness sessions. It also aims to further increase access to integrated GBV/SRH services (with a focus on Clinical Management of Rape services) through mobile brigades and in Health Facilities. UNFPA also commits to continue procuring Female Dignity Kits and post-rape kits to cover the needs of vulnerable women and survivors of sexual violence. UNFPA as a lead of the GBV AoR will continue to provide strategic direction to the implementation of prevention, mitigation and response to GBV in Cabo Delgado. This will be done through strengthening leadership by increasing human resources, strengthening the GBV Capacity of GB AoR partners, and community resource persons. UNFPA will support co-coordination through an International Non Governmental organisation to enhance continued commitment to leadership of the AoR in Cabo Delgado.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

UNFPA will prioritize dignity and safety of the target beneficiaries in line with the do-no-harm approach. With a long presence in the North, with field offices in Pemba, and Nampula, UNFPA is very familiar with the situations of communities and the dynamics of IDPs/host communities. GBV service mapping will be updated on a regular basis to ensure referral pathways are well-understood by target groups. GBV data will be protected and treated with confidentiality as GBV case workers will be trained on the GBVIMS and data protection protocols. UNFPA ensures during the project’s life refresher sensitization sessions on PSEA to partners, staff and GBV SC members. Women and girls as well as civil society organizations and mainly women-led organizations will be consulted, engaged and their voices listened to during all phases of the project cycle. Mitigation measures will be taken to avoid discrimination among beneficiaries. UNFPA and the GBV AoR will conduct GBV risk assessments to identify GBV and protection risks to women and girls, and develop strategies to mitigate these risks. UNFPA will also make sure to collaborate with protection cluster colleagues to identify and reach the most vulnerable women and girls.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

GBV is both a cause and a consequence of gender inequality, disempowerment of women and girls, and socio-cultural notions of gender construct that disempower and subject women and girls to harmful social and cultural practices. This project aims to address the gender norms that perpetuate GBV through GBV and gender awareness, and empowerment activities that aim to reduce gender inequalities that perpetuate GBV and harmful practices against women and girls. In creating a more gender-aware society, the project will in particular target boys and men. When boys are brought up to value and respect the rights of women, rates of GBV are proven to reduce significantly. Age considerations will be built in the design of interventions in the project. The project targets Women and Girls of reproductive age (WRA 15-49 years), but also young adolescent girls (10-14 year) because of considered and proven vulnerability of youg adolescent girls in the target provinces to GBV, especially early and child marriage. In addition, in the design of Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSS) age is a particular consideration, with spaces for girls set apart from those of older women in order to have girls-friendly services that are easily accessible to and meet the specific needs of girls.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Disability InclusionExplain how the project will identify people with disabilities of different sex and age and remove barriers to their access to assistance.(maximum 200 words) Women and girls living with disabilities due to physical, mental, intellectual, vision and hearing impairments are perceived as different, and/or have less power and status therefore they may be more exposed to GBV because of this. Women and girls with disabilities survivors of GBV face barriers to access information and services, therefore UNFPA with its partners will be committed to make WGSSs accessible to them (e.g. physical accessibility in the space), and make adjustments to mobile brigades outreach. Training/sensitizations on disability inclusion in GBV programming will be offered to GBV AoR members, GBV service providers, social workers, social activists, GBV focal points. GBV safety audit exercise will include questions related to GBV risks/barriers faced by IDPs women and girls with disabilities in the IDP sites. GBV SC key messages will be integrated with disability and inclusion key messages for the communities. Women and girls living with disabilities will be consulted at all times on how to reach excluded and marginalized groups of women and girls and Dignity kits distribution modalities will be adapted taking into consideration COVID-19 restrictions and women and girls with disabilities to ensure distribution is accessible by them.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Environmental considerations will be mainstreamed within every stage of the project cycle to ensure minimal impact to the environment UNFPA will strengthen knowledge of IPs staff on the environmental impact of their activities. WGSSs and mobile brigades will adhere to waste disposal and treatment standards. Environmental awareness components will be integrated into community sensitisation initiatives. The GBV response commodities will be made with environmental considerations. The Dignity Kits in particular, come with a set of reusable sanitary pads and re—usable face masks that are environment friendly
