Project Summary

Bay and Bakool regions are among the most affected regions in Somalia by protracted droughts, flash floods and locust swarms, causing high displacement and acute humanitarian needs. Baydhaba hosts high number of IDP (392,274 IDP) and has 110,905 primary school aged children. About 91,259 children (36,504F; 54,756M) are estimated to be in schools, while 162,239 are out of school children (OOSC) and 1,381 are IDP children with disability (CWD). In Diinsoor, 14,112 are primary school aged children, among those only 3,135 (1,163F; 1,972M) are enrolled in 8 primary schools, with 10,977 being OOSC and 226 CWD. In Ceel-barde there are 1,720 primary school aged children, with only 698 (414M;312F) enrolled in 3 public and IDP schools, while 1,032 (568M; 464F) are OOSC and 50 children are CWD. In Xudur, only 4,384 children (2,436M;1,948F) are currently enrolled in the 16 public and IDP schools, while 10,692 children (5,988M; 4,705F) remain out of schools and 151 are CWD (FCA needs assessment 09-10/20).
Major findings of FCA assessments in the four districts showed enormous shortage of accessible and gender friendly infrastructure such as latrines, handwashing facilities and classrooms, lack of learning and teaching materials, and inadequate number of qualified teachers especially female teachers. Girls face tremendous barriers in accessing to education due social norms that restrict their enrolment, retention and movement. The existing education system also reflects the persistence of these norms and does little to promote change in perception, attitude and behaviours. Children with disabilities met significant challenges such as strong stigma, discrimination and misconceptions, limited disability friendly infrastructure, poor learning environment and lack of specialised teachers trained in special education needs. Caregivers also faced with challenges in covering foods, school fees, uniforms and scholastic materials. Many of IDP children face significant health risks and psychological distress. Child protection and safeguarding mechanisms in schools are weak, due to lack of protection knowledge and relevant trainings among key education personnel. This has compromised the safety and well-being of the children, especially for girls and CWD in accessing education.
Aligning with the 2021 Somalia Humanitarian Response Plan, Education Sector Strategic Plan and Covid-19 Emergency Response Plan for Education, the initiative aims to restore and maintain access to safe, protective, inclusive and continuous learning opportunities for crises and emergency affected primary school boys and girls in Bay and Bakool regions of South West State of Somalia. The project will put a strong emphasis on an integrated approach to education focusing on access, quality of education, protection, child safeguarding, gender equality, conflict sensitivity, and disaster risk reduction (DRR) in the design, approach and activities.
The project will remove barriers to primary education for 7,365 affected children (3,314F;4,051M), prioritising children from the most vulnerable groups such as IDPs, girls, CWD and destitute host community children. About 736 children with disability (331F;405M) will also be targeted (10%). Particularly, 40% IDP children, 60% destitute host community children and 45% of girls will benefit from the support.

FCA will partner with GREDO to implement some components of the project, particularly in relation to rehabilitation and construction of infrastructure, community mobilisation and protection mechanisms in schools. FCA will engage Somalia Institute for Special Education Needs and Disability (SISEN) for capacity building on inclusion and SEN for teachers, MoE and other education personnel.
FCA has excellent capacity to scale up the approach and apply best practice in the four target locations built on its ongoing EiE response in Bay and Bakool regions and solid expertise as a well-known and trusted Finnish education actor.

Implementing Partners

GREDO, is a Somali NGO established in 1992 with strong expertise and experience in agriculture and livelihood, food security, WASH, heath, nutrition and EiE. GREDO has a very strong presence in South West State (SWS) through its head office in Baidoa and 3 regional offices in Bay, Bakool and Lower Shabelle. GREDO is currently partnering with FCA for the implementation of an Education in Emergency Project in Hudur, under the support of ECHO Humanitarian Implementation Plan 2020. It also implements a food security project, “Short term emergence response project –(STRERP)”. GREDO’s EiE intervention concentrates on literacy, numeracy, ABE and particularly TVET programmes with support from DFID and ECHO through Concern Worldwide.
FCA will partner with GREDO, as a consortium member, to implement some components of the project based on its existing expertise, ongoing experiences and strong field operation in the target locations. Partnership agreement between FCA and GREDO will be signed by the two partners prior to the project implementation detailing roles and responsibilities in delivering the project results. In the implementation, FCA will be responsible for the overall management and operation of the project, particularly in delivering all the three components. GREDO will emphasise on construction and rehabilitation of new TLS and WASH facilities, community mobilisation and awareness in relation to education for all, protection and Covid-19 mitigation measures, and school-based protection and child safeguarding mechanisms including referral pathways, given its field presence and great accessibility in the target locations.

Project Activities

The proposed project aims to restore and maintain access to safe, protective, inclusive and continuous learning opportunities for crises and emergency affected primary school boys and girls Bay and Bakool regions of South West State of Somalia. About 10% of the primary beneficiaries will be children with disability (CWD) who will benefit from gender and disable friendly infrastructure, uniforms and scholastic materials, trained teachers on special education needs and school feeding programme. Protection and child safeguarding mechanisms including focal points and referral systems will be effectively set up in the schools to address protection risks of children especially girls and CWD. Objective 1: Ensure emergency and crises affected children have access to a safe and protective learning environment• Rehabilitation and construction of Temporary Learning Spaces (TLSs) with child friendly and gender friendly and inclusive WASH facilities (latrines and handwashing facilities)• Distribution of handwashing materials and frequent disinfection of school premises and classrooms to mitigate Covid-19 outbreak among learners • Implementation of the Global Coalition to Protect Education from Attacks (GCPEA) safety measures in target schools including mapping of school facilities to identify barriers to safe access, evacuation plans, first aid kits and training, and early warning measures • Community mobilisation on children’s rights to education especially for girls and CWD to promote their enrolment and retention in schools • Social mobilisation to sensitise camp leaders, community leaders and local authorities on safe access to and at schools for all children in accordance with the Safe School Declaration Objective 2: Ensure vulnerable children are engaged in lifesaving learning that promotes personal well-being and social cohesion• Recruitment and provision of monthly incentives for the teachers to support their basic needs, with the emphasis on female teachers and teachers specialised in special education needs (SEN). • Provision of scholastic materials and uniforms for the enrolled learners through a one-off conditional mobile cash transfer to the caregivers • Provision of emergency school feeding for the most vulnerable crises affected learners through monthly cash mobile transfer • Procuring and distribution of sanitary materials for adolescent girls to support their enrolment, attendance and retention in schools.• Distribution of safe drinking water to target schools for daily use and handwashing • Establishment of school-based child safeguarding and child protection mechanisms (appointment of child protection focal points and referral pathways) • Trainings for child protection focal points on psychosocial support to improve child protection, prevention of exploitation and sexual abuse and Special Education Needs (SEN) for CWD in line with the government’s disability policy. • Establishment of children’s clubs with a special focus on children’s rights, anti-bullying, and conflict resolution • Community sensitisation on healthy hygiene practices, Covid-19 preventive measures and a violent free learning environment with zero tolerance to all kinds of child abuses including bullying in and out of schools Objective 3: Strengthened capacity to deliver effective and coordinated education in emergencies preparedness and response within the education system• Capacity building of teachers using learner centered pedagogy and TICC inclusive education, protection, and socio-emotional teaching and learning • Training of CECs members in school management, child protection and school safety• Training of head teachers and school administrations on leadership and management emergency preparedness and response planning and coordination. • Advocacy with government and humanitarian actors for mainstreaming inclusion, gender, protection and SEN for equitable access to education by the most vulnerable children.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) to target population affected by crises and emergency has become the most relevant, practical and innovative solutions that could timely address the acute needs of the affected population since such a response could reach the affected groups faster and in the most efficient way. In addition, rather than substituting markets through provision of free goods, integrating mobile cash assistance in EiE response will also support local markets to recover from crisis through the injection of the capital. Through the proposed intervention, FCA will use conditional mobile cash assistance to transfer the money for the caregivers of all enrolled children in the target schools to ease their financial burden by covering costs for school uniforms and scholastic materials. Additionally, selected number of the most vulnerable children (about 20% of enrolled learners) will be supported with emergency school feeding programme through conditional monthly mobile transfer to promote their enrolment and retention in schools. The one-off mobile cash transfer on school uniforms and scholastic materials will enable the caregivers to enrol their children especially girls and CWD in schools. The most vulnerable children from the most vulnerable groups such as CWD, girls from IDPs and destitute host communities, orphans or children from single household headed families/with foster children, and children from minority and traditionally marginalised clans will be specifically targeted to receive monthly mobile cash transfer for a period of 10 months throughout the academic school year. The assistance will enable these vulnerable children to address their basic food needs, and promote their nutrition and well-beings, enrolment and regular attendance in schools. Vulnerability criteria and selection procedure will be thoroughly developed at the beginning of the project, together with MoE, camp managers and other actors, based on the agreed vulnerability criteria. The actual selection will be done through a thorough process which will involve a comprehensive assessment of the target beneficiaries with relevant actors. CECs will also be engaged in the formulation of criteria, selection and monitoring processes to ensure ownership, transparency and accountability. Following the initial assessment, FCA’s Senior Project Manager and Education Officer, and GREDO field officers in each district will verify results and individually assess HHs selected for the transfer. The beneficiary list with detailed information on HHs including marital status, age groups, sexes, disability/malnutrition condition and previous history in receiving humanitarian assistance will be compiled and shared with other relevant stakeholders before operation. Tracking forms indicating status of enrolment and attendance of their children in schools on monthly basis will also be developed and closely monitored. The cash assistance for uniforms and scholastic material will be done through a one-off transfer, while the cash assistance for school feeding programme will be distributed on a monthly basis for a period of 10 months, according to a schedule agreed upon with beneficiaries, authorities, and mobile phone company. The post distribution monitoring will be done by FCA MEAL team in Baidoa and Mogadishu as well as GREDO field team. The project will ensure alignment with cash transfer tools and protocols implemented by the Cash Working Group in Baidoa. Complaints Response Mechanisms will be set up at different levels to manage potential complaints or reports on misuse of the money and corruptions. The independent investigation will be carried out for any reported case. Necessary actions and measures will be taken to address the issues and the issues will also be reported to the donor.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

The selection of mobile cash transfer is based on FCA ongoing Cash and Voucher Assistance in EiE projects funded by MFA Finland and ECHO in Baidoa and Hudur of SWS. The selection of the modality was also built on the outcomes of the market and needs assessment carried out by FCA in Baidoa in 2019 and recently in Hudur and Elberde of SWS conducted in September and October 2020. The assessments aimed to assess market, its functionality, and feasibility of mobile cash transfer in relation to EiE response in Baidoa, Hudur and Elberde, the major operational districts of FCA for EiE response. Hormud, the mobile phone operator in Somalia, was consulted about the feasibility of mobile cash transfer in the three districts. FCA also met with NRC and Save the Children to learn about their experiences. In-depth market assessment including the functionality of the markets in each target location will be carried out at the beginning of the project, before the actual transfer during project implementation.

Mitigation Measures

The project anticipates three major risks during the implementation of the proposed activities. Mitigation measures are carefully designed and included in project cycle to address the risks and effectively deliver the project: 1. Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services.- FCA will actively engage with camp managers, local leaders, CECs and local communities to assess the potential risks before registration of the beneficiaries to access security risks. - Staff and local partner involved in the response are continuously trained on 'do no harm' approach and conflict sensitivity. This will help the implementing team timely assess and identify potential risks. - Given its excellent established relation with the local authorities in Bay and Bakool regions through its EiE and local governance programmes, FCA will closely engage the respective authorities and community groups during the registration of the beneficiaries which will be carried out in the safe place such as schools and community halls. - Distribution of the humanitarian assistance especially school uniforms, scholastic materials and school feeding programme will be done through mobile cash transfer, directly to the caregivers. This will reduce exposure to violence and risks during the distribution. 2. Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.)- As protection is central to the project approach, FCA and GREDO will ensure that the community members particularly caregivers and the children are aware of the protection services that will be set up in the target schools- Community mobilisation and sensitisation will focus on safe access to school, child protection and corporal punishment. - FCA will also train teachers and other education personnel on psychosocial support and other aspects on prevention of exploitation and sexual abuse and SEN to ensure that the teachers are able to early identify the sign of trauma and abuse among children and offer appropriate support.- At the beginning of the project, FCA and GREDO will map out all the available protection and referral services by other actors in the target locations and information about these services will be shared with the teachers, caregivers and other community members for effective referral and services. 3. Risk that marginalised groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. - As right based organisation, FCA adheres to right based approach in its programming. Inclusive participation of all communities especially the marginalised groups is ensured in all project cycle from design, to implementation, monitoring and evaluation. - Advocacy and awareness raising on education for all, especially girls and CWD will be carried out in the surrounding communities to ensure caregivers’ attention and active participation in key processes of the project.- CECs will be established/revived and include fair representation of women, youth and marginalised groups selected among the parents, teachers, care takers and traditional leaders. - Sensitisation and active involvement of the traditional leaders from different clans/community groups will promote communal awareness on inclusivity and importance of girls and CWD’ access to learning opportunities. 4. Risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity in targeted project area- The project will engage with the camp managers and local communities to timely access information on evictions. - FCA will also build capacity of CEC members on how to manage education facilities during evictions. - The project will work with local authorities and landowners

Please provide further explanation

The project is gearing the response and interventions towards the longer-term durable solution to displacement, promoting development and humanitarian nexus. FCA will closely engage with the local authorities, MoE of SWS, teachers, head teachers, CECs, parents, learners and other education actors throughout the project cycle to ensure their active participation in the project activities and to benefit from the assistance. This will promote long-term capacity building, ownership and sustainability beyond project lifespan. The support will have long-term impact on the status and well-being of the primary target groups. In particular, the education in emergencies provided to the learners will promote their lifelong learning, give them hope and prospect in life and particularly help prevent early child marriage, child labour, child soldier recruitment by the armed and militia groups. Different trainings on school leadership and management, and school supervision including measurement of learning outcome, emergency response and coordination provided to head teachers, school administrations and other education personnel such as District Education Officers will in the long term build local capacity and ownership, contributing to promoting effective service delivery to the people in needs in the target districts. Closely working with the existing community groups such as CECs and local communities will promote communal activism for the effective management of the schools, contributing to enhancing safety for the children in accessing quality education and safe learning environment. Capacity building of the teachers will contribute to building the human resources especially qualified teachers in Somalia. Integration of protection, special education needs and psychosocial support in the trainings of the teachers and other education personnel will directly address protection needs and needs of the most vulnerable children especially CWD in accessing quality education. The trained teachers will remain as pools of human assets in the target communities that could be utilised by other humanitarian and development actors in the future. Provision of mobile cash transfer to caregivers to cover school uniforms and scholastic materials and particularly school feeding programme to the most vulnerable children will contribute to address the poverty, food insecurity and vulnerability of the target people of concerns, promoting the children’s well-being and positive coping mechanism and resilience of the target groups in the long-run.
