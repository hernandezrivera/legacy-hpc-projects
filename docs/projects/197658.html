Project Summary

Syria is one of the world’s most complex humanitarian emergencies laded with ongoing hostilities, resulting in widespread destruction of civilian infrastructure, including health and agricultural facilities leading to food, nutrition and related insecurity.
The proposed action seeks to protect and enhance food security and livelihoods of conflict‑affected highly vulnerable IDPs and host communities in North West Syria (NWS) (Aleppo). In addition, the targeting strategy will ensure assistance reaches the most vulnerable IDP and host HHs at greatest risk of protection concerns, specifically female-, child- and elderly-headed households, PWD and PLW.
This will be achieved through an integrated programme of assistance including repeated mixed‑modality life‑sustaining food assistance (monthly assistance to the most vulnerable households) and emergency lifesaving food security assistance in addition to bakery and livelihoods support. The complementary package of livelihoods support will increase household resilience through the promotion of entrepreneurship and the development of small businesses along with the improvement of agricultural production and increasing the ability of targeted households to meet food needs. The bakery intervention will assist bakeries in filling the gaps in supply chain for flour and yeast. Further the bakeries intervention will also assist un-employed youth is setting up small bakery outlets to support income and will contribute to developing entrepreneurial skills.

Modality:The planned activities will have a repeated support for a period 10 months through our food kits, food vouchers, micro grants and direct contracting.
Data Collection and Monitoring-The monitoring will be through our well defined M&E systems. Monitoring will be diligently executed starting from Verification, Distribution Monitoring, Post Distribution Monitoring and Satisfaction Surveys. The data will be collected online, primarily using tablets and Kobo tools. We deploy several tools to include Excel and SPSS to analyze and report. A robust system is in place for complaints receipt and redressal, with dedicated teams available for handling and resolving complaints. A robust system of lodging complaints is already in place through communications tools such as WhatsApp, SMS, email, and complainant boxes.
Reporting:We will ensure prompt reporting on all of our projects through regular filing of donor and 4W reports and ad hoc reporting requirements.
CVA Support Systems – A fully automated and online Cash Voucher System developed internally and utilized for last 6 years will be deployed. The system is flexible to use paper vouchers or cards and can be of single or multiple use. To complement our voucher programs, we have framework agreements with Fund Service Providers that provide cash distributions through formal arrangements. Due to this system, our response time is short.

3 - Needs

The overall People in Need has increased from 14.6 million in 2022 to 15.3 million in 2023, including 5.3 million IDPs, 9.9 million residents, and 52,700 returnees.According to WFP remote food security monitoring, in 2022, reported facing food shortages and engaging in some food coping mechanism to maximize the use of little available resources. For instance, households adopting IPC crisis or beyond food coping alike mechanisms reached 50 percent, although 15 percent of households had to withdraw children from school, increasing risks for child labor and related protection concerns, 52 percent of households made tough decisions of restricting food consumption for adults in favor of children, 63percent households reported reducing the number of usually accustomed meals households borrowing money to buy food reached as high as 58 percent. As many as eight percent of households had to sell assets due to lack of food or money to buy food in addition to more than 80 percent who reported not having any assets to sell should they have needed to, indicating high levels of depleted means at households' level. As many as 90 percent of households reported relying on markets as main sources of consumed foods.In addition, a cholera outbreak was declared by the Syrian Ministry of Health(MoH) in September 2022. The outbreak triggered a significant increase in waterborne diseases. Between 25 August and 5 November 2022, 35,569 suspected cases were reported, including 68 deaths(HNO2023).

4 - Implementation of activities

Direct funding from BHA is sought to respond to critical FS gaps in Suran, Al-Bab and Mare sub-districts through the provision of 10 rounds of repeat, in-kind FS assistance targeting 3,500 IDP and host HHs. Proposed action is anticipated to start in October2022 with 2 of total 10 rounds of repeat assistance during 2022 and 8 rounds delivered in 2023. The proposed action will compliment with in-kind RTERs, 1-month food ration to meet emergency needs of a further 1,000 HHs newly affected by conflict. Funding will provide 6 rounds of assistance to 2,500 host and IDP HHs in Suran, Al-Bab, Mare and Ar-Rae sub-district from June through to December 2023, with total 8 rounds repeat assistance to be delivered during 2022. Each food kit=21.6KG, and incloud the contents: Short-Grain Milled White Rice, Coarse Grain Wheat Bulgur, Brown Lentils, Red Lentils, Dried Chickpeas, Vitamin A Fortified Sunflower Oil, Iodized Salt, Tomato paste, Ghee, Sugar, Canned Fish - Tuna, and Olive Oil.A mixed-modality approach is proposed, with an estimated 500 HHs receiving cash-based food voucher assistance (average $60 per HH per month) and the remaining 3,500 HHs receiving in-kind family food rations Funding is sought to restore and protect agricultural livelihoods and build household resilience for 350 HHs for Micro Gardens. Funding is sought to support 2 bakeries servicing 46,000 HHs. 136 of flour and yeast plus minor renovation. Microgrants will be provided to 100 youth to set up bakery outlets.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project will follow cross-cutting protection mainstreaming guidelines as per the protection cluster guidelines, including collection of sex and age disaggregation of information, participation, access, and an understanding of how different needs will be met. We design our projects with an emphasis on meaningful access, safety, dignity and non-discrimination so that all potential beneficiaries will benefit from inclusive service provision to the greatest extent possible. Moreover, by applying lessons learned from previous interventions, we will engage with local councils, relief committees and other key actors to mitigate risks faced by women and children, in particular, and to build an environment that guarantees basic human rights. To reduce risks or harm to vulnerable populations in the face of conflict sensitivity, beneficiaries in Syria will be systematically involved in all stages of the project cycle wherever possible. Accessibility measures will be built into assistance delivery, including the option for home delivery to beneficiaries unable to safely access distribution points due to mobility or other restrictions. To mitigate against protection risks, we work closely with LCs and other administrative bodies to ensure open and accountable feedback mechanisms for beneficiaries served. Refresher training on NGO PSEA and Child Protection policies were delivered during 2020 to ensure all staff understand how to identify and correctly report any SEA or CP concerns


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Syria faces the most complex humanitarian emergencies laded with ongoing hostilities, resulting in massive numbers requiring assistance. With the deteriorating situation, coping mechanisms fail to lead to serious protection issues. This is further complexed by the change of roles where females head the households traditionally held by the male population. These insecurities come with protection issues, particularly GBV and child labor. Coupled with increased hostilities and COVID, it resulted in negative coping mechanisms such as early marriages and child labor. A safe estimate informs that 42% of the affected population faces one or more protection issues.Big Heart adopted a core concept in all interventions to make PSEA mandatory in all projects and activities. All BH staff, volunteers, and contractors will get orientation sessions about PSEA policies and sign CoC.To counter any risks, all project locations will include banners and complaint boxes that deliver messages from accountability teams regarding the complaints and feedback channels in addition to a stand-alone system regarding reporting and response of any PSEA case, starting from receiving the report to the investigation process, and provide aid and support to the survivor till the closure of the case after achieving the objectives of the investigation, in addition, strong awareness campaigns are designed along with a robust Complaint Redressal Mechanism to handle complaints.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

We monitor risks real‑time and will continue to follow for rapid adjustments to planned activities as necessary. The following potential risks exists and appropriate measures are applied to program design: Risk 1 Authorities: LCs and local authorities (including AFAD) remain supportive of the NGOs activities and continue to cooperate with established Standard Operating Procedures designed to ensure targeted and transparent delivery of assistance on the basis of need alone. We continually meet the local authorities to ensure optimal cooperation. Risk 2 Verifications: 100% house-to-house beneficiary verification reduces the risk of inclusion or exclusion criteria while close coordination with other FSL actors reduces the risk of duplicationRisk 3 COVID 19: Established guidelines for COVID-19 prevention measures for all field operations continue to mitigate against the risk of transmission Risk 4 Security: Security is continuously monitored and context in targeted areas allows ongoing access by NGO staff. Risk 5 Protection: Protection risk will be identified earlier and mitigated through established systems. We have an established Risk Advisory Department managed by expatriate staff. We are continually update our contingency plans to adapt to the changing context in Syria, these plans detail various scenarios and required actions for mitigating risks, including operational and programmatic changes. Risk Mitigation is updated on a monthly basis.
