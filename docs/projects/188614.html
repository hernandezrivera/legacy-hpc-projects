Project Summary

Within the broader Syria response, Mercy Corps is a leader in humanitarian coordination, chairing the Syria INGO Regional Forum (SIRF), and the NES NGO Forum. Mercy Corps further provides unique, granular analyses of the context for the different coordination mechanisms through the Humanitarian Access Team (HAT) and engages in coordination at all levels to provide services, which keep the humanitarian community informed in order to adapt and design appropriate responses.
Syria International NGO Regional Forum (SIRF): In light of the increasingly regional nature of the Syrian Crisis, and the humanitarian and political effects on neighboring countries, The SIRF is an INGO-led regional forum based in Amman, Jordan, set up in April 2013 to give voice to INGOs conducting humanitarian responses in the Syria region and to facilitate their collective engagement. The SIRF Representative is the internal port of call for all 58 SIRF Members and its 12 Observers, and is also the main external interlocutor for all regional humanitarian and development inter-agency coordination mechanisms led by the Regional Refugee Coordinator, the Regional Humanitarian Coordinator and the Resident and Humanitarian Coordinator for Syria. SIRF exists to serve the needs of its members and advocate on behalf of populations affected by the Syrian Crisis, articulated around four core functions: coordination, representation, advocacy and information management. To achieve this end SIRF targets 45 WoS High level meetings and 3 policies and guidelines released by WoS coordination groups led/co-led by Mercy Corps.
NES NGO Forum: The NES NGO Forum is the lead coordination body for NGOs working from Iraq and within NES, into accessible parts of Aleppo, DEZ, Ar-Raqqa and Al-Hasakeh Governorates in Syria, as endorsed by the WoS Strategic Steering Group. Due to the unique coordination arrangements in NES and limited UN cross-border operation, the NES NGO Forum plays an expanded role, and leads an NGO-hosted sector coordination system, with core functions similar to UN OCHA field offices in other contexts. The NES Forum’s objective is to contribute to a principled, appropriate, accountable, and effective NGO response through inter-agency coordination, information management, representation and advocacy. The forum has made significant strides in ensuring a cohesive coordination structure and that NES response actors are well-represented. The NES NGO Forum continues to lead needs identification, strategic planning and response monitoring around the humanitarian response in NES. The NES Forum also enables humanitarian space for NGOs through leading on negotiations with local authorities around NGO access and protection concerns, and direct engagement with the UN. Additionally, the Forum seeks to enhance communication and information-sharing across hubs through management of inter-sector data flow through 4Ws and response matrices. The NES Forum targets are as follows: (1) Number of international and Syrian organisations actively coordinating in the proposed area of work: 38 members actively participating; (2) Number of 4W reports drafted per month: 4.
Humanitarian Access Team: The HAT plays a critical role in the transmission of granular reporting and analysis to the wider humanitarian community, taking a WoS learning approach. The HAT maintains an online map-based web platform with critical humanitarian and conflict-related data that is published on www.humanitarianaccessteam.org. The HAT targets: (1) # of organisations with user accounts to Humanitarian Access Team (HAT) information platform: 330; (2) % of organisations reporting relevance of web platform for programmatic decision- making: 75%.

4 - Needs

SIRF: The humanitarian response to the crisis in Syria remains one of the most complex in the world. Increasing humanitarian needs, acute access restriction and segregation in aid modalities result in fragmented response. Despite progress made since the creation of the Whole-of-Syria approach in 2014, the July 2015 Operational Peer Review and the 2016 WoS Review have highlighted the need to strengthen coordination and sharpen information sharing & analysis to deliver a more predictable, efficient and principled response. Coordination in NES: Households across NES are facing a continuous cycle of crises, as the spread of COVID-19 in the region compounds the vulnerabilities that were already exacerbated by a decade-long conflict and the October 2019 Turkish incursion, the effects of the Lebanese banking crisis and the consequent devaluation of the Syrian Pound and the constant worsening of the socio economic situation over the last 12 months. The NES Forum has continued strengthening its operations as the main coordination body for the NES NGO response, comprised of international and local NGOs. The NES NGO Forum has worked to fill the gap in humanitarian coordination (strategically and operationally), negotiation, representation and advocacy for the response.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk analysis and conflict sensitive programming is a cornerstone to Mercy Corps protection mainstreaming. Support to coordination systems in Syria aims at safe, informed and protection oriented decisions. Mercy Corps endeavors to ensure that: 1. Information and coordination provided supports relevant, protection sensitive and community sensitive programming. There are protection risks associated with poor humanitarian planning and response exclusion of particular groups, exacerbating tensions between communities and with authorities. Informed decision making and coordination with relevant groups is based in liaison with actors and staff with experience in the Syria context and analysis. 2. Information provided by coordination mechanisms and the HAT is accurate and allows for informed analysis. Coordination support aims to mitigate against protection risks associated with programming in unsafe locations for beneficiaries and staff. Support is adjusted to the context, in providing daily, weekly and monthly updates where relevant to our partner organizations. Dedicated on-ground field staff support the accuracy and relevance of analysis and guidance provided. 3. Advocacy by NGOs is relevant and informed. Coordination forums and the HAT support appropriate analysis for informed advocacy. Coordination bodies are engaged with the wider WoS response to ensure operational concerns of partners are adequately reflected in inter-agency coordination and represented.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Participant safeguarding is central to Mercy Corps’ values, and we uphold a zero- tolerance tolerance approach towards any form of sexual exploitation and abuse. In order to prevent safeguarding violations of any kind, all staff, casual labor and external contractors receive orientation and training, and are required to sign the Code of Conduct. The safeguarding training is comprehensive and is delivered to all Mercy Corps and partner staff. Where Mercy Corps and partner staff have higher levels of contact with participants, they will receive tailored capacity strengthening on Protection Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and will participate in quarterly events, which address PSEA concerns in target communities, and facilitate the roll-out of PSEA tools and preventative actions. This process is led by our WoS Safeguarding Manager and is supported by a network of focal points within Mercy Corps. Partner staff will have the opportunity to risk assess their own areas in terms of safeguarding and create proactive action plans. Community participants will be informed on what safeguarding is and how they can report any concerns through sensitisation work across programmes. In addition, external contractors and staff such as suppliers or Third Party Monitors (TPMs) are regularly trained by department focal points who have completed tailored Training of Trainer (ToT) safeguarding training.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Mercy Corps closely coordinates our contingency planning alongside the Humanitarian Access Team (HAT) which is hosted by Mercy Corps. Along with INSO, OCHA and cluster analysis, Mercy Corps operational planning strives to minimise risk to beneficiaries, partners and staff. 2021 is a challenging operational context in northern Syria. The the humanitarian situation in north Syria deteriorated in 2020 due to the economic crisis and COVID-19 amongst other factors. According to the HAT’s Jan 2021 Report the rapid depreciation of the Syrian pound since mid-October 2019 has strained the beleaguered economy of Syria and greatly diminished the already tenuous purchasing power of many Syrians. This report analyzes how business owners and workers respond to rapid currency depreciation, and quantifies the relationship between the daily wage rate of unskilled workers and the price of essential items across the zones of control in Syria during a period of rapid and uncontrolled depreciation of the pound from October 2019 to October 2020. Using the same data, predictions have been made on wage rates and food affordability across all Syrian zones of control for 2021. Mercy Corps remains in close coordination with our donors and partners for appropriate contingency planning to enable and adaptable programming.
