Project Summary

This project will directly addrress WASH needs and provide mitigation and response support on COVID19 Pandamic in Borno and Yobe States. This project is aligned with the 2020 HRP Strategic Objectves and WASH Sector Objectives. This project is also aligned to the WASH sector response to COVID-19 guidance and priorities.
Nigeria, the country with the highest population in Africa, is currently facing one of the most critical humanitarian crises on the African continent. The Boko Haram related conflict has forced millions people to flee their homes within the country.
The conflict has resulted in an estimated 7.1 million individuals in need of humanitarian assistance in 2019 – more than 50% of the entire estimated population of the three affected States. In addition to this humanitarian landscape in accessible areas, for 2020 the humanitarian community has identified approximately 1 million individuals staying in hard-to-reach areas with limited to no access to humanitarian assistance.
The deterioration of the health system due to insecurity and physical damage to health and WASH facilities has impacted the disease preparedness and services system resulting in an increase in morbidity and mortality related to childhood communicable diseases especially those that are preventable. Data provided through WHO weekly bulletin of November 2019 as well as Nigeria Centre for Disease Control (NCDC) Situation Reports of 2019 on outbreaks, reports increasing new cases of cholera, COVID19 and Lassa fever and other communicable diseases.
Moreover, the outbreak of the COVID-19 in Nigeria, like many other countries of the world, has become the focus of government at all levels in both preparedness and response approaches. Most affected states, i.e. Lagos, Kano, Ogun and Abuja (the Federal Capital Territory), Borno and recent detection of case in Yobe have become most active in these responses. Other states of the federation have also taken measures to ensure the virus does not find its way to their region. While most of these interventions have been urban-based, a number of hard-to-reach areas and communities have been poorly covered expecially in awareness creation and sensitisation including services support in terms of health and provisions of WASH/hygiene items. With the scourge of violent conflicts from the Boko Haram insurgents in the North East, much of the population have become victims of the crisis with many deaths, displacements, and loss of livelihoods while the continuation and fear of attacks have made many lose coping capacities. A large number of these people have clustered in both formal and informal Internally Displaced Peoples' camps. While some of these over-crowded camps are recognised by the governments, many are not. Basic supportive interventions have been given to some residents of these camps by governments and organisations, however, the increasing rate of population due to displacement and births have increased further needs. These communities and IDP-camps are over-crowded with poor hygiene facilities, limited access to health facilities and livelihood thus making the people more vulnerable and succeptible to many health hazards which have been recorded in the past. This vulnerable and isolated population become unprepared targets which has not been provided with the knowledge and tools to ensure adequate information, sensitisation and awareness creation about the pandemic and support in the areas of WASH and NFIs with a view to save lives of the most underserved and exposed population.

1 - Needs

The crisis, largely triggered by the a regionalized armed conflict, is one of the most severe protection crises in the world, with civilians bearing the brunt of ongoing hostilities, malnutrition, food insecurity, displacements, and dire conditions including over-congestion and severe shortages of shelters across camps and communities hosting IDPs. Social safety net and socio-economic resources are depleted with most populations depending on meagre and overstretched humanitarian services. Already with poor WASH services, the COVID-19 pandemic, these population are faced with multiple criseis including the collapse of already overstretched health system, food crisis, protection, etc. should COVID-19 enter the state. This, therefore, thus makes prevention and respopnse very essential. It is very critical to put measures in place as part of a preparedness plan in order to mitigate its occurrence, and to avoid its spread, especially amongst the IDPs who are less informed, living in congested conditions, and hence very vulnerable to the disease.REACH Multi Sector Need Assessment (MSNA) of November 2019 reports that in Yobe State, 63% of households were found to have severe or extreme humanitarian needs with 49% of those households, this severe or extreme severity of needs was primarily driven by an LSG in WASH, for 17% by an FSL LSG and for 14% by a combination of both. Maternal and child malnutrition is a significant public health issue, as well as a social and economic problem in Nigeria. About one-third of children under 5 years are stunted (more than half in the north of Nigeria). Each year an estimated one (1) million Nigerian children die before their 5th birthday (NDHS Survey 2013 Report) and malnutrition contributes to nearly half of these deaths. Borno and Yobe remain among five (5) states where GAM is above the warning threshold and SAM is above WHO’s SAM crisis threshold. Underlying contextual factors contributing to deteriorating WASH services in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States include the vulnerability of the populations to climate shocks, ongoing conflict dynamics putting communities vulnerable to epidemic outbreaks. More immediate causes are primarily related to poor WASH, health behavior associated with malnutrition, including poor infant and young child feeding practices amongst caregivers, poor household water treatment and poor adherence to handwashing practices.COOPI being a member of Yobe Cholera Task Force, and based on its Disaster Risk Reduction DRR assessment conducted by COOPI, IMC and ALIMA (December 2019) and COOPI’s Livelihoods Community Based Participatory Planning (CBPP) conducted in December 2019 in Geidam and Damaturu LGAs, it is appeared that the majority of areas of targeted LGAs do not have a cholera and other epidemics and disasters preparedness structures to meet the current and future needs. The WASH situation in Potiskum, an LGA with the highest population density in Yobe state is vulnerable due to very limited support from WASH actors, deteriorating infrastructure, and an essential lack of awareness campaigns. Coupled with the deteriorating nutrition situation in the state, there is need to scale up WASH coverage with improved and sustainable quality of assistance.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Beneficiaries communities, especially women will be involved in decision making on various aspects of project implementation, since most of the activities are directed to increase knowledge and build capacity of beneficiaries. As Rights Holder, beneficiaries in this project, either as member of Water User Committee, students in schools, or even just as listeners of radio programme, are implementing the project together with COOPI. In this sense, COOPI ensures beneficiaries are directly involved in the project implementation, so that their opinions will be always surely incorporated in the implementation of the project. COOPI’s current programs involve community resilience groups will be engaged to WASH interventions including COVID19 and cholera preventions activities.An expat WASH Project Manager with experience in Cholera prevention and response will have the overall responsibility of the on-going monitoring of activities implementation and will be assisted by Senior WASH Officers and Program Development Officer in coordination office, who will be supervising work of Hygiene Officers and WASH Technician in each project location for the software component of the project. Further, Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms will be strengthened and reinforced through team of Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) Assistants will be present at each LGA to collect complaints from the beneficiaries. The complaints will be categorized, and they will also be used as a tool to find solutions.
