Project Summary

The residents of South Sudan have consistently experienced worsening circumstances ever since the nation gained independence in July 2011. The hostilities between the government and opposition faction in December 2013 exacerbated these conditions even further. Despite the endorsement of the Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in the Republic of South Sudan (R-ARCSS) in September 2018 and the establishment of the Revitalized Transitional Government of National Unity (RTGoNU) in February 2020, the populace continues to confront a multitude of challenges. These challenges include persistent intercommunal conflicts, adverse climate variations, obstacles in accessing resources, and the impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic. These ongoing issues have led to continuous displacement of populations, droughts, floods, which have severely affected livelihoods, as well as the health of individuals, particularly women and children.
There are significant hazards associated with the delivery and immediate postpartum period for mothers and newborns, including a higher likelihood of complications for babies born with low birthweight, premature births, or being small for gestational age. The risks also include birth asphyxia and perinatal and neonatal infections, which account for approximately 40% of deaths within the first month of life for children under the age of five.
Among children under the age of five, there is a substantial burden of disease, as evidenced by mortality rates of 98, 63, and 40 per 1,000 live births for under-fives, infants, and neonates respectively. Malaria, diarrhea, and pneumonia are responsible for over 75% of deaths among children under the age of five, with malaria accounting for over 50% and playing a major role in the high levels of malnutrition among children in the country.
The utilization of maternal and child health care services is low, with only 19% of pregnant women receiving antenatal care at the fourth visit, 44% having skilled attendants during birth, and just 9.7% receiving postnatal care. Family planning uptake stands at 2.5%, all of which contributes to a maternal mortality ratio of over 1,150 per 100,000 live births, one of the highest figures in the world. The lack of skilled healthcare providers, essential medications, medical supplies, and equipment, as well as poor referral systems for maternal care services and limited capacity of County health departments to provide effective governance in the healthcare system, have all contributed to limited access to basic emergency obstetric and newborn care (BEmONC) and immunization services. Immunization coverage is exceedingly low at 49% in the country, leaving children more susceptible to vaccine-preventable diseases. This low routine immunization coverage can be attributed to the shortage of skilled human resources at the grassroots level for vaccination operations, high turnover rates for the limited number of skilled personnel available and accessing challenges due to insecurity and recurring flooding.
This project will provide support to Primary Health Care Units (PHCUs), Primary Health Care Centers (PHCCs), and the following activities will be carried
 Child health services
 Maternal and neonatal health services
 Basic Essential Obstetric and Newborn Care
 Services addressing sexual and gender-based violence
 Distribution of procured crucial medicines and supplies with cluster partnership.
 Preparedness and response for Covid-19
 Disease surveillance and outbreak response
 Quality improvement and supervision.
To ensure that these services reach individuals facing limited access to healthcare, our project will regularly conduct mobile outreach clinics. Additionally, community-based health services will be implemented through the Boma Health Initiative (BHI), which includes a component dedicated to promoting safe motherhood. This comprehensive approach will strengthen community resilience and provide essential services.

2. Project activities

The program will tackle the identified healthcare challenges by aligning with global standards, principles, national policies, and specific technical guidance from the cluster. Our integrated approach combines strengthening the healthcare system, enhancing maternal and child health services, increasing immunization coverage, and addressing malnutrition. Help aims to enhance the availability of high-quality, life-saving primary healthcare services for the most vulnerable populations, with a specific focus on women and children. This effort seeks to decrease child and maternal mortality rates in Yirol West, East, and Awerial Counties. The Basic Package of Health and Nutrition integrated services will be delivered through the Primary Health Care Centers (PHCC), Primary Health Care Units (PHCUs), hospitals, and Boma Health Initiative (BHI- Government Community Health Strategy) structures. These comprehensive life-saving services encompass child health care, maternal and neonatal health care, BEmONC, services addressing sexual and gender-based violence, distribution of cluster supplied essential medicines and supplies, disease surveillance, outbreak response, and quality enhancement and supervision. To ensure that these services are accessible to the most vulnerable populations who have limited healthcare access, the project will conduct regular mobile outreach clinics. Additionally, community-based health services will be provided through the Boma Health Initiative (BHI), which includes a safe motherhood component. This approach will help fortify community resilience and ensure the provision of essential services, even in situations where communities face unexpected challenges and are challenging to reach. Help's mission is to ensure that by the end of 2024, young girls and boys under the age of five, adolescent girls, and women of reproductive age in Awerial, Yirol East and West Counties experience improved and equitable access to high-quality health services and embrace appropriate health-related behaviors. This initiative aims to decrease maternal and under-five mortalities by extending the reach and availability of standardized primary healthcare services through an integrated service delivery model that prioritizes people, offers comprehensive care, and is continuous in nature. The proposed project will be executed in Awerial, Yirol East and Yirol West Counties, with a specific focus on serving a total of 397,169 individuals, including 86,286 in Awerial, 123,085 in Yirol East, and 187,798 in Yirol West.The Project Outputs include:• 1. Improved Capacity of Frontline Health and Community Workers to provide flexible, integrated services for the management of common childhood illnesses to young children in Yirol West, Yirol East & Awerial Counties• 2: Improved access to quality maternal and child health services, including antenatal care, safe delivery practices, and essential care for pregnant women, adolescent girls, newborns, and infants in Yirol West, Yirol East & Awerial Counties• 3: Government and other partners have increased capacity to deliver routine and supplementary immunization and respond to disease outbreaks• 4: Increased access to an integrated package of health and nutrition services for IDPs and host communities in Awerial and Yirol East Counties.To achieve the above several activities will be done:• Child Health Services: All health facilities will conduct routine health education sessions on child health, administer routine immunizations, implement IMNCI, ensure rapid response and referral for complicated cases, distribute Long-Lasting Insecticidal Nets (LLITNs), provide vitamin A supplementation, promote appropriate infant and young child feeding practices, conduct nutrition screenings, and refer individuals to nutrition therapeutic programs.• Maternal and Neonatal Health Services: This includes provision of health education sessions, ANC, safe delivery, PMTCT and referral, Sexual and GBV, etc
