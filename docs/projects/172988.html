Project Summary

Evidence suggests that the likely prolonged restrictions implemented by the Libyan authorities to control the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic across the country will continue to have an impact on international and domestic food supply chains. Mobility and supply chain limitations, combined with the general economic impacts of COVID-19 have resulted in higher prices, as well as loss of income due to increasing unemployment and prolonged disruptions to businesses and people’s livelihoods.
Migrants and refugees are particularly vulnerable to these risks, as they often rely on daily labour. According to DTM’s Migrant Emergency Food Security Report, released in May 2020, 72% of migrants relying on daily casual labour stated that it had been difficult to find work due to movement limitations linked to COVID-19. According to the same report, 56% of all migrants in Libya had to compromise their food intake during the difficult months of the pandemic.
The situation is even more complicated and unpredictable for those in detention centers, where the nutrition needs of the vulnerable continue to grow. In 2020, on average 3,000 migrants and refugees were detained in government-held detention centers in Libya at any given time.
The aim of the project is to provide emergency meals and nutritious packages to vulnerable migrants and refugees upon disembarkation at DPs and in DCs, including pregnant and lactating women, children, elderly and people with medical needs.

1 - Needs

Despite the prospects for a durable peace following the signing of the ceasefire in November 2020, the situation in Libya remains volatile, as a result of years of protracted conflict alongside political and economic instability. Moreover, the effects of the COVID-19 pandemic and the preventive measures and curfews have negatively impacted the already precarious socio-economic situation, especially for the most vulnerable, including migrants, refugees and internally displaced persons (IDPs). In addition to the effect of COVID-19 on people’s ability to work, closure of borders to control the spread of the COVID-19 in Libya has impacted international and domestic food supply chains and has resulted in an increase in the prices of many essential items, including food. This has increased difficulties for low-income and already vulnerable populations or those newly out of work, to afford basic food and goods. Against a backdrop of COVID-19's, it is likely the caseload of vulnerable populations will increase in 2021 as the pandemics socio-economic impacts unfold. Significant changes to lines of control that took place in June, with GNA-affiliated forces re-taking Tripoli, Tarhuna and nearby towns, saw displaced people slowly starting to return to their home areas. Coinciding with the start of the COVID-19 pandemic, the number of migrants and refugees in Libya has steadily decreased, from 626,000 people in April to 585,000 people in August (DTM migrant report round 32). According to the 2021 Libya Humanitarian Needs Overview, in 2020, nearly 699,000 people are estimated to be in need of food and livelihood assistance. This includes 77,000 displaced people, 77,000 returnees, 415,000 non-displaced Libyans, 123,000 migrants and 7,000 refugees. This is a doubling, compared to 2019, with the most significant increases among displaced and non-displaced Libyans, mostly due to the conflict, which continued to displace families and the negative effects of COVID-19 on livelihoods. Migrants and refugees have also been disproportionally affected by movement restrictions further affecting their access to markets and livelihoods, as most of them are employed in temporary or daily labour and can no longer afford basic needs goods. According to DTM’s Migrant Emergency Food Security Report, released in May 2020, 72% of migrants relying on daily casual labour stated that it had been difficult to find work due to movement limitations linked to COVID-19. According to the same report, 56% of all migrants in Libya had to compromise their food intake during the difficult months of the pandemic. Almost a third of all migrants and refugees are food insecure, with the highest number of migrants in Benghazi and refugees in Tripoli. With the unpredictable nature of the situation and the ongoing detention regime for migrants and refugees, the nutrition and food needs in detention continue to grow. In 2020, on average 3,000 migrants and refugees were detained in government-held detention centers in Libya at any given time. While IOM will not support direct food assistance at detention centres (DC) as a regular practice due to its potential in supporting continued incarceration of persons held in detention, it is ready to provide emergency food rations in exceptional situation, as emergency life-saving measure. The emergency life-saving assistance will be extended also at disembarkation points (DP), in line with the Food Security Sector policy paper on the Provision of Direct Food Assistance in Detention Centres, following rescue operations.

2 - Activities

This project will include the provision of emergency food assistance to vulnerable migrants at disembarkation points, and of emergency meals and nutritious packages to vulnerable migrants and refugees in DCs. This provision of nutritious food packages for vulnerable populations includes pregnant and lactating women, children, elderly as well as people suffering from medical conditions. This project will assist 2,194 migrants in detention centers and 1,101 migrants at disembarkation points. In line with the Principled Framework for the provision of food assistance in DCs developed by the Migrant and Refugees Platform’s partners and endorsed by the Protection and FS Sectors, IOM will support migrants in DCs upon request of DCIM and for a maximum of 5 days by providing ready-to eat cooked meals. Whereas, for migrants in DPs, food assistance will be a one-time provision of energy biscuits upon disembarkation after being rescued/intercepted at sea.
