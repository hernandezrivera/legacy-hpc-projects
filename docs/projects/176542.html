Project Summary

In line with the FSL cluster strategy for 2021, ACTED will contribute towards improving food security of over 850,000 beneficiaries (141,667 HHs) through provision of in-kind and cash food assistance, enhancing emergency food production though distribution of livelihood kits and reducing dependency on food and agricultural inputs through resilience building activities such as training and market support functionality.

1 - Needs

The border closure and restrictions on movement and disruption of supply chain due to COVID- 19 had further affected depreciation of South Sudanese Pound which resulted in further inflation with staples at 200-400% higher than last year, and 400-600% higher than 5-year average. The ongoing sub-national and inter-communal violence and natural disasters such as floods and droughts contributed towards loss of assets, displacement and low agricultural productivity of the population. Eastern Equatorian farmers further endure invasion of pests such as locusts. These contributed towards the worsening food security in the county. According to the latest IPC findings, more than 7.2 million people are expected to face severe acute food insecurity during the lean season of 2021. This represents 60 % of the population. Up to 4.7 million South Sudanese are expected to be in IPC Phase 3 – Crisis, 2.5 million in IPC 4 – Emergency and 31,000 in IPC 5 – Catastrophe, with some payams in Famine like conditions. In addition, more than 1.4 million children under 5 and 483,000 pregnant and lactating women will likely be malnourished in 2021.

2 - Project activities

1. Undertake general food distribution or unconditional cash/ voucher transfers for the most severely food insecure (IPC 4 & 5, refugees and POCs), with blanket supplementary feeding for children under 5 and PWLs in Wau, Jur River, Awerial, Bor South and Maban counties. 2. Provide conditional assistance in the form of food through Food for Assets activities for RES in Jur River, Bor South and Maban counties. 3. Protect livelihoods through main season seeds & tool and fish & vegetable kit distribution (IPC 3, 4 & 5) in Wau, Jur River, Awerial, Bor South, Maban and Magwi county. 4. Implement activities to help sustain community coping mechanisms and build resilience in IPC 4 & 5; measures to combat: Post harvest losses (PHL); pest surveillance & control especially FAW and DL in IPC 3. 5. Support market functionality: scaled up support for market dependent households in Jur River county for IPC 3 and above HHs.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Market analysis is a crucial part of ACTED’s cash and voucher activities and are incorporated into the response. Before undertaking any cash or voucher activities, ACTED will conduct a detailed market analysis and routinely monitor prices to ensure the market is functional and has the sufficient capacity to meet the needs.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash and voucher programming is used as complementary to the in-kind food aid provision in ACTED’s programming. Cash and voucher modalities will be preferred where possible. This includes preference from beneficiaries and availability of items in local markets as well as capacity of the market to absorb such transfers as in areas directly affected by disaster, functioning markets are either not available or slow to recover from the shock with limited availability of goods due to hampered access or damaged infrastructure. ACTED will implement both conditional (cash for work) and unconditional (emergency needs for the most vulnerable households). The conditional modality, which is cash for work for market rehabilitation construction, will have a two-fold benefit; supporting the households financially while improving access to market. The unconditional modality will facilitate access to a wider range of items and empower the beneficiaries to choose the specific items that their household needs, in locations where the markets are functional.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

During sensitization of beneficiaries on CRM mechanism, ACTED informs beneficiaries about PSEA and provides clear instructions on how to report such cases utilizing CRM present at the site of implementation. ACTED explains how such sensitive cases are handled to encourage reporting.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

As per its standard operating procedures for CRM, throughout the project, ACTED will ensure that multiple channels of communication (e.g.: help desk, staff registering complaints, hotline etc,) are available for ACTED to receive feedback from communities including the most vulnerable members such as individuals living with disabilities and individuals who are not literate. Furthermore, ACTED will have at least one women staff member in help desks to encourage women to register their feedback to ACTED.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

During the implementation project activities, beneficiaries will be sensitized on the complaints and response mechanism in camps, and on how to submit concerns or complaints to ACTED. Moreover, help desks at project sites will be installed at central locations and equipped with visibility materials.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

ACTED’s independent AMEU will continuously register the complaints and feedback regularly of the target population and insert them into ACTED South Sudan’s learning database. The findings will be discussed at least on a monthly basis with the project implementation and project development teams to ensure that the feedback is incorporated into the ongoing project as well as for the design of upcoming projects.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

ACTED counts on a robust internal mechanism to combat and prevent child safeguarding and PSEA. All ACTED staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct which includes ACTED’s Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse Policy and Child Protection Policy. The staff will receive refresher trainings on these policies on a regular basis. ACTED will take the necessary measures to ensure that the communities receiving WASH items will have safe access to distribution sites by selecting an accessible location. Vulnerable and/or marginalized groups will be included in community consultations to ensure they are included in decision-making processes and receive the assistance provided by ACTED. ACTED will conduct inception meetings with external stakeholders including local authorities to ensure that the scope of the project and any selection criteria is widely understood.
