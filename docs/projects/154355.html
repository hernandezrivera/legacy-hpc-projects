Project Summary

The humanitarian crisis in Iraq is entering a new phase. Combat operations against the Islamic State of Iraq and Syria (ISIS) have ended and hundreds of thousands of displaced people are returning to their homes and communities. Yet the impact of conflict and displacement in Iraq on education has been stark. All affected children have witnessed violence and suffered disruption to their learning. Educational facilities have been destroyed and a shortage of teachers – who have suffered underpayment and lack of investment in their development – has meant that quality education has suffered. The Education Cluster estimates that a total of 2.6 million children will require assistance to access education in 2019. Through the proposed project, Save the Children (SC) will continue to provide Education assistance to the most vulnerable communities, targeting conflict-affected boys and girls and their families across Ninewa, Dohuk, Salah Al-Din, Diyala, and Kirkuk in 2019.

Needs

Children face challenges to access education in Ninewa, Diyala, Dohuk, Kirkuk and Salah Al Din governorates in particular, due to lack of space, unsafe environments, low quality of teaching, poor governmental policies and support, in addition to significant protection-based concerns and barriers. Due to the conflict and related displacements, many children have been out of school for 2-3 years, while the quality of teaching being offered is insufficient to adequately catch-up children who have lost critical years of schooling. Children not engaged in schools are more likely to marry early, engage in child labor or be recruited by armed groups. During Save the Children’s Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSN), conducted in January 2018, 22%-26% of adult respondents indicated their children did not attend school. Further assessments conducted by SC in March 2018 revealed that 50% of parents report that their children do not have access to safe, quality education. Lack of adequate sanitation facilities, and lack of knowledge and appropriate practices with respect to hygiene are making schools unsafe for children, as are outbreaks lice, leishmaniosis, and other diseases.SC’s MSNA revealed that households spent, between 6% and 15% of their household income on school expenses for children. With household monthly income comprising only $240 - $560 USD per month, any school expenses may be a substantial burden. Up to 26% of adult respondents also indicated that their children did not attend school. Inability to pay fees or buy school supplies, or lack or transportation options were the most common reasons for school drop-out followed closely by disability. Further, SC’s Annual School of Education Report (ASER) indicated poor literacy levels for school-aged girls and boys. Around 35% of children, aged 9-13, could not read four simple sentences from a second-grade textbook; more children were not able to identify letters and words. Corporal punishment remains endemic schools, and one of the priority concerns of children, as recorded in focus group discussions with both girls and boys. Boys have also reported fear of authorities (ie police, militia) stationed close to schools as a push factor from education; whilst caregivers have reported fear of violence on the way to school and at school as a factor keeping them from sending girls to school. SC’s MSNA confirmed that on average, the numbers of girls enrolled in school per household were lower than those of boys. Girls are at risk of exclusion, violence, and harassment, which can lead to increasing incidents of girls being pulled out of school and forced into early marriage in an effort to preserve familial honor. Mothers may have little say in decision making where male members of the family decide how resources should be used, especially support to girls’ education. This imbalance might result in girls having no voice to defend their right to education and development.

Activities

Save the Children will implement the following education activities across Ninewa, Kirkuk, Salah al-Din, Diyala, and Dohuk governorates: • Provision of nonformal education• Support the training of teachers and other education personnel in emergency education, life skills and PSS to improve quality• Support the training of teachers and other education personnel in pedagogy (literacy and numeracy) to improve quality• Capacity building for staff and partners on education in emergencies, education, gender equality & inclusion, and child rights • Establish and train Parent Teacher Associations (PTAs)• Distribution of teaching and learning materials • Rehabilitation of damaged learning spaces • Rehabilitation of gender safe WASH facilities in learning spaces• Support the registration and enrolment of children into formal schools and TLS • Support the provision of winter supplies• Support the provision of learning materials, textbooks, stationary and school bags• Support the provision of school furniture and equipment

Protection Risk Analysis

Access: Many are unable to access urgent humanitarian assistance, including child protection and mental health services and quality education, and have limited options for the full reinstatement of their rights and achievement of a durable solution to their displacement. In addition, children across Iraq continue to be denied their right to education as a result of the targeting, use, and destruction of schools during conflict. Protection issues will continue to be significant should social tensions arise, particularly in communities where local populations are perceived as having supported ISIS. There are specific concerns for the long-term futures of children recruited or used by parties to conflict, those in detention, and those born to ISIS members who are at risk of being abandoned. Hundreds of thousands of people, including tens of thousands of children, have been exposed to extreme violence, danger, uncertainty, and denial of human rights, resulting in stress that will require immediate and long-term support and care. Inclusion: For women and adolescent girls, the risks of gender-based violence (GBV), in particular sexual violence, is widespread. The abuse of women and girls, particularly from religious and ethnic minority communities, has included systematic and targeted rape, abductions, sexual slavery, mutilation, executions, torture and forced marriage. Girls in particular are at risk of exclusion, violence, and harassment, which can lead to increasing incidents of girls being pulled out of school and forced into early marriage in an effort to preserve familial honour. For girls and boys with disabilities, traditional ideas and a lack of resources about disabilities result in families hiding these children from public view and services, which could provide them with the opportunity to learn skills and have a meaningful life.

Mitigation Measures

Access: SC will advocate for durable solutions, freedom of movement, and access to services for all boys and girls their families in Iraq. Advocacy efforts will be a key part of any SC activities on the ground, both at national and governorate level, to ensure safe and dignified access to education, protection and mental health services, and fulfilment of basic needs. SC will provide safe and protective school environments, build the capacities of teachers and community members, and raise awareness among communities toward protection services to ensure children have improved well-being and opportunities for the future. Inclusion: SC believes it is critical to directly address gender discrimination and promote gender equality to ensure no harm comes to children, to support their development to reach their full potential, and to advance our vision of a world where every child attains their equal right to survival, protection, development and participation. Through a gender mainstreaming approach, SC will make a concerted effort to ensure that gender equality is promoted in all stage of the proposed activities. SC will be gender sensitive by ensuring balance in staff recruitment, conducting activities in gender safe spaces, training staff/partners/community on gender equality and inclusion, and conducting awareness raising sessions on the importance of education for girls and boys. SC has been engaging directly with girls, boys, women and men on identifying gender specific needs and considerations across all programming. This intervention aims at equally targeting boys and girls in all activities and where possible ensuring a 50/50 participation as relevant. Inclusion of children with disabilities into everyday activities involves practices and guidance designed to identify and remove barriers such as physical, communication and attitudinal that hamper children’s ability to have full participation in society, the same as people without disabilities. Through all activities, SC plans to identify children with disabilities, support them and their families with PSS when necessary, provide of assistive technologies (ATs) that can be used to help a person with a disability fully engage in life activities. ATs can help enhance functional independence and make daily living tasks easier through the use of aids that help a person travel, communicate with others, learn, work, and participate in social and activities.

How is your project accountable to affected population

The SC’s accountability system provides opportunity for the communities to receive information about the project and to give direct feedback or make complaints about the delivery of program interventions. SC regularly provide accessible information to affected populations on program activities based on preferred communication methods and information needs. We share this information in languages, formats and media that can be easily understood by children and communities we work with. SC’s information sharing strategy covers the following:• Information sharing about our organization, our core values, the staff behaviours and the way we work• Information about our approach, project activities/interventions and services that will be provided.• Information about selection criteria• Information about the feedback mechanism itself explaining the clear process on how it works, for what purpose; and how the community/children can provide their feedback/complaints using what tools etc Through the life of each project, additional sessions will be organized to update the communities on project progress, challenges and changes in activities. A multi-pronged feedback and complaints mechanism is introduced in all project locations and shared widely using posters, business cards and community gathering meetings. The following mechanisms are in place for all sectors: • Telephone toll-free hotline: A telephone hotline is established and the number has been widely disseminated to beneficiaries through different communication means in the targeted areas. WhatsApp messaging and Viber call have also been set up as alternative channel for receiving direct feedback• Suggestion boxes: Suggestion/complaint are established in the relevant locations where possible, to allow people/children to provide their complaint/feedback directly.• Direct visit and interaction: During the regular monitoring visits, SC MEAL team members organize FGDs, community meetings and one to one interaction with the beneficiaries, boys and girls and communities. Complaints received through SC systems are logged, investigated, addressed, and outcomes shared, aiming at immediate resolution and improvements. Complaints of serious nature (if any) are investigated and responded following a standard organizational protocol. Communities are regularly provided with feedback as to how their input was used. In order to make sure that the complaint and feedback mechanisms are accessible to all people, a group consultation (including girls and boys, women and men, elderly and children and adults with disabilities) are conducted through the project life, to understand if the established community feedback mechanisms can be easily accessed, useful and which information sharing and feedback channels the local community prefer and trust most. The ensure the safety of SC feedback mechanism, the places where community will be conducted and suggestion boxes will be placed, will be carefully assessed by SC with regards to its safety and accessibility. The consultation will be conducted during the day and close to the place where people live. SC will ensure that all complaints and feedback will be treated confidentially and the focal person will be trained in information protection procedures.
