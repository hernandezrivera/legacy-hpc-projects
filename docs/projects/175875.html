Project Summary

The project is aimed to support 1,810 internally displaced population (IDP’s) recently resettled at Ngalane, districts of Metuge. The intervention is intended to the increase and diversify food production; training in sustainable, resilient, and environmentally protective agriculture. It aims to empower women in decision makings that affect their lives and those of their families contributing to equal access to resources and benefits.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

CESAL has been working with some of the most vulnerable rural population in the areas of agriculture. Cyclone Kenneth, Covid19 and the ever expanding conflict in Cabo Delgado has destroyed agricultural sector, exarcebated by rural impoverishment and the effects of climate change, increasing the risk of food insecurity. The target population are 362 IDP’s families recently relocated to Ngalane site in Metuge. INGC along with FSC and CCCM have already mentioned the availability of 255ha and 415 plots have already been demarcated. Allocation of land will begin shortly. Camp residents need tools to clear land and seeds to start planting. CESAL is prepared to give agricultural kits to the beneficiaries using own funds (from other emergency project) and with the support of HRP fund and start the implementation of 3 agricultural demonstrative camps (6 to 8ha). These camps have already been established in villages in Mecufi and Metuge and have proved sucessful. The camps will have an short-cycle crop (beans and maize), horticulture and agroforestry. Solar irrigation and gravity distribution systems and training in new techniques related to transformation and conservation of products. These activities are to be replicated in their own machambas. Within this project economic empowerment of women are integrated to promote the leadership of women in decision-making groups. Beneficiaries: 362 families (indirect 1,810 people)

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

1. Distribution of agricultural inputs such as staple crop and vegetable seeds, fertilizer, pesticide and equipment; and related training.2. Provide farmers with equipment and training to support crop pest management, post-harvest handling, storage and processing3. Rehabilitation of agricultural related infrastructures including irrigation system, postharvest structures and markets.Activities will take place within the resettlment camp in Metuge where CESAL already works and has a good relationship with institutions. Furthermore we collaborate with clusters. The work will be via CESAL technicians that will select beneficiaries (vulnerability criteria to be defined) that commit to work in demonstrative camps (short cycle crops, horticulture, agroforesty and fruits, irrigation systems, wells, seeds and products storage) and then replicate in their own machambas. Previous projects were very sucessful. The following vegetables were produced (lettuce, onions, tomato, pepper and other) and improved families diets. Furthermore, some of the outputs were sold in local markets contributing to improve the livelihood of families.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

CESAL is, as mentioned, committed to SADI programming globally; headquarters and national staff will be available to provide training to staff working on the project to ensure commitments and principles are upheld. CESAL adheres as far as possible to global standards (CHS, Sphere, Humanitarian Charter and Minimum Standards), while also adhering to the “do no harm” principle. These approaches are also designed to guaranteeing equity for all involved, meaning that the action we are proposing will guarantee that also the most vulnerable (children, women and elderly) and affected communities will see their rights respected regardless their gender, race, religious beliefs, income and physical attributes. This will be facilitated by the fact that the staff is being trained on PSEA rules and guidelines in collaboration with the protection cluster. Complementary activities will be carried out in accordance with cluster guidelines assuring the participation of the beneficiaries and guaranteeing their rights to be heard and involved.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

The target community was involved in the identification of needs and risk management, including most vulnerable groups, reducing negative effects depending on the intervention. Beneficiaries will be sensitised on their rights and responsibilities and engaged in all stages of the project cycle decision making, beginning with mobilisation and sensitisation as well as selection of the right holders. This will be done through planning meetings at community level during which community management committees (CMCs) will be formed. The CMCs will comprise a wide spectrum of the beneficiaries including adequate representation from women, men and youth. The CMCs will be involved in all the stages of project implementation which will also include monitoring and evaluation. The beneficiaries will be given the opportunity to provide feedback on interventions success, compliance, failures and challenges. The project staff will interact with beneficiaries and handle complaints. Complaints and resolutions arising from the programme will be channelled through the community elected emergency committee. The information gathered during the monitoring process will be shared with the beneficiaries during meetings at the sites. Linha Verde hotline will be used and beneficiaries sensitized on how to use it, another method are the complaints boxes to be placed in selected locations.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

CMC’s will participate in decision making, ensure fair representation of vulnerable members and people with disabilities in their localities and follow the agreed upon selection criteria to identify direct beneficiaries, facilitate coordination with the community and resolve any conflicts related to the project at community level. The vulnerable groups largely contributed to the various assessments through provision of first-hand information that has shaped the content, priority needs and helped to identify the vulnerable most affected individuals and households within their localities. According to this, priority should be given to people with disabilities.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The project takes it account the impact it has on the environment as it relies on agroecological production pratices that guarantee sustainable and resilient livelihoods to the effects of climate change. By using solar irrigation and gravity distribution systems it is environment-friendly. It also promotes the use and consumption of locally available products. A baseline assessment has already been made and an endline report is to be done to assess the impacts on the environment. Participatory and regular meetings within the communities and beneficiaries are to be held to ensure the impacts and techniques on the environment continue to regarded.
