Project Summary

This project targets vulnerable refugee and host communities in three different States: North Darfur, Kassala and Gedaref with multi-sector activities. In North Darfur, the project is part of the South Sudanese refugee response in Al Lait locality and targets 300 individuals with livelihood and resilience support. As part of the Eastern Sudan refugee response (Eritrean and Ethiopian refugees), the project targets 128,546 individuals in El Fashaga locality in Gedaref State and and Wad El Helew locality in Kassala State. The individuals are targeted with multi-sector activities, including the following areas of intervention: (child) protection (700 beneficiaries), WASH (92,436 beneficiaries), Livelihood (930 beneficiaries), Energy/Environment (300 beneficiaries) and direct support through NFI distributions (7,000 beneficiaries). The project aims to to respond to the humanitarian needs of the target population as well as strengthening their resilience to become self-sustaining and enhance peaceful coexistence. Mass hygiene messaging though radio as well as recreational activities for youth groups (football tournaments, cultural events) will benefit both host communities and refugees alike.

1 - Needs

Child Protection: Family poverty and lack of livelihoods exposes refugee children to protection risks, including child labour, early marriage and out-of-school. Targeted programmes for adolescents and youth are needed, including life skills training, secondary education, vocational training and recreational activities. Community-based child protection mechanisms and capacity building or child protection actors and partners, including authorities, are important. Vulnerable individuals include: UASCs, teenage girls and adolescents and youth. Children need protection from child labour, exploitation and other forms of abuse. WASH: In camps, water supply systems and piping networks are older and in need of rehabilitation. Water shortages are common in camps due to broken systems and increased demand. Diesel generators are difficult to maintain given fuel shortages. Shagarab camps have poor drainage, and stagnant water issues require vector control support to mitigate vector- and water-borne illness. Poor road access to most camps makes waste removal difficult, which drives vector breeding and illness risk. Desludging is needed to mitigate vector breeding and improve overall sanitation situation in most camps.High demand from host communities, and water system relied on for agricultural activities and livestock for both refugees and host communities. Women and girls are in need of menstrual hygiene support in camps. Livelihoods: Estimated 46% of camp-based refugees are living in extreme poverty. The highest rate of poverty is in Kilo 26 camp (82%) and Abuda camp (75%), while the lowest is in Shagarab II and III (31%). Refugee movements outside the States where the camps are located is restricted, which makes access to livelihoods challenging. Shortage or fluctuation of rainfall restricts access to agricultural lands. High inflation rate and the broader economic situation also affect the livelihood opportunities. Vulnerable individuals include: single-headed female households and persons with disabilities. NFI/Shelter: New arrivals require shelter and NFI kit distribution; Majority of refugees are unable to purchase shelter materials on local markets, and refugees face issue with broken shelters and materials gaps for repair Energy: Refugees are heavily reliant on biomass energy in form of firewood, charcoal and plant residues. Firewood is scare and not available close to camps and access is becoming increasingly difficult for women and children. Very high prices of alternative sources of energy.

2 - Activities / Outputs

In the area of child protection 700 host community members and 700 refugees will benefit from refugee child protection sensitization initiatives to contribute to peaceful coexistence: Among this activity, youth recreational activities will specifically target 500 youth refugees and 500 youth from host communities with 4 football tournaments, 2 cultural fairs (including cultural dances, traditional music, poems, messaging on peaceful coexistence, joint food preparation). 200 host community and 200 local government will benefit from awareness raising sessions on children rights. Through the strengthening of community-based protection structures a total of 700 refugees will benefit. The rehabilitation of one child friendly space with the provision of child recreational items and facilities will be supported. In the area of WASH a blanket hygiene promotion and messaging campaign through cascading approach, incl. radio transmission will reach at least 109,000 (between 5 and 60+ years old) refugees in 5 camps. The distribution of Hygiene/menstrual kits will target 6,000 women during one year (2000 women (new arrivals); replenishment of kits for 4000 women). The WASH infrastructure will be supported through the construction/rehabilitation of 2,000 meters of water piping, 20 water water collection points; installation of 20 water storage tanks (capacity 1,000 L). An average of 3,500 refugees per camp will directly benefit from new/rehabilitated water infrastructure.In the area of livelihoods, 1,410 refugees will benefit from skill development activities and in-kind support such as training on food processing, fuel-efficient cook stove making; Village Saving and Loan training; provision of agricultural inputs such as seeds, tools and livestock. Women are a specific target group and 10 women groups (150 individuals) will be formed and receive basic managerial skills. From each group 3 champion women will be selected to receive further entrepreneurial training, mentoring and small start-up grants to set up their own micro-businesses. In the area of energy & environment 300 refugees will benefit from training on production/sale or distribution of fuel efficient cook-stoves. 2,000 individuals (refugees and host community members) will benefit from capacity building on environmental conservation. By the establishment of 3 tree nurseries for production, at least 6,000 tree seedlings will be produced and planted. This will be done in connection with the (re)activation of Environmental Clubs in schools in refugee camps and host communities. In the area of NFI/shelter a total of 7,000 refugees will benefit from NFI (e.g. kitchen sets, hygiene kits) and/or shelter material distribution/support.

B+: Medium environmental impact with mitigation

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Energy and Environment is a specific sub-sector in the project. In the camp setting, natural resources are limited and prone to depletion. The project aims to promote the use of fuel-efficient cooking stoves which minimize the energy needs. The establishment of tree nurseries and tree planting activities as well as the sensitization of the communities incl. children for environmental aspects will promote environmentally sustainable practices and minimize negative impacts from deforestation.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project follows a do no harm approach and promotes the safety and dignity of the target population. This is achieved through a participatory approach which puts emphasizes on participation and empowerment of the target populations, incl. the specific needs of vulnerable groups and how they are able to benefit from the project activities without discrimination. The projects has a special focus on child protection and seeks to sensitize refugee and host communities alike for child rights and refugee projection aspects. A feedback and response mechanism is in place and offers the target population to address any concerns via direct interaction with project staff during activities/monitoring visits, via complaint/feedback boxes or a hotline. The feedback is processed and a response issued within an appropriate amount of time. Whenever possible, feedback informs the review of project activities/approaches.
