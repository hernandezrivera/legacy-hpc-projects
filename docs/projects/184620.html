Project Summary

Based on recent report of displacements in Hiraan, Banaadir and Galmudug regions due to unending conflict, prolonged drought and flooding. HIWA is proposing response with mobile teams providing combined protection support services to both IDPs and host communities. HIWA is planning to provide emergency GBV and child protection services to vulnerable newly displaced women, men, boys and girls in Hiraan, Banaadir and Galmudug Regions.
To safeguard and ensure the most vulnerable are reached, HIWA will target 30,000 displaced women, men, girls, and boys from both IDPs and host community. it will also give priorities to people living with disabilities who are in need of urgent humanitarian assistance. This project will fill gaps in service provision where government and humanitarian support is currently insufficient. Provision of services in general and protection services, in particular, have been significantly challenged by the limited access. As a result, HIWA aims to support the current situation in Hiraan, Banaadir and Galmudug Regions while anticipating an influx of flood affected people due to the expected rain.
HIWA will implement activities in Hiraan, Banaadir and Galmudug regions. Methodologies and approaches will be validated and lead by HIWA Protection Coordinator and HIWA field monitoring team to ensure harmony in the conduction of assessments and evaluation of needs. HIWA is proposing the following interventions to expand lifesaving GBV and child protection services to vulnerable populations in Hiraan, Banaadir and Galmudug Regions. The complexity of the operating environment requires multifaceted approaches that are tailored to each location including:
1). Mobile GBV and Protection emergency response services in Hiraan, Banaadir and Galmudug Regions which will expand protection and GBV emergency response through teams based in Hiraan, Banaadir and Galmudug Regions.
2). Community outreach and engagement activities to disseminate key information on Child protection, including information on the availability of services, and promote positive social norms to improve the safety and well-being of boys and girls e. SGBV, harmful practice, family separation, recruitment/CAAFAG, and Children’s rights.
3). Increasing the capacity of community-based child protection mechanisms (CBCPM) to prevent and mitigate Child Protection risks through multi-layered, community based psychosocial support interventions to reduce vulnerabilities and risks to children by building a protective environment at family and community levels.
4). Provide specialized Child protection case management to at-risk boys and girls. The guiding strategy will be twofold: combining collection, analysis and dissemination of relevant information through community outreaches; and direct support of identified cases through comprehensive case management (including case management for boys and girls and psychosocial support and referral to other specialized care and ensuring appropriate follow up and care where necessary.
5) Promote the inclusion of marginalized, minority groups and people living with disabilities in leadership positions and encourage their active participation in the camp management and decision making. Children living with disability as well as parents and children from marginalized and minority groups will also be targeted and their responses and feedback be considered further. This would promote long term relationship and strengthen trust with the HIWA team.
6) Establishment of Hotline for collection of Community feedback and complaints to strengthen community response and easy service delivery. CFM will be established in every areas we operates.

1. - Projects activities

GBV Activities.Activity 1.1: GBV Case Management. Provision of GBV response support to boys, girls, and women targeting 1800 (1300 women and Girls) including the provision of GBV service package (essential medical services including CMR/PEP within 72 hours, psychosocial support to identified survivors).Activity 1.2: Psychosocial support through temporary women and girl’s friendly spaces providing psychosocial support, recreational activities, and non-formal education activities. Through counselors and community, health workers provide community-based psycho-social support targeting 1500 GBV survivors and women at risk.Activity 1.3: Provision of material support for the most vulnerable beneficiaries through the distribution of 500 solar lanterns as an emergency response targeting women at risk of GBV. Activity 1.4: Provision of material support for the most vulnerable beneficiaries through the distribution of 5500 dignity kits as an emergency response targeting women and girls at risk of GBV. Dignity kits consist of Somali dress (dirac), shawl (gabasaar), sanitary pads regular, women underwear, pieces of bar soap, packets of powder Soap, petticoats (gorgorat), head scarf (shaash). Due to the vulnerability of women and girls, especially those that experience rape, HIWA will prioritize meeting their menstrual needs as well as restoring their dignity after an incident of sexual violence. Activity 1.5: Awareness raising activities targeting community wide on prevention messages and awareness sessions on SGBV, harmful practice, family separation, recruitment/CAAFAG, and Children’s rights as well inform on available referral pathways. Target 15,000 (10,000 women and girls). Activity 1.6: In collaboration with the relevant GBV sub-cluster region leads on service mapping, update and strengthen local level referral pathway for improved coordination in service provision.Activity 1.7: Conduct GBV safety audits in different IDP settlements(20 IDP sites). Implement GBV safety audits,. HIWA Outreach Workers will coordinate to implement safety audits that examine aspects pertaining to protection and safeguarding to ensure the safety of men, women, boys, and girls, including people with disabilities. Safety audits will be implemented in locations where newly displaced IDPs settle or where they have not yet been implemented. PWD face greater risks in the camps and HIWA would promote and encourage their inclusion in the various camp committees and taking part in decision making in the IDP camps and host community. 2. Child ProtectionActivity 2.1: Provision of gender sensitive structured mental health and psychosocial support services for girls and boys, including adolescents, and children with disabilities.Activity 2.2: Care givers Psychosocial support, parenting messages, positive parenting and other kind support leading to improved relationships with the children under their care.Activity 2.3: Individual child protection case management services including identification, documentation tracing and reunification and other services that meets children’s unique needsActivity 2.4: Facilitation of nutritional support to foster care families for the child being placed under a foster family, this goes into reducing economic burden to the foster family for absorbing extra person to feed during the family tracing process.Activity 2.5: Community Based Child protection Groups will be trained, target, on how best to support the identification, documentation, tracing and reunification of unaccompanied and separated children including ensuring appropriate follow up and providing alternative care where necessary.Activity 2.6: Conduct safety audits, child safety walks and child led participatory mapping exercises in target location to identify and document urgent child protection risks and needs faced by girls and boys(including PLWD) , including CP related issues, as well as grave violations of human rights especially of children.
