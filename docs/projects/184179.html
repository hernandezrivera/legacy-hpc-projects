Project Summary

The protection needs of the IDPs and the vulnerable urban poor of the host community in Lower Shabelle, Middle Shabelle, and Galgadud is very critical. Lower Shabelle and Middle Shabelle are among regions that have the most acute protection needs with ‘emergency level’ needs at severity score 4 according to HNO. The recent armed conflict led to massive displacement of more than 18,500 households who fled from Guriel of the Galgadud region in search of safety to nearby villages (UNHCR FLASH ALERT # 15). There is relevant calm now in the area, but people remain outskirts of the town. Children's lives and their well-being is threatened by conflict, disease outbreaks, poverty, displacement, and unemployment, causing high levels of life-altering traumatic injuries and stress.
This project targets 8250 direct beneficiaries in CP1 and 4200 of duty bearers and community members in GP. It is expected to reach other thousands of community members as indirect beneficiaries.
This project has a strategy of focusing more on duty bearers and caregivers by training them physically while observing the COVID -19 SOPs on protection child rights. It has a prevention strategy of general awareness raising on issues of child protection and prevention of family separation besides promoting good hygiene practices. 4200 duty bearers and community members will receive protection training and awareness of child rights initiatives, whereby 1988 Community based foster care families will be established and trained on basic child rights, parental skills, and basic PSS. They will be trained on the identification of cases and protection issues within their communities and the referral system. The project aims to reach 4400 girls and boys, including adolescents, children with disabilities, affected by the conflict and other emergencies through the provision of 5 CFSs community-based recreational and basic psychosocial support activities through existing and newly formed community structures. 600 girls and boys, including adolescents, children with disabilities at risk will also receive individual case management services that meet their unique needs. The project will seek reunification of 1262 UASC with their primary caregivers or appropriate alternative care through IDTR process, and 600 children will be supported with emergence material assistance/reunified kit, including 40 children with disabilities.
Refreshing training for 24 caseworkers. The project will organize 12 awareness events, campaigns, and public drama sessions on child protection issues facilitated by children and adolescents (girls and boys) attending the CFS to advocate for children’s rights and the inclusion of the most vulnerable children.
The project will ensure the inclusion and adequate consideration of children's voices with community-based and local governance mechanisms.
New Ways has been working in the target regions for a long time and implemented different projects in different sectors. In 2020, NWO has completed the protection projects successfully in Galgadud and Middle Shabelle funded by the SHF, and a total of 10,300 beneﬁciaries were reached. NWO is currently implementing a protection project in Lower Shabelle supported by UNICEF. Our team on the ground shares monthly protection reports with the protection cluster and stakeholders. Protection measures are mainstreamed to all NWO projects in their operational areas including implementation of this intervention and will coordinate and with other agencies to develop a robust and functional referral system and solid case management processes.
In addition, the project adheres to the ‘do no harm' and humanitarian principles.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

4.1-NWO's experience in working with different communities will be the asset of overcoming this RISK. Our senior field officers are trained on PSEA, community mobilization, and communication skills. The project team will be trained on the humanitarian principles and 9 commitments before even they meet the project target beneficiaries. They have to learn and understand the traditional norms 'XEER' between clans. The team will ensure that elders, youth, and women from minority groups are onboard from the beginning. Non-discrimination and DO NO HARM principles are among values that NWO adapts. When entering a community the team members find out who is there, who has the power, who is the gatekeeper, then the team will start meeting with the community members including youth, women, and children. In the Somalia context, the clan is like an ID, before delivering services NWO conducts a quick assessment on community composition and analysis it. Once it is done then the strategy to approach will be next. Mainly the key strategy is dialogue and FGD with duty bearers and community members. Transparency and collective planning will follow, whereby New Ways clearly inform all members about the importance of inclusiveness and collectivity. 4.4- High insecurity currently exist in Galgadud region and parts of Middle and Lower Shabelle Regions may affect the target project beneficiaries to reach the service facilities. For mitigation purpose, the project team will liaise with the authorities and community elders for security concerns. They will be consulted the appropriate locations that are suitable to establish the child safe and friendly spaces. On the other hand, all other activities such the training and workshop events, case management tasks, community gatherings will take place mostly in our service delivery facilities or secure private venues in the target districts. NWO has implemented humanitarian and development projects in all the target districts and has a good experience of the accessibility and risks that exist in those areas. Usually, there will be daily security assessments and the information will be shared with all stakeholders. the project ensures that that the target beneficiaries, particularly children are served in accessible locations in a peaceful environment. New ways will ensure lack of documentation does not exclude individuals from accessing services - New ways will ensure the location, distance and access routes to the project facilities and services are accessible to all members of the community, including children and the most vulnerable groups. - New ways will introduce special arrangements for persons unable to access project facilities by providing transportation/taxi services, facilitating accompaniment arrangements (e.g. youth groups assisting the children, elderly, persons with disabilities; community networks).
