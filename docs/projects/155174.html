Project Summary

The overall goal of WHO, within the Communicating with Communities working group is to save lives by creating awareness and encouraging appropriate behavioural actions that reduce health risks. To achieve this, WHO will build on work already done in this sector, and engage with the communities and provide appropriate health information, both on chronic health issues and disaster-related needs, based on feedback on their perceptions and beliefs on health that shape their behaviour, of both the refugee and host communities including the more vulnerable groups such as women and adolescents, to help them adopt healthy behaviours. Health needs vary according to seasons, as different diseases peak with the change in weather conditions. Therefore a sustainable system of understanding people's evolving health needs and their perceptions and beliefs that may affect their actions towards health, and reaching out to them, especially women and adolescents, who are both a majority and among the most vulnerable, to create awareness on health risks and help them adopt pro-health behaviours, is of critical importance. Following the risk communication strategy, WHO will undertake studies to gather community perceptions and beliefs that affect the health and health seeking behavior of the community with due consideration to the gender, social cohesion, and accountability to affected population. WHO will develop appropriate risk communication materials and reference documents on priority areas. The needs of different population sub-groups and their feedback on interventions will be taken into consideration while formulating activities targeted at improving healthcare services.

Objectives

Needs

When almost a million Rohingyas arrived in Cox's Bazaar since August 2017, fleeing violence, they were in poor physical and mental state, having suffered terrible violence. A year on, various reviews have revealed that the Rohingya refugees' health needs remain great, but awareness about health and for improved health-seeking behavior is low in the communities. In the area of maternal, sexual and reproductive health, awareness among women on available services and their benefits are low. Not many women opt for institutional delivery. Routine immunization uptake is low, due to rumors around vaccination. Mental health is a major problem, yet few people recognize the symptoms or know how to get help or to overcome the stigma that can be associated with being mentally ill. Outbreaks of acute watery diarrhea and acute respiratory diseases continue, and while people know about hand hygiene, in reality it is not practiced, perhaps because people do not realize that it directly helps protect them from illness. Health needs also vary according to seasons, as different diseases peak with the change in weather conditions. Therefore a sustainable system of understanding people's evolving health needs and their perceptions and beliefs that may affect their actions towards health, and reaching out to them, especially women and adolescents, who are both a majority and among the most vulnerable, to create awareness on health risks and help them adopt pro-health behaviors, is of critical importance.

Activities or outputs

To achieve the objective, WHO will build on work already done in this sector, and engage with the communities and provide appropriate health information, both on chronic health issues and disaster-related needs, based on feedback on their perceptions and beliefs on health that shape their behavior, of both the refugee and host communities including the more vulnerable groups such as women and adolescents, to help them adopt healthy behaviors. The activities selected under this project is based both on the community information needs on health as revealed by various surveys conducted by CwC partners, and identified by the health sector evaluations. They are aligned to the CwC and health strategy as determined in the Joint Response Plan 2019 and the Risk Communications Task Force, of which WHO is co-chair. WHO plays a critical role in bringing together health, WASH, Nutrition sectors and CWC working group to address emerging health crisis.The activities include:• understanding, technically reviewing and documenting, through surveys and other mechanisms, the levels of awareness on a range of health issues, including NCDs, sexual and reproductive health, mental health, and community perceptions and beliefs that affect the health and health seeking behaviour of the community with due consideration to the gender and accountability of the affected population and social cohesion. • establishing a system for regular engagement with the communities, particularly women and vulnerable groups, to understand their health needs and perceptions, including rumors that prevent healthy behaviors, and provide them with information tailored to their needs, and support them to adopt behaviors that enhance their health. • training community health volunteers to reach out to vulnerable groups and convince and support them to adopt healthy behaviors.• identify seasonal and disaster-related health risks, develop evidence-based messages and creative material and ways to engage with the community to provide information to mitigate those risks.• WHO has a separate disaster risk reduction/contingency plan which includes risk communication to respond to crisis that may emerge after any emergency.
