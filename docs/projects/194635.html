Project Summary

World Vision appreciates that there has been on going clashes in Sudan that began with Khartoum state but spilled over to most of the states. For World Vision implementation areas, most affected is South Darfur, South Kordofan and East Darfur. Blue Nile has enjoyed relative peace in this conflict compare to the other states. The disruption of stability has generated a number of movements from less table areas to those with relative peace and hence IDPs being created. The creation of IDPs has created additional health challenges including limited health care structures, movement of infectious diseases, movement of behaviours and the advent of increased gender based violence in the households.
Due to the ongoing conflict, There is been limited communication and a continuous security assessment by World Vision. There has been observed insecurity and inaccessibility of most of the project areas. For example, South Darfur is inaccessible, and this coupled with the mushrooming of IDPs has generated the need to accelerate lifesaving interventions in other areas. For this emergency response, World Vision is prepositioning to venture into other states like Aljazeera and Sinar in localities that have more and more IDPs.
Currently World Vision plans to increase coverage through the promotion of access and utilisation of life saving health care services. Planning to follow the population where it is since this is where the greatest needs for survival are. The implementation of the mobile clinic has shown great reach. For sample our Mobile clinic in Damazine is far reaching in providing integrated Health, Nutrition, MHPSS and CMR services to the IDPs and vulnerable host communities.
Further still, the World Vision National office strategy is aligned to the Health strategy plan and is quickly aligning to the HRP 2023 updated. The Main focus is on alleviating vulnerability among the population and ensuring universal access to health services. Targeting Pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) and Children under the age of 5 (CU5) World Vision will work to support essential public health functions with focus on strong primary health care in 40 localities in over 70 primary health care units including generating mobile clinics to service the IDPS. This will focus the implementation of Sudan’s minimum health care package and working with Community health workers to promote resilience at the Household level.
In this regard, World Vision plans to participate in the strengthening of emergency preparedness, response, and all-hazards emergency risk management. The Plan is to deliberately embed mobile clinics, clinical management of rape and prevention of sexual exploitation during calamities. All states will have a preparedness plan that plugs into the World Vision Global emergency response, which will be used to complement the efforts from other donors.

1 - Needs

There has spike in the number of people in need (PiN) of humanitarian assistance increasing by a 57%. This has been brought by the destruction of an already weak health system due to the ongoing conflict. The increase in PiN has repel effect on the actual health care available for life saving. The current resource envelope sit aside to prioritize health care Is currently being looked at as insufficient. For example it is 25% more on a dollar required to care for a patient compare to when this HRP began in January 2023. This thus has an effect on the actual reach if additional resources are not set aside for life saving health care services and hence far reaching on morbidity and mortality. There has been an increase in the violation in human rights due to the conflict. For example the complex and interconnected protection issues in this protracted crisis are worsening in the current context of limited access to humanitarian services, rights, justice, and accountability. This has therefore put a strain on the issues that are sprouting including the gender based violence, rape of teenagers mostly of female orientation, neglecting of children, limited accesses to PSEA services and almost no referal system available for such cases. The raise in GBV and rape cases actually calls for an immediate need for services addressing the medical side of the outcomes, looking at the physiological and mental health worse outcomes due to violation of the rule of law. Protection and safeguarding for children and adults is key. Even though some states have got caught up in this conflict and war, this has led to an increase in the exodus from those deemed insecure to states that are enjoying relative peace. This has led to an increase in the number of IDPs and hence camps. Some individuals have moved to stay with their relatives in these safe states. This has put a strain on the needs for health care and therefore created a strategic shift from our traditional 4 states to venturing into other states like Aljazeera and Sinar in addition to Blue Nile. The conflict has further widened the sexual and reproductive health coverage gap for the worse. This has been due to limited supplies, infra structure and capacity; emergency obstetric and new-born care (EMONC) coverage. The situation has catastrophically impacted by the armed clashes, resulting in casualties including amongst pregnant women who are in need of pre and postnatal care in the midst of significant displacement.

2 - Activities/Outputs

World Vision plans to support the support the current crisis bearing in mind that it’s still an evolving emergency with almost little known in terms of the actual health care needs. World Vision is implementing an emergency crisis plan under the guidance of the security team. The plan is revised on a monthly basis looking at how the conflict is unfolding. The focus for Health is to promote access and utilisation of Health lifesaving services. Our current strategy is to establish as many as possible mobile clinics and teams that are offering integrated health, nutrition, MHPSS and CMR services at as many as possible IDPs and Vulnerable host communities in addition to the PHUs. World Vision will support 70 PHUs in the 40 localities upon a clear security analysis since protection and safety of the staff is key. Short of that, World Vision will establish Mobile clinics run by qualified teams to offer the services during day. The Sudan minimum health care package shall be implemented to promote access and utilisation of Health care services. Working in the communities through the structures like community health workers and community midwives. The plan includes ensuring that in areas with no access to PHUS, the mobile teams are initiated to offer the basic services including the expanded program on immunisation. World Vision plans to contribute to trauma care for the displaced and those injured. Working through the PHUs and the mobile teams to ensure immediate trauma care for the affected. World Vision will be implementing the MhGAP for mental health services at the facility and outreach level. This process above will be complemented by implementing the referal pathway to specialized care. Referal will be for all clients that have wounds that required more care. However, the referal pathway will include all patients in the PLW category and for CU5. World Vision will also focus on support for maternal newborn and child health, focus on community based ANC services, clean birthing processes and the expanded program of immunisation including the zero dose children. Implementing community interventions through the timed and targeted counselling, every woman will be reached with appropriate messages to ensure some actions and behaviours are taken to ensure a safe pregnancy and appropriate child care for the children in the under 5 years. World Vision contributes to ensuring that early disease dictation and support is offered. The EWAR at the state level is supported through weekly outbreak reporting strategy. Capacity building of their locality staff to submit to the state authorities. The community structures to identify any infectious disease cases and alert the PHUs to offer care to the identified cases. The EWAR will be inter connected to the HMIS through the DHIS2. The contest in Sudan calls for working towards community cohesiveness and resilience. Part of this is the safe guarding, child protection and prevention of any gender based violence at the household level. World Vision will focus on identification of child maltreatment, women and those affected by clashes. Clinical management of rape will also be included in the program. This will be supported by supporting the structures for referal for and mental health support of all the affected cases.
