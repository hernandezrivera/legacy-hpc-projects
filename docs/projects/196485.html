Project Summary

The project will aim at increasing the NWS communities’ access to health care and reduce their morbidity and mortality. The projecty will aim at providing medical material to 30 health facilities with drugs, disposables and devices and financial support to 13 health facilities with stipends and running costs. The health facilities include the only cardiac-surgery center in northern Syria G0703210 !
, 4 Mobile emergency units (Mobile unit IRW1, Mobile unit IRW2, Mobile unit IRW3, Mobile unit IRW4) 2 dialysis centers (B0204011, G0704017) as well as 4 PHC centers (G0703072 ! , G0704008 !, G0700001) and 1 blood bank ( G0700013!) and 1 physiotherapy center (G0700177 !).
This support will be coupled with protection outreach work and referral support. The beneficiaries will reach 600,000 individuals during 2023. Trainings to 150 professional health staff will be conducted in subjects such as GBV case management, information management and health facilities management. Furthermore, thalassemia items will be distributed to 6 thalassemia centers in NWS covering all of their needs for 2023.
Kidney failure drugs such as immunosuppressants to 89 kidney transplant patients in NWS covering their needs for 2023.

3 - Needs

As of May 2022, the total number of functional health facilities in NWS sits at 372 compared to 442 in the 4th quarter of 2021(representing an 18.8% decline), divided into primary health facilities and hospitals as follows: 170 PHCs, 63 Hospitals, 45 Mobile Clinics, 42 specialized care centres, and 52 other health facilities. The destruction of major health care facilities and drug factories throughout the conflict has also had dire consequences affecting the availability of health care and medication. This has created a major gap while also increasing the pressure and the workload on the remaining healthcare facilities and healthcare providers.people suffering from non-communicable and life-threatening chronic diseases such as diabetes, kidney failure, asthma, epilepsy, cancer, and cardiovascular illness are at an increased risk of dying or developing complications as access to life-saving medications and essential treatment is becoming increasingly inaccessible or unavailable due to poverty, scarce resources, lack of funding, and lack of specialized health facilities and professionals to treat said illnesses. The fragility of the health sector has appeared significantly in NW Syria as a result of the huge number of war injuries left by the war over the past years and the resulting disabilities, and the inability of available physical therapy and rehabilitation centers to meet the medical needs of this group.

4 - Implementation of activities

Health facilities will be supported through financial and in-kind methods. As of 2023, 14 health facilities are to be supported with both full financial supports covering running expenses as well as stipends of health staff. This will be done with in-kind drugs and disposables support, and running costs covering all of the needs of the health facilities. The support will be discussed with partner organizations through bi-monthly cluster meetings at relevant taskforces especially dialysis and thalassemia in which IR is a regular participant. Also, the support will be discussed with the heath directorates of Aleppo and Idleb in order to ensure no duplication is planned in the short to medium term. The delivery of the support will be both mobile for the Mobile Emergency Units and static for the remaining specialized health facilities. The specialized health facilities include the first and only free of charge cardiac surgery center in northern Syria in the town of Qah. This cardiology center will be supported with stipends running costs as well as all the needed drugs, disposables and equipment. Referral services will be supported in coordination with the Idleb Health directorate in order to ensure proper coordination and effectiveness.Trainings in GBV case management as well as protection and inclusion will be given to health facilities staff working at both supported health facilities and those not supported by IR.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Violence against health facilities: Preparing emergency plans in case of high-risk environment (increase of violence affecting the area of the facility) namely providing guidance on the staffing, equipment relocation and facility schedule. Communications established with the safety and security department of IR and the administrators of the facilities. This communication is to update immediately in case of any risk or emergency affecting the area of the health facility.Gender Based Violence cases during travel to health facilities: Under this project, UIYV will train the health workers on management of GBV cases which will increase the number of personnel ready to handle such cases at target health facilities. Also, UIYV will support referral system with designated staff and transportation to assist the GBV victims. This will increase access to specialized medical services for the GBV victims. Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse: All IR employees shall sign UIYV code of conduct prior to starting their activities, this code of conduct includes sections on proper behaviour and measures taken in case of disorderly conduct. Also, a whistleblowing policy inside IR office is clarified to all personnel ensuring that any complaints pertaining sexual exploitation or abuse is taken seriously and confidentially. The contact details for reporting such incidents will made available for UIYV partners and target communities.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Conflict settings place women and girls at acute risk of poor health outcomes and sexual and gender-based violence. Health services are often the first, and, on many occasions, the only point of contact for GBV/SGBV survivors seeking assistance. Complaints and feedback communication hotlines and boxes will be put in place at the project locations and IR Turkey follow up response to the complaints and feedback collected.SEA/safeguarding risks Field staff are in position of strength providing aid and having decision making powers, SEA is a possibility. In order to prevent such actions, PSEA will be integrated into all capacity building trainings in order to reach the biggest number of staff as possible and a PSEA course will be given to all the field team of IR. Furthermore, strong and accessible complaints mechanisms and regular field visits will help ensure any such case gets detected quickly and survivors assisted safely.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The existence of many checkpoints inside Syria, which may limit the access to the target area:IR will coordinate with local partners and local committees who will help in facilitating the movement of the items to the target areas. All required documentation will accompany all shipments of goods along with experienced staff who are familiar with the situation and the checkpoints.No access to certain target areaIslamic Relief can cancel the inaccessible target areas from the distribution plan. Islamic Relief will reallocate the goods to more secure and accessible areas. On certain occasions, delaying the distribution for a limited time until accessibility is again possible.Closure of the Turkish borderIR will coordinate with Turkish authorities and the Turkish Red Crescent to enter the goods through relief cross points and to have exceptional border passing permissions.Escalation of hostilities and targeting of the medical facilities supported.IR will support development of a contingency plan or evacuation plan for each health facility in case the health facility becomes unsafe.Cholera Outbreak in NWSSome of the supported health facilities will have to be devoted to treat scpecifically on cholera cases.
