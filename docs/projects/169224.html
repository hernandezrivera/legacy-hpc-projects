Project Summary

The school year will start in the next few months and children/youth have already missed out on most of the past school year due to COVID-19. With the infrastructure of schools, learning institutions and safe spaces being affected by the blast, there will be a need to ensure the necessary rehabilitation has been completed as soon as possible. The intervention planned by Right To Play (RTP) can therefore be divided into 2 main components.
Project 1: Access and rehabilitation of schools and safe learning spaces
RTP will rely on its experience in enhancing public spaces and educational spaces in order to ensure that education and recreational activities can continue in areas affected by the explosion. The rehabilitation will be based on the needs assessment by MEHE, and will be conducted in close coordination with all stakeholders (including UNESCO, UNRWA, UNICEF). Some spaces have already been identified and other can be added based on coordination efforts. The analysis of needs and design of the spaces will ensure inclusion at all levels and addressing accessibility barriers (nationalities excluded, different physical abilities, gender dynamics. Children and youth will be involved in the design and implementation, in line with urban planning guidance to engage communities.
Project 2: Access and readiness of children to schools
RTP will be engaged in the Back to School Campaign directly related to the blast, ensuring information sharing, community events, material support to enrollment and follow-up with lead agencies, MEHE and UNRWA. The project will aim to prepare children, youth and teachers in the areas affected to the school year (hard and soft components). The project will be implemented at the community and school levels to ensure outreach and enrollment support. It will include the following activities: play-based approaches and providing psychosocial support (PSS) to students and teachers, provision of equipment and material for digital learning/connectivity and support to teachers in ensuring physical-distancing and health measures in classrooms.
RTP can engage youth who have been previously trained on specific professions and who can support in the rehabilitation and back to school campaigns. The professions include: painting, carpentry, metal work, healthcare (applied to elderly care), food processing and gastronomy, coding & robotics, solar panel installation, social animation, organic or urban agriculture, logistics & warehouse management, electrical efficiency. These professions will be needed throughout the blast response, whereby coaches and youth can learn new skills while working on the rebuilding and recovery initiatives. This will provide also additional support and learning opportunities for youth who are at risk of drop-out.
Affected and at risk children and youth will be targeted, with equal participation of boys and girls, from different nationalities and backgrounds, ensuring special focus on engagement of children with disabilities. The project will be based on the Do No Harm and SPHERE frameworks with a gender transformative approach to empower girls and the most vulnerable youth in Beirut/Mount Lebanon.

1 - Needs

With more than fifty percent of the population in Lebanon now being considered within the poverty line, children and youth are greatly affected by the consequences of the socioeconomic deterioration or the direct and indirect repercussions of the Beirut blast. More than 48,000 children in the area have been affected by the school infrastructure damages but also the shock of the explosion. This is added to the on-going health risks in terms of covid-19 pandemic and subsequent school closures. Project 1: Access and Rehabilitation of schools and safe spacesRTP is basing its intervention on secondary data which had identified spaces in need of rehabilitation. The team has also started field visits to specific locations to start preparing for technical assessments and relevant procurement needs. In total, reviews have identified 67 private schools and 92 public schools which were affected. There would be a bigger number of educational spaces and public spaces also in need of various rehabilitation work. Project 2: Access and retention of children to schools With more families not affording to send their children to schools, practical solutions will be needed to ensure basic costs are covered (textbooks, transportation, distance learning and connectivity). Also, the psychological impact of the shock will still be heavy on children, youth and teachers who will need to vent and express their traumas so that learning can be effective. These stakeholders will need the PSS and mental support to adapt to the academic year. Vulnerable youth groups (13 to 20 years) are typically undermined by relief efforts as the focus usually goes to children education or adult livelihoods activities. These youth have however a lot of potentials and have shown, after the explosion, that they can be the active members of society which demonstrate hope and solidarity. They will directly be engaged to ensure they are also enrolled in learning institutions and have learning opportunities.

2 - Activities

Project 1: Access and Rehabilitation of schools and safe spaces- Start on-field assessment and cleaning activities to support local actors (involving volunteers and staff) - Resume coordination with MEHE, UNRWA, UNESCO, UNICEF, municipalities, to inquire on rehabilitation needs of schools and public spaces. - Ensure children and youth groups are formed and engaged from early planning and design activities.- Conduct rehabilitation-specific assessments to damaged schools and public spaces to produce Bills of Quantities.- Conduct assessments for accessibility to feed into an inclusive approach of PwSN and a child-centered renovation - Conduct feedback discussions with communities and caregivers to ensure a participatory approach in decision making prior to initiation of activities - Seek relevant approvals to initiate rehabilitation works - Initiation and completion of rehabilitation works of damaged schools and public spaces- Engage youth through vocational initiatives & professions such as painting, carpentry, social panels installation etc.- Provide the needed resources for the youth groups implementing professional initiatives in the communities so that this initiative is a hands-on learning experience in a profession and at the same time is enhancing access to schools- Monitor progress of rehabilitation works using participatory feedback mechanisms - Evaluate the effectiveness of rehabilitation through mixed methodologies and participatory initiatives Activities within this project 1 are expected to be completed by end of October the latest. Project 2: Access and retention of children to schoolsActivities within this program will be part of a longer term education program which will include retention and literacy/numeracy components. The main areas which would be covered in this appeal include:- Support to teachers and educational coaches in the communities for improvement of their psychosocial support, use of play methodologies for PSS and providing solutions for the blended learning methodologies. These activities can be implemented online (on an adapted network) or through small group sessions maintaining covid-19 safety precautions.- Back to school campaign design and implementation, along with the major actors in the blast response, to increase enrollment in affected areas. This includes material support to provide equipment, electricity, textbooks, as well as connectivity & distance learning solutions.- Establish a guidance unit to outreach and refer children and youth to suitable educational institutions- Monitoring and evaluation of the program, engaging children and youth in the processes Activities within this project 2 are expected to be completed by November 10th the latest.
