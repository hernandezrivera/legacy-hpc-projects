Project Summary

The project aims to increase conflict-affected people’s purchasing ability to cover their basic needs by providing unrestricted cash assistance. The project intervention meets complementary with other projects of Caritas Ukraine that provide assistance with in-kind aid, conditional grants and vouchers, livelihoods components as well as community activation activities. In frames of protracted crisis, assistance with MPC grants is targeted on the most vulnerable people with high gaps in coverage of basic needs, low purchasing power and ability to restore their livelihoods. The main factors that ensure effectiveness of the assistance are related to access of beneficiaries to banking system and markets as well as sufficiency of assistance to cover gaps in basic needs. The intervention is focused on conflict-affected people living in GCA – 43% of in the 0-5 km distance from the contact line and t he other within the 20 km zone.
Conflict affected older people are particularly at risk. Most rely solely on their pension to cover basic cost of living, and this is often insufficient to cover food, medicines and utilities. (2018 Baseline report, HelpAge International). Low income causes households to reduce their food expenditure as well as other expenditures to pay for utilities. (Food security & Socio-Economic Trend Analysis - Eastern Ukraine, March 2018). It is anticipated that more than 2.5 million people in the Donetsk and Luhansk oblast will have problems in allocating financial resources to purchase sufficient solid fuel for heating, pay the utilities, and purchase winter clothing, to prepare themselves and their families for the next winter.
Overall goal: Basic needs of 3,520 IDPs and residents of buffer zone are secured
Outcome: Beneficiaries have improved coverage of basic needs
Output: Household obtained monthly assistance amounting 970 UAH per individual covering 4 months (a total of 14,080 grants to be distributed during this 2-year project.)
aProject uses such feedback mechanisms from beneficiaries as on-line form, boxes for complaints and recommendations, hotlines in each centers. In addition, regular post distribution monitoring allows to improve the quality of activities
Gander age equity of beneficiaries and protection issues for access to the assistance is taken into account in design of distribution process as well as accountability mechanism and participation of local communities. To provide access to the most vulnerable people in need project uses several approaches, including:
- high-level access is provided by mobility of teams who assist people in remote areas, especially rural ones with bad infrastructure
- targeting of people who are exactly in high-scale humanitarian crises is provided by selection of the most vulnerable affected people with highest needs paying strong attention to the income level and coordination with other local aid providers
- protection is provided by home visits to households to evaluate critical needs and gender roles
- mitigation of tensions between those who obtained assistance and those who did not is provided by participation of local activists in settlements of distribution in process of registration, needs evaluation and distribution approach.
- sustainability of beneficiaries is provided by using possibilities to redirect beneficiaries to Caritas transitional and social projects as well as services provided by other NGOs and governmental agencies.
Caritas Ukraine as implementing partner of interventions of Caritas Germany funded by GFFO has experience of efficient implementation of humanitarian projects including assistance with MPC grants since 2015. Interventions in the first half of 2019 will be covered by the approved and currently running project (15.12.17-31.07.19). For the following 1,5 years a follow-up project is planned. The activities are implemented through 4 local centers located in Mariupol, Kramatorsk, Severodonetsk and Zaporizhya. Capacities include highly trained field staff and management, safety& security system and MEAL system that are integrated at the field level.
Assistance with MPC grants will be fulfilled by mobile teams that include medical workers, psychologist and social workers who responsible for relevant type of material assistance. Comparing to in-kind type of assistance that can cover immediate needs, MPC has advantage in relevance because people spent cash also on hygiene, water, clothes. According to PDMs conducted by Caritas in 2018 on MPC , 59% of cash was pent on food, 21% - medicine, 11% - hygiene, 5% - clothes, 2% - savings and 1% - other. Depending on group of vulnerability and type of settlement, relevance is different. For ex. families with many kids and lactating mothers spent more than 20% on hygiene and more than 65% on food but elder people spent only 4% on hygiene but 27% on medicine.

Needs

Food Security While some positive trends lately observed in GCA, 825,000 people (general population, including IDPs) are still facing different levels of food insecurity in Donbas and humanitarian needs remain high on both sides of the Socio-economic situation in eastern Ukraine has changed dramatically since the beginning of 2014. Overall in Donetsk and Luhansk GCA, food insecure population decreased from 410,000 people in 2017 to 267,000 in 2018. Among those, around 13,000 are still severely food insecure. The share of the front-line population with inadequate food consumption in GCA reduced to 16% in comparison to 21% in the winter period. However, situation has deteriorated when compared to 2017 pre-winter level of 13% and should be considered as a seasonal trend. It is estimated that 30% of female-headed households have poor and borderline levels of food consumption and therefore continue to be more vulnerable than male-headed households in GCA as well as 19% in NGCAThe amount of social payments including pensions have stayed relatively the same low during the 2014-2017 period. Even following the pension reform in October 2017 food insecurity has improved for some pensioners, however, latest data highlights that the increase in is not sufficient to cover rising prices. At the same time, the actual costs of the WFP monitored food basket in Ukraine increased significantly between June 2017 and June 2018 and reaching the highest historical level of UAH 996 in June 2018Winterization The population on both sides of the ‘contact line’ has a growing seasonal needs for items related to winterization. The cost of heating has risen dramatically, and there is a nation-wide effort to improve energy efficiency, households along the “contact line” rarely have resources to invest in insulating their homes.More than 2.5 million people in the Donetska and Luhanska oblasts will have challenges in allocating financial resources to purchase sufficient solid fuel for heating, pay the utilities, and purchase winter clothing, to prepare themselves and their families for the next winter.In the coming winter, more than 300,000 vulnerable families (3/4 of whom in NGCA) may be forced to limit even their expenditures for food if not assisted in the purchase of sufficient solid fuel for heating, in the payment of utilities, and in the purchase of warm clothing and other essential winter items.Physical healthThe conflict has isolated rural GCA health facilities from major health centers located in urban areas in NGCA due to increased distances, travel times to essential health services and impeded access to referral hospitals and pharmacies.The irregular supply of medicines, medical supplies and diagnostic equipment deprive those highly dependent upon the availability of life-saving care, such as insulin-dependent diabetics; those in need of hemodialysis and blood bank services; and mothers and infants in need of appropriate pre- and post-natal care. Obstetric and prenatal care, along with childbirth services are lacking and are difficult to access.While most health care services are considered to be free of charge, real out-of-pocket costs borne by the affected population have significantly increased (e.g., transport, diagnostics, and medications) deterring many and further diminishing their health and resilience.ProtectionWomen and girls face higher vulnerability due to crisis-induced risks such as increased exposure to violence and exploitation. This increase in gender-based violence further exacerbate overall needs. Gender- and sexual-based violence (including conflict-related) remain much underreported and are not adequately addressed through available outreach and health services.

Activities & outcomes

Result: At least 50% have improved coverage of basic needs by using multipurpose cash assistanceActivity: 1.760 crisis affected persons receive multipurpose cash grants amounting 970 UAH per month via debit cards for a duration of four months in 2019Geographical scale of activity: Dontetska and Luganska oblasts, 20 km along contact line, GCA.Methodological issues:- HH obtains 4 monthly cash payments amounting 970 UAH per month per member via bank card of the HH head.- selection will be provided by Caritas workers with Scoring System tool to identify level of vulnerability. After registration system allows to evaluate level of vulnerability by summarizing scores of criteria related to the registered household.- criteria of vulnerability are following: HH with pregnant/lactating mother, HH with many kids, Single-headed HH, HH with disabled members (1 and 2 group of disability), Elder people (60+), HH with laying and serious ill members, HH with destroyed home due to conflict, HH with members who lost job because of conflict, HH with low income. Each criterion has its score. Identification and revision of scores undertakes participation of responsible social workers and analyzing of PDMs and feedback of beneficiaries.- level of monthly income per household and other types of material assistance that were given before are strongly taken into account during selection process- home visits will be provided by mobile teams to evaluate living conditions and gender roles to ensure that assistance will be used for vulnerable members.- to ensure efficiency of assistance, coordination with UN agencies and other NGOs will be provided to avoid duplication.
