Project Summary

Somalia continues to face triple humanitarian threats; desert locust, floods and the COVID-19 Pandemic. Among the 5.2 million in need in Somalia this year, 3.5 million are women of reproductive age. Although some COVID-19 restrictions have been eased in the country, the food insecurity situation has already deteriorated due to the ongoing locust infestation. The triple crises have exacerbated pre-existing vulnerabilities of women and girls and affected livelihoods for the poor families and internally displaced persons in most parts of the country. Also, the number of women and girls facing abuse including sexual violence has significantly increased due to restrictions on movement, as well as economic and social stresses induced by the COVID-19 pandemic. According to GBV IMS report about 1599 GBV incidents from January to February 2020. Out of the 1599 Incidents, 72 percent accounted for intimate partner violence, sexual assault accounted for 12 percent and rape 13 percent, psychological emotional abuse at 9 percent, physical assault at 52 percent, forced marriage at 4 percent and denial of resources and opportunities at 9 percent. Sexual violence, particularly rape within the drought and conflict hot spots account for 15 percent. 75 percent of the GBV survivors are IDPs and 97 percent of these are female. Furthermore, the protection cluster has concerns that the protective environment for the IDPs and civilians affected by the drought and conflict remains weak and requires strengthening to respond.
The proposed project is designed to provide immediate lifesaving services and risk mitigation to GBV survivors and vulnerable women and girls in the IDPs and host communities affected by floods, droughts and conflicts in Daynile, Dharkenley, Kahda districts of Mogadishu, and Afgoye and Marka district of lower shabelle Region through the following interventions: - Provision of Clinical Care for Sexual Assault survivors (CCSAS), Case management (CM) Psycho social support (PSS), tailored material support including emergency support for safe spaces and dignity kits to survivors of GBV and extremely vulnerable women affected by the floods and conflicts. Addition, SOYDA will provide transport cost to GBV survivors to facilitate access to post exposure prophylaxis in different health facilities. This project will strengthen service mapping to facilitate appropriate referrals, coordination and linkages in service delivery to ensure quality comprehensive services to gender-based violence (GBV) survivors
SOYDA is one of the few organization actively providing GBV/CP services and case management in Lower Shebelle supported by SHF. 173 GBV cases were reported over the last 8 months (SOYDA GBVIMS, January 2020- August 2020); 32% were rape and 48% were physical/domestic violence. Over the last year SOYDA has been building capacity in case management and clinical management of rape survivors (CMR). Currently there are no safe shelters or material support for survivors to access services. It is necessary to improve the quality of care by 1) CMR technical support for health staff in the two operational hospitals, 2) reception of GBV cases and provision of comprehensive case management, 3) supply and management of post-rape drugs in coordination with UNFPA And MoH, 4) material assistance and emergency support at the women friendly spaces intended to improve the protective environment and respire their dignity.
Communities, authorities and humanitarian actors have limited knowledge awareness of GBV risks and consequences and have limited practical involvement for GBV prevention and response. Non-GBV humanitarian actors across all sectors, and the local government authorities have identified this gap in knowledge and practice. Although SOYDA have been working with GBV AoR to establish referral system and increase awareness of services in target areas, there is still a need to strengthen engagement of communities, service providers and authorities

Project Activities

1. Provision of GBV services for 500 GBV survivors including case management, referral to safe shelter, basic emotional support and health referral to the main health facilities in Daynile, Dharkenley, Kahda districts of Mogadishu, and Afgoye and Marka district of lower shabelle Region2. Provision of life saving material assistance 3000 female headed households (dignity kits, emergency/shelter kits and transport cost) to GBV survivors based on individual assessments by GBV case workers. The material assistance will also be allocated to provide emergency safe shelter by already identified foster families. Foster families will be on standby in case an emergency safe shelter is needed to secure the protection of women, girls and GBV survivors. It is envisaged that the foster families will host the survivor for a period of up to 3 months. Tailored NFI/Shelter items will be provided to the survivors’ host to assure hospitality in a dignified manner. Provision of life saving material assistance refers also to emergency food (rice, flour, sugar and oil) for GBV survivors (based on the IASC guidelines on MH and Psychosocial support in emergency settings Geneva 2007 – first layer of the intervention pyramid called basic services and security) 3. Capacity building on Clinical Management of Rape Survivors (CMR) for 40 health staff from 12 MCHs and outreach centers that is managed by SOYDA. Among the participants GBV focal points will be identified and supported to provide case management and referral at the well- functioning MCHs of in Daynile, Dharkenley, Kahda districts of Mogadishu, and Afgoye and Marka district of lower shabelle Region (GBV cluster service mapping will be baseline for the selection of MCHs). In addition, Under the proposed intervention and in close collaboration and coordination with UNFPA and MoH SOYDA will ensure the availability of complete post rape treatment kits (PRTKs) within the health facilities to increase the provision of compassionate, competent and confidently clinical cares services to GBV survivors 4. SOYDA will mobilize 60 PSS counselors to deliver one on one and group psychosocial support and counselling for traumatized women and girls in the IDP camps. The counselors together with community mobilizers will sensitize the community and provide basic psychological support to the GBV survivors, to early or forced marriage and vulnerable girls. 5. SOYDA will conducted awareness sessions for the floods affected communities to raise awareness about services available, Importance of timely of cases and COVID 19 effects and prevention. . Awarenesswill include:A. Information on how to prevent COVID-19, such as hand washing and physical distancing; 6. B. Information on how to recognize signs and symptoms of the disease and the importance ofreporting without fearing any repercussions;C. Information about modes of transmission and risks of infection, to effectively combat myths thatStigmatize children/GBV/PwSN survivors or children of survivors;D. Dissemination of COVID-19 specific health referral pathways and hotline numbers (Will be done in line with the approved guides.); E. Information and positive messaging to clients, parents and caregivers in distress as a result ofCOVID-19 due to quarantine, physical distancing and illness of dears ones, including messaging on how to communicate to children regarding COVID-19, to mitigate stress for the children. 7. Conduct 3 days training of 16 female Community Safety Mobilizers on GBV monitoring reporting and referral. The trained Community safety Mobilizers will lead in monitoring and reporting and will link with the existing one stop centers and safe houses as well as other available services for defective reporting

Mitigation Measures

For the risk of violence's during distribution, SOYDA will mitigate by ensuring that (1) information on service and vulnerability criteria are well explained to avoid conflict from those not eligible, (2) additionally those not meeting criteria if vulnerable or are in need can also be referred (connected to) relevant service providers. (3) SOYDA will also engage the community members in the supporting protection of the vulnerable as well as schedule distributions away from military and (4) where possible, respond to the individual needs to minimize the long movement or long distribution lines / ensure accessible also to people with mobility impairments and/or mobile services to reach those, who cannot access the facilities. SOYDA will mitigate the risk of exclusion of the marginalized by (1) actively reaching out to individuals with disability or (and even partners already working with them) to incorporate their views and provides relevant services (2) ensure the plan include disaggregate outcomes for boys, girls and those with disabilities hence proper representation of all interested group within the society including elderly and vulnerable women. (3) Building capacity if staff to understand and avoid multiple forms of discrimination on the basis of race, colour, sex, language, religion, political or other opinion, nationality, ethnic, indigenous or social origin, property, birth, age or other status. (4) Promote general public awareness on information to persons with disabilities about available support and assistive services, including as well as other forms of assistance, support services and facilities.For the risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms, SOYDA will (1) Create awareness on the existences and how to use the mechanism (inform and educate the public) (2) Ensure the public access feedback in timely manner or are informed on actions taken based on their complains / reports or compliments). (3) Ensure the tools for complains are child friendly and also easily accessible even with people with disabilities. (4) When complaints and require investigations SOYDA will ensure it does not put the reporting individual at any risk
