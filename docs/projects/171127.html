Project Summary

The impact of over 50 years of continued military occupation in the West Bank on the protection of Palestine refugees is wide-ranging. Palestine refugees face a range of serious protection threats, including: threat to life; liberty and security; destruction or damage to homes and other property; forced displacement and restrictions on freedom of movement and access to natural resources, markets and other livelihood opportunities.
This action is based on UNRWA's protection mandate for more than 860,000 Palestine refugees in the West Bank. Particular focus is given to the needs of Palestine refugees living in 19 refugee camps, where the Israeli Security Forces conduct repeated military operations. The action seeks to address the acute needs of numerous Palestine refugees exposed to conflict-related violence and the risk of displacement and/or forcible transfer.
The project has two key aims, (1) to enhance the protective environment for Palestine refugees exposed to protection threats through protection advocacy - through persuasion, mobilisation and denunciation, and based on monitoring and documentation of protection incidents, including alleged IHL and IHRL violations. This advocacy aims to promote both protective policies and practices as well as the accountability of duty bearers.
The second aim (2) is to respond to protection threats, linked in particular to the risk of displacement and/or forcible transfer, but also conflict related violence more generally - mitigating the consequences of these and reinforcing the resilience of affected persons. This is achieved through a combination of shelter support to refugees who have been displaced and/or who have had their homes damaged or demolished, and through the provision of targeted case management to these individuals. Shelter support is provided through cash transfers, case management services are provided to affected persons through dedicated protection social workers. Cash assistance addresses immediate needs linked protection threats, whilst case management services improves access to other UNRWA services and specialised partners, and reinforces the resilience of affected persons.
This action works directly towards to two of the strategic objectives under the HRP:
STRATEGIC OBJECTIVE 1: The rights of Palestinians living under occupation, including those living under the blockade and other restrictions, are protected, respected and promoted in accordance with International Humanitarian Law (IHL) and International Human Rights Law (IHRL), while duty-bearers are increasingly held to account.
STRATEGIC OBJECTIVE 2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in the West Bank including East Jerusalem is contextualized as a protracted protection crisis, in the context of over 53 years of military occupation. In the current context, UNRWA has identified five major protection concerns: 1) injury or death as a result of the use of force by Israeli Security Forces (ISF) during security operations; 2) forced displacement and risk of forcible transfer; 3) presence and expansion of illegal Israeli settlements; 4) restrictions on freedom of movement; and, 5) widespread exposure of children to violence and risk of detention by Israeli authorities. The excessive use of force and its impacts on Palestine refugees continues to be a major protection concern. Regular security operations conducted by the ISF in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, have continued in 2020, even in the face of the ongoing COVID-19 emergency. ISF operations often result in live ammunition injuries, excessive tear gas exposure, property damage and exacerbate severe societal stress. Palestine refugee camps are hot-spots for confrontations and violence. Through its monitoring and documentation, UNRWA identifies multiple concerns regarding the excessive use of force. This precipitates severe psychological stress, physical injury and also fatalities. Such injuries can often lead to long-term physical disability. In the first nine months of 2020, 1,430 Palestinians were injured and 20 were killed, all of these fatalities were attributable to the ISF. Moreover, the first nine months of 2020 show record levels of settler violence, even during relatively calm periods during COVID-19 lockdowns in the spring of 2020. The settler violence incidents aimed at Palestinians include use of live rounds and physical assault, harassment and intimidation, and burning and uprooting of trees - settlers have uprooted or burned an estimated 7,760 productive fruit trees belonging to Palestinians during the first nine months of 2020. In 2020, displacements have increased in the first nine months compared with the same period in 2019, which itself had an alarmingly high level of displacements, with a 15 per cent increase in overall displacements. Palestine refugees continue to be disproportionately affected, accounting for around 35 per cent of those displaced this year, while they only represent 25 per cent of the total population. In the first nine months of 2020, 245 Palestine refugees were displaced in the West Bank; of these, 112 were female, including 46 girls under the age of 18. Children accounted for 44 per cent of all refugees displaced. Demolitions remain a major trigger of forced displacement and dispossession. In 2020, UNRWA recorded reports of a 14 per cent increase in demolitions, with demolitions of refugee-owned structures increasing by 27 per cent. A total of 159 Palestine refugee structures were demolished by Israeli authorities in the first nine months of 2020, 157 of them due to a lack of Israeli building permits and two punitive demolitions, which are specifically prohibited under international humanitarian law. With respect to both conflict related violence and displacement, children are particularly affected by these traumatizing practices and the psychological impact can be severe on their development. Schools across the West Bank continue to be adversely impacted by military incursions and settler violence. Protracted periods of profound stress have inter-alia undermined Palestinian communities' pro-social coping strategies and resiliency - having a particularly detrimental impact on the protection of children, gender equality and gender based violence (GBV), the protection of persons with disability and the elderly. Elderly household members and people with disabilities are also particularly vulnerable following military operations, settler violence and displacement given their reliance on external support to meet day-to-day needs. Furthermore, studies suggest that sexual and gender-based violence is increasingly used within Palestine, as a negative coping strategy to address feelings of powerlessness and hopelessness, prevalent as result of the occupation and humanitarian crisis. Research published in 2010 and 2017 established that men who reported experiencing occupation related violence were more likely to perpetrate intimate partner violence and sexual harassment. The 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview indicated an increase in the caseload of GBV among women and girls, and there are indications that the impact of COVID19 has led to a further deterioration. Additionally, as socio-cultural norms support and reinforce the notion that the home is the domain of female household members, displacement and property damage also has a particularly detrimental impact on women and girls. Refugee communities throughout the West Bank are subject to a coercive environment in which they face a multitude of threats. In 2019 UNRWA undertook community needs assessment - the assessment involved in-depth field work and direct engagement with vulnerable groups in selected communities, including persons with disabilities, children and the elderly, with specific efforts also made to ensure gender and age balance in the field assessments. As a result of this assessment, key protection issues facing 16 target communities were identified and ranked, culminating in a final consolidated list of focus issues that serve as a basis for prioritization for protection advocacy work under this action. The top priority issues are located in some of the most vulnerable communities in the West Bank, incl. East Jerusalem; the Bedouin communities of Abu Nuwar and Khan al Ahmar, as well as Hebron H2, Azzun, Silwad and Barta Sharqiya. These communities will be targeted under this action, in addition to the refugee camp locations identified to be most at risk of exposure to the excessive use of force; Jalazone, Arroub, Shufat, Aida and Daheishe Refugee Camps.

2 - Activities

Outcome 1: Enhanced accountability and improved practices of duty-bearers vis-à-vis their obligations under International Human Rights Law and International Humanitarian Law to Palestine refugees in the West Bank.Activity 1.1: Incidents of IHL and IHRL violations in targeted communities concerning excessive use of force, violations against children and those linked to the risk of forcible transfer are systematically and thoroughly documented. UNRWA will prioritize the documentation of rights violations that concern particularly vulnerable individuals such as people with disabilities and elderly persons, thereby assisting these individuals to claim their rights.Activity 1.2: Where consent to do so is secured and when necessary, documented IHL and IHRL violations are addressed on a systematic basis with relevant authorities during meetings or through protest letters /letters of concern. Different platforms of engagement are used in this process; from real-time interventions through telephone contact, to scheduled meetings and official letters.Activity 1.3: Donors and other international stakeholders are sensitized to the impact of IHL & IHRL violations on Palestine refugees when relevant and possible (through briefings, field trips to affected areas, presentations at relevant forums abroad, etc.). The importance of ensuring that a diversity of Palestine refugee voices and perspectives are represented throughout briefings, including but not limited to: people with disabilities, the elderly, youth and women, will be integrated in the process.Activity 1.4: Mobilization activities through International Human Rights mechanisms, briefings to diplomats and lobby organizations, field visits for politicians and diplomats to emblematic communities, background briefings for journalists, and briefings for relevant forums abroad. Particular attention will be given to the gendered nature of the protection threats, also of assessing the particular needs of vulnerable at-risk groups. Specific briefings for donors will be developed to look at the gendered impact of occupation/rights violations on Palestine refugees in the West Bank.Activity 1.5: Public advocacy initiatives on protection issues affecting Palestine refugees in the West Bank. The gendered impact of military operations will be highlighted throughout these activities.Activity 1.6: Action to ensure that all UNWRA programs in the delivery of their services respect and uphold the humanitarian principles, and also to mainstream protection principles into overall service delivery. Outcome 2: Refugee households impacted by protection threats are better able to cope. Activity 2.1: Refugee households affected by demolitions/evictions & damages following military operations/settler violence are identified and assessed by UNRWA. The assessment takes into consideration particular protection-related vulnerabilities, such as those of elderly-, child- or female-headed households, or those with large numbers of dependents, including households with persons with disabilities, and prioritises assistance accordingly.Activity 2.2: Following assessment, services (case management, emergency cash assistance and individual service referrals) are provided to affected Palestine refugees in line with identified needs. Referrals are made for a range of services tailored to beneficiary needs within the sectors of MHPSS, shelter, disability, child protection, legal services, WASH, food security, social welfare, education, health, human rights advocacy and GBV services.Activity 2.3: Monitoring of cash assistance and case management services.Activity 2.4: Community wide prevention and response initiatives. In terms of the role of the beneficiaries in the design, implementation and evaluation of this project this can be summarized as follows: At the design phase for Outcome 1, beneficiaries have been directly involved in the design of the activities, including through participation in committees, focus group discussions and surveys to develop dedicated advocacy strategies at the community level, UNRWA ensures that the inputs of vulnerable individuals are appropriately represented in this process, as described below. These strategies are the basis of action taken under this project. During the implementation routine engagement with community stakeholders is carried out to ensure the appropriateness, impact and relevance of activities. The Community Assessment undertaken in 2019, which was conducted with comprehensive community engagement, identified a prioritized list of the most vulnerable communities in the West Bank, incl. East Jerusalem, and guides the department's focus and prioritisation. The assessment involved direct engagement with beneficiaries, including persons with disabilities, children and the elderly, with efforts to ensure gender and age balance in the field assessments. It is updated on a semi-annual basis in order to enable a dynamic shift of the department's response to changes on the ground. At the design phase for Outcome 2, beneficiaries were involved at the community level in terms of identifying the drivers of vulnerability and risk for those affected by protection incidents that are responded to, this included direct involvement in the piloting phase of eligibility tools. In the implementation phase beneficiaries are directly involved as they are the drivers of their individual case plans with are developed in coordination with the social workers, furthermore they have opportunities to raise concerns and complaints through a dedicated free-to-call hotline, these are complemented by direct efforts to obtain feedback from persons with barriers to access the hotline (e.g. the elderly, persons with disabilities and those without access to a phone). In addition beneficiary inputs are provided in terms of their assessment of whether the assistance provided met their needs, and about additional needs that were outstanding.

Formal internal reviews will be conducted on a quarterly basis, allowing UNRWA to adapt its approach to enhance its impact. As part of this process UNRWA actively seeks input from all of the key stakeholders, including affected populations, diplomats and donors, and duty bearers to assess the effectiveness of its work. With respect to Outcome 2, UNRWA has an established monitoring system in place. Following the opening of a case, UNRWA conducts routine monitoring visits to households. In the case of households affected by demolitions will be carried out up to one month after the incident, while households affected by damages to their private property due to military and military operations will be carried out up to three months after the incident. At the closure of a case UNRWA conducts a closure survey with each household to assess the effectiveness and impact of the action. UNRWA has also established a dedicated free complaints hotline to enable households to voice their concerns and seek redress, this is accompanied by a formal process to review contested cases. All these elements feed into routine quarterly assessments that are drawn up to track the effectiveness of impact, and provide opportunities for adaptation of approach. A final evaluation will be done at the end of the project cycle, as a basis for future programming. UNRWA is planning to introduce a field-wide complaints and feedback mechanism for all its services, which is due to be piloted and potentially expanded throughout the field in 2021. One interface for this mechanism is due to be a mobile application which is downloadable onto devices and can be used to flag individual concerns, launch complaints and submit feedback to UNRWA. The mobile application, a prototype of which has already been developed, has specific features to facilitate access for persons with disability, as well as other vulnerable groups. Parallel work is being done to identify appropriate mechanisms for feedback and complaints in order facilitate access for vulnerable groups, including child-friendly mechanisms, mechanisms for persons with disabilities, the elderly and persons without access to devices compatible with mobile applications.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

In UNRWA's Protection Strategy (2012), the prioritisation of safety and dignity is explicitly address as a key protection standard where UNRWA programmes deliver services in a manner that ensures the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and staff. UNRWA ensures that its actions do not cause unintended harm or result in reduced protection of beneficiaries, communities or staff. On a biannual basis all of UNRWA's activities are subject to a protection mainstreaming audit, which assesses the integration of protection principles in UNRWA's work. This audit included extensive interviews and focus group discussions with beneficiaries, and sought to assess UNRWA's application of the four main protection principles, including the principle of Do No Harm. The most recent protection audit was completed in 2019, and a number of recommendations were developed, which are now being followed up with the support of a dedicated Protection Officer, funded under this action. UNRWA has a well established process during its monitoring and documentation procedures of obtaining informed consent (and assent in the case of minors) for recording and use ofinformation that affects them. This consent process was recently enhanced with the introduction of additional guidance to field workers and training. In 2019 UNRWA developed a new information management system for the collection of sensitive protection monitoring and documentation data; specifically the system now separates out biographical data from incident related data, with individual cases being only referred to through generated case code. In 2019/8 UNRWA developed a dedicated feedback hotline for the use of all beneficiaries under this project, this hotline is available free of charge to beneficiaries both in East Jerusalem and elsewhere in the West Bank For the purposes of ensuring that briefings involving affected persons, are carried out in a way that prioritises the safety and dignity of these persons, UNRWA has developed an internal guideline for ethical practices in the context of briefings, field visits, communications and others. This includes a checklist to guide all staff working in this capacity. UNRWA has developed, in coordination with the West Bank Protection Consortium, a dedicated vulnerability assessment tool in order to prioritise assistance to the most vulnerable refugees. This tool was pioneered by UNRWA and has since been adopted by other members of the Consortium and is to be applied throughout the West Bank. Under Outcome 2 of this project, within the CaseManagement response UNRWA ensures that vulnerable individuals can access assistance by external referrals, UNRWA has developed dedicated case management SOPs and internal/external referral pathways, to ensure that the comprehensive needs are met. In the planning of its advocacy approach and strategy UNRWA engages with a range of community representatives, including protection committees, village councils, camp resident committees – as well as holding numerous focus group discussions including for women, children, youth and the elderly in communities that we are working with. In the context of the COVID19 response, additional steps taken to ensure UNRWA's activities prioritise the safety & dignity. UNRWA has adapted its case management in response to difficulty of physical access to concerned individuals/households. UNRWA has established a dedicated psychological consultation hotline available to all refugees. UNRWA has existing guidelines for disability inclusion, gender equality, PSEA and child protection which are all fully applied in this project. Finally, UNRWA developed guidelines for remote documentation and monitoring, including a do-no-harm assessment, and has also established guidelines to prioritise field work in the context of COVID19 risks.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNRWA as a service provider is dedicated to the application of the humanitarian principles in all of its service delivery, and takes it responsibility in this regard very seriously. Specific measures are taken to ensure that UNRWA's services are neutral and impartial in nature, including through regular monitoring throughout its operations. For the purposes of this project, UNRWA has developed, in coordination with the West Bank Protection Consortium, a dedicated vulnerability assessment tool in order to prioritise assistance to the most vulnerable refugees. This tool was pioneered by UNRWA and has since been adopted by other members of the Consortium and is to be applied throughout the West Bank. UNRWA takes measures to ensure that its assistance under this project is available to all persons, and takes dedicated action to ensure that individuals with specific needs are reached. UNRWA takes actions to ensure that persons with disability, with movement and access restrictions, the elderly, women and children are targeted during outreach and communication - and also are key stakeholders in all community interactions and in accountability processes. Services delivered are tailored to the needs of individuals, including for those with specific needs, the case management services delivered are developed in coordination with the affected persons and are guided by their specific needs. Specific needs according to age, gender and disability are identified at the initial assessment stage of case management, for those affected by damages to residential property, and those exposed to displacement. The individual case plans are tailored to respond to these needs, for example provision of services linked to mobility issues amongst the elderly. In terms of the work in advocacy UNRWA ensures that in its documentation and monitoring dynamics including gender, age and disability are factored into needs analysis, trends analysis and UNRWA conducts a number of thematic briefings per year to focus on the specific needs of vulnerable groups. UNRWA has developed dedicated field briefings on disability, gender and GBV, children and the elderly for this purpose. Further, UNRWA has implemented alternative transfer modalities, using electronic wire transfer to beneficiaries’ bank accounts and cash transfers or checks for those who don't have bank accounts in response to access restrictions due to the COVID-19 emergency. UNRWA has also developed a remote documentation procedure and guidance, conducting documentation/ verification of incidents remotely, including over the telephone, using additional sources and community contacts. This approach is coordinated with OHCHR and other actors engaged in documentation and monitoring. UNRWA expanded its engagement with 22 vulnerable at-risk refugee communities ensuring continuous community monitoring and coordination within UNRWA of needs, concerns and proposed responses through telephone contact with key community stakeholders. UNRWA has demonstrated an ability to readjust its operations to effectively face the operational and movement restriction-related challenges arising from the COVID-19 emergency. For example, new guidelines for staff on remote investigations of serious human rights violation have been created. The UNRWA Protection Department has also taken the lead in enhancing the accountability to affected persons (AAP) in UNRWA's service delivery in the context of COVID-19, establishing online platforms for information sharing and feedback, hotlines, and conducted dedicated outreach. Finally, the COVID-19 emergency has increased demands on UNRWA and its services and presented challenges to establishing appropriate communication with communities, as well as to accountability mechanisms in the context of COVID19 restrictions. The Protection Department has led the establishment of dedicated AAP lens to the COVID19 response, including drafting key communications to the refugee community.
