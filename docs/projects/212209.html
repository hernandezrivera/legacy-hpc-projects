Project Summary

Muzun for Humanitarian & Development (MHD) has prepared this proposal to save lives, support and restore water, sanitation, and solid waste management and reduce protection risks among the displaced population and host communities by providing essential WASH supplies and services, and enhancing hygienic practices.
Support and restore water, sanitation and solid waste management systems to ensure continuity of services for the most vulnerable affected people in Syria
Muzun for Humanitarian and Development presents this proposal to assist 41716 beneficiaries (8075HHs), including women (9803), men (9386), boys (11472), girls (11054), and 47467 households in Jandairis, A'zaz, and Maaret Tamsrin sub-districts.
Muzun's main goal in this project is to focus primarily on WASH, aiming to enhance access to safe water, sanitation, public health, environmental protection, and emergency solid-waste management response.
1: Water pumping through the existing water network, using generator and solar system technologies, and water tanks for five months, and providing water trucking. (Water pumping, m3–35L/per day for drinking, cooking, and hygiene).
24710 people have improved access to water through the operation and maintenance support to the water systems.
The total number of beneficiaries is 24,710. The average monthly water pumping is 25,945.5 cubic meters.
2- Ensuring proper water quality through chlorination and quality monitoring. Muzun will provide safe drinking water to five sites. This will be achieved by chlorinating the water. Chlorine pumps are already connected to the water stations in the targeted areas.
95% of households whose drinking water supplies have a free residual chlorine (FRC) > 0.2 mg/L.
3: Regular solid waste management to improve access to solid waste management services for 41710 people.
4: Regular maintenance of WASH facilities, including rehabilitation of sewage and drainage, so that 24,710 people can access improved sanitation.
5: Conduct hygiene promotion campaign in the targeted Location to reach out 41716 people
Additionally, Muzun will prioritize the needs of persons with disabilities (PwDs), women, and older adults by involving them in the project's implementation. This approach aims to improve their access to life-saving services, especially in areas like latrines and garbage bins. Ultimately, Muzun's efforts will uphold humanitarian and protection principles among the beneficiaries in the targeted IDP sites, with a specific emphasis on the "do no harm" principle. Muzun will also conduct hygiene promotion sessions to educate the community on proper hygiene practices, such as handwashing and safe water storage. These sessions will be interactive and tailored to the specific needs of different groups within the community.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Additionally, Muzun's Humanitarian and Development field team will identify shelters with people with disabilities and special needs and adjust the water taps to be accessible and acceptable for PwDs by implementing necessary maintenance.Muzun for Humanitarian and Development, through the field team (female and male), will reach out to all targeted beneficiaries to conduct awareness sessions and community engagement during the project duration. This will include distributing brochures with useful information related to PSEA and accountability for affected population services. The awareness sessions will focus on PSEA, accountability to affected populations, service mapping, and feedback and complaint mechanisms. Furthermore, Muzun will establish feedback and complaint mechanisms in the targeted IDP sites by installing complaint boxes and posters in the camps, as well as activating a hotline for SEA complaints.Finally, the project aims to address the issue of limited access to safe water. By pumping water through existing networks and ensuring quality through chlorination, the project will enhance access to safe drinking water, thereby reducing the risk of waterborne diseases. Regular maintenance of WASH facilities and the construction of latrines will improve sanitation, reducing the risk of fecal-oral diseases and enhancing overall public health. Implementing regular solid waste management activities will reduce environmental pollution and health hazards associated with improper waste disposal.By prioritizing persons with disabilities, women, and older adults, the project will ensure that these groups have equal access to WASH services, thereby reducing inequalities and improving overall well-being. Hygiene promotion sessions will educate the community on proper hygiene practices, reduce the spread of diseases, and promote healthier living conditions. Addressing solid waste management and promoting hygiene practices will contribute to environmental protection and the preservation of natural resources.
