Project Summary

CAFAD FSL HRP 2023 plans to reach 80,000 beneficiaries segregated by age& gender based on FAO standard criteria. The appeal aims to reduce the number of people in IPC3, 4 and 5 in the selected counties (Uror, Nyirol, Canal/Pigi, Fangak, Leer and Kapoeta East & North) through emergency food assistance, complimentary livelihood and livestock support. These together with efforts to increase resilience and recovery of the disaster-affected population are to create strong communities that can withstand shocks and reduce over-reliance on relief. The proposed activities are linked to 2024-2025 FSL HRP Strategic objectives 1, 2 and 3.
In alignment with FSL CO1, CAFAD plans to support 30,000 men, women and youths with ACL, Cash and Voucher assistance in the selected counties targeting 60% women of the total beneficiaries and roles mirroring those on men in leadership positions.
CAFAD plans to support 30,000 HH in the target counties with main season crop, vegetable and fishing equipment aligned to FSL CO2. Also, 300,000 cattle, shoats, dogs, chicken and donkeys will be vaccinated against CPPP, CCPP, rabies, RVF and other endemic conditions in the selected counties and 15% of the vaccination target will be targeted with treatment against prevalent disease conditions in the counties including worm infestation, diarrhea, pneumonia, trypanosomiasis and ectoparasites. In addition, 150 CAHWs (25 in each county) will undergo refresher training on vaccination, treatment and disease surveillance in livestock.
In order to reduce the over-reliance on food aid, CAFAD also proposes to support 3000 persons including women, men and youth with training/ capacity building activities marrying with FSL HRP CO3. The selected beneficiaries in each county will undergo training on farm production, income generation, nutrition sensitive food security, DRR, climate smart agriculture and reduction of post-harvest losses.
Women needs, rights, prevention of GBV and their voices will be taken into consideration in all the interventions and ensure integration, inclusion of youths in peace building. Accountability to the Affected Persons (AAP) will be taken into account by formation/training of gender-inclusive committees with complaint and feedback mechanisms including regular monitoring/review meetings in all interventions.

1. Needs

The latest IPC data shows that 5.83 million people (46 percent of the population) are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity classified as IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse), with 1.64 million people in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) in South Sudan. Between July and September 2023, 31 of the 46 counties in IPC AMN Phase 4 (Critical) including Fangak, Uror, Nyirol and Canal/Pigi in Jonglei State targeted under this response. CFSAM report indicated that with a projected population of about 12.9 million in mid-2023, the overall cereal deficit in South Sudan in the January-December 2023 marketing year is estimated at about 485,400 tones, 10.2 percent below the deficit estimated for 2022, and two percent below the 2018-2022 average.Rainfall started late in Jonglei, below average and lower than that of the previous year resulting in stressed crops and replanting by farmers. (UNFAO, CLiMIS August 2023). The impacts of the dry spells were serious in Kapoeta East & North in Eastern Equatoria. In other parts planting of sesame and rice was disrupted, and maize and groundnuts were stunted. Other areas had to do gap filling.Four consecutive years of record flooding has led to widespread displacement, the destruction of livelihoods and the loss of arable land – contributing to rising hunger (WFP 2023). The effects of these concurrent climate shocks are compounded by rising food and fuel prices and the protracted conflict.In Canal/Pigi, an IRNA conducted by CAFAD and other humanitarian partners on September 2023 reported that flash floods swept away most of the crop from last year that left impact this year coupled to the current lower than normal rainfall. Alam, Chiew and Kurwach. Remaining options are environmentally hazardous and fragile i.e., charcoal burning.According to the figures shared by local authorities (RRC), about 1,800 families consisting of 10,930 were reported to have arrived in Nyirol County from both Sudan and Ethiopia during the periods of June-August 2023. At least 546 (5%) of estimated individuals are considered most vulnerable, the affected population could be dissected in to four vulnerable groups: the children, elderly, lactating mothers, and those with endured illness among the returnees from the Sudan and Ethiopia. These families are in dire needs of livelihood support; including food and other basic needs as they are only surviving on wild vegetable and fruits, while some other were dependent on the host communities, sharing the limited food mainly from the WFP’s general food distribution, malnutrition has significantly increased and among children and aged persons, health facilities are overwhelmed and incidents of the GBV have been reportedly hiking in number during these four months of the returnees influx.SMART surveys conducted in Kapoeta North, Canal/Pigi and Fangak found 13.3, 17.8, 18.8 percent rates of acute malnutrition respectively (FEWS NET, September 2023). This was associated with early exhaustion of food stocks due to last year’s flooding and ongoing high food prices resulting in poor household access to food at the peak of the lean season. In other areas this was driven by a combination of poor household dietary diversity and child feeding practices, and high child morbidities.

2. Project activities

All targeting and transfers will be area specific and in consultation with all relevant stakeholders. 1: Emergency food production: main season agriculture seeds & tool distribution; fishing, vegetable & crop kits 1.1: Main and dry season agriculture inputs are provided to 30,000 vulnerable women, men, girls and boys headed households in Uror, Nyirol, Fangak, Canal/Pigi, Leer and Kapoeta East & NorthCrop seeds (3 kg sorghum, 2kg maize, 2kg cowpeas), fast maturing vegetable seeds assortment (50gm Okra, 30gm green pepper, 20gm onion, 30gm Tomato, 20gm Collard, 30gm amaranthus and 30gm pumpkin seeds), fishing gear (1 monofilament, 1 box of 100 hooks and 2 twines) and a “no harm” hand farming tool (maloda or hoe according to preference) for each beneficiary distributed to increase food production. Time lines: Purchase or Preposition and distribution of crop, vegetable and fishing kits will be done in conjunction with the pipeline manager for FSL UNFAO.Rationale: Distribution will be done in early in the day (08:00 am 17:00 pm) and in an open area to ensure safety and protection for women and girls against sexual abuse and exploitation (PSEA). 2: Livestock treatment and vaccination; livestock disease surveillance, Community Animal Health Workers Training and inputs support, cold chain establishment and maintenance, prevention & treatment of common livestock disease conditions Main activity: Livestock treatment and vaccination campaign; 300,000 livestock heads targeted for vaccination including cattle, shoats, donkeys, dogs and chicken, 15% of the animals treated plus 150 CAHWs (re)trained for 7 or 14 days in the target counties. Time lines: Targeting for mass treatment, vaccination campaigns will be maximized during the dry season when the cattle are easily accessible near the homesteads. Rationale: Refresher training of CAHW teams will be planned in the dry season in order not to disrupt their agricultural activities at the onset of rains in May. The “one health” approach will be used in the treatment and awareness campaigns especially when dealing with zoonotic diseases in the target locations 3: Conditional food assistance through ACL, Cash and voucher modality3.1: Community participatory identification of productive assets for creation/rehabilitation targeting 20,000 IPC 3,4 and potentially 5 population in the target locations. This is to build resilience and assets benefiting at least 50% women and girls. 4: Support to seed fairs using locally sourced seed; seed security & multiplication strengthening/ Support market functionality assist traders to re-supply markets/ local procurement for FAO ITFs & aggregation of produce for WFP assistance Rationale: This will target 3000 vegetable and crop kits locally sourced from farmers and traders in the target Counties. A voucher system will be employed for seeds procurement. Accountability with complaint and feedback mechanisms will ensure inclusion of vulnerable target groups. 5: Training & capacity building: both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production of 3000 farmers, community nutrition volunteers (50% being women) and establishment of vegetable and crops farmer schools mentor programs for the farmers in the taget areasMain activities 5.1: Support value chain addition through agriculture, fishery and livestock products/ by-products preservation & marketing through trainings for 3000 HHs.
