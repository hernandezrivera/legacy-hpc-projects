Project Summary

District. Under the WASH sector, humanitarian organizations have continued to meet the growing needs (including response to Coronavirus-19) of the affected population (girls, women, boys, men, people living with disability [PWD] and the elderly). World Health Organization (WHO) declared Covid-19 a pandemic of public health emergency of international concern (PHEIC) and Bangladesh is one of the Asian Countries impacted. Currently, the camp context with cramped insanitary living conditions is experiencing multifaceted needs/WASH gaps elevating the Public Health exposure risks for women, girls, men, boys, persons with disabilities and the elderly further worsening their vulnerability. The WASH interventions are designed to address the sector level prioritized WASH needs/gaps for all gender and age groups in the camps and continue to build on the ongoing programs for refugees and the host community. CARE's response approach prioritize community engagement through direct participation in sessions/forums for all age groups and gender through religious and culturally relevant groupings including persons with disability.
CARE’s response interventions will focus on complete WASH components (water, sanitation and hygiene) with an intewrgrated approact to Civid-19 response. In this project, CARE proposes to develop two new water networks, upgrade platforms for 50 DTWs and ensure functionality of the piped water networks with additional tap stands, maintain existing systems including periodic water quality testing. Shallow wells with hand-pumps will be decommissioned to reduce health risks. The project will construct 100 new gender segregated latrines, 50 latrines for persons with disability, upgrade 30 latrines to meet the menstrual hygiene management needs for women and adolescent girls. CARE will prioritice community engagement approach to address gaps in hygiene promotion through involvement of all age and gender categories including adolescent girls and women. RANAS, CCC-PLTH, Child focused hygiene promotion, Risk Communication and Community Engagement approaches will be used in hygiene behavior change activities to engage different gender and age groups within the targeted camps/blocks to address most of the Public Health concerns such as acute watery diarrhea (AWD), Covid-19, etc.

1 - Identified Needs

Improved Water Services. 54 percent of refugees in the camps are now accessing chlorinated water through piped distribution networks as partners have achieved 102 waternetworks while 68 are still under construction over the 189 planned as part of the Water Network Plan. The remainder continue to access water from tube-wells fitted with handpumps. Water quality surveillance shows that 75% of water analysis done at household level were without fecal contamination and 58% with adequate chlorine residual. 88% of households reported having enough water to meet all domestic needs but local disparities exist especially in Teknaf area (55% of refugees reporting enough water in camp 26).Access to Sanitation. Despite 99 percent of households reporting using pit latrines in the camps and acceptable quantitative standards reached (18 persons per latrine and 38 persons per bathing cubicle), many qualitative challenges remain with 22% of sanitation infrastructures requiring O&M and 10% being built in flooding areas. Sanitation coverage remains challenging due to lack of space in the camps with the highest density (e.g: 1E, 1W, 2E, 2W, 3, 9, 10). 68%8 of households reported having a private space inside or attached to their shelter for bathing but those bathing places sometimes associated with laundry (56%) and dishwashing places (52% ) are unformal and often unhygienic. In camps 15 and 16, 20% and 27% of households have visible feces around their shelters (J-MNSA, 2019). The shallow latrines (with five concrete rings) initially constructed, dilapidated latrines/bathing cubicles (37% women use makeshifts for bathing) and non-functional fecal sludge management (FSM) represent significant gaps in WASH service. One of the major concerns regarding sanitation is the quality of the fecal sludge treatment as facilities require upgrade and improvements. With 500 cubic meters of Solid Waste produced daily in the targeted area, SMW remains one of the main challenges as only 51 percent of the refugee households are disposing waste in designated area and 27 percent reporting the presence of garbage near their houses.Hygiene Promotion and Community Engagement. Appropriate hygiene practices remain low despite the fact that progress have been observed partly due to fear from Covid-19 and focus on hand-washing promotion. As a result, 98% of the refugees increased handwashing practices since the COVID-19 outbreak and 95% reported having access to soap. Those improvements need to be confirmed on longer-term through both practice on knowledge as only 60% of the refugees can mention three critical time to wash their hands. In respect of Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM), 1.3 MHM kits were provided to women and girls on average between January to September11 instead of one every trimester. Sector findings show common unsanitary hygiene practice in the camps. 46% of children practice open defecation, only 32% of households wash hands after handling feces and 20% of women and girls practice safe disposal of MHM materials.
