Project Summary

The districts of Memba, Meconta and Mozambique Island (Nampula Province) face multifaceted challenges exacerbated by the proximity to conflict areas and subsequent displacement of people, including children. There are several limitations in children’s access to education and hygiene services, with most schools lacking proper infrastructures and material. The region hosts a vulnerable population, including internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and host communities. There is a pressing need for interventions addressing education, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), as well as basic WASH initiatives, in order to foster resilience and stability by creating an environment more suitable for allowing children to exert their right to education and psychosocial well-being, and ultimately contributing to the overall development of the region.
Project Objectives:
1. Education in Emergencies (EiE): The primary goal is to provide quality education to children under 18, focusing on reintegration, skill development and psychosocial well-being.
2. MHPSS: Implement tailored MHPSS programs, including specific teachers’ trainings, to address trauma, anxiety and psychosocial challenges faced by children.
3. WASH: Integrate water, sanitation and hygiene initiatives to ensure a healthy and conducive learning environment.
This project seeks to adopt an integrated approach including components of EiE, MHPSS and WASH. The activities which constitute this integrated approach are:
A1. Infrastructure intervention (EiE and WASH): to create a more adequative environment which can enhance school retention and foster learning, the project will intervene on the infrastructural component of schools, both where classes take place and in accessory hygienic-sanitary components. This activity will include:
a. Construction of TLS and classrooms reconstruction.
b. Construction of gender-sensitive latrines.
c. Construction of hand-washing stations.
d. Construction of new borehole with handpump.
A2. Provision of education and recreation material (EiE and MHPSS): to eliminate barriers to education, the project will provide materials such as textbooks, notebooks, and other essential school supplies. This activity will include:
a. Distribution of Learners Kits.
b. Distribution of school feeding.
c. Distribution of school desks and other equipment.
d. Distribution of Recreational Kits.
e. Distribution of Teaching Kits.
A3. Implementation of children’s support actions (EiE and MHPSS): to bridge educational gaps and ensure that children can quickly catch up with their peers, as well as to give children much-needed psychological and counseling services, the project will include:
a. Implementation of Accelerated Learning Programs (ALP).
b. Training of teachers on MHPSS.
c. Creation of a school-based psychosocial support (PSS) mechanism.
d. Implementation of Peacebuilding and Citizenship Trainings and Activities.
e. Training and Creation of School Councils.
The direct beneficiaries of this project are:
- 2.550 children (under 18), distributed as follows:
Memba: 1.500, including 100 children with disabilities (CwD).
Meconta: 800, including 50 CwD.
Mozambique Island: 250, including 15 CwD.
- 43 teachers, distributed as follows:
Memba: 25
Meconta: 12
Mozambique Island: 6
In terms of gender equality, a gender-balanced approach is proposed, ensuring equitable proportions of boys and girls in each District. The beneficiary children will be targeted with all three components (EiE, MHPSS and WASH), whereas teachers will be the beneficiaries of ad-hoc MHPSS trainings.
In terms of strategy and stakeholder engagement, TDH IT will collaborate with local authorities (SDJET, SDSMAS and SDPI) as well as community leaders for effective project implementation. TDH IT is currently implementing a two-year project in Memba and has already carried out recent interventions in Meconta, which favor the cooperation with local stakeholders and consistency of the overall program.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project “Integrated approach of Education in Emergency, MHPSS and WASH activities in the Nampula Province” has been designed based on Terre des Hommes Italy (TDHIT) active participation to the Education Cluster and its past and current interventions in Nampula Province. The project has been structured in order to give a significant contribution to the achievement of the HRP’s three objectives: 1 - To provide equitable access to safe and protective education services to girls, boys, and CWD of 6-17 years old in emergency through formal, non-formal and ALP by 2023 in 3, 4 and 5; 2 – To improve the quality of education through providing emergency training to teachers and education personnel; 3 – To strengthen and improve learning environment by ensuring access to improved drinking water source and access to hygienic latrines. To grant children’s access to education and hygiene services in three priority District of Nampula Province (Ilha de Moçambique, Meconta and Memba), the project intends to create an environment enabling children to exert their right to education and psychosocial well-being through a holistic approach to education, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) and water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH). The project is structured in order to achieve three main objectives:- provide quality education to children under 18.- implement tailored MHPSS programs.- integrate WASH initiatives for a healthy learning environment.Mozambique’s Northern region, including the Province of Nampula, hosts a vulnerable population which includes internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and host communities themselves. The target group has been selected based on our assessment in the area of intervention and in a proportional manner on the basis of Terre des Hommes Italy’s work in the target areas, with an on-going two-year intervention in Memba and recent ones in Meconta, as well as the identification by the Education Cluster of People in Need (PiN) in the three District, which amounted to 286 for Ilha de Moçambique, 913 for Meconta and 1933 for Memba (for a total of 3132 target people among IDPs, Returnees and Host Communities).The majority of direct beneficiaries of this project are 2.550 children under 18 years of age, corresponding to 81,4% of the Cluster’s overall target group in the three priority Districts in Nampula. They will be targeted with all three components (EiE, MHPSS and WASH) and are distributed proportionally, as follows:- Memba: 500 IDPs, 300 Returnees and 700 Host Community, for a total of 1500 target children, of which 750 females and 750 males, including 100 children with disabilities (CwD).- Meconta: 400 IDPs and 400 Host Community, for a total of 800 target children, of which 400 females and 400 males, including 50 CwD.- Mozambique Island: 100 IDPs and 150 Host Community, for a total of 250 target children, of which 125 females and 125 males, including 15 CwD.Among the direct beneficiaries there are also 43 teachers, which will be targeted with ad-hoc MHPSS trainings and are distributed as follows:- Memba: 25.- Meconta: 12.- Mozambique Island: 6.As suggested before, the intervention adopts a gender-balanced approach by targeting an equal involvement of boys and girls with its activities, as well as by training both male and female teachers.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

In the framework of the first activity (infrastructure intervention) a set of sub activities will be realized:- Setting of TLS: 5 TLS will be installed near 5 schools in Memba. Each TLS will serve 5 teachers and 300 students. TLS will be the spaces where the ALPs will take place.- Distribution of educational kits: The kits will be distributed by the team at the beginning of the year, during the school registration period. The activity will take place in Memba, Meconta and Ilha de Moçambique.- School feeding: School snacks will be allocated weekly in schools for a period of 3 months. The activity will take place in Memba.- Accelerated Learning Programs (ALP): ALP classes will be given within the TLS and schools with appropriately trained tutors and with constant monitoring by the organization's staff. The activity will take place in Memba, Meconta and Ilha de Moçambique.- Recreational kits: The kits will be allocated by the project team and recreational activities will be organized around the TLS in Memba and targeted schools in Ilha de Moçambique.- Classroom Reconstruction: In Meconta 4 classrooms will be rehabilitated and/or reconstructed. Each classroom can serve a target group of 3 teachers and 200 students (divided into morning and afternoon classes). Each rebuilt classroom will benefit from 70 desks and 70 chairs for students, 1 desk and 1 chair for the teacher, and 2 blackboards. In the framework of the second activity (Provision of education and recreation material)the sub-activities that will be implemented in the three intervention districts will be the following:- School based Psychosocial support (PSS): In Memba the project team will guarantee regular psychosocial support to TLS students (300 per TLS). In Meconta and Ilha de Moçambique the target group will be 200 students for each school.- Peace Building and Citizenship Training and Activities: this activity aims at improving dialogue and increasing ability to resolve internal conflicts. The activity will take place in Memba. Each training lasts 3 days and will benefit a total of 1,500 children- Training and Creation of School Councils: The process of creating school councils will be accompanied in the beneficiary schools of the Memba. Each training lasts 3 days and will benefit a total of 1,500 children.- Teacher Training on MHPSS: The teachers of the beneficiary schools will be trained in MPHSS so that they can be the focal points within their school in situations of need. In district an average of 5 teachers per TLS (25 in total) will be trained to ensure ongoing psychosocial support for students. In Meconta and Ilha de Moçambique an average of 3 and 2 teachers per school will be trained, respectively. The training will long 3 days.- Teaching Kits: The teaching kits will be distributed in the three districts and include the UNICEF standard kits for teaching supplemented by other basic school materials (blackboard, etc.). In the framework of the third activity (Implementation of children’s support actions), the following subactivities will be implemented:- Gender-sensitive latrines construction: In Memba district 5 latrines will be built nearby the TLS (one per TLS) in order to allow students to have access to safe WASH infrastructure. In Meconta and Ilha de Moçambique districts 4 and 6 school will benefit from the construction of latrines, respectively- Construction of Hand washing station: The activity will take place in Memba where 2 washing station will be built nearby each TLS- New borehole with handpump: The borehole with handpump will be built nearby the TLS (one per each 5 TLS) in order to allow students to have access to safe WASH infrastucture

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

- Poverty and hunger: many families in Nampula Province live in poverty and many children drop out of school early to work with their parents. To mitigate this risk specific back-to-school awareness campaigns and support for families will be put in place. A school feeding program will be introduced as evidence from THDIT experience suggest that even hunger can be detrimental to children’s regular school attendance. - Gender inequality can reduce participatation: there is a need to ensure that girls have access to education and that there are no gender biases in the curriculum or teaching practices. The project team will address those cultural practices underlying this difference by campaigns on relevant topics (GBV, early marriages, etc.). - Violence: Nampula Province has experienced violence and conflict in recent years. This can clearly have a negative impact on education, leading to dropouts and discontinuity in learning. The project will address the trauma that students may have experienced and provide a safe and peaceful learning environment, trying to recover learning gaps through ALP. - Financial: The fluctuation of exchange rates can result in a decrease in the value of funds received for the activities. However, TDHIT will have a well-developed contingency plan that outlines how to deal with unexpected events such as funding shortfalls or operational issues. - Extreme climatic events and natural disasters: phenomena such as cyclones and floods are more and more frequent in Mozambique. The project will carefully monitor the climatic alerts, but also prevent damages by selecting the locations for activities and constructions, in areas which are less prone to flooding. There will be also community sensitization on climate change during the intervention. - Operational: There are supply chain risks, such as delays or disruptions in some educational or recreative materials. This risk can be mitigated by well-advanced planning, establishing strong relationships with suppliers, and having backup suppliers in place. Risks related to monitoring and evaluation, such as the lack of accurate data or the failure to use data to improve the program, will be mitigated by investing in robust M&E systems and ensuring that program staff are trained to use e collect data. - Safety & security: The main safety and security risks in the Province of Nampula include criminal activities and armed attacks. TDHIT will have a security plan and a security focal point that will ensure the application of the plan for guarantee the security of all TDHIT staff. The movements will be coordinated by a logistician in advance. Furthermore, continuous training of TDHIT personnel on the safety measures will be promoted. TDHIT has a strong relationship with authorities, community leaders and communities. In addition, the risk of theft is frequent in Nampula: to mitigate it, appropriate segregation of duties and safety measures will be put in measures, with limited and controlled access to warehouse facilities and a tracking system of items as inventory and stock control. - Lack of acceptance by local authorities and communities: to foster ownership by local communities and the project sustainability, as a precondition the team will introduce the project to provincial and district authorities to obtain the appropriate authorizations and subsequently at community level. The project will invest in communication and information dissemination efforts to ensure that stakeholders are informed. - Political: Changes in government policies or community leadership can have an impact in the programme implementation. To mitigate this problem the programme is aligned with the national education priorities. One of the possible political risks is including Schools being used as center for election and election census (prior to election day) which could affect and reduce even more the learning time.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The project was designed with the participation of the main stakeholders in the intervention areas in such a way as to be able to ensure its implementation without encountering resistance from local authorities and interested communities. On the contrary, these actors were consulted in identifying the beneficiary schools and the spaces where the needs in the area are greatest.To ensure meaningful access to the services provided by the project a series of measures will be implemented:- publicization and dissemination of the activities: the activities will be promoted in the intervention communities through communication campaigns that include information meetings with local authorities and the populations of the communities, door-to-door visits by project activists, involvement of school teachers, distribution of information and awareness material and on the beneficiary selection criteria.- selection of beneficiaries based on the real needs identified both during the project writing phase and during the implementation of the activities. In particular, the project staff, through an information sheet, collects the main information necessary to have the profile necessary to identify the beneficiaries of the action- identify schools as the nerve center of the activities: according to a consolidated methodology by TDH IT, schools are the starting point of the activities. In fact, the main activities of the project take place in schools but they are also the place from which multi-sectoral interventions can be activated which, in addition to educational support, include nutritional, psychosocial and emotional support.- Collection of feedback from community actors and beneficiaries on ongoing activities: the monitoring plan developed at the beginning of the project includes the collection of feedback from direct beneficiaries, their families and community actors in order to be able to improve the intervention during construction on the basis of active participation in the improvement of the activities by all the people involved. Particular attention is dedicated to a meaningful participation and empowerment of women. Women and girls are the most at risk. IDP sites and host community locations lack safety conditions and basic assistance, leaving women and girls exposed to ongoing risks of GBV. At the same time, for girls school participation and access to education is exacerbated by cultural barriers that do not see school as a priority. To ensure full access for girls to school activities, families will be made aware of the importance of school participation and the minimum conditions will be provided to ensure the participation of children, both girls and boys, in activities. The infrastructure will also include the construction of gender sensitive latrines. In choosing the teachers who will be focal points for distributions and psychosocial support, an equal gender distribution will be sought to ensure the protagonism of women in the activities and recognize their needs in the implementation of the activities.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The inclusion of people with disabilities is transversal to the proposed intervention. TDHIT has long experience in Mozambique and around the world in the inclusion of people with disabilities in its projects, always trying to put PwDs at the centre, from project design to its implementation, possibly with the direct involvement of PwDs associations. For the proposed action, partner associations were consulted and the proposed activities will be adapted to the theme thanks to the distribution of school kits which will also take into consideration the possible needs of children with disabilities, compatibly with the available resources. The infrastructures (TLS, rehabilitation of school classrooms, latrines, washing hand stations) will all be built in such a way that access is guaranteed for people with disabilities. Psychosocial support training for teachers and direct psychosocial support for children will take into account the dynamics related to disability. The monitoring of the activities includes disaggregated indicators also for people with disabilities so as to be able to constantly monitor the impact of the project on this aspect and, where necessary, improve the ongoing intervention.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

In order to minimize any negative environmental impact of the activities, the project foresees to implement certain good practices. 1. For those activities involving constructions (TLS, reconstruction of classrooms, latrines, wells, handwashing stations), all leftovers from construction materials will be correctly disposed of; at the same time, to the largest possible extent without negatively impacting the quality of the infrastructure, local materials will be preferred whenever available. 2. For the WASH infrastructures which the project intends to provide (latrines, wells, handwashing stations), hygienic regulations will be strictly abided to avoid leaking or other dispersal of contaminating substances in the surrounding environment and water sources. 3. For the production of children’s activities, both recreational and educational ones, the use of recycled materials will be incentivized: not only will this stimulate the creative process of children, but also their green consciousness and their basic understanding of the anthropogenic pressure on the environment. 4. Nampula Province has proved vulnerable in the last years to the impacts of climate change, including increased frequency and intensity of extreme weather events. The project will raise awareness about the causes and impacts of climate change and provide students with the knowledge and skills to adapt to its effects.
