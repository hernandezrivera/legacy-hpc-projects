Project Summary

As a result of the complex emergency facing Maban, Nasir, Ulang, Maiwut and Longechuk Counties; the majority of people in the region are in need of assistance. While the operational environment in the targeted areas remains challenging, life-saving support in key sectors remains critical. RI’s proposed program, “Food Security and Livelihoods Support to Conﬂict Affected Populations”, aims to address the critical food security needs.
The program will provide agricultural inputs and farmer trainings on good agronomic practices to 8,000 targeted beneﬁciaries to be able to produce in the 2021 cropping season to restore production capacity of households’ dependent on food aid. Promote climate smart agriculture and support the establishment of permagardens.
Interventions will be closely linked to nutrition activities and will be aimed at increasing emergency food production, improving diet diversity and by providing coping strategies for vulnerable people. RI will establish demonstration gardens and conduct trainings with PLW’s visiting the site. Additionally, RI will establish demonstration gardens at static protection spaces and conduct trainings on best agronomic practices and nutrition to women within these sites. RI will also train Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs) who will share key messaging and conduct cooking demonstrations at community level
Majority of the support will target households with children under the age of ﬁve with a focus on those with severe and moderate acute malnutrition, pregnant and nursing women, and female-headed households, which are more likely to be food insecure than male-headed households but also seeks to support other vulnerable populations directly or indirectly affected by the conﬂict, including IDPs and returnees.
RI recognizes the roles played by men and women during this diﬃcult time; the program will therefore ensure equitable involvement of both men and women during implementation of the project. Selection of beneﬁciaries will give equal opportunities to men and women, boys and girls and registration of beneﬁciaries will take the priority needs of both men and women into consideration. All program activities are closely in line with Food Security and Livelihoods Cluster priorities, and include gender and protection mainstreaming where appropriate. RI has an outstanding reputation and has gained the trust of the beneﬁciary communities which will leverage on to enlist the support of local authorities and generate considerable interest and participation from the community to successfully implement this program.

1 - Needs

The current scale and coverage of the humanitarian response across Upper Nile remains inadequate to meet humanitarian and food security needs. Insecurity remains unpredictable; inter-communal fighting, including cattle raiding, has caused spikes in tensions, displacement, and deaths. The burden of repeated conflicts and the chronic crisis will continue to affect the most vulnerable people such as the elderly, children, women and people with disabilities. The protracted conflict and its aftermath has reduced the availability of and access to health services, safe water, food and livelihood opportunities has consequently exacerbated protection concerns. Through various reports and assessments, including RI’s food security assessments, SMART surveys, observations and consultations, both with men and women, RI has identiﬁed widespread needs regarding access to food, among the host communities, IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host community members. RI’s rapid Food security assessment (February 2021) have lost their livelihoods and have limited access to cash/income sources. This, together with distorted markets and increased prices, renders access to food very diﬃcult as indicated by RI’s current program participants. These ﬁndings only support the ﬁndings by IPC survey that led to place Ulang, Nasir, Maiwut and Longechuk counties under food insecurity emergency (IPC Phase 4) and Maban in IPC 3. While the IPC places Longechuk in Phase 3 between December 2020 and March 2021, it is projected that Longechuk will move to IPC phase 4 between April and July 2021. The IDPs and refugees are particularly vulnerable and many of them reported having switched to negative coping strategies which include both selling their assets and decreasing the number of meals per day per person. The latter is directly linked to the high malnutrition cases reported among the children under ﬁve years old in the ﬁve counties. The prevalence of childhood diseases in the targeted Counties is directly linked to the malnutrition situation.According to the latest IPC analysis in October/November 2020, this situation is compounded by high food prices in the markets and very limited access to land for cultivation. The presence of Fall Army Worm (FAW) in the counties and flooding reported in 2020 will likely result in a poor harvest. In Ulang the GAM rate is highly above the WHO threshold of 2.5%. The IPC classiﬁcation is projecting 24% of the population of Nasir, Longichok, Ulang and Maiwut to be in Phase 4, and access to markets may remain limited due to unpredictable insecurity. In Longechuk and Maban the GAM rate is above WHO emergency threshold. The analysis has identiﬁed key shocks including the floods, increasing food prices, and the fact that 57% of households do not own livestock. Food insecurity remains one of the immediate causes of malnutrition in the county. With the conﬂict still ongoing, factors driving food insecurity, including high food prices, restrictions on safe movements, worsening hygiene and access to water, and below-average agricultural sector labor opportunities are all likely to continue to deteriorate food security outcomes. Poor households will likely face further food consumption gaps as they exhaust coping strategies and as disruptions to livelihoods continue.

2 - Project activities

1. In-kind provision of agricultural kits (crop and vegetable seeds and tools) for main and dry season crop production and ﬁshing kits will be distributed to 11,200 vulnerable households in Longechuk, Maiwut, Nasir Ulang and Maban Counties. 2. Provision of training to 1,800 farmers on good agronomic practices and post-harvest handling. Training will be provided to groups of farmers through demonstration gardens to improve on agricultural skills for better yields. RI expect to support farmers with training on new technology, agricultural techniques, crop and seed storage to be able to increase production. 3. Establishment/strengthening of Village Savings and Loans groups (VSLAs) to promote the culture of savings and investment among vulnerable population. VSLAs have to a large extent supported vulnerable families in absorbing shocks due to unplanned emergencies including crop failure and it’s seen as a kind of insurance mechanism. RI will support formation of 15 VSLA groups comprising approximately 20 members each with priority given to women. 4. Promotion of kitchen gardens. RI will support the establishment of Kitchen gardens through the Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs). The CNVs will be trained on the importance of vegetable in diets and will be trained on how to establish nutritious kitchen gardens. 5. Cooking Demonstrations. The project will engage women’s groups to promote best practices for the utilization of nutritious foods through cooking demonstrations. These cooking demonstrations will be geared towards promoting child and infants balanced diets based on the use of locally available foods. Using the availability of diverse production of nutritious vegetables, the project will collaborate with the existing nutrition intervention already being implemented by RI to conduct an analysis of the food that will be available on the market and to develop recipes. The emphasis will be on giving simple and practical instructions to the parents, through a participatory approach, about feeding their children a high-energy nutrient-rich porridge made from locally available nutritious foods. 6. Establishment of 25 Vegetable demonstration gardens at the Static nutrition sites across Maban, Longechok, Maiwut, Ulang and Nasir. Beneficiaries from the nutrition facilities will be referred to the kitchen gardens where they will be trained on how to grow nutritious vegetables in kitchen gardens and benefit from the livelihood kits distributed by the project. These demo plots will serve as agriculture-nutrition education sites for caregivers of children under 5 (CU5) and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) who require additional nutritional support. This will ensure that treated nutrition cases do not relapse given the close link between food/livelihood security, health, WASH and nutrition.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

All RI staff and volunteers who are based in the four locations are trained on RI's PSEA and safeguarding policies, and ways to report incidents. Each RI field office has safeguarding champions who are given more intensive training on RI's safeguarding and PSEA approach. During project kick-off meetings with communities RI will inform communities of the PSEA policy, who the safeguarding champions are, what constitutes PSEA, emphasize that all project activities are delivered free of charge and that beneficiaries should not be asked to accept anything in exchange for any goods or services. RI will place posters and visuals outlining RI's policy and where community members can report at distribution sites. Additionally RI will have a help desk manned by RI staff at each distribution site, to collect complaints and feedback and to oversee distributions. Finally, RI's independent MEAL team and project management staff from Juba will conduct ad-hoc monitoring visits to project sites, meeting with community members and will reiterate the training and RI's position on PSEA.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Messages will be provided in both verbal form as well as through visual aids. Mass messaging for all groups will be provided to whole communities to reach beneﬁciaries. To reach the most vulnerable including women, girls, the elderly, and disabled, speciﬁc messaging including house-to-house visits and messaging by female staff will be provided.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

RI will conduct sensitization and mobilization prior to start of project, through consultations with community leaders, both formal and informal. Chiefs, project management committees, VSLA groups and women/youth groups will be provided with full details of assistance provided.The project introductory meetings in all project areas will include explanation and orientation on how to make feedback and complaints and how the feedback and complaints will be analyzed, and when feedback on complaints and requests received will be given back to the community. Moreover; RI staff and local authority will be trained on how to orient, collect, record beneficiaries’ feedback and complaints received, as well as how and when to respond. All staff will be trained on core data protection and confidentiality principals also. RI has already conducted an AAP study in each location proposed which asked a statistically significant sample size what the preferred means of both receiving information and giving feedback were. Overall, individuals felt more comfortable giving feedback verbally through RI staff or community leaders. Interestingly, females preferred female community leaders or direct to RI staff. Further, all participants stated they preferred to have information shared verbally from RI staff, and then community leaders. A significantly fewer cohort stated they preferred giving feedback to local authorities. The verbal nature of feedback and information was emphasized throughout and is unsurprising given the literacy levels of the wider population in all counties, and the limited/no access to electricity and mobile phone connection for more advanced feedback mechanisms e.g SMS, hotlines or email. RI will ensure to have the following community engagement throughout the project lifecycle:- Project kickoff and close meetings with Community Leaders, and womens leaders, local authorities, - Monthly FGDs with disaggregated cohorts of the target population- baseline and endline KAP surveys- in-depth interviews with sample beneficiaries- case studies. - PDM for cash and inkind distributions-Post Harvest Assessments- complaints hot-desk and AAP champions at trainings and distribution activities

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

RI will use the beneficiaries’ feedbacks to shape and adapt into project design and implementation. RI will conduct post distribution monitoring to get beneficiaries feedback on satisfaction on content, quantity and quality of the inputs and will change / adjust the kit content, quantity and quality based on the feedback. RI will gather beneficiaries feedback on all FSL activities including training, VSLA groups etc hardware activities to shape and adapt to beneficiaries feedback in future program design, and where possible during the lifetime of the project for any repeat activities that may be taking place and implementation. Additionally, RI's MEAL team will conduct quarterly FGDs with community members to gain feedback on RI's FSL project activities. RI's response to feedback will be shared back with community leader. Action plans will be developed with RI's project team and where appropriate actions will be followed up to improve the implementation of the project.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

The project considers speciﬁc protection needs of all population groups in the following ways: - At all distribution sites a safe space for PLWs and mothers/caretakers of children will be established for breastfeeding, childcare, and socialization (promoting sharing of experiences). - Rights awareness messaging is disseminated prior to all distributions and through PMCs -Referral mechanisms to health, nutrition, and SGBV care are established and will be utilized for cases of illness/diseases, malnutrition, and SGBV – identiﬁed cases of illness and malnutrition will be immediately referred to the nearest health/nutrition facility. Self-reported cases of SBGV are referred to the protection team for care and case management. – Vulnerable groups including elderly, households with malnutrition, PLWs, and the disabled are prioritized for programming. Barriers to access include conﬂict, and swampy, blocked roads due to rain and wet roads. To address barriers to access, RI will utilize community mobilizers as well as mobile teams to reach the most vulnerable where RI may not be able to reach. Additionally, RI regularly engages in RRMs to reach IDPs and returnees - where static services may be inaccessible. Moreover, in all operational ﬁeld sites RI maintains means of movement including boats, motorbikes, donkey carts, and vehicles to reach all targeted beneﬁciaries. Inequalities are addressed mainly through the prioritization of women in beneﬁciary selection as well as in decision-making processes. Women are prioritized for hiring, and are consulted on all decision-making procedures to ensure their voice is heard and considered. The threat of coercion and community tension is mitigated through involvement in programming by local leaders and beneﬁciaries, to ensure transparent and accountable mechanisms. While ensuring that all community members have equal access to the program, RI designs all its activities to be gender-sensitive in South Sudan cultural context and refrains from challenging gender norms to a degree that might create protection issues. That concerns both the selection and distribution of inputs in the communities. RI will go directly to the communities to deliver the inputs and where necessary, female staff will deliver inputs and provide community sensitization and outreach to female beneﬁciaries. Community leaders will be involved in each step so they encourage women to participate. A security lens will be applied to targeting of communities and households in order to mitigate any potential conﬂict related to program exclusion. While ensuring vulnerable households with women and children are the primary beneﬁciaries, the program is designed with sensitivity to the South Sudan cultural context. Additionally; RI is committed to promoting a productive, safe, and secure work environment in order to protect its staff, beneﬁciaries and assets. As the current conﬂict continues to evolve in South Sudan, RI will undertake several measures to reduce security threats to the lowest level possible. RI works in some of the most diﬃcult and dangerous places in the world, including Afghanistan, Somalia, and Iraq and has a global reach in security training and guidance through established agency-wide security protocols. Every RI oﬃce has an appointed security focal point that is responsible for advising, developing, and updating local security procedures for the protection of staff and beneﬁciaries. Also, RI receive information regarding the security situation through the UN Department of Safety and Security and the International NGO Security Forum. RI will use these resources to keep its staff, beneﬁciaries and assets safe.
