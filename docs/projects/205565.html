Project Summary

In line with the Food Security and Livelihoods cluster strategy for 2024, ACTED will contribute towards improving food security of over 91,000 beneficiaries through provision through resilience building activities such as training and market support functionality and multipurpose cash targeting primarily food security needs.
To address these needs, ACTED will continue to play a role of a strong FSL partner and conduct activities in line with the following cluster objectives:
CO1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 4 and 5)
CO2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock
CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks & contextual changes
Acted strategy is aligned with the geographic prioritization provided by WFP by including Counties with IPC5 population and IPC4 counties with pockets of hunger, violence and climatic shocks in its response. To ensure a comprehensive humanitarian support, Acted will also aim to integrate FSL beneficiaries into its other sectoral programming with a focus on WASH and will engage with other humanitarian partners (including health and nutrition sectors and FAO) to encourage synergies. All Acted activities will include Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) components to ensure that livelihoods activities are resilient to climate change consequences.

1. Needs

Years of conflict, increased exposure to climate change effects and structural gaps have left more than 2.2 million South Sudanese internally displaced and 8.5 million people in need of humanitarian assistance across South Sudan. Based on the September 2023 – July 2024 IPC analysis from November 2023, 46% of the population living in South Sudan is facing high levels of food insecurity with 4.150 million people in South Sudan experiencing Crisis level of food insecurity (IPC Phase 3), 1.63 million people in Emergency situation (IPC Phase 4) and 35,000 people face a catastrophic food insecurity. Moreover, the food security situation will worsen during the lean season as 56% of the population is expected to need food assistance from April to July 2024 due to seasonal constraints. Even though the proportion of people facing food insecurity has been slightly reduced since 2019, the needs across South Sudan remain high due to poor harvests due to unproductive production techniques, loss of harvest due to floods and conflict, limited access to humanitarian assistance, internal displacement and restricted access to Housing Land and Property (HLP). In addition, as of October 30 2023, the conflict in Sudan triggered the massive displacement of 347,249 new arrivals of which 84% of returnees and 16% of refugees who crossed the border to find shelter in South Sudan. This raises concerns for the already precarious country’s food security; northern regions of South Sudan were particularly affected due to the disruption of traditional supply chain and trade with Sudan while suffering from the additional demographic pressure of new arrivals. Due to these structural and contextual factors, current estimations for September 2023 show that most counties of the country face either a Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or Emergency (IPC Phase 4) food security situation (FEWSNET). Newly arrived households are particularly vulnerable in this context with 88% of newcomer households experiencing moderate or severe hunger in the past 30 days, resulting in 99% of new arrivals being obliged to use consumption-based coping strategies (skipping meals, reducing portions, etc.) according to WFP needs assessment. It is expected that 6.8 million individuals will need food security and livelihoods assistance in 2024 of which 3.96 million only could be reached by humanitarian partners considering the decreasing funding environment. Due to their enhanced vulnerabilities, women, girls and disabled people would be particularly exposed to food insecurity and the lack of livelihoods opportunities and should be considered as priority beneficiaries for assistance. Despite the high emergency needs in the country, Acted believes that it is important to also support communities’ to build their resilience to ensure that individuals are able to break out of the dependency cycle and can sustain themselves. To this regard, Acted activities focus mainly on resilience building in Counties that are not considered as priority according to 2024 intersectoral prioritization. It is paramount to ensure that communities have the knowledge and tools to have continuous access to food security sources, especially despite the repeated man-made and natural shocks and considering the demographic pressure linked to the arrivals of an estimated 270,000 returnees in 2024.

2. Project activities

In line with Food Security and Livelihoods Cluster strategy, Acted will implement the following activities in Western Equatoria, Eastern Equatoria, Central Equatoria and Jur River aiming to strengthen communities resilience and access to livelihoods: Activity 3.1.2. Good Agronomic practices and Activity 3.1.3. Integrated Pest Management through farmers training using Training of Trainers (ToT) and Farmer Field Schools (FFS) approaches to maximize community ownership of the training and sustainability of the learnings. Activity 3.1.4. Fisheries Training to existing fisherfolk on modified fishing traps and sustainable conservation methods. Activity 3.1.5. Provision of water pumps, Irrigation training/support including low technology techniques such as water harvesting earthworks, with a focus on established farmer associations and farmer groupsActivity 3.2.3. Village Savings/Loans Associations with an emphasis on establishing clear links between farmer groups and VSLA to facilitate farmer groups access to growth opportunities. Activity 3.2.4. Processing Equipment for value addition through the construction of honey and fruit processing facilities in South Sudan breadbasket (Western Equatoria) to improve food conservation practices. Activity 3.2.5. Training on value addition of farmer cooperatives and farmer associations to reduce food loss and increase livelihoods opportunities. Activity 3.2.6. Vocational & other enterprise/business related support through the support of micro and small enterprise and provision of start up capital Activity 3.2.7. Provision of hives for bee-keeping livelihood groups following training (Activity 3.2.5)Activity 3.3.2. Support downscaling of climate information-flood early warning information through radio programmes in partnership with local authorities and community-led DRR committees Activity 3.3.6. Fuel efficient stoves distributed to vulnerable women to support their food security while decreasing exposure to Gender Based Violence while collecting fuel for cooking. In parallel, Acted will implement the activity 1.1.6 Conditional cash distribution to the most vulnerable household based on the South Sudanese Minimum Expenditure Basket (SSMEB). This activity will be implemented within Acted mobile interventions based on displacement triggers. While the exact location of the rapid response component is yet to be determined as Acted deployment for these multi-sectoral interventions is based on displacement triggers, in the current proposal Acted has estimated that it will be deployed in priority in the intersectoral priority counties 1 (here Fangak and Ayod) Acted will ensure to include returnees in its FSL activities with a focus on Jur River throughout the implementation of activities 3.1.2, 3.1.3, 3.1.4. 3.1.5, 3.2.3, 3.2.5, 3.2.6, 3.3.2. All FSL activities will be coordinated with protection, WASH nutrition and health partners to ensure that the most vulnerable individuals have access to a complete assistance package and improve their overall nutrition and well-being. In parallel, a specific attention will be granted to mainstream Disaster Risk Reduction component to ensure that communities benefit from resilient livelihoods with a focus on floodings in Jur River.
