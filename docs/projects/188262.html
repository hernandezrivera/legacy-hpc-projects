Project Summary

The Syrian crisis is one of the worst humanitarian crises of our time, as it has claimed hundreds of thousands of lives and led to widespread destruction of civilian and agricultural infrastructure, including homes, schools, health facilities, water supply, and irrigation systems. Today, 13.4 million people in Syria require humanitarian assistance – an increase of 21% compared to 2020.The crisis, which has lasted for more than ten years, has also caused more than 12 million people to flee their homes. The economy has also suffered irreparable damage since the crisis began, with GDP falling by 60 percent, while increases in commodity prices are at an all-time high. It is estimated that more than 90 percent of the population now lives below the poverty line. The COVID-19 pandemic has also accelerated this economic downturn by further reducing income generation opportunities. It is estimated that more than 50 percent of the population is unemployed. Therefore, in order to improve the food security of the resident and displaced population, mitigate the negative coping mechanism, and restore income-generation opportunities to increase the state of food security, their livelihoods, and the population’s participation in economic life, WATAN Foundation designed the following axes taking into consideration the priorities of Food Security & Livelihoods cluster:
A. Food Assistance support
1- Cash and Voucher for food assistance [for 200 households]
2- Food basket distribution [for 200 households]
3- Ready-to-eat rations distribution [for 300 households]
4- Support the bread value chain and bakery rehabilitation support [for 5 bakeries, 1,000 beneficiaries]
B. Agriculture activities support
5- Supporting the wheat value chains [for 500 farmers];
5.1- Distribution of agricultural inputs including irrigation and harvesting costs, provision of technical extension sessions.
5.2- Rehabilitation of bakeries, flour mills, and silos for storage.
5.3-To purchase wheat from farmers and use it either in bread production (through a contract with flour mills and bakeries) or in wheat cultivation for the next season by contracting new farmers.
6- Supporting the olives value chains [for 500 farmers]
6.1- Distribution of agricultural inputs including irrigation and harvesting costs, provision of technical extension sessions.
7- Supporting the vegetable value chains [for 400 farmers];
7.1- Distribution of agricultural inputs including irrigation and harvesting costs, provision of technical extension sessions.
7.2- Supporting food processing and vegetable marketing.
7.3-Rehabilitation of canals, wells, and pumps through cash for work and service providers.
7.4- Support the solar-powered energy resources that support irrigation activities.
8- Supporting the legumes value chains [for 400 farmers];
8.1- Distribution of agricultural inputs including irrigation and harvesting costs, provision of technical extension sessions.
9- Supporting intercropping agricultural systems that include small-medium farming activities and home gardening in specific areas [for 500 farmers]
10- Support small business grants linked to agriculture activities [for 200 beneficiaries]
C. Livestock support
11- Supporting fodder crops cultivation [for 600 livestock breeders]
12- Vaccination campaigns (especially (PPR), LSD, bovine ephemeral fever, sheep pox, FMD), training, and support for local animal health service providers, [for 20,000 livestock breeders]
13- Support livestock value chains for smallholder dairy [for 400 livestock breeders]
14- Support poultry value chains [for 200 livestock breeders]
15- Support small business grants linked to livestock activities [for 200 beneficiaries]
WATAN Foundation will participate in the monthly reporting to the Food/Security Cluster and any other stakeholders to highlight any gaps, and participate in any innovative solutions that can enhance the living conditions of the affected groups in Northwest Syria.

4 - Needs

During the second half of 2021 hostilities have re-intensified along front lines in northern and southern Syria, triggering new displacements and destruction as well as continued violations of IHL and IHRL. At the same time, the long-standing needs of an estimated 6.9 million IDPs remain staggering, particularly for the over two million people in 1,760 informal settlements and planned camps, often hosted in inadequate shelters and limited access to basic services. Throughout 2021, the macroeconomic context has continued to deteriorate sharply in Syria. The combined effects of currency depreciation, soaring prices, reduced fiscal spending, widespread job loss, and unilateral coercive measures have plunged additional segments of the population into humanitarian need. As a result, families have taken on more debt and they increasingly resort to harmful coping mechanisms. These include child labor, child marriage, and the sale of productive household assets. Food insecurity has grown; Syria ranked among the 10 most food-insecure countries globally by mid-2021, with an estimated 12.8 million people considered food insecure (OCHA 2022). The suggested Interventions by WATAN will support FSL sectors in the local communities. WATAN will target both IDPs and host communities equally, ensuring the equal participation of males and females. To maximize its impact and avoid duplication, the intervention addresses the gaps identified by the FSL cluster.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

WATAN will apply the standard commitments to gender and diversity in emergency programming including mainly, and not limited to, Commitment to Dignity, Commitment to Access, Commitment to Participation, Commitment to Safety, and Gender-based Violence (GBV) prevention and response and Child protection. As specific actions are taken to reduce the risk of GBV, projects inputs distribution will be conducted by a distribution team consisting of male and female members. Distributions will be carried out during daylight hours and in locations reported to be safe by women and girls. GBV consultation services will be in place to ensure safe, confidential, and appropriate systems of care for survivors who report experienced GBV violence. Food assistance is given on the basis of vulnerability only.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

WATAN will publish PSEA material and inform BNFs and non-BNFs about their rights and have a complaint mechanism in place as well as building the local capacity in terms of protection issues through conducting appropriate training such as PSEA As for WATAN’s staff, all of its staff once hired WATAN to conduct a PSEA and safe gardening as well as code of conduct and humanitarian principles training. Also, WATAN is an active member in the protection cluster and always applies the referral mechanisms.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- Overall security risk: Monitoring of the overall security situation will be done through information received from specialized UN agencies international organizations and international NGOs. WATAN will keep close contact with its implementing partners to ensure the feasibility of the project but more important to ensure the safety of the field staff in areas targeted by the action. WATAN will maintain daily contact with local councils of the beneficiary communities to ensure instant communication of any change in the local security situation and prompt reaction. - Environmental contamination and hazards to humans by the LS & AG Activities: To prevent exposure of the local population to left-over injection needles and used vaccine, medicine flasks, and some AG inputs, all hazardous waste is collected by the vaccination teams and destroyed in incinerators. - COVID-19 risk: During the implementation of activities, all protective measures are applied to avoid the spread of COVID-19 infection during vaccination campaigns and training programs (avoid crowding, social distance, providing masks and gloves for all persons involved in project activities. WATAN will ensure that all sterilization materials (Alcohol 90 % and Soap) are available during the implementation. In the event of any COVID-19 cases amongst field team members, operations by the affected team are halted immediately and the entire team undergoes a 14 days quarantine before resuming field activities.
