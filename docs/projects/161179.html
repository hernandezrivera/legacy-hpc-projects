Project Summary

This project aims to deliver low cost, high impact essential services to the communities of Bor South, Duk and Twik East counties through high quality healthcare and integrated protection services.
In cooperation with the County Health Departments (CHDs), the State Ministry of Health (SMoH), the State Ministry of Social Welfare, and local implementing partners, MDM will support the provision of high-quality health services in 29 health facilities, including one state referral hospital, support the Boma Health Initiative in 3 counties, operate 5 mobile clinics that serve 20 remote communities, integrate GBV and CMR services in 17 health facilites, and improve access to mental health and psychosocial support services in Bor South, Duk, and Twic East. The project will target 25,000 people in need, with a specific focus on IDPs and returnees.

1 - Needs

The MdM South Sudan 2020 project was designed to address the priority needs that have been identified through various internal assessments as well as external cluster assessments and will build upon the current MdM activities implemented in 2019. Duk, Twic East and Bor Counties are among the counties severely affected by conflicts that interrupted provision of basic life-saving primary health care services due to destruction of basic social services. The counties are inhabited by an extremely vulnerable and traumatized community majority being women, girls, boys, and elderly people who are physically and psychologically exposed to a multitude of stressors. Access to basic services in the area is in many cases non-existent with population who are subjected to protracted conflict, poor economic conditions and disruption of livelihoods. In addition, demand for basic health services is high and 60% of the population does not have consistent access to health care. According to (SSHHS 2010), only 44% of the population is within walking distance of a functional health facility (HSDP 2012-2016). Indicating a need for mobile clinics that reach rural areas. The Counties health situation is fragile with extremely low health care coverage, high infant mortality and under five mortality and morbidity rates. The high poverty and long history of conflict has left an already very basic health care system extremely weakened, with severe shortages of health workers and vandalized health facilities leaving vulnerable communities with limited access to health services in addition to the huge influx of returnees. The county primary health care system has many challenges, such as insufficient qualified staff, poor infrastructure, inadequate health facilities, lack of drugs and medical supplies despite of significant efforts made by the MoH and NGOs. The 2019 activities have had a positive impact on the quality and accessibility of healthcare services in Bor South, Duk and Twic East counties, and have particulary improved health care services in 10 rural island and mainland communities of Bor South. The project has also contributed to the sustainability of local health care services by increasing the capacity of local organizations and institutions. The 2020 project will build on these successes and aims to provide more holistic and comprehensive services. Based on a comprehensive needs assessment conducted by MdM in August 2018, there are significant MHPSS and GBV needs in Bor South County and wider Jonglei State. Communities, especially women, children and other marginalized groups, expressed a wide variety of MHPSS concerns and many people discussed exposure to GBV due to the long conflict, isolation, and lack of an effective protection system. Despite a significant proportion of the population being affected by mental health conditions, services are grossly inadequate, especially at the primary health care level. Local authorities lack the technical capacity and resources to address the mental health conditions, psychosocial problems, and incidence of gender-based violence that the community is facing. There are no qualified psychiatrists or psychologists in the districts of Bor South, Duk and Twic East, and very few in South Sudan as a whole. The main needs this project will address include:1. Limited access to comprehensive primary, secondary and tertiary health care services in Bor South, Duk and Twic East counties, specifically in remote communities and highly populated areas2. Need for increased access to quality SRH services, specifically in remote communities3. Lack of integration of protection (SGBV/CMR) programs into essential primary health care the services at all levels.4. Effectively no access to mental and psychosocial health services at the community or hospital level.

2 - Activities

This project will deliver low cost, high impact essential services to the communities of Bor South, Duk and Twik East counties through high quality healthcare and integrated protection services. In cooperation with the County Health Departments (CHDs), the State Ministry of Health (SMoH), the State Ministry of Social Welfare, and local implementing partners, MDM will support the provision of high-quality health services in 29 health facilities, including one state referral hospital, support the Boma Health Initiative (BHI) in 3 counties, operate 5 mobile clinics that serve 20 remote communities, integrate GBV and CMR services in 17 health facilites, and improve access to mental health and psychosocial support services in Bor South, Duk and Twic East. Access to healthcare will be improved at community level through the Boma Health Teams, PHCUs, and mobile clinics. The clinical package offered at community level will include management of the most common causes of childhood illness, nutrition, safe motherhood and newborn care, control of common communicable diseases, gender and social inclusion (support to GBV), strengthening of early warning systems, and vital statistics. PHCCs will ensure more comprehensive primary healthcare for all age groups, with services 24/7. At the PHCC level, the clinical package will include health education and counseling, child health, immunization, nutrition, maternal/newborn health, reproductive health, curative services, gender and social inclusion, laboratory, pharmacy, referral services, and WASH. Secondary and tertiary health care will be improved in Bor State Hospital as the primary reference hospital in the region, with a focus on CEMONC and paediatric services, through supportive supervision, capacity building of staff, minor rehabilitations, improved WASH and IPC, and pharmaceutical supply. The hospital level will offer the same clincial package as a PHCC with the addition of surgical and specialized services. Local partner John Dau Foundation was selected to deliver health and MHPSS/GBV services in Duk and Twic East counties due to their many years of experience operating in these areas, high level of acceptance and comprehensive knowledge of the context in terms of challenges and needs. MdM has also partnered with Sudan Medical Care to deliver mobile medical clinics in remote areas of Bor South County as they have been based in the area and a key health actor in the state for many years. Their unique knowledge of the context and rural communities gives them an advantage in implementing services effectively. MdM will also implement MHPSS activities in 6 health facilities, 3 mobile clinics, and 10 communities ofBor South, Duk and Twic East to improve resilience and increase awareness of mental health issues common in the counties. Training and incentives will be provided to community facilitators to conduct awareness sessions on various topical issues in order to increase awareness and to facilitate referral pathways. Mental health counselors will be trained and placed in strategic health facilities to provide high quality mental health services. Clinical staff will also be trained in MHGAP to increase access to specialized mental health services in Bor South, Duk and Twic East. GBV services for beneficiaries will be improved in all health facilites through CMR trainings of clinical staff and regular awareness sessions and educational campaigns in the communities. Basic case management will be provided by counselors placed in the facilities who are trained in GBV and trauma informed care.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes, beneficiaries' rights to health is explicitly recognized through this project.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes, displacement affected communities are one of the primary beneficiaries of the project, as many IDPs have relocated to Bor Town in recent years. Access issues related to distance from static health facilities are addressed through mobile clinics, especially in island communities comprised of IDPs.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

MdM will work directly with the County Health Departments and Jonglei State Ministry of Health, aiming to provide quality healthcare for all and building stable health systems through empowerment, capacity building and development. Assessments, identifying needs, strategy planning, monitoring and evaluation will be done jointly and in partnership, adjusting to priorities and planning cycles of the CHD. To ensure health system strengthening (HSS), the 6 building blocks of HSS will be considered: health service delivery, health workforce, health information systems, access to essential medicines, financing and leadership/governance.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, the project implements the Boma Health Initiative, a national objective. It is also aligned with the National Refugee Return Framework, as it promotes health system strengthening.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

At the moment, the protection environment is sufficiently stable. Returnees and IDPs from other areas are arriving to Bor Town in steady numbers to access improved services. Patient data from the referral hospital in Bor indicates that population and demand for services is increasing.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, the project works closely with the local health authorities to ensure sustainability of the project. It also works in partnership with local organizations, specifically John Dau Foundation and Sudan Medical Care, to allow for handover to NNGOs operating in the area.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Special efforts will be undertaken to address the protection risks associated with the project. The affected population will be in the program design through an assessment and participatory discussions that include questions on their basic needs and preferred ways to address them, access to channels of information, as well as protection risks associated A beneficiary feedback mechanism emphasizing on two way communication will be in place. The priority will be to offer a variety of channels to raise feedback or complaints, so that affected populations with different needs will be able to access and use the mechanism. Beneficiaries will be consulted on their preferred mechanisms to give feedback, ensuring that people with different vulnerabilities and characteristics are part of this assessment. Furthermore, to ensure staff neutrality, the mechanism will be handled by the MEAL team, who will receive on-the-job training, including training on protection, in order to ensure that feedback and complaints, especially any complaint on any case violence and PSEA will be handled in an appropriate, confidential and time efficient manner. At the same time, clear SOPs and referral pathways will be developed, including handling critical or sensitive cases that will be registered on a separate database with limited access and will be handled within 24 hours. Other practical measures will be taken in the activity design to minimize risk of exposure to violence as a result of distance to medical services. Most notably, mobile clinics to remote areas will be prioritized as a modality that reduces the need for women to travel to reach high quality maternal and child health, SRH and GBV/CMR services. The health workers running the mobile clinics will receive training on GBV and mental health topics in order to better address protection needs that are identified in the rural communities.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

During the first months of the interventions, the MEAL Officer will collect data from the affected population on their preferred way to receive information, give feedback or complaints and the channels will be adjusted as necessary. Throughout the project, different channels will be in place to provide accessible and timely information to affected populations. Beneficiaries and other stakeholders will receive information about the project activities. This will be channeled through different ways including verbal communications and others based on beneficiary preferences. The mechanism will have multiple access points (community consultations, telephone and email) and information about its availability will be posted at all health facilities. Beneficiary feedback be will be recorded, analyzed and presented in a systematic way. Emphasis will be placed on closing the feedback loop, meaning that the person who raised a complaint will always receive feedback on their case and that as far as possible, beneficiary data will be used to inform programmatic decision-making. Every three months the MEAL officer will produce an accountability report and a workshop will take place to discuss main findings and decide on action points.
