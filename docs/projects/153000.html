Project Summary

The overall objective of this project is to reduce the risk of injuries and deaths posed by Landmines/ ERW to a zero level among IDPs/ Returnees, vulnerable people and conflict affected populations and humanitarian aid workers, and to provide a safe access to land, communities and reducing the threat of Landmines/ ERW to a zero level among IDPs/ Returnees, vulnerable people and conflict affected populations and humanitarian aid workers.
Through land release activities including survey, clearance and marking of landmine/ ERW contamination, this project aimed at achieving the following primary objectives;
1. Restored safe access to land, and reducing the risk of death/ injuries from landmines/ ERW among vulnerable people, conflict affected populations, and humanitarian community,
2. Providing a safer environment for vulnerable people affected by conflict, IDPs/ Returnees in settlement areas, as well as refugees and host communities in Abu- Karshola locality with other localities in South Kordofan state, and El Kurmuk and other localities in Blue Nile state, where IDPs/ Returnees are able to return to their home areas, having access to community support services and resume their livelihood activities,
3. Increased access to timely protection assistance to prevent serious risk to the safety and dignity for conflict- affected vulnerable people and communities in South Kordofan and Blue Nile states,
4. a comprehensive and accurate information on the exact landmine/ ERW contamination is provided.
This project targeted the release of 3 million square meters through land release and opening up of 100 kilometers of humanitarian roads for the benefit of 100,063 beneficiaries included in this number a total of 7,840 beneficiaries to Mine risk Education. these targets to be achieved by two Integrated MRE/ Multi- Tasking Teams, Two Manual Clearance Teams and One Road/ Route Verification and Clearance Team.

1 - Needs

The states of South Kordofan and Blue Nile remain as two of the highest contaminated states in Sudan with landmines and ERW. (HNO 2019) estimates, in South Kordofan and Blue Nile states more than 224,131 vulnerable people including IDPs, returnees and refugees on the move are particularly vulnerable to landmines and ERW contamination, putting them at risk of injury or death. In addition, more than 200,349 people living in or moving through areas affected by landmines or ERW, in need for humanitarian assistance which could be hindered or limited for the presence of landmines/ERW. To date, the level and impact of contamination in certain areas in South Kordofan and Blue Nile states especially in and around SPLM-North controlled areas remain largely unknown for the prolonged years of conflict, where unreported accidents and incidents from landmines and ERW still claiming lives of the vulnerable affected population including men, women, boys and girls. As of November 2019, NMAC has registered in its IMSMA database a combined total of 163 hazardous areas in both South kordofan and Blue Nile states, with a registered total of 647 victims in South Kordofan state (447 injured and 200 killed) including 252 men, 29 women, 81 boys, 24 girls and 261 unknown victims. And in Blue Nile state, the registered total number of victims has reached 453 persons (304 injured and 148 killed) included 265 men, 16 women, 86 boys and 7 girls with 79 unknown victims. Furthermore Sudan has a commitment to meet its obligations under Article 5 of the Anti-Personnel Mine Ban Convention (APMBC) by removing all known anti-personnel mines by April 2023.

2 - Activities / Outputs

In response to the humanitarian situation in South Kordofan and Blue Nile states and the risk posed by landmines and ERW. NUMAD’s approach has been developed using its knowledge of the area and extensive operational experience in implementing mine action projects in Sudan specifically in South Kordofan and Blue Nile states. NUMAD designed an adaptable approach to the humanitarian needs of mine- impacted communities for protection and has a robust logistics plan which will be sustained by an effective and responsive management capacity along with good working relationships NUMAD has with local communities, local governments, NMAC, UNMAS- Sudan, other NGOs and other stakeholders. NUMAD will use its mine action capacity which consists of seven teams included two Integrated Explosive Risk Education and Multi- Tasking Teams, two Manual Clearance Teams and one Route Verification and Clearance Team (RVCT) to implement this project. The teams will be supported by management capacity in Kadugli and Damazin, and in Khartoum NUMAD HQ.NUMAD’s teams will be deployed over a time-frame of seven months during 1 February – 31 December 2020 excluding the raining season period, into priority areas tasked by NMAC to carryout Explosive Risk Education and Community Liaison, Land Release operation, and humanitarian route survey, verification and clearance in order to reduce risk of explosive ordnance accidents, enhancing the safe movement and increasing access to land for productive use by the local population, IDPs, returnees, refugees, and humanitarian aid workers delivering humanitarian assistance, and opening routes for humanitarian workers to enable humanitarian aid projects to take place. The project activities and outputs are;Activities: • Obtain the relevant signatures on the Technical Agreement to approve the project activities,• Conduct refresher training and on-site accreditation for the seven teams (2x RE/ MTTs, 2x MCT & 1x Road Verification and Clearance Team) • Deployment of the teams to priority areas tasked by the NMAC,• Establishment of temporary camps in remote locations,• Liaison and sensitization of local communities to NUMAD’s land release activities and to the risks posed by landmines/ Explosive Remnant of War through explosive risk education/ community liaison sessions conducted by the integrated explosive risk education team,• Investigation and subsequent clearance of new explosive hazards found by the integrated explosive risk education team as they are found so the new hazardous areas can be immediately recorded as cleared on the IMSMA database,• Implementation of land release activities including non- technical and technical survey, EOD spot tasks and clearance as identified by the community and tasked by NMAC in addition to conduct humanitarian road/route survey, verification and clearance in humanitarian priority roads/ routes,• Handover ceremonies conducted in communities to ensure they have confidence and knowledge of released lands. Outputs:• 40 Hazardous Areas are subjected to land release process to confirm the presence or absence of landmines/ ERW and subsequently released back to communities,• 3,000,000 sqm released back to communities through non- technical and technical survey, clearance, and EOD spot tasks,• 100 kilometers of humanitarian roads/ routes surveyed, verified and cleared for delivery of humanitarian assistance,• 100,063 beneficiaries (including men, women, girls and boys) of land release activities and road/ route verification and clearance,• 224 sessions of Explosive Risk Education delivered to 7,840 beneficiaries from men, women, girls and boys to raise their awareness on risk related to explosive hazards and to collect information about explosive hazards known to the communities,• 100% of items of explosive ordnance destroyed and reported to the NMAC in IMSMA format,• 100% of new hazardous areas reported to the NMAC in IMSMA format.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

NUMAD strives wherever possible to minimise the environmental impact of clearance operations, particularly those pertaining to the road/ route verification and clearance where roads/ routes contaminated with landmines/ERW could be blocked with overgrown areas and thick trees especially in South Kordofan and Blue Nile states, as well as the removal and demolition of mines/ explosive ordnance could start fire. The removal and demolition of mines/ explosive ordnance in a controlled setting as detailed in NUMAD’s SOPs which recommended the use of protective work with safety measure to be applied reduces the potential disruption to the immediate environment, which may otherwise have detonated independently causing potential injury or damage to surrounding wildlife.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

This project will be a continuation and part of current NUMAD’s Mine Action Intervention in South Kordofan and Blue Nile states. Thus its design was carried out in close liaison with local communities, local government, NMAC, UNMAS- Sudan and other stakeholders, who are also direct beneficiaries of the project. Male and female beneficiaries will also be consulted at the individual assessment stage for each tasked area in order to understand their specific requirements for project design, and the beneficiary community will continue to be implicated at each stage of the project throughout its implementation. Land release, road/ route verification and clearance operations will be prioritized in order to meet the most pressing needs, as judged by the communities themselves, according to information gathered during the survey and community liaison activities.Also NUMAD’s Code of Conduct that observed by all NUMAD’s staff and team members will be maintained during all time staff members are deployed under NUMAD’s project to ensure that NUMAD’s staff posed no harm to, or adversely affect local communities.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

NUMAD is accountable to affected communities it serves and its donors and NGOs community, and is committed to work in harmony with other humanitarian partners to serve the humanitarian needs for the vulnerable population, thus engaging in NGOs' networking as a mechanism for mainstreaming, and accountability to affected people as well as learning forum where best practice will be shared. NUMAD throughout the period of the project will use the NUMAD’s Community Liaison capacity to ensure that men, women, boys and girls, local authorities and humanitarian workers are consulted in baseline survey, survey and prioritization of areas for land release and humanitarian roads/routes so their opinions on the needs are heard and valued. NUMAD will ensure through recruiting male and female mine risk education/community liaison officers within the teams, that information flow between the beneficiaries and NUMAD’s project staff is maintained including feedback from direct beneficiaries, local governments, NMAC, UNMAS- Sudan and other NGOs.Also NUMAD recognizes that sexual and racial harassment create an intimidating, hostile and offensive working environment and unreasonably interferes with an individual's work performance, and may adversely affects an individual's employment opportunities. Sexual and racial harassment will not be tolerated by any member of staff. Employees who witness sexual and / or racial harassment, and / or who feel they are the target of abuse or are facing discrimination on these grounds have the right to report it in confidence to a Manager in confidence. NUMAD takes all such claims seriously and will respond with investigations and disciplinary actions if necessary (including dismissal in some instances), and in some cases, could lead to criminal prosecution. The results of such investigations and disciplinary actions if requested by respective authorities and / or required as part of reporting requirements to the donor or stakeholders will made available.
