Project Summary

Over January 2023 - January 2024, INTERSOS conducted Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessments and education-specific analyses in multiple sub-districts in Hama, Idleb, and Rural Damascus. Conflict has left a large number of schools either partially or entirely damaged, leading to overcrowded classrooms within the functional schools, lacking supplies, equipment and adequate WASH facilities. Socio-economic vulnerability of the HH is a key factor for not attending school and pushing boys into child labor and exposing girls to higher risk of early marriage. Overage students are at high risk of not re-entering education also because of the scarcity of secondary schools. Large needs for teachers’ professional development was reported. Adults and children have been differently affected, reporting signs of PTSD, depression and isolation, anger and violent behaviors. Teachers themselves show signs of distress and are in need of PSS. CwDs are particularly impacted by the crisis and continue being excluded from education services. In 2024-2025, INTERSOS will continue integrating its education program with protection (HSYR24-PRO-212371-1), livelihood (HSYR24-ERY-212363-1), and health (HSYR24-HEA-211951-1) ones to ensure a holistic response to the increasingly complex needs of the local populations. Targeting RD, Hama and Idleb - possibly expanding to Deir-ez-Zor, INTERSOS will focus on improving safe and quality access to education and enhancing the quality of teaching/learning. Identified protection risks are: education facilities not being safe, physical punishment, acts of violence and bullying, lack of quality services and referral expose children and survivors to harm, enhancing risk of stigmatization, community lack of acceptance of GBV/CP messaging, refusing protection activities. To address these risks, the following mitigation actions will be adopted: rehabilitate school and WASH infrastructures to ensure safe and inclusive access, training/coaching on code of conduct provided to school personnel, CP awareness raising, PFA/PSS alternated to formal classes, service mapping developed and regularly updated, protection awareness sessions, community engagement to identify protection concerns and define mitigation actions; age/gender-appropriate capacity building for community volunteers/leaders; trainings on Protection, CP, GBV, PSEA, Safe Identification and Referral are mainstreamed. INTERSOS has global PSEA/CP policies applying to all personnel and suppliers, incl. trainers. A country-specific strategy ensures SEA risks/mitigation strategies/procedures are tailored to the context. INTERSOS staff must sign a PSEA/CP declaration and attend PSEA/CP induction/refreshers (PSEA/CP trainings are also provided to partners). In-country PSEA Referent and 6 PSEA FPs are appointed to receive reports about alleged SEA acts and follow up in compliance with relevant guiding principles, ensuring safety of survivors. Reports can be made in person, to a dedicated email address, by phone or through INTERSOS CFM boxes. Complaints are then referred to the PSEA Referent and investigated. Programme risks are mitigated through robust monitoring and reporting mechanisms, capacity building for staff and partners, and clear engagement protocols with SARC/line ministries. Safety and access risks are mitigated via security assessments, capacity building on relevant protocols, and close coordination with key actors (OCHA, SARC, line ministries, other partners, community networks). Close supervision of the compliance with internal SOPs, rigorous monitoring of financial expenditure and financial planning, frequent due diligence and pre-qualification processes, regular market research and procurement in stocks reduce financial and procurement risks. INTERSOS Contingency Planning Process details risks and mitigation measures for each target governorate.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Promoting integration between Education and Protection, Health and Livelihood responses, INTERSOS will operate in coordination with MoE and DoEs under the relevant sub-MoU. On the one hand, the intervention is designed to enhance the physical/infrastructural environment of local schools. Rehabilitation works will include priority civil and electrical works, painting, repairing of doors and windows, installing water tanks and water pumps, and installing solar panels (these activities are also included in the ERL response). This will be complemented by the provision of the needed teaching material, furniture and equipment, including ventilation and heating systems. Such intervention will promote a safe and conducive teaching and learning environment, preventing disruptions of the education processes and ensuring adequate space and resources and accessible premises for PwDs. Furthermore, by restoring partially functional schools, the response will contribute to promote OOSC re-enrolment in school and children’s continuation of learning, where school drop-out and being out-of-school is partly caused by class overcrowding, lacking furniture and teaching/learning supplies, and physical conditions of the school infrastructures (not accessible or lacking gender segregated WASH facilities). On the other hand, the intervention will contribute to quality education and enhanced students’ learning and wellbeing. The capacities of teachers/school personnel will be strengthened on education topics such as active learning, EiE, classroom management and positive discipline, or inclusion, but also CP, GBV risk mitigation and safe identification and referral. PTAs will be established and their members trained on cross-cutting topics (e.g. inclusive education, gender mainstreaming, CP, school health protocol). To promote community ownership and active participation in child education, PTA-;ed initiative will be materially supported. Children will be directly supported through NFE alternated with structured and semi-structured PSS. Remedial classes will be conducted in order to strengthen learning outcomes and continuity of learning, while SLP will promote the reintegration of OOSC into formal education. Wherever possible, INTERSOS will also support national exams for grade 9th and 12th, supporting children who cross the Lebanese-Syrian border or the frontlines to undertake their exams. Such activity will ensure learning continuation and empower displaced children progressing their education, while this kind of support is critical in terms of conflict sensitivity, promoting positive interactions/engagement between communities that are divided by the ongoing conflict. In 2024-2025, INTERSOS plans to reach 19,620 individuals with 2,043,712 USD budget requirement, including 7,070 individuals (903,612 USD) in 2024 and 12,550 individuals (1,140,100 USD) in 2025.
