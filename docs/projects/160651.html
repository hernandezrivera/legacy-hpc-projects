Project Summary

The proposed projects seeks to support 5,000 vulnerable households (30,000 individuals) to regain their livelihoods by providing farming inputs and training on better agricultural methods in crop production as well as vegetable production. From this number, 300 female headed household (1,800 individuals) will be purposefully targeted for to receive additional support in the form of an unconditional cash transfer. In addition, the project will also target 1,000 (6,000 individuals) fisher folk households with training on better fishing methods accompanied with distribution of fishing kits. Therefore, in total, 6,000 households (36,000 individuals) will be targeted. Part of the selection criteria of beneficiaries will be based on the Food Consumption Score (FCS) and Coping Strategy Index (CSI). WFP approved tools will be used to ensure that the correct data is collected.
Activities will be aligned to the seasonal calendar and based strictly on the livelihood activities of target communities in the target area i.e. farming and fishing. During lean periods, vegetable farming will be promoted while crop farming will be carried out during the rainy season. In between, fishing activities will be promoted to complement both crop farming and vegetable farming. This mix of activities is aimed at protecting crop seeds from consumption and also serves as a protection strategy towards discouraging adoption of negative coping practices within households. The expected outcome of the intervention is the rebuilding of livelihood assets for target population as well as improving their dietary intake. The multiplier effect of this is the improvement of the coping capacity of target beneficiaries. IDPs, returnees and host communities will all be targeted since the project is needs driven.
To avoid duplication of efforts, activities will be coordinated through Food Security and Livelihoods cluster at county, state and national levels. Regular and timely reporting will be provided through progress reports as well as though the 5W matrix.

2 - Activities

The project seeks to address the following cluster objectives as follows;CL4/CO1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5, 4 & some 3). Cash intervention targeting female-headed households: 300 female-headed households will receive an unconditional cash transfer of $80 in addition to the minimum agricultural kit. The main selection criterion in this case will be a weak CSI in addition to being the head of the household and primary breadwinner. CL4/CO2: Enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing and livestock support (in IPC 5, 4 and 3). The following kit will be provided: a). Vegetable seeds: Amaranth (20g), Onion (20g), Tomato (20g), Eggplant (20g), Kale (sukuma wiki) (20g), Pumpkin (30g), Watermelon (30g) and Okra (50g).b). Crop seeds: Maize (5kg), Sorghum (3 kg) and Cowpeas (5kg)c). Fishing kit: Fishing twine (2 spools), Monofilament (1 coil) and Hooks (2 boxes)d). Farming tools: Hoe (1pc), Rake (1pc), Maloda (1pc), Axe (1pc) and Watering can (1pc). The target community will be consulted on suitability of the above recommended kit and their views taken into consideration before the procurement process starts. Distribution of agricultural inputs will be accompanied by training on better agronomic technics including but not limited to land preparation, use of farm tools, vegetable production, cereal production, water for production & irrigation, post-harvest control, pest & disease control. Distribution of fishing gear will be accompanied by training that is designed to complement traditional ﬁshing methods with a view to increasing productivity. Cross cutting issues: Gender; 1. Gender disaggregated information and vulnerability criteria (FCS & CSI) will be at the base of the beneﬁciary selection process for all project activities. 2. Female headed households will be deliberately targeted. 3. Follow-up assessments will be designed so as to gather gender and age speciﬁc information to effectively design and report on project activities. 4. Focus group discussions (FGDs) will be encouraged and held separately for the different groups to effectively identify gender speciﬁc needs. 5. Project activities will consider the different roles played by different sexes, age groups and age sets. 6. Project activities will encourage equal access to and participation in project activities. 7. Emphasis will be placed on encouraging women and the youth to be more involved in agricultural activities. Environment:1. Issues such as grazing areas, water points, farming land, irrigation for off season production will be included in project activities to address climate change and adaptation. 2. FCA has a well-deﬁned climate tool that acts as a guideline on how to incorporate climate change and adaptation in projects. 3. Rain water conservation will be promoted to cater for lean periods. 4. Soil conservation will be taken into consideration while farming. 5. Proper irrigation methods will be encouraged to maximize water and minimize wastage. 6. The use of animal manure and domestic waste (compost) will be encouraged. 7. The use of traditional (organic) means of pest and weed control will also be encouraged as a means to protecting the environment. HIV/AIDS: 1. Awareness raising and referencing of cases will be promoted.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

The rights of beneficiaries will be legally recognized. FCA is committed to promoting the Rights Based Approach as follows;1) Participation: By increasing ownership and participation of beneficiaries in the planning processes. Participation of target communities is one key aspect to achieve this. Communities will be consulted on matters related to vulnerability selection criteria and the entire selection and registration process. At the same time, they will be accorded a platform to provide feedback on areas of improvement or concern to ensure a positive impact.2) Accountability: A forum will be created aimed at bringing together duty bears, right holders and other stakeholders to openly discuss issues related to project work. 3) Non-discrimination and equality: In particular, people living with disabilities will also be targeted for assistance as a way of contributing towards eliminating the stigma. 4) Empowerment: Targeting female-headed households with cash distribution will put them in the driving seat to manage their own finances. This will depend on consultations with the various communities to ensure a culturally sensitive approach to avoid creating imbalance within homesteads or communities.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Pibor County is home to IDPs who will be targeted in this intervention. The intervention is designed so as not to hold IDPs in their areas of refuge should they wish to return to their areas of origin or when the time is right. The perspectives of IDPs will be taken into account by ensuring that there is a representative of IDPs in the beneficiary committee. To promote participation of beneficiaries, FCA will encourage regular exchanges with beneficiaries which will also give an opportunity for FCA to report back to communities as well as receive feedback on ongoing activities and whether there are areas of improvement.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project seeks to address basic humanitarian needs without contravening existing government or community systems. These are key stakeholders without which project activities would not achieve the intended impact. FCA is actively involved in peace work in Pibor and the GPAA in general which involves local authorities, traditional and religious leaders. This project will be linked to these existing efforts that aim to promote peace as well as peace dividends.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

As with the general FCA program objective, the proposed project is linked to the South Sudan Development Plan’s (SSDP) objective for economic development, which seeks to facilitate diversified private sector-led economic growth, and sustainable development, which improves livelihoods and reduces poverty, albeit in a humanitarian setting in the short and mid term.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

FCA has been in Pibor for over 5 years and has a long-term strategy in the area. FCA also works in both humanitarian and development contexts. As an exit strategy, FCA will continue to emphasize on linking Relief, Rehabilitation and Development (LRRD) by building this into humanitarian projects and consolidated through development projects when the time is right. The proposed project, will promote group dynamics as well as offer training and skills that will remain with the beneficiaries beyond the project period. Livelihoods assets distributed will also continue to benefit beneficiaries for several seasons after the project is over. At the moment, the Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA) is safe enough for the proposed activities. Generally, the cessation of hostilities in many parts of the country has brought positive change which can be exploited to promote livelihood activities. FCA continues to invest heavily towards tackling intra and inter community conflict through its peace programming.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

FCA will invest in group dynamics by encouraging farmers and fisher folk to form groups. This will ensure that training is carried out more efficiently and cost effectively. This makes it possible to hand over these same groups to other existing or future projects in the area. At the same time, distribution of crop seeds will continue to be encouraged by encouraging farmers to save seeds for future agricultural seasons. Proceeds from output generated from farming and fishing can also be reinvested into acquiring more inputs to expand livelihood activities.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Exclusion from consultations and decision-making process for marginalized groups (8.1), women (8.2) and youth (8.3) will be mitigated by ensuring that a beneficiary committee is formed which includes a representative of each of these groups. In addition, a representative for people living with disabilities, IDPs, returnees and host community will also be included in this beneficiary committee. To mitigate against interference of external stakeholders in beneficiary selection (8.12), the project will make use of scientific methodologies of beneficiary selection e.g. the FCS and CSI will complement community participatory methods in ensuring that the most vulnerable individuals are targeted. This will be complimented by the work of an all-inclusive beneficiary committee (aforementioned). This will create little or no room for manipulation from external stakeholders.
