Project Summary

The conflict in Syria is entering its ninth year and is ranked a Level 3 crisis, resulting in 13 million people in need of humanitarian assistance. In Dara’a governorate, where the uprising originally started in 2011, up to 300 000 children are in need of assistance in education and psychosocial support. Education services are especially poor in communities to which inhabitants are returning to after a period of displacement.
The operational strategy of Fida will focus on providing safe learning spaces and psychosocial support for primary school aged children. The aim is to target two sub-districts of Kherbet Ghazaleh and Dara’a Al-Balad in southern Syria. Twelve schools will be rehabilitated, including classrooms and latrines. 5250 school children will receive school supplies. Out-of-school children and academically challenged students, including children with disabilities, will receive catch-up and remedial education during the summer to enable them to take part in the new school year.
Teachers report that they feel ill-equipped to provide psychosocial support to students who have lived through the crisis and are often traumatized. Therefore, 100 teachers will be trained to provide psychosocial support for their students and to identify specific traumas. 800 children will improve their resilience in crisis through attending a summer school with activities that support their educational and psychosocial well-being. 400 parents of children will receive PSS group support to enhance their ability to provide PSS support for their children.

Needs

A needs assessment was conducted in November 2018. Due to lack of access to Dara’a for international staff and the sensitivity of data collection in Syria, a methodology was selected combining a desk review with observations from local partner staff in Dara’a, who visited schools and interviewed teachers and local authorities. The main sources of the desk review were the HNO 2018, HRP Monitoring Report Jan-Jun 2018, and the No Lost Generation Education report 2018. The assessment found that there is widespread destruction of school facilities and that teachers feel ill-equipped to provide psychosocial support to students. There is a special need to target the sub-districts of Kherbet Ghazaleh and Dara’a Al-Balad as residents are returning there, but there are no actors providing safe spaces at schools. There are 945 school buildings and six education compounds in Dara’a governorate. Up until now, the Directorate of Education has assessed 311 schools and the process continues. Out of the assessed 311 schools, 50 schools are totally damaged, 78 are partially damaged and 116 need light rehabilitation. By the beginning of the new academic year, no access to education is guaranteed in many locations due to lack of eligible and usable school buildings. Schools need repairing of windows, doors, classrooms and playgrounds as well as basic rehabilitation of hygienic sections including toilets and safe drinking water. Many children are still out of school for various reasons. Some lost the education opportunities and access to education over recent years of crises. Some were obliged by their parents to leave school as families turn to negative coping mechanisms in such as early marriage and child labour. Research has also shown that lack of hygiene facilities especially deters girls from attending school, in order for both boys and girls to feel safe and comfortable using the facilities, it needs to be made sure that they are separate. There has been a lack of provision of PSS services to children and teachers in Syria. This is supported by the teachers in Dara’a. The deficiency of PSS support has been partly due to lack of prioritisation and funding, and partly because of a lack of specialised agencies to deal with structured PSS. The subdistrict of Dara’a Al-Balad has acute needs in both protection and education, scoring five out of six in the HNO severity scale in both. Kherbet Ghazaleh, Mzeireb, Tassil, and Izra all have acute and severe needs in these sectors . Kherbet Ghazaleh and Dara’a Al-Balad have seen a large displacement of people, some of whom are returning now to a shortage of education services, due to which the assessment recommends targeting these areas. The authorities say that the recommended areas are not served by other NGOs, UNICEF or UNHCR. According to MECC the number of PwD has increased significantly during the crisis. Additionally, where there were many services for PwD before the crisis, most have been closed except for some services that are provided by local organizations. According to the ministry of social affairs, Dara'a Governoratehas 13,108 PWDs. These figures represent the highest number among other governorates. The number of people living with disabilities at the moment at sub-district level need to be assessed further.The assessment team is aware of one centre for children with disabilities, offering services to children with autism, down syndrome and speech impediments.

Implementation of activities

1.1 Twelve schools will be assessed to ascertain their rehabilitation needs, the BOQs will be made and local contractors will be selected for the rehabilitation 1.2 Twelve schools will undergo basic rehabilitation including classrooms, doors, windows, painting, latrines, and hand washing facilities. If there is room in the budget playgrounds will also be rehabilitated. Rehabilitation will prefer solutions that promote an inclusive environment for children with disabilities. 1.3 School kits will be distributed to 5250 students. The kits include school supplies such as: writing utensils, notebooks, a school bag, a water bottle and personal hygiene items. 1.4 Home visits will be conducted to identify 6-8 year old out-of-school children for their selection in the catch up summer classes. This assessment will include identifying and including children with disabilities. 1.5 Students who struggle academically in school will be identified and provided with remedial summer classes as a part of the summer schools. 2.1.1 Referral pathways will be identified for the teachers to be able to refer students with psycho-social issues and/or trauma to professionals. 2.1.2. 100 teachers will be trained in child protection, inclusion of children with disabilities, and in how to provide Psycho-social support (PSS) to students including referring of severe cases of trauma to other actors during the academic year and employ them to provide PSS and education activities in the summer schools. 2.2.1 Children in the two communities will be identified to attend the summer school, including children with disabilities. 2.2.2. Summer schools will be conducted for 800 school aged children with appropriate PSS activities such as mechanisms of discharge through art, sport and team activities, “your story is my story” approach in enhancing expression of feelings and retaining self-confidence. 2.3 PSS will be provided to 400 parents of children attending the summer school, focusing on family relations, to be carried out in group sessions of maximum 12 people per group.
