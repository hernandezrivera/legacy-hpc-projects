Needs

The food security situation in Sudan continues to be in a bad state as a result of many years of conflict coupled with limited livelihood options, environmental degradation, climate variability, and low agricultural productivity. Of late localized crop failures and substantial increases in food prices have made the situation worse. For instance, the price of sorghum increased 77% from January 2017 to January 2018 . The cost of agricultural inputs, such as seeds, tools, fertilizers, and animal medicines, has similarly increased thereby contributing to the reduction of purchasing power at the household level. The shortage of fuel (40-65% of required fuel was provided by government ) and high prices of inputs during the planting season meant that lower yielding crops were planted in the past agricultural season and this reduced the yield potential of the season that witnessed normal rainfall amounts. The combination of all these factors has a negative bearing on the majority of IDPs, refugees, returnees, and host communities resulting in limited access to food.Desertification and reduced fertility of previously agriculturally productive areas is evident in South Darfur. This is due to poor soil fertility management, climate variability and loss of top soil due to erosion.Above average rainfall was received in South Darfur in the just ended agricultural season and this was favorable for crop production and pasture regeneration. As a result food security outcomes were expected to improve between October 2018 and January 2019 and most areas in South Darfur would maintain or improve to Minimum (IPC Phase 1) and Stressed (IPC Phase 2) on the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification methodology. However, it is anticipated that the food security situation will deteriorate to Emergency (IPC Phase 4) in areas hosting IDPs and conflict areas such as Jebel Mara. In Nitega locality which boarders Jebel Mara, for example, a harvest assessment conducted by FAO, the ministry of Agriculture, and NGOs (including World Vision) in December 2018 showed that there is already a grain deficit of 24,317 metric tons before the lean season has even begun. The condition of pasture, water and livestock was also found to be poor.Prospects of a normal rainy season in 2019 are not good according to FEWSNET and local experience which believe that after 2 relatively good consecutive rainy seasons in 2017 and 2018 the region is due for a poor one. This calls for concerted efforts to adopt climate smart agricultural techniques that will minimize the effects of such an eventuality.World Vision Sudan conducted a multi-sector needs assessment conducted in November 2018 in Buram, Kass, Beliel, Nyala North, Mershing, Nitiga, Almalam, Rahead-Albirdi and Alradoum localities. The main source of food as reported by families is purchasing from the market (70%) while 26% mainly get their food from their own production

Activities

WVS will work with the Ministry of Animal Resources to identify Community Animal Health Workers (at least one per village) and build their capacity through training, mentoring, and facilitation of learning from peers. The training will cover animal husbandry, livestock nutrition, diagnosis, and treatment. Trained Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) will conduct periodic monitoring of livestock condition and engage in diagnosis and animal treatment where required. The project will help re-stock by purchasing goats from areas of South Darfur with surplus. Improved male goats will be procured from certified suppliers and distributed to the villages improve the quality of offspring from the newly supplied goats and those already available in the villages. To increase production and productivity in the targeted villages, the project will provide beneficiaries with agricultural inputs including field crop and vegetable seed, and hand tools to enable them to grow crops for consumption. The distribution will go hand in hand with training in crop husbandry and vegetable production with the support of government technical staff.

Impact on environment/natural resources

The project uses climate smart agricultural technics to help communities adapt to climate variability. These include planting of improved short season crop varieties, crop spacing and diversification, and soil and water conservation. Establishment of nurseries and using the seedlings in the afforestration efforts will mitigate the impact of climate change.Natural resource management is part of the project as soil and water conservation measures in crop production are aimed at preserving the environment while at the same time preserving soil fertility that contributes to increase production.
