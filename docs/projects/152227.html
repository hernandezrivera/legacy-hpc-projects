Project Summary

OBJECTIVE: Improving the well-being and strengthening the resilience of extremely vulnerable displaced children, adolescent and their families affected by the humanitarian crisis in Borno State through psychosocial support, protection services, community based awareness raising activities and through strengthening the technical and project management capacity of local CP organizations with a special focus to reduce the prevalence of violence against children.

Needs

From the 24 October till the 4th of December 2017, Tdh has conducted several assessments on child protection and psychosocial distress among the conflict affected population in Dala Lawanti (Jere LGA), Mafa (Camp A, Camp B and the host communities), Damasak, Kukawa LGAs and some field visit to Rann, Banki and Bama. The findings of the assessments show that children suffers from psychosocial distress, stigmatization, corporal punishment, drug abuse, emotional harassment, sexual harassment, high incidence of sexual abuse and transactional sex is also reported. As example, in northern LGAs, 54% of key informants questioned by Tdh teams cited being aware of sexual violence against children and adolescents while 75% affirmed that children do manifest emotional and behavioral problems. Despite strong efforts, local actors can’t face all the most important identified needs of the children.

Activities

Community outreach teams will be trained on identifying, monitoring, and referring these cases to service providers, and consortium partners will provide awareness raising on risks, prevention practices and services available. Child Protection (CP) cases will receive individual case management and material assistance as needed, with protection case workers following up on developed case plans. Community-based and focused psychosocial support will be provided for children in child friendly spaces/clubs, while caregivers will be able to access safe spaces where they will participate in the design, implementation and monitoring of prevention activities in their communities.
