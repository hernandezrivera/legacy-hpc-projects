Project Summary

Provision of Emergency Health Interventions to the vulnerable Population (IDPs, returnees and the host community) project will focus on the major causes of morbidity and mortality in Nasir, Fashoda and Ulang. It will improve access to integrated basic health care and avert excess morbidity and mortality among 41,913 women, children and men. The project targets the most vulnerable population whose right to access health care services is in jeopardy following the continued inter-communal violence, floods, the Covid 19 pandemic and crisis in Sudan (Khartoum) and Ethiopian, Gambella. Of the targeted 37,022 people; there will be 6,917 host community, 6,253 IDPs, and 23,852 returnees respectively. The project will support provision of critical lifesaving interventions aligned to the Humanitarian Response Plan criteria and the South Sudan Basic Package of Health and Nutrition through a network of 26 PHCUs, 12 PHCCs, 2 Hospitals and 8 mobile clinics in hard to reach locations. The project activities will focus on prevention and case management of epidemic-prone diseases such as malaria, pneumonia, acute watery diarrhoea, cholera, and measles through both static and outreach services; provision of essential medicines and medical supplies accessed through the core pipeline; capacity building of staffs through on-job mentorship, disease surveillance including EVD and COVID 19, and routine monitoring of health information through strengthening the EWARS/DHIS2 system.
The clinical package will include; Health education and promotion, curative outpatient and inpatient services, routine immunization including supplementary immunization activities focusing on measles and polio, Maternal New-born and Child Health Services (MNCH), and HIV/TB services including e-MTCT. The project will integrate Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) at PHCCs and Hospital; management of severe cases of malnutrition with medical complication at the stabilisation centres; identification, and referral for Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS); and Water Hygiene and Sanitation (WASH) at health facilities.
This project will contribute to addressing the needs of the 148,083 of the estimated 3.2 million women, children and men that will need basic essential health services in South Sudan in the year 2024 and hopefully contribute to a reduction in high maternal mortality rates (789 deaths per 100,000 live births) and under five mortality rates (90.7 deaths per 1,000 live births) in women and children and mitigate the transmission of vaccine preventable and other infectious diseases.

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to face severe health crisis due to multiple factors. About 8.9 million people in the country need one or more forms of humanitarian assistance, especially in the counties most affected by IDPs (Internally Displaced Persons), returnees and disease outbreaks(Measles). The health system in South Sudan is unable to cope with the emergencies and provide life-saving services. It relies heavily on international aid and faces many challenges, such as lack of funding, staff, medicines, and equipment. The people suffer from hunger, violence, and poor health. Women, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities are the most vulnerable groups. They have limited access to health care and face high risks of death and illness In 2024, the humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that more than two-thirds of South Sudan's population, 8.9 million people, are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 since 2021 of which 5.5 million will require health interventions of which 50% are mainly females and 52% children. The country’s health system and infrastructure are among the poorest in the world. Health facilities are poorly equipped and staffed. There is a severe shortage of trained health professionals in all categories. Thus, people rely on inadequately trained or low skilled health workers to provide their healthcare. A quarter of internally displaced and returnees reside in settlements further than 5km from a functional health facility. The Sudan crisis that began on April 15, 2023, caused a rapid influx of returnee populations into Upper Nile through the Renk Border Point. Fashoda County has been one of the most affected as it borders Sudan to North (South Kordofan State) and is one of the transit states for persons escaping conflict in Sudan. The returnees integrated themselves into the host communities and IDP settlements. In eastern Upper Nile there has been an increased influx of returnees from the refugee camps in Gambella Ethiopia. An estimated 14,000 South Sudanese returnees who fled the ongoing conflict in Sudan are classified in Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) phase 5 (Catastrophe). Upper Nile State has 56% of its population facing Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) phase 3 or worse. In the three counties of Nasir, Ulang and Fashoda an estimated 148,083 South Sudanese returnees who fled the ongoing conflict in Sudan and are returing from Ethiopia are classified in Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) phase 5 (Catastrophe). Upper Nile State has 56% of its population facing Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) phase 3 or worse. Nasir and Fashoda county, the location of the proposed intervention are classified as Phase 3 (Crisis level). (IPC South Sudan Acute Food Insecurity Malnutrition Sep2023_July2024_report). The situation has severely disrupted access to essential lifesaving services while the accessible facilities have registered high increments in common morbidities such as diarrhea (20,153), Malaria (58,231) and respiratory infections (34,265) leading to severe shortages of essential lifesaving medicines. 2. Project activities

UNKEA will prioritize the delivery of life-saving health services in line with the Humanitarian Response Plan. The focus will be on providing primary health care that is aligned to the South Sudan Basic Package of Health and Nutrition through a network of 26 PHCUs (9 Nasir, 4 Ulang,13 Fashoda ), 12 PHCCs (5 Nasir, 3 Ulang,4 Fashoda), 2 hospitals (Nasir and Kodok), and 8 mobile clinics.The clinical package will include; (a) Conduct Emergency preparedness and response: UNKEA will build train and mentor health workers to effectively implement infection prevention and control in the facilities, perform early reporting of suspected cases, collect samples and facilitate preliminary isolation of suspected highly contagious cases including COVID 19. UNKEA will ensure that supported health workers compile quality data for submission in the EWARS/DHIS platforms. (b) Provide case management for common causes of morbidity. UNKEA will train, equip and facilitate health workers to provide static and mobile curative outpatient and inpatient services focusing on common causes of morbidity such as malaria, pneumonia, diarrhea, and measles. Support will include the provision of incentives, medicines, medical equipment, HMIS tools, and job aids. (c) Provide routine and supplementary immunization activities. UNKEA will support the health workers to provide both routine and supplementary immunization activities targeting vaccine-preventable diseases. Support will include training on Immunization in Practice, developing and implementation of micro plans, requesting and management of vaccines, and monitoring of uptake of vaccine services.(d) Support management of SAM with medical complications. UNKEA will train clinical officers at the supported PHCCs and hospitals to manage cases of SAM with medical complications at the established stabilization centers. The project will work closely with the nutrition departments and ensure the establishment of a robust referral and follow-up mechanism(e) Strengthen WASH in health facilities. The project will support the establishment of gender-disaggregated pit latrines, construction of incinerators and placenta pits, provision of handwashing facilities, training of health workers in Infection Prevention and Control, and provision of waste segregation buckets. In addition, handwashing will be promoted at all health education sessions. UNKEA will also integrate assessments for WASH at health facilities in its implementation activities. (f) Strengthen sexual and gender-based violence services through training in CMR: UNKEA will ensure that all clinical staff especially the clinical officers are able to provide CMR services. The health workers will be provided with CMR kits received from UNFPA, and GBV registers for easy documentation. (g) Provision of Mental Health and Psychosocial Services (MHPSS). UNKEA will train health workers in the identification, referral, and management of patients with mental health issues. Health workers at the lower facilities (PHCUs) will focus on identification and referral, while those at the PHCC and hospital will manage the referred cases. (h) Monitor uptake of services by special groups. The project will ensure that special groups such as old people, women, and people living with disabilities are reached and are using services. Tools such as the disability tracking tools will be used to monitor service uptake.i) Conduct health awareness and promotional activities
