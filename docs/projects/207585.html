Project Summary

The proposed project aims to protection in Emergencies for wellbeing of Rohingya refugee girls, boys and adolescents and their caregivers and host communities which are aligned with sector objective it also contributes to the protected from all forms of violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect through strong child protection mechanism and well-coordinated age, gender, and disability responsive child protection services. The intervention will contribute to the empowerment, basic rights, and well-being of girls, boys, and adolescents and their caregivers in Rohingya camps (1w, 2E, 2w, 5, 6, 7, and 10) and host communities in Cox's Bazar, and the target group will be selected for discussions with camp management, the Sector and Child Protection Sub-sector (CPSS), and host authorities. The main selection criteria will focus on girls and boys aged 3–12 years, adolescents aged 13–18 years, who have no access to child protection services with persons with disabilities, female-headed households, and people from the LGBTQ community (if applicable).
The project set of activities are establish and strengthen community-based child protection structure, provide recreational activities through group activities for boys and girls, Early Childhood Development (ECD) Intervention, provide structured-sustained psychosocial support through group activities boys and girls, Train community-based child protection structure members on child protection approaches, capacity building training for CP actors and non-CP actors and relevant stakeholders. Overall, this project is an essential step towards creating a safer and more secure environment for our future generations. The project 1. identification of CP cases in their territory; 2. identification of individual children, adolescents, and youth at risk of CP; and 3. consultation with their respective families and caregivers, as well as communication with relevant service providers. 3. Conduct regular meetings and discuss the status of CP in their community; 4. Support the organization of courtyard sessions, day observation, campaigns, and other awareness-raising activities on CP issues. 5. Regularly coordinate and collaborate with authorities, relevant sectors, and actors
The project will also focus on developing protective skills for vulnerable children, adolescents, and youth and motivating stakeholders to act to prevent violence against children (VAC) issues and respond to individual cases as well. The project will focus on reducing the risks of children, adolescents, and youth from disasters and hazards by providing community-based awareness raise at camp level on child protection with specific focus on relevant child protection issues within a given community.The project’s monitoring approach will reflect the dual purposes of learning and accountability for results, within a challenging operational context. During project implementation, Educo-Bangladesh and project partners will engage the target population through formal and informal consultations and data collection exercises with adolescent girls and boys, women and men, using a gender-integrated and child-friendly approach with appropriate safeguarding. Develop a detailed monitoring plan, referencing expected results, indicators, and activities as defined in the project's (probability mass function) PMF. The Educo MEAL team, with support from Educo HQ, will retain overall responsibility for the development, oversight, and use of the MEAL system, in full collaboration with the partners.
The project is being implemented by Educo together with four local partner organizations: JCF, FIVDB, SKUS, and GUK. As per J-MSNA 2023 CPSS findings, the project aims to contribute to strengthening better protection from all forms of violence, abuse, exploitation, and neglect through strong child protection systems and well-coordinated age, gender, and disability-responsive child protection services.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

As per CPIMS, child protection concerns are based in camps. 35.91% neglect, 11.73% psychological distress, 9.88% physical abuse 8.07% child labor and 5.60% physical disability or impairment. Moreover, 66.5% of children never participated in the child protection program for refugees. 81.6% from the host community, 3.1% from refugees, and 3.3% from the host community reported children (<18) residing in the out-of-home household for different reasons: married and left the house (43.4%), left the house to seek employment (32.1%), compared to host married and left the house (4.5%), left the house to seek employment (17.1%). 3.6% of children (below 18 years old) are working to contribute to HH income; 3.8% are hosts. Considering the above problems, the initiative will address the identified needs of children and women in Rohingya camps and host communities by ensuring access to their basic protection and education rights consistent with the humanitarian principles of humanity, neutrality, impartiality, and independence. The focus will be on providing high-quality child protection services to children at risk of abuse, neglect, violence, exploitation, and severe distress. The children will lead awareness-raising campaigns on child protection. It will facilitate access to learning opportunities in protective and inclusive environments, act to continue the prtection of children who have no access to child protection services, sensitize families and communities on the importance of girls' education, rehabilitate learning spaces, and provide teaching and educational materials.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project will enhance protection mainstreaming, which involves integrating protection principles to ensure access, safety, and dignity in humanitarian efforts as well as to ensure child protection services in the camps and adjacent host communities. The project will plan to minimize harm and enhance well-being, reducing vulnerability based on priority. Besides that, the projects should also encompass the Protection Mainstreaming Framework's eight core components according to CPSS guidelines.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Educo has been mandated by its core values, policy and procedures to be transparently accountable to children and and ensure thisr accountability through proper education and and ensure safty and child friendly space in learning center.In line with this, in this project we will apply a set of accountability tools and techniques to those we will work with and for (children, parents and other stakeholders). A space will be created for them to express their opinion on the project, its activities and its impact, and for Educo to use the feedback to continuously improve. The national Suggestions, Complaints and Compliments (SCC) committee in the country office will develop and introduce the SCC mechanism to receive and respond to SCCs and adjust feedback regularly. After consultation with the participants, a proper feedback mechanism will be installed in the CPMs, relevant spaces and partner offices. Educo and project partners will have a common agenda at project meetings, training/workshops and other events, to receive questions regarding SCCs and respond accordingly. In addition improved enrroleled and attending rate through providing quality child protection service as well as engage their parents.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Educo has established and practice child friendly PSEA policy where all children more access are smoothly as well as To ensure staff uses their power responsibly and upholds the principles set by codes of conduct when engaging the affected communities, in particular any forms of sexual abuse and exploitation (SEA), Educo has established the Suggestions Complaint and Commendation (SCC) system to receive feedback and ensure all PSEA cases are based on the policy. In addition we trained a team for instant reporting on PSEA issues .
