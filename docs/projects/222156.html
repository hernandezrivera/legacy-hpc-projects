Project Summary

Over the coming year 2025, DRC through its country-wide mobile and area-based response approaches, plans to improve access to shelter and non-food items assistance to populations affected by new and protracted displacements due to natural disasters and man-made crises in hard-to-reach locations across South Sudan and static areas respectively. These will include newly displaced, returnees and host community populations facing difficulty in accessing shelter and NFI assistance for a dignified life. To effectively combat exposure to adverse weather conditions, infection risk, and other protection concerns, DRC is committed to providing lifesaving emergency shelter and non-food items to populations affected by emergencies across the country as a decisive first line intervention. Furthermore, through the DRC’s static programs, essential emergency and transitional shelter assistance to vulnerable protracted IDPs, returnees, and host communities in Upper Nile, Unity and Abyei. Very cognizant of issues related to HLP, DRC will work in collaboration with key stakeholders including the line ministry and community leaders especially host communities to ensure concerns related to HLP are tackled before installation of emergency and transitional shelters especially in new and also protracted displacement locations for a positive coexistence.
DRC will ensure a needs-based intervention through a thorough needs analysis. These will be done in coordination with both the state and the national SNFI clusters and other relevant coordination mechanisms including NWAG and ICCGs to ensure that assistance is provided to the most in need populations. As a state lead coordinator for SNFI, DRC will continue its coordination role in Malakal as the lead partner for the Upper Nile state SNFI sub-cluster coordinator.

1. Needs

Just over 6 years since the signing of the revitalized peace Agreement in 2018, South Sudan’s humanitarian needs still remain high, with 9.3 million expected to be in need in 2025. Out of these, about 4.2 million individuals will need SNFI assistance which is nearly a doubled increase from previous year which stood at 2.2 million. Increased displacement from Sudan, severe flooding, and economic turmoil have led to a growing number of people in need of humanitarian assistance in South Sudan. These drivers of acute humanitarian needs seem unlikely to subside in 2025. According to UNOCHA South Sudan flood snapshot dated November 8th, 2024, 1.4 million people were affected by flooding in 43 counties and Abyei Administrative area, with Jonglei and Northern Bahr el Ghazal states comprising more than 51 per cent of the affected population. Over 379,000 individuals are displaced in 22 counties and Abyei. In the recent flood interventions, the IDPs stated that the water levels would remain high until February. Many homes are submerged in water, and most of the shelters have collapsed. This means that after the water level goes down, IDPs still need to reconstruct their shelters. With the grass for thatching shelters destroyed by the flooding, reconstruction will be a challenge unless the flood-affected IDPs are supported with emergency shelter materials such as plastic sheets for covering the roofs of the shelters. Malaria has also been reported in Jonglei, Unity, Upper Nile, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Central Equatoria and Western Equatoria states. This has overwhelmed the health system and exacerbated the situation and impact in flood-affected areas. According to the recent South Sudan flood snapshot and severity mapping of November, about 35 counties have been ranged in categories 1 and 2 as being severely affected and will need immediate humanitarian assistance. Since June 2024, severe flooding has exacerbated humanitarian challenges, destroying crops and critical infrastructure and displacing tens of thousands of people posing both immediate and long-term risks to public health and livelihoods. Poor agricultural production may trigger an atypically early onset of the 2025 lean season and could worsen already critical rates of acute malnutrition, which, according to available data, have reached their highest prevalence since the 2013- 2018 crisis in some areas and are rising across the states. Priority should be given to food, shelter, and healthcare, which were consistently identified by the flood-affected IDPs as the urgent unmet needs in most areas. Cognizant of these highlighted needs and the funding limitations, the influx of over 800,000 returnees to South Sudan as well as the displacement of 1.4 million with flooding in 2024, the SNFI cluster through its partners plans to target 1.37 million individuals out of 4.2 million individuals in need of SNFI assistance in 2025. With an intent to mitigate against exposure to adverse weather conditions, infection vectors and other protection risks, DRC plans to provide lifesaving emergency shelter and NFIs to emergency affected populations as a first line immediate intervention Country-wide while through its static programing, DRC plans to provide emergency and transitional shelters assistance to vulnerable protracted IDPs, returnees and host communities facing inability to meet their basic shelter and NFI needs in Upper Nile, Unity and Abyei. DRC will effectively implement an area-based approach through its strategically situated offices in Malakal, Ajuongthok, Bentiu, and tentatively Abyei. This strategy is designed to decisively address both emergency and transitional shelter needs for individuals living in former PoC sites and long-term displacement areas. that have witness significant influx of returnees from the Sudan crises.

2. Project activities

Activity 1: DRC, through its mobile response approach, plans to conduct cluster coordinated emergency SNFI needs analysis and responses in new and protracted displacement locations. Priority will be given to hard-to-reach areas with severe and critical shelter/NFI needs and high needs for other urgent humanitarian assistance as categorized by the SNFI state focal points person, SNFI national cluster, and shelter operation working group. The SNFI needs analysis findings will then inform the appropriate course of action. DRC plans to conduct a total of five (05) needs assessments in conflict, flood, and returnees concentrated in hard-to-reach locations across South Sudan during the project period. Activity 2: DRC-MRT will conduct the distribution of emergency shelter and NFI assistance through its mobile response modality. Preceded by the need’s analysis and approval by the S/NFIs cluster. DRC’s mobile response team will deploy a response team to conduct beneficiary verification and registration using the cluster targeting criteria. The most vulnerable among the affected population will be targeted. These will include PWDs, persons with serious and chronic medical conditions, child-headed households, female-headed households, elderly without support, pregnant and lactating women, households with a high number of dependents, single parents taking care of many children, et cetera. Due to the high humanitarian needs, and where appropriate, DRC will use its own stock or Core Pipeline items to respond to critical emergency Shelter and NFI needs as flagged by the cluster across South Sudan. The Shelter and NFI kit will comprise the items in the cluster standard S/NFI kit. . DRC plans to respond with both its own stock and emergency SNFI martials from the core pipeline. Activity 3: Emergency shelter construction. Through its area-based approach in Upper Nile, Unity and Abyei DRC will construct emergency shelters to provide safe and appropriate living spaces for new displacements recorded within the intervention locations. Structures will be adapted to local context for acceptance while using locally available materials that reduces vulnerability to protection risks and extreme weather conditions. Activity 4: Construction of transitional shelters. Employing our area-based approach in DRC’s static locations outlined above, DRC will construct transitional shelters for vulnerable persons targeting mainly persons with specific needs. Locally sourced and environmentally friendly materials will be used, and the wall structure mudded with earth, including door fittings, to provide security and privacy. Besides sourcing construction materials locally, DRC will also use local labor for the construction of these structures as this can enhance access to income for the local community members. Part of this cost accounting for the local materials and un-skilled labor based on the bill of quantity will be delivered as direct cash to the beneficiaries after providing the required local materials and labor for the construction. Activity 5: Repair and Rehabilitation of temporary shelters. DRC will conduct the repair and rehabilitation of emergency and transitional shelters in Malakal PoC in Upper Nile and Bentiu in Unity State . This is to enhance the privacy and protective environment of shelter structure mainly for persons with specific needs. This will include mudding the walls and replacing the lock on doors and windows.
