Project Summary

In response to the ongoing crisis, food insecurity and malnutrition rates in the selected counties, Aid Link Organization (ALO) seeks to support 40,000 IPC and crisis affected individuals, including women, girls and men with seasonal livelihood kits, food assistance for assets (ACL) while offering nutrient-dense supplementary food and capacity building.
In alignment with FSL Cluster objective 1 and in collaboration with WFP, ALO plans to support the crisis affected communities with different forms of food assistance including humanitarian or conditional food assistance and/or livelihoods support. The plan is to distribute cereal (sorghum), pulses, oil, and salt to 15,000 HH (6,000 women, 4500 men, 1500 boys and 3000 girls with at least 5% of these being PLWD) to address their immediate hunger needs, especially during the lean season, and save lives amongst the increasing number of people requiring urgent humanitarian assistance within the counties. ALO will also distribute a Multi-Purpose Cash through the financial service provider using the cash in envelope delivery mechanism, to most vulnerable households to meet their critical needs during the lean seasons. The intended impact of ACL is to contribute directly to achieving Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 2: End Hunger, achieve food security and improved nutrition, and promote sustainable agriculture, whilst simultaneously contributing to several other SDGs.
For 2024-2025 cropping seasons, ALO will offer composite support to 25,000 vulnerable households to enhance emergency food production through distribution of drought &pest tolerant seeds, farming equipment and fishing gear. Linked with the areas seasonality, these activities will be implemented either during the rain (MSR) or the dry season (DSR) periods. For the DSR, the livelihood support package will be composed of vegetable seeds and fishing equipment. For MSR, livelihood support package will be similar to DSR in addition of crop seeds and a farming tool.
Linked with FSL CO3, ALO also plans to train a selected percentage of farmers on adaptive food production, income generation skills targeting farmer field schools, small grants for petty trade for village savings and loan schemes targeting 2000HH (800 women, 600 men, 200 boys and 400 girls) in all the target counties.
ALO will mainstream protection in all the planned activities. Community leaders and AAP committees will be oriented on gender sensitive intervention so that they are aware and carry on the awareness within the community to prevent or mitigate the occurrence of gender-based violence related to the distributed inputs. Awareness sessions will be conducted on the importance of gender and disability inclusion in the humanitarian assistance.

1. Needs

In South Sudan, the overall cereal deficit for January-December 2023 cropping year is estimated at about 485 400 tonnes, that is10.2% below the deficit estimated for 2022, and 2% below the 2018-2022 average (WFP/UNFAO CFSAM report 2022). Floods destroyed an estimated 130 000 hectares of farmed land, in 2022 resulting in the projected loss of 65 000 tons of cereals. This is going to have a detrimental effect on the impacted populations' ability to secure food.FSNMS R29 2023 indicated that more than 88% of new arrivals from Sudan crisis are more food insecure in comparison to the host communities. Majority of them (87%) had poor or borderline food consumption in the last 7 days. A report by FSC in May 2023 indicated that food and fuel prices inflation went up immediately after the Sudan crisis. The cost of the minimum food basket also increased in the returnees destinations. REACH assessments in Nasir indicates revenge killings, fighting between armed groups, and insecuritywith population displacement as the highest threat to food security and disruptions to livelihoods.An IRNA conducted in New Fangak/Phom Payam of Fangak County showed that by May 2023, Fangak County was hosting 315 HH returnees from Sudan dwelling under trees in deplorable conditions with no food and basic NFIs. The persistent IPC phase 3&4 in the South Sudan (selected locations included) is attributable to sporadic subnational conflict, underlying poor macro-economic conditions, limited income-generating opportunities, sluggish livelihood recovery prospects, and localized poor harvests (FEWSNET September 2023). In the recent IPC analysis, Canal/Pigi and Fangak Counties remain of high concern, where some households are likely still in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) due to multiple years of asset and livelihood erosion further worsened by the South Sudanese returnees and conflict-related disruption to trade flow with Sudan.Nutritional SMART survey conducted in June in Akobo East showed ‘Critical’ (15-29.9 percent) rates of acute malnutrition that was associated with early exhaustion of food stocks due to last year’s flooding and ongoing high food prices.Women and girls in Akobo, Uror, Nyirol and Canal/Pigi are expected to fulfil traditional gender roles, including taking care of children and elders, cooking, fetching clean water and collecting firewood. Especially during acute food insecurity periods, it is normal for women and adolescent girls, in their primary caregiving roles, to be deprioritized during internal allocation of meals within their households. Chronic poverty and acute food insecurity continues to be the driving force of child- and/or forced marriage faced by adolescent girls, as they are more likely to generate higher dowries and increase the overall asset base of their families, providing a temporary economic reprieve

2. Project activities

1.0. Activities to enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock supportActivity 1.1. Asset creation and food distribution for 14500 foods insecure HHs in Akobo, Fangak, Canal/Pigi, Uror and Nyirol CountiesActivity 1.2. Complimentary agricultural inputs provided to vulnerable women, men, girls and boys headed households in all the target Counties Rationale: Preposition livelihood inputs through FSL pipeline manager UNFAO. Activity 1.3. Conduct livestock diseases surveillance, treatment and vaccination campaigns, training for CAHWs, cold chain establishment and maintenance, control of common livestock disease conditions. Timelines: Targeting for mass treatment, vaccination campaigns will be maximizedduring the dry season when the cattle are easily accessible near the homesteads.Rationale: Training of CAHWs will be done in the dry season so as not to disrupt their agricultural activities at the onset of rains in May. The “one health” approach will be practiced in the treatment and awareness campaigns especially when dealing with zoonotic diseases in the target locations.2.0. Activities to Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks Activity 2.1 Support to seed fairs using locally sourced seed; seed security and multiplication strengthening/ Support market functionality assist traders tore-supply markets/ local procurement for FAO ITFs & aggregation of produce for WFP assistance Rationale: This will target to procure 1500 vegetable and crop kits locally sourced from farmers and traders in Akobo, Uror, Canal/Pigi and Fangak Counties. A voucher system will be employed for seeds procurement. Accountability with complaint and feedback mechanisms will ensure the inclusion of vulnerable target groups. Activity 2.2. Training and capacity buildingRationale: both climate adaptive and absorptive on-farm production skills will be enhanced for 10000 farmers, through training, establishment of vegetable and crops farmer schools mentor programs for the farmers in the selected countiesActivity 2.3. Support agriculture value chain addition and marketing for 5500 womenRationale: support to women-led village savings and loan groups, market information system, and reduction of post-harvest losses through the introduction of appropriate preservation technologies like hermetic storage bags.
