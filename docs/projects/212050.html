Project Summary

According to the HNO 2024, The situation in northwest Syria (NWS) is characterized by a combination of deteriorating economic conditions, ongoing impacts of conflict, and poor access to essential services, which continue to drive widespread humanitarian needs. Over 70% of families in the northeast and northwest regions struggle to afford basic needs, including shelter, food, and medicine. Many households, 37.7% in northeast Syria (NES) and 30.4% in NWS cannot meet basic needs even with full-time employment. Households in NWS face a severe income-expenditure gap, with monthly incomes at 85 USD and expenditures at 183 USD, resulting in a 53.5% shortage. The recent earthquake has led to a significant escalation in hardships, shifting from stable business-based livelihoods to more precarious casual and temporary jobs, resulting in decreased income and job stability.
In the health sector and waste management, Early Recovery and Livelihoods (ERL) activities aim to address immediate and protracted humanitarian needs by supporting the maintenance of and improving equitable access to essential services. This includes rehabilitating electricity networks, promoting renewable energy, and providing material and equipment support to service providers. Specifically, the sector focuses on the light rehabilitation of schools, hospitals, and infrastructure, prioritizing debris/waste removal and recycling to ensure safe service access for vulnerable groups.
The project proposal for northwest Syria addresses critical humanitarian needs by focusing on enhanced medical waste management through incineration in Mare and Daret Azza. These initiatives aim to prevent environmental contamination and improve healthcare services for over a million residents and internally displaced persons (IDPs). To ensure the project's success, protection risks, prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), and other risks, assumptions, and mitigation measures are carefully considered. This includes protecting vulnerable groups, such as women and children, from potential harm, training staff on PSEA, and implementing robust monitoring and evaluation systems to identify and address risks. Assumptions about political stability, access to resources, and community cooperation are also considered, with contingency plans in place to address potential challenges. Additionally, the project emphasizes community engagement and feedback mechanisms to ensure interventions meet beneficiaries' specific needs, promoting ownership and sustainability.
Main Objectives and Impact of the Project.
Enhanced Medical Waste Management through Incineration in Mare and Daret Azza:
Purpose: To effectively manage the escalating medical waste in regions heavily populated and burdened with IDPs, the project will operate two incinerators. These facilities will address the increased medical waste in Mare and Daret Azza, where the need for waste disposal is acute due to the high population density and IDPs.
Outcome: The incinerators are set to manage a significant volume of medical waste, with an annual target of approximately 282.6 metric tons, excluding weekends. about more than a healt hwaste mangement for more than one million person will be burned through these two incinerators.
To conduct capacity-building activities for female health workers, SRD will first assess the current skills and knowledge health gaps through surveys or interviews. Based on this assessment, we will develop tailored training programs and workshops focusing on maternal health, reproductive health, and gender-sensitive care topics. These activities will be conducted in accessible locations, considering female participants' specific needs and constraints. We will also provide mentorship opportunities and on-the-job training to reinforce learning and ensure the practical application of new skills. Additionally, we will collaborate with local partners and stakeholders to leverage resources and maximize the impact.
