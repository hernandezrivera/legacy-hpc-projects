Project Summary

The multifaceted crisis in Syria creates continuous cycles of vulnerability, disproportionately impacting those most in need, in 2024 about 16.7 million people are assessed to need humanitarian assistance across Syria.
As for food security and agricultural sector there are 15.4 million in need in 2024 (HNO)
The rise in prices of agricultural inputs, such as agricultural tools, seeds, fodder, and veterinary medicines, all of which led to a decline in the agricultural sector, in addition to the need is still to provide farmers with agricultural trainings that will help the adoption of more effective climate smart agriculture (CSA) approaches and good agricultural practices (GAP), This will help crises -affected farmers enhance or boost food production despite the high cost of agro-chemicals (FAO- Agricultural Inputs and Commodity Price
Bulletin in the Syrian Arabic Republic, September 2023–Issue #21) Daraa - As-Sanamayn, and Homs - Al-Qusayr is one of the neediest areas for agricultural services.
GOPA-DERD will implement activities which follow conservation agriculture (CA) and climate-smart agricultural approaches. Activities will improve the utilization of resources, by growing certified and adaptive seeds and applying innovative agricultural practices; Practical technical trainings in Farmer Field
Schools (FFS) will allow farmers to apply practices that reduce the cost of farming and improve the quality and quantity of crop yields, which will be reflected in their market values by setting up food processing businesses, vulnerable community members without access to land will receive access to income generating activities. In some areas project will also coordinate with the ERL Sector and the (CWG) to ensure that CVA, (CFW) in food processing activities. The beneficiaries of food processing units will get cash to purchase the raw materials needed for their works once at the beginning.
The selected areas based on HNO 2024 will be Homs/ Qusair and Daraa/ Al-Sanamayn
Number of direct beneficiaries from this project will be (340) and indirect (1000 ben).
GOPA-DERD also will implement light rehabilitation and/ or creation of water harvesting
and irrigation systems in selected locations and enhance the capabilities of farmers in marketing.
Risk assumptions and mitigating measures:
-Relations within communities worsen due to tensions over resources, poorly understood selection criteria: project team will follow a do-no-harm approach, the selection process will be transparent. Criteria will be based on needs, in order to prevent selecting the farmers.
-Agricultural production might be negatively impacted by climate/weather conditions: Consistent monitoring of crop yields and rain levels. Activities will focus on enhanced water saving techniques, following climate-smart agricultural approaches.
Animal and plant diseases: Provision of vet services and awareness. Same as, utilization of diclofenac-free medications and Farmers will receive locally adapted plant varieties.
Risks of PSEA and strategy:
- Sexual exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries by suppliers or humanitarian personnel
- There are unreported cases of victims:
GOPA-DERD is committed to protecting all staff, beneficiaries, partners and other individuals it collaborates with from sexual exploitation and abuse at all times especially vulnerable young adults, women and children who are particularly at risk of sexual exploitation and abuse. GOPA-DERD takes seriously all the complaints and concerns about SEA with providing immediate and confidential mechanisms for reporting potential or actual SEA through: Staff members of all levels who have reason to believe that a SEA incident may occur within the organization or through another cooperating local or international employee must report either through a specific e-mail used solely for this purpose, or, by calling the SEA hotline to report violations, they will receive the appropriate follow-up by PSEA FP.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Overview of activities: Activity 2.1: distributing of agricultural inputs, the inputs will include seeds, fertilizer, pesticide, and equipment; coupled with related training like (Farmer Field School: vegetable farming, Fruit trees farming):In Homs/Al-Quseir 75 beneficiaries, in Daraa/Al-Sanamayn 75 beneficiaries, they will get seeds, fertilizer, seedling, pesticide and equipment, and training within FFSActivity 2.3: increase livestock production – this will include provision of small livestock, animal feed distribution, artificial insemination, targeting vulnerable small – scale breeders, including the provision of related training like (Farmer Field School: livestock breeding, Poultry breeding): In Homs/Al-Quseir 50 beneficiaries, in Daraa/Al-Sanamayn 100 beneficiaries.They will get fodder for their animals Activity 2.4: provide emergency livestock treatment, seasonal vaccinations, and training for veterinary services, including community animal health workers:In Homs/Al-Quseir 50 beneficiaries, in Daraa/Al-Sanamayn 100 beneficiaries. They will get treatment, and veterinary services and Optimal care training within FFS.Activity 2.5: support market driven income- generating activities (IGAs) , including well- adapted vocational training (for example, food processing, input production units, etc):Create 2 units in each sub district: In Homs/Al-Quseir and Daraa/Al-Sanamayn.They will take cash for work to purchase the raw materials needed for food processing, once at the start of work and they well get vocational and business training.Activity 3.2: support light rehabilitation of irrigation system, irrigation canals, wells, water and system:Depending on the need in the areas, the required irrigation rehabilitation will be determined. It is possible to rehabilitate simple agricultural irrigation channels or install collective irrigation pumps that operate on solar energy or equip water harvesting ponds.Activity 3.1.4: Establish/ strengthen collective productive units at community level (food processing, fodder, compost, dairy collection / processing, governance, renewable energy, and training):- Marketing training: implementing one training for beneficiaries covering a maximum of 20 beneficiaries in Homs/Al-Quseir 20 beneficiaries, in Daraa/Al-Sanamayn 20 beneficiaries. - Organizing markets: 1 event in each sub-district:Organizing a bazar to promote beneficiaries’ products (including the costs of transporting them to the popular markets, materials, brochures and other needed logistics) Inviting all beneficiaries (including farmers and beneficiaries of food processing units to encourage them to start-up their business. Prepare some promotional corners in local markets or Malls to link with local community and potential merchants to promote their products.The total direct beneficiaries of the activities: 340 - Homs (Qusair): 145 beneficiaries - Daraa (Al-Sanamayn): 195 beneficiaries.The indirect beneficiaries are 1000.
