Project Summary

The 2021 Syria Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) sets out the framework within which the humanitarian community will respond to large-scale humanitarian and protection needs in Syria throughout 2021, flexibly leveraging the various response modalities. Capturing these capacities and efforts under a single strategic framework, the plan aims to increase the effectiveness of the response by improving the identification of needs and gaps, reducing overlap and duplication, and monitoring context changes and progress against collective agreed response results. The Humanitarian Coordinator for Syria and the Regional Humanitarian Coordinator for the Syria Crisis lead and coordinate humanitarian action inside Syria. OCHA, in line with its global mandate, supports the humanitarian leadership in effectively coordinating the response inside Syria. To do so, OCHA maintains country offices in Syria and Turkey, and a regional office in Amman. OCHA requires sustained levels of funding to be able to maintain and strengthen effective humanitarian response coordination from these various offices, facilitate strategic and operational coordination platforms, provide information management services, manage pooled funds, support humanitarian access analysis and negotiation, ensure civil-military coordination, and provide public information and advocacy on the
humanitarian situation. Funding requirements for this project reflect the needs of OCHA's three offices directly involved in the Syria response.

4 - Needs

Humanitarian needs in Syria remain large-scale, multi-layered and extensive after years ofhostilities. An estimated 13.4 million people continue to require some form of humanitarian assistance, with 6 million people in extreme and catastrophic need across Syria. An estimated 6.7 million people remained internally displaced, with around 448,000 spontaneous IDP return movements recorded in 2020.The UN estimates that in 2020 close to 90 per cent of the population lived below the poverty line, and 60-65 per cent in extreme poverty, with pockets of acute and chronic malnutrition persisting in certain areas.Despite some reduction in violence, Syria remains a major protection crisis, with civiliansexposed to multiple protection risks related to ongoing hostilities; the effects of new andprotracted displacement; and the depletion of socio-economic resources triggeringnegative coping strategies.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

While not directly implementing response activities, OCHA will continue to support effective mainstreaming of protection throughout the wider humanitarian response, including all stages of the humanitarian programme cycle, as well as advocacy on protection priorities as identified by the humanitarian leadership in coordination with protection partners.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

OCHA recognizes the risks of instrumentalization of aid in the Syrian context and the effects this may have on the protection of vulnerable people. OCHA also recognizes risks related to GBV, SEA, and safety of humanitarian workers and the potential negative affect this may have on affected populations. OCHA will support inter-agency efforts to mitigate protection risks for humanitarian workers, such as the SSG-endorsed Duty of Care Framework for humanitarian organizations working in Syria, and the work of the WoS PSEA network. OCHA will support and strengthen inter-agency PSEA mechanisms to mitigate the risk of sexual exploitation of abuse by humanitarian workers. OCHA will continue to disseminate information to its staff on the zero tolerance for SEA and sensitize them regularly about the need to prevent any SEA. This includes facilitating all staff completion of mandatory training on PSEA; facilitate that all new and current staff in the field sign the SG’s Bulletin; Conduct regular internal dialogue with all staff to ensure ongoing awareness. Moreover, OCHA will also continue deepening the mainstreaming of PSEA into humanitarian processes, through integration of PSEA messaging into key multisectoral tools, assessments, and guidance documents used by organizations in the WoS response. Mainstreaming PSEA through humanitarian planning and programming also includes ensuring that the ISG, SSG and HLG engage in strategic PSEA discussions in support of advocacy efforts.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

OCHA will strive to mitigate the above-mentioned adverse effects by promoting principled humanitarian action from all the hubs involved in the Syria response and quality programming by partners. It will do so in collaboration with humanitarian partners, including the Protection sector and its associated AoRs, and the PSEA advisor. It will also continue to put affected populations at the centre of the humanitarian response and use feedback collected from affected communities to better visibilize and meet needs. The potential non-renewal of the Security Council Resolution enabling UN humanitarian response from Turkey into Syria would result in a significantly altered response and coordination environment, for which OCHA in conjunction with partners is engaging in planning to mitigate.
