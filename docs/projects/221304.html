Project Summary

This initiative aims to provide quality alternative education to displaced children in the Southwest region of Cameroon with a view to their reintegration into the formal education system. This will be achieved through the deployment of a nine-month accelerated education program for out-of-school children aged 9 to 12, teacher training, improvement of learning conditions, both in terms of host structures and psychological support, and improved access to protection services, including civil documentation and specialized protection services (CP). Finally, and in order to ensure the continued schooling of children after their reintegration into formal school, the intervention will support vulnerable parents, including single-parent families, widowed and single parents, households of more than 5 children, households with people living with disabilities and elderly guardians, in developing or strengthening their livelihoods and improving their living conditions to limit the impact of the socio-economic environment on children's access to their rights."
Goal: To provide quality education to displaced children in Southwest Cameroon.
Methods:
Nine-month accelerated education program.
Teacher training.
Improved learning conditions (infrastructure, psychological support).
Enhanced access to protection services.
Long-term goal: To ensure children remain in school after reintegration by supporting their families.
Target beneficiaries: Out-of-school children aged 9-12, particularly those from vulnerable households.
In essence, the program seeks to provide a comprehensive solution to the educational challenges faced by displaced children in Southwest Cameroon, addressing not only their immediate educational needs but also the underlying socio-economic factors that might hinder their learning.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

In September 2024, the Northwest (NW) and Southwest (SW) regions had 583,113 internally displaced persons. Between January and August 2024, 34 direct attacks on schools were recorded. These attacks, coupled with socioeconomic vulnerabilities, the separation of children from their families due to the crisis, and lack of access to civil documents, have contributed to the out-of-schooling of over 210,108 children in the Southwest region. Out-of-school children are particularly exposed to protection risks, including gender-based violence, discrimination, exploitation, psychological distress, and limited access to basic rights and services. Both boys and girls are severely affected by the impact of the crisis on education, especially unaccompanied children. According to IOM MSNA dated October 2024, education is the most urgent need of displaced persons in the Fako department of the Southwest region, particularly in peri-urban areas that host the largest number of displaced persons. Access to education for vulnerable children has been hindered by the destruction of educational infrastructure in villages of origin, the lack of civil and legal documents, and the absence of safety nets for effective reintegration. Although nearly 560 public schools are still operational in the Southwest, re-enrollment has been mainly hindered by the inability of parents to pay school fees. Also, 64% of the region's population needs access to civil documentation to access basic services, including education. Access to civil registration documents (including birth certificates and national identity cards) has become increasingly difficult for children due to the government's withdrawal from many villages. Affected populations have prioritized other needs and their purchasing power has decreased, preventing them from paying administrative and transportation fees associated with obtaining documents. Some documents have also been lost during attacks and/or population displacement. The absence of a birth certificate limits children's access to formal education, as they cannot register for exams or enter secondary education without a birth certificate. For children whose birth was not declared within the first 90 days, the procedure for obtaining a birth certificate is complex and costly and requires a supplementary judgment. Moreover, the humanitarian situation has had a significant impact on the living conditions of vulnerable families and has not allowed parents and guardians to prioritize their children's education over the past 7 years. Despite efforts by various humanitarian actors to meet the educational needs of children in these regions, only 46% of the required education funding for 2024 has been covered two months before the end of the year. Given the educational needs in the Anglophone regions and the need to adopt a more agile approach, the Norwegian Refugee Council (NRC) intends to adapt the Accelerated Learning Strategy based on the Anglophone curriculum currently under approval, reducing it to one school year to allow children to reintegrate into primary school within one year. This reduction in duration will reduce the cost of the intervention and allow it to be replicated in many schools.
