Project Summary

Our intervention includes distribution of agriculture inputs, train local farmers on how to use tools and new techniques and skills. This project will provide an opportunity to the most vulnerable households including both males and female to enhance their agricultural techniques and skills and improve their capacity to generate their own incomes and to adopt to shocks. The purpose is to enhance and strengthen community coping mechanisms to be able to generate their own incomes through agricultural activities using new skills. The project will target 7000 households internally displaced persons (IDPs), Returnees, Host Community and local community affected by crisis in Payinjiar (Ganyliel and Nyal) .

1 - Needs

Based on IPC report 2019, Greater Panyijiar rated under IPC 3 because of shortage of food, nutrition concern, insecurity and flooding that have disrupted the households means of livelihood and have decreased their capacity and ability to produce and make them depend entirely on food assistance and provision of agricultural inputs. This project is to address food insecurity, nutrition issues and empower most vulnerable households with new skills, provision of agricultural inputs and tools. it will also address dependency on food and agricultural input to strengthen household ability in Greater Panyijiar and Mayendit South through development of skills of local black smiths to produce agricultural tools locally. With the majority of Household directly or indirectly dependent on local-level production in small scale and humanitarian food assistance, climate variability presents a particular threat to human-well being and food security. There are scale-dependent relationships that must be considered in assessing the vulnerability of household to climate variability. This project will present methods for analyzing food security and household resilience at rural level. In particular, conceptual approaches are to articulate the resilience of most vulnerable household at community level.
