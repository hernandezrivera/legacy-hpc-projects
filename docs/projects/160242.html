Project Summary

Based on Sept 2019 projection IPC report released and FSL Cluster Food Security Context and drivers of food insecurity. FSNMS,FSL Cluster weekly updates, assessments, REACH multi-sector factsheets updates, FEWSNET,FAO /WFP operational updates indicates that food security situation continues to deteriorate caused by different drivers.
ASCO will respond basing on the cluster objectives. The crisis affected population are severely food insecure and increased nutritional requirements. Focused Counties Aweil East, Wau, Jur River and Yirol East Counties are classified as IPC Phase 4 with GAM prevalence of 15.0 – 29.9% based on IPC key messages released on Sept 2019.
The ASCO’s focus geographical area of response are classified in emergency IPC Phase 3 &4 acute food insecurity. The food security and nutrition needs has increased significantly driven by inter related factors including rising food insecurity, high morbidity rates, limited access to safe water and sanitation resulting to negative impact on infant and young child feeding practices and women.
It’s important to respond by provide lifesaving management of food insecurity and acute malnutrition for the most vulnerable groups and at risk by rebuilding the communities resilience through assets preservation to vulnerable population considering gender to save lives and improve on food security.
Women and children are severely food insecure and have increased nutritional requirements. Aggravating factors for malnutrition have become more prevalent. This continues to worsen people’s food insecurity and nutritional status. The GAM level being above the emergence threshold GAM prevalence above 9.9%. The focused Counties affected with IPC phase 3 & 4 with severity of acute malnutrition GAM rate of >15% and increased nutritional requirements.
Priority will be given to vulnerable beneficiaries, influx of internally displaced persons, returnees and host communities. People at risk of malnutrition in crisis affected areas, young children and PLW are not able to meet their basic nutrition requirements. ASCO will Support in crop, vegetable and fisheries production to increase food production to combat food insecurity.
ASCO will increasingly recognize the response needs of different groups: IDPs, vulnerable host population, returnees and persons with disabilities will be prioritized with focus on food production for the vulnerable households by enhancing food production through complementary livelihood kits by strengthening, promote communities capacities households food security livelihoods and access to nutrition by scaling up provision and distribution livelihood kits to the most severely affected households and protect their existing and increase resilience production.
ASCO 2020 response will focus on crop, fishing and vegetable activities that will be used to strengthen household’s food security and resilient livelihoods of the affected populations.
Accountability to the affected beneficiaries with complaint and feedback mechanisms. The project will provide beneficiaries with information about the assistance. ASCO targets 72000 beneficiaries, ensure women 19500, girls 18500, boys 17500 and men 16000 including people with disabilities have access to feedback and complaint mechanisms so that corrective actions to address their specific protection and assistance needs.
Women will be involved in participation in project design, implementation and monitoring of activities and information sharing to express their views. Project will increase availability of GBV prevention and response services in where many women are suffering from different forms of GBV.
ASCO compliments Nutrition, health and WASH to integrate food security into other sectors. Food security and livelihood status is compromised where people are exposed to high level of infection due to unsafe and insufficient water supply, limited access to basic healthcare, poor food security and inadequate sanitation.

1 - Needs

The drivers and underlying factors of food insecurity in Aweil East, Wau, Jur River and Yirol East Counties are based on the needs assessment. The Counties are affected and face multi context crisis: inter communal conflict, flooding due to heavy rains in some parts, cattle raiding, food insecurity, economic crisis, disease outbreaks and the collapse of infrastructure, healthcare and social services.The counties mostly face natural catastrophic situation seasonal and climatic shocks most particularly floods and drought spells increasingly due to large scale displacement resulting in limited food stocks, crop diseases and pests including the Fall Army Worm affecting crops, livestock diseases that are further undermining people’s resilience by depleting household assets. Seasoning flooding experienced in flood affected areas and other parts of western, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and lakes including household’s settlement in flood prone areas in focused CountiesFlooding due to heavy rains has caused massive destruction over 12000 households in four focused counties were affected by the flooding. Thousands of people have been displaced while several household’s livelihoods disrupted and claiming lives of many. The affected communities remains grim as the affected population remains displaced in prone areas. Aweil East, Wau Jur River and Yirol East Counties have been hit and affected by conflicts. The Inter communal conflicts between communities disrupted the livelihoods of many households. These resulted to devastating consequences on pastoral communities and their livestock.Livestock disease morbidity and mortality also have undermine stock survival, productivity and off take with many displaced families having already lost all their animals through the need to sell or looted during conflict. Livestock are moving back to the toic whereby milk becomes less available in household level.The worsening situation is also attributed mainly to the ongoing economic crisis and Inter communal conflicts between communities that affected households’ livelihoods, farming activities affecting more than 12000 households. The economic crisis is further exacerbating the situation impacting on food security indicators, increasingly for the market dependent urban populations with decline in purchasing power affecting households due to high prices on the market commodities, reduced trade flow, decreasing agricultural production and expanding insecurity, making food security situation highly precarious to Phase 4 based September 2019 IPC projection report released, FSNMS, REACH Mult- sectorial factsheets updates, FEWSNET updates and FAO/ WFP operational and FSL Cluster reports.In terms of nutrition, acute malnutrition has worsened in focused Counties with extreme critical level of severity Acute Malnutrition GAM rate of >15%. Increases in Acute malnutrition is attributed to severe food insecurity, widespread insecurity, displacement, poor access to services, high morbidity, extremely poor diets and poor sanitation and hygiene. The project will target different beneficiary groups in focused Counties, Vulnerable, IDPs, Returnees, Youth, and Children with identified needs by consultation of each groups. It is the extremely poor populations who are the most vulnerable and suffering most. IDPs, host communities and returnees including elderly people with disabilities male and female, children under 5yrs children, PLW and women headed households in the communities have been compelled to adopt negative coping strategies for survival.The project shall involve men, women, boys and girls appropriate and equitable participation in program activities to gain relevant skills. However, 50% of women in the project activities will be consulted including persons with disabilities and will be on the recipient end of the livelihood kits to enhance food production and enhance resilience and recovery.

2 - Activities

ASCO South Sudan will focus on lifesaving activities during project implementation to meet the immediate food security and livelihood needs in the focused counties in consideration of the cluster objectives• Unconditional In-kind food assistance by general food distribution assistance by providing emergency regular life sustaining and life-saving food assistance to prevent famine, improve food consumption, improve dietary diversity and improve coping strategies for the most vulnerable population. • ASCO will support emergency food production: main season agriculture seeds and tool distribution: fish, vegetable and crop kits.• Emergency food and livelihood support: fishing kits or rapid response kits fishing kits or cow pea and other three variety of fast maturing vegetable seeds.• Reducing pest and crop disease e.g Fall Army Worms surveillance, detection, prevention and eradication.• Reduction in post-harvest losses and introduction of appropriate technologies.• Livestock vaccination: disease prevention and treatment especially zoonotic e.g. Rift Valley Fever and East Coast Fever diseases. • Sustaining lives through conditional in-kind food assistance, Conditional food assistance through food for assets food for assistance. Focusing on seasonal food assistance with other multi sector integrated actions particularly with Protection, WASH, Nutrition and Health actors. • In addition, ASCO will provide emergency training and build capacity to work with community beneficiaries on post-harvest techniques involving women, men, and youth girls and boys in terms of improving the value chain for food products through better processing, storage preservation of seeds and marketing of agricultural products. • Support emergency fisheries and aquaculture activities for communities living along the rivers and dependent on fisheries for their livelihood. This will be achieved by distribution of fishing gears to vulnerable households for fish catch to supplement their diet.• Train targeted selected beneficiaries and build capacity fishermen and women on fishing methods, preparation, preservation and marketing skills to supports the building of resilience to improve food security and livelihoods.• Train selected beneficiaries and build capacity the vulnerable households on marketing skills to supports the building of resilience and empower the vulnerable beneficiaries comprising of women and men, youth Boys and Girls including government local authorities carry out local trade which will enable them generate income to Support their livelihood.• ASCO will also train the selected beneficiaries and build capacity household’s beneficiaries on appropriate emergency crop and vegetable production new technology and modern methods of agricultural farming to increase food production.• ASCO will Support to seed fairs as well using locally sourced seed; seed security and multiplication strengthening as well as Support market functionality• Support value chain addition e.g. through milk and fish preservation and marketing• Training and capacity building: both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production particularly on farmer field schools, demonstration plots, income generation and vocational training etc. • Multi sectoral models: livelihoods, nutrition sensitive food security, hygiene, sanitation.• Diversification and restoration of livelihoods, small grants for petty trade, village saving and loan schemes and micro finance etc.• ASCO will carry out monitoring and evaluation and post distribution impact crop, and fishing assessment and reporting to its donors.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes: The protection environment is sufficiently safe and stable because the risks are unlikely to occur since access to food security and livelihood assistance is allowed by the authorities in focused counties in Western, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and lakes states. Generally no access hindrances by the government. The focused counties are relatively calm and most agencies operates in. According to the Relief and Rehabilitation Commission Coordinators, all humanitarian agencies are absolutely allowed to provide humanitarian assistance to the affected and vulnerable population with protection environment.There are no access constraints in focused Counties. Currently the Counties primarily physical in nature though with a few Impassable roads affecting access due to floods in prone areas following the ongoing rain seasonHowever, the road network is functional and with regular flights in airstrips being in good condition; however it is a significant distance from affected populations and are operational for humanitarian and commercial services. All food security and other humanitarian actors are encouraged to carry out humanitarian activities, assistance to the vulnerable population within the countiesASCO also in consideration will monitor measures access to the beneficiaries to gather information on satisfaction of beneficiaries in terms of timeliness of food security services.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Aid Support Community Organization involve in partnership strategy and engaging with local communities, local and international organizations on ground to ensure that sustainability of the project implementation activities will be handed over to static partner, the community stakeholders and the other food security partners actors. Aid Support Community Organization provides a right based humanitarian assistance as we build on the affected population’s capacity and enhance their food security resilience. Food security and livelihood programming offers a range of advantages in many contexts, one being more empowering approach to ensure the right to saving with dignity.ASCO will strongly engage full participation of the community during the project implementation and equipped them with the necessary knowledge on sustainability of the project after ASCO exits the project. This involved the local authorities, food security and livelihood sub cluster focal point and other partners.On the other hand, Aid Support Community will train beneficiary groups who will continue to work as volunteers in their communities and it will agree that ASCO will hand over its Food Security Project activities to long term partner on ground.
