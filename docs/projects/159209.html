Project Summary

Section 1 – OVERVIEW
An estimated 2.1 million Somalis are in Crisis and Emergency (IPC Phases 3 & 4) and face significant
consumption gaps through December 2019, an extended hunger season following the poor performance
of the 2019 Gu season rainfall. An additional 4.2 million Somalis are in IPC 2 (Stressed) and engage in
negative coping strategies to meet their food and non-food needs. In total, 6.3 million Somalis face acute
food insecurity through the end of 2019. The food security situation reflect the cumulative effects of
below-average rains experienced across most parts of Somalia during the 2018 Deyr (October-December),
followed by harsh weather conditions during the dry 2019 Jilaal (January–March) season and the delayed
and poor performance of the 2019 Gu (April-June). These consecutive seasonal poor performances have
been exacerbated by limited recovery from the extended impacts of the severe 2016/2017 drought. An
estimated 2.6 million Somalis across Somalia remain displaced primarily due to conflict, lack of livelihood
opportunities and drought.
Section 2 – AFFECTED POPULATION
The Bay-Bakool low potential agro-pastoralist and Guban pastoral livelihood zones are in Emergency (IPC
Phase 4) and represent hot spot rural population for priority response interventions. Most parts of
northern and central Somalia are also classified as Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and require urgent humanitarian
assistance. Rural livelihoods that are classified in IPC Phase 2 during the current projection period
but have been in worse phases in the recent past also considered priority in terms of response
The two IPC products (maps and population table) generated by FSNAU following seasonal assessment
informed the determination of People in Need (PIN) and severity analysis of the cluster. The acute food
insecure population table (Oct-December 2019) shared by FSNAU included in living standard consequence
in calculating the inter-cluster PIN1
for 2020 HNO. The projected IPC phase of the area (livelihood zone or
settlement) and historical trends of IPC phases of the area have informed the prioritization of affected
areas for inter-cluster HNO PIN. The PIN of the FSC is determined to be 4.7 million people out of the 6.3
million people in IPC 2 and above. This PIN figure further classified into the five severity classes on Intercluster PIN as provided by OCHA (None/Minimal (1), Stressed (2), Severe (3), Extreme (4) and Catastrophe
Section 3 – ANALYSIS OF HUMANITARIAN NEEDS
Widespread acute food insecurity and malnutrition persist in many parts of Somalia despite concerted
efforts to reversing this trend. The projected acute food insecure population of 6.3 million Somalia (IPC 2
and above) represents a 36 percent increase compared to the figures for 2018 Gu but it is comparable to
the estimates for 2017 Gu. Acute food insecurity considered as “living standards” consequences because
of its direct effect on people’s ability to pursue their normal productive and social activities and meet their
basic needs in an autonomous manner. The food consumption gap and livelihood change (loss of
livelihood assets and strategies) are some of the manifestation of acute food insecurity.
