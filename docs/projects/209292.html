Project Summary

The HRP for 2024 highlight that total food availability in Sudan for the 2023/24 consumption year is expected to be significantly below average due to below-average domestic cereal harvests, i depleted food stocks and bureaucratic and insecurity-related challenges impeding food assistance deliveries.
The Food Security Analysis report in Sudan for the period October to February 2024 shows that about 17.7 million people across Sudan (37%) are suffering high levels of acute food insecurity, being classified in Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) Phase 3 or above; among whom 4.9 million (10%) are in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency), and 12.8 million (27%) are in IPC Phase 3 (Crisis). This reflects the magnitude of the food insecurity across the country. Not only that; the situation is expected to further worsen due to the ongoing conflict.
Currently access to food is counter affected by a number of interacting factors which include (1) wide scale displacement due to conflict, (2) the harvest have been affected by spread of fighting at harvest time, especially in Aljazira and South Kordofan, (3) decline in areas cultivated by food crops during 2023 rainy season due toof poor financing of the production inputs resulted from collapse of banking system, (4) blockage of roads that used to transport commodities from production areas and (5) restricted access to movement of humanitarian assistance that blocked aid agencies to deliver food supplies to some acutely food insecure areas. The IPC3 is higher in Khartoum (38%), Darfur States 38%, 35%, 32%, 30%, 26% in (West, East, Central, South and North Darfur respectively), whereas it is 33% in South Kordofan and 24% in Aljazira. The IPC 4 scale is higher in Khartoum (22%), West and Central Darfur (17% each), South Kordofan (15%), South Darfur (14%), North and East Darfur (12% each) and Aljazira (7%). These threatening figures are expected to increase during the lean period when produced stocks deplete and labor opportunities shrink while the conflict resolution is not seen on the near horizon.
The overall aim of this project is to support the most vulnerable and conflict-affected populations in Darfur, Eljazeera, Khartoum, Red Sea, Gadaref, South Kordofan, and the Northern State. The key objectives of the project are to: 1) Reduce acute food insecurity and save the lives of extremely vulnerable populations affected by conflict and natural disasters by providing emergency agriculture assistance to the underserved populations; and 2) increase access to food security and livelihood diversification for the newly displaced and most extremely vulnerable conflict-affected populations through cash based transfers.
The UPO proposed project will focus on intervening in the most affected areas and will prioritize assisting the people most at risk and, most vulnerable groups in collaboration and coordination with the FSL cluster. Our interventions include enabling at risk people to access immediate food (e.g. through cash and food assistance) and supporting them to secure future food through agricultural inputs and seeds for women, men, boys, and girls, as well as conducting extension training and vaccinations for livestock. The project will also ensure the provision of emergency livelihood start-up kits, agricultural inputs, and tools for the most vulnerable HHs. Awareness campaigns will be conducted for the most vulnerable population, both female and male, on environment protection, gender involvement, and equality. In addition, 40% of this program will be cash through distribution of vouchers, cash for work, and cash for business.
The project will be implemented in close coordination with the relevant actors, ensuring that gender-sensitive programming and investment, including protection, are adhered to during the implementation. UPO focuses on maintaining an inclusive, sustainable, and accepting implementation environment that results in community collaboration and ownership.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

Under supervision of the MEAL and Quality Manager, UPO will establish effective feedback mechanisms and Community-Based Complaints Mechanism (CBCM) to allow the communities to raise their feedback timely and transparently to incorporate community’s feedback and address problems rapidly, including for sensitive issues like protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) and Safeguarding by humanitarian or development workers. The project staff will be trained and oriented on SEA, as well as service providers, suppliers and contractors. Community accountability focal persons will be selected from each target community (a woman and man) and trained to facilitate the process. In line with UPO’ MEAL policies and procedures, will consult communities in establishing an appropriate feedback and response mechanism to give each community the opportunity to identify and report issues on program quality and protection concerns. Phone numbers will be allocated for the communities we work with to use in raising their concerns. The system will address both sensitive and non-sensitive issues.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

UPO is operational in eleven states in Sudan implementing programs in different sectors. UPO is closely coordination with the different sectors and sector leads in planning and implementing its operations. UPO will ensure active participation in the Inter-Agency meetings, assessments and other missions. We will also share results of our own assessments with the relevant sectors and inter-agency meetings. UPO as chairing the National NGOs forum will represent and reflect the national NGOs agenda and concerns in the inter-agency meetings and inter-sector coordination meetings and activities.
