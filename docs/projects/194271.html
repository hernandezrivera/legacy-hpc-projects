Project Summary

Twic East County is located in a low land in between Duk and Bor County within Jonglei state and located at the following coordinates N07*09'48.40" Longitude - E03*24'20.71" Altitude - 421.3m. It is clear that the current political crisis coupled with the current flooding and economic crisis in the county has eroded the development efforts from both the government and non-governmental organizations. This has been notable with the setback on infrastructure development, livelihoods and government’s ability to provide services and or implement public policies especially education policies among others to increase access to services by ensuring the citizen have all they need to be safe and protected. As a result, the human resource that has been instrumental in all sectors of lives has also been demoralized due to economic crisis, displacement, inadequate services, un-enabling learning & protection environment, limited access to basic services such as education, Protection, health, Water, Sanitation and Hygiene despite high prevalence of COIVD-19, HIV/AIDS and other communicable diseases.
According to IRNA report initiated by OCHA on Twic east County through CARE and LWF in October 2021, the entire County has been affected by the devastating floods leaving IDPs in the schools’ premises, churches, commissioner’s residential compound, Traditional court premises, Education office, bench court and other government facilities including hospital are occupied.
Therefore, an inter-sectoral intervention is highly envisioned to address different needs among the target population. In schools and school community, the need for mainstreaming protection, health, hygiene, sexual reproductive health and Gender based violence across different education activities. This approach will help in reconstruction and restoration of dignity, hope and livelihoods of the returning IDPs including cattle keepers.
The most affected group by the flooding are children aged between 3-17 years , Adolescents, youth and Elderly as all sources of livelihoods were all swept away including classrooms leaving the few who are attending classes under the trees in High lands or in the Government administrative units. There is a risk of these children and youth losing more days and turning to criminal gangs & cattle raiders in the communities if not engaged on education and other activities relative activities.
The main objective of this intervention is to ensure that women, men, girls and boys have safe access to quality basic gender-responsive, ability challenged accessible services including water, sanitation and hygiene, nutrition, education, Protection and health including sexual and reproductive health . This is so relevant in the restoration of hope through multi-sectoral activities. In addition, the provision of integrated services among the IDPs, returnees and the hosts will enhance and promote peace, social interaction and cohesion in and around the communities.

1 - Needs

Twic East community is faced with displacement (flooding) and influx of returnees with out repatriation which is resulting to inadequate basic services such as shelters, water, health, Hygiene and Sanitation services. According to the IRNA assessment conducted by UNOCHA in Twic East through CARE international and LWF in October 2021 and by CSI in April 2022 confirmed that IDPs settlement is informal and spontaneous in nature and most people lack basic services because their items were lost during the abrupt evacuation. During IRNA, some people air out that their biggest fear would be an outbreak of diseases as there are no essential drugs available in the main health facility(hospital) and in schools. Most people prefer going back to their homes but the flood phenomenal pose a lot of fear to them, cases related to sexual harassment are reportedly happening in the Island though community does not speak up about such scenarios, children have no access to education, girls are engaged in domestic duties and considered as source of wealth by their parents, inadequate information and sensitization on importance of girls education, girls rampant drop out of schools due to force marriage, poor living conditions, Poor hygiene and sanitation remains challenging needs. In addition, children (boys and girls) do not observe hygiene despite high prevalence of COIVD-19, HIV/AIDS and other communicable diseases hence, need for school community preventive information, capacity building on life skills, Inadequate scholastic materials in schools. However, the Multi-sectoral intervention is highly recommended to deliver services in an integrated manner. Three (4) thematic areas of Education in Emergency (formal and informal) in Primary, Early childhood Development (ECD), Pastoralist Education (PE) and Accelerated learning program are of necessity. Therefore, CSI plans to conduct mobile education in cattle camps in Twic East County as an advancement of need to protection and fulfillment of children rights including rights to education, right to food and right to be loved.The need for institutional capacity building (training teachers, education managers and PTAs), create safe, protective and non-violent environment, provision of quality drinking water, provision of dignity kits as well as promotion of gender sensitive education in schools and among the school communities is a core demand. In addition, peace education, adolescent development, formation of school clubs such as girls’ education movement (GEM) , adolescent and peace clubs members will form an integral strategy for mobilization of girls and boys to school (ECD, Primary, ALP and FAL).Therefore, an inter-sectoral intervention is highly envisioned to address different needs/demands and capacity gaps identified among different categories of targeted groups. In schools, the need for mainstreaming protection across different activities remains crucial.Need for learning/education centers; this is very vital given the return of some IDPs including cattle keepers The communities will be sensitized through community meetings “on the important of sending their children to school without gender segregation”, on the same note, the community leaders will act as education agents during any public or community gathering. Such efforts will pave the way for sending more children to schools among the IDPs, host and the returnees’ communities regardless of gender or location. The need to provide sexual reproductive health services such as community respect for dignity for all, gender responsive mechanism and provision of dignity kits.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CSI will always make sure that people living with disabilities are involved through the project cycle by representing the in project structures such as the beneficiaries identification committees, distribution committees and during the project inception workshops.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

CSI has a complaint handling Mechanism in addition to safeguarding policies which allows the community and other stakeholders to report their complaints and receive responses as per the policy. Complaint reporting channels are well spelt out in the CSI CHMs. Affected population and stakeholders will be sensitized on CSI complaint handling and feedback mechanism/policy to promotes stakeholders to complaint of CSI and staff conduct and work. Focal persons to receive the complaints is identified and complaints problems to follow-up include: Infringements of rights e.g., discrimination based on gender, age, ethnic group etc; sexual harassment, issues of participation or empowerment, issues of accountability and transparency, corruption and abuse of entrusted power. CSI staff code of conduct and Prevention of Sexual Abuse, Exploitation and Harassment (PSAE) policy aim at protecting the right holders from abuse.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CSI has PSEA policy with clear guidelines on reporting mechanism. This policy coupled with other polices (child safeguarding, compliant handling and staff code of conduct) categorically spelled out organization's zero tolerance to Sexual exploitation and abuse. this policy is made public and integrated into the organizational community engagement tools which are made available to our beneficiaries and stakeholders.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiary involvement throughout strategy development, community dialogue, baseline and midline surveys and beneficiaries feedback will define the project and enable flexible and adaptive programming which responds appropriately as the context evolves.
