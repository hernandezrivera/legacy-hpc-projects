Project Summary

The HALO Trust will continue to deliver an integrated Mine Action approach in northwest Syria involving Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE), Non-Technical Survey (NTS), Explosive Ordnance Disposal (EOD), mechanical clearance and Victim Assistance (VA).
HALO will provide EORE to men, women, boys and girls, with a focus on community members most at risk of accidents (including children, teenagers, IDPs, agricultural workers and rubble removal workers). HALO will also continue delivering ‘Training of Trainer’ (ToT) workshops to NGO workers and Community Focal Points to improve the relevance, integration and reach of EORE messaging across all communities and relevant humanitarian sectors.
Following the completion in 2020 of HALO's contamination impact survey work in all accessible communities in NW Syria, capturing the scope of explosive ordnance (EO) contamination in the region, in 2022 HALO started conducting detailed non-technical survey (NTS) to define precisely the types of hazards and technical requirements for disposal. NTS will continue in 2023 and provide local authorities, humanitarian actors, the Gaziantep cluster network, and the donor community with a greater understanding of EO threats, including specific locations.
HALO’s NTS work enabled the training, equipping and deployment of a disposal capacity to reduce the presence and impact of EO in highly vulnerable communities in a part of the country where HALO is the only INGO able to provide these highly specialised services supported by a sector-leading global EOD advisory team. HALO’s two mixed-gender EOD teams are currently destroying one explosive item per day. They are focusing on agricultural areas where the removal of explosive hazards not only contributes to casualty reduction but also boosts economic activity. In addition to the deployment of the EOD capacity, HALO will train and equip a mechanical demining team that will use an armoured excavator in early 2023. This will allow to safely tackle threats that are too dangerous or time-consuming for manual clearance.
The support to survivors of EO accidents will continue through a Victim Assistance (VA) component. HALO will provide victim assistance case management services, providing referrals and transportation to specialised services for survivors of EO accidents.
HALO acknowledges the requirement to avoid activity duplication within the MASC as well as addressing gaps in providing EORE (to children, adults, CFPs, and NGO Focal Points), NTS and EOD. This will be achieved through ongoing and regular coordination with other members of the MASC to which HALO is co-chair, as well as engagement with local authorities, communities, and other NGOs throughout the implementation of the project.

3 - Needs

There is widespread and ongoing contamination of communities and agricultural land by landmines, cluster munitions, rockets, mortars, artillery projectiles and IEDs. The 2021 Mine Action Review reports that 50% of people are at risk and over 33% of communities are possibly contaminated, notably Aleppo, Idleb, Ar-Raqqa, Deir-Ez-Zor and Quneitra. HALO’s survey from 2018 to 2020 confirmed the presence of explosive contamination in 41% of accessible communities surveyed, with approx. 1.6 million people living at risk from EO in the northwest, where very limited clearance has taken place. By contrast, iMMAP reports that in the northeast, over 25km² of contaminated ground has been released. HALO’s assessments indicate 3,500 EO-related accidents in NW Syria, resulting in approx. 4,500 casualties, over 600 in 2020 alone. This puts pressure on agricultural land and natural resources, further increasing risk of accidents. There is also insufficient public healthcare provision - the 2021 HRP identified a 50% funding shortfall in VA services - which often creates long-term physical and psycho-social problems. UN OCHA lists in their Access Severity Overview (Oct. 2022) “presence of mines and UXOs” as one of the top three humanitarian access constraints. The protracted nature of the conflict constitutes a barrier to behaviour change, as people feel less wary of EO, seeking scrap metal from it and treat it as a negative coping mechanism in the absence of other livelihood opportunities.

4 - Implementation of activities

HALO will conduct all activities directly. EORE activities will involve the delivery of safe messaging through community-based sessions with flexible options including interactive school-based sessions, social media campaigns, and usage of cartoons and comic books. Considering the different risks that certain gender, age, and occupation groups are exposed to, materials tailored for specific groups will be used during sessions. NGO workers and CFP will be trained through ToT. HALO will prioritise PwDs as ToT participants and continue to explore ways to guarantee inclusion of PwDs in EORE. NTS will confirm hazardous areas by validating previous EO survey data with an emphasis on suspected minefields, IED fields, and multiple EO-contaminated areas. Collected data will be shared with UNMAS and used to inform prioritisation of disposal and clearance operations. EOD will dispose of accessible surface hazards, in accordance with priorities determined and agreed upon by HALO technical staff. Mechanical clearance will target sub-surface hazards along with threats that are too dangerous or time-consuming for manual clearance, such as contaminated wells and irrigation canals. VA will consist of providing case management to EO survivors, including referral and transportation to specialised services. MEAL Activities: HALO MEAL staff will compile monitoring reports & feedback forms, while HALO’s FM spot checks, KIIs, and FGDs will validate and inform project implementation.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

- Familiarisation may cause the civilian population to become overconfident to threat of EO (Likelihood: medium; Impact: high): Emphasis on “Stop, Warn and Report” messaging in EORE and introducing EOD team hotline for further interventions. - Difficulties accessing girls and women (Likelihood: medium; Impact: high): Current teams already include female staff. HALO will seek to achieve gender-balanced recruitment for field staff to facilitate the consideration/implementation of appropriate activities for inclusion of girls and women. - Trained local EOD capacity may become overconfident and injury to team members &/or local community members during survey/disposal tasks (Likelihood: medium; Impact: medium): Staff trained to IMAS standards and supervised by Technical EOD Advisor through regular quality assurance checks. Teams will deploy with an ambulance, first aid equipment, and communications. - Risk that local authorities may dictate mine action priorities and they would not be in line with humanitarian objectives (Likelihood: high; Impact: medium): Recruit local staff to build trust and to promote community participation. Highlight humanitarian priorities by using contamination data while engaging with local authorities. - Teams perceived as having a biased affiliation, exposing them to harm (Likelihood: low; Impact: high): Teams are well known/trusted in their areas of operation and coordinate with local authorities to facilitate safe access.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

HALO has zero tolerance of abuse of human rights in any part of the organisation or its supply chain and partner organisations. HALO conducts due diligence checks on partners, contractors and suppliers to ensure that correct processes and practices, values and standards are in place to ensure the protection and safeguarding of staff and communities. Gender-balanced recruitment, training, and code of conduct requirements for project staff will be applied to ensure appropriate protection-sensitive interventions. Possible risks and mitigation strategies:- Staff exposed to, or guilty of, GBV, sexual exploitation, and abuse (Likelihood: low; Impact: high): Mitigation - all project staff are trained in PSEA, gender mainstreaming, and 'Do No Harm'. Activities are designed to consider the needs of men, women, boys, and girls. Each beneficiary has access to feedback and complaint mechanisms. Ongoing M&E through field visits. - Safeguarding incident in particular gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse during activities (Likelihood: low; Level of impact: High): All project field staff will receive initial and refresher safeguarding training which includes PSEA, GBV, gender mainstreaming, and the principles of 'Do No Harm'. EORE activities are designed, taking into consideration the needs of children and adults. A leaflet including the feedback mechanism (WhatsApp number and an email address) will be provided to each beneficiary.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- Access: Regular contact made with local, governorate and national authorities ensures concerns are addressed quickly and avoids withdrawal of approvals. - Under performance against project activities: A mobilisation phase builds relationships with local authorities and refresher training is provided. Conservative beneficiary targets are established for project activities. Project design is based on experience in NW and S Syria. Formal project kick-off meetings and monthly project reviews and strong MEAL mechanisms will be in place. - Recruitment & retention of essential field personnel: HALO has demonstrated capacity to recruit good quality staff. The maximum number of suitable staff from any concluding projects are recruited for the next project and competitive salaries offered. - Security situation deteriorates preventing/restricting project activities: Daily security situation monitoring informs work plans. Activities can be quickly diverted to respond to changes in the security environment, with the principal challenge being widespread security deterioration. HALO will then suspend activities and review to identify areas where work can be safely conducted.
