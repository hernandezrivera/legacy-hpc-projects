Project Summary

In order to address severe food insecurity experienced in Kukawa and Mobbar LGAs, ACTED is proposing a food security intervention to provision 4,000 households with critical food security and livelihoods assistance. ACTED will implement a flexible approach through the use of cash vouchers and income generating activities with a focus on agriculture in order to provide emergency access to income to ascertain food security for the most vulnerable population groups in Kukawa and Mobbar LGAs, with specific emphasis on providing food assistance to women-headed households in hard to reach areas by pushing out from LGA capitals, contingent on the evolving security context in these LGAs.
Assistance will be provided to IDPs and host communities in order to relieve pressure that large-scale displacement has placed on local food resources. Program activities will specifically focus on targeting women-headed households, and child-headed households, and will work in collaboration with REACH to utilise conclusions drawn from the Hard To Reach Assessments conducted in Kukawa and Mobbar LGAs to identify new areas of intervention, as well as liaise closely with protection partners to receive referrals for program enrolment of beneficiaries.
ACTED will work to identify local vendors Cash vouchers will be facilitated through the use of the red rose platform, and through identification of local vendors able to partake in the program. Additionally, ACTED will implement an IGA component by provisioning one-off agriculture inputs in the rainy season, specifically targeting women-headed households with access to land, in order to build resilience of displaced and conflict-affected communities. Protection mainstreaming is a core component of program design, with specific emphasis on inclusion of vulnerable population groups as primary target beneficiaries, and as such fifteen percent of the overall budget will be dedicated to delivery of protection mainstreaming integrated activities.
Key protection principles that underpin humanitarian interventions have informed the design of the project, and will be actively present as a core component of implementation. ACTED will ensure that activities conducted prioritize the wellbeing, safety and dignity of beneficiaries at all times. Activities will avoid incurring any harm, will ensure meaningful access to food security assistance, and will collaborate with stakeholders to identify the most vulnerable beneficiaries for enrolment.

Needs

OCHA’s September 2018 Humanitarian Situation Update for North-East Nigeria, indicates that 6.1 million women, girls, boys, and men remain in dire need of life-saving assistance in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states, after almost a decade of crisis in the region. The protracted conflict in northeast Nigeria, and resulting displacement of approximately 1.8 million people, have systematically chipped away at community livelihoods and resilience capacities, disrupting income-generating activities, and leaving communities without the ability to absorb shocks, or the financial means to meet their basic needs of water and food. Continuing cycles of violence have seen agricultural land destroyed and abandoned, further aggravating the already precarious food security situation. Despite wide-ranging humanitarian assistance across the northeast, food security levels are still of serious concern, with continued support urgently needed. Much of Borno state is currently classified as “stressed”, with FEWSNET forecasts through January 2019 predicting that large populations in the northeast remaining dependent on food assistance to meet needs, facing phase three “crisis”; while populations in inaccessible areas with acutely limited access to income and livelihood activities are facing phase four “emergency”. With the HRP only 48.7% funded as of July2018, there is a serious risk that the humanitarian response will prove inadequate in the face of worsening food security needs. As conditions worsen, the fluidity of population movements poses a particular challenge in reaching the most vulnerable, especially with many areas still inaccessible. Within the period of 17 to 23 October 2018 alone, IOM recorded 2,717 movements, including 2,018 new arrivals (DTM ETT Report 89, 2018). Further, in the same period, IOM reports that out of 199 children aged between 6 and 59 months that received nutrition screenings in Borno State, 11 recorded MUAC scores of yellow, and 12 children had MUAC scores of red. Kukawa LGA remains among the LGAs recorded as receiving new arrivals in this time frame placing great strain on already depleted local resources, while Mobbar LGA remains fraught with instability, significantly hampering the ability of families to manage their own food security as access to livelihoods is severely restricted. Women and children remain the most affected by the ongoing crisis, critically requiring humanitarian assistance in order to manage their food security. Yellow and red MUAC readings from nutrition screenings on Under-5s remain alarmingly high, and with the agricultural season ending access to means for food security is anticipated to drop, specifically impacting women and children living in hard to reach areas where markets and access to food inputs have significantly reduced due to the crisis. WFP are anticipated to halt ongoing GFD programs in Mobbar LGA in 2019, leaving a significant gap in service provision for conflict-affected families, still unable to manage their own food security due to the impact of the ongoing conflict on livelihoods accessibility. ACTED proposes to contribute to fill this gap through a hybrid approach of providing cash-voucher and IGA agriculture-inputs to the most vulnerable families in this area, specifically targeting women-headed households.

Activities

- Security assessment conducted in Mobbar and Kukawa LGAs at outset of program, and on continuous basis through implementation, to identify villages outside of LGA capitals (Damasak and Kukawa Town), where activities can be implemented. The objective of this activity is to continuously extend the reach of humanitarian intervention into hard to reach areas, with specific focus on providing food assistance to the most vulnerable women-headed households. - Beneficiary identification: beneficiaries will be identified through inputs from the REACH Hard to Reach Assessments, beginning in November 2018. The Hard to Reach Assessment conducted in Kukawa and Mobbar LGA will provide significant input in determining most vulnerable but accessible population groups; in Mobbar the assessment will encompass Damasak, Zulum, Umarti, Chamba, Kareto and Layi districts. The most vulnerable households, including households that will no longer be receiving GFD assistance in 2019, will be targeted for enrolment under this project. - Market Assessment in areas of implementation for mapping of markets, available goods, needs of communities, etc. in order to ensure cash vouchers provide beneficiary access to life-saving food resources - Vendor identification and training for participation in the cash voucher program; training of vendors on participation in the cash voucher program; continuous monitoring of vendor participants- Provision of vouchers to food insecure households in Mobbar and Kukawa LGAs, with specific focus on targeting women-headed households: beneficiaries will receive 28,264NGN per month, in harmonisation with ACTED's ongoing ECHO-funded food security program in Kukawa LGA. This monthly distribution is based on UNHCR food basket calculations for five-member households, at 23,851 NGN plus contingency per month; training beneficiaries on voucher utilization, participating vendors, etc.; monitoring of beneficiaries' access to increased food security through enrolment in the program; - Provision of one-off agriculture inputs to households with access to land for farming in the rainy season in Mobbar and Kukawa LGAs; - Protection mainstreaming through community sensitisation: ACTED will work closely with local authorities, community associations, and communities prior to distribution activities in order to ensure understanding of project activities by the local population, including beneficiary selection criteria. Sensitisation efforts will place emphasis on targeting male population groups in order to raise awareness of how crisis affects women, this component is designed to generate buy-in and acceptance of the community as primary target beneficiary households will be women-led. - Monitoring & Evaluation: ACTED's independent AME Unit will continuously monitor project outputs; all distributions will be followed by a post distribution monitoring survey in order to assess beneficiary satisfaction with selection and distribution processes, as well as to determine the ability of distributed provisions to satisfy household needs. Further, a research component will be administered under this project in order to determine how cash impacts women's access to markets, and how this impacts overall household wellbeing. -Upholding protection mainstreaming: All ACTED staff are signatories to the ACTED Code of Conduct, which includes adherence to ACTED's policy on PSEA,
