Project Summary

The Gender in Humanitarian Action Working Group (GiHA WG) for the Rohingya Refugee Crisis Response in Cox’s Bazar is the coordination body that provides cross-sectoral and inter-organizational coordination to ensure the integration of Gender Equality and Empowerment of Women and Girls (GEEWG) standards in humanitarian action. GiHA WG is co-Chaired by UN Women and UNHCR. Based on self-nomination, it is comprised of Gender Focal Points from each of the sectors, as well as members from UN agencies, INGOs, local NGOs and CSOs known to be strong advocates of gender mainstreaming/equality. UN Women maintains the secretariat role of GiHA WG.
Under GiHAWG, the Gender Hub provides the leadership on: technical assistance, capacity development, and advocacy and knowledge management on gender mainstreaming and gender-targeted action to amplify women's agency, leadership, representation, and voice throughout interventions.
To achieve this, the project aims to enhance coordination mechanism, leadership and gender-responsive programming capacity among humanitarian actors including women-led organisations in Cox’s Bazar. This will build on the Gender Hub's three-year scheme in 2019 and for 2022 the technical pillars will focus on:
Coordination and Technical Assistance: Provides sound technical advice and hands-on support to cross-sectoral and inter-organsational support on better integrating gender equality and women and girl’s empowerment throughout the response strategy. Coordinate and co-lead the development, implementation, monitoring and review of sectors' Gender and Inclusion Action Plans (GIAPs) based on the annual sector’s strategic objectives in JRP 2022 by supporting sector coordinators, Gender Focal Points, and implementing sector partners. Strategic support to the Women's Empowerment Leadership Technical Working Group's (WELTWG) to strengthen local women’s leadership and their representation at different local decision-making platforms.
Capacity Development: Map the existing capacity of international and local organizations, including Women Led Organisations (WLO), for bridging humanitarian and development interventions to promote gender equality, protection, and resilience for women and girls. The capacity gaps will inform the capacity development interventions and the Gender Hub will facilitate and support capacity development (training, exchange visits, learning circles) among key stakeholders (implementing partners, local governments, CiCs, WLO, organizations serving gender-diverse populations, and women with disabilities) in identifying opportunities for increasing access to funding to achieve GEEWG commitments.
Communications and Knowledge Management: Lead the gender analysis through updating the annual Gender Profile for Rohingya and host communities in Cox's Bazar and develop Gender Alerts for recurring emergencies ie. Monsoon. Develop and disseminate knowledge products and communications materials on good practices, guidance notes on GEEWG through different platforms ie. digital. Lead joint GEEWG advocacy campaigns and events e.g. 16 days of activism against GBV, International Women’s Day, World Refugee Day, to amplify the campaign on GEEWG, prevention of GBV, feminist voices and more.

1 - Identified Needs

Pre-existing gender inequalities, including gender-based violence and norm-biased discrimination, continue to be intensified following the Covid-19, according to the 2020 Rapid Gender Assessment. This intensified loop disproportionately impacts women, girls, and the most vulnerable groups (such as the gender-diverse population, sex workers, and all gender groups with disabilities) in the Rohingya community, who have limited prospects of livelihood and access to basic services, information, and decision-making processes. The situation will remain the same in the coming years. Furthermore, the women and girls of the host communities have been affected by barriers to accessing social services, livelihood options, and disrupted social norms and relations. In addition, there are protection concerns, so supporting the Women, Peace and Security (WPS) agenda and the women's peacebuilder networks are both crucial. Four years after the Rohingya influx, strengthening women's leadership, improving opportunities in decision-making structures, and transforming socio-cultural norms are essential to ensuring the human rights of women and girls in camps and host communities. Beyond simply being identified as vulnerable victims, Rohingya women and girls also contribute to the resilience of their families and communities. Family structures are changing, which is allowing for new gender roles to emerge. Inter Agency Humanitarian Evaluation (IAHE) has identified over 50 elected, self-organized, and mobilized Rohingya women leaders and women-led organisations throughout the 25 camps to advocate for their rights. Women leaders in the Rohingya and host communities, women-led CSOs, and volunteers and frontline staff of L/NNGOs will benefit from cross-sectoral training. Furthermore, according to UN Women's joint research, transformative leadership training modules are needed to enhance the resilience of these women-led networks. Men’s and boys' engagement, a proven prerequisite for addressing gender inequalities, is also less practiced among partners. Preliminary findings from The Comprehensive Gender Analysis 2021 revealed the need to strengthen gender implementing capacity by leveraging existing human capital, particularly in women’s leadership, and rights, at the community and organisational levels. Achievements have been made by various sectors, but there are still knowledge and capacity gaps among humanitarian staff about the importance and need to mainstream gender and protection across the response; a lack of issue prioritization; and persistent gaps in inclusion and access to services for people with specific needs. These gaps include the collection of sex, age, and disability disaggregated data, the inclusion of substantive women's participation and leadership, providing targeted livelihood opportunities for women and gender-diverse populations, and insufficient protection mechanisms for women and girls. These factors have continued to impede the implementation of effective gender-responsive policies and programs throughout interventions. According to the IAHE on GEEWG case study on Bangladesh published in 2020, gender-focused capacity development is further complicated because of reliance on junior personnel in agencies who are focal points but not gender experts. Gender Hub's learning assessment 2019 recommended that trainings should be tailored to sector-specific needs using diverse approaches. Sector Strategic Response Plans need to ensure gender equality as a priority to guide project partners in designing and coordinating projects that meet the distinct needs of women, girls, boys, and men equally/equitably. This can be accomplished by an enhanced humanitarian coordination mechanism and gender-responsive humanitarian programming through the GiHA WG coordination role and through Gender Hub's technical leadership to achieve the needs and aspirations of Rohingya refugees as well as the affected host community.

2 - Activities or outputs

Coordination and Technical Assistance: 1. Lead the development, implementation, and monitoring of sector Gender and Inclusion Action Plans (GIAPs) by supporting sector coordinators, Gender Focal Points, and implementing sector partners. 2. Provide technical assistance in the development of gender-responsive annual Joint Response Plans (JRPs). 3. Accompany Gender Focal Points (GFPs) to design, implement and monitor GAM-compliant projects. 4. Coordinate GiHAWG to provide leadership on cross-sectoral and inter-organisational support to ensure the integration of gender commitment and policy throughout the intervention. 5. Increase the Women's Empowerment Leadership Technical Working Group's (WELTWG) capacity to strengthen local women’s leadership and their representation at different local decision-making platforms. Capacity Development: 1. Map the existing capacity of international and local organizations, including WRO and WLO, for bridging humanitarian and development interventions to promote gender equality, protection, and resilience for women and girls. 2. Facilitate and support capacity development (training, exchange visits, learning circles) among WLOs, WROs, implementing partners, and other organizations (e.g. local governments, CiCs, organizations serving gender-diverse populations, and women with disabilities) in identifying opportunities and modalities for increasing access to funding to achieve GEEWG commitments. Communications and Knowledge Management: 1. Lead gender analysis e.g. Update Gender Profiles for Rohingya and host communities in Cox's Bazar to support the gender-responsive programming, advocacy, and capacity development. 2. Engage local women-led organizations in developing and disseminating knowledge products, communications materials, and theatre for development (TFD) events to promote their inclusion efforts for vulnerable groups (GBV survivors, women with disabilities, and gender-diverse people) and to showcase their gender mainstreaming advocacy. 3. Sensitize sectors, working groups, and Gender Focal Points (GFP) through key women public figures and social influencers using digital campaign tools (explainer videos, online film contest, web storytelling, social media quizzes). 4. Develop IEC materials to promote social cohesion through humanitarian actors managing gender-biased disinformation, mis-information, malformation, and myths.
