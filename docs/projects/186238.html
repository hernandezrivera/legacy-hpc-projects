Project Summary

INTERSOS proposes to promote a protective environment for conflict-affected people, in particular IDPs with a focus on Child Protection and GBV. Four main elements will be addressed: 1) Prevention of child abuse, neglect, and exploitation; 2) Prevention by addressing the risks of sexual and gender-based violence and responding to the immediate needs of survivors of GBV, 3) Material support for the most vulnerable IDPs with NFI; and 4) identifying and strengthening existing positive community protection mechanism.
Outputs to prevent and respond to Child Protection needs and GBV: Creation of 175 Child Friendly Spaces (Fako, Meme, Manyu, Kupe, Mezam, Momo, Boyo) where children and caregivers would be able to improve their protection environment and theirs capacities through the active participation in the existing Protection structures that INTERSOS supported during the year 2021, Integrated GBV Case management for boys girls and their caregivers, share INTERSOS’ Hotline, provision of psychological first aid (PFA), referral and accompaniment to specialized services, individual follow up, Psychosocial Support (PSS) inside and outside CFS facilities though individual & group counselling, community based recreational and PSS activities, basic skills acquisition and informal education, MH support for children and caregivers through individual case management and referrals to specialized services, Targeted material assistance for identified protection needs: Auto-referral for dignity kits to vulnerable women and girls including PwSN of reproductive age; Auto-referral for Mama kits for vulnerable pregnant and lactating women and girls, Auto-referral for cash for protection: Community capacity building on Child Protection and GBV principles & personal/relational accountability, Material support for the most vulnerable IDP household through the distribution of contextualized NFI case by case.
The overall objective of the project is to improve the protection environment of children and caregivers affected by the crisis and foster a community-based approach for the protection of the most vulnerable IDPs by contributing to the reduction of child abuse and gender-based violence (GBV) through promoting socio-cultural norms that respect age/gender equality, through holistic care of children and caregivers affected by the crisis, and GBV survivors by promoting their recovery and resilience. Also, the empowerment of community members and groups in identifying, referring and responding to protection concerns.
The project aims at initiating key changes in behaviors and socio-cultural norms in order to sustainably reduce child abuse and GBV.
Through awareness-raising campaigns, capacity building of caregivers and the active involvement of community leaders, communities are called upon to adopt attitudes that are protective for children and respectful of gender equality.
CFS are key avenues to providing confidential medium for disclosure of psychosocial distress and GBV without the fear of stigmatization, they also provide caregivers and survivors an opportunity to build support networks through peer-to-peer activities and build on their recovery and resilience through activities that foster social interactions.
Though this project, INTERSOS will integrate the CP response to his spectrum of protection activities in a systematic approach, based on the Community based strategy that INTERSOS is implementing and the Protection monitoring mechanisms already in place covering 10 divisions of NWSW, INTERSOS will be able to early identify cases of child abuse and to promptly address the protection needs of children affected by the crisis. The complementarity of this project with the ongoing protection program, and the recognition that INTERSOS gained in NWSW regions which greatly facilitate humanitarian access in hard-to-reach areas, are valuable assets that will impact the quality and the promptness of the assistance

1 - Besoins / Needs

Protection issues: In the NWSW, the violence between the Non- State Armed Groups (NSAGs) and the State Forces has resulted in several protection concerns such as summary killings and arbitrary arrests, destruction of properties, population movements, family separation, lack of a protective environment in schools, child labor, children recruited into armed groups, sexual and other forms of gender-based violence against men, women, boys and girls, and many other concerns which are all interwoven and connected to the general state of heightened insecurity. Child Protection: With school-aged children and adolescents forced to stay at home due to the ongoing conflict, resultant effects are an increase in early pregnancies, forced and early marriages, survival sex, child labor, forced recruitment of adolescent boys into non-state armed groups, child abuse neglect and exploitation, unaccompanied and separated minors, and dangerous and negative behaviors and coping mechanisms where children’s rights are constantly abused. Sexual and gender-based violence: Women and children are at particular risk of facing incidents of Sexual and Gender-Based violence as well as abuse, neglect, and exploitation. INTERSOS Protection Monitoring has recorded a significant increase in SGBV since the beginning of the crisis: in the last 9 months (March-November) INTERSOS alone registered 547 cases of GBV in the NWSW regions (305 cases of rape). Cases of SGBV that are often reported by the women and girls are mainly rape, sexual exploitation, forced and early marriages, physical and psychological violence, and harmful cultural practices. These have had an impact on the health and economic situation of women and girls, increasing their vulnerability. Girls and women who are heads of households as a result of the recruitment or radicalization of their husbands and sons engage in transactional sex to support their families and survive or in exchange for the freedom of their husbands and children. Some of the pregnant girls perform unsafe abortions that endanger their health. Survivors require lifesaving GBV response services to deal with the consequences of GBV and recover and capacity building to ensure self-protection through community-based protection mechanisms. An increasing trend of men victims of GBV violence is alarming the protection environment of men not taking part in the conflict. In response to the abuse of private property an increasing need in household items is registered.
