Project Summary

This Initiative seeks to address the systemic challenges hindering marginalized communities from enjoying their right to optimal health including Mental Health and Psychosocial Support Services. By recognizing and confronting the multifaceted barriers faced by vulnerable populations, we aspire to create a blueprint for a more inclusive, accessible, and compassionate healthcare system. The initiative is grounded in the belief that health equity is not merely a goal but a moral imperative. It calls for a transformative approach, one that transcends traditional healthcare paradigms and proactively addresses the social determinants of health that disproportionately affect vulnerable populations. The goal is clear as we set out on this journey: to create a healthcare environment in which every person may obtain the healthcare they require to live healthy, full lives, irrespective of their socioeconomic situation, location, or cultural background. We work together and employ creative approaches to weave a tapestry of health equity that does not leave anyone behind

1 - Needs

For 2024, more than 5.3 million people across the BAY states were identified as requiring health assistance. The affected people are primarily concentrated in rural and hard-to-reach, security-compromised areas. Around 1.5 million returnees are in areas of relocation in the three states, and about 1.8 million IDPs are living in camps and host communities. Shifts in the security environment, particularly in rural areas and outside urban areas, have resulted in reduced health staff. Additionally, there are persistent challenges in securing medical supplies in the most affected areas. Numerous health partners have experienced difficulties deploying health personnel and providing medical supplies and commodities in remote areas due to the rising cost of helicopter transport. These challenges are compounding already weakened basic health services (such as immunization, maternal and child health services), nutrition services, and WASH services in IDP camps, which further increase the risk of communicable disease outbreaks. Congested camp conditions expose women and children to sexual abuse, as well as sexually transmitted diseases. In contrast, primary, secondary, and tertiary healthcare facilities and staff are present in areas where access to services has improved, such as major towns and urban areas. Gender-Specific Needs: Several critical gender needs will be addressed in the health sector, including timely access to basic healthcare services, gender-specific health issues of women and girls, such as reproductive health, SGBV, mental health, and the availability of female healthcare providers, as women and girls may prefer to be attended to by female healthcare providers, particularly for sensitive health issues, adequate menstrual health management and inclusive, gender-sensitive healthcare facilities. Projection of needs It is anticipated that in 2024, affected people will remain at significant risk of epidemic-prone diseases, like cholera, measles, diphtheria, and viral hemorrhagic fevers, due to low access to essential healthcare, seasonal patterns, lack of access to potable water and sanitation infrastructure, and cumulative socio-economic vulnerabilities because of continuous displacement. Women and children will continue to be the most vulnerable to disease outbreaks, especially cholera and diphtheria. 2 - Activities

the project is targeting 500 beneficiaries across Jere, Gujba, Geidam, Madagali and Michika LGAs of Borno, Yobe and Adamawa States. this population includes 1000 IDPs, 2000 host Communities and 500 Returnees vulnerable populations. the population comprises of 2000 women and 1500 men. activities to be conducted includes; Healthcare Outreach Clinics: Regular healthcare outreach clinics are conducted in underserved and remote areas, offering essential health services, preventive care, and health education to vulnerable populations. Mobile Health Units: Deploy mobile health units equipped with medical professionals, diagnostic equipment, and medications to reach communities with limited access to healthcare facilities. Telemedicine Services: Implement telemedicine services to connect healthcare providers with vulnerable populations, allowing for remote consultations, follow-ups, and access to medical advice via digital platforms. Community Health Workshops: Community health workshops can raise awareness about preventive measures, healthy lifestyles, and the importance of seeking timely healthcare. Tailoring them to specific populations’ needs can make them more effective. Education and resources can empower individuals and communities to take control of their health. Culturally Competent Healthcare Training: Healthcare professionals can better cater to diverse needs by improving cultural competence through training sessions. Patient Navigation Programs: Establish patient navigation programs to guide individuals from vulnerable populations through the healthcare system, assisting with appointment scheduling, transportation arrangements, and overcoming logistical barriers. Health Screenings and Vaccination Drives: Conduct regular health screenings for common conditions and organize vaccination drives to ensure that vulnerable populations have access to preventive care services. Collaborative Partnerships: Provision of Specialized and Non-Specialized Mental Health and Psychosocial support (MHPSS) services, established of confidential counselling room at the Health facilities for MHPSS assessment and support services, support in referrals and provision of Psychotropic drugs to severe Mental health cases in close collaboration with Federal Neuro-psychiatric Hospital Collaborate with community-based organizations, NGOs, and local stakeholders to amplify the impact of healthcare interventions. Pool resources, share expertise, and coordinate efforts to address healthcare disparities comprehensively. Financial Assistance Programs: Develop and implement financial assistance programs to alleviate the economic burden on vulnerable populations seeking healthcare services. Technology-Based Health Education: Utilize technology platforms, such as mobile apps and online resources, to disseminate health education materials tailored to the literacy levels and cultural contexts of the target populations. Training and Empowerment of Community Health Workers: Train and empower community health workers from within vulnerable populations to act as liaisons between the community and healthcare providers. Advocacy for Policy Change: Engage in advocacy efforts to influence policy changes that address systemic barriers to healthcare access for vulnerable populations.
