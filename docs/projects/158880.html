Project Summary

Project Objective :
Is to enhance the income of vulnerable and poor female and male farmers in the most affected areas of the Gaza Strip.
Project Summary :
The project is mainly assisting vulnerable and poor female and male farmers by providing them with one or more of the following services:
* Rehabilitation of damaged open lands
* Rehabilitation of existing water wells run by electricity or fuel
* Installation of solar systems for agricultural water wells
* Rehabilitation of existing water ponds
The project activities will be implemented all over the Gaza strip. NDC will partner with 5 local registered cluster NGOs who have extensive agricultural experience to implement the project. In addition, the project activities will be completely coordinated with the Ministry of Agriculture and the Food Security Sector.
The estimated number of direct beneficiaries is 925 female and male farmers while the total number of the farmers and their families is 5272. The estimated total budget for the project is $ 2,089,288.
The project's objectives match the HRP 2020 (part of 2018-2020 HRP) oPt, Strategic Objective country #( 3): The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued.
Furthermore, the project is specifically in line with the Food Security Sector (FSS) objective #(1) The resilience and the productive capacity of vulnerable households are restored/enhanced and livelihoods protected.

1 - Needs

Two million Gazain under Israeli blockade, electricity crisis and the unstable political and security situation continue to prevent access to livelihood opportunities and limit the free movement of people, goods, and services. Where an estimated 62% of households are severely or moderately food insecure (HNO2020), Moreover, the rates of unemployment are increased which exceeded 52% in 2019 (PCBS 2019). Additional, the irregularity of water availability caused severe losses to farmers as the production sharply decreased in terms of quality and quantity. Besides, the Great March of Return (GMR) has greatly affected the lives of farmers in border areas Access Restricted Areas (ARA) who are still suffering from limited access to their lands to cultivate, also the damages derived from the GMR. and According to the Ministry of Agriculture are still 20% of arable lands still not planted and farmer suffering from difficulties of practicing agriculture leaving in (ARA ) and, about 400 farmers have been directly affected due to the GMR demonstrations since March 2018 Moreover, Some 3,000 farmers have their livelihoods disrupted due to the ongoing crisis. In the meantime, all farmers in Gaza Strip are still facing a lack of access to agricultural inputs at affordable prices. (HNO2020). NDC is partnering with five NGOs operating within the food security sector. These NGOs have extensive experience in implementing agricultural projects including rehabilitation of damage lands, water wells, water ponds, and installing solar systems for water wells, which are all vital components towards building resilience against food insecurity in the Gaza context. During October 2018, nine focus groups were conducted (and the results were reconfirmed by 30 farmers through individual interviews in October 2019) by three local NGOs in different areas of Gaza Strip to discuss the main agricultural demands and stand on the top priorities to help developing the agricultural sector and improving the farmer's lives. 2 focus groups were conducted in the north and attended by 33 persons (16 males and 17 females). One focus group was conducted in Gaza city and attended by 17 men. 2 focus groups were conducted in Khanyounis and attended by 45 persons (18 males and 27 females). 4 focus groups were conducted in Rafah and attended by 90 persons (43 males and 47 females). In total, 9 focus groups were attended by 185 persons (94 males and 91 females. Besides, the participants in the focus group's age were between 20 to over 60 years old. During the needs assessment, farmers, both men, and women indicated that open land rehabilitation, water wells rehabilitation, and rehabilitation of existing water ponds and construction of new ones are the top priority. All the farmers indicated that utilizing solar systems to irrigate their lands is the highest priority due to the severe lack of electricity and the high costs of fuel. Solar systems will provide them with water at very low costs. Due to the nature of these activities, there was no significant difference in needs between male and female farmers and also the same also applies to the different age groups. Farmers, who are smallholder farmers with minimum assets, little or no access to livelihood enhancing methods have been selected through a consultative process undertaken by the partner NGOs. The process included gap analysis to identify indicators of food insecure or at risk of food-insecure HHs that are excluded from the socio-economic development process on grounds such as locality, poverty, and gender. The selection is further based on a community gap analysis, involving village councils, cooperatives, HHs who lost their source of income, small farmers, livestock breeders, and women-headed HHs. Besides, farmers who are suffering from severe electricity cuts and facing considerable difficulties in running their water wells and pumping systems and thus are not able to irrigate their fields will be also considered.

2 - Activities

This project is designed in a participatory manner taking into consideration the interest of the project's stakeholders. Beneficiaries' dignity, independence, contribution to food security are achievable through the project's activities. Selecting criteria based on the identified needs, gender equity and sensitivity without any bias. The project is highly coordinated with MoA and the FSS during the design process The project targets three main categories. The first is the affected farmers in border areas Access Restricted Areas (ARA) who are still suffering from limited access to their lands to cultivate, also the damages derived from the GMR through rehabilitation their damaged land (375 donums will be rehabilitated). The second is male and female farmers whose agricultural assets such as fields, agricultural water wells, The third category is farmers who are suffering from the sever electricity cuts and facing considerable difficulties in running their water wells and pumping systems & thus are not able to effectively irrigate their fields. It is expected that 925 (At least 165 female farmers and 760 males' headed household) will directly benefit from the project's activities. Their immediate families’ members with 5272 persons will benefit also. At the start of the project, local advertisement will be published in each of the five governorates to ask farmers to submit applications to benefit from the project based on predetermined general criteria such as being small farmers own less than 5 dunums, affected in the last wars, located in the target areas. More specific criteria will be developed for water wells rehabilitation and solar system installation such as licensed water wells, irrigating at least 40 dunums, located in a safe areas. The advertisement will be published in mosques, local NGOs premises, local committees locations, etc. Five selection committees from NDC and its NGOs partners, local committees’ members, local figures will be formed to select the best applications that meet the criteria. Final lists will be developed and passed to the ) MoA to get their feedback on the selected beneficiaries. Based on the comments of the MoA, a final list of beneficiaries will be developed. NDC will be responsible for the overall management of the project activities, procurement, financial disbursement, monitoring and evaluation, and reporting. Five local NGOs partners will be responsible for implementing the project activities on the ground, form the five local committees, finalize the beneficiary list in coordination with MoA, and implement the project activities on the ground. In implementing the project activities, NDC will coordinate with the MoA, FSS, & INGOs. Coordination with NGOs, local committees, municipalities, & other governmental bodies will be the role of the five partners. The project is a multi-year project. Most of the current activities such as water wells rehabilitation and installing solar systems for water wells will start in year one and continue in the second and third year. In addition, open land rehabilitation will also continue. The main reason is that the needs are huge and it is not expected that they will be met in one year. This is clear from the cluster targets and needs as determined by the FSS. Also, the project will build on some of the activities/outputs achieved in the first year for the second and third year. As example, the water wells rehabilitated in the first year, will be targeted to operate them utilizing solar/photovoltaic systems in the second year. Hence, solar systems will be implemented in the first, second, and third years. Project activities: • Open land rehabilitation targeting 1,250 dunums distributed equally all over Gaza Strip • Agricultural Water Well Rehabilitation (7 in Gaza and 3 in Khanyounis) • Install Solar/photovoltaic Systems for 15 water wells (all over Gaza Strip) • Agricultural Water Pond Rehabilitation (30 in Middle area, 30 in Khanyounis and 30 in Rafah)

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

NDC will ensure the dignity and safety of the targeted farmers by conducting the following four steps: 1. Before implementing the project activities: NDC and its 5 partners NGOs will conduct 20 awareness raising workshop (four in each governerates) about the project objectives, outputs, and activities. This to ensure that all the farmers are fully aware of the project and if any of them thinks or believes he will be negatively affected he/she will have the chance to inform NDC and its partners NGOs of his/her concerns. Consequently, NDC and its partners NGOs will immediately take into account this framer concerns and will solve an issues that might negatively affects him/her. 2. Beneficiaries selection: Beneficiaries will be selected on standardized social and technical vulnerability selection criteria consistent with humanitarian principles and practices and through gender-appropriate activities 3. Throughout the implementation of the project: NDC will conduct 3 community scorecard sessions in each governorate: one session at the start of the project, a second in the middle of implementation, and the third towards the end of the project. In total 15 scorecard sessions will be implemented. These community scorecard sessions will ensure that no harm is done to any member of the targeted community and will also work on improving the quality of services provided by the project. 4. Complaints mechanism: NDC has an active complaints system that will be also available to the local communities to use if any harm has been done. NDC will take corrective actions immediately.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project activities will be published and communicated in the targeted areas. Adds will be placed in governmental departments, public entities, NGOs facilities, mosques, supermarkets, etc. Furthermore, five local committees will be also formed in the target communities. One of their roles is to communicate the project activities to the local communities and ensure that target beneficiaries have equal and impartial access to hese activities. In addition, NDC and its 5 partners NGOs will conduct 20 workshops (four in each governerates) in the presence of local community representatives, including women association, key persons, and farmers of both sexes to discuss the project objectives and activities with the targeted communities. Also, an active complaints mechanisms will be operating so that any beneficiary who has any problem can use this complaints system, Additional priority in the selection will be given to the female-headed household as well as people with disabilities to empower them and strengthen their ability to face their challenges ,. Furthermore, all the project activities will be coordinated with the Ministry of Agriculture and the FSS and hence ensure equal and impartial access to the project activities.
