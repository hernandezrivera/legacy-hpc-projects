Project Summary

Summary
IMC proposes 1-year emergency integrated water, sanitation and hygiene intervention to respond to the needs of vulnerable and conflict affected IDPs and host communities in four regions of South Central Somalia; to respond to water, sanitation and hygiene needs of internally displaced people and vulnerable host communities. International medical Corps(IMC) will target Bay (Baidoa), Banadir (Mogadishu/Wadajir), Middle Shabelle (Jowhar) and Mudug (Galkacyo South) where it has been providing an integrated health, nutrition, protection and WASH intervention. To complement and improve the WASH intervention, IMC has noted critical gaps in the course of implementing WASH activities. It is on the basis of these identified needs that IMC is proposing interventions that are feasible and appropriate at this time. Access to water and sanitation remains a challenge in almost all IDPs and hard to reach rural villages. While IMC recognizes the effort of partners in mitigating the water and sanitation access constraints the need for improved access to safe water and sanitation remains a challenge. IMC will work with other WASH partners in the area to avoid duplication of services and will therefore work towards ensuring the already existing services are improved. IMC underscores the importance of ensuring WASH related gaps contributing to mortality and morbidity are mitigated, therefore IMC proposed water, sanitation and hygiene(WASH) interventions are suited for the needs and the context to ensure an effective and flexible response to emergencies throughout the program period. IMC will ensure there is adequate sector integration for all the WASH sub-sectors (Hygiene promotion, water supply & sanitation infrastructures and environmental sanitation) in the targeted areas.
All activities will ensure that protection concerns voiced by women related to risk of GBV especially in IDP settlements are taken into consideration. Women, men, girls, boys and persons with specific needs will be consulted at all stages of the WASH intervention. Special consideration will be given to enable access and facilitating water collection for disabled people. Sanitation infrastructure will be sex segregated and clearly marked, and will be equipped to enhance privacy. Hygiene promotion activities will involve women and men; and activities will be timed to fit around women and men’s other work schedules. Hygiene promoters will discourage sending children to collect water in large containers and to be in charge of disposing solid waste. Gender balance and equal pay and non-traditional work for women and men to challenge gender roles stereotypes will be ensured. Safe programming and mainstreaming gender at all stages will be ensured. Program staff will be trained on the application of WASH cluster guidelines. IMC will implement and hold its staff accountable to global Minimum Standards for Gender Equality and Women’s Rights. The integration of protection and gender staff in the project team will help ensure the implementation of key activities beyond basic safe programming and Do No Harm approaches.

Project Activities

Sub-sector 1: Water SupplyActivity 1a. IMC proposes the rehabilitation of 10 shallow wells across the four regions of operation. The expected number of beneficiaries per hand pump shallow well is estimated to be 500 people based on flow rate of 16 l/m as per the Somalia WASH cluster guideline and SPHERE standards. IMC WASH engineer will assess the technical aspects of the rehabilitation works to be undertaken in line with the Somalia WASH cluster guidelines. This includes verifying the water yield, depth of the well, water static depth, possible source of contamination, mode of abstraction of water and many others. Activity 1b. IMC will install solar powered pumps to 6 high yielding shallow wells with sufficient and sustained water yield of more than 5m³/hour. The water will be delivered through gravity from an elevated 10m³ water reservoir to 6 tap stands strategically located around the water source Activity 2. Emergency water trucking (EWT) - IMC proposes to undertake short term water distribution through water vouchers to 5 most affected Baidoa IDP settlements (Seef-dheer-330HH, Jubada-dhexe-250HH, Banow-85HH Qansaxdheere-300HH, and Haluul & Harqaan-155HH,) serving a population of 1,120 households (6720 individuals) Activity 3. IMC proposes rehabilitation of 3 strategic boreholes in the hard-to-reach nomadic villages of Galkacyo South district. Activities include: replacement of generator spare parts; replacement of submersible pump spare parts; repair and replacement of borehole rising pipes; and rehabilitation of water storage facilities and animal troughs. Activity 4. WASH team will conduct routine water quality monitoring and treatment of the water storage facilities with chlorine to maintain acceptable Free Residual Chlorine (FRC). Batch chlorination by trained staff ensures minimum levels of FRC is met before distribution. Dosing rates at the storage tanks will yield sufficient residual chlorine at the point of consumption. Sub-sector 2: SanitationIMC proposes construction of 80 shared household latrines, bathing spaces in targeted IDP settlements in Baidoa, Jowhar, Mogadishu (Wadajir) and Galkacyo south (Xarxar IDP camps). Each latrine will be shared by an estimated 3 households (at most 20 individuals) as per SPHERE standards Sub-sector 3: Hygiene PromotionActivity1. IMC proposes to carry out routine and continuous hygiene promotion activities at four levels: communities, schools, water points, and health facilities. Health education and hygiene promotion will target school children as the entry point and change vehicle for their families and communities. At the community level, the program will undertake massive health and hygiene education and promotion campaigns. Community-based hygiene and sanitation resource persons called Community Hygiene Promoters (CHPs) Activity 2. IMC is proposing the procurement and distribution of 4,500 hygiene kits to targeted IDP camps in the 4 targeted areas. IMC integrates WASH activities with health, nutrition and protection.

Mitigation Measures

On the risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral servicesfor survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosivehazards, etc.), IMC will enhance protection mainstreaming in all its WASH services. IMCthrough its Community Hygiene promoters (CHPs), and Community Liaison focal person will engage community stake holders on its available services and as well enhance community education, mobilization and awareness on available Protection services in its areas of operation. IMC as well via its other funding sources will ensure referral pathways is available for protection survivors. On the risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities; IMC will engage WASH committee, community elders, local authority and other relevant stakeholder’s inproject design, implementation and closure as well as in response to emergencies. Forbeneficiaries with disabilities and or with limited mobility, IMC will ensure its rehabilitated/constructed water supply and sanitation infrastructure are disability friendly to have ramp/easy access paths to accommodate persons with impaired mobility. IMC will ensure there is adequate integration and linkages with Nutrition/WASH/Protection services in the targeted areas to minimize distance travelled to access complementary humanitarian services.Standby Emergency Rapid Response Teams (ERRTs) comprising of key staff will bemaintained to enable quick respond to trauma emergency/outbreaks includingcholera, AWD, measles when a threshold is attained. Beneficiaries seeking servicesregardless of age, sex, clan affiliation, workplace and household will be treated equally. Different sociodemographic groups will be consulted on their specific needs and designs. On staff recruitment particularly community Hygiene promoters will be recruited from beneficiary communities and will be representative of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, marginalized ethnic groups). IMC will form gender and ethnic group balanced Community WASH Committees and community solid waste management volunteers to play an active role in identifying solutions and the decision-making processes, promoting a sense of ownership and improving the relevance and sustainability of the WASH services. On the risk that beneficiariesare not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms, IMC maintains a complaints and feedback phone number that allows anonymous submission of feedback. Timely corrective response or feedback will be maintained and logged. Throughout the project cycle, IMC will ensure that it communicates with affected populations in formats and media that are easily understood, respectful and culturally appropriate.
