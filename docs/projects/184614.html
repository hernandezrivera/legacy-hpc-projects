Project Summary

The project aims to increase WASH services to the over 6.4 M people in need in Somalia targeting districts that are categorized as stressed or severe in the severity scale, those that are hard to reach and those that have reported AWD/cholera cases. The project targets approximately 6,552 vulnerable IDPs 11,448 host communities with a special focus on vulnerable segments of the community including 7 803 women 9,900 children 1,800 PLWD and 875 elderly people. In line with HRP strategic objective 1, specific objective 1.2, this project will contribute to the WASH cluster objective of delivering life-saving WASH assistance to reduce acute needs among most vulnerable settlements and communities through emergency water tracking, water vouchers, provision of hygiene kits and hygiene promotion activities. Lifesaving Wash assistance will target hard to reach communities in Garbaharey and Dolow districts and IDPs households in Banadir and Dolow. This project is also aligned to HRP strategic objective 2, specific objective 2.1, contributing to WASH cluster objective of restoring access to safe and equitable basic WASH services to vulnerable communities and in schools. This will be achieved through rehabilitation of water infrastructures including shallow wells, water pipe network, elevated tanks and water kiosks. Solar pumping systems will be installed in boreholes and shallow wells to reduce the O&M of community water systems. Community mobilizations and capacity building in management of water projects and O&M of solar system will be done to ensure active community participation in the implementation and management of the water facilities. This project will also contribute to the WASH cluster objective of restoring access to basic sanitation and hygiene in schools in Abudwak and Dollow, through rehabilitation of gender segregated latrines with lockable doors from inside with hand washing facilities. All the WASH infrastructures proposed in this project are designed with consideration of the safety, equity and easy accessibility by vulnerable groups such as women, girls, PLWD and the elderly. The target beneficiaries will actively participate in the selection of the sites for water facilities in their locations that will be strategic to serve households within approximately 500m distance that will be in open and clear places to ensure safety of users. Additionally water kiosks will be designed to ensure easy water collection by girls, women, elderly and those with disabilities. For water trucking water collection points will be selected by the beneficiaries and will be at about 500m from the households and in open places for safety purposes. The water collection will be planned such that the elderly and PLWD will have separate times to collect their water. The latrines constructed in schools will be strategically located to ensure safety of users and dignity of users. Hygiene kits will be collected from reasonable distances from the beneficiary households for safety of the beneficiaries as they carry them home. Use of water and hygiene kits vouchers will further enhance safety and equity of vulnerable groups targeted in this project. Mainstreaming of protection and gender in this project will contribute to HRP strategic objective 3, specific objective 3.1 as well as the WASH cluster objective of maintaining safety, equity and accessibility of the vulnerable groups (including women, boys, girls, elderly and people with disabilities) when accessing WASH services. This project will build on WASH and COVID 19 interventions implemented by NAPAD (2019, 2020 and 2021) in Galgadud, Jubaland and Banadir regions that includes rehabilitation of water infrastructures such as water tanks, water pipe networks, installation of solar water pumping systems, installation of handwashing facilities, provision of hygiene kits and mass hygiene promotion using the mass media and door to door promotion by community hygiene

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

A voucher is safe and does not present any risks to the beneficiaries, unlike inkind support. With the voucher it is easy to specifically target more vulnerable persons such as women, PLWD, elderly thus this is chosen to ensure more inclusivity in the targeting. The voucher will also help in strengthening local markets as local suppliers will be used to offer the WASH services. This will not only avoid disruption of the supply of WASH products in the markets that may be occasioned by in-kind supplies from outside but will also inject cash in the local markets.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

NAPAD currently has staff working and residing in the targeted project districts who continuously provided information on the local markets during the design of the project. The staff monitored the trends in the availability of different essential WASH products in the local markets and the prices. Additionally they gathered information on the quality of different WASH products in the market.Observations in the target district markets is that the markets have sufficient supply of quality WASH products. The generally stable markets informed the choice of UCT in the project.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Exposure of displaced women and girls to sexual violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements, distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and services the project will 1. Design and construct WASH facilities that preserve the safety and dignity of its users. 2. Build separate toilets and bathing facilities for males and females. The facilities will be marked in pictorial form for illiterate users and work with the community to ensure they are used by the indicated sex. 3. Provide secondary enclosures around facilities or put locks on the doors to latrines to ensure the security of beneficiaries.This will be discussed with beneficiaries to consider their preferences. 4. Raise platforms and construct ramps at water points to ensure safe manual lifting to all, especially children, pregnant women, and persons with disabilities. 5. Consider safety risks to children. For instance, the project will consider the size of the drop hole; provide smaller jerry cans for children to collect water to avoid potential injury, and consider their physical capacity in designing water pumps. 4b. Protectionmainstreaming: Beneficiaries risk exposure to violence during registration/distribution 1. Ensure the location and access routes to WASH facilities and distribution points are safe. Water sources will be located in visible, central locations and not more than 500 meters from settlement and will be in lighted places. 2. Avoid placing all facilities or distribution points near military installations or areas prone to violent attacks. 3. Ensure that any weapon bearers should not be present inside distribution points. 4. Educate women and girls to walk in groups to distribution points on fetching water to reduce threats to their safety and their dignity These arrangements will protect beneficiaries who may face theft, intimidation, sexual assault, and extortion whilst taking their hygiene kits home. The hygiene kit will also be of appropriate size for easy transportation. 5. Identify central locations for distribution points-beneficiaries should not have to traverse long distances or pass through conflict zones to access distribution points. Additionally, distributions will take place in daylight hours to give beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall. Where necessary conduct door-to-door distribution and avoid large distributions which put beneficiaries at risk of attacks and pillage. 4d the project will be 1. Transparent with the affectedpopulations by providing them with accessible and timely information on selection criteria for targeted assistance, organizational procedures, and processes that affect them. Additionally, the project will set up accessible, confidential, and well-understood feedback mechanisms. For instance conduct feedback surveys while distributing hygiene kits, installing feedback boxes at WASH facilities, and provision of staff phone lines 2. WASH staff and committees should be representative of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, PWD) and will be given protection mainstreaming training so they can identify protection issues proactive responding to them effectively. 3. Ensure WASH staff, implementing partners, and volunteers working with affected populations understand, sign and adhere to a Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries. 4. Ensure all staff, implementing partners, and volunteers involved in distributions and direct contact with beneficiaries havereceived training in the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). 5. Provide clear information to the distribution actors and affected population explaining that beneficiaries do not have to pay or provide services/favors to anyone in exchange for receiving WASH assistance. Especially women and girls.
