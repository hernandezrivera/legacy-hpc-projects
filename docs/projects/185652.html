Project Summary

To address the identified critical needs of the both Rohingya refugees and host community facing the deterioration of shelters, NRC Shelter Programme will provide safe and dignified shelter and energy solutions to both Rohingya refugees in the camps, and vulnerable crisis-affected populations in the host communities of Ukhiay & Teknaf Upazilla, Cox's Bazar District. NRC will conduct all TSA3 and TDK activities through the IOM pipeline (IOM will provide all materials), with Sector’s strategy and coordination with all stakeholders including beneficiaries, RRRC, CiC, IOM, Sector and other IPs.
In the Rohingya refugee camps, NRC will distribute TSA3 and TDK to 4000 households (HH) in camp 8E, among them 7% EVI (280HH) will receive both porter and construction support. NRC will distribute cash for NFI to the most vulnerable 230 HHs in Teknaf host communities. Throughout the process, the shelter beneficiary’s family will decide their needs and will be given maximum choice to select materials based on their need. They will be engaged through awareness campaigns, focus group discussions and feedback sessions. Households lead construction, Engineers will technically counsel and ensure that minimum construction standards are met. Beside this, technical assessment and distribution will take place in emergency response. NRC will closely work with site management and protection team to identify and support protection related issues. Furthermore, Intersectoral analysis will take place to identify the most vulnerable, considering age, gender, disability, diversity and specific needs of individuals, and NRC will provide full support with porter, carpenter and volunteers. The team are regularly trained on Protection, Gender, Diversity and PSEA to ensure all interventions are mainstreamed with cross-cutting issues. Protection Mainstreaming principles – a) safety, dignity, no harm, b) meaningful access, c) accountability d) participation & empowerment will be promoted throughout the project implementation; while the CFM (hotline for complaints and feedback) will be constantly upgraded in line with the NRC CFM Policy. NRC has also engaged the PSEA Network to train staff and volunteers during the project period, and designated PSEA focal point for reports concerning NRC staff.
NRC will ensure priority lines and shaded space are provided in the distribution points for women, older people and people with disabilities, Separate training for males and females by male and female staff, and the inclusion of female volunteers for distribution and supervision of female-headed households. NRC will involve women, older people, PwSN in small scale, lightweight works during TSA3 implementation like cleaning, bamboo lattice making, count materials during unloading/distribution, token writing, thermal scanning, rope distribution etc.
In an emergency situation, NRC will deliver life-saving shelter assistance to 1000 HHs in most acute needs focusing on providing an immediate support in camp 8E (block B) in the event of a disaster (weather, fire etc.) as well as reviewing scope to respond outside of the Rohingya response. NRC will also increase its preparedness and responsiveness to environmental and climate-driven disasters through emergency response – this will include prepositioning of shelter materials.
The project also aims at mitigating and addressing tensions between the Rohingya and local communities generated by the HLP disputes, which will be addressed by the NRC Information, Counselling and Legal Assistance (ICLA) Programme. The HLP case management will be provided at the camps 8E, 8W, 9, 24-27, and 2 host community locations (Teknaf and Raja Palong). When needed, ICLA team will support the HLP TF with HLP assessments, development of guidance notes and advocacy papers.
PDM will allow beneficiaries to report their level of satisfaction with the distribution and distribution items. The COVID-19 guidelines will be followed during the project period.

1 - Identified Needs

The needs of the Rohingya refugees and host communities have been identified as a result of the Aug 2021 Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessments, which identified shelter materials as the critical priority needs in the Rohingya camps and access to income generating activities, conditional cash and other NFI are also critical need in the host communities; where 36% of Rohingya households reported not having made improvements to their shelter (in the 6 months prior to data collection).Shelter & Wash, Education and ICLA of NRC worked previously in Camps 8E. Shelter worked with TSA 2 and also in fire response in camp 8E in 2021. It would be an ideal location for TSA 3, given the significant needs, and also since community relationships are already established and there is good awareness of vulnerable individuals. TSA2 assistance was provided one year ago, thus, existing shelter materials provided to the displaced population and improvements in camp infrastructure remain insufficient for protection against adverse weather events. The recent monsoon season weakened the shelters. Untreated bamboo has deteriorated due to beetle infestation. The project is also informed by the 2022 Guidance PWG Note and the CDR needs assessment conducted by the NRC Bangladesh in June-July 2021, which have shown that roughly one in ten refugee households is engaged in tenancy relationships with the host community landlords and face rent extortion and threats of forced eviction; there is an increase in requests for relocation due to safety security concerns in and around the camps and within the camp consolidation initiatives; there is a lack of collaborative dispute resolution skills and information that can help resolve the ongoing HLP disputes, especially for disputes that affect refugees.Climate related disasters (Flood, cyclone, landslides) and fire are leading to increase vulnerability of communities living in the camps, and most likely to increase further displacement. Affected communities are facing lack dignified living in secured shelter. Therefore, in an event with humanitarian consequences, the project will also address the immediate needs of affected people who are in most acute needs providing shelter/NFIs.
