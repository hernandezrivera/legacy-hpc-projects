Project Summary

This initiative aims to provide quality alternative education to out-of-school displaced children in the Northwest and Southwest regions of Cameroon, in view of supporting their reintegration into the formal education system. This will be achieved through the deployment of a nine (9)-month accelerated education program for out-of-school children aged 9 to 12, training teachers, improving learning conditions, both in terms of reception structures and psychological support, and improving access to protection services, including civil documentation and specialized protection services.
In order to ensure that children continue to attend school once they have been reintegrated into the formal education system, the project will support parents, particularly single-parent families, widowed and single parents and elderly guardians, in developing or strengthening their means of subsistence and improving their housing conditions, in order to limit the impact of the socio-economic environment on children's access to their rights. Remedial courses will also be developed as an accompanying measure, and delivered pupils in the centre, during their first year back into formal elementary school, and to other vulnerable pupils who need extra support and encouragement in their studies.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Education remains one of the most pressing needs for children affected by crisis in the Northwest and Southwest regions, particularly in the peri-urban localities that host largest number of displaced persons. Unfortunately, schools are under heavy attack, with more than 96 incidents against education between January and August 2024 reported (source-Cameroon Education cluster, 2024). Access to education remains a major challenge in both regions, and despite an increase in enrolment data, an estimated 865,029 school-age children are in need of humanitarian assistance, including 195,648 IDPs (joint education cluster assessment, 2024) . Access to education for vulnerable children has been hampered by the destruction of educational infrastructure in villages of origin, the lack of civil and legal documentation, and the absence of safety nets for their effective reintegration into formal education. In addition, the humanitarian situation has had a major impact on the living conditions of vulnerable families, and for the past 7 years has prevented parents and guardians from prioritizing their children's education. Despite the efforts of various humanitarian actors to meet the educational needs of children in these regions, only 46% of the education funding required in 2024 has been covered, 2 months to the end of the year. Needs have also been exacerbated by structural challenges in the education sector: the lack of trained teachers to ensure quality education, and of classrooms to strengthen the capacity of host schools; the lack of WASH infrastructure in schools to improve the learning environment, and of suitable educational alternatives to enrol those thousands of children who have lost the right to education, support for pupils in formal and non-formal schools, through capacity building on their roles and responsibilities and finally, psychosocial support, a framework for the recovery of teachers, students, and communities in a safe and protective learning environment, thus strengthening the resilience and well-being of pupils. Given the educational needs in English-speaking regions and the need for a more agile approach, NRC intends to adapt the Speed School approach (Stratégie de scolarisation Accélérée Passerelle (SSAP), in collaboration with Cameroons education Administration to enable children to return to formal schools.
