Project Summary

WHO project aims to decrease avoidable under five mortality and morbidity related to severe acute malnutrition through improved access and quality of inpatient care for the Sever Acute Malnutrition (SAM) with medical complications in 134 stabilization centers operating in health facilities. The project includes curative, preventive and promotive measures in the same time. The activities will address the clinical life-saving interventions in Stabilization centers targeting the patients/children with SAM and the mothers/care-takers through IYCF counseling and young children nutrition awareness to lower relapse and prevent SAM cases in the same households. The project activities and costing factored in 49 SCs are supported by UNICEF(11 SCs) and Partners NGOs (38). The SCs supported by NGOs will require only Medical kits/ secondary level and lab investigation support, along with IYCF training and counselling, and UNICEF will cover 14% of the caseload through the 11 SCs, most of them in big towns.
Main activities: Training of the health and nutrition staff of 85 SCs (all except the ones supported by UNICEF and NGOs) to include IPD SAM initial training (7 days) and refresher training (3 days), provision of guidelines, Job Aids and IEC materials to all participants from the 85 SCs. WHO will procure and distribute to 123 SCs (all except the UNICEF supported SCs) medicines (SAM inpatient Kits – secondary level care), lab reagents for investigation essential for case-management, medical equipment and other supplies based on the needs identified together with MOH and partners.
To improve the quality of care and cure rate in SCs, WHO nutrition and public health officers present in all targeted states will conduct jointly with SMOH regular supportive supervisions in all targeted 134 SCs conducting on the job training, coaching, and adherence to the national treatment protocols.
Routine nutrition surveillance at HF level/SCs, essential for trends and gap identification and effective planning of activities will be enhanced through training, provision of recording and reporting tools for 123 SC (all except UNICEF supported), and better integration into HFs reporting mechanism to promote sustainability. WHO nutrition team will provide technical and operational support to the MOH at federal and state levels to regularly analysis the data of SAM inpatient care services (using the agreed performance indicators) to identify the trends, and challenges dis aggregated by sex and age. The information will be disseminate to all relevant stakeholders through nutrition cluster bulletins, and briefing during the meetings led by UNICEF and MOH. Effective coordination of WHO with all partners (Nutrition, Health, food security) will promote an improved referral system between the SCs, TSFP, OTP, and community. Linkages with communities will be strengthened through raising awareness and social mobilization conducted by trained community mobilizers/CHW in coordination with other UN. The modalities and subjects for these activities will be guided by the community members suggestions collected through exit interview and focus group discussions during supervisions. The project activities also includes small repairs of 25 SCs in order to ensure better environment for work and improved hygiene in the SCs.

Needs

Sudan continues to have high prevalence of global acute malnutrition (GAM). The results of MICS survey conducted in 2014 (published in 2015) showed the national prevalence of GAM at 16.3%, and SAM at 4.5%, which are above the emergency threshold for intervention (WHO standards).In reference to the Ministry of Health, HNO 2019 reported that of the 2.8 million children suffering from wasting (GAM) annually in Sudan, some 693,924 suffer from Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). 11 out of the 18 states have acute malnutrition prevalence above 15% at emergency threshold. The new waves of displacement since 2014, 400,000 new IDPs in 2014, 250,000 in 2015, more than 100,000 in first quarter of 2016), along with 1,2 million refugees are important aggravating factors. Poor rains (El Nino) and floods over the past few years led to increase in the food prices. The economic challenges faced by Sudan in 2018 further destabilized the food security and nutrition status especially amongst the most vulnerable. The projections for 2019 HRP show that about 53,628 children suffering of SAM with medical complications will be in need of in-patient care. Without specialized care these children risk of death is extremely high. In spite of some progress in regard with access to, and the coverage of nutrition services over the past years, the assessments for the TFU/SC situation in Sudan result (WHO & MOH 2016 and 2018) came up with a big gap in regards with access to SAM inpatient care services including capacity, quality of the services/staff qualification, availability of free of charge medicines, routine IYCF counselling for the mothers, and the quality of reporting and monitoring of SCs. In addition, the SCs/TFU in the capital of states and big localities, do not receive sufficient support from the humanitarian partners as these locations are usually excluded during the prioritization exercise, while the bulk of cases are treated in big hospitals. In 2018, 26,003 SAM inpatient children (with medical complications) were treated (FMoH report) in all SCs that represent a coverage of around 55% of the HRP target/expected caseload of 2018. This shows that the geographical coverage of SAM treatment services remains low, especially for the inpatient treatment. The main causes are; unavailability of proper outreach services to early detect the cases, insufficient financial and human resources investment by the Government and Humanitarian Community for scaling up SAM services through integration into selected PHC health facilities and all hospitals; poor access due to insecurity and remoteness in some of the most affected areas; insufficient health staff trained and skilled for the inpatient treatment SAM, and high turnover. Out of 184 localities, only 102 have at least one Stabilization Center.

•Technical support for the State and localities for the management and coordination of SAM inpatient care ensured by the WHO nutrition officers in Khartoum and targeted states. •Nutrition assessments, surveys and studies •Procurement and distribution of 800 WHO SAM kits (secondary level care) for 123 SCs (UNICEF SCs and caseload excluded), and equipment for 123 TFUs as requested by MOH and partners at around USD 180/patient. The budgetary calculation excluded the costs of therapeutic milk and RUTF for 134 SCs provided by UNICEF that also covers around 14% of the caseload (11SCs). • Repairs of 20 SCs in Darfur, W N, B N and Kordofan states to improve the infection prevention, and optimization of the work environment •Establishment of 3 Centers of Excellence and Professional training for SAM inpatient care in 2 states initially. •Procurement and distribution of laboratory reagents for routine testing for inpatient care for 123 MOH and NGOs SCs. •Updating and disseminating training materials, job aids, and IEC materials for 123 SCs, and nutrition partners. •Capacity building for health and Nutrition staff on SAM IP •Support the IYCF counseling (tools and training) for the mothers and care taker in all targeted SCs(134) •Joint supportive supervisions with MOH (every 3 months), aiming to improve the adherence to the treatment protocols. •Exit interviews during supervisions and dissemination of the findings on communities’ perception •Nutrition surveillance: training, reporting and recording tools, analysis of dis-aggregated data for dissemination to identify the trends and challenges. Support the integration of nutrition reporting into HF surveillance for sustainability. •Strengthen the linkages with communities through social mobilization in coordination with other UN agencies and NGOs to promote early detection, referral and treatment. • Contribute to the development of Sudan Multi-sectoral nutrition plans with related sectors (include Regional and HQs support)

Impact on environment/natural resources

Environmental protection of biological and chemical contamination is part of accountability to affected population. Working with patients who mostly suffer from communicable diseases creates real risks for staff, other patients, care=takers, environment and thus the communities. WHO will support and reinforce strict adherence to Universal Precautions (as per WHO/MOH guidelines) through provision of necessary materials such as safety boxes, self-degradable disinfectants, and personal protection equipment. In collaboration with WASH and Health partners support for availability and use of incinerators, pits for sharps, hand washing facilities in each SCs/HFs will be provided to ensure the protection of the staff, patients, care-takers, environment and communities. During each supervisory visit the environmental protection will be checked and the staff will be given orientation during the on job training. The training will include message on environmental protection from biological contamination as part of safe waste disposal and improved community level sanitation. Expired medicines will be destroyed (incineration) as per WHO guidelines and in the presence of health authorities.
