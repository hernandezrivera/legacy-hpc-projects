Project Summary

The Overall objective of the project is to improve Food security and Nutrition status of vulnerable people in Central Darfur, South Kordofan, Blue Nile and White Nile states. Geographical locations targeted by the project include 53 health facilities with catchment population found in 11 localities including Tadamon, Ghadeer, Elreef Elshargi, Zalingei, Wadisaleh, Bendasi, Mukjar, Al Diwaim, Guli, Kurmuk and al Tadamon.
The programme will provide integrated and timely assistance to vulnerable populations affected by disasters, conflict, and recurrent shocks/crisis (directly prohibiting recovery) in hard to reach and newly accessible areas. Priority will be given to, returnees, IDPs, nomadic and vulnerable host community members. Children, Adolescent Girls, and Pregnant and Lactating women (PLW) will remain the main target group. Total estimated number of beneficiaries are: Nutrition: 25,661; Health: 103,304 people WASH: 78,022 (M: 26,130; F: 24,120); FSL: 133,902 (M: 65,612; F: 68,290): and GBV: 39,732.
Food security aims at increasing access to Food and livelihood for 22,317 households in target locations through three major interventions which includes provision of cash assistance for food (Target: 9,960 HH), livelihood cash transfer for IGA/assets (Target: 6,638 HH) and provision of agricultural inputs such as seeds and tools (Target: 5,719). AAH will link Humanitarian response with resilience and development intervention including conducting Value chain assessments, Socio-economic inclusion, Participatory Resilience Analysis Measurement (PRAM), Community action, participatory capacity, and vulnerability analysis (PVCA), Community managed disaster risk reduction (CMDRR), Holistic management and natural resource management.
In WASH, AAH focus on improving access to Water and Sanitation services and hygiene promotion. Sanitation facilities and Water source construction or rehabilitation, Water quality monitoring, WASH in health, Hygiene promotion distribution, Distribution of hygiene and dignity kit are main interventions. WASH interventions are linked with nutrition and health services and practices.
The goal of Health and Nutrition is to deliver evidence-based quality integrated health and nutrition service and practices for vulnerable people at facility and community level. Major nutrition intervention includes CMAM, IYCF, Micronutrient interventions while health interventions focus on outpatient consultation (IMCI), reproductive health and EPI. Nutrition service will be delivered in 53 OTP/TSFP and Eight stabilization integrated with primary health care and WASH. In CMAM, A total of 25,661 individuals suffering from acute malnutrition are targeted including 3,661 children with SAM and 22,000 children and PLW with MAM (U5 MAM: 19813, PLW: 2188). IYCF counseling will be delivered to 24,611 at facility by skilled counselors and community level though Mother support group. Vitamin A, Iron-folic acid and MNP distribution will be implemented in a coordination to reduce micronutrient deficiency in children and PLWs.
AAH focus on Gender Based Violence (GBV) which is significant protection issue as target areas have recurrent communal conflicts which may increase risk of women for physical or emotional violence. As such, the project targets 39,732 people through capacity building (1,058), support vocational and life skills (370) and community awareness creation (38,304). All health worker and community health.

1 - Needs

Since fighting broke out between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) in multiple cities across Sudan on 15 April 2023, at least 866 people have been killed and over 6,000 injured1. IOM estimates that 1,965,946 people have been newly displaced internally while 522,707 flee to neighboring countries seeking refuge. The number of people in need of humanitarian assistance has increased from 15.8 million, estimated in November 2022, to 24.7 million in May 2023, representing a 57% increase3.Approximately 82.2% of Sudan’s population experiences barriers to access healthcare (MSNA 2022) and nearly 79% live under poverty threshold (SDG report 2021). Food insecurity forecasts indicate that as rural areas enter the hunger gap period and if the conflict continues, the number of food insecure people in Sudan is expected to increase by up to 2.5 million, to 19.1 million people across the country in the next three to six months. According to the interagency assessment May 2023 conducted in White Nile, the food security status is ‘poor’ , all IPDs have no source of income and rely on ‘insufficient meal’ with less preferred food. Food insecurity rates in Central Darfur (45%), South Kordofan (23%), White Nile (22%) and Blue Nile (50%) are among the highest of the country (CFSVA June 2023). About 40 per cent of the population in phase 2 of the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) could slide into phase 3. 38% of households resort to the food-based negative coping strategy and 57% of the households had to resort to livelihood based coping strategies, focusing on immediate food needs, and depleting their assets (CFSVA June 2023). According to revised Nutrition Cluster HRP 2023, a total of 4.6 million children under-five and women in Sudan are estimated to be suffering from Acute Malnutrition. The National Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) remains at 13.6%. According to S3M 2018 survey, the GAM rates in CD, SK, BN and WN states are 16.5%, 7.8%, 7.2%, and 9.6% respectively with wide difference among localities. The GAM rate in Ghadeer and Tadamon localities of South Kordofan has increased to 18% and 17.2% respectively (ACF SMART survey report April 2022). Overall malnutrition rates in CD and SK are above WHO emergency threshold (>15%). Sudan maternal mortality rate is among the highest in the world, at 311 per 100 000 livebirths (Wharton 2020). 50% of children’s acute malnutrition here is associated with repeated diarrhoea or worm infections related to poor WASH conditions (UNICEF). Access to improved water source is low with recorded 29.8% in WN, 34% in BN, 62% in SK and 12% in CD. Population with access to improved sanitation facilities are 28% in WN, 56% in BN, 43.3% in SK and 16.8% in CD (ACF Multi-sector KPC survey report 2022). Women’s low literacy rate in Sudan (compared to men) limits their access to complete, accurate, timely, and gender-focused information that could otherwise support services fundamental to addressing preventable issues, such as disease outbreaks. Percentage of literate young women (15-24 years) in CD (27.4%), BN (36.1%) and SK (49.2%) is below the national percentage of 59.8%. The percentage of girls married before the age of 18 in SK (46.7%), CD (54.6%) and BN (50.1%) are higher than national percentage of 38% (OCHA 2022). As per ACF gender analysis findings, majority of men, boys, girls, and women interviewed consider domestic violence against girls and women as a norm. Sudan has the highest Female Genital Mutilation (FGM) rate in the world where 82.65% women and girls of reproductive age group are survivors (S3M 2018).

2 - Activities/Outputs

The programme will continue to focus on Four core sectors, by promoting synergies and complementarities. In Nutrition and Health, AAH will focus on delivering integrated high impact health and nutrition services to vulnerable children, adolescents and women, provision of quality treatment for acute malnutrition cases integrated with primary healthcare and increase knowledge and skills of households and caregivers on key household practices. Interventions include screening, referral, and treatment of acutely malnourished children and PLW , IYCF services, outpatient treatment (IMCI), reproductive health (ANC,SBA, PNC & FP), vaccination and health education. To ensure quality of service, the program will provide multiple capacity of training for health workers including SAM management, IMCI, EPI , BeMONC. Health facilities will receive adequate therapeutic products, Essential drugs and medical equipment's, IPC supplies, furniture's , stationaries, monitoring reporting tools. Effective referral system will be established for smooth transfer of cases from community to facility, and within nutrition and health services. Baby-Child Friendly spaces will be created and supported to provide cognitive and emotional stimulation for crisis affected women and children through psychosocial and protection support. In WASH, AAH will maintain regular WASH assistance for the most vulnerable populations to ensure communities have access to safe water, dignified sanitation services and increased good hygiene practices. The proposed activities include: drilling of new boreholes, upgrading of existing hand pumps into mini water yards, construction of water treatment plants, construction/rehabilitation of Gender-sensitive dignified latrines, incinerators and hand washing facilities in health facilities, strengthening capacity of water users’ committees and health facilities staff to ensure proper use, operation and maintenance of WASH services. To conduct water treatment at HH level, AAH will distribute chlorine tablets (0.33mg/l) targeting HH with PLW and children with SAM/MAM. Furthermore, the project will carry out rapid water quality tests to measure prevalence of E. coli bacteria. To improve menstrual and reproductive health, AAH will distribute dignity kits for targeted women/girls. The project will also distribute hygiene kits for households. AAH also will carry out regular hygiene promotion sessions aiming to prevent spreading of diarrheal disease and COVID- 19 including implementation of baby WASH. In FSL, in line with HRP and FSL sector objectives, AAH will focus on promotion of beneficiary resilience and reduction in beneficiary aid dependency through multi-purpose cash assistance, income-generating activities and distribution of agricultural inputs, home garden kits, tools and seeds. AAH will also mainstream disaster risk management into FSL activities with emphasis on climate change. Beneficiary selection will be based on vulnerability criteria including HH with acute malnourished children and PLW, Female headed HH, elderly and disability. HH registration, selection and beneficiary verification by community will be undertaken before delivering assistance.Gender and protection will be mainstreamed throughout the programme for effective and impactful programming, to contribute concretely to GBV risk mitigation. AAH will focus on bridging gap of access of girls and women to essential services and ensuring their participation through programme cycle, sensitizing people on GBV at community and facilities engaging local actors. AAH will prioritize reinforcement, activation and / or establishment of referral pathways for enhancing support for GBV survivors, as well as supporting free psychosocial counselling (by phone). The project activities contribute to resilience and early recovery. Percent of Life saving activities that contributes to resilience per sector includes Nutrition 61%, Health 50%, FSL:33.3% and WASH: 57%.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

ACF has conducted brief and simple project level environmental screening using NEAT+ tool to assess sensitivity of affected environment, categorizing risk to environment and affected community. In addition, Environmental and social management framework (ESMF) was complete recently considering Central Darfur, South Kordofan, White Nile and Blue Nile. ESMF has assessed national and international legislations, policies and regulation including World bank environmental and social standards (ESS). Environmental baseline data were collected on climate, water source, soil type and Ecosystem and biodiversity (Geography, Vegetation, flora and fauna). Based on these assessment, AAH has identified and categorize environmental risks and impacts based on six defined level impact including category of impact, nature of predicted impact, consequence of predicted impact, duration of impact, spatial scope and likelihood of occurrence. Environmental risks and impacts identified include water scarcity and contamination, low capacity of solid waste management, risk of deforestation, loss of biodiversity, land and soil degradation, release of Infectious material from health facilities, Onsite and offsite waste pollution including nutrition products sachets, air pollution. Considering project activities, Most of these risk assessed are categorized as adverse in nature but unlikely to occur with minor consequence and localized effect. The mitigation measures against identified environmental risks included protection of water sources, ensure water outtake does not exceed the replenishment of the water source, Segregate waste at each point of generation and ensure proper handling of waste disposal, Avoid plastic and non-degradable waste for kit packaging, reduce vector breeding sites , protection of vegetation and avoid cutting during the rehabilitation of water sources, ensure use of environmentally friendly materials, Promote Holistic Management (regeneration of soil fertility using animal impact),promote Natural Resources Management, Application of Agroecological and climate smart agricultural practices and technologies, Optimize use of trucks/transport of materials, equipment and vehicles will be turned off when not in use, debagging of cement will be done in an enclosed area by workers to avoid air pollution, avoid indiscriminate burning of wastes at the site to reduce air pollution. Mitigation measures also include promoting and planting trees at health facilities and community and use of solar panel for submersible hand pumps in deep bore hole. The project will consider 3-5% of budget to invest of activities that prevent and mitigate environmental risks and bring positive impact.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Gender dimension at all stages of the intervention will be considered through an active and inclusive participatory approach, working closely with CAFA and JMCO and all stakeholders on the ground, ensuring appropriate responses throughout the ground for each target group: girls, women, boys, men and disabled groups. Activity data will be collected and disaggregated by sex , age and Disability where possible, and the results of their analysis will be used to adjust the program. AAH will also ensure gender balance in each deployed team, including maximizing women's and girls' access to the project and facilitating communication, awareness and exchange of information. AAH will also ensure that any arising doubt on protection or gender-based violence related incidents are identified and forwarded to the relevant partners for appropriate action. According to AAH gender assessment findings in Central Darfur, south Kordofan, blue Nile and white Nile, girls are being subjected to early marriage (indirectly related to multi layered crisis in recent year) and boys and their families are refusing marriage if girls have not had FGM(even though it has been banned in Sudan this year). Also, girls and women are exposed highly to protection concerns during floods outbreak due to collapse of infrastructure, especially water and sanitation. AAH strongly adheres to the principles of Do No Harm and given the specific context will ensure close monitoring of project implementation and post-implementation in order to ensure supported rights holders are not subjected to negative externalities as a direct result of AAH or LNGO activities. Furthermore, Protection will be at all cases considered and AAH, CAFA, JMCO will continue to actively work together with partners to ensure Protection remains a topic on the radar. AAH works in close collaboration with the UN Clusters (WASH, Health and NUT and the technical working group), which remind and include protection rules in the response so that all actors are in line with the same standards. Therefore, AAH will follow the clusters’ recommendations together with their own standards against GBV. As with any emergency, there will be a Protection working group which includes the specific actors who are tasked with protection. AAH will also ensure that any arising doubt on protection or gender-based violence related incidents are identified and forwarded to the relevant partners for appropriate action. Through proposed intervention, AAH will provide sensitize communities, including male community and religious leaders on gender discriminating practices and provide dignity kits for girls and women of reproductive age. Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) is also mobilized by AAH MEAL through multiple channels (hotline numbers, complaint boxes) in order to maximize potential for having feedback from marginalized community members and enable them to have direct access to AAH, for reporting on any concerns, including protection issues.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

AAH will involve the technical services as well as administrative and customary authorities in order to ensure the smooth running of the activities and to guarantee the quality of the work carried out. Furthermore, the project beneficiaries will be mobilized at all stages to ensure ownership and sustainability of the work. In its intervention, AAH will adopt approaches that encourage the needs of the most vulnerable people to be taken into account when targeting and carrying out activities, thus helping to meet their immediate needs. AAH has conducted multi sector need assessment in targeted states to understand the nutrition, health, WASH and FSL through consultation of key informants and focus group discussion with women, mew, boys and girls. Recommendation from the community are considered on the design of project including the need to recruit more female health workers and Community volunteers. AAH has consulted beneficiaries through PDM regarding their current needs and gaps, what type of food and non-food items they suggest for future distributions and whether they prefer cash distribution over in-kind assistance. The results showed that most of the beneficiaries suggested Cash grant while some suggested food items such as oil, maize, sugar, rice, wheat, lentils which is what AAH distributed in 2020.Contents of the different food and NFIs Kits and selection criteria are displayed using banners/communication materials at distribution sites with the information to beneficiaries on channeling their feedback/complaints to AAH. The project teams will organize awareness and information sessions with beneficiaries on project’s objectives including dates and periods for each activity. AAH teams will use 'talking points' as way of formal introduction to each community upon arrival in order to avoid any confusion or dispute regarding the project and beneficiary selection process. During that time and subsequent visits by staff and volunteers, all information will be shared with beneficiaries about the feedback mechanism established by AAH. The reception of complaints and feedback from the populations of the intervention areas, partners, authorities, NGO staff and the different actors is done through complaints boxes at villages committees’ level and through CRM (including hotline numbers)which will be distributed to targeted beneficiaries. In addition, informal feedbacks from beneficiaries will be collected during the monitoring activities. Each information request or complaint received is recorded, documented, categorized and managed accordingly. The ethic committee at HQ level in Madrid would be involved for complaint and feedback related to corruption, fraud, PSEA.
