Project Summary

Ongoing conflict in eastern Ukraine continues to weaken child protection systems creating
new risks for affected boys, girls and their caregivers. COVID-19 pandemic as well as quarantine restrictions added new challenges for conflict-affected families, whose resources are already scarce. To respond to the urgent needs, UNICEF is planning to strengthen child protection systems (C01) and to provide direct services to affected children and their families (CO2). Specifically, UNICEF will continue to 1. provide psycho-social and protective services to children and their caregivers (including to survivors of gender-based violence, children affected by violence and children and caregivers affected by mines and unexploded ordinance) (P02), 2. implement activities aimed at prevention of violence and mine-risk education) (P01), and 3. ensure capacity building to
community professionals working with children (P02). In addition, UNICEF will carry out activities directed at improving parenting skills among caregivers to strengthen relationships between caregivers and their children and promote their wellbeing. Taken together, these activities are aimed to strengthen local response systems, including availability of
specialized services, as well as to ensure direct service provision where needed services
are not available.

1 - Needs

The situation in eastern Ukraine remains volatile. Some 3.8 million people, including 1million children, are currently affected and over 1.5 million are displaced. Nearly 14,000 civilians have lost their lives or were injured in Eastern Ukraine because of the conflict. Of particular concern are those children living within 20 kilometres from the 'contact line’ inthe government-controlled and non-government-controlled areas. As of 2020, 6291 children from Donetsk and Luhansk region obtained a formal status of victims of armed conflict. They continue to experience recurrent shelling and violence. Disruptions in humanitarian access, the limitedcapacity of local institutions, weak rule of law and restrictions imposed on protection monitoring in non-government-controlled areas have all severely weakened the protective environment. Deteriorated family and community support mechanisms, daily stressors and loss of hope for the future continue to cause distress for children and their caregivers.This led to economic problems and continuous psycho-social needs. In addition, caregivers, highly affected by stress and hardships, often lack parenting skills, thus, failing to ensure the nurturing and caring environment for their children. Landmines and explosive remnants of war (ERW) continue to be a major concern in eastern Ukraineleading to death, injuries and disrupted livelihoods. While the largest concentration of mines and other ERW are in areas along the 'contact line,' they can also be found scattered throughout much of Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts. When children or caregiversare severely impaired from a landmine and/or ERW accident, it negatively impacts their physical and psycho-social health, their education/training and the ability to sustain their livelihoods. At the same time, limited services exist to support mine or ERW victims, and often their needs go unaddressed. Furthermore, ongoing hostilities and economichardships led to increased family- and gender-based violence and neglect of children. This is especially true in the face of COVID-19 pandemic, which have led to increase of domestic violence and violence against children cases (based on data from La Strada hotline and Ukrainian Foundation for Public Health GBV mobile teams) restricted access to social services and benefits, freedom of movement for the most vulnerable families residing in non-government controlled areas. According to the National Police statistics, 1157 cases of domestic violence were addressed in Donetsk and Luhansk region within the first nine months of 2020 with 792 out of them reported by children. While protection of children's rights, responding to gender-based violence and family violence are parts of the National Human Rights Strategy 2021, limited prevention activities services exist for survivors of violence. Community services are understaffed and cannot effectively respond to the problems, especially in the conflict-affected areas. Since the introduction of COVID-19-related quarantine, EECPs mostly remained closed with limited exceptions for so-called “corridors” arranged at Stanytsia Luhanska and Novotroitske EECPs according to pre-agreed lists of those allowed to cross the contact line. Closure of EECPs has been a significant barrier for the most vulnerable people from NGCA to access essential services in GCA and has blocked the process of state-recognized birth registration for newborns. Based on FOM report by OSCE, some 44% children who had been born in NGCA (both Luhansk and Donetsk regions) were registered in GCA, meaning that their families had to travel with newborns without access to basic child-friendly services at the EECPs even before the quarantine.Moreover, in September 2020, series of massive wildfires have killed four people and ravaged 22 villages in Luhansk region (GCA), having partially or completely destroyed housings of over 130 families with children.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

In order to address the above needs and achieve positive outcomes for children, the following activities will be undertaken: - Awareness raising and information dissemination among boys and girls, their caregivers and professionals on gender-based violence, violence against children, key risks affecting children; sessions for caregivers on positive parenting, COVID-19 informational materials (P01) - Psycho-social support (PSS) - UNICEF will continue to provide PSS, case management and referral services to boys, girls, men and women including to survivors of gender-based violence and violence against children as well as those children in alternative care arrangements and with a risk of separation from families (CO2/P02) - Mine Victim Assistance - UNICEF will provide assistance to victims of mine and ERW accidentsand will work towards strengthening national and local response systems (C02/P02) -Capacity building/CP system strengthening - UNICEF will provide capacity building to community professionals working with children, as well as to caregivers, to enable them to better respond to conflict-related child protection(CP) risks. In the context of quarantine restrictions capacity building activities will be conducted both offline and online, in format of webinars. The key areas include gender-based violence, violence against children, mine risk, child protection in emergencies and conflict-related child protection risks. -EECPs intervention - UNICEF will support the governmental coverage of essential services at EECPs by means of arranging mother-and-child oriented services for caregivers with access to private and equipped space for newborns feeding, baby changing station, sanitation services and dissemination of information about child protection risks and available child-oriented referral options at the neighboring communities (C02).-Individual protection assistance- To support local population affected by the wildfires in Luhansk region, UNICEF will provide multi-purpose cash-based assistance to the families with children, who have partially or completely lost their housing. The grants will be meant to cover their basic urgent needs to ensure decent level of living and access to services (C01). Additionally, as Sub-Cluster lead, UNICEF will dothe following: 1. Organize meetings to review child protection risks and interventions, current accountability practices, gaps and opportunities 2. Ensure ongoing capacity building of local partners on key child protection issues (Child Protection in Emergencies, Child Protection Minimum Standards, etc.) 3. Organize and facilitate training for sub-clusterpartners to raise awareness and build capacity on accountability to affected population. Reporting on the results achieved will ensure that disaggregated data is available (sex, age, disability). The following Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) mechanisms will be used during implementation of the projects: -Core Commitments to Children (Emergency handbook) -Harmonized Approach to Cash Transfers framework -UN Protocol on Allegations of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (involving implementing partners) -Child Centered Disaster Risk Reduction -UNICEF Guidance on Risk-Informed Programming - Guidance on Including Children with Disabilities in Humanitarian Action
