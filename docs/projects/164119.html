Project Summary

Shafak's health program aims to contribute in the provision of access to life-saving and life-sustaining humanitarian health services for people in need and conflict affected population in northern Syria. The proposed intervention will prioritize internally displaced people and other vulnerable populations especially (women ,children and people with disabilities) in the targeted areas. The intervention will be implemented through:
1. Continue supporting a comprehensive package of health services including: Primary Health Care, and Mental Health in one of the existing health facilities in northwest Syria. The motivation of this project is to continue providing health services in the already established and equipped centers to the population in western and southern rural Aleppo with outreach and referral activities to the higher level of health services. The project will comprise two convergent components: primary health and mental health care, and their activities will be provided through the following health centers:
a)- Comprehensive PHC Center with basic obstetric and neonatal care (level 4)
2. The provision of Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (EMONC) services and
management of SRH-related diseases in addition to the pediatric health care through supporting the specialized hospital for women and children which provides maternal and child health services to most vulnerable people in the rural of Idlib, Marretmasrin and through outreach teams. (HF-Code: G0700073).
3- Continue Supporting 2 dialysis centers 1 in Idlib and 1 in Qah with running costs
and dialysis kits. In addition to conduct capacity building and training for the medical
staff of the two centers:
a)- Qah Dialysis center in Al-Hydaya Hospital.
b)- Shafak Dialysis center-Idleb: (HF-Code: G0700074).
4- Continue Provision of Secondary Health Care and obstetric services through Expanding an existing health facility in rural Idleb to Include a Surgical Unit and a Labor Ward.
5- Continue Support the implementation of social mobilization activities for the Routine Immunization & Campaigns funded by UNICEF and implemented by Syrian Immunization Group (SIG) and ACU.
By achieving the above components, Shafak aims to contribute to enhancing the health status in the targeted areas.

4 - Needs

Primary Health Care Proposed Facilities:According to the latest HeRAMS reports, updates from the health cluster and Aleppo Health Directorate, there are only two PHC facilities that provides BeMONC services in the whole of the western and southern rural of Aleppo; one of them is Abin PHC center (in Abin Samaan community) and is currently supported by SRD, while the other one, proposed to be supported under this project, is Al Jeena PHC center whose previous support ended in March 2020. By indicating the above, the need to support Al Jeena center was clearly identified.RH Proposed Facilities:According to the needs assessment conducted by the Reproductive Health Working Group and UNFPA in March 2019 related to women and girls further threatened in North-Western Syria, In Idleb governorate, an estimated 918,000 women and girls of childbearing age are affected by the crisis NWS in Syria. The proposed RH facility is approved by the WHO to be part of the health network project phase 5 which will start at the beginning of Dec 2019. This project aims to enhance health system coordination mechanism and provision of health services, so the proposed RH facility will be a referral center for all PHC and BeMONC centers in the surrounding areas. Dialysis Proposed Centers:According to IPC assessment of dialysis units in northern Syria which conducted by SRD with coordination with WHO, the Estimates indicated that there are approximately 600 dialysis patients (only 6% under 18 years old) receiving care in 20 dialysis centers across the region. There are approximately 7,000 dialysis sessions delivered per week.Of all dialysis patients receiving treatment at the 18 hemodialysis centers in northwestern Syria, according to the screening conducted under the supervision of WHO/Gaziantep Office, more than 50% of patients (300 patients) were infected with HCV and about 5.4% of patients (32 patients) were infected with HBV, the screening reported also 1 positive case of HIV.

5 - Implementation of activities

In accordance with Shafak’s cross-border strategy, we will be responsible for directimplementation in the target locations coordinating with and gaining approval from healthauthorities in NW Syria, building on their extensive network, and facilitating access. Localcommunities will be involved in Shafak’s capacity development efforts and suitabletraining. Shfak will be responsible for ensuring that quality and accountability standardsare met, will provide required training to staff and ensure that contractual requirement areadhered to and met. Further we will be responsible for all program and financial reportingto the donor. Shafak will also continue to coordinate with relevant cluster and othercoordination bodies in Turkey. This joint program will be conducted through three mainphases as the following: Phase I: - Recruitment of needed staff for each project. -Procurement of needed equipment, devices, drugs, consumables, tools for training andtools for implementation. Phase II: Launch of: - Outpatient services, comprehensivereproductive health services and chronic disease treatment in all facilities. - Emergencysurgery, emergency obstetric and new-born services in the hospital. - PHC services. -Coordinate with health partners to identify locations of mobile clinics. - Providingspecialist care for renal failure patients. Phase III: - Reporting, data analysis, monitoring and evaluation.At the start of the project, Shafak will conduct a project opening meeting with Shafak staff. The project activities and timeline will be discussed and all data gathering tools, including monitoring links on specific tools, will be shared and agreed upon. Throughout the project, all beneficiary data for all activities will be collected and recorded, disaggregated by sex and age. Shafak teams will send their activity reports weekly to Shafak office in Gaziantep.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project overall objective aims at obtaining full respect for the rights of the individualwhere they need it the most. Throughout the project life cycle the principles of protectionmainstreaming are incorporated. All activities are designed to avoiding causing harm andprioritize safety and dignity of targeted populations, insuring meaningful access,accountability, and participation through several, action such as: • The project is designedto meet the needs of the community especially women, boys and girls considering thecontext, tradition, and norms in the community • Location of facilities will be accessibleand easy to reach for all people., also safe and away from any military location. •Community will be clearly and widely informed about the date, location and timing of theservice provision, and their opinion will be highly considered. • Team staff will be trainedto be skilled in delivering PFA, identifying CP&GBV cases and refer them to adequateservices. • Insure that the location is suitable for women, girls, people with disabilities,children headed household, old people, and other affected categories in the community---outreach. • Distribution and providing services for specific categories mentioned abovewill be to the door. • Teams will be trained on PESA, Child safeguarding, and will sign thecode of conducted related. • project team will set up appropriate mechanisms throughwhich affected populations can receive the information they need, provide feedback onthe activities. accountability officer will be responsible for information provision,collecting and responding to feedback and will report directly to HQ to ensuretransparency • Feedback will also be continuously gathered in line with standardizedcenter management protocol and monitoring and evaluation procedures, and doing soshall help improve protection mainstreaming. Shafak will apply the Whole of Syria Health Sector 2020 Protection Risk Analysis (PRA) recommendations.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Airstrikes and Shelling over NWSShafak would make sure to follow below measures in response to this expected risk:• security assessment of the operational areas to avoid sites that have been targeted before.• Prevent and decrease crowds in front of the center.• Equipping the staff with VHFs.• Training the staff on how to deal in cases of shelling and conduct practical training.• Equipping the center with fire extinguishers• An evacuation plan for center ready and communicated to all staff• An emergency contact list is available at the center.• Staff to be aware of communication protocols Risk 2: closing of Bab al-Hawa BCP that can resulted in a shortage of consumables, equipment, and kits. Shafak would make sure to follow below measures in response to this expected risk:• Monitoring the general security situation in the border area. • provide each center with consumables and medical kits that are enough for each center to work for 3 months at least.• using a different route for securing the supply chain process instead of Bab al-Hawa BCP by using Bab al-Slama BCP near Azaz in northern-countryside of Aleppo. Risk 3: Deviation of aid.Shafak would make sure to follow below measures in response to this expected risk:• Informing field team not to comply with any request from a person or armed faction to give them vouchers.• Stopping the work and withdrawal from the center, relocation also can be an option according to the situation, if there is any threat from the military factions, and then report the subject case to our donor.• An ATC Check to be done for all suppliers\ vendors before signing the contract with them. Risk 4: shortage of qualified technical staff for recruitmentShafak would make sure to follow below measures in response to this expected risk:• Multi-shift approach will be followed to allow flexibility in recruiting part-time staff.• Salary scale will be in line with Health Directorate salary scale.
