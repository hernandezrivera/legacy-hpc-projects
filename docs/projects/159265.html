Project Summary

This project aims to mitigate and respond to the mental health crisis faced by young people in oPt, which results in increasing high-risk coping mechanisms. The focus of this project is on young people (10-29 years), who are often left behind, as interventions often target children or older adults. In emergencies, young people are particularly vulnerable to the risk of mental health and psychosocial consequences and destructive behaviors. Young people in oPt are exposed to high levels of traumatic experiences and violence through e.g. the Great March of Return, escalations in violence, harassment (GBV), movement restrictions, house demolitions, imprisonment etc. Which correlates with widespread mental, behavioural and emotional issues. There is a humanitarian responsibility, to both protect and address young peoples’ needs and to engage them in humanitarian preparedness, response and recovery, where they play a vital role. MHPSS is underdeveloped at a national level and in most instances, life-saving MHPSS services and information remain out of touch and sparse, especially those offering adolescent and youth-sensitive services for the unique needs of young people. It is vital that adolescent and youth-sensitive services and information are developed, implemented and sustained. There is still a level of mistrust in terms of confidentiality and stigma hindering from seeking assistance. Capacities of communities need to be built to ensure critical access to MHPSS by tackling cultural and societal barriers leading to stigmatization and misconceptions of mental health.
This project will contribute to objective 1 of the Health and Nutrition cluster. UNFPA plans to respond to the growing need of MHPSS among young people covering communities in Gaza, Area C, H2 and EJ. It will be complementing WHOs work with MoH with a focus on providing services by focusing on NGO health facilities and health providers.
The interventions will cover the following key areas:
Provision of mental health and psychosocial first aid to adolescents and young people, including training for service providers in the detection of symptoms, assessment and treatment as well as referral pathways and triage of mental health cases.
Delivery of community awareness and education sessions on mental health, stigma, negative/positive coping mechanism and misconceptions around depression, suicide, violence, and similar; delivery of tailored community-based training on adolescent and youth mental health.
Integration and standardization of the provision of adolescent and youth-sensitive services in developed specific spaces within educational institutions (i.e. university) and NGO clinical (PHC) fixed and mobile settings to increase accessibility and support the use of adolescent and youth sensitive MH services and information.

1 - Needs

The protracted humanitarian crisis in oPt is characterized by high levels of traumatic experiences, leading to a widespread crisis in mental health, which is underreported, under-treated, and underfunded. Essential for mental health and wellbeing is the emotion of feeling safe and secure, and the loss of psychosocial security has led to an inability to satisfy needs. The increased urgent need in Gaza is due to deterioration of health, escalations in violence, exposure to traumatic events and the “Great March of Return”, leaving an estimated 187,800 people in severe need, including 19,282 males and 18,723 females under 18 years. A high number of young people (10-29 years) in EJ and Area C are exposed to a coercive environment with high levels of violence by settlers, harassment, movement restrictions, imprisonment and house demolitions. People with mental health needs at a severe and moderate level in need reach 62,266 in EJ and Area C, including 31,880 males and 30,386 females (HNO 2020). The Palestinian population is young, with 69% under the age of 29 years and the majority between 10-19 years (PCBS). The continuous exposure of traumatic and violent experiences contributes to progression in high levels of mental, behavioral and emotional issues among young people. Which is shown in the high rates of depression, post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), anxiety and high-risk mechanisms and behaviors in form of substance abuse (especially in Gaza and EJ), violence (including GBV), and unprotected sex (possibly leading to STIs and unplanned pregnancies) (Youth in Palestine: UNFPA). Gender and age-differences, form of vulnerabilities and residency influence exposure and risk to traumatic experiences affecting the mental health and psychosocial issues and coping strategies, accessibility to services, responses by the health sector and society and health-seeking behavior which determine the needs for MHPSS intervention. For instance, females often have higher needs for specialized intervention and more affected by social norms (MH Strategy 2015-2019; UNFPA) while adolescent boys and young men resort to riskier coping strategies (UNFPA). Some of the most vulnerable youth groups are PWDs reaching 2.5% WB and 4.2% Gaza (PCBS), those affected by GMR, orphans, spatially vulnerable, GBV-survivors, out of school, juveniles, young women and adolescent girls/boys, Bedouins and refugees (UNFPA; MH Strategy 2015-2019). How young people are supported in the preparedness and response is critical and have lasting impacts on the recovery process of individuals and communities. It is crucial to build resilience through targeted life-saving humanitarian responses. MHPSS including psychosocial first aid and community-based support must be an integrated part of humanitarian response to meet the urgent need in providing equal and impartial adolescent and youth-sensitive services. Health services are often not seen by young people as “friendly”, limiting how many seek services. In-service training and mentoring in integrating adolescent and youth-sensitive services (mhGAP and other standardized guidelines) are required through capacity building of personnel to ensure advanced, safe, non-judgmental and disability sensitive individualized case management. In combination with integration of MHPSS for young people into fixed and mobile clinics, including ensuring existence of service provision in designated safe and secure spaces. The existing social stigma and sociocultural norms among the broader community increase vulnerabilities of young people and hinder them from seeking life-saving assistance. Delivery of community awareness sessions and community-based training on MHPSS, MH, stigma, misconceptions connected to vulnerable groups, coping mechanisms is of importance in increasing the urgent accessibility and acceptability of services and building a crucial protective and self-helping environment with high levels of preparedness and resilience.

2 - Activities

Activity 1. Delivery of critical MHPSS for young people in Gaza, Area C, H2 and EJ. MHPSS for young people will be provided by integrating adolescent and youth-sensitive MHPSS into NGO fixed, mobile health clinics and home visits. Services will be provided in safe and confidential spaces. Continuous consultations with young people, key stakeholders, community health workers and other health professionals will be conducted to ensure the provision of MHPSS meet urgent needs. MHPSS will be incorporated into existing youth-friendly centres present in Gaza and WB. Mobile clinics and home visits will contribute to reaching out to people with no access to fixed PHCs due to remote location, vulnerabilities (ensuring services are available and accessible for PWDs) and existence of cultural norms hindering seeking life-saving care e.g. within post-operative outreach programs for those with new disabilities as a result of the GMR. Activity 2. Conduct mentoring and in-service training for NGO health providers (PHCs) to increase capacity building in managing and providing adolescent and youth-sensitive MH services and information (4 Gaza and 4 WB). The training will focus on NGO health providers, complementing WHO’s work with MOH PHC-services. It will cover how to provide individualized MHPSS for young people, including meeting the specific needs of PWDs by incorporating guidelines. NGO health care workers also need to be trained on how to train others for e.g. using mhGAP guidelines and manual, they can conduct community group sessions and disseminate the information. Activity 3. Conduct 8 trainings for community workers and mobilizers on MHPSS (Y-Peer Educational Network and PMRS volunteers). Trained community workers to provide services and tailored sessions. The training as per mhGAP guidelines will include PFA, PSS, mitigation of coping mechanisms and stress management, usage of mhGAP algorithm to increase engagement in detection, monitoring and assessing early signs. Also, trained on how to identify and assess the needs and communicate with PWDs. Further, gain local perspectives on stigma and misconceptions and messages and principles. Delivery of community awareness and community-based sessions will address young peoples’ mental health, incorporating specific needs of PWDs, specific MH gender and age-related needs, coping mechanisms, detection of early signs, stigma and misconceptions of vulnerable groups and MH etc.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

An email to be established for usage when needed help e.g. informative purposes, cases of referral and complaints. Feedback will be provided after all activities (training and sessions) by anonymous surveys (online and paper-format) or included evaluation exercises. Young people who have received care will get the option to do a satisfaction survey and/or leave feedback in a complaint box (established at both fixed centers and mobile clinics, which will be regularly checked). All the mechanisms and information about complaint mechanisms will be available and accessible in different formats in order to meet peoples’ different needs and disability-inclusive. A schedule will be developed for field visits covering both results-oriented and finance monitoring, which will be conducted throughout the year to track and verify a purposeful and correct implementation. The results-oriented will for instance examine the quality of activities e.g. provision of adolescent and youth-sensitive MHPSS and inspect purchased and installed equipment are utilized correctly. Meanwhile the financial will inspect how resources are managed and if used responsibly according to purpose. A project committee will be established with key stakeholders to oversee implementation and take decisions accordingly. Gained feedback/complaints will be addressed and used in the monitoring, evaluation, accountability and learning processes. It will generate valuable insightful information about the project’s course, to assure and improve meeting the needs through the planned humanitarian actions. As stated previous, feedback from all target groups will feed into evidence-based management decisions. Management decisions and changes depend on the nature of received feedback e.g. change in targeted communities, more and/or change in training and support for health professionals and community workers, need for scaled-up or reconstructed sessions and initiatives, reallocating funds etc.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

UNFPA will act upon the gained understanding with the priority to consider and uphold the rights and to not cause any negative impact and harm. Safety and security will be guaranteed by upholding dignity and protection in the provision of services in friendly spaces. NGO health providers will be trained and mentored to deliver non-judgemental, disability sensitive and high-quality adolescent and youth-friendly services ensuring confidentiality, privacy and respecting the wishes about the care of those seeking help, including meeting the specific needs of PWDs. At the same time, the specialized personnel needs to be supported in delivering services and upholding their own right and dignity through being treated equitably and fairly. In relation to health facilities (both fixed and mobile), safety will be reached by identifying and securing surrounding areas minimizing threats and vulnerabilities. Infrastructure adaptations will be made if needed e.g. ramps, railings, braille signs to NGO health facilities in order to make services available and accessible for everyone. Including taking relevant measures for locations where information sessions will be held which need to take into consideration different needs, in particular PWDs (accessibility to and within facilities, sign language translator, accessible and available information to all). Also, if needed providing separate waiting areas for males and females, specific service hours and the availability of both female and male staff is essential. At least one qualified NGO health professional will be working in the clinic. For young people, it is important to provide a physically, emotionally and socially safe and supportive environment. Inequities and divides among them and their communities must be considered, and ensure the participation in sessions or receiving care do not get backlash from community or family. Meaning, culture and customs bust be respected throughout the whole cycle in order to ensure that people are not put at risk. Further, negative effects on the local economy and livelihoods have been addressed by the project not building a parallel system for the provision of health services, instead strengthening local capabilities, by utilizing existing fixed resources. The responses through this project will, therefore, be carefully coordinated to aim for complementary structures. Further, it considers the impact on the environment that can affect safety, rights, and dignity of people, aiming for a low level of carbon footprint and to handle materials and equipment in an environmentally friendly manner based on available options throughout all activities and the whole implementation.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

This project is designed to provide equal and impartial accessibility without barriers and discrimination through all planned activities. Impartiality is upheld through striving for a large outreach and accessibility by coordination with other operating actors in combination with continuously conducting needs assessments. The project will primarily target identified vulnerable young people at risk by providing crucial MHPSS through existing health clinics (fixed and mobile) and home visits. It is of importance to highlight their rights to health care and how/where to obtain it by comprehensive awareness and informational campaigns. The project is based on the needs of young people, but take into consideration possible differences in urgent needs, faced risks and faced traumatic experiences, which are gender-or age-specific, vulnerabilities (including specific needs of PWDs) and connected to location of residency. The project takes into consideration the arrangement of peoples’ access and availability to services including adapting services and information (developing a specific alternative communication strategy in order to ensure reaching everyone, especially those at risk of exclusion). It will include both fixed, mobile clinics and when relevant home visits. Barriers will be tackled that may restrict freedom of movement and/or people from seeking help, for instance, making sure all disabilities are considered (people with reduced mobility and non-mobility-related disabilities), and those who cannot access services due to for instance socio-cultural norms and restrictions by tackling stigma and misconceptions at all levels. Mobile clinics will especially have the role to enable services and information to reach people in more vulnerable communities. Further, community action is in focus by delivering tailored sessions and initiatives based on different outreach strategies by community workers (peer educators and volunteers) within communities targeting different population groups, including PWDs, which will have a big outreach including both direct and indirect target groups. It will take into consideration needs based on gender, age and vulnerabilities and disabilities since the need for MHPSS and accessibility to services are different. It will ensure that selected communities will gain a better understanding and knowledge about MH and psychosocial wellbeing/issues, coping mechanisms, available MHPSS, vulnerable groups etc.
