Project Summary

Afmadow and Kismayo in Lower Juba are areas with persistent high acute malnutrition rates. Both areas have IDP and urban poor as vulnerable populations whose state is affected by multiple aggravating factors.
In Afmadow acute malnutrition state is stressed. GAM among Kismayo IDP and urban is Serious. SAM rates however remain high, beyond the recommended sphere threshold increasing the risk of death among children 6-59 months. Acute malnutrition among PLW in Kismayo is stressed (FSNAU, 2022).
Among IDP food insecurity was reported as high mainly due to social economic impact of COVID 19. WRRS is currently implementing integrated health and nutrition interventions in Afmadow and Kismayo that it intends to sustain beyond 2021. This continuum of care accorded by integrating nutrition programs for treatment of acute malnutrition has proved to be effective in reducing prevalence and thus the organization is working hard to ensure the program is sustained.
In all locations services will be offered through both mobile and fixed sites. Mobile teams will cover rural areas where services hardly reach so as to increase coverage, while fixed facilities will cover the town areas and IDP sites where services are within a comfortable working distance. CHW will also be employed to ensure the population is encouraged to use these services and that some services are offered at village/community level to increase coverage and use of available nutrition services.
WRRS plans to scale up and include integrated nutrition and health interventions in the camps where it has operational presence. Besides scaling up, WRRS plans to integrate with other sectors that address underlying factors of acute malnutrition ,with protection remaining a cross cutting theme.
All sites will offer treatment of acute malnutrition (MAM and SAM) for both children 6-59 months and pregnant and lactating women, who remain vulnerable to acute malnutrition, and prevention services for the same vulnerable groups.
Project will also include: identification and referral of protection victims, prevention of COVID 19 and hygiene promotion with distribution of hygiene kits and risk communication.
Proposed interventions will include all gender groups, marginalized groups and the disabled who are often left out or given unequal treatment. Disabled will participate at all levels of the project cycle. Staff will be trained on disability inclusion as to eliminate discrimination. All data will be dis aggregated according to age, sex and disability. APP and protection will also be mainstreamed. The project will adhere to set government policy and guidelines and any other MoH protocol, while observing national and adopted international health guidelines.
Coordination with health actors and those from other sectors will be enhanced.
Staff will be trained in revised IMAM guidelines, IYCF and community mobilization.
All this will be done through 3 fixed sites and 3 mobile teams covering both IDP and host community.
