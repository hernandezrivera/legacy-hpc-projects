Ensuring uninterrupted access to quality Sexual Reproductive Health & Rights (SRHR) for displaced and vulnerable women, girls, youth & adolescents in the midst of the Covid-19 pandemic in Northeast Nigeria

Start Date

31/12/2021

End Date

30/12/2022

Project Summary

To mitigate the impact of COVID 19 on the health care of Women, Girls, Adolescents, and Youth: This project will increase access to lifesaving basic Sexual & Reproductive Health (SRH) services for vulnerable IDPs, returnees, and host communities in the Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY) States. Comprehensive SRHR services including family planning will be provided to women of reproductive age including pregnant women, adolescents’ girls, young people, and men in communities and health facilities observing strict COVID 19 Infection Prevention Protocols (IPC), using appropriate Information Education & Communication (IEC) as well as Behavioural Change Communication (BCC) strategies. The survivors of obstetric fistula complications will also benefit from supported referral and specialized services. Medical equipment, supplies, Reproductive Health (RH) Kits, dignity kits, and other items essential for SRH services will be procured and prepositioned to ensure uninterrupted service amidst COVID 19 restrictions to access and attendant utilization of essential Human Resources for Health. To ensure smooth coordination, advocacy, and leadership of the Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) working group under the health sector is to be strengthened, work with other health partners to channel support to the maternal health program and other specific SRH response; advocate at the health sector level to ensure uninterrupted access to maternal health, and non-diversion of healthcare resources at the expense of pregnant, lactating women and adolescent in dire need of SRH services. The humanitarian crisis in Northeast Nigeria has been ongoing for 12 years has led to the need for essential SRH services. The project aims to ensure the continuation and strengthening of essential SRH services across the BAY States.

1 - Needs

As the COVID-19 pandemic rages on with new variants ever since it was declared as a global pandemic by the World Health Organization (WHO) in the year 2020, the fatigue continues to grow on COVID-19 IPC protocols. This has increased the number of women unable to access sexual reproductive health services, especially family planning facing unintended pregnancies, gender-based violence, and harmful practices. It may surge as an increase in new infections apart from STIs and HIV/AIDS, which can lead to health facilities to focus on COVID-19 at the expense of essential SRH services for women, lactating adolescents, and girls including those that are disabled. It is evident that the enormous scale of COVID-19 is having on women, girls, adolescents, and youth in need of essential SRH services as health systems become overloaded, facilities closed or only provide a limited set of services to women and girls, and many choose to skip important medical check-ups through fear of contracting the virus. The pandemic is deepening inequalities, and millions more women and girls now risk losing the ability to plan their families and protect their bodies and their health plunging them towards financial impoverishment and a deleterious cycle of diminishing health. During public health emergencies, human and financial resources are often diverted from various health programs to respond to infectious disease outbreaks. Sexual and reproductive health services are being impacted by the pandemic and must be prioritized. Before the emergence of COVID-19, health facilities in the affected states of Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe were damaged as a result of the conflict. There has also been a significant disruption of vaccination campaigns and other essential health services for children and other vulnerable groups in Hard-to-Reach (HTR) areas. In addition, funding has been a major challenge. In 2021, the health sector had received only 10.2 percent of its funding requirements as of August. The humanitarian situation is expected to worsen due to new COVID 19 variants and uncertain if the vaccines developed can protect women and girls. The ongoing conflict across the BAY States experienced by the health system includes disrupted health system in many inaccessible LGAs, non-functional health facilities, lack of health care staff, overwhelmed response capacity of international humanitarian agencies, and lack of resources. A tightening of pre-existing movement restrictions, economic deterioration coupled with strained public services will further increase humanitarian needs and compound existing operational challenges. It is critical that all women have access to birth spacing and contraceptive options by choice, quality safe birth, the continuum of antenatal care to postnatal care, including essential screening tests according to national guidelines and standards, especially in epicentres of the pandemic, where access to services for pregnant women, women in labour and delivery, and lactating women is negatively impacted. Vaccine hesitancy is already a problem in the BAY states of the North East, at such more community-level sensitization and awareness on the usefulness of available COVID-19 vaccination critical to achieving some level of uptake and prevention of new COVID-19 variants. With the declaration to close IDP camps by the Borno State Government (BSG) to return and relocate IDPs, the SRH needs of those that have and being moved must be factored in planning, especially as they will be settled in communities already in dire need of assistance or locations without required basic health infrastructure. In addition to the already targeted groups, the number of special groups in need of protection and livelihood assistance has increased with the repentance and returns ex-combatants and individuals captured by Boko Haram, along with their families.

2 - Activities

UNFPA will continue to work with the State Government in the BAY States and partners to prioritize the needs of women and girls of reproductive age and to respond urgently during the challenging public health emergency. Our priorities will continue to focus on strengthening health systems, procuring and delivering essential supplies like PPEs to protect health workers against nosocomial infections, ensuring access to sexual and reproductive health and gender-based violence services, and promoting risk communication and community engagement. The following activities below will be carried out by the State Governments, partners but supervised by UNFPA:• Train more health workers, particularly doctors, midwives, and Nurses on the COVID 19 IPC protocols, risk mitigation and prevention of stigma and discrimination, and engage them and other relevant cadres in the sensitization of Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW), Adolescents, and Young People (AYP) on the COVID-19 infection symptoms and related prevention and hygiene messages.• Protect health workers (in particular midwives, nurses, medical officers, obstetricians, and anaesthesiologists) by ensuring that basic protection (PPE) is available (gloves and masks, gowns, and protective lenses.• Provide technical guidance on reinforcing infection control measures within health facilities, including triage flow and segregation of neonatal and maternal health units in exceptional cases and according to the decision of governments. Pregnant women with respiratory illnesses must be treated with utmost priority due to the increased risk of adverse outcomes, and Antenatal, neonatal and maternal health units must be segregated from suspected and confirmed COVID-19 cases.• Ensure women’s and girls’ choices and rights to sexual and reproductive health are respected regardless of their COVID-19 status, including access to contraception, Antenatal Care (ANC), emergency contraception, and post-abortion care.• Ensure women and girls access to healthcare is maintained, including sexual and reproductive health services with special attention to PLW, AYP among vulnerable populations including persons living with disabilities and persons living with HIV. • Ensure PLW, AYP with suspected, probable, or confirmed COVID-19, including women who may need to spend time in isolation, have access to woman-centered, respectful skilled care, including obstetric maternal screening tests, fetal medicine, and neonatal care, as well as mental health and psychosocial support with readiness to care for maternal and neonatal complications. • Strengthened coordination through working with the State Ministries of Health and relevant line ministries, and the implementing partners to ensure availability and access to essential SRHR including maternal and newborn health services. • Support supply chain (modern contraceptives, maternal and new-born life-saving drugs, and supplies, maternal health equipment, IPC material and supplies, educational and counseling materials) In humanitarian contexts like COVID 19, this should include the implementation of the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) for Reproductive Health in Emergencies.• Support scale-up of COVID 19 testing to PLW accessing care and galvanize partner support to ensure that the most in need of critical COVID 19 vaccination receive it when it becomes available.• Provision of basic essential quality healthcare services to affected IDP returnees and host communities irrespective of their COVID-19 health status• Improve the provision of comprehensive Sexual and Reproductive Health Services in the BAY States, including procurement and prepositioning of essential medicines and life-saving RH Kits for effective management of survivors of sexual assault, delivery of other RH services, and management of complications.
