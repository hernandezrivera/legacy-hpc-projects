Project Summary

The proposed project to improve the protection of individuals and communities, identify, prevent and address protection risks where NRDC will target 22,037 individuals of IDPs, Host communities and returnees (women, girls, men and boys) including the most vulnerable populations of elderly, people with disabilities, children, women. In NRDC’s previous implementation, protection concerns were identified through the complaints and response mechanism and a number of protection gaps were identified including protection along distribution points, distance from households to the distribution collection points, issues around safety and accessibility to markets, Gender Based Violence, access to services, legal aid and counseling thus including community protection interventions in the current project will help to protect internally displaced persons, returnees and host communities from violence and abuse and ensure an enabling safe environment through which the most vulnerable women/girls and men feel safe. The number of returnees in Manyo, Fashoda and Panyikang continue to increase, with most vulnerable affected virtually struggling to meet ends needs, this has exerted pressure on humanitarian actors to intervene in providing emergency Humanitarian assistance, by extending the intervention assistance and also increasing the number of target beneficiaries will leverage our support in most vulnerable, returnees, IDPs and Host communities in this area. NRDC will continue to strengthen its collaborations and partnership with local authorities, stakeholders, community leaders and Community structures, as such this structures will be supported through capacity building workshops and training in protection, gender sensitivity and inclusion, peace building, and support ownership for sustainability. In the long term we expect these communities to be resilient, self-sustaining and be able to cope with the support of the local support structures established. NRDC will increase its field monitoring and support through regular interactions with community beneficiaries ensure response can effectively reach those in need, and local actors are strongly supported to work closely with communities, particularly existing community structures while promoting a holistic approach to integrating activities in line with priority needs of most vulnerable this will increase efficiency and effectiveness of NRDC to improve programme delivery. NRDC will ensure protection concerns is captured in protection information products, such as assessment reports, monitoring reports, and protection snapshots, allowing for appropriate and relevant advocacy with the clusters, humanitarian partners, and other main stakeholders to mitigate risks arising from the consequences of conflict and the resultant displacement. It is important to note that NRDC is already active in these Counties and has been implementing a range of integrated projects including unconditional cash program, women empowerment through skills training, protection mainstreaming, market support, cash-for-work and provisioning of agricultural and fisheries inputs. NRDC protection programming will ensure evidence-based interventions, at the individual and community levels, ensuring appropriate action is taken to prevent and respond to protection risks while particularly focusing on existing community and government structures.

1 - Needs

According to the January 2019 IPC assessment, projections for May-July 2019, 6.87 million people in South Sudan (60% of the population) will be severely food insecure, including 50,000 people in state of famine. In the Upper Nile region, 630,000 people (61% of the population) projected to be severely food insecure during the same period, including 5,000 people in state of famine. Food insecurity remains the main concern in the project areas. Needs remain much higher than the overall humanitarian response can address. WFP food distributions are conducted in all targeted areas, but only target approximately 25% of people in need. 69% of households in Upper Nile have access to land for farming, but only manage to produce an average of 2.9 months of food for the household per year. Acute food insecurity is driven by conflict, low crop production, cattle raiding, presence of IDPs and influx of returnees, high food prices, restricted market access, poor market functionality and poor road conditions. The weakened community structures have over several years of instability, resulted in numerous protection concerns, which affect different demographics differently. REACH (2019 Fact Sheet) reports that in Panyikang the main protection concerns for boys below the age of 18 are related to conflict. Women and girls face sexual and gender-based violence as the main protection concern, as reported by 81-100% of assessed settlements in Panyikang, 61-80% in Fashoda and 21-40 % in Manyo. In Panyikang, 44% of assessed settlements report the presence of landmines or unexploded ordnance .Social cohesion has been weakened over the past years in the targeted communities, due to large scale and repeated displacement, as well as influx of displaced populations from elsewhere in the country. This has taken a toll on communities, which have traditionally provided protection and support. For example, in Fashoda, IDPs in 38% of assessed settlements report poor relationships with the host community. Competition over scarce resources has divided people, often along clan or tribal lines, but also between neighboring communities. They are less willing to share resources, such as water points, agricultural land, or grazing land and water points for livestock. The expected influx of returnees, as the peace agreement takes hold, has the potential to put more pressure on communities and may aggravate underlying tensions further. While a steady stream of returnees continues to return to their places of origin access to land and housing is still a challenge especially in areas of return, it is likely that the majority is waiting to see evidence of full implementation of the peace agreement. Insecurity impedes access to services in many areas . In Panyikang, 61-80% of assessed settlements report safety fears when accessing their preferred water source, while 44% indicate that health services are not accessible due to insecurity and 11% fear for their safety when accessing education services. Even with these security concerns, 41 -60% of assessed settlements in Panyikang, and 0-20% in Fashoda and Manyo, report continued arrivals of new IDPs in the area. At the same time, 81-100% of assessed settlements in Panyikang, Fashoda and Manyo, all bordering Sudan, report the presence and continued influx of returnees. While in Fashoda the main concerns focused on military presence, water shortage, lack of infrastructure and limited access to services, respondents in Panyikang were much more concerned with inter-community and government-opposition conflicts but also with limited access to services. In all areas, early and forced marriage and sexual abuse are perceived as major threats to women and girls, while forced recruitment and lack of job and business opportunities are seen as major threats to men and boys. In all areas, people report not feeling safe at night especially when military personnel are present. Police and chiefs are seen to maintain law and order.

2 - Activities

NRDC's Protection activities will focus on protection risk prevention and protection analysis. The proposed activities are: 1. Mobile and static protection assessments and protection monitoring. The protection data collected will be processed, analyzed, stored, and shared with relevant actors. The data collection and monitoring activities, will done through community based protection networks (CBPN), monitoring visits, focus group discussions, key informant interviews, observation and population data gathering.2. Community based protection risk mitigation and activities to reduce vulnerabilities and enhance the resilience and coping mechanisms of communities facing protection risks. These activities include: community based protection networks and structures that will be trained to identify protection risks, and supported to mitigate these risk. 3. Produce and share monthly static protection assessments and reports to inform protection and multi-cluster response and advocacy in Fashoda, Panyikang, Malakal and Manyo Counties.4. Form Payam Peace clubs among women and youth from among the host community, IDPs and returnees as part process of ensuring conflict-sensitivity to the support durable peace will be critical for sustainability of the actions this will include recreational activities for youths.5. Establish/strengthen CPCs comprising men and women of the Payams identified this committee will be elected through community meetings who are as representative of the community as possible (general assemblies) in order to ensure accountability and credibility. The CPCs will be trained on Protection principles and they will identify the main threats they face, and the actions they can take to mitigate them.6.Produce energy-saving stove through formation and training of women groups, youth groups in the different Payams this is a Protection-cum-livelihood activity and is part of the risk reduction community‐based protection actions. The Project will support the implementation of fuel strategies to particularly reduce displaced girls and women’s exposure to violence and harm when they collect firewood this is anticipated to be therapeutic and of benefit to their emotional/psycho social well-being, this activity will provide an alternative income generation for the women engaged in production and sale of traditional alcohol.7. Establish safe and friendly spaces for women and girls as an entry point for provision of other services including GBV services and referrals, psycho-social support.8. Create legal awareness to the returnees, IDP’s and host communities on HLP to ensure they are aware of their rights and provide referrals for legal representation where necessary.9. NRDC will Support legal empowerment for displaced persons by provision of information, legal counseling, representation and mediation so that the full range of options for resolution of disputes can be understood by them. This will enable them to determine the process that is most appropriate for their situation. 10. Support legal assistance for displaced women to engage with all forms of dispute resolution mechanisms available to them at the local level.11. Train local leaders, law enforcement agents and local-level structures and should support government and local authorities to ensure that statutory law provisions promoting equal HLP rights are applied by customary and religious dispute resolution mechanisms. 12. Cash transfers to selected beneficiaries (conditional cash e.g CFW).

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

To increase the self-resilience of targeted households, NRDC will improve the ability to actively involve and promote the participation of the IDPs and returnees affected by displacement and conflict including PWD and PSN, as we believe that they are most important actors in their own protection and survival. NRDC will provide a rights-based humanitarian assistance as we build on the affected populations’ own capacities and enhance their resilience. The planned project offers a range of advantages in many contexts one being a more empowering approach to secure the right to life with dignity and creating awareness on human rights and access to legal representation. NRDC will also implement activities that are aligned with the principle 4 of the CHS and ensure that people know their rights, their entitlements and participate in decision making process from the start to the end of the project.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

NRDC ensures that all members of the communities are involved (community participation) including the most vulnerable groups in the targeting process. Putting into consideration their own protection risks/ benefits and how they deal with them at the community level by knowing community based self protection mechanisms, field staff are trained on the protection and risks of sharing the beneficiaries data by having security codes for beneficiary data,Including age, gender diversity. Ensuring that individuals and groups will equally be able to access assistance and that the centers or project sites will be accessible to all people despite gender, age and diversity (e.g. ethnic groups, political or religious beliefs) and during assessments, targeting, design. Implementation and monitoring. Sensitization of beneficiaries will include protection messaging e.g. around decision making, gender,people living with disabilities. Selection of beneficiaries from different communities will also be considered to reduce tensions and conflicts. The complaints and feedback mechanisms entails a referral mechanism should any protection-related feedback need the involvement of protection actors for follow up and this will be accessible to all community members.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Given current levels of unmet needs in Upper Nile this is a considerable challenge that requires development actors and humanitarian organizations to work together and NRDC is working in synergies with other actors in Panyikang, Fashoda and Manyo in addition NRDC is also implementing an ongoing livelihood project by distributing seeds and tools, fishing kits to the vulnerable households, rural development to strengthen local production capacities, value chain, markets and CTP program in Panyikang, Fashoda and Manyo this project will be linked to the proposed project to create synergies and promote longer term resilience even beyond the end of the project implementation. Through NRDC's vocational training initiatives in Manyo for vulnerable youths, the project will link these beneficiaries by giving some of them cash through cash for work and linking them to the markets, and as well engaging the women/girls and boys in VSLA activities to increase their capital enabling them to move from their vulnerability levels and be able to support their families, live in a safe and dignified environment while engaging in peace activities to promote peaceful coexistence and peace dividends. It is also important to note that NRDC is actively involved in the triple NEXUS approach between development, humanitarian response and peace. NRDC will work closely with the local government stakeholders, line ministries and the RRC in order for by in and to achieve community ownership and continuation after the project closure.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The proposed project is aligned to the National Social Protection framework (approved by the Transitional National Assembly in 2015 and launched in May 2016) which provides for enhancing the capacity of South Sudanese ( Women, girls, boys and men) to manage socio economic risk and volatility by increasing their resilience to shocks and improve access to opportunities generated by economic growth. NRDC's proposed activities will support the vulnerable communities have access and provide a safe environment for the opportunities for their economic growth which will therefore improve their general protection.The project is also aligned to the National Disability and inclusion Policy (2013) as NRDC will ensure the inclusion of PWD in the programming especially women and girls with disabilities in increasing their opportunities to benefit from the proposed activities and to promote their access to services as they are more likely to face sexual exploitation and abuse and verbal abuse.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The general safety and security situation in the area of implementation has greatly improved. There is relative calm in in the Fashoda, Panyikang and Manyo Counties except for minor incidences reported due to economic hardship and scarcity. Weapons and small arms are less visible as the Army Barracks has been moved very far from the civilian population this has resulted in a massive influx of returnees especially in Dethwok, Athidwai, Kaka, Tonga, Panyikang, Pakang and Kodok, from Sudan, Malakal PoC and as far as Juba returning to their respective home and it is envisaging with relative peace and stability in the entire region, the numbers of returnees are expected to increase in the year 2020. Fashoda County continues to receive refugees the Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC) reported 1900 individuals in Aburoc,4000 individuals in Kodok and 2074 in Tonga who are refugees returning from Sudan and settling in the area. An increasing number of IDP's are expected to seek durable solutions in 2020, although movement will continue to be fluid as people explore options for return, relocation or local integration in Panyikang, Manyo and Fashoda Counties. Especially at the outset, people may return temporarily or seasonally to farm and access assistance, and to check on the status of their property or evaluate their options regarding housing, land and property, access to livelihoods, services and support available; as well as confidence in safety and security. General improvement of the security situation and political goodwill especially after the signing of the recent peace deal and assurances from government on safety, are the most prominent pre-condition for return cited and this has seen the returns in areas like Kodok and Aburoc increase and the establishment of the UNMISS base in Kodok has given confidence to the population of protection and security.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

NRDC will emphasize on coordination between humanitarian and development approaches during the implementation of this project as this essential to allow crisis-affected populations to overcome the need for emergency assistance and for strengthen resilience to crises. Ensuring that humanitarian assistance continue to focus first and foremost on immediate life-saving activities, but at the same time build in durable solutions from the start in order to reduce aid dependency.NRDC will prepare ‘lessons learnt’ chapter of the progress reports containing analysis and recommendations for sustainable protection and livelihood activities and make sure the local community members, relevant stakeholders, potential supporters and donors are involved in the discussion on sustainable approaches. The local community structures including local government, traditional leaders will be trained, supported and involved in all project implementation processes so as to ensure continuity even after the project has ended.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

NRDC works in collaboration with local stakeholders seeking to build both local capacities as well as to ensure programming is adapted given the peculiarities of each context, as part of the planning and kick-off of any intervention, community consultation occupies a key function of allowing beneficiaries and community representatives to inform NRDC of the concerns, needs and existing capacities of each community. Through this, opportunities to address the direct and most-pressing concerns of peple with special needs are identified and acted upon, providing immediate support to the most vulnerable households affected by displacement within each community. Issues of protection, conflict sensitivity, and relevance are addressed through community consultation, as well as through the complaints and feedback handling systems through which community concerns are channeled through NRDC staff and community representatives, enabling reflection on ongoing activities and course correction when necessary. NRDC is committed to strengthen accountability to the affected populations hence engage with beneficiaries and stakeholders in assessments, programme design, targeting, implementation, Complaints Handling Mechanism (CHM) and M&E. The affected population is consulted through feedback, baseline and post-distribution assessments to ensure that the specific needs of women, men, girls and boys and people with special needs are included in project implementation.
