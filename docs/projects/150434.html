Project Summary

PCDCR seeks to respond to emergency protection, and psychosocial needs of GMR injured individuals and their families in Gaza, Khanyounis and North Governorates and to reintegrate them in their community. When designing the project, PCDCR conducted several discussions with CP and MHPWG. It will continue coordination with CPWG, and other current service providers through the implementation of the project to avoid overlapping and duplication in the interventions or targeted beneficiaries.
PCDCR will form three emergency response teams-ERTs; each consists of two counselors and a case manager. The ERTs will be ready to respond to protection, and psychosocial needs of GMR injured individuals (adults and children) and their families. The teams will conduct 1500 assessment home visits for GMR injured individuals aiming to assess their different needs. Then, the teams will conduct regular home visits along the project period to respond to the identified needs of each patient of 1500 injured individuals. The teams will provide different psychosocial and protection services as follows. Structured psychosocial support services will be provided for 1500 injured individuals and 1500 of their families including: 1) psychosocial first aid sessions for 450 injured children and their families (420 boys, 30 girls); 2) psychosocial group counseling for 225 injured children (210 boys, 15 girls) and 675 injured adults (625 males, 50 females); 3) individual counseling for 150 injured children (140 boys, 10 girls) and 450 injured adults (420 males, 30 females); 4) group therapy sessions for 37 injured male adults and 8 injured boys with disabilities; 5) stress management sessions for 225 women (mothers and wives of injured individuals); and 6) reintegration of injured individuals within their families and community through 100 groups awareness sessions for 1500 families (200 men, 1300 women). Additionally, to respond to multi sectorial needs of injured individuals including homes’ adaptation and shelter needs, case management and referral services will be provided for 30 injured children (27 boys, 3 girls) and 90 injured adults (83 males, 7 females).
PCDCR is committed to the five levels of citizen participation and engagement in the different stages of the project management cycle. When identifying the targeted communities, PCDCR took in consideration the statistics of MOH about injured individuals and their families and coordinated with protection cluster to avoid overlapping with other service providers. Through assessing the needs and priorities of the targeted groups (injured individuals and families of people killed or injured), PCDCR applied the first and second levels of citizen participation “to inform” and “to consult” through conducting interviews and focus groups with injured individuals. Also, PCDCR applied the third level “to involve” through enabling the target groups and other CBOs in the targeted communities to participate in the design of the project activities and interventions based on their identified needs. Additionally, PCDCR will apply the fourth and fifth levels of "to collaborate, and to empower" in the implementation of the project through forming 3 local follow up and support committees from 30 community members including women, men, youth and elderly people. These committees will participate effectively in implementing and following up the project activities, and they will have influential role in monitoring and evaluating the activities. They will share their feedback, comments and opinions with the project staff and partner CBOs to improve the quality of the project.
To enhance accountability, PCDCR will conduct two meetings with beneficiaries in the targeted communities to enable them provide their feedback and comments about PCDCR performance and staff behavior in the project. PCDCR will consider the beneficiaries’ feedback in the remaining activities and interventions of the project. Moreover, PCDCR will develop a complaints mechanism (via complaints Box) inside the partner CBOs for beneficiaries and (via PCDCR website) to let beneficiaries share their feedback, comments and opinions about the services provided and the behavior of the staff with PCDCR and project management.

Needs

The last six months have witnessed a further deterioration of the overall humanitarian and protection situation in Palestine and mainly in Gaza Strip. The more than a decade old internal Palestinian division continues to push Gaza and the West Bank further apart. Additionally, the Israeli imposed blockade on the Gaza Strip since 2007, despite relaxation of some blockade-related restrictions in recent years, remained Palestinians in Gaza ‘locked in’, denied free access to the remainder of the territory and the outside world. Moreover, the escalated rates of poverty and unemployment, which reached 53% for Poverty and 49.1% for unemployment and more than 60% for unemployment among youth (15-29 years) in early 2018 in Gaza Strip according to PCBS. All these threats caused a sense of frustration and psychosocial pressure within the community. The prevailing sense of frustration has fueled the regular demonstrations along the fence between Gaza and Israel that began in March. From 30th of March until 22nd of September 2018, 191 people have been killed. The total figure of people injured stands at 20,833. From this total, 10,071 were treated at the TSPs and immediately discharged and the remaining 10,762 injured patients were transferred for the hospital treatment (including 1,845 children (17%), and 703 female (7%)). The total number of amputations was 77, including 14 children and 1 female, and the total number of patients with paralysis due to spinal cord injury was 12 individuals, WHO, Sep 2018. In June 2018, PCDCR has conducted Rapid Need Assessment to identify the psychosocial & protection needs of injured children & their families through conducting Focus Groups and distributing questionnaires among GS governorates involving 150 (136 males, 14 females) injured children and 150 of their families.Assessment reveal injured children and their families indicated the same needs with 93% of injured children are in need to psychosocial support while 95% of their families affirm this need. Also, 80.5% of children are always thinking about their injury condition while 79.2% of parents sharing this concern with them. Regarding physical reactions, 70% of injured children feel severe difficulty in movement due to their limbs injuries while 71.39% of their parents assured this suffering. Also, almost of 73% families and children confirmed that children spend most of their times in anger status.Female sample was small (7%) of total injuries, but the researchers registered their concerns which differ from males. These concerns could be concise in culture &norms of society that give social acceptance & opportunities for males with disabilities more than females while females have lack of opportunities in making social life and marriage which put more burden and pressure on females and require more psychosocial care.More recently, in Oct 2018, PCDCR has conducted three focus group discussions with 45 injured adults and their families (29males, 17females) from Gaza, Khanyounis and North Governorates to identify their needs. The injured adults and their families mentioned the most significant problems they suffer: 1) psychosocial problems: anxiety, fear, stress and nightmares. 2) Coping problems due to amputation and injury: difficulty in using bathrooms and in climbing stairs. 3) Differentiated needs of injured females: more complicated psychosocial problems due culture of society, where injured females have lack of opportunities in making social life and marriage. Difficulty in running the house and do their responsibilities towards husbands and children mainly for married injured women. In addition, lack of consideration of gender needs when providing services to injured women. 4) Differentiated needs for women (wives and mothers) of injured individuals: psychosocial stress, difficulty in dealing with injured individuals and psychosocial burden.
