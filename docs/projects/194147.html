Project Summary

Due to influx of refugee from different countries, Sudan is hosting more than 1,130,547 refugees and asylum seekers, 61% of the population is out of camp in host communities and urban areas, while 39% is settled in camps primarily in White Nile and East Sudan. South Sudanese make up the majority of refugees along with refuges from other neighbouring countries such as Eritrea, Central African Republic, Ethiopia, and Chad. Sudan also hosts refugees who have fled the wars in Syria and Yemen to seek safety in Sudan. Khartoum hosts the largest number of the refugees living in urban settings. Based on the last update shared by UNHCR, this includes 306,156 individual’s of which 102,052 are part of female-headed House hold, 4302 persons with disabilities, and 1668 Unaccompiand and Separated children (UASC) living in different localities (Elljablian&Elsalam, Jebal Auliya&Omdorum-Naifasha ,Mayo).
Children and adolescent girls and boys experience different child protection risks and concerns: physical and humiliating punishment, neglect, sexual abuse like rape and exploitation for daily work. These in turn leads to various issues like poor child development outcomes, teenage pregnancy, attempted suicide among girls and boys, and school drop-out. Agencies and Government departments that are meant to provide crucial support and services face challenges in terms of funding, staffing, logistics and transport capacity. As a result, there is inadequate protection interventions including lack of appropriate case management and Family Tracing Reunification in camps.
The project is designed around two outcomes - each representing CPiE responses and interventions which build on the appropriate lifesaving services such as case management interventions to the most vulnerable children, including UASC, children living with disabilities, and out of school children by facilitating access and referral to services, family tracing and reunification, cash or in-kind assistance, and placement in alternative care as needed. This project will also include psychosocial support services (PSS) for individuals and group using the child friendly spaces approaches. Moreover, the project will strength community engagement and participation through establishment of Community-based child Protection networks (CBCPNs) that will monitor and respond to individual cases of children affected by any type of abuse, exploitation or neglect. Referral systems will be improved as part of strengthening local protection systems in the refugee camps. The project will also train relevant staff members and stakeholders.
This project will also provide full and non-discriminatory essential protection services for Persons with specific needs (PSN), including persons with disability, through proper identification assessment, facilitating access and referral to services. Further, it will support the most vulnerable refugee, asylum seekers, and host community members with multi-purpose cash based on established vulnerability criteria based on UNHCR CBI strategy and SoPs. All this interventions will be based on the Child Protection Minimum Standard (CPMS), Plan International guidelines and Sudan SoPs.
The project will also address GBV concerns through direct materials support of dignity kit to 500 individuals and awareness campaigns. The project will further support 1000 (750 in White Nile and 250 children in Basundah in Gadaref) with education interventions and 50 teachers in White Nile. The project will further reach out to 25000 refugees in White Nile with WASH and Health interventions through various Health and Nutrition and WASH activities that also include awareness campaigns.

1 - Needs

According to the Sudan Refugee Response Plan 2022, an assessment then established that the BANVA indicate that most refugees in Sudan suffer from moderate to high essential needs vulnerability and experience greater vulnerability than their host communities. In Kassala, White Nile, and West Kordofan, however, refugees and host communities exhibit similar levels of vulnerability. Furthermore, refugees in camps and camp-like situations have higher essential needs... According to Sudan Humanitarian Needs overview 2022, More than 3.2 million children need life-sustaining humanitarian support to continue or resume their formal education. The report also indicates thatThe population groups most in need of humanitarian assistance to continue their education include IDPs, refugees and returnees, host community members facing severe food insecurity, nomadic populations, children with disabilities and, in some communities, girls. Approximately 7 out of 10 primary-school-aged and nine out of 10 secondary-school-aged refugee children are not receiving any formal education, with refugees from South Sudan particularly affected.Refugees from Eritrea, Ethiopia and South Sudan face additional barriers to accessing education due to the language of instruction differing from that of their countries of origin, making it challenging for them to integrate into the Sudanese national education system. Additionally, families of refugee children are often unable to cover school fees and associated costs. Many refugee children work outside the home to supplement the family’s income. Up to 60 per cent of the population in need of protection services in Sudan are children who continue to be exposed to protection threats of recruitment, family separation, abuse, neglect, abduction, exploitation, and violence. Children with disabilities are among the most vulnerable. Family separation is an ongoing concern and is the third-highest protection concern reported among the new and protracted IDP and refugee populations.The Sudan HNO 2022 reports that, there are 2.9 people in need in 2022 for GBV prevention (this includes 200K refugees), mitigation and response, including mainly women and girls from localities that are prone to conflict and tribal clashes, hosting refugees as well as disasters, with a high percentage of households engaged in negative coping mechanisms. According to the first nation-wide, qualitative GBV assessment, “Voices from Sudan 2021”, physical and sexual violence are prevalent both inside and outside the home, and sexual violence is more pervasive in rural and conflict-affected communities including refugee camps. Access to services remain particularly weak in conflict-affected and refugee hosting areas of South Darfur, North Darfur, and West Kordofan, White and Blue Nile as well as East Sudan states. Refugees continue to be hosted in many states including Khartoum, where women and girls continue to face numerous protection risks exposing them to GBV.The Sudan Humanitarian needs Overview 2023 reports that there are 11 million in need of WASH services and refugees' community contribute 0.8 million of the 11 million people in need, like wise under the Health sector there are also 11 million people in need and the refugee community again contribute 0.8 million people of the figure in need. Under the nutrition sector the Sudan Humanitarian needs Overview indicates that 4 million people will be in need of nutritional services support, still the refugees contribute a significant figure of 0.1 million especially when one is comparing against their population ration to the national population.
