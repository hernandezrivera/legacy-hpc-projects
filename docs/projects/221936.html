Project Summary

This project aims to enhance food security and community resilience in Aweil East and Abyei, targeting 29,000 beneficiaries, including IDPs, returnees, and host communities. Key activities include distributing seeds, tools, fishing kits, and quick-maturing vegetable seeds to boost food production. Capacity building through training on demonstration fields will cover best management practices such as disease management, post-harvest handling, value addition, and marketing. Women and producer groups will be formed and trained with ox ploughs to expand agricultural land. The project will also procure, operate, and maintain a cold chain for safe storage of livestock drugs, conduct livestock vaccination and treatment campaigns, and train Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) on disease investigation, vaccination, treatment, and best management practices. Additionally, 10 groups (75% women) will be trained on income-generating activities and provided with business startup kits. Community agricultural extension officers will receive training on crop production techniques to support local farmers.

1. Needs

South Sudan remains one of the world's countries with extreme poverty despite its wealth of natural resources. The low level of agricultural production has left a high percentage of the population living in extreme poverty compared to neighboring countries. Children face acute and severe malnutrition, which is a major cause of child mortality. Since before independence from Sudan, the people of South Sudan have relied on food aid from the World Food Programme (WFP) and other humanitarian agencies to save lives. While saving lives remains paramount, there is a need to strengthen grassroots resilience and recovery processes to build long-term solutions to food shortages, high morbidity and mortality, chronic poverty, and underdevelopment in South Sudan.To achieve these long-term solutions, it is essential to invest in sustainable food security, nutrition, and community resilience to crises caused by war, diseases, and natural disasters.As of September 2024, the population of Aweil East and the Abyei Administrative Area are facing severe challenges. According to the latest IPC Phase Classification report, both regions are classified at IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) due to a combination of factors including severe flooding, deteriorating macroeconomic conditions, extremely high food prices, ongoing conflict, and a high burden of returnees.In Aweil East, the floods have severely impacted agricultural livelihoods, with many households losing crops and livestock. The flooding has also damaged homes, critical infrastructure, and disrupted health services, increasing the risk of disease outbreaks. Immediate action is needed to maintain and save livelihoods by improving access to farming, fishing, and livestock-safeguarding.In the Abyei Administrative Area, the situation is similarly dire. Flooding has displaced thousands of people, destroyed crops, and disrupted essential services such as health and education. The population urgently needs food, shelter, health, nutrition, water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) services to cope with the ongoing crisis.To address these needs, it is crucial to support the development of local businesses, agriculture, livestock, and fisheries in both Aweil East and Abyei. This will help strengthen food security and build resilience against future crises.Additionally, the need for a cold chain in animal vaccination in Abyei and Aweil East is crucial due to several factors. Livestock in these regions are highly susceptible to diseases, which can spread rapidly and cause significant losses. Vaccination is essential to prevent outbreaks, but vaccines must be kept at specific temperatures to remain effective. The regions face logistical challenges, including poor road networks and long distances between vaccination sites. Maintaining a cold chain ensures that vaccines remain potent despite these challenges. Livestock is a critical source of livelihood for many families in Abyei and Aweil East. Healthy animals provide milk, meat, and income, which are vital for food security and economic stability. Effective vaccination, supported by a reliable cold chain, helps protect these assets.To address the need for a cold chain, we would implement a comprehensive cold chain system that includes refrigerated transport using vehicles equipped with refrigeration units to transport vaccines from central storage to remote vaccination sites. We would establish cold storage units at strategic locations to ensure vaccines are stored at the correct temperatures. Additionally, we would provide training for local veterinary staff on the importance of maintaining the cold chain and how to handle vaccines properly. Implementing a monitoring system to ensure the cold chain is maintained throughout the distribution process and conducting regular maintenance of equipment would also be essential.A cost-recovery plan would be necessary to ensure the sustainability of the cold chain system. This plan could include charging a nominal fee

2. Project activities

1) Distribution of seeds, tools, fishing kits and quick maturing vegetable seeds. 2) Capacity building and training on demonstration fields on best management practices such as disease management, post-harvest handling, value addition, and marketing. 3) Formation of women or producer groups to be served and trained with ox plough to expand agricultural land. 4) Procuring, operating and maintaining the cold chain for safe storage of livestock drugs. 5) Livestock vaccination and treatment on common diseases. 6) Provision of drugs and training of Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) on disease investigation, vaccination and treatment and best management practices to increase output. 7) training 10 groups (25 each; 75% women) on income-generating activities. 8) Provide business startup kits for each group. 9) Train community agricultural extension officers on crop production
