Project Summary

The project is targeting 8,000 direct beneficiaries (4,000 women, 2,000 Adolescent girls, 1,200 men, and 800 boys) from IDPs, Returnees and Host community, 15 schools will be considered 5 in Leer and 5 Mayendit Counties of Unity State, 5 schools in Ayod county in Jonglei State. 8 WGFS with startup kits and small grants to make re-washable sanitary pads cheap, affordable to communities and distributed to critically vulnerable households and schools free of charge.
The purpose of the project is the preservation of dignity and mitigation of GBV risks through the empowerment of girls of reproductive age, mothers, pregnant mothers and lactating mothers on how to make re-washable/reusable sanitary pads.
Sufficient energy-saving cooking stoves will be distributed to reduce GBV risk and social-economic empowerment of women and girls in Leer, Mayendit and Ayod of Jonglei and Unity States. This project will respond to the culturally and social-economic development that varies in project locations through the establishment of WGFS, provision of GBV case management and PSS services to affected persons.
Women and girls will be trained on how to make energy-saving cooking stoves to reduce risks related to frequent movement far away to collect the firewood in the bushes which expose women and girls to various GBV risks including kidnapping and rape.
Girls/women in rural areas generally use a combination of whatever materials they have at hand for managing menstruation which is often unhygienic some time resulted in discomfort and infections.
To address this, MHA will train the women and youth and introduce a re-washable/reusable sanitary pads to reduce GBV related risk, absent from the schools and social-economic empowerment of women/adolescent girls/Youth groups.
An estimated 6.4 million affected by the crisis in South Sudan, about 32,000 women, and girls are at risk of experiencing sexual violence. Increased insecurity, dry season and high poverty levels over 85% are likely to worsen the situation unless there is a sustainable response. According to GBVIMS reports indicates that girls and women between 12 to 25 years old are the most affected.
Out of an estimated 6.4 million affected by the crisis in South Sudan, over 32000 women and girls are at high risk of experiencing sexual violence. Culturally almost all states of South Sudan, women are charged with the responsibility of taking care of the whole family and are exposed to high risk of GBV because of spending all the time trying to work for the family, fetching water, firewood, and going for food distributions.
Building on MHA GBV project experience, a project funded by UNESCO which we are implementing in Leer and Mayendit, this new proposed project will enhance participation of women and girls both in communities and in schools thus training will reach all targeted beneficiaries including indirect community people.
For sustainability of the project, MHA will work with all key implementing partners including the government to strengthen the implementation of institutional policies, standards, and practices that drive action and accountability on GBV and gender equality in humanitarian service delivery. Through WGFS, there will be knowledge and skills that will be provided and will be taken back to their communities because they will be empowered economically as well for immediate application knowledge.

1 - Needs

According to MHA's current data for Leer and Mayendit Counties of Unity State, over 34,000 IDPs returnees have been registered since Oct 2018 -July 2019 and there are still frequent arrivals of the IDPs from different POCs sites within the country including neighboring countries. High influx of returnees are leading to increasing of GBV related violence mostly perpetrated by armed youth and other elements putting pressure on limited unprofessional law enforcement institutions and the impeded traditional leadership and customary law, communal revenge over past killings, cattle rustling between communities, clan disputes among others remained potential threats to many lives of women and girls and is a serious concern which need to addressed through this project. Ayod County is the hub for returnees/ IDPs returning from the camps. The situation has calmed but more effort is needed to address the route cause or migrate the negative impact quickly and sustainably,With the project site which serves as an assembly/cantonment site of the troops, there is a need for GBV activities including protection monitoring and awareness-raising around the area to ensure that any violation is deterred and cases are addressed promptly. There are several hard to reach villages and islands where most people took refuge and they have poor hygiene and sanitation challenges and morbidity is a growing concern. Culturally, women are charged with the responsibility of taking care of the whole family and are exposed to high risk of GBV because of spending all the time trying to work for the family, fetching water, firewood, and going for food distributions. MHA is planning to empower women and adolescent girls with practical skills on energy-saving stoves which help reduce GBV related cases like rape, defilement, abduction among others.According to MHA research almost all over South Sudan, no accurate information about menstruation and the need for good management of menstrual hygiene is limited, among girls, boys, and adults. Menstruation is a topic that is not openly discussed, especially with men and boys almost all over South Sudan. Women and adolescent girls are experiencing taboos and attitudes which are leading to shame, distress and unhygienic or risky practices mostly adolescent girls are affected and the majority cannot discuss it at home. Limited information is given in schools, and many teachers feel unprepared to address the issue, even if it is part of the school curriculum. Affordable and appropriate hygienic-sanitary wear is largely unavailable. MHA formed WGFS empowered with skills and start-up kits for sustainable, cheap and sometimes free re-washable/reusable sanitary pads which will be distributed to schools and communities. MHA Needs Assessment in June 2019 in Leer and Mayendit Counties, we realized that almost 95% of households use firewood as the main source of energy for cooking and in some areas, vegetables and plant residues are used for cooking and lighting. An estimated 6.4 million affected by the crisis in South Sudan, about 32,000 women, and girls are at risk of experiencing sexual violence. Increased insecurity, dry season and high poverty levels over 85% are likely to worsen the situation unless there is a sustainable response. According to GBVIMS reports indicates that girls and women between 12 to 25 years old are the most affected. Out of an estimated 6.4 million affected by the crisis in South Sudan, over 32000 women and girls are at high risk of experiencing sexual violence. GBV is a life-threatening, global health and human rights issue that violates international human rights law and principles of gender equality. In emergencies, such as conflict or natural disasters, the risk of violence, exploitation, and abuse is heightened, particularly for women and girls as per UNFPA’s “Minimum Standards for Prevention and Response to GBV.

2 - Activities

Establish and streamline 8 Women and Girls Friendly Spaces (WGFS) 2 in Leer and 2 in Mayendit, 4 Ayod where women and girls easily access resources and information target 500 individuals, 300 women and 200 girls.MHA will create and empower 6 groups including 90 women, 60 men, 30 adolescent girls, 25 boys to become agents of change, to provide basic life skills to adolescent girls and boys toward GBV prevention and mitigation measures and to ensure self-sufficiency, livelihood and skills training which will reduce violence and mitigate risk factors associated with SGBV. Practical training of women and girls of reproductive age on sufficient energy saving stoves on fixed and moveable stoves as GBV mitigation measures in both schools and at the community level. women and girls will be empowered with practical skills on re-washable sanitary pads and start-up kits will be provided for 120 women and 150 girls 6 groups will be also trained on Energy-saving cooking stoves and they will transfer the skills to their communities, 200 women, 100 men, 100 girls, and 100 boys. They will be also oriented on Group Saving and Loans Association (GSLA) Methodology. They will be trained on financial literacy, market research and MHA will link them to potential dignity pads buyers.Raise awareness on GBV and services available for survivors and vulnerable populations reaching 1,800, women, 1,000 men, 500 girls, and 320 boys, using different means of communication including radio talk shows, churches, community meetings, WGFS and group dialogues to reach the community. MHA will also use different IEC materials including posters and t-shirts with different social messages to raise awareness on GBV related issues both in schools and in communities. MHA will build on the existing community-based network more focus will be put on their training of men and youth to transform them to become change agents toward GBV prevention and risk mitigation measures at the community level. MHA has already designed a GBV training manual with funds from UNESCO Which will be useful tools in this project, training the frontline workers, community volunteers, and community-based GBV key actors to on GBV case management, PSS, ethical treatment of survivors and GBV case management to provide individualized psychosocial support for survivors of GBV. Community volunteers including women leaders, women groups and social workers of other agencies will be selected and trained which will enable them to identify GBV survivors and refer them for appropriate case management. The trained community volunteers will serve as referral entry points for survivors in their communities.Strengthen and Establish GBV Referral Pathway to enhance access to essential GBV services and protection mechanisms in all the project locations, MHA will work with other partners on ground remapping and updating GBV referral pathway with a functional hotline, which will be shared among beneficiaries and partners for incidents reporting case follow-up and referral. Development of well researched, documented and training manual on both energy-saving cooking stoves and re-washable/reusable sanitary pads developed with key lessons learned, successful stories and best practices identified during project implementation for shared learning and replication to MHA and other agencies who may want to scale up the interventions to other States of South Sudan.Monitoring and evaluation. GBV sub Cluster M&E tools will be utilized. Group visits to check how their businesses are progressing. Conduct GBV assessment and quarterly coordinated safety Audits among other key M&E approaches will be used involving project staff.Distribution of dignity kits to 1000 women and 1000 girls.Provision of direct cash support to 178, women, 100 girls and 100 boys to cover the protection related gaps and referral.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, all key project stakeholders will be actively involved starting from planning up to the final stage of project evaluation. MHA believes that participatory M & E is not just a matter of using participatory techniques within a conventional monitoring and evaluation setting but it is about radically rethinking who initiates and undertakes the process, and who learns or benefits from the project interventions. Project communities and local authorities will be engaged in all stages of the implementation cycle for example in planning meetings, registration of beneficiaries, implementation, M&E and in identifying the criteria for inclusion and exclusion. They all participate during the process of conducting the Vulnerability Assessment Tool (VAT) for beneficiaries mapping, verification, and validation. Community-Based specific committees comprising of communities and local authorities will be selected, empowered and some of them will work as project volunteers as well. MHA will also use local community and local authorities as volunteers for example community trainers, post-project monitoring; baseline, midterm, and end of project evaluations. Project local authorities and communities will be also engaged in community feedback mechanisms. Community-Based Protection Networks, cultural leaders, line ministry community-based technical staff, community leadership, other implementing partners will be actively involved in the project implementation as an MHA exit strategy. Using this strategy, MHA is surer all government/community systems will be actively linked and collectively involved up to the final stage of the project

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, MHA will focus on supporting the improvement of community based traditional structures and mechanisms in coordination, strengthening relations between different stakeholders at the local level and also work with local authorities and community leaders using Community Based Protection Networks (CBPNs) MHA already has in Leer, Mayendit and Canal/Pigi Counties. MHA will closely work with stakeholders operating in the location and community-based networks thereby directly building their capacity to respond to humanitarian and development needs in the future. Community training will be focused on increasing knowledge and skills on sustainable energy-saving and re-washable/reusable sanitary pads integrated with GBV training and special attention will be put to youth and school going pupils to be the young leaders of change who will eventually pressure the government and other GBV service providers for quality GBV responsive interventions for example advocate for free dignity kits to critically vulnerable schools. During the project, a beneficiary feedback and complaints response mechanism will be implemented to ensure equal participation of all groups. Transparency within the community with all the ongoing activities and clear timeframes will help in preparing for contingency planning when MHA will end the project. MHA will share, as necessary, given information with key partners and stakeholders in the location working in the area, community leaders, religious leaders, local authorities, and institutions schools and health centers). Clear handover with local communities, authorities will be done to ensure a smooth transition.
