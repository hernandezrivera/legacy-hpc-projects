Project Summary

Lifesaving & Comprehensive health care services including NCD care and Cervical cancer screening using NIROG electronic medical record (EMR) system. Major goals are to improve NCD care dliverly (e.g. Hypertension, Diabetes, Asthma, Anemia, Gestaional diabetes, pre-eclamptic hypertension, etc), detection and teatment of precancerous lesion for cervical cancer using 'see-and-treat' module, and expansion of paperless, digital EMR system to strengthen coordination among the service providers in the FDMN camps for better information management and monitoring to help achieve standardised and accountable health service delivery.

Strengthen health sector coordination, information management and monitoring towards achieving rational, standardised and accountable health service delivery

Needs

1): Needs of FDMN (Rohingyas): Ensuring adherence to quality, respectful implementation of the minimum standards for services in health facilities is a continuous challenge for FDMN (Rohingyas) population. These are shortages in Non- Communicable disease (NCD) management, laboratory diagnostic capacity, screening for diseases among the survivors such as cervical cancer, and specialized services (skin & ENT care and other health care services for people with palliative needs). An overall health care waste management solution is needed including safe disposal of expired medicine. Rational use of medicines to reduce risk for antimicrobial resistances in an emerging priority which requires engaging with private drug shops as well as health facilities in the camps. There are critical gaps in comprehensive emergency obstetrics & new born baby care, family planning provision and availability to 24/7 comprehensive clinical management for gender based violence survivors. Referrals for emergency medical and obstetrics cases. Surgery, Trauma, and complication for NCD / Chronic diseases remain challenging and costly. Availability of individualized patient’s medical record (EMR) for longitudinal tracking for NCDs and access to quality data to help guide appropriate responses are needed. 2): Needs of Host community: Many of the same needs are observed in the host community particularly regarding health care, waste management, rational use of medicines, and Risk factor of Communicable disease facility based deliveries. Notably while the proportion of household who reported taking on debt in the previous month was lower among the host community (45%), the debt levels for medical expenses are higher suggesting insufficient access to free health services. There is a need to strengthen government health facilities and encourage accountability to improve quality of services .NGO- clinics in host community areas needed to engage In outreach among both host and FDMN(Rohingyas) population encourage utilization of their services.

Activities or outputs

1.Essential health care services including preventive and curative care for NCDs such as Hypertension, Diabetes, Asthma, Anemia, Gestational diabetes, pre-eclamptic hypertension, will be provided2.Strengthen health care services including laboratory and diagnostic capacity for Rohingya refugees and Bangladeshi host community following the essential service package for health post. 3.Using Mobile-ODT and Thermo-coagulator, in collaboration with Brown University, to provide Cervical Screening for women aged (30-65 years), treatment : Thermo-coagulator for CIN1 and CIN2 (in-situ cancer), at the HAEFA centers. Beyond >CIN2 cases will be referred to Cox’s Bazar hospital. 4.Using our existing field-proven paperless digital EMR system [NIROG], provide high quality healthcare by systematically capturing demographic information, age and gender, Individualized health data, diagnosis and treatment plans for all patients examined. This EMR system is used to electronically register and track all patients (with photo, and government-issued registration ID) and would allow for chronic and repeat patients to be treated more efficiently and accurately by easily and instantly accessing their current and past healthcare data stored in the system. Patients are also tracked as referrals (to BRAC's DOTs, UNFPA maternity centers, WFP's nutrition center for MAM, MSF hospital, etc.) to further strengthen healthcare sector coordination and monitor the response and quality of the health services provided. Easy, accurate and timely reporting of health screening, diagnosis and treatment data is also an important objective and benefit of using this EMR system. The NIROG EMR allows the HAEFA team to be completely in line with the accurate reporting requirements established by the GOB authorities and will be integrated with the GOB Shared Health Record (SHR) system in MIS, DGHS, MoHFW, to transfer any and all required data in a safe and secure encrypted manner. The data captured by the NIROG EMR would be owned by the GOB and MIS, DGHS, MoHFW. Please note that our medical centers are housed in the MOH (government medical camps) of Kutupalong (camp 1W) and Balukhali (camp 9), and we have been working in close collaboration with the government’s DGHS, MoHFW. We provide quality treatment with medication free of cost for 250 to 300 patients per day, around 6,000-7,000 per month, for NCD (hypertension, diabetes, asthma, anemia, gestational NCDs, and others including reproductive cancer screening for women) and Communicable Disease including Tuberculosis. Every patient above 12 years of age are screened for BMI (Ht. & Wt.), hypertension (BP), diabetes (RBG, FBG), anemia, COPD (peak-flow), high-risk pregnancy (GDM, PET), and Tuberculosis. An effective referral system comprised of the following has been established: we refer for malnutrition to the World Food Program's center; pregnancy with complications to UNFPA and other maternity service providers; and survivors to women friendly spaces and trauma centers by our female HWs. We collect sputum from Presumptive TB cases for microscopy at our medical centers in Kutupalong and Balukhali, which are then transported to NTP/BRAC microscopy centers for diagnosis and treatment. This can be further extended to provide traceability for other conditions using the NIROG EMR system. For LOCAL HOST COMMUNITIES. We have been providing treatment services to the local host communities in both Kutupalong and Balukhali camps. In Balukhali, there is a large group of local population and the relief workers from local community people who are doing businesses like grocery stores, small shops, transportation, mosques, etc. are receiving treatment from our medical centers every day. We also provide services for NCD and communicable disease including TB screening to the host community living in close proximity to the camps. Each patient's NID is recorded in NIROG EMR for future medical record retrieval for chronic diseases.
