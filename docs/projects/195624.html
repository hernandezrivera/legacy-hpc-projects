Project Summary

Alaska Health Project South Sudan plans to drill 25 new boreholes in Fangak County in 2023, some of which will have elevated flood resistant platforms. Locations to be targeted with new bore infrastructure may include IDP and static health/nutrition sites. In addition to new borehole infrastructure, AHPSS will focus on rehabilitating 5 existing boreholes with elevated platforms for flood resilience. AHPSS also plans to repair and maintain existing bore infrastructure including repair and maintenance on water yards as needed.

1 - Needs

According to the 2022 HNO, Water Sanitation and Hygiene conditions in Jonglei State were found to be severe and completely inadequate. The report cites Fangak county as one of the worst off areas in South Sudan. In this region, only 39% of the population reported having enough water to meet their household needs. This number dropped to 34% in more rural areas. Given recent flooding and conflict in Fangak County, the situation has likely grown worse since the 2022 HNO was published. Since the report, an estimated 60,000 new IDP have settled in New and Old Fangak alone according to the RRC. Thus, current water infrastructure in Fangak County does not have the capacity to meet the growing demand. According to the 2022 HNO, the presence of water-borne diseases in children under 5, for example diarrhoea, is also indicative of poor WASH conditions. The provision of clean water for vulnerable populations has a significant impact on the prevention of waterborne disease and malnutrition. Given the last of clean water in Fangak County, and the worsening humanitarian situation within the county, the need for additional water infrastructure is urgent. This project will directly address the need for clean water by establishing new water infrastructure for vulnerable populations around Fangak County.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

Fangak county has suffered from severe flooding for multiple years and flood trends suggest that this level of flooding will continue. Due to this new flooding cycle, people are settling in areas of the county not previously inhabited. This project will assist to create new borehole infrastructure in areas where displaced individuals have settled, thereby preventing future displacement. In addition, this project will adapt existing borehole infrastructure to be more flood resistant thereby reducing the need for individuals to move in times of severe flooding.
