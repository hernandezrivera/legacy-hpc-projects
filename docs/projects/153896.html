Project Summary

In line with guidance and priorities determined by the Shelter-NFI (S-NFI ) Cluster, UN-Habitat will provide transitional shelter solutions for extremely vulnerable returnees in underserved and recently retaken areas across Zummar Sub-district in Nineveh Governorate. Technical support and appropriate local materials will be provided for the construction of 400 transitional shelters using dried mud-bricks, prioritizing households whose original homes were assessed as falling within category 3 (severely damaged) and 4 (destroyed) war-damaged houses in the area. Cash for work transfers for the production of bricks and construction will also be provided. Support to Housing, Land and Property (HLP) rights will be integrated during the planning and implementation phases of the project. UN-Habitat will ensure the HLP status of beneficiaries in order to prevent any land and property disputes. The project will directly benefit 2,400 extremely vulnerable people needing assistance for a durable return to their areas of origin, including 400 women, 400 men, 800 girls, 800 boys.

Needs

According to the office of the Mayor of Zummar, 75 per cent of the housing stock in the villages of Zummar Sub-district has been destroyed as a result of ISIL’s occupation between 2014 and 2017. This situation has been confirmed by a UN-Habitat damage assessment team and the S-NFI Cluster. The list of damaged properties provided by the Cluster further confirmed the total destruction of 2,480 houses in the villages of Ain Mane’a, Al Jaziraa Collective, Barzan, Botee, Grafik North, Hamad Agha 1 and Aziziya. This village impact data also correlates exactly with information provided by the Mayor of Zummar. In response to these urgent needs, UN-Habitat implemented a pilot project addressing transitional shelter rehabilitation with support by the Iraq Humanitarian Pooled Fund in the latter half of 2018. It is estimated that 60 per cent of the housing units in the areas mentioned above are traditionally constructed, using dried mud-bricks as a base material. A high number of returnees continue to live in critical conditions around their heavily damaged houses. Due to the high number of houses destroyed, emergency shelter support is therefore essential to ensure the safety, protection and dignity of extremely vulnerable populations living in tents and habitable sections of heavily damaged dwellings in this area. This includes female-headed households, families living with children below six years of age and families living with people with disabilities. The construction of shelters using dried mud-brick is common practice and an extremely cost-effective rehabilitation option. It is also culturally appropriate as this is part of local construction practice in the area. The final list of villages will be decided in coordination with the national and northern S-NFI Cluster colleagues as well as Zummar local authorities. Beneficiaries’ selection criteria will be based on:• vulnerability with a specific focus on female-headed households, pregnant/lactating women, families with disabled and elderly people, as well as families with children below six years of age;• degree of damage to the property (categories 3-4 for transitional shelters in Zummar Sub-district);• confirmation of occupancy or full property documents;• women will be the predominant target group of beneficiaries for cash for work activities, constituting up to 30 per cent of the total number of beneficiaries. The intervention will reach a minimum of 2,400 individuals, including 400 women, 400 men, 800 girls and 800 boys.

Activities

UN-Habitat has considered critical gaps existing in the targeted areas of Nineveh Governorate and will work in consultation with the Cluster to direct its response, ensure adequate coverage and avoid duplication of activities with other partners. UN-Habitat’s strategy is to maintain as much flexibility as possible in order to implement activities in areas where the largest and most severe needs exist. Therefore, coordination with the Cluster and the HLP Sub-Cluster will be central in the determination of target locations and activities. Beneficiaries will be selected in accordance with established vulnerability criteria and the level of damage of the property. At the beginning of the project, a community mobilisation team will conduct community meetings and door-to-door visits to inform community members about the project. Specific sessions will be organised for women and authorities to ensure their participation in cash for work activities. The community mobilisation team and UN-Habitat engineers will then conduct detailed vulnerability assessments, including the HLP status of the households, and the level of damage sustained by the houses. Only houses falling clearly with categories 3 and 4 will be considered under this activity. Community engagement will be essential during the design phase. This could be facilitated by encouraging families to select from a number of designs and and material choices. UN-Habitat will provide technical skills, training activities, materials and cash for work to 400 extremely vulnerable families to build transitional shelters in dried mud-bricks. For families unable to conduct construction activities by themselves, specific support will be provided by UN-Habitat and the cash for work beneficiaries who benefited from the training sessions. In addition to support for the manual production of mud-bricks by the households themselves, beneficiaries will receive all the required materials to complete the units, including windows, doors and roofing materials. UN-Habitat engineers will support community members and ensure construction is conducted in accordance with minimum standards. Full market assessments will be conducted during the planning stages of the project. The intervention will ensure a standard of 5.5 sqm/person or 33 sqm/household corresponding to six people per transitional shelter, as per the Iraq S-NFI Cluster standards. The average cost per unit will be US$2,000 which also adheres to Cluster specifications. This amount is constituted of US$1,500 for the raw materials for mud-bricks and construction materials plus approximately US$500 for cash for work, inclusive of operating overhead costs. The average amount to be paid to each beneficiary will be approximately US$438, which meets the minimum standard for unskilled labour in Iraq, which also adheres to the guidelines of the cash for work SOPs. The possibility of providing cash in lieu of materials will be explored. This activity will be conducted using a cash for work approach, directly supporting the beneficiaries where the intervention takes place. Training will be provided to cash for work beneficiaries in brick making and building techniques. 120 female-headed households, or 30 per cent of the total caseload will be targeted, while the remaining 70 per cent, or 280 households will be male-headed. While there are fewer female-headed households than male counterparts in the targeted area, they will be prioritized taking into consideration their vulnerability. Trainees will support the most vulnerable families unable to conduct the construction activities for themselves. The cash transfer will be conducted in a minimum of three instalments. The disbursement of the first tranche will be released after successful completion of the mud-bricks, while the second tranche will be contingent on successful completion of the walls of the structure up to roof height. The third and final tranche will be released upon successful completion of the structure. A local NGO will be in charge of implementing the cash for work component. Support to HLP rights will be integrated during the planning and implementation phases of the project. UN-Habitat will ensure the HLP status of beneficiaries in order to prevent any land and property disputes. This approach will also guarantee that the project respects the principle of ‘do no harm’.Activity breakdown per activity:• 1.1: Final identification of targeted areas in coordination with the S-NFI Cluster, local authorities and communities;• 1.2: Identification, selection and registration of beneficiaries;• 1.3: Capacity building of returnees and stayees in emergency repairs and construction of transitional shelters, training on mud-brick fabrication and building construction work for cash for work beneficiaries;• 1.4: Provision of materials and three cash for work disbursements;• 1.5 Construction of transitional shelters.

Protection Risk Analysis

The following activities were assessed as having the potential for protection risks associated with the activities of the project:a) Beneficiary identification, selection and registration processes at project initiation stage: despite the overall extreme levels of vulnerability across Zummar Sub-district, it will be essential that priority be accorded to the most vulnerable households, especially female and youth-headed households. Additionally, families with elderly and or differently-abled relatives (who may not necessarily find it easy to represent themselves), or those who might find challenges in having community members or leaders represent them must be a priority;b) Participation in training opportunities: related to the above-mentioned point, some households may have limited ability to provide a primary bread-winner or substitute family to participate in training and capacity-building opportunities provided by the project, thereby losing out on the benefits of these developmental opportunities;c) Participation in mud-brick fabrication and house rehabilitation activities: some families, such as single-headed households or those with differently-abled family members may find it challenging to readily participate in the full extent of these activities; d) Cash transfer registration and disbursement processes: where local cultural norms often reinforce the dominance of male-family members, the project will need to ensure that registration of the appropriate family member is achieved in an appropriate way, ensuring that no harm is done in this process. The project will also need to proactively ensure that up to 30 per cent of households receiving cash transfers are female-headed. Cash disbursement processes will also need to ensure that individuals are not placed in vulnerable positions when receiving the funds – and that this is undertaken in a safe, secure and private manner, based on the positive learning experiences of similar previous projects;e) Local prevailing security situation: Although the military operations against ISIL have ended in the Nineveh Governorate, the security situation remains complex and volatile in retaken areas, including due to the threat posed by explosive hazards. f) Project site clearance, screening and selection: to ensure the safety of the teams and beneficiaries, emergency repairs and transitional shelter solutions installations will be conducted in safe locations. g) Role of missing or destroyed HLP documentation: this is also an issue that challenges households’ right to submit their claims for compensation. Many IDPs and returnees may be unable to collect and prepare the required documentation to submit their claims. Additionally, time and physical distances between IDPs’ current residence and the location of compensation committees often dissuade them to pursue their claims.

Mitigation Measures

a) Beneficiary identification, selection and registration processes at project initiation stage: UN-Habitat Community Mobilization Officers will work closely with local authorities (e.g. Mayor’s Office), community leaders, mukhtars and other community representatives during this phase and related processes, to ensure that the rights and needs of potentially under-represented or vulnerable households are not overlooked;b) Participation in training opportunities: UN-Habitat field engineers and Community Mobilization Officers will work closely with beneficiary households to identify appropriate and eligible family members who can participate on behalf of the family and benefit fully from these activities;c) Participation in mud-brick fabrication and house rehabilitation activities: As described above, UN-Habitat field engineers and Community Mobilization Officers will work closely with beneficiary households to identify appropriate and eligible family members who can participate on behalf of the family and benefit fully from these activities;d) Cash transfer registration and disbursement processes: UN-Habitat Community Mobilization Officers will work hand in glove with the national NGO implementing partner to ensure that cash transfer registration and disbursement processes (through a registered and experienced financial service provider) are executed in a gender-sensitive and appropriate manner;e) Local prevailing security situation: UN-Habitat has comprehensive security procedures in place for taking account of any security deterioration and for situations that would require evacuation. This includes the suspension of operations up to evacuation. All UN-Habitat staff will adhere to the Security Plan. Furthermore, the security situation is monitored on a continuous basis and essential information shared with project staff;f) Project site clearance, screening and selection: The project will only be implemented in areas that are clear of explosive threats. If necessary, removal and disposal of debris as well as clearance of unexploded devices will be completed in collaboration with the Government to ensure that the plot is cleared prior to any project activities are commenced; g) Role of missing or destroyed HLP documentation: HLP rights will be integrated during the planning and implementation phases of the project. UN-Habitat has a dedicated HLP Officer who will ensure that the HLP status of beneficiaries is taken into close consideration in order to prevent any land and property disputes.

How is your project accountable to affected population

The principle of “do no harm” is at the heart of UN-Habitat’s activities across the country. The approach employed by the project will ensure the participation and needs of beneficiaries during the identification of locations and beneficiaries, implementation and evaluation phases of the project. The Cash Working Group's Vulnerability Assessment Tool will be employed to assess and identify the highest priority beneficiaries for the project. Prior to any activity, UN-Habitat’s Community Mobilization Officers will consult with community leaders, including mukhtars, to ensure all activities are planned and implemented with the support of the community. Exclusive sessions for female-headed households will provide them with a safe and comfortable space to ensure their participation in all phases of the project. In regard to cash for work for the bricks and construction, 30 per cent of the beneficiaries will be female and 70 per cent will be male. Surveys and impact assessment tools will be developed and conducted by UN-Habitat’s Community Mobilization Officers at all stages of the project’s implementation. HLP will also be a key component of the do no harm approach employed under this project. Finally, the action will prioritize female-headed households, families with pregnant/lactating women, families with children below 6 years of age, and families with differently-abled people among the extremely vulnerable returnees targeted by the project. UN-Habitat engineers will constantly supervise activities to ensure adequate standards and trainees will support the construction of transitional shelter for the most vulnerable families unable to conduct activities by themselves. An international Programme Officer will maintain oversight for the day to day management of the project and will remain in constant contact with field operational staff, UN-Habitat senior management, S-NFI Cluster colleagues and the donor. The Programme Officer will also conduct periodic spot checks and post-distribution monitoring visits to the project sites. A robust complaints and response mechanism will be mainstreamed across all project activities maximizing the role of UN-Habitat's Community Mobilization Teams in close collaboration with the mukhtars, local authorities and other appropriate community structures. A confidential complaints hotline number will also be made available to all project beneficiaries where any complaints or feedback will be processed in a private and appropriate manner by an objective member of UN-Habitat's management team.
