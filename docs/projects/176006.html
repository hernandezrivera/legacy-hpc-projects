Project Summary

Sudan Evangelical Mission (SEM) will provide support to conflict-affected and most vulnerable girls, boys, youths, women, and men in Jur River County of Western Bahr El Ghazal State, Mundri East, and Mvolo Counties of Western Equatoria State in South Sudan where we have a static presence to reach 18,620 beneficiaries (5750 boys, 5750 girls, 3560 men, and 3560 women).
We propose this project that will contribute to achieving South Sudan’s HRP objectives of child protection cluster (PO 1) by the provision of critical protection-related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of the most vulnerable boys, girls, men and women; and (PO 2) by preventing and mitigating protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience.
The activities of this project are aimed at:
• Provision of community-based psychosocial support activities for girls, boys, adolescents, and caregivers of children with disabilities (including children with epilepsy/ nodding syndrome). To reach 8000 children (4000 boys and 4000 girls) and 1000 adults (500 men and 500 women).
• Provision of training sessions on parenting and comprehensive sexuality education to parents/ caregivers of children with disabilities (including children with epilepsy/ nodding syndrome) to reach 3000 parents/ caregivers (1500 men and 1500 women).
• Provision of comprehensive case management services and referrals to children with disabilities (including children with epilepsy/ nodding syndrome) to 500 children (250 boys and 250 girls).
• Conduct community awareness-raising on disability inclusion targeting18,620 beneficiaries (5750 boys, 5750 girls, 3560 men, and 3560 women).
Our proposed activities above will contribute to providing direct support and referrals to children with disabilities and their families in line with CPMS standard 18 on case management in consideration of the best interests of the child while creating a caring and protective environment that meets the child’s physical, emotional, social, cognitive and spiritual needs through training of parents/ caregivers who will promote the healthy development of their children and keep them away from harm according to CPMS standard 16.
Community awareness raising on inclusion and protection of children with disabilities will enable durable solutions in the community through engaging community members in dialogues that promote respect of rights of children, combating discrimination of children with disabilities while providing practical examples of inclusion.
We will ensure accountability to our beneficiaries by creating an avenue for Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) between this project and our target community that we shall further strengthen by sensitization of channels that they can use to submit complaints and feedback.
SEM will also undertake other priority CP cluster activities like capacity building of CP actors and volunteers as well as supporting community-based child protection mechanisms.
We shall build the capacity of 120 (60 male, 60 female) frontline service providers and community volunteers, including county-level government staff like social workers and other personnel working at the Child and Social Welfare Department. We shall provide training and mentoring to the service providers and volunteers to improve their capacity to identify, report, refer and support children in need. The training will focus on areas like CPiE, case identification, and referrals to child protection actors in Jur River, Mundri East, and Mvolo Counties.
We will also target strengthening community structures and mechanisms including community-based child protection committees (CBCPC) and child protection help desks in schools, health facilities, and nutrition facilities. We shall achieve this by mobilization of community members to form committees / reactivate existing committees and engaging them to take active roles in issues affecting children.

1 - Needs

An IOM’s Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) field assessment in Kuajiena Payam of Jur River County on 25 February and 18 March 2020 to obtain more information about clashes between pastoralists and farmers indicated that 35% of the community that fled from Yikana were children, and whose urgent needs were water, shelter, and non-food items. Such children are at risk of exposure to violence and abuse and therefore create an urgency to provide protection services that facilitate their safe access to their most urgent needs at such times.The Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2021 indicated that protection concerns are especially high for girls risk for early marriage, and denial of education particularly for girls and children living with disabilities. Decades of conflict, poverty, and poor access to services made people with disabilities (PWD) particularly fragile. PWD are often stigmatized, marginalized, excluded, and left unable to access the support needed to live in dignity as equal members of society. This project will seek to promote respect for the rights of such children and promote access to services among boys and girls with disabilities.Similarly, a REACH situational overview in Western Equatoria from April- September 2020, humanitarian needs in Western Equatoria State (WES) increased due to a deteriorating security situation, displacement, unusually heavy rainfall and flooding, and COVID-19 related movement restrictions. However, due to insecurity, bad road conditions, and flooding, the food security situation has raised concerns in Greater Mundri where this project will be implemented. Therefore, the provision of interventions that aim at increasing the food security of families with children with disabilities becomes a priority. The food security department within SEM will facilitate mobilization and training of the parents/ caregivers of children with disabilities on agronomic practices as well as support the distribution of seeds and tool kits for them to participate in farming vegetables and field crops.Despite increased insecurity across WES in Q2 and Q3, the proportion of assessed settlements reporting most people felt safe remained relatively high and stable throughout the reporting period (from 89% in March to 81% in September). Such conflicts expose women, men, girls, and boys to protection risks and violations due to widespread conflict that causes multiple shocks and stresses. This demands robust child protection interventions that we propose through the provision of life-saving services that will include the provision of community-based psychosocial support activities for girls, boys, adolescents, and caregivers of children with disabilities; provision of training sessions on parenting and comprehensive sexuality education to parents/ caregivers of children with disabilities (including children with epilepsy/ nodding syndrome) and provision of comprehensive case management services and referrals to children with disabilities.Access to healthcare was reportedly hindered by the distance to health facilities, lack of essential medication exacerbated by impassable roads, and insecurity, with Greater Mundri and southern payams of Maridi County being the most affected. Seizures, indicating a prevalence of nodding-disease, continued to be the main reported health problem in Mundri East (39%) and Mvolo (33%) counties in September. We shall use caseworkers who are already trained on assessment, and management of children with disabilities who are located in Mundri East and Mvolo counties to offer case management services specifically to this category of children with nodding syndrome/ epilepsy. After the assessment, we shall refer the children to treatment sites in Mundri PHCC and Lui Hospital. They shall provide further care at homes in form of activities of daily living, PSS, education on safety, and support their parents to provide care to them.

2 - Project activities

Sudan Evangelical Mission (SEM) has been implementing activities on child protection that target children with disabilities for over 15 years in South Sudan and has skilled staff with experience on issues of children with disabilities and familiar with the proposed project areas of Mundri East, Mvolo, and Jur River County.We propose this project that will contribute to achieving South Sudan’s HRP objectives of child protection cluster (PO 1) by the provision of critical protection-related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of the most vulnerable boys, girls, men and women; and (PO 2) by preventing and mitigating protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience.The following are the activities that we shall undertake:Activity 1: Provision of community-based psychosocial support activities for girls, boys, adolescents, and caregivers of children with disabilities (including children with epilepsy/ nodding syndrome). To reach 3000 children (1500 boys and 1500 girls) and 3000 adults (1500 men and 1500 women). We shall provide psychosocial support services to boys, girls, and their parents/ caregivers of children with disabilities. The PSS will be conducted by trained caseworkers and will include recreational sports, as well as training on the artwork, beadwork, leatherwork, and other skills. Activity 2: Provision of training sessions on parenting and comprehensive sexuality education to parents/ caregivers of children with disabilities (including children with epilepsy/ nodding syndrome) to reach 450 parents/ caregivers (225 men and 225 women). The parenting training sessions will aim at giving them skills on care provision, activities of daily living, managing stress and burnout that arises while giving care to children with disabilities, how parents will create support networks, and handling sexuality issues of their children with disabilities.Activity 3: Provision of comprehensive case management services and referrals to children with disabilities (including children with epilepsy/ nodding syndrome) to 900 children (450 boys and 450 girls). We shall focus on the identification of children with disabilities, assessment of their needs, formulating care plan and referrals to health centers for medical management of epilepsy/ nodding syndrome and other health needs/ inter-agency referrals for other needs. We will also follow up on the children referred for care to ensure closure/ access to referred services was successful.Activity 4: Conduct community awareness-raising on disability inclusion targeting 18,620 beneficiaries (5750 boys, 5750 girls, 3560 men, and 3560 women). We shall conduct awareness and prevention messaging through our caseworkers and established community-based protection networks targeting men, women, boys, girls on the rights of children and inclusion of children with disabilities.Activity 5: Conduct staff/frontline workers trained on CPiE: we shall build the capacity of frontline service providers and community volunteers, including county-level government staff like social workers and other personnel working at the Child and Social Welfare Department. We shall target 120 (60 male, 60 female), frontline workers. Activity 6: Strengthening community structures and mechanisms: We will target strengthening/ establishing 9 CBCPN (3 in Jur River, 3 in Mundri East, 3 in Mvolo); 6 child protection help desks (2 in Jur River, 2 in Mundri East, 2 in Mvolo); and 12 parent support groups (4 in Jur River, 4 in Mundri East, 4 in Mvolo).

3 - Project indicators

We shall measure the success of this project using the following indicators:1- Number of, girls and boys including children with disabilities provided with focused and non-focused MHPSS and other related services: we shall increase the number of children who access quality PSS services and improve their well-being.2- Number of women and men who receive psychosocial support, parenting messaging, and participate in parenting sessions: we shall increase the number of at-risk families who are able to provide quality care for children, while communities actively support family-based care and provide comprehensive sexuality education to children with disabilities.3- Number of women, men, boys, and girls including with disabilities with protection concerns supported through case management and referral services and CBA: we shall increase the number of children who are adequately assessed, supported, and referred to various services within their community that will meet their diverse needs.4- Number of women, men, girls, and boys including those with disabilities reached through awareness-raising activities: we shall increase the number of children with disabilities, their parents/ caregivers who are empowered to make their own decisions and have a voice in issues that affect them as well as communities that promote inclusion and non-discrimination of children with disabilities.5-Number of staff/frontline workers trained on CPiE, including case management and referral pathways and PSS: we shall seek to increase the number of frontline service providers who have been trained with an emphasis on changing their knowledge attitudes and practices on referrals, case management.6-Number of CBCPN established: we will aim at establishing CBCPN who will work towards a common goal of preventing and responding to child protection concerns in their areas through the action plans that they will implement to build a protective, enabling, integrating, and peaceful environment for children.

We will use feedback from the beneficiaries to adapt our modalities of service provision to adequately meet the needs of our targeted beneficiaries and stakeholders. 8.11. Mitigation Measures

Lack of access to/awareness of referral services for children and survivors of protection incidents (GBV, child protection) will be mitigated through maintaining a current list of local resources, intersectoral services that are available in Mundri East, Mvolo, and Jur River Counties and make it a standard part of the information available to survivors of protection incidents and their family/ caregivers. We shall further disseminate information through leaflets, posters, and information cards to the community to reach as many people as possible. Unsafe and unethical management of data, especially disclosure of sensitive personal data on beneficiaries- By making use of our code of conduct, all our staff shall be expected to abide by the clauses that refer to the safety of data and responsible use of collected data. This will prevent the distribution, publishing, or sharing of any collected data without the consent of the beneficiaries and SEM to safeguard the confidentiality of our beneficiaries.Complaint and feedback mechanisms will be established via suggestion boxes that will be strategically placed in public spaces and display of phone numbers for complaint and feedback focal points as well as dissemination of the information to all project stakeholders to provide an avenue where they will freely share complaints and feedback on this project. We shall aim to facilitate communication among the less literate and persons with disabilities during this project. We shall improve the access to services among people with disabilities by adequately consult them while ensuring reasonable accommodation to overcome barriers that may arise during project designing, and implementation stages. We shall also include using sign language interpreters for persons who are deaf, and personal assistants for those with physical and intellectual disabilities.
