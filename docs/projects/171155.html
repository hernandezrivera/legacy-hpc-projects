Project Summary

Due to the reported rise in COVID-19 related violence in households and communities, UNRWA will provide immediate response services to gender-based violence survivors and child protection cases among the refugee community. This will be achieved through structured psychosocial and case management services targeting child protection and GBV cases in addition to children and women under protection risk. Services will include: 1. Psychosocial First Aid hotline 2. Remote support groups for women and children at protection risk 3. Case management and referral to mental health services. To complement those services, through this project UNRWA will provide beneficiaries with cash, dignity kits, and children’s kits according to need.

1 - Needs

Palestine refugee women and girls in the West Bank face obstacles to enjoying their rights and freedoms from various sources, including life under occupation, and certain forms of patriarchal dominance within their own society. Further, escalations in violence, and severe restrictions on movement have resulted in economic stagnation, limited human potential, and led to widespread deterioration in psychological well-being of Palestine refugee women and girls. All this compounds the vulnerabilities of Palestinian women to gender-based violence (GBV), poverty and limited employment opportunities, while simultaneously restricting their participation in the public sphere. As violence and exposure to violence continues across the oPt, it is noted that a number of academic studies have found direct linkages between exposure of men to occupation-related violence and intimate partner violence directed towards women. The COVID-19 pandemic has disproportionally affected women and girls in several ways and exacerbated their vulnerabilities. The risks of experiencing household violence during times of crisis are often exacerbated by factors such as emotional stress, economic strain, and shifting roles and responsibilities among family members. When combined with lockdowns and other movement restrictions, homes within the context of COVID-19 can become potential pressure cookers of GBV, as drivers of violence increase, while survivors and those at risk are more restricted than ever in terms of their ability to seek safety or other necessary forms of support. Domestic tensions from the lockdown increased the risk of abuse, as UNRWA social workers have been noticing an increase in the intensity of violence among the cases they have been managing in terms of frequency and severity of abuse. The Women's Centre for Legal Aid and Counselling (WCLAC) reported 69% increase in consultations and the Palestinian NGO SAWA an increase of 20%. Due to movement restrictions and lockdown measures imposed, face-to-face interaction, and access to GBV services and safe spaces have been significantly reduced or, in most cases, completely suspended, thus creating a response gap both for survivors and those at risk of GBV. Furthermore, women in home isolation or in isolation facilities lack access to CP, MHPSS and GBV services. The increase in domestic violence risk has a direct impact on the children of those families who end up being either victims of violence or at least witnesses of this violence. This further compounds the vulnerability of children, as even before the COVID crisis, Palestinian children were being exposed to different types of violence on a daily basis. A Violence Survey conducted by PCBS in 2019 indicated that 20.6% of the boys and 13.7% of the girls under 11 were exposed to severe physical violence while 36% of children between 12 and 17 years old were exposed to violence from one or both parents. Furthermore, 19% of children (12-17 years) experienced one form of violence at their schools in the West Bank while 9% of children experienced one form of cyber violence (blackmailed, harassed, insulted via social media networks). Palestine refugee children in camps also usually witness violence regularly due to armed conflict. In this context, it is essential to tackle not only the direct health consequence of the pandemic, but also the very serious socio-economic and protection impact on Palestinian refugee women and children associated with this crisis. Enhancing prevention, detection and response to gender-based violence cases during COVID-19 pandemic is therefore the key priority presented in this proposal.

2 - Activities

The project will strengthen UNRWA’s capacity to prevent, detect and respond to GBV and Child Protection cases under the new reality of COVID-19. Services will include: 1. Psychosocial First Aid hotline 2. Remote support groups for women and children at protection risk 3. Case management and referral to mental health services 4. Cash, dignity kits, and children’s kits. The project will be implemented by the Agency’s Child and Family Protection Programme within the Relief and Social Services Programme (RSSP). As a prevention tool, UNRWA’s Relief and Social Services Programme will activate two types of support groups: 1. Support groups for refugee women at risk of GBV 2. Support groups for refugee children at protection risk. The Agency established those groups in 2013 but had to stop them in 2020 due to the pandemic. The project will focus on camps’ resident due to the scarce resources in those locations. The Agency uses a peer-to-peer approach in the two types of groups that follows international standards and guidelines. It focuses on children and women’s participation and rights and uses a structured approach for psychosocial support as follows: - Stage 1: Awareness and Trust Building –psychosocial counselors work with the group to build the team, and their trust. Participants also engage in activities that increase their awareness concerning their stressors, and improve their communications, leadership, advocacy, problem-solving, stress management, and self-protection skills.- Stage 2: Therapy – Participants engage in deep expressive therapy work, including music, art, drama, and role-playing therapy. - Stage 3: Family counseling – family counseling help beneficiaries address internal family stressors, and communication problems. Through this project, 10 support groups (5 women groups and 5 children groups) will meet weekly over a 6-month period. Each support group will benefit 15 Palestine refugees with a total of 150 beneficiaries (75 women and 75 children). Support groups will be conducted remotely during the pandemic. Children groups will receive kits that include educational and therapeutic toys. They will be utilized within the groups in games that will help them in identify stressors and deal with them. The kits will also be used to encourage siblings’ healthy interaction and bonding during the pandemic. UNRWA will also create audiovisual and printed materials that will be used to raise awareness on violence-related issues and mechanisms to combat them for children and women. For detection purposes, UNRWA’s RSSP uses different tools. In March 2020 UNRWA established a hotline managed by skilled social workers. The hotline is used to provide callers with psychosocial first aid during the pandemic and refer them to services upon need. This project will strengthen the hotline and better equip its staff to detect and refer protection cases to other services. Detected protection cases will be referred internally (including to case management services) and externally according to the risk level, needs of the case, and the acquired consent. In addition, Support groups are effective in detecting cases. RSSP also receives cases from UNRWA’s Education and Health programmes that have psychosocial counsellors providing mental health support to Palestine refugees.All detections and referrals within UNRWA follow the standard operating procedures (SOP) established by the Agency in 2019. It set the guidelines for detecting, categorizing, and referring GBV and Child protection cases. The SOPs laid down clear internal referral pathways and set different forms such as consent and assent forms. It included child protection specific guiding principles that took into consideration the age group of those children. In 2019/2020, UNRWA trained all its frontline staff on the safe identification and referral of protection cases. All Social Workers were trained on the SOP and are aware and experienced in how to make a best-interest determination for children, obtain informed assent, and apply child safeguarding during their interactions with children.In terms of response, UNRWA will be providing case management services to detected child protection and GBV cases, also in line with the SOPs. This will be led by an experienced team of 4 case managers supported by 50 Social Workers. The Protection and Neutrality Department and its Child Protection Officer will be providing technical support as required. During the pandemic, case management services will be provided remotely, which the staff are trained to do. Case management services that will be provided through this project include, mental health support – such as individual and family counseling, referral to specialized services such as legal counselling, and referral to ministries for high-risk cases. All services will be provided to beneficiaries after a thorough discussion with the cases concerning their needs, as well as acquiring their informed consent/assent. Furthermore, cases, mainly GBV, will be provided with:1. One-time cash assistance to be able to respond to immediate needs and avoid negative coping mechanisms. An eligibility tool will be used to choose beneficiaries. 2. GBV dignity kits that will include information on where women can access GBV services. The kits will contain items in line with UNFPA standard dignity kit such as sanitary pads, underwear, hand soap, toothbrushes and toothpaste in addition to other items that were selected based on the basic needs of Palestine refugee women. Child protection cases, and children of GBV and domestic violence survivors will be provided with children kits identical to the ones that the children support groups will receive. They will contain educational and therapeutic toys such as sand clay, puzzles, a game, a drawing book, and pencil crayons. The kits will be used as a tool to aid the psychosocial support services, such as individual or family counseling, the children are receiving.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

“Do No harm” is one of the main GBV guiding principles and approaches according to UNRWA minimum standards. The service providers have been trained to respect confidentiality, safe data storage and privacy of GBV survivors in any form of consultation, counseling or personal information sharing to avoid stigmatization. Also, GBV service providers have robust experience in ensuring GBV survivors are protected from harm and in providing psychological and emotional safety to survivors who may be at high risk from the perpetrators or from their family members due to notions of family honour. All UNRWA staff are required to follow the GBV Survivor Centred Approach to provide meaningful access to services, ensuring accountability mechanisms are in place as well as participation and empowerment of GBV survivors. UNRWA works to ensure the best interest of the child and child safeguarding as stated in the Case Management SOPs and its guiding principles. All UNRWA social workers have been trained on the implementation of the Case Management SOPs and are aware of how to make a best-interest determination and obtain informed assent, and more generally apply child safeguarding in the implementation of their interactions with children. With respect to assent/consent, this was part of a Case Management training package that was delivered to social workers, and is integrated into the overall approach of the Relief and Social Service Programme. Specifically with respect to assent, caregivers are typically responsible for giving consent for their child to engage with external actors until the child reaches 18 years of age, unless there is credible evidence to suggest that they pose a risk to the child. Additionally, as a general principle, age appropriate permission to proceed with interventions (informed assent) should also be sought from children over the age of 6. Similar to informed consent, informed assent can only be secured once the child is aware of what information is being sought from them, what it will be used for, and how it will be shared. To ensure informed consent/assent, staff must ensure that the survivor/individual fully understands: 1) The services and options available; 2) Potential risks and benefits to receiving services/sharing of their information; 3) How information that will be collected and how it will be used, including how non-identifying data could be used for data collection and trend analysis purposes. 4) Confidentiality and its limits. 5) Informed assent is the expressed willingness to participate in services by those under the age of majority. In the context of COVID-19, UNRWA’s facilities are safe for beneficiaries and staff and respect precautionary safety measures, including physical distancing and hygiene promotion, as defined by health authorities. Furthermore, all staff received ad hoc training on upholding hygiene measures, prevention and mitigation measures.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNRWA services are open to all refugees and UNRWA has a specific obligation to mainstream protection across its services and operations, respect and promote humanitarian principles and made a clear commitment to protect and uphold Palestinian refugees’ rights. UNRWA since 2016 has a dedicated Protection and Neutrality Office and human resources dedicated to guarantee the provision of its services is done in an impartial and non-discriminatory way. In fact, UNRWA regularly assesses the level of compliance with Protection standards of the services provided to refugees through a Protection Audit. Families can directly request assistance to UNRWA social services as well as protection cases are identified across UNRWA services. The project, targeting children and GBV survivors, aims to provide assistance to a number of child and GBV survivors facing neglect, exploitation and deprivation of their rights. Identification of children and GBV survivors will happen inside schools, through home visit done by social workers, and health centres. Possible beneficiaries seeking remedies will be informed and supported, without any discrimination or barrier and through the project will receive targeted and evidence-based assistance. An individual and family approach is conducted to guarantee violations are halt and possibly a safe environment is restore for victims. Professional case managers follow up the cases until the pattern of abuse is stopped or relieved.
