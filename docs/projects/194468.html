Project Summary

In line with the identified needs and gaps, CARE International in South Sudan aims to provide a comprehensive package of GBV response services, which will be complemented with the prevention activities that address the root causes of the GBV in Juba, Magwi, Ikotos, Torit, Kapoeta North, Budi, Wau, Jur River, Koch, Rubkona, Pariang, Bor South, Pibor, Twic East, Duk and Akobo. The interventions will be provided through static centers and through mobile and outreach teams. Static Women Girls Safe Spaces (WGFS) will be used as entry points to reach out to the communities, while outreach sessions by mobile teams will also be used as means of engaging with communities. The WGFSs will serve as the centers where women and girls can come together and socialize while also improving their psychosocial wellbeing. In order to do that CARE will provide structured and unstructured PSS activities. The unstructured activities will take the form of recreational activities which will be determined in consultation with the women and girls, i.e.. knitting, baking, sewing, painting or handicrafts. The structured activities on the other hand will aim to promote overall life skills of women and girls. Similarly, CARE will train women and girls on usage and set up of Fuel Efficient Stoves as part of GBV risk mitigation measures. These efforts will be supported with experience sharing sessions among women on cooking or sharing recipes to promote cohesion and communication among women groups. CARE will also offer income generation activities to the women and girls who fit into the activity criteria, which will include vulnerable individuals, survivors, female-headed households and so on.
Through the WGFSs, CARE will provide GBV case management services to the survivors, while ensuring updating (or creating referral pathways) referral pathways and service mappings for these locations to provide safe referrals to the services. As part of the individual case management plans, CARE will also provide one-time cash assistance to support the GBV survivors, when deemed necessary according to the developed SOPs. Community members who need legal advice and/or need to attend legal information sessions will also be provided with legal support at the WGFSs. Women and girls of reproductive age, who fit into vulnerability criteria set up in line with UNFPA's guidance will also be provided with dignity kits to ensure they can maintain their dignity and mobility in a crisis context.
CARE will also additionally provide awareness raising sessions to raise awareness on key gender and protection topics, as well as gender equality through mobile teams and at the WGFSs. This will help to engage men and boys in the discussions. Similarly, CARE will lead on the primary prevention activities by engaging men and boys through structured curriculums such as EMAP and Program RA.
In order to ensure that the staff are well equipped to provide support to the communities, all frontline staff and community mobilizers will be trained in various topics including case management, PSS, GBV risk mitigation and GBV core concepts. CARE's previous and existing presence in the aforementioned locations, as well as GBV programming experience in these locations will help to ensure community buy-in and prevent backlash from the communities.
CARE will partner with UNIDOR to implement CP activities to provide case management, PSS, referrals to support child GBV survivors.

1 - Needs

CARE has identified the following needs through its current operations in the proposed locations, meaning first-hand information on the current situation, as well as recently conducted assessments in the locations and the secondary data sources. Gender-based violence (GBV) is alarmingly present in all corners of South Sudan with an estimated 2.6 million people risk facing GBV in 2022, presenting a 25% increase compared to 2021. Additionally, the risks seem to be higher for the IDPs, as around 57% of the IDPs express a greater concern/fear about GBV, specifically rape and forced marriage (HNO, 2022). Although the services seem to be quite limited in remote locations, there also seems to be an overall lack of information on the available services. Similarly, among those who reported cases of GBV in 2021, 97% seem to be women and girls, which points out to the fact that women and girls are disproportionately affected by the GBV in South Sudan (HNO, 2022). The most common types of GBV are physical assault, sexual violence and emotional abuse. Studies also show that GBV cases are highly underreported and inconsistent due to several reasons which include stigma, fear for further violence, fear for isolation by family and friends or mistreated by community and family, fear for not being able to find a husband and lack of access to GBV services. Of the cases which get reported, failure to take action against perpetrators has created hostile environment for survivors. Lack of GBV services and low quality of available services is another key reason of under-reporting. The access of women and girls to livelihoods opportunities apart from the agricultural ones seem to be limited, while an overall access to income generation opportunities can help empower women and girls. Lack of financial resources, as well as transportation and access challenges, constitute barriers overall in South Sudan for local communities which prevent in occasions the access to GBV services. The proposed intervention locations by CARE which are all graded as three, four and five in terms of severity of the needs by the GBV Sub-Cluster of South Sudan. In line with the HNO 2022 findings, CARE during a recent assessment (December 2021) has found that physical violence and domestic violence are the most common types of protection risks followed by lack of basic services and inability to access services. Additionally, it was found that in the locations where there were floods, populations and vulnerable families are posed to higher protection risks, where they fail to access even basic services due to access challenges as well as the lack of services. It was particularly reported that older people and people with disabilities face challenges accessing services and are at the increased risk of violence. According to the Lessons from South Sudan report (2018) war increases forms of GBV – including forced marriage and those other forms of endemic violence, such as intimate partners violence are more prevalent during conflict compared to CRSV. It also confirms that violence against women and girls, gender inequality and conflict are often mutually reinforcing and that during times of conflict, gender inequality remains the core cause of GBV. Despite the prevalence of GBV, there is still insufficient services to respond to the needs of survivors compounded with limited knowledge on availability of services. These all have been compounded by the climate change across. It should be also noted that there has been increased cases of PSEA across the country which require specific attention during the operations. When it comes to CP, HNO 2022 also shows that separated children, child survivors of GBV, early marriage, child pregnancy, child labour and children with disabilities with limited supported assistance are some of the issues that need addressing. Lack of access to documentation is also a pressing issue for children in South Sudan, as it prevents access to services.

2 - Project activities

Through its specialized GBV programming, CARE will continue providing specialized and integrated GBV services through the static centers and mobile outreach, which are: 1) Establishing static WGFSs to provide access to women and girls to services; 2) Providing comprehensive quality and timely survivor-centered GBV case management services to individual GBV survivors; 3) Providing one-time cash to the survivors identified through case management to meet their basic needs or address urgent protection risks (transportation, documentation, meals etc.); 4) Conducting structured and unstructured PSS activities (including recreational activities); 5) Providing adolescent girls learning initiatives to mitigate risks of early and forced marriages through using tailored curriculum; 6) Establishing and coordinating/updating GBV referral pathways and service mapping; 7) Providing income generation activities for the women and girls at WGFSs and later on supporting the graduates with starter kits; 8) Providing legal support to the survivors and community members through one-to-one consultations and group information sessions; 9) Leading inclusive awareness raising sessions with the community members on key gender and protection topics through awareness raising group sessions and special days celebrations (i.e. 8th of March, 16 days of activism); 10) Conduct capacity building trainings for the stakeholders including local organizations, local authorities and women leaders on GBV basic concepts, PFA and GBV guiding principles; 11) Provide capacity building support and mentoring to the field staff and community mobilizers in the project locations; 11) Conduct gender analysis at the project locations to understand needs, aspirations and capacities of women, girls, men and boys as well as the household dynamics; 12) Lead primary prevention activities with men and boys through structured curriculums; 13) Provide dignity kits to women and adolescent girls of reproductive age; 14) Provide women and girls with Fuel Efficient Stoves trainings as part of GBV risk mitigation measure. Under the child protection component, CARE will work with UNIDOR 1) to provide case management to children; 2) to provide psychosocial support to them; 3) to provide awareness raising and information dissemination; 4) to establish/update referral pathways; 5) to provide capacity building trainings to key stakeholders. In doing these, CARE will ensure the access of people with disabilities by addressing the environmental, institutional and attitudinal barriers. Specific budget lines will be allocated to addressing these barriers to facilitate access.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

For each project location, CARE will analyze access to goods and services with an AGD lens; and try to understand market systems related to protection, e.g. alternative care, health, legal services, transport, education, birth registration. These will be analyzed on a case-to-case basis, while also the potential protection risks and benefits of market interactions in the community and among traders will also be considered.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

CVA provides the survivors with a dignified option to address their immediate and pressing protection risks and the evidence shows that CVA interventions in GBV programming can be highly beneficial in response activities.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

In order to ensure these inherent risks are mitigated, CARE will train all project staff in PSEA, reporting mechanisms and how to handle sensitive data. CARE will ensure that the static centers are located in a safe and secure place by consulting the community on the locations. At the beginning of the project, CARE will coordinate with the local authorities to explain project activities to prevent interference. Additionally, CARE will map all existing services and create referral pathways to ensure safe and confidential referral of the disclosed cases.
