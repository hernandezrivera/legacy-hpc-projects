Project Summary

As conflicts and recurrent environmental shocks continue to drive people out of their places of origin, resulting in unpredictable and sudden displacements, requiring a quick response from humanitarian actors to deliver essential relief. According to the CCCM cluster, 2,241,328 people will need humanitarian assistance in 2022 due to the lack of durable solutions, continued conflicts, and recurrent shocks.
As such, shelter and non-food item needs remain significant across displacement-affected communities in Somalia, with recurrent displacement trends underpinning protracted needs for shelter and NFI assistance in the proposed locations. Therefore, the proposed action will target locations in Kismayo, Hargeysa, Ceel Afweyn, Laasqoray and Baidoa districts. The project will benefit 16,446 individuals (2,741 HHs without double counting). Under this, ACTED proposes an in-kind distribution methodology instead of a voucher system to allow for prepositioning and rapid emergency response when the need arises. The storage and transportation of the materials to the distribution points and related costs are managed by the selected competent and qualified suppliers, a requirement in the contract signed between ACTED and the suppliers. In parallel with the provision of emergency shelter and NFI support, and in line with the primary objective of working towards facilitating recovery and access to durable solutions, ACTED will provide transitional shelters to IDP households living on land protected by land tenure agreements in IDP sites that have stable land tenure agreements. Significant rates of eviction render durable shelter solutions appropriate only in cases where land tenure agreements exist and provide a critical foundation for persons facing protracted displacement in the assertion of pathways to recovery.
In order to assess the efficiency of the assistance provided, level of satisfaction of the beneficiaries with the items received and the overall impact, ACTED will conduct post distribution monitoring 6 weeks after the distribution of ESK/NFI and post construction monitoring 3 months after the handover of transitional shelters to analyze the impact and draw lessons learnt for future programming.

1. - Projects activities

To respond to the urgent or acute needs of the population affected by displacement and environmental shocks, ACTED will provide NFI kits to 1,361 HHs (8,166 individuals), emergency shelter kits to 1,330 HHs (7,980 individuals) and 50 HHs (300 individuals) with transitional shelters. Caseloads per district will depend on vulnerability of communities, size of populations with humanitarian needs. Prior to the distribution of the kits and construction of transitional shelters, a transparent beneficiary selection and registration process will be undertaken, ensuring that those that need the assistance the most are prioritized following a standard selection criteria. As such, ACTED will adopt a targeting approach based on vulnerability rather than status. For example, in urban displacement contexts where IDPs often mix with unsupported urban poor host communities, the intervention will look at the needs of these populations that live in the targeted informal settlements, rather than displacement status, in line with one of the key principle of the area based approach and to reduce the risk of creating tensions over resources or assistance.To ensure program quality, assess the efficiency of the distribution process, level of satisfaction of the beneficiaries with the items received during the distribution and the overall impact of the assistance ACTED’s independent Appraisal, Monitoring & Evaluation Unit (AMEU) will conduct Post Distribution Monitoring 6 weeks after the distribution of ESK/NFI and Post Construction Monitoring 3 months after the handover of transitional shelters to evaluate the impact and draw lessons learnt for future programming.Additionally, ACTED will use multi-sectoral CRM mechanism to create a clear accountability structure that beneficiaries can rely on. In an effort to address overcrowding conditions and prevent the spread of contagious diseases within informal settlements, ACTED will support site decongestion initiatives wherever possible, and this activity will target 400 HHs (2,400 individuals).

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

ACTED enjoys a strong presence and working relationship in the target districts, and is well versed with the prevailing security situation and community dynamics and is therefore well placed to carry out interventions in those geographical locations. ACTED will target new arrivals and evicted households with provision of emergency shelter and NFI kits: this will provide at least short-term relief to household in serious distress because of displacement, often connected to widespread violence, insecure land tenure especially in south central Somalia. ACTED will regularly monitor the situation and those with protection needs be referred to specialized protection partners for immediate protection assistance, following referral pathways in site, and to other services available, such as nutrition and health. Though outreach workers and camp management mobile teams, ACTED will target with new arrivals with information dissemination activities, to make them aware of the services available, and will ensure that their needs and views and represented within the community governance structures. ACTED will regularly report new arrivals and new sites to the National and Sub National Cluster. Whenever possible, ACTED will try to expand on-going activities in order to target newly formed sites not receiving assistance. When not possible, ACTED will advocate for new funds and ensure to report to other humanitarian actors the most urgent needs of the target locationsPrior to any distribution, a transparent beneficiary selection and verification process will be undertaken involving local authorities and community leaders to ensure that those that need the assistance the most are prioritized following a standard selection criteria. Additionally, ACTED will ensure that specific needs of women, children and elderly are included when designing and implementing the shelter and NFI activities. For instance, the shelter and NFI kit content will be tailored to those needs, supporting dignity, based on consultation. Distributions plans for Shelter and NFI kits will include mitigation of most common risks, making sure the distribution sites are accessible and safe, risk of looting is mitigated. During the selection and building of the shelters, ACTED must work closely with local authorities to ensure that the site on which the shelters are built is either community land owned by the beneficiaries or government-owned land. If the government donates the land, ACTED will make sure the paperwork is completed correctly and shared with the beneficiaries Moreover, ACTED has Appraisal, Monitoring & Evaluation Unit (AMEU) in charge of the quality control of project activities, measuring and assessing the project’s achievements against output indicators. The AMEU operates independently of the program team and reports directly to the country coordination. A CRM hotline number is provided to the beneficiaries to report any issues related to selection/registration and distribution. In order to further assess the impact and effectiveness of the assistance provided, the AME Unit will undertake one post distribution and construction monitoring for the distributed kits and the constructed shelters to evaluate and document impact, effectiveness, relevance, beneficiary feedback and lessons learnt.ACTED has an existing policy on Sexual Exploitation and Abuse which is readily available to all staff members. This comprehensive document is part of the code of conduct handbook signed by all ACTED staff upon beginning interaction with ACTED. Strict adherence to this policy is expected within the organization and in case of violation, internal reporting mechanisms exists which grant the employees a voice. With regard to employee to beneficiary interaction, the PSEA also provides clauses on this and repercussions in case ACTED employees infringe on this. Beneficiary hotline numbers exist and shared with all beneficiaries and posted in all sites, which ACTED, is active.
