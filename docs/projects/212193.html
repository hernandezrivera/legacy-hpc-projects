Project Summary

Aligned with the strategic objectives of the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), the Syria Relief project for 2024/2025 aims to enhance access to critical primary health services, particularly focusing on lifesaving and life-sustaining interventions. The project seeks to achieve this by bolstering antenatal care, expanding the availability of quality health services through operational primary health care facilities (PHCs) and a mobile clinic. This initiative also emphasizes the augmentation of reporting capabilities for early warning systems and the enhancement of the capacity of healthcare providers and community health workers.
The projected target for 2024/2025 is to benefit 300,000 individuals by operating seven PHCs and one mobile clinic, currently HERAMs coded and supported by Syria Relief. Each PHC will have a comprehensive team GP doctors, pediatricians, midwives, pharmacists, nurses, nutrition counselors, and community health workers to conduct outreach health awareness activities. The project will ensure the provision of essential supplies such as medicines, medical equipment, and cleaning materials, including sanitizers, to support healthcare services. SR pharmacies will play a crucial role in providing essential medications aligned with WHO's essential medicines list, facilitating timely patient access.
To enhance project effectiveness, healthcare workers will undergo comprehensive training, focusing on maternal, newborn, and child health (MNCH) services. Additionally, 50 health staff members will receive training or retraining on mental health topics, including mhGAP, Problem Management Plus (PM+), and Psychological First Aid (PFA). Another 50 health staff members will be trained or retrained on GBV first-line response, covering Clinical Management of Rape (CMR), GBV in emergencies, and GBV referral. Furthermore, 150 health workers will receive training on infection prevention and control (IPC).
Nutrition services will be integrated into the targeted PHCs to improve access to essential health services for affected individuals in NWS. The project will prioritize outreach services, including health promotion activities and home-based specialized medical care for individuals with chronic conditions, people living with disabilities (PwD), and at-risk pregnancies.
This comprehensive approach is based on needs identified through assessments conducted by the Syria Relief team in January 2024, and other secondary reports such as the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2024. To ensure high-quality implementation, the Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) department will support various monitoring activities, culminating in a final project evaluation during the Project Cycle Management (PCM) phase. The evaluation will provide credible and useful information, enabling the incorporation of lessons learned into the decision-making process.
