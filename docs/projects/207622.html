Project Summary

In 2024, UNICEF's prevention and response program will reach out to 16,811 girls, boys, women, and men, of whom 1,576 individuals have disabilities. The primary focus will be on delivering a comprehensive prevention, risk mitigation and response program to address gender-based violence (GBV) in camp 25 and the Whykong host community of Cox's Bazar.
UNICEF will follow a public health approach to GBV that prioritizes prevention and ensures interventions are rights-based and value for money. Guided by the IASC Minimum Standards, Interventions will Do No Harm and be in the Best Interests of survivors and will be based on robust needs analysis. This approach involves multifaceted actions with the goal of: enhancing access to survivor-centered services; assisting and supporting survivors to address the consequences of GBV; preventing and mitigating the risk of GBV by creating safer environments; and Identifying strategies to challenge harmful practices and social norms by strengthening community-based protection systems; and harnessing the inherent capacities of children, adolescents, parents, and communities to prevent GBV, including intimate partner violence, sexual violence, child marriage, and fostering social change toward a more robust protective environment.
UNICEF will provide integrated & age-responsive GBV/CP services that strengthen protection systems as illustrated in the socio-ecological model. Through a blend of approaches including Safe Spaces for Women and Girls (SSWGs) and other service hubs, UNICEF will ensure that survivors and those at-risk of GBV have access to comprehensive case management services including specialized MHPSS, dignity kits, GBVIMS+, care plans and referrals.
UNICEF's specific focus areas include building the capabilities of case workers through training, mentoring, and on-the-job coaching; maintaining safe spaces; engaging and empowering girls, boys, women through awareness raising and community outreach to transform gender norms and foster respectful relationships; mitigating GBV risks across other sectors including WASH, Education, Nutrition, and Health, using safety audits; providing life-skills based empowering programs for children and adolescents aged 10-18; Implementing Girl Shine curriculum to prevent & respond to child marriage; and Implementing EMAP, a behavior transformative approach which targets adolescent boys, and men. UNICEF will advocate for equitable access to justice for GBV survivors; engage women and girls in skills development activities; provide guidance on ASRH; and improve access to MHPSS for recovery and resilience.
UNICEF will engage religious leaders, Mahjis, CiCs, traditional leaders, and other gatekeepers, in behavior change initiatives aimed at reshaping attitudes, behaviors, and harmful norms contributing to violence against girls and women. These discussions aim to involve men and boys as allies in addressing gender-related issues, encouraging their active participation in sustainable behavior change.
UNICEF will mainstream Emergency Planning and Response through DRR, including pre-positioning supplies. UNICEF will promote social cohesion and peacebuilding within and between communities through promoting free flow of accurate information, coordination, and joint activities. Child Safeguarding and PSEA will be mainstreamed. UNICEF will lead the training of partners and ensure mechanisms are in place in line with the humanitarian code of conduct. AAP through complaints and feedback will be ensured.
UNICEF will continue to maintain robust coordination with key entities like the GBV Sub-Sector, case management working group, Gender in Humanitarian Action working group, PSEA, Age and disability WG, Protection Emergency Response Units, Camp Focal point, and other relevant teams at the field level to ensure harmonized, quality, and collaborative GBV services. UNICEF will also support organizational development of government counterparts and implementing partners.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Approximately 931,574 people are estimated to be residing in Rohingya refugee camps, with 52 percent of them being children, according to the GoB/UNHCR Fact Sheet dated August 31, 2023. This population heavily depends on humanitarian assistance. Children and women, especially Unaccompanied and Separated Children, female-headed households, persons with disabilities, and those with alternative gender orientations, are exposed to various forms of violence, including trafficking, child marriage, child labor, sexual violence, and Gender-Based Violence (GBV). These vulnerabilities are exacerbated by restricted movement, protection risks, and natural hazards like monsoons, cyclones, and landslides, which adversely affect the safety and well-being of the population in both the camps and the host community.Negative social norms and harmful practices, including gender discrimination and child marriage, persist as indicated in the JMSNA report from September 2023. Reductions in food rations due to funding cuts and an increase in climate-related disasters continue to contribute to factors that heighten the occurrence of all forms of violence against children and women, child marriages, and GBV incidents. According to the GBVIMS Q2 report for 2023, Intimate Partner Violence accounted for 84 percent of reported GBV incidents, with 98 percent of these incidents occurring in survivors' homes where children and other family members live. The impact of IPV extends beyond survivors, affecting children and adolescents who witness the violence. Child marriage remains a significant concern, although incidents reported through GBViMS are relatively few. Cases of child sexual violence continue to be documented in the camps, comprising 3% of GBV cases. The JMSNA in 2023 revealed that child marriage is a coping mechanism for many families struggling to care for their children. Although women and girls are disproportionately affected by GBV, studies have also shown that men and boys are significantly affected by violence, including sexual violence (Women's Refugee Commission (WRC), 2023).In addition to the camps, an estimated 544,461 individuals in the host community are in need of humanitarian assistance. According to the J-MSNA report from September 2023, a substantial percentage of females reported the need for protection services. Due to deeply ingrained patriarchal social norms and human rights violations, women and children remain at a higher risk of exposure to all forms of violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse, child marriage, and GBV.While robust GBV interventions for responding to, preventing, and mitigating risks do exist, significant gaps still need to be addressed to improve the quality of services and access. These gaps include:i) Limited access to quality multi-sectoral referral services (health, psychosocial support, legal assistance, safety).ii) Inadequate capacity among actors and the social workforce.iii) Cultural barriers hindering disclosures and normalizing GBV incidents.iv) Inadequate engagement of key gatekeepers and adolescents in challenging negative social norms and harmful practices.v) A lack of knowledge about available services, as highlighted in the JMSA 2023 report.Bangladesh is prone to climate change and natural disasters, which can exacerbate the vulnerabilities of communities, leading to increased incidences of GBV including child marriage. Humanitarian crises also disrupt the normal functioning of child protection systems, making it challenging for UNICEF and its partners to provide timely and effective interventions to prevent child marriage. UNICEF remains committed to collaborating with the government, NGO partners, sector/sub-sectors, and community structures to prevent, respond to and mitigate GBV among the most vulnerable individuals in the Rohingya refugee camps and the host community.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project's primary goal is to ensure that Rohingya refugees in camp 25 have equitable access to a comprehensive package of Gender-Based Violence (GBV) services in a manner that is predictable, efficient, timely, transparent, and accountable. The project aligns with the one-camp approach and aims to establish common strategies, systems, protocols, and tools whenever feasible, following a 50% coverage approach in line with rationalization.UNICEF and its implementing partners will continue to deliver a consolidated package of services within the camp, thereby promoting equity in service provision. The project will collaborate with the government, UN agencies, NGOs, the donor community, as well as relevant sectors and working groups to prevent gaps and overlaps, fostering a more coordinated and effective response.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The Project is founded on protection principles and strives to foster meaningful access, safety, and dignity for children and women in the Rohingya refugee camps and host communities. The project will employ global protection mainstreaming toolkit and SPHERE standards to incorporate protection principles throughout the project cycle, including non-discrimination, meaningful access, safety & dignity, ‘do no harm’, accountability, participation, and empowerment. By leveraging service delivery nodes, especially Safe Spaces for Women and Girls, and by conducting extensive community outreach to reach the most vulnerable, the Project ensures that access to assistance and services is provided without discrimination or barriers. All our activities are led by the Do not Harm principle and survivor and rights-centered approaches. Our interventions aim to enhance rights-based, reliable, and quality humanitarian programming, considering gender, age, sex, disability, specific needs, and cultural appropriateness. Activities are designed with a protection-mainstreaming lens, promoting safety, dignity, meaningful access, participation, and empowerment of beneficiaries across the response. Additionally, the Project's design incorporates an information and feedback mechanism, empowering the affected population to demand accountability for the adequacy of interventions and allowing UNICEF and its implementing partners to effectively address concerns and complaints. UNICEF will also ensure that all partnerships are signed based on a previous and transparent assessment of the organizational capabilities to prevent and respond to SEA/SH during the execution of their contract, in line with the zero-tolerance policy advocated by the UN system.

3.3 - Age & Disability

The Project's primary objective is to prevent and respond to Gender-Based Violence (GBV). It includes age-specific interventions, encompassing psychosocial support activities and adolescent life skills-based learning. To guarantee access to services for individuals with disabilities, UNICEF offers outreach mobile services within the community to ensure the engagement of everyone. All interventions are tailored to ensure that all beneficiaries, particularly children and persons with disabilities, can actively engage with our services and interventions and derive maximum benefit from them.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Embedded within the project design is an information and feedback mechanism that empowers the affected population to demand accountability for the adequacy of interventions. This mechanism enables UNICEF and implementing partners to address concerns and complaints effectively, thus ensuring Accountability of the Affected Population (AAP).Specifically, the AAP efforts focus on:i) Ensuring the active participation of women, men, boys, girls, and persons with disabilities and diversities in assessments, decision-making processes, and programming.ii) Enhancing UNICEF Information Feedback Centers (IFCs) and community-based groups in camps and the host community to facilitate the receipt and transmission of feedback to and from the affected populations regarding their concerns and the services provided.UNICEF will also provide support to partners in strengthening AAP by conducting risk assessments and enhancing staff capacity to take appropriate measures. Additionally, engaging the community and target groups throughout the needs assessment, program planning, and implementation process using standard assessment tools will be a priority, with a specific focus on the inclusion of children with disabilities and their caregivers.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

UNICEF maintains rigorous standards and protocols concerning Child Safeguarding and PSEA. Upholding a zero-tolerance policy for SEA as well as sexual harassment, UNICEF actively promotes this principle throughout the organization, among IPs, vendors, service providers, and beneficiaries. The organization is dedicated to preventing SEA and promptly providing support to victims.Internally, UNICEF fosters an organizational culture that champions a safe, respectful, inclusive, and empowering working environment, completely free from sexual exploitation and abuse. Regular PSEA and child safeguarding training are organized for UNICEF staff, emphasizing the importance of these standards. Additionally, UNICEF plays a crucial role as a key member of the PSEA Network.UNICEF rigorously assesses its Implementing Partners to ensure a high level of compliance with these standards, mandating a low-risk rating. Our commitment extends to developing appropriate Information, Education, and Communication materials on PSEA and child safeguarding to raise awareness and sensitize affected populations about the Complaint and Feedback Mechanism. All partners are required to have multiple feedback and reporting mechanisms, including tools like U-report, complaint boxes, and hotline numbers for PSEA and child safeguarding. They also appoint AAP focal person as necessary.UNICEF conducts annual refresher training for partners and monitors their performance to disseminate training to national staff, volunteers, community leaders, and all community members. The training focuses on safe identification and confidential referral of cases. Furthermore, UNICEF ensures that all partners are members of the PSEA network and have established policies on child safeguarding, whistleblowing, and PSEA.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The primary goal of the Project is to enhance the resilience and preparedness of communities supported by UNICEF. Our IPs play an active role within the Protection Emergency Response Units (PERU) and coordinated emergency response teams for natural disasters. Their involvement in PERU demonstrates a proactive stance in emergency response, facilitating swift and effective interventions during natural disasters to mitigate their impact on affected populations. Additionally, UNICEF partner staff, including volunteers, receive training to respond to disaster-related emergencies, aiding community members during acute and post-disaster phases. DRR and climate change considerations are seamlessly integrated into all of UNICEF’s activities and programs. This integration ensures that DRR measures are embedded within community-based protection activities, thereby strengthening the overall resilience and preparedness of the communities served by UNICEF.To bolster DRR efforts through our programs, UNICEF undertakes several key actions: conducts GBV assessments and safety audits that incorporate disaster risk identification; allocates a budget across all projects specifically for emergency response-related activities; provides on-site psychosocial support (PSS) to beneficiaries, particularly women and girls, during emergencies; trains partners on emergency preparedness and response, encompassing fire safety assessments & drills; empowers communities, especially women and girls, to become change agents, fostering resilience, sustainability, and equitable development in the face of climate change and disasters; prepositions, stores, and distributes customized dignity Kits to reduce vulnerabilities of affected women and girls during acute emergencies; encourages eco-friendly practices by advocating for the use of sustainable products in construction and renovation and employs solar panels to reduce reliance on fossil fuels across all facilities.
