Project Summary

Protection needs remain high across all camps, exacerbated by secondary impacts from COVID-19. Children face serious risks including trafficking, sexual exploitation, child labour, and missing out on opportunities for formal education and vocational skills development (UNHCR, October 2020). Psychologists remain in short supply and are unable to meet the Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS). Child marriage and early child-bearing pose serious physical and psychological risks to women and girls with 19% of surveyed women and girls in camps and 24% in host communities are married by age 15 (Innovations for Poverty Action, 2019). The Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment shows that rates of child marriage among the Rohingya have increased by 9% since the beginning of 2020. 17% of married girls (ages 15-19) have experienced violence as compared with 4% of unmarried girls of the same age (Gender and Intersectionality Report, 2020).
RI will extend protection services to six camps and five host communities with the goal of “enhancing access to child protection services to children and adolescents in the Rohingya camps and host communities”. Activities will include; conducting community consultations to design the project; mobilizing and equipping communities with knowledge and skills; structured community awareness campaigns; provision of multi-sectoral integrated services; adopt a community based approach for all prevention activities; Strengthen community-based case management and prioritize do no harm; and support communities to identify and implement community-led initiatives to prevent and responding to child protection. With the support of technical staff, volunteers provide PFA home and community based psycho-social support. RI will ensure that all activities consider effects of COVID19, Monsoon and cyclone. The project will continue to collaborate with the Child Protection Sub-sector (CPSS) and other sectors including GBVSS, Site management, WASH, health, and protection.

1 - Identified Needs

Children and adolescents have the following needs; 1) Psychosocial support services due to psychosocial distress caused by conditions in the camp, family challenges, and effects of their fight to Bangladesh 2) Adolescent life skills and vocational training to address effects of child labour and quip adolsecnts with skills that will be helpful in future.3) Case management and other referral services to address the immediate and urgent needs of most at risk children and adolescents.4) Knowledge and information on prevention and response to the child protection concerns.5) Specialized services, protection and inclusion of children and adolescents with specials needs.6) Parental needs including parenting training, psychosocial support, managing daily stressors, and increased social communication.7) Learning needs for adolescents including access to basic education, pre-school, pre-vocational

2 - Activities or outputs

-Establish CP multipurpose centres to be used to mobilize communities for protection and well-being of all children, to provide structured and sustained MHPSS, CP case management services, resilience program and activities for adolescents which including life skills, pre-vocational training and informal education. -Case management with specialized mental health support for children who are at risk of exploitation, abuse and violence and other CP services to meet their needs. -Problem Management Plus (PM+) intervention for parents to deal with regular distress and manage daily life problems which will lead to better development and relationship with children-Structured Positive Parenting training for caregivers-Life skill, pre-vocational, resilience and peace-building activities for adolescence. -Structured and sustained MHPSS support for children and adolescents-Support community-led initiatives-Community-led messaging on key Protection and Child Protection issues (awareness session, drama, home-based messaging.-Establish or strengthen CBCP groups to prevent and respond to CP risks in the community -Social cohesion initiatives to improve the quality of the relationship between host and refugee-Community-led initiatives led by adolescents-Conduct KAP surveys on CP concerns-Train all community volunteers and CBGs in community-led messaging including on COVID19 and Do No Harm principles-Train staff and volunteers in CP including PSEA, safeguarding, RI code of conduct-Support volunteer groups to work as PERU during Monsoon and Cyclone
