Project Summary

UNICEF as the provider of last resort, has the accountability for covering critical needs/gaps remaining as a results of new population displacements or diminished capacity/departure of other humanitarian WASH partners in any state throughout Nigeria.
The project is aimed at addressing life-saving WASH assistance to the most vulnerable people affected, displacement (new or continued), flood and mitigate the effects of the current cholera outbreak in the BAY States. This action will also provide complimentary and crucial pipeline support to WaSH partners to better respond to emerging crises and reach an estimated individuals with critical supplies, improve sector coordination within the WaSH to ensure quality and timely assessments and analyses, improve coverage and reduce duplication of services.
Over 516,108 people will be provided with sustained access to safe water through regular water point chlorination (shock chlorination and bucket chlorination) and campaign at community level. Water quality control and emergency water treatment of both clear and turbid water for 50,000 households will be emphasized in communities without communal water sources or relying on rivers and streams as their sources of water. 535,000 people will be provided with hygiene supplies.
The objective of the project is to facilitate the provision of safe water and practice of personal hygiene through sustained chlorination of water points, safe water chain including use of clean containers for water storage and the use of Aqutabs for household water treatment and provision of replenishment hygiene kits and other WASH NFIs.
The envisaged outcome is prevention of disease outbreaks such as cholera, diarrhoea and hepatitis. UNICEF through the common supply will procure water treatment chemicals (Calcium Hypochlorite 65-70% - HTH, Aluminum sulphate, PUR sachet and Aqutabs), inline chlorine dosers and hygiene supplies. These WASH supplies will be prepositioned and used to provide lifesaving WASH services in the hot spot locations affected by displacement (new or continued), cholera and flood. Procured supplies will be distributed to WASH sector partners by UNICEF in line with the WASH sector's needs/gaps analysis and prioritization in response to emergency needs, at the request of the WASH sector coordinator using the sector supply pipeline procedure to access the stock.
This will be based on satisfactory needs assessment and justification coupled with other compounding factors such as availability of stocks, ensuring no overlapping with other responses, strategy and soundness of the planned response, as well as the sector priority.
The annual requirement of WaSH supply pipeline for 2023 is estimated at $23,000,000 This estimate was done collaboration with WaSH sector partners and based on the humanitarian response plan (HRP).
To meet the demands of the provider of last resort (POLR) responsibility, UNICEF may need to mobilize 50% of the funding requirement i.e ($11,500,000) to provide the initial lifesaving response to the gaps, prioritizing areas receiving newly displaced people, disease outbreak such as cholera, hepatitis or other WASH-related diseases including covid-19.
UNICEF will work together with the sector coordinator and through UNICEF Facilitators to monitor the use of the supplies. These supplies will supplement routine supplies distributed in these locations, and they will target the lifesaving needs of IDP and newly arrivals and host population. UNICEF will ensure that storage is secure, clean, dry, well ventilated, no exposed to direct sunlight and not too hot. WASH sector will maintain an updated common pipeline

1 - Needs

The conflict in northeast Nigeria has left an estimated 8.6 million people in need of humanitarian assistance. Among the three affected states, there are currently 2.2 million internally displaced people: 1.5 million people have returned home since August 2015 and over 800,000 live in areas that are inaccessible to humanitarian organizations. This dynamic context requires strong sector leadership to ensure appropriate humanitarian preparedness and response capacity, adherence to humanitarian principals, develop strategies and technical standards and support the implementation of nexus activities and/or the full transition to early recovery where feasible. There are currently 36 WASH partners providing life-saving services under the 2022 HRP. WaSH service delivery is hampered by high congestion in camps, limited space to source adequate water supply, poor hygiene practices, open defecation, organizational capacities and chronic underdevelopment. Household level handwashing options have been explored but widespread scarcity of basic WASH NFIs such as soap, detergent, water storage containers due to limited quantity available in markets and high prices have further constrained the household to ensure improved hygiene, increasing communicable diseases. According to the MSNA 2022, 5 million people need support to meet their basic WaSH needs and sector partners aim to reach 3 million people.The 2022 rainy season coupled with flash flood has wreaked havoc among vulnerable communities, leading to the loss of lives, destroying shelters and livelihoods, contaminating water sources. According to SEMA (State Emergency Management Agencies) reports, since July more than 513,000 people have been directly affected by the flooding triggered by heavy rains, including an estimated 222,000 in Borno, 160,000 in Yobe and 131,000 in Adamawa). The contamination of water sources from flooding increased the risk of waterborne disease in the BAY region which is already facing a severe cholera outbreak. In many internally displaced persons (IDPs) camps, shelters have been submerged or have collapsed under the intense pressure of the heavy rains. Many schools have been closed either due to the flooding or because they are occupied by affected communities as shelters. Based on severity ranking of flood damage, reported cholera cases and number of contaminated water sources, 27 LGAs across the BAY States have been identified, namely: In Borno (7 (LGAs) Dikwa, Jere, Kala/Balge, Maiduguri, Mobbar, Monguno, Ngala, Adamawa (14 LGAs) Demsa, Fufore, Girei, Lamurde, Madagali, Michika, Mubi North, Mubi South, Numan, Shelleng, Song, Toungo, Yola North, Yola South and Yobe (6 LGAs) Damaturu, Fune, Geidam, Gujba, Gulani, Jakusko. Outbreaks have been declared in all three states. Since January, over 14,000 cases of acute watery diarrhoea (AWD)/cholera and at least 443 deaths were confirmed in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States as of 1st November. Borno has 81 per cent of reported cases (11,000), followed by Yobe (2,400) and Adamawa (200). At many locations, overcrowding in camps and host communities, coupled with poor sanitation and hygiene practices, has fueled the spread of AWD/cholera across the three states. Lack of essential WASH items has put at risk millions of vulnerable populations in BAY states with consequences of especially:a) Increase of Acute Watery Diarrhoea, including health and nutrition concerns, further increasing the risks of children (especially U5) and women.b) The delayed capacity of emergency response; the IDP population who are mostly targeted at the peak of displacement will have to wait longer or miss out entirely on critical WASH items.c) Loss of dignity to schoolgirls and women who benefit from responses conducted through the common pipeline.d) Lack of support for new displacements including returnees. The pipeline offers flexibility to support the influx of IDPs due to conflict.e) Negative coping mechanism including protection

2 - Activities

UNICEF will implement seven key activities (outlined below) through various partnerships, Long Term Agreement (LTA) contracts with selected partners and State Govt Agencies under the Basic Cooperation Agreement with Federal Government of Nigeria and Annual Work Plans with the state governments in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe.The project has the following main activities to protect the dignity of the new arrivals and to ensure reduction of risk to deterioration of the vulnerability of the displaced persons or of their health and nutrition status upon arrival: a) Enable water quality provision and monitoring through safely chlorinating water and testing it.b) Provision of immediate response WASH kits and Dignity Kitsc) Ensure sustainable availability of core items, while ensuring cost efficiency due to economies of scale, enabling uniform, and coordinated responses.d) Offer a solution that addresses the complex logistical challenges through prepositioning improving capacity to respond to critical humanitarian needs.e) Streamline accountability in all processes of needs verification, response and post-distribution monitoring.f) Timely response and capacity to support urgent needs, especially beneficiaries affected by health, hygiene and nutrition issues.g) Restore dignity amongst the affected population through the provision of hygiene kits and menstrual hygiene items This project will focus on 31 LGAs with high IDP influx, flood and cholera in BAY States. In Borno LGAs 11 (Bama, Gwoza,Dikwa, Jere, Kala/Balge, Konduga Maiduguri, Mafa Mobbar, Monguno and Ngala). In Adamawa, 14 LGAs (Demsa, Fufore, Girei, Lamurde, Madagali, Michika, Mubi North, Mubi South, Numan, Shelleng, Song, Toungo, Yola North and Yola South). In Yobe, 6 LGAs (Damaturu, Fune, Geidam, Gujba, Gulani, Jakusko) where UNICEF will support AWD/ flood response.
