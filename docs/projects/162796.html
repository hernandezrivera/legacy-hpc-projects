Project Summary

CRS will focus on providing a full package of WASH interventions and will adopt a participatory approach, mobilizing communities in the design and planning of WASH activities. CRS programming takes into account the specific needs of each segment of the community while giving special consideration to the most vulnerable, including children and people with disabilities. Women will also greatly benefit as they will be involved in the water user committees (WUC) which we intend to have 50% women representation will have a stronger voice with regards to management of water resources and they shall be able to use this platform to raise their profile within the community. Improvement of sanitation facilities at homestead level will benefit the whole household and most critically women and vulnerable persons. At the moment, in most rural areas, women and vulnerable persons have to go very far from the homestead to defecate. This poses great challenges to them in terms to their personal security. With improved sanitation, they shall be able to access latrines even at night within the homestead. Bringing WASH facilities closer to homesteads ensures that the old and sick are able to access these facilities without straining themselves by walking far distances.
CRS will ensure adverse environmental impacts are mitigated. Pumping tests shall be conducted on newly constructed water sources to ensure safe abstraction rates are determined. Water User Committees shall be trained on water conservation to promote prudent use of water resources. CRS staff will work closely with the community to ensure that local materials used in construction are sources sustainable and where possible use of byproducts of agriculture such as harvested crop stocks and other by products are reused in construction of latrine superstructures.

1 - Needs

Red Sea State (RSS) falls between latitude 16º and 22º North and longitudes 35º and 37º East. It is divided into two distinct physical areas: the coastal strip, the hilly area and the flat western plains. Its total area of about 218,887km2 is divided ten localities (mahallias), The topography of the State is diverse, with the desert comprising almost one third of the total area. RSS is classified as arid to hyper arid and experiences extremely low, unevenly distributed and variable rainfall, with 60% of the State receiving rainfall less than 100mm per annum, mostly during the period of November and February. The State is also characterized by extremely high-water deficit on both monthly and annual scales, with relatively high humidity and temperatures usually in the range of 22-41Cº. Climate change and related natural disasters (floods, droughts, dry spells) have strong bearing on the socioeconomic situation of communities and households and can contribute to the number of people requiring humanitarian support. Environmental degradation and unsustainable use of natural resources are both contributory factors and consequences of the humanitarian situation in Sudan. Moreover, Sudan’s limited water supply from non-Nilotic sources also gives rise to humanitarian needs. The presence of Red Sea and Red Sea Hills is responsible for the challenging climate characteristics of the State, which negatively affects water sources. The spread of diseases is high in red sea state. However, access to safe drinking water is limited, with much of the ground water requiring desalination due to the influence of the Red Sea. The RSS is the highest state practicing open defecation in Sudan.In December 2019 CRS participated in inter agency mission to assess needs of people affected by heavy rains in populations in Tokar. The initial findings in the population of over 40,000 individuals are not accessing to clean water because all the 6 water distribution points of the locality are out of services. as well 10 households latrines are collabsed and the remain are un used becase its dangrous and able to collabse. CRS currently Implementing food security and liveleihood project and from the observation and the filed visit report of CRS staff indicated there is huge gap in these localities and find these communities have no access to adequate WASH facilities as well have poor hygiene practices.CRS multisectoral (Nutrition, FSL and WASH) project in Haya, Tokar and Dordaib localities ended last September 2019, although the project included some WASH activities but the gap was huge and these communities have very limited access to adequate WASH facilities as well have poor hygiene practices.Although in the initial result of S3M conducted in 2018 compared to result of S3M conducted in 2013 doesn't shows any improvement in the status of situation even it went worse is some localities in terms of Sanitation Haya, Halayb and Tokar are become worst than before.

2 - Activities / Outputs

The communities targeted by the Improving and sustaining access to WASH services for Vulnerable People in Red Sea State are primarily host communities, with livelihood activities mainly based on mixed agro-pastoralism, Drilling 2 new hand pumps, Repairing and rehabilitation of 10 of the existing hand pumps, Rehabilitation of one water yards, Rehabilitation and upgrading of existing hand dug wells, Upgrading of the existing potential hand pump to mini-water yard, Establish and train Water User Committees on water resource operation, maintenance, management, water quality monitoring and treatment, Establishment of community based sanitation and hygiene promoters committees and training on community led total sanitation (CLTS), Conduct periodic community mobilization and awareness sessions on best hygiene practices, Establish solid waste management system among the community and support them with necessary tools.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

CRS mainstreams protection into all programs so that activities (i) prioritize safety, dignity and access, in addition to avoiding causing harm, (ii) ensure equality and meaningful access, (iii) include mechanisms for beneficiary accountability, and (iv) emphasize participation and empowerment to meet SHF minimum requirements. CRS prioritizes the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and avoids causing harm by preventing and minimizing as much as possible any unintended negative effects of its interventions that can increase vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. The project will ensure that targeted beneficiaries have safe access to assistance and services based on need and without discrimination. Efforts will be made to accommodate people with specific needs, ensuring access to services. Through the CBCM, target populations will be able to measure the adequacy of interventions and address concerns and complaints, thereby supporting the development of self-protection capacities and assisting people to claim their rights, including – not exclusively – the right to shelter, food, water, sanitation, health, and education. The project will ensure that services provided are accessible to people with disabilities. CRS will adhere to Sphere Principles to implement activities under this project according the following: 1. Prevent: Enhance the safety, dignity and rights of affected people, and avoid exposing people to further harm;2. Respond: Reduce the impact of physical and psychological harm that arises from violence, coercion, deliberate deprivation and other threats; and3. Remedy: Assist people to claim their rights and access appropriate remedies.These three principles are interdependent and can be carried out at the same time. The principles promote the right to dignity in three ways:• mainstreaming protection risks, activities and related information across all humanitarian programs;• integrating specific protection objectives into assistance projects; and• promoting specific and/or specialized protection activities that address specific protection risks and violations.In addition, CRS takes care to design program activities that consider findings from a risk analysis. The agency also actively looks for ways to minimize threats and work with specific at-risk groups, and the organizations they trust. Such approaches help CRS understand how best to address needs and any positive or negative consequences. In consultation with relevant line Ministries, The Humanitarian Aid Commission (HAC) and the communities, CRS will design activities that keep girls, boys and women safe by, for example, protecting against or preventing child recruitment, abduction or separation from family. Special consideration will be provided to the needs of at-risk members of the community, such as separated and unaccompanied children, child-headed households, adolescent girls and boys, children with disabilities andchronic illnesses, and young parents.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The project will be designed in a manner that humanitarian assistance will be provided in line and in proportion to the needs identified and documented in targeted areas, in adherence with SHF guidelines. CRS will involve the beneficiaries and respond to their feedback when planning, implementing, monitoring and evaluating its program. CRS’ accountability includes four pillars i.e. risk management, capacity assessment and monitoring the performance of implementing partners, monitoring and reporting, and project and partner auditing. CRS will provide space for communities to shape their own recovery and for CRS and partners to better deliver on commitments to stakeholders, including the people CRS serves and the resource partners who make assistance possible. CRS will ensure timely information sharing among stakeholders regarding targeted communities. CRS will focus on various approaches to collect information for community-based indicators. As an example, community score cards ground CRS’ monitoring and evaluation in appropriate and inclusive feedback from affected communities and their leadership through each phase of the project cycle. Feedback channels not only respect the right of affected communities to have a say, but often also improve the efficiency and effectiveness of aid delivery. CRS will establish effective feedback mechanisms and a Community-Based Complaints Mechanism (CBCM) so that CRS and partners can incorporate feedback and address problems rapidly, including for sensitive issues like protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) by humanitarian or development workers. CRS and partner staff will be trained and oriented on SEA.In line with CRS’ Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) policies and procedures, CRS will consult communities in establishing an appropriate feedback and response mechanism to give each community the opportunity to identify, report and seek redress for program quality and protection concerns. The system will address both sensitive and non-sensitive issues while upholding confidentially and non-retaliation principles. Examples of the format of each mechanism the project is considering include monthly feedback meetings between CRS staff and community members, a phone number where communities can report sensitive issues, and community leadership committees. Specifically, CRS will link project beneficiaries with existing feedback mechanisms in the area for formats like: (1) a complaint desk, where beneficiaries can lodge a complaint and receive a response in a reasonable timeframe; and (2) Active outreach to project beneficiaries by the MEAL Officer, who will take findings from random beneficiary satisfaction visits to regular program management meetings, or (3) suggestion boxes to offer confidential feedback as appropriate.
