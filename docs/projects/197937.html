Project Summary

The security context in the North-West and South-West regions (NWSW) remains volatile, marked by continuous violence, abductions, kidnappings, killings, unlawful arrests, and destruction of property. This is aggravating humanitarian needs, as affected people continue to flee their homes, seeking safety in the bushes and neighbouring communities (OCHA Sitrep. No. 44, June 2022).
The population of these two regions continues to suffer the consequences of violent incidents, either being targeted directly or being caught in IEDs explosions, crossfire, or military raids. Partners reported multiple displacements across the NWSW caused mainly by violence. Over 7,212 persons were reportedly displaced to nearby bushes and villages with Manyu (SW) and Bui (NW) being the most affected. Most of the displacements are pendular and temporary, as displaced persons return to their homes once the security situation allows ((OCHA sitrep. 7 December 2022).
There are over 2 million people in need of humanitarian assistance according to the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022 with 1.4 million people targeted for assistance.
There are over 592,600 internally displaced persons and 417,516 reurnees (former IDPs and refugees) in the South West and South West Region (HNO 2022, MSNA NWSW, OCHA March 2022).
This project aims to:
• Improve sustainable access to safe drinking water and basic sanitation by vulnerable people in high IDP concentration communities in the South West and Littoral regions.
• Reduce the risk of poor hygiene-related morbidity and mortality around communities affected by the crisis and cholera outbreak.
The objectives will be achieved through the implementation of the following activities:
• Construction/rehabilitation of undeveloped water points and non-functional water points respectively in affected communities,
• Protection of constructed water points with dead and live fences.
• Formation and training of community water and/or management committees and support to their functioning,
• Engaging in hygiene and sanitation promotion and supporting individual households with financial and technical support to enable them to construct their household latrines.
Results:
• 08 water points constructed and 07 water points rehabilitated
• 20 water management committees established/revamped, trained, and supported to better manage water points,
• 1,450 vulnerable households provided with critical WASH kits,
• 300 households supported with squatting plates and cement to realize household latrines
• 20,000 people sensitized on behaviour change communication on safe hygiene and sanitation practices.
• 10 - 20 EPDA staff capacity on PSEA reinforced and 100 beneficiary households sensitized and trained on PSEA by trained EPDA staff.
• 20 critically threatened water points protected with dead fence to avoid water point encroachment and possible contamination

1 - Besoins / Needs

Continuous displacement of population due to insecurity, the insufficient investment of the Government of Cameroon in basic social services, the foreseeable resurgence of cholera outbreak, the continuous threats from COVID-19 pandemic and the insufficient response provided by humanitarian actors in the South west and Littoral regions continue to exacerbate the water and sanitation need of IDPs and host communities including in the neighboring regions (Littoral and West regions). The last MSNA surveys conducted reveals that 55% of assessed villages collect water from an unimproved water point. In some divisions of the Southwest region (Ndian and Manyu), at least 75% of assessed localities collect water from an unimproved water point. The three main challenges relative to water are the insufficiency of improved water point, the long distance (due to insufficiency of safe drinking water point), lack of containers to collect and store water and encroachment into water points which may cause contamination through human activities. Concerning sanitation, although open defecation is low (2.4% of assessed villages), utilization of pit latrine without slap is predominant (78.8%) among assessed villages. This represents a high risk of contamination of surface and groundwater used by population in concerned localities. In the South West and Littoral regions, the outbreak of COVID-19 in late 2019 and Cholera in 2021 have further compounded and exacerbated the situation with increased morbidity and mortality among affected communities with IPDs and host families most at risk because of the shared WASH facilities and crowded homes. This project plans to address the following needs:Recruitment of project staff: 04 additional manpower recruited to ensure efficiency and effectiveness. Some experienced staff members in EPDA will directly be implicated in the project.Training on PSEA: A training of trainers’ workshop shall be organized and 10 - 20 EPDA staff will be trained and will in return train 100 households on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Training of water management committees: Some community members will be selected and trained on water supply network and water point/ toilet management techniques. Construction or rehabilitation of water points: 8 water points shall be constructed and 7 rehabilitated to improve water access. This will include water catchments, pipeline extension with tap(s), and climate resilient boreholes. These will provide at least 750 beneficiaries (450 females and 300 males). Construction of Household latrines: 300 households will be supported with squatting plates and cement such that each household will benefit from two squatting plates and a bag of cement serving about 15,000 persons.Awareness creation: 20,000 persons will be sensitized on safe hygiene and sanitation practices including cholera outbreak and COVID 19 preventive measures. 1,450 households will each be provided with critical WASH kits. EPDA will ensure that there is accountability to the affected populations through sensitization. A complaint and feedback mechanism will be put in place in the beneficiary communities. EPDA will also account its actions to the donor by providing regular project reports (monthly progress) and the sharing of field pictures will be on a rolling basis. GBV and protection shall be mainstreamed in aspects GBV victim identification and referrals. The project shall be implemented through a community-based in order to improve ownership. This community approach enables a rapid move in water and sanitation ladder from open defecation to improve and non-shared basic sanitation facilities. Communities will be mobilized and trained on community water management as well as public toilet management. Host communities will be trained to construct household latrines with technical and material support.
