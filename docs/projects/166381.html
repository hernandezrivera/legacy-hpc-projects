Project Summary

As of 17 April, 2020 493 COVID-19 cases were confirmed in Nigeria, and the following factors may contribute to increased spread: weakened health system, high population concentration, lack of access to potable water, insufficient sanitation infrastructure, and inadequate awareness of prevention measures. This is exacerbated in conflict-affected states such as Borno. About 600,000 IDP children attending some form of learning in the camps and host communities will be affected by the stoppage of learning activities. In addition to closing schools the Federal government has closed all non-essential services including child services which indicates a strong need for integrated education and child protection services to support the most vulnerable and marginalized children.
Depending on the duration of school closure, children may lose the learning and pychosocial skills, or the entire school year and find themselves into other tasks helping the family.
There is also a risk of losing volunteer teachers on the fact that they shall no longer receive honourariums coupled with the pre-existing scarcity of teachers that was a serious barrier in accessing education. This crisis shall negatively impact the program at the school resumption. There is also concern that empty classrooms could be occupied by internally displaced persons (IDPs) in over-crowded IDP camps, or by military, or identified for use as isolation facilities for COVID-19 cases.
ROHI intends to continue education activities at the camp schools with more activities geared towards preventing the spread of the coronavirus. Children will be mobilized through awareness activities to attend classes in the existing camp TLS. Classes will be conducted through different timings of up to four shifts with only few children in attendance in order to maintain the culture of social distancing. Through community participation, ROHI will engage adolescents in each camp who will be trained on COVID-19 hygeine practices and self learning approaches enabling them to follow up of children as part of blended self learning (part of sessions in TLSs and part in children’s tents/huts). On other fron, the adolescents will lead the awareness campagins on best hygiene practices and COVID-19 related messages in IDP camps. Since small group approaches wil be employed in IDP camps to provide opportunities to as many children as possible, adolescents will also take part in the tutoring sessions with the children in the camps through UNICEF’s blended self learning model and thereafter follow up their assigned group of children to their houses to ensure engagement wile they are away of the TLSs as part of the self learning model, children will be provided with instructional materials in reading and numeracy. The adolecents will be trained also to provide coaching activities for the children outside the school premises. Digital learning platforms will be set up in each of the camps to provide additional learning through the four shifts. Projectors, TV and Radio will convey different educational messages while also providing awareness messages on COVID19.
The Education in Emergencies Working Group through the Strategic Advisory Group has developed and published a COVID19 strategic response plan, however needs are vast, and an adequate response requires support from partners. In order to support the response plan, ROHI has developed a program which outlines 3 main objectives:
• Prevent the spread of COVID-19 and mitigate risks of SEA among learners, teachers, parents and School Based Management Commitees (SBMC).
• Provide continuous education support programs through self learning and digital learning platforms using trained adolescents with the integration of pychosocial activities.
• Establishment of community participation through adolescents, teachers and SBMC to create continuous awareness of COVID19 and respond to WASH gaps in camp schools.
