Project Summary

Support the vulnerable population in area C of the West Bank to meet their food needs
The proposed intervention supplement the NPA Palestine Country Strategy 2016-2019 in
Gaza and the West Bank and is aligned with the Food Security Cluster objective 1: “The
resilience and the productive capacity of vulnerable households are restored/enhanced
and livelihoods protected”. It also fits under FSS activity 1: Rehabilitation and provision of
productive assets and inputs, for farmers, herders, breeders, and fishers, including
promotion of technical capacities and small-scale production units, and using the BBB
(Build Back Better) approach for increased resilience. NPA proposed intervention
contributes to the following 2018 2020 Humanitarian Response Plan for oPt Strategic
objective 3 as follows: “The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted
crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and
other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued”. The proposed intervention is part of
a multi-year program. Over a three years, the program will be implemented using the
same activities, but for new beneficiaries and new possibly geographical areas which will
be prioritized later based on the Ministry of Agriculture needs assessment and in
consultation with the FSS team and other relevant actors.

Needs

Palestinian communities in Nablus, Qalqiliya and Tulkarm areas are among the most vulnerable in the West Bank, where a combination of Israeli policies and practices has resulted in fragmentation of land and shrinking space for Palestinians, undermining their presence. In particular, demolitions and forced evictions deprive people of their homes and disrupt livelihoods, leading to entrenched poverty and increased aid dependency. The continuous settler attacks, land confiscation and the need for securing access to land are the main justifications of NPA proposed interventions in these areas. Agriculture dependent communities in Area C, are vulnerable to food insecurity. Those small scale farmers/households have acute needs to secure their livelihood and lessen the impact of the restrictions imposed by the Israeli occupation constrained by their limited economic access to food. They are also subject to protection concerns and are at risk of forced displacement, due to the interplay of multiple factors, including demolitions, settler violence, movement restrictions, lack of access to services which deprive people of their homes and disrupt livelihoods. This Leading to entrenched poverty and increased aid dependency. Such violations would constraint farmers and household access to their lands. Further, their traditional income generating activities are becoming unsustainable as even the most basic support is subject to increasingly stringent Israeli policies, (e.g. the confiscation of pipes essential for the functioning of irrigation systems). As of September 2017 the level of demolitions and confiscation of agricultural assets at the household level remains high. In addition to food security issues, the cost of agricultural implements represents a significant barrier to small farmers in resuming their livelihood. In the agricultural sector, women's participation is about 13.1% (compared to 7.8% for men) although many times unaccounted for and unpaid. However, their role and production capacities is often not recognised or supported. Women have traditionally played an essential and active role in small scale, urban agriculture. Seemingly, many women enter the agriculture sector because there is no other choice (for work/for survival). Women in general lack the technical knowledge to practice farming activities, due to the fact that their access to training opportunities or extension services is very limited. Moreover, women’s access to agricultural input is much limited as it is usually the ‘men’s job’ to communicate with the outside world. Through the project, training/orientation sessions will be implemented in consultation with both men and women in order to ensure their availability, ability and access to the training due to their geographical locations. Also, women particular needs will be analysed in depth and considered. Special sessions will be organized for those beneficiaries who are not able to attend the regular training sessions due to their preoccupation with either family issues or work related issues. Officially, 11% of the Palestinian households are headed by women. According to the World Food Programme, “One third, of households headed by women are food insecure, compared to a quarter of households headed by men.” Therefore, the project will prioritize female headed households, where they will be supported with home gardens to enable them to meet their short-term basic needs. The signs of impact and sustainability for home gardens was observed during the three previous years with NPA interventions in the West Bank. Project beneficiaries, indicated that the income they received through this component made a major contribution to improving their consumption and preventing them from incurring further debts. A participatory approach will be applied and participation of women in the different project stages will be specifically addressed in this project.

Activities

The Project output is: “The agricultural production capacity of 280 small scale farmers/ households in the areas that are/have been most affected by the occupation in Area C of the West Bank is restored”. This intervention address Nablus, Qalqiliya and Tulkarm areas, and targets small scale farmers and female headed households who are unable to resume agriculture work, due to lack of access and affordability of the key agricultural production inputs. The project is designed to increase the target groups’ resilience through providing the means to the farmers to resume their livelihood. The strategy focuses on developing the households’ productive assets that would improve household food self- sufficiency and potential income. The resulting income would enable households to address immediate and essential food and nutrition needs, hence decreasing their food insecurity. The program will provide agricultural inputs to the targeted farmers to be able produce in the cropping season. This will lead to improve production, increase the average income from agriculture while enhancing the purchasing power for the most vulnerable population affected by food shortages. Criteria for selecting beneficiaries, including but are not limited to the following: - The applicant is a conflict affected household, who is pursuing farming as the primary livelihood and is unable to resume agriculture work because of insufficient resources to afford input cost. - The applicant is food insecure with underemployed family members and is in need of support to restore their livelihoods. - The applicant’s farm must be owned, accessible and have a reliable water supply. NPA sees partnership as a two-way cooperative relationship, sharing the complementary resources of various partners and thus power to fulfil the objectives. One Palestinian NGO in partnership with NPA will implement the activities of the project. Local partner will identifying the vulnerable groups; selecting beneficiaries; ensuring that the project is progressing according to plan; organizing the information and training for beneficiaries and suppliers. Prior to any project implementation, NPA will strictly coordinate the final definition and selection of localities and beneficiaries with line ministries offices, and with any other organization working in the same area and sector of activities. Activity 1: training for both small scale farmers and female headed households. 1.1 special separate training will be conducted for female headed households on orchard management (providing manure, soil tillage, water conservation, pruning, pest management). 1.2 small scale farmers will receive training on sustaining soil fertility by soil sterilization and the use of organic fertilizers. Activity 2: distribution of Input voucher scheme 2.1: distribute 320 input vouchers for 160 small scale to support livelihood recovery and restoration of productive assets. The small scale farmer can will bene t from vouchers for exchange of agriculture inputs (improved seeds, seedlings, fertilizers, irrigation tools, farm tools etc.). 2.2: distribute 240 input vouchers for 120 female headed households to support livelihood recovery and restoration of productive assets. Female headed households can exchange the vouchers for receiving a home gardens components and also she can choose what she prefer out of the pre identified list as follows :( (Improved seeds, seedlings, fertilizers, farm tools etc.) Activity 3: Input voucher redemption: Voucher can be redeemed for a pre identified list of agricultural inputs, including seedlings and crop seeds; tools such as, hoes, shovels; organic fertilizer, pipes and tanks for irrigation networks; and metal fencing. 3.1: 160 small scale farmers redeem vouchers for essential seeds and farm tools (seed and fertilizer, etc.) 3.2: 120 female headed households redeem vouchers for home gardens components (seed and fertilizer, etc.)

During the implementation of the project activities, beneficiaries’ safety and dignity will be fully considered. Besides, NPA will avoid causing harm or any unintended negative effects of the proposed intervention which can increase people's vulnerability to risks. Further, NPA will tackle any potential barriers which would affect beneficiaries’ access to project services. Furthermore, NPA partners will set up an accountability system to address beneficiaries’ concerns and complaints. NPA will ensure active community involvement in the project. Moreover, NPA and local partners will consult with local community leaders and Community Based Organizations as well as project beneficiaries including women and female headed households. In the first stage of the project implementation NPA partners will conduct a baseline survey to map the situation before project activities commence and provide the data for indicators to monitor change. A monitoring, evaluation, accountability and learning (MEAL) plan will be designed at the preparation phase of the project. NPA's field staff will be engaged in household visits, community mobilisation and orientation sessions, shop monitoring, and beneficiary redemption monitoring. Progress against agreed upon milestones would be measured and reported on in the partners monthly progress reports, submitted to the NPA. Final progress, financial and audit reports describe the activities implemented, their objectives, participants, results obtained (outputs and outcomes), challenges encountered and how these were managed, lessons learnt and recommendations will be prepared and delivered to the donor. The Project monitoring plan includes gender specific indicators and all quantitative data to be collected will be disaggregated by gender. Monitoring will be at two levels, including adequate monitoring tools: 1. Monitoring implementation in the field: day to day monitoring of activities is the responsibility of partner field staff. This will include shop monitoring, prices monitoring, beneficiary redemption monitoring, transfer of payments, and overall compliance. 2. Monitoring Food Security indicators, socioeconomic indicators and general project impact on the people participating is carried out through partner field staff and is analyzed jointly with NPA. A Baseline, Post Distributions Monitoring, and an end-line survey will take place covering the key project outputs.
