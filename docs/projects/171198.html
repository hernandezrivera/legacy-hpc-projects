Project Summary

The proposed project intends to improve emergency and sustainable adequate safe water, sanitation facilities in the IDPs and host communities living in six regions (Middle Shabelle, Galgadud, Bay, Nugaal, Waqoyi Galbed, and Hiraan). Specifically, Mercy corps will rehabilitate 10 strategic boreholes in regions Bay, Galgadud, Hiraan and Middle Shabelle, installing them solar power to reduce cost thereby increasing sustainability. Moreover, the rehabilitation works will cater to the installation of elevated storage tanks that hold 25 meters cubic water with the full and equal participation of women and men in the household, community or institution to support construction and rehabilitation of appropriate WASH facilities.
Mercy Corps will conduct pipeline water extensions to IDPs centers in urban to ensure continuous supply of safe drinking water. The pipeline extension will reduce the distance women and children covered in search water exposing them to protection risks such as sexual abuse. Mercy corps through it is a local implementing partner (JF) will rehabilitate 30 shallow wells in lower Shabelle, on the other hand, Mercy corps will rehabilitate 20 shallow wells in Bay and Hiran region while addressing sustainable water issues. Additionally, Mercy Corps will develop infiltration wells for riverine communities in the middle Shabelle and Hiran region to enhance access to safe drinking water. Mercy Corps will also provide lifesaving water trucking to 500 HH for a period of three months. inadequate access to sanitation facilities and increasing risks associated with poor sanitation in IDPS and poor host communities, Mercy Corps plans to construct 400 gender-sensitive, flood-proof with solar lighting latrines and handwashing facilities across the six target regions, in order to address the shortage of household water containers and general safety of water at the household level through the use of water treatment tablets and the use of different containers for storage and collection. During implementation process mainstreaming of gender and disability will done through setting of quotas to ensure disable and women get their fair share. This will be done through participatory process where communities will be sensitized and educated on the importance of considering minority groups such as disabled. Moreover, Mercy Crops will ensure this groups are represented in the selection committee and local community governance to ensure they well represented and their issues articulated.
To roll out the cost recovery- based water supply system, at the outset of the project, Mercy Corps will establish community-based WASH committees (in total 120 committees). These WASH committees will be the community representatives’ entities and formed with a gender balance and proportionate inclusion of vulnerable groups and ethnic minorities to ensure that the decisions of these committees are accepted and honored across the community. In some locations where men often dominate WASH committees, auxiliary women-only WASH committees are formed to managed water points and acts, as a collective voice for women regarding gender-related issues. The projects aim to reach 6,983 households comprising 9,379 men. 14,085 women, 9,035 boys and 9,401 girls. In addition to that mercy corps is currently working in IDPs and host communities in Baidoa, Dhusamarreb, & Balcad implementing Somalia Emergency and Early Market Recovery (SEAM) Programme funded by USAID/OFDA aimed at life-saving interventions through water provision, sanitation, and hygiene services. The proposed project will complement Mercy corps response efforts and that will enable service delivery to the most deserved in terms of WASH services, A total of 1500 persons will be reached through hygiene promotion training and hygiene promotion forum campaign as part of Mercy corps contribution towards enhancing and eradicating the affected population towards AWD outbreak

Project Activities

• Conduct construction of pipeline water extension with elevated water tanks for 20 IDP camps in urban centers to increase access to safe drinking water • Conduct construction and rehabilitation of 50 community-owned public shallow wells that serve the target communities with the view of increasing it is yield and reducing the time taken to fetch water• Conduct construction and development of 20 infiltration wells with an elevated water tank to pump clean water in riverine areas prone to flood to ensure continued access to reduce the risk of waterborne infection for vulnerable groups e.g. elderly, children, and disable • Conduct rehabilitation and solarization of key 10 strategic boreholes to increase efficiency, sustainability, and effectiveness in the long-term provision of water to rural host communities • • Conduct treatment of water points to reduce the risk of infection • Provide training of 200 male and female community personnel, teachers/ToTs and health staff (50% female & 10% Disable participants) to communicate key hygiene messages effectively, including hygiene practices to address water-borne diseases, COVID-19 preparedness, and the risk of open defecation practices; and form hygiene promotion groups that will, in turn, conduct hygiene campaigns in their respective settlements• Conduct construction of 400 VIP latrines with solar lighting to reduce the risk of contamination of water thereby reducing the water-borne and related diseases for host and IDP communities • Distribute 3,000 sanitation and hygiene kits to 3,000 households including people with disabilities of the flood affected population in villages in the project target areas. • Provide life- saving water trucking to 500HH during the pick hour of the drought/floods to mitigate it is effects on vulnerable host communities and IDPs • Promote and conduct dissemination of key hygiene messages, and practices addressing also underlying causes of malnutrition to both women and men equally in communities, and key institutions (nutrition feeding centers, health facilities, schools, water fetching points)

The project will address the humanitarian needs of the displaced people andvulnerable hosts’ community in a manner that drives beyond service delivery, toone that builds resilience and the ability to withstand future shocks; preservessafety and dignity in order to improve the lives and self-reliance of displacedpopulations while simultaneously addressing the impact on host communities.Mercy Corps will work with all respective stakeholders starting with theparticipation of affected persons, the WASH, Protection, and Health ClusterCoordination team at the local and national level and the relevant GovernmentMinistries. The participation of all stakeholders will build a strong foundationfor disaster risk reduction through empowerment, synergies of approaches, andcomplimentary programming across sectors. Hygiene kits beneficiaries andbeneficiaries of sanitation and water assets will be identified jointly with IDPLeaders and local authorities; this will ensure effectiveness in addressing realneeds which is necessary for sustainability. In line with Mercy Corps principles,the implementation will be anchored on beneficiary buy-in and ownershipmechanisms accountable to the beneficiary, the donor, other fellowimplementing partners, and the local administration. This will help identify andassign roles and responsibilities at the local community level, activation of localstructures is necessary for sustainability. Also, MC has in place accountabilitymechanisms to ensure the beneficiary and stakeholders get detailed andaccurate information relevant to the project and they can also provide feedback.Mercy Corps has a team of technical and administrative managers and officersat various levels who can ensure the quality of the intended outputs guided by aset of standards including the SPHERE. Training of CHWs, teachers, and localauthorities in various subjects including DRR, COVID will improve capacity to sustainthe interventions and reduce their risks to future shocks.
