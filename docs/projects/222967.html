Project Summary

The "Strengthening Perspectives for the Future and Resilience of Refugees and Host Communities in Cox’s Bazar Project" is a comprehensive initiative addressing the complex challenges faced by Rohingya refugees (FDMNs) and vulnerable host communities in Cox’s Bazar. Structured around three objectives within the Food Security Sector (FSS), the project aims to make a meaningful impact through capacity building and targeted support in agriculture, livestock management, cash-for-work, and grants support for income-generating activities (IGA).
Addressing the objectives#2 of FSS, The activities focuses on Camps 17 and 19, aiming to assist around 700 FDMN individuals with a goal of at least 50% female participation. Caritas Bangladesh (CB), in collaboration with camp management, will use existing CB-managed WASH and Shelter facilities in Camps 17 and 19 as training centers. Participant selection will follow FSS guidelines, community consultations, and inputs from camp authorities. Each training batch will consist of approximately 20 participants who will receive technical training on crop production and homestead gardening. Upon completion, participants will receive in-kind resources or and grant support to begin applying their skills and reduce food insecurity through increase income. Additionally, certain community infrastructures will be upgraded as part of disaster preparedness efforts through cash-for-work activities. This not only reduces local disaster risk but also provides temporary employment opportunities for vulnerable households.
In the host communities, Objectives 3 and 4 will be implemented in Rajapalong and Ratnapalong unions. Under Objective 3, the project will target approximately 300 vulnerable households for participation in IGAs, livestock rearing and management, poultry farming, or cash grants for livestock. Prior to receiving these grants, selected participants will receive technical training to enhance the sustainability and success of their activities. By supporting diverse income sources, the project seeks to mitigate household-level vulnerabilities, especially those exacerbated by the Rohingya influx, which has reduced livelihood opportunities and contributed to tensions between FDMN and Bangladeshi communities.
Under Objective 4, community-based risk reduction activities will be conducted through cash-for-work initiatives aimed at developing infrastructure that reduces local risks while providing temporary employment for vulnerable households. About 600 households, with a minimum of 30% female participation, will benefit from these activities. Additionally, CB will organize capacity-building training for local disaster management institutions, including the WDMC and UDMC in Rajapalong union, to strengthen community resilience to future risks.
Throughout both host and refugee-focused activities, CB will address essential cross-cutting issues, including Gender-Based Violence (GBV), inclusion of people with disabilities, and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR). CB will also provide a dignified working environment, with facilities tailored to the needs of all participants, including accessible toilets for men, women, and people with disabilities. In cases where project participants lack alternative childcare options, CB will establish a child corner during the activity’s accomplishments.
By prioritizing these cross-cutting concerns, the project seeks to substitute a holistic approach that ensures the dignity, safety, and inclusion of all involved, laying the foundation for lasting positive change in both Rohingya and host communities in Cox’s Bazar. The project will be implemented in close coordination with FSS, camp management, and other relevant sectors, sub-sectors, and networks, including Protection, GBV, PSEA, and associated agencies as well.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

In 2023, overall vulnerability continued to rise in both Rohingya and Bangladeshi host communities, with levels significantly higher than pre-pandemic years. Among Rohingya households, 97% showed moderate to high vulnerability, far surpassing the 69% in the Bangladeshi community. Notably, the percentage of highly vulnerable Rohingya households rose by 3 points from 2022 to 86%, largely due to ration cuts and economic shocks like inflation, which began affecting the community in mid-2022 (REVA-7)The initial ISNA findings reveal significant needs and gaps in both FDMN and Bangladeshi host communities. Key challenges include income source vulnerability, low-income levels, limited household income generation, and a growing reliance on casual labor. Specific findings include: Around 73% of households have at least one income source, with 79% relying solely on one. This dependence on a single income source heightens vulnerability to disruptions, emphasizing the need for income diversification to bolster resilience. On the other hand, Over half (55%) of households earn below 5,000 BDT per month, with a median income of 3,000 BDT (MSNA 2023). These low earnings often fail to meet basic needs, indicating the urgent need for skill development programs that foster sustainable income-generation. Nearly half of households (47%) now depend on casual labor, up from 38% in 2023. This trend reflects a shortage of stable employment and highlights the importance of developing long-term, secure income sources.Regarding the women engagement in economic activities, only 8% of the total engagement in income-generating activities comes from women, pointing to significant gender disparities. Empowering women through skill development and creating economic opportunities for them can not only increase household incomes but also promote gender equality and community well-being. In addition, only 35% of individuals in these households actively contribute to household income, and there are stark gender and age disparities in these contributions. Men aged 25-60 serve as primary earners, contributing 23% of household income, while women in the same age group contribute only 2%. Among youth aged 18-24, men contribute 9% to household income, but women contribute merely 1%. This reveals an urgent need for livelihood and skill development programs that promote women’s economic participation, while also engaging youth as part of the workforce.Based on ISNA and REVA-7 assessments, a comprehensive Food Security Sector (FSS) strategy is essential to address the pressing needs of both FDMNs and host communities. The strategy should prioritize support for food security and resilience among Rohingya refugees through climate-smart, context-specific food production activities and early response actions. Additionally, it should focus on strengthening household food security, nutrition, and resilience for host communities by promoting climate-smart food production, efficient food management, and market-linkage initiatives. To improve the socio-economic status of host communities, enhancing natural resource management and building disaster risk management capacity are critical, particularly through measures addressing climate change impacts, early warning systems, and proactive early actions.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Protection mainstreaming is a fundamental approach in Caritas Bangladesh (CB) for the humanitarian efforts. It ensures that all project activities prioritize the safety, dignity, and rights of project participants, especially the most vulnerable groups. . This includes creating safe and inclusive environments, promoting gender sensitivity, safeguarding children, and ensuring accountability to affected populations.By integrating protection principles into every aspect of the project, CB aims to create an environment where everyone—particularly women, children, the elderly, and persons with disabilities—can engage safely and with dignity. To achieve this, CB implements regular training, capacity-building initiatives, and monitoring mechanisms to identify and address protection risks proactively. This commitment ensures that the project remains responsive to the diverse needs and rights of all community members.

2.3 - Age & Disability

Caritas Bangladesh will strongly address the disabilities issues for the project. In a necessary case, CB will receive technical support from CBM, CDD, HI (Humanity and Inclusion) or and other relevant organization.. CB will actively screen and identify the people with disabilities, using the Washington Group Questions (WGQ). This comprehensive approach ensures that we are attuned to the diverse needs within our target population. A key aspect of our commitment is to target around 12% of persons with disabilities in the Camp and 7% in HC within the total target for the project. In order to ensure a dignified working environment, CB will deploy both women and men staff and manage separate and accessible toilets for women, men, and people with disabilities. In applicable cases, Caritas Bangladesh (CB) will set-up a child corner for the children of project participants who have no alternative option for care. Also, CB will allocate fund for the relevant activities and will ensure linkage with relevant organization and network as much as possible. This intentional focus aims to promote equitable access and participation, fostering a more inclusive project environment. Recognizing the significance of inclusive programming, we are dedicated to providing sector-specific training on inclusive practices for our staff and volunteers. This training will enhance their understanding of the unique challenges faced by individuals with disabilities and empower them to contribute effectively to our inclusive initiatives.Through these commitments, our project not only aims to meet the standards of inclusivity but also strives to go beyond, actively contributing to the empowerment and well-being of individuals of all ages and abilities within our target communities.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project is directly aligned with the needs and perspectives of the communities to serve, promoting transparency and responsiveness. CB will follow the community led approach to identifying the proper project participants as a selected dignified criterion. CB is dedicated to fostering community participation in the evaluation process. To achieve this, we will develop a comprehensive plan that actively involves community members in shaping evaluation methodologies. Moreover, the results of these evaluations will be shared with the communities in an accessible format, ensuring transparency and accountability in our project outcomes.CB will organize minimum bi-yearly consultation meetings to evaluate the project's progress. These sessions will serve as valuable opportunities for community feedback and engagement. The insights gathered during these consultations will be integral in shaping the lessons learned workshop at the working level, creating a continuous feedback loop that informs and enhances our project implementation.For AAP Effective complain and feedback mechanism is in place through setting Opinion & Complaint box in all offices, camp facilities, distribution points and majhi blocks, dedicated Hotline and e-mail account. CB conducts PDM and beneficiaries feedback survey regularly. These feedbacks are processed, and regularly analysed by the Safeguarding committee and MEAL team and shared with higher authority to action & program team to re-design the programming.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

CB also follows a comprehensive PSEA & Safeguarding Policy to this humanitarian context. In the unwavering commitment to the principles of Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), our project places paramount importance on the safety and dignity of all individuals involved. To achieve this, CB pledge to conduct mandatory PSEA training for 100% of our staff, including volunteers, ensuring they are equipped with the necessary knowledge to prevent and respond to instances of exploitation and abuse. Furthermore, our project is dedicated to fostering awareness among beneficiaries through the prominent display of PSEA materials, such as posters, fliers, and videos, incorporating key messages and information about accessible Complaint Feedback Mechanisms. These mechanisms, designed to be confidential, culturally appropriate, and accessible, will provide a secure channel for reporting concerns and feedback, thereby creating an environment where the well-being of all individuals is prioritized and safeguarded. As same previous, CB will allocate sufficient budget in 2025 for PSEA activities, including capacity building, monitoring, and the development or and contextualization of relevant materials as well.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The project will ensure special attention to upholding the environmental sustainability. The project participants will get three types of awareness sessions on Household preparedness to reduce disaster risk and climate change issue, Early Warning Systems & Community’s responsibilities and how to protect productive livelihood assets during emergencies and adapt to climate change, and how long-term impacts of climate affect food security. The community people will also be motivated to protect the soil erosion through roadside plantation, keep their surrounding clean with waste management and reduce use of single use plastic for their daily chores. They will also be made knowledgeable and motivated about not to cut trees indiscriminately and to plant trees at their homesteads. CB also closely coordinates with Energy and Environment Technical Working Group (EETWG) under ISCG in order to address all the issues related to environment and receive guidance on current and new approaches. CB will give priority to preventing environmental pollution in the camp and HC.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project incorporates a strong Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) measures tailored for the Rohingya camps and the host community where natural hazards such as cyclones, fire, heavy rainfall, landslide and flash floods pose significant risks. CB has established an Emergency Team trained for rapid disaster response, supported by an Emergency Stockpile of essential materials. A detailed contingency plan outlines standard operating procedures for emergencies, ensuring coordinated response efforts with clear roles and communication protocols along with FSS and Area Focal Agency. Training is provided to staff, volunteers, and local stakeholders in disaster preparedness and response, empowering them to effectively manage emergency initiatives within their communities. The key messages of emergency preparedness will be shared with respective FDMNs and host community project participants through trained volunteers and staff as well. In the host community, CB organizes emergency preparedness simulations to raise community awareness and demarcate Emergency Team responsibilities, enabling a rapid and effective response during disasters.Through these EPR measures, CB aims to strengthen the resilience of Rohingya communities in the camps and HC, ensuring they can withstand and recover from natural disasters while meeting their basic needs.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project will actively engage of around 25% youth aged 15-24, offering them opportunities for agriculture and homestead gardening training within FDMN camps. Youth participants may also be involved as volunteers and referred to relevant sectors. This approach aims to empower young individuals, fostering livelihood skills, long-term development, and income generation.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project actively promotes self-reliance and resilience for refugees. Through ongoing capacity development, follow-up, and referral activities, beneficiaries receive essential training and support. This may include providing equipment or in-kind resources to enable self-employment opportunities, encouraging self-reliance within their communities. This approach empowers refugees to build long-term resilience and reduces their dependency on external assistance.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

The project aligns closely with the District Development and Growth Priorities (DDGP) by addressing key focus areas, including enhancing food security, promoting social cohesion, and strengthening disaster preparedness. Through income generation initiatives such as agriculture and livestock management support for host communities, the project directly contributes to the DDGP's goals of improving livelihoods and fostering economic sustainability. A significant emphasis is placed on inclusivity, offering targeted support to vulnerable groups, such as women-headed households and peopele with disabilities. This approach aligns with the DDGP’s commitment to social protection and gender equality. Also, DRR measures, including capacity-building for local DMCs and the updated of local infrastructure through CFW, directly support the DDGP’s priorities of improving disaster resilience and promoting sustainable development.
