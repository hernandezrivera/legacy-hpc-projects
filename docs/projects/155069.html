Objectives

SO1. Improve equitable access to and utilization of quality lifesaving and comprehensive primary and secondary health services for crisis-affected populations with special focus on sexual reproductive, maternal, neonatal, child and adolescent health; mental health and psychosocial support; and non-communicable diseases. SO2. Ensure the prevention and timely response to outbreaks of diseases with epidemic potential and prepare for other health emergencies including monsoon and cyclone.SO3. Improve health seeking behavior and utilisation of essential service package among refugees and host populations through community engagement, with special attention to gender considerations and vulnerable groups.

Needs

The need to provide comprehensive health care services to the 1.3 million displaced Rohingya and vulnerable host population in Teknaf and Ukhiya in Cox's Bazar remains a challenge despite humanitarian health agencies scaling up their emergency responses to meet the increasing demand. Women of reproductive age and unvaccinated children make up the majority of the displaced population who live in overcrowded and difficult conditions in the camps, with an ever-present risk of landslides, flooding and cyclones causing further disaster. Furthermore, many refugees are experiencing extreme anxiety and psychosocial stress. In 2018, IOM rapidly scaled up its health programing to meet the urgent needs of the displaced population with a complementary approach of establishing health facilities in temporary structures. IOM ensured the deployment of trained staff, supply of life saving medicines, and responded to disease outbreaks (Diphtheria and measles) through participation in immunization campaigns and community outreach. There is still immense need for service and coordination improvement. Routine vaccination coverage for children is low at 60%, only 30% of deliveries are facility-based, and availability/accessability of activities for adolescent health and Mental Health Psychosocial Services (MHPSS) is less than 50%. Despite many operational health facilities, especially health posts, there are still few (< 30%) facilities providing inpatient care and comprehensive Primary Health Care (PHC). Furthermore, according to IOM's internal assessment, there is a huge need to adequately address non-communicable diseases, Mental Health and Psychosocial Supports needs, and other specialized cares - such as surgery, dental, eye and psychiatric care services - at the health complex levels/hospitals. GBV and protection services also need to be fully mainstreamed and integrated into facility-based practices and referral pathways.

Activities or outputs

IOM will continue to be an active partner of the Health Sector both at strategic and implementation levels. With more than 250 health professionals, including 40 medical officers, IOM directly operates 12 health facilities in the camps in Ukhiya and Teknaf. Additionally, IOM complements human resources, medical supplies and services at 9 Government health facilities. Among a total of 23 IOM-supported facilities, 2 of the facilities are managed by an implementing national partner NGO – Research, Training and Management International (RTMI) who provide comprehensive curative and preventive services. IOM provides preventive and promotive health care services through national implementing partners through a network of 304 Community Health Workers. IOM currently operates a fleet of 12 ambulances to meet the referral needs of the community - transferring complicated emergency cases to secondary/tertiary level health facilities. On average, 54,500 patients a month receive treatment through an IOM supported/operated facility. In 2019, IOM will improve the quality of available services, with a particular focus on infection prevention and control. Through increasing the number of health post conversions into Primary Health Care Centres (PHCC), previous PHCCs have the opportunity of upgrading to secondary health services - from their temporary structures to pre-fab stable structures. These alterations will increase accessibility to health services, provide good patient flow between services and ensure dignified health treatment for all beneficiaries. IOM will increase the number of camp-based health facilities providing 24/7 Comprehensive Sexual Reproductive Health (CSRH) activities, with specific focus on adolescent health, and Basic Emergency Obstetric Care (BEmOC) - therefore increasing the capacity for facility-based deliveries. IOM will also investigate integrating the routine Expanded Programme for Immunization (EPI) in all health facilities including health posts. This will be decentralized in coordination with Ministry of Health (MoH). Furthermore, IOM will continue supporting nine strategically identified MoH Health facilities with staffing, medicines, commodity supplies and infrastructure support including renovation. All curative services will be further complemented by preventive community-based services through IOM’s network of existing, trained Community Health Workers (CHWs). IOM will continue to partner with two national NGOs who provide preventive programming through the Community Health Worker network. IOM will support the outbreak and emergency response efforts of the Health Sector through deploying Mobile Medical Teams (MMTs) and surge teams during emergencies. IOM will continue to support Medair in operating and providing primary health care services in two health facilities in camp 8E and 8W. IOM anticipates that Non-Communicable Diseases (NCD) will be a forthcoming challenge for the Rohingya population, based on facility data. As such, IOM will play an active role in NCD surveillance and introduce innovative interventions to face this future challenge. In collaboration with Doctors worldwide, IOM will build the cacity of medical doctors working for the Rohingya Humanitarian Response through three training sessions. In coordination with the MHPSS technical WG, IOM will scale up its MHPSS programmes comprehensively both at the community and facility level targeting the most vulnerable groups. All health facilities managed by IOM will have a decentralised MHPSS team and will link with community-based activities. Under joint programming with the MoH in 2019, IOM will be supporting an additional seven Community Clinics and seven Union level centres (Health Sub-Centres and Family Welfare Centres) as part of health system strengthening. All IOM programmes will give special attention to the most vulnerable individuals including, youth, girls, boys, women and elderly people with key and specialized support.
