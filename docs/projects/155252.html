Objectives

Objective 1. Ensure and sustain timely provision of life-saving food assistance for Rohingya Refugees. Objective 2. Promote self-reliance opportunities for Rohingya and enhance the livelihoods and resilience of host communities.

Needs

Trends of food security indicators, as shown by June data , suggest that overall food security amongst the Rohingya refugee population has improved, which is likely due to the regular and effective general food assistance program through in-kind and voucher programs. Under existing conditions, the refugee population remains 100% reliant on food assistance to sustain the minimum daily required kilocalories per individual and for diet diversification. This dependency is due to several factors such as: lack of opportunity for own food production, limited financial and physical access to food, movement restrictions, protection issues and limited access to cooking fuel and sources of energy. Thus, sustaining the food assistance of the population is paramount as breakage in the food supply pipeline could deepen the existing emergency and threatens the survival of the vulnerable Rohingya population. To address dietary diversity, response actors are moving forward with complementary activities to the general blanket food assistance through supplementary/fresh food vouchers and hot meals provision. However, the roll-out has been somewhat challenged by competing priorities of the immediate food needs and funding commitments. Relocation and displacement, largely due to floods and landslides, however; also, increasingly linked to inter-communal conflict and continued regular flow of new arrivals, have contributed to an increased caseload of rapid food assistance required. The fragmentation of families into smaller family units is also contributing to an increased household caseload. The food and nutrition security of the poorest amongst the host community is a growing concern. Many have lost access to previously farmed lands (often government forest land) and have simultaneously lost work opportunities to cheaper refugee labour market. Forest products on which they previously depended are no longer available. It has also become increasingly clear that the response actors will need to increase their focus on host community food security, particularly in hard-hit communities. In addition, natural disasters and deforestation may prolong and worsen food insecurity situation for both host communities and Rohingya and exacerbate the likelihood of natural disaster and its impact on both communities.

Activities or outputs

1) General Food Assistance (In-kind, eVoucher and/or cash for food) for all Rohingya refugees in 29 out of 31 new camps, and 2 registered refugee camps. Please note ICRC provides food assistance in 2 camps (Camp 21 and 22). Total projected caseload: 920,000 refugeees. 2) Rapid Response for displaced families, new arrivals and protection referrals. This is in addition to the Blanket food assistance in previous activity. Total projected caseload: 184,000 refugees throughout the year.Contingency plan for 184,000 beneficiaries cumulatively, which include new arrivals, displaced population due to natural disasters and intra-community conflict, and protection referrals throughout 2019. Rapid response will include a week of HEB ration (1 carton/100 packets per HH) and one-off GFD ration (maximum two food baskets per household). 3) School Feeding Programme (SFP) for children in learning shelters in new camps and schools in registered refugee camps. Total projected caseload is average of 267,000 refugee children per month.SFP is currently supporting 279,000 children across 1,165 learning centers in the refugee camps and 557 schools in host population across Ukhiya, Teknaf, Pekua, Kutubdia and Maheskhali Upazillas of Cox’s Bazar. Refugee children receive 50gm HEB per school day, meanwhile host children receive 75gm HEB per school day. The ration in refugee camp is reduced due to existing general and supplementary food assistances. Beneficiary caseload in the refugee camps will increase from 219,000 children in January to 315,000 in December to align with the scale up plan of education sector. In host population, the programme will support 146,902 children enrolled in the primary schools. 4) Enhancing Food Security and Nutrition for most vulernable and ultra poor families in host communities. Most of the direct beneficiaries are women participants. Total caseload will be 40,000 women participants in Ukhiya and Teknaf (read: households), i.e. 200,000 host population as direct beneficiaries. EFSN programme provides a package of socio-economic empowerment for the ultra-poor families, with women as primary participant. Every beneficiary receives a monthly subsistence allowance of BDT 1,050 for two years, and livelihood grant of 20,000 BDT. The grant value was increased from 15,000 BDT following recommendation of ‘Income Generating Activity Feasibility Study’ conducted by WFP in mid 2018. The current caseload in September includes 20,000 beneficiaries in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazillas. In 2019, 10,000 households will be added in Ukhiya and Teknaf under first phase of SAFE project; another 20,000 households from Maheskali, Kutubdia and Pekuwa Upazillas will be added in April. The total participants at the end of year is set at 50,000 women across five Upazillas of Cox’s Bazar. 5) Self Reliance for refugees: WFP and partners aim to help refugee women and adolescent girls enhance their self-reliance by strengthening their skills and knowledge, as well as providing access to resources and training. Participants will receive monthly Cash for Training of 1,000 BDT and livelihood grant of 15,000 BDT. Currently 2,100 participants are enrolled in the programme from September 2018 to January 2019 under a 6-month pilot. Another 5,000 participants will be enrolled in February, and scaled up to 15,000 participants from September through to the end of year.
