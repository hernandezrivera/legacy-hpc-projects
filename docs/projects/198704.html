Project Summary

This is an Education project to be implemented in Gujba and Potiskum Local Government areas (LGAs) in Yobe state and implemented by Life at Best Development initiative (LABDI). The goal of this project is to improve access to quality early childhood development, care, pre-primary and Primary education to crisis affected girls and boys 3 to 17 years old. The project will be implemented in line with the Nigeria 2022 humanitarian response plan, Nigeria education cluster priorities and sustainable development goal (SDG) 4 on quality education.
The proposed project will target an estimated 6,573 (6,398 children 3 to 17 years and 175 teachers/School based management committees (SBMCs)) individuals. The project will adopt a capacity building and stakeholder engagement approach to promote continuity of learning for the target children even when normal learning is disrupted due to for example flooding, conflicts and other unforeseen emergencies. The key activities will involve: Training of teachers and SBMCs from both public and private basic schools on remedial education, catch up programme, accelerated education and psychosocial support (PSS) to be ensure that they are in a position to meet the needs of the students. Purchase, pre-positioning and distribution of education material for home or alternative learning sites will also be done alongside continuous mapping of out of school children (OOSC) and identification of alternative safe learning spaces for children. Community leaders will also be trained on all aspects of home-based schooling and alternative learning solutions to enable consultative planning and delivery of a timely, appropriate and evidence-based education-in emergencies response. Networking solutions for teachers and volunteers that would support continued learning in the alternative learning sites will also be piloted and supported. All stakeholders will have to undergo a mandatory child protection, prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) training and parenting without violence provided by LABDI. The project will work closely with protection sector to provide PSS to teachers, children and parents. The WASH sector will also be actively engaged to provide hygiene and sanitation facilities for the alternative learning sites.

Throughout this project, LABDI will continue working on building local capacity in all interventions for sustainability and a form of promoting self-reliance in beneficiaries. Qualified individuals and especially women from women-led organizations, CBOs and associations from the two LGAs who qualify to work in the project will be prioritized. Further, LABDI will build local capacity through continuous training and refresher training of the officers, teachers, volunteers and all contracted and casual staff to ensure continuity of activities in case of any exit (planned or forced). All activities will be implemented in close coordination with the government and community to enable take over at exit. As this is a project to promote uptake of alternative coping strategies, local solutions for example encouraging formation of a network of teachers and the community leaders through WhatsApp or other online platforms and regular meetings will be established for lessons learning and sharing of best practices and to be in a position to respond rapidly in case of a crisis. The project is expected to run for nine months starting October 2022 up to June 2023.

1 - Needs

Yobe state is among the three priority states in dire need of humanitarian assistance in Northeast Nigeria the others being Adamawa and Borno. The security situation in the region remains fluid and has evolved into a protracted crisis since 2009 leading to massive displacement of people and denying them access to basic essential services. In 2022, this has resulted into the displacement of approximately 2.2 million people in Yobe, Borno and Adamawa states and further left 8.4 million people in need of humanitarian assistance. Further, 1.9 million people have been projected to need Education services in the country with 0.01M and 1.18M being women and children respectively. Pre-conflict, the area was characterized by low socioeconomic development, poor infrastructure, weak governance structures, and income inequality which fuel and further result from the conflict. This continues to pose access constraints by humanitarian actors depriving more than 1.1M people of much-needed lifesaving interventions in hard-to-reach areas and posing a risk to food security, WASH, Education and nutrition situation and causing multiplication of hotspot locations. Although the effects of education do not have immediate effects of morbidity and mortality like health, nutrition and WASH needs, education is a key contributor to breaking of the cyclical chain of poverty equipping children with survival and coping skills and improving their resilience in future. Further, education is a basic right for every child and should be promoted to provide them with a chance for a better future. In Yobe state, multisectoral humanitarian assistance is needed for approximately 1.6 M in 2022 who comprise of internally displaced people (IDPs), returnees and host communities. The vast majority comprises of host communities who are exposed to new threats and the possibility of displacements due to floods, upsurge of IDPs and returnees who are relocating to the LGAs with neither the necessary infrastructure nor amenities to address their basic social service needs nor guarantee everyone safety and security. The population influx which is also occasioned by floods and insecurity, without expansion of amenities is overstretching existing humanitarian capacities on the ground. According to the REACH “Yobe Floods: Displacement and Humanitarian Needs Brief”, July 2022. Flooding in Gujba has led to massive destruction and it’s projected to be one of LGAs to be affected adversely by the floods. The LGA has also been classified by OCHA to be among the 24 priority LGAs needing humanitarian assistance as a result of flooding, displacements in some parts due to organized armed groups (OAG) and IDPs who have moved into the area. Further, the report highlighted flooding in Potiskum. Floods cause displacements leading to high levels of OOSC. Surveillance data from Life at Best Development initiative (LABDI) indicates an upsurge of OOSC with no proper structures in place to provide alternative learning to children or get them back to schools.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

LABDI consistently adds a gender lens to its “Do No Harm” analysis, especially in conflict areas like Northeast Nigeria, because applying such a perspective can help teams to identify special opportunities to avoid worsening the conflict and exposing people to further hazards and violence, including sexual or gender-based violence(SGBV) and/or undermining local protection coping mechanisms. Furthermore, LABDI specifically targets vulnerable female-headed households for assistance, as these find themselves in a uniquely vulnerable situation, because of a shortage of alternative income generation opportunities – with the few available typically being occupied by men. This leaves women with fewer, safe potential livelihoods and, therefore, in greater need of food assistance. The Fuel Efficient Stoves distribution will also be targeted at women. In addition, LABDI trains all project staff, vendors and volunteers on Do No Harm principles and PSEA. All staff volunteers and vendors are required to read and sign the Code of Conduct, including LABDI PSEA policy. LABDI will also rely on protection master trainers to increase participant’s awareness of PSEA.
