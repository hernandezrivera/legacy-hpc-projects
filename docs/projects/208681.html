Project Summary

The project, "Empower360: Catalyzing Sustainable Livelihoods and Entrepreneurship for Northeast Nigeria's Displaced Communities, Refugees and Returnees through Innovative Vocational Training and Technical Education," addresses the challenges faced by those affected by insurgencies in the region. Developed by the Augustinian Centre for Advocacy Justice and Peace (ACAJP), the initiative aims to facilitate sustainable reintegration by adopting a "train and retain" approach. The project encompasses activities such as stakeholder meetings, six months of vocational training of marketable skills, such as tailoring, shoe and handbag making, catering, and ICT for beneficiaries, a workshop on entrepreneurship and business management, and a unique six-month employment engagement within ACAJP's vocational center.
A distinctive feature enhancing sustainability is the employment strategy, whereby beneficiaries trained in various skills will transition into staff positions within the institution. This not only provides practical work experience but also ensures the active application of acquired skills. Moreover, the vocational activities is envisioned to function as a commercial hub, generating returns from its activities. Importantly, a percentage of these returns will be reinvested back into the institute, creating a self-sustaining financial cycle. This holistic approach not only promotes the economic viability of the vocational center but also contributes to the long-term success and autonomy of the project, aligning with the overarching goal of fostering sustainable livelihoods in the Northeast.

1 - Needs

The project aims to address diverse needs among the internally displaced people, refugees, and returnees in Northeast Nigeria who have been adversely affected by insurgencies. The assessed needs that the project endeavors to tackle include:Skills and Employability: The beneficiaries are faced with a lack of marketable skills, hindering their ability to secure employment. The project seeks to address this need by providing vocational training in key areas such as tailoring, shoe and handbag making, catering, and ICT, enhancing their employability and equipping them with valuable skills.Entrepreneurial Knowledge: Many individuals in the target group may lack the necessary knowledge and skills to start and manage their own businesses. The workshop on entrepreneurship and business management is designed to fill this gap, empowering beneficiaries with the know-how to establish and sustain their enterprises.Economic Empowerment: Displaced communities often face economic challenges due to the disruption of livelihoods. The employment engagement phase of the project, where beneficiaries transition into staff positions within the vocational institute, directly addresses the need for economic empowerment, providing stable income opportunities.Reintegration Support: Internally displaced people, refugees, and returnees often experience challenges in reintegrating into their communities or reintegration to a host community. The holistic approach of the project, encompassing training, employment, and entrepreneurial support, aims to facilitate a comprehensive reintegration process, fostering a sense of belonging and self-sufficiency.Long-Term Sustainability: The project recognizes the need for sustained support beyond the initial training period. The "train and retain" methodology, coupled with the commercial activities of the vocational institute, directly addresses the need for long-term sustainability, ensuring that the impact endures well beyond the project's duration.By addressing these assessed needs, the project strives to create a holistic and sustainable framework that not only imparts immediate benefits but also establishes a foundation for the long-term well-being and empowerment of the displaced communities in Northeast Nigeria.Importantly, The project strategically leverages the existing Augustinian Vocational Center and its equipment to enhance the effectiveness of vocational training. By utilizing the center's infrastructure and resources, the project optimizes efficiency and reduces costs. Additionally, it enables seamless integration with the ongoing activities of the vocational center, fostering a collaborative and mutually beneficial partnership. The existing equipment provides a valuable resource for hands-on training, enhancing the learning experience and skill acquisition for the beneficiaries. This collaborative approach ensures that the project builds upon the foundation of the Augustinian Vocational Center, maximizing its impact and sustainability.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The project employs a comprehensive set of modalities to ensure protection mainstreaming throughout its implementation. Firstly, a commitment to inclusivity is evident through targeted outreach and tailored communication strategies, actively engaging members of marginalized communities, minority groups, people with disabilities, women, girls, and youth in all phases of the project. This ensures that the voices, needs, and perspectives of diverse demographic groups are recognized and considered. The project places a strong emphasis on gender and age considerations, actively involving women, girls, and youth in consultations and decision-making processes. This commitment recognizes the unique challenges and opportunities that different demographic groups may face and aims to tailor interventions accordingly. To foster peaceful coexistence, the project strategically focuses on skills development, employment opportunities, and economic empowerment. By addressing these key areas, the project aims to positively contribute to community cohesion, reducing the likelihood of conflicts over resources and promoting a harmonious environment. In response to the critical issue of sexual exploitation and abuse, the project has established robust measures, including a comprehensive communication strategy for Protection Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). Training sessions for staff and beneficiaries are conducted to raise awareness and sensitivity, and a confidential complaints mechanism is in place to promptly address any concerns. Protection mainstreaming is embedded throughout the project, incorporating safeguards, risk assessments, and community feedback mechanisms. This ensures that the project actively mitigates potential harm, upholds the rights and dignity of all participants, and creates a safe, inclusive, and empowering environment. By adopting these modalities, the project aims to not only address immediate needs but also contribute to the long-term well-being and resilience of the communities it serves.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project is committed to ensuring that feedback is systematically collected from the most vulnerable community members through a multifaceted approach. This involves creating inclusive consultation spaces that consider the physical and communication needs of individuals, ensuring accessibility through local languages, visual aids, and community leaders. Collaborative efforts with community-based organizations and networks that have established relationships with vulnerable groups will further integrate feedback mechanisms into existing community structures, leveraging established trust. To promote open communication, the project will implement anonymous feedback mechanisms, providing avenues such as suggestion boxes and designated community focal points. Regular check-ins and follow-up sessions will be scheduled, fostering ongoing dialogue and trust-building with vulnerable community members. Cultural sensitivity training for project staff ensures respect for cultural nuances during feedback collection, and flexibility in engagement methods accommodates diverse preferences. This comprehensive strategy aims to create an inclusive and responsive feedback process, enhancing the project's accountability and ensuring interventions align with the specific needs of the community's most vulnerable members.
