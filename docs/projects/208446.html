Project Summary

Strengthening access to safe, quality, equitable and inclusive basic education for 500,000 conflict-affected girls and boys aged 3 to 17 years and 5,000 youths in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe States of Northeast Nigeria.
Specific Objectives:
1. Strengthen access to basic education for 500,000 children (ensuring 51% girls, and 14,000 children from Tsangaya and Islamiyah schools) through the provision of essential learning materials, dignity kits, support to enrolment drive campaigns (EDC) and Girls for Girls (G4G) programme.
2. Provide access to remote learning opportunities to 10,000 conflict-affected children (~50% girls).
3. Provide continuous professional development opportunities to 5,300 teachers.
4. Construction of TLS/Classrooms and gender-sensitive WASH facilities to increase access to safe learning spaces for at least 10,000 conflict-affected children.
5. Increase access to learning, training and skills development for 5,000 youth (50%females; aged 15 - 24)
6. Enhance the response and protection capacity of education service providers in school governance in emergencies through establishing or re-activating and training 2000 School-based Management Committees (SBMC) members in targeted LGAs on effective school management.
7. Strengthening the Education Management Information System (EMIS)/Annual School Census (ASC) in providing timely & reliable data.
8. Support the scale-up of EduTrac which is used as a survey tool to collect data fromschools using SMS messages and as a governance monitoring tool and improveaccountability.

1 - Needs

Since 2009, Borno, Yobe and Adamawa (BAY) states in North-East Nigeria have been impacted by civil armed conflict. The long crises had left approximately 8.4 million people in need of humanitarian assistance on an estimated total population of 12.4 million , including 8 million children. Of these, 2.2 million are internally displaced; 1.5 million are returnees who lack essential services and livelihoods; and 3.9 million are members of communities who are also in need of assistance . The fragility of the context makes the crisis one of the most acute in the world, and all humanitarian actors predict that the conflict will continue, generate new displacement, and steadily constrict access. The impact of the crisis on the education system has been severe, leaving generations of children without learning opportunities. 1.9 million (1.3M Borno; 430,000 Adamawa; 167,000 Yobe) conflict-affected boys, girls, and education actors are without access to basic quality education . According to the displacement tracking matrix 2021, 56% per cent of displaced children across the BAY states do not attend school. More broadly, one in three children ages 6 to 11 in the north-east are out of school , compared to one in four nationally and in the north-central, north-west, and south-south zones and one in five in the south-east and south-west. In addition to large numbers of out-of-school children, the education system struggles with low completion and transition rates. In the early grades, primary one to three, only 40 per cent of children are participating in education. This number substantially reduces to 4 to 17 per cent at the secondary level, the average secondary completion rate for Nigeria is 44 per cent but is much lower at 18 to 25 per cent in the BAY states . Moreover, the crisis disproportionately impacts the most vulnerable groups, namely girls and children with disabilities, leaving them even further excluded from quality education. For example, the gender parity index for public primary enrolment in these states ranges from 0.83 in Yobe to 0.91 in Adamawa and 0.93 in Borno. By gender, the out-of-school rate for girls in Adamawa is 29 per cent compared to 19 per cent for boys. In Borno and Yobe, however, the rate for boys is higher: 45 per cent for boys compared to 16 per cent for girls in Borno and 53 per cent for boys compared to 27 per cent for girls in Yobe .The lack of foundational literacy and numeracy (FLN) among school-aged children is both a consequence of low participation and transition rates and a factor that further drives drop out in the north-east region. Over 70 per cent of all children who have completed primary school in the north-east region cannot read a simple sentence (compared to 56 per cent nationally), and 29 per cent cannot add numbers (Nigeria Education Data Survey 2015). These challenges in education access, participation and learning outcomes are driven by a complex interplay of both demand- and supply-side factors. Poverty, for example, remains the greatest barrier to accessing education, with parents being unable to afford education fees and levies. Social and religious norms impede education access, especially for girls and particularly at the secondary level, placing them at risk of child marriage and dropout. Formal education continues to be perceived as a “Western” system in the BAY States and is thus perceived negatively, and nonsecular, Islamic and Qur’anic education is often preferred, especially in rural communities. Although the government, supported by development partners, has introduced Integrated Qur’anic Education (IQE), the number of IQE schools remains insufficient to address current needs, especially in hard-to-reach communities.

2 - Activities

In 2024, UNICEF will focus on strengthening access to safe, quality, equitable and inclusive basic education for 500,000 conflict-affected girls and boys aged 3 to 17 years (51% girls) and 5,000 youths in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe States of Northeast Nigeria. UNICEF will embark on the following specific activities: ACCESS: 1. Construct/rehabilitate 5 temporary learning spaces and classrooms 2. Construct and rehabilitate 5 gender-sensitive WASH facilities in schools and learning spaces with connecting water sources.3. Provide 15,000 conflict-affected children with remote learning. 4 Provide basic learning material (stationery and school bags) to 500,000 school-age children. 5. Provide dignity kits to 30,000 girls. 6. Provide access to learning, training and skills development for 10,000 youth (aged 15 - 24).QUALITY: 1. Support 5,000 teachers with continuous professional development and in acquiring the qualification for teaching primary grades. 2. Train 5,000 teachers on psychosocial support (PSS). 3. Provide life skills to 15,000 conflict-affected children (51% girls).Response Capacity of Education Service Providers:1. Train 2,900 School-Based Management Committee (SBMC) members on SBMC effectiveness, protection of schools and provision of education services to out-of-schoolchildren, education-in-crisis, and education for vulnerable children including girls and disabled with a focus on hard-to-reach areas. 2. Support the scaling up of EduTrac and strengthening of the Education Information Management System (EMIS) to provide timely and reliable data.

The population at risk would be identifi edconsidering the specifi c vulnerabilities that underlie these risks, including thoseexperienced by men, women, girls and boys, and groups such as internally displacedpersons, older persons, persons with disabilities, and persons belonging to sexual andother minorities. Primary consideration will be given to UNICEF’s accountability toaffected populations, to identify, understand and support their protection measures.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The proposed action is defi ned by daily interaction and collaboration with affectedcommunities through our third-party facilitators in targeted LGAs and IDP camps,including support to community-based structures such as the SBMC’s and theirparticipation in public awareness-raising, referral processes and collaboration inidentifying and implementing initiatives which contribute to improving the protectiveenvironments. Furthermore, UNICEF ensures independent consultations with specifi cgroups, including women, girls and children, to ensure their voices are heard and ideasare incorporated into all activities. As part of the emergency response activities,benefi ciaries will be consulted through regular monitoring visits and post-distributionmonitoring exercises, during which individuals will be consulted on matters includingthe quality and relevance of assistance received. UNICEF short text messages (SMS)based U-Report tool will be used to allow benefi ciaries to provide feedback orcomplaints. The U-Report 24x7 Hub receives complaints, reports and feedback fromcommunities, analyses the data and shares with relevant sections in UNICEF and itspartners for action. As reports and complaints regarding other services handled bydifferent agencies come in, they are shared with the Sector to take action. Each case isfollowed up to provide feedback to the community members who laid complaints.
