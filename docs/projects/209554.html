Project Summary

ROHI is proposing a 12-month project focused on providing access to quality education in emergency and protection response for conflict-affected children including women and girls in DIKWA, BAMA and GWOZA LGAs in Borno State. The project is aimed at building community resilience, strengthening services and using community-based approaches to strengthen Education, Child Protection and GBV mechanisms.
The project result area will support localisation and women-led organisations lead initiatives towards the protection of women and girls through a collaborative effort. We will conduct capacity development assessments, build the capacity of local organizations, and foster partnerships. Together with women-led groups, we will design and plan initiatives, and empower communities to actively participate.
To support protection from GBV and child protection during emergencies, we will implement targeted initiatives like women and girls empowerment in IGA, provide clinical services for GBV survivors, life skills training for adolescent sessions girls and women and positive parenting sessions. The project will also focus on supporting persons with disabilities by providing assistive devices to promote their accessibility and inclusivity. 
We will ensure accessible feedback and accountability systems through establishing systems tailored to the needs of the community. Advocacy efforts especially for the domestication of VAPP law/Child Rights Act and awareness campaigns will amplify the impact, while monitoring and evaluation will ensure effectiveness. We aim for sustainable, community-driven protection for women and girls, fostering a sense of ownership and empowerment.

1 - Needs

The NE region of Nigeria confronts an intricate crisis marked by armed conflict, displacement, and severe humanitarian challenges, significantly impacting education and exacerbating the prevalence of Gender-Based Violence (GBV). The crisis has been characterized by massive and widespread abuse against civilians including killings, rape and other sexual violence. This devastating reality translates to a substantial deprivation of education for nearly 2 million children, illustrating the magnitude of the crisis (EIEWG, 2023). The absence of sufficient educators exacerbates the challenges faced by marginalized and displaced children, women and girls diminishing the prospects of receiving a comprehensive and quality education. Conflict exacerbates the impact of GBV where traditional gender roles have shifted, often putting women and girls at greater risk of GBV. Communities continue to practice harmful traditional norms and practices that continue to put women and girls in a disadvantaged position. Early and forced marriage has resulted in girls dropping out of school, reducing their chances to compete for the already limited socio-economic opportunities. GBV survivors, especially those abducted by Boko Haram, and their children born out of the sexual violence they experienced continue to face social isolation and stigma adding to the psychosocial distress they already encounter.
