Project Summary

UNICEF in coordination with WHO will support MOH to continue COVID-19 vaccination through the COVAX facility and through other bilateral donations. UNICEF is contributing to most of the ten pillars of COVID response:
Pillar 1: Coordination, planning, financing, and monitoring
Pillar 2: Risk communication, community engagement, and infodemic management
Pillar 3: Surveillance, epidemiological investigation, contact tracing, and adjustment of public health and social measures
Pillar 4: Points of entry, international travel and transport, mass gatherings and population movement
Pillar 5: Laboratories and diagnostics
Pillar 6: Infection prevention and control (IPC), and protection of the health workforce
Pillar 7: Case management, clinical operations, and therapeutics
Pillar 8: Operational support and logistics, and supply chains
Pillar 9: Strengthening essential health services and systems
Pillar 10: Vaccination
UNICEF will focus on the procurement, importation, shipment and transport of vaccine doses to arrive in Syria, improving cold chain system, capacity building for the health workers, and risk communication, community engagement & generating demand. It also includes monitoring and evaluation
This project aims to ensure availability of sufficient cold chain equipment and proper infrastructure and storage to store the COVAX vaccines allocated to Syria as agreed with GAVI with COVAX facility. This equipment will be part of the countrywide cold chain system used for routine immunization programme as well as supporting the COVAX vaccines at different levels. This will contribute to the resilience and strengthening of the health system through building strong infrastructure for routine vaccination prog, COVAX vaccines, and any future vaccines needed to respond to public health emergencies.
The project will strengthen the capacity of health staff and increase the outreach skills. It will contribute to the improvement of the quality of vaccination services through real-time data collection for monitoring of cold chain and communication interventions.
The project will ensure effectiveness of all received COVAX doses until reaching final beneficiaries as MOH is now targeting all citizens 18 years of age and above. Women and men with disabilities will be reached through outreach mobile vaccination teams through which they will have access to the registration & vaccination services. Women and men with disabilities are among the priority groups for COVAX vaccination.
The project will focus on protection of health workers while performing their duties to vaccinate the targeted population through providing them with personal protective equipment and preventing the negative effects of medical waste on human health and the environment by providing incinerators.
The project also includes a series of advocacy events (workshops, webinars, orientations) with professional associations, influencers and NGOs, government officials through partnership agreements with NGOs, professional associations and education institutions for capacity building, community engagement and crisis communication. This will be done in coordination with Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE) task forces in all hubs, including the North Eastern Syria (NES) RCCE task force. Local NGOs are playing a vital role in RCCE and will be supporting all community-based awareness activities & demand generation interventions. Also included is a partnership with media agencies to conduct evidence driven media campaigns, evidence generation & behavioral insights to support the implementation of national roll-out action plan. The project includes operational activities to transport COVAX vaccine in NWS, monitoring and evaluation for all interventions including for the outreach activities.
UNICEF will work in all subdistricts, including NES & NWS. This includes 270 SD with 181 of them in severity 3 & 4, and 89 in severity 2, but UNICEF will cover all with the vaccination.

4 - Needs

Since the 1st reported case of COVID-19 on 22 Mar 2020 till 31 Dec 2021, a total of 164,000 confirmed cases reported, with 5,800 deaths. SARS-CoV-2 infection is classified as under community transmission, including for healthcare workers. COVID19 continues to disrupt the already-fragile health services & systems due to low levels of COVID19 vaccination, in addition to other factors like low adherence to preventive measures. By the end of 2021, 1.26 million people had been vaccinated against COVID19, or 7.8 per cent of the population in Syria. This is below the target of 20 per cent for 2021 as Syria did not receive its first allocation of vaccines through COVAX until May 2021, with only 2,875,840 doses received by the end of 2021. 2,017,000 doses were received for NWS; 6.7 percent of the population is vaccinated. No specific assessment of health facilities has been done in NES. From Dec 2021 through Q1 2022: 10,948,100 doses expected to arrive in Syria. UNICEF will support MOH to run 200 mobile vaccination team and 2,050 fixed vaccination centers (including 46 hospitals) to provide COVAX vaccination all-over the country. 1,350 health workers will be trained on topics related to C19. Challenges: the main challenge is the low-uptake of the vaccine, and it will be targeted through media campaigns and RCCE. another challenge is the safe disposal of the huge quantities of syringes, UNICEF will support MOH with incinerators to ensure this safe disposal and environmental safety.

5 - Implementation of activities

Syria’s NDVP defines all aspects related to COVAX. Vaccination is planned in three stages to vaccinate 70 per cent of the population over 18 years by end of 2022 (13.5 million people). UNICEF will support vaccinations through health facilities operated by MOH, Ministry of Higher Education and local NGOs, in addition to the mobile vaccination teams. with GAVI support, UNICEF will procure and deliver COVAX vaccines to central MOH vaccine stores, while WHO will support distribution to governorates except NWS. Around 2,050 health facilities & 200 mobile teams who will provide COVAX will be provided with needed cold chain, PPE and equipment for safe waste management, in addition to incinerators to destroy syringes at central level.The main challenge is low vaccination acceptance, to overcome this challenge; an advocacy, communications and community engagement strategy will be implemented in coordination with WHO and local partners. UNICEF will run several rounds of behavioral insights evidence generation with media agency as a time series to monitor and track the shifts in attitudes & behaviors towards the vaccine, this will help to conduct evidence driven campaigns for demand generation. Local NGOs will support in RCCE and demand generation. UNICEF will also monitor cold chain and supply delivery as well as communication and demand generation to promote the uptake of COVAX. UNICEF monitoring will focus on these two specific areas, to ensure quality and timeliness of support.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Exposure to COVID-19: Healthcare workers affected by COVID-19 and included among the 164,000 official cases confirmed to date. Mitigation: Providing health workers with PPE, risk communication & continuous adherence to physical distancing. Violence against health workers: Mitigation: Report on attacks against healthcare through SSA, MRM & Health Cluster Flash Updates. Utilize Syria Humanitarian Notification System.Gender Based Violence: Women & girls are at acute risk of sexual & GBV. Mitigation: affected population should have access to MISP at health facilities. Health staff should be trained on providing survivor-centered first-line support, clinical management of rape & safe referrals. Training health workers will be in synergy & integrated within UNFPA GBV program to ensure availability of full complementary training. Ensure confidentiality of all patients.Community Engagement & Participation: UNICEF promotes community engagement throughout the programme cycle. Mitigation: The presence of six Field Offices ensures closer presence in the field to engage communities, assess & monitor the needs. Field facilitators help to verify community participation and needs where staff have no access. UNICEF introduced Third Party Field monitors to strengthen AAP as well as its M&E to its program. The UNICEF social, behavior change team is involved in development of evidence driven strategic and systemic RCCE strategies including production & and dissemination of messages.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse:During the course of their work, aid providers come into contact with vulnerable people. Humanitarian workers have access to goods & services that put them in a position of power over the community. Healthcare providers should strive and commit to preventing sexual exploitation and abuse both on organizational and programmatic levels.Mitigating measures:- UNICEF PSEA policy in place forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and inclusive to accountability measures.- UNICEF has effective and comprehensive mechanisms to ensure awareness-raising on SEA amongst personnel, with continuous training on the topic.- UNICEF trained all IPs on PSEA.- UNICEF has effective recruitment & performance management of all staff.- UNICEF is participating in UN impartial assessment of needs and risks, and understand the vulnerabilities & capacities of different groups, and established reporting and feedback mechanisms through hotlines and SMS.- UNICEF Provides information to communities & people affected about the programmes it is implementing and what UNICEF intends to deliver as well how to report concerns & complaints.- UNICEF established community-based complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance and referral. - UNICEF ensures proper investigation and follow up of each reported case, including referrals to specialized GBV service providers where applicable and implementing measures to guard against retaliation or discrimination.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Access (crossline & cross-border): UNICEF is advocating with authorities to guarantee access to all communities & assigning responsibilities to the different hubs to reach local agreements with controlling parties to ensure access to COVAX vac. Low acceptance/high hesitancy: C4D & RCCE plans are in place with media campaign for demand generation & increase vaccine acceptance. IPs are playing critical role in demand generation through community engagement & disseminating correct messages on vaccine & stopping the spread of myths. Procurement delays for Cold Chain Equipment: CCE assessment conducted to identify additional needed equipment with deployment plan, UNICEF is following with supply division in Copenhagen to ensure timely delivery of needed CCEs.Humanitarian principles are upheld, & services delivered equally through ensuring equitable access & expansion of reach to the most vulnerable communities, regular monitoring & orientation of IPs on those principles, continuous follow-up & feedback from beneficiaries. Devaluation of local currency & price increase: This led to increased cost of all commodities including medical consumables, this will continue in 2022 due to the economic crisis & continuous sanction on Syria. Mitigating measures: UNICEF will adjust the costs of supplies & HR within its partnership agreements with IPs in coordination with other UN agencies to ensure continuity of service in high-severity areas, & consistency of rates among different agencies.
