Project Summary

The nutrition situation in South Sudan remains alarming, about 1.4 million children under five years are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition in 2021 based on same season historical data of food security and nutrition monitoring system, SMART nutrition surveys and admission trends for 2020. This is the highest caseload for acute malnutrition since the start of the crisis in December 2013. Similarly, the nutrition situation in Aweil East Aweil South and Panyijar remain above the WHO emergency threshed with GAM rate of >15 %, only Rumbek East is at alarming status, but have multiple aggravating factors that include, flooding, sever food insecurity, sub optimal practice of maternal infant and young child feeding, and high rate of under-five mortality and morbidity. According to June 2019 SMART survey report, Aweil East GAM rate was 15.5 % which is at “critical status” similarly the SMART survey report reveals that in Panyijar, the nutrition situation is critical with GAM rate of 15.4%.
The proposed intervention intend to continue and scale up the emergency nutrition responses through 68 static OTP/TSFP (17 in Aweil East, 10 in Aweil South,18 in Panyijar, and 23 in Rumbek East), three Stabilization Center (two in Panyijar, one in Aweil South (SC) , six (2 in Aweil East, 2 in Aweil South, 2 in Panyjar RRM mobile team). Two new OTP will be established in Aweil East. The IRC will implement integrated CMAM and MIYCN in all sites. Promotion of optimal nutrition and prevention malnutrition among young children (0-23 months through implementation of MIYCN to ensure vulnerable children have optimal growth and protected from irreversible malnutrition. Strengthening community participation and use of community structure through active engagements of the affected community through strengthening of MtMSG, increase engagement and role of CNV in prevention activities and regular community sensitization sessions. Scaling up of the existing nutrition response to ensure timely service availability at all time. Scale up family MUAC to ensure early detection of acute malnutrition that contributes to early detection. GBV and child protection will be provided for 80 the health and nutrition team, quarterly joint supportive supervision will be held with SMOH and CHD. Besides human rescore, IRC will have kitchen garden and cooking demonstration, there will be strong referral linkage with health FSL and protection services. An estimation of 25,162 SAM children, 42,600 under five and 34,332 PLW MAM will be reached through this action across all implementation area in OTP and TSFP, respectively. The team continue with the capacity building of CHD, and primary health care service providers including IRC nutrition staff. The program train health and nutrition staff on CMAM and MIYCN to ensure the nutrition service is provided timely. Rapid response team will be deployed to hard-to-reach area to ensure nutrition services are accessible and vulnerable groups have got timely treatment subsequently mortality and morbidity associated with malnutrition will be reduced.

1 - Needs

According to the current IPC AMN, 53 counties are in IPC AMN phase 3 (serious) and above. Out of these, 29 counties are in IPC AMN Phase 4 (critical) while 24 counties in IPC AMN Phase 3 (Serious), of the counties 17% in Greater Bahr el Ghazal State. In South Sudan, the key drivers of acute malnutrition are multifaceted including severe acute food insecurity, sub-optimal feeding practices, high prevalence of disease, inadequate sanitation conditions and hygiene practices, and limited access to health and nutrition services driven by sub national conflict, floods and COVID-19 related disruptions. The cumulative and sub-sequent effect of drivers in the country continue to impact on the nutrition situation and malnutrition remains a public health problems with an estimated 1.9 million people who need treatment for acute malnutrition in 2021. Out of the 1.9 million people, a total of 1,392,259 are under five children in need of nutritional assistance. All three IRC implanting counties fall under IPC3 with Aweil South county classified as IPC 5 or catastrophic phase. The nutrition situation in Aweil East, Aweil South and Panyijar remains above the WHO emergency threshold (GAM rate >15 %). According to the FSNMS round 26 report, the Proxy MUAC GAM rate of, Aweil South and Panyijar were 16.5% and 22 % respectively. According to June 2019 SMART Survey report, Aweil East GAM rate was 15.5 % which was at “critical status” similarly, the SMART survey report reveals that in Panyijar, the nutrition situation is critical with GAM rate of 15.4%. Rumbek East relatively better, however, majority of the county's area was flooded last year with anticipated worsen situation and the nutrition admission data also revealed high burden of acute malnutrition in the county. Sub-optimal practice of MIYCN remains prevalent in South Sudan especially complementary feeding practice is inexpertly low. Though EBF practices seem to have been seen improving, the MMDD, MAD and MMF remain extremely low with significant contribution in high prevalence of malnutrition (both acute and chronic). According to the FSNMS round 26 report the minimum dietary diversification remain low, in unity MMD is at 31% Lakes 23 % and NBEG is at 21 %. Similarly, minimum acceptable diet in all state remain extremely low, only 4.5 % of the expected children getting the minimum acceptable diets in a similar rate was reported in NBEG. 4.5%0 in lakes, also 5.5 % of children are getting minimum acceptable food are in Unity, 4.5 % lakes- and NBEG. According to the nutrition cluster estimation, a total of 13,020 SAM children 64,289 under five 32,712 PLW with MAM need nutritional assistance and IRC intend to reach though this proposed actions. IRC targets to reach a total of 25,162 SAM under five children and 42,600 under five and 34,332 PLW with MAM (Aweil East -93,43 SAM, 14,556 under five and 13,020 PLW with MAM Aweil South, 6,556 SAM, 9,840 under five and 7,800 PLW with MAM, Panyijar-3,180 SAM 6,984 under five and 2,568 PLW with MAM and Rumbek East-5,983 SAM 11,220 under five and 10, 944 PLW with MAM ), to reach through 68 OTP/TSFP and six RRM sites. An estimation of 2,516 SAM children with medical complication will be treated and reached with impatient nutrition service through three IRC supported SC through MIYCN program with an estimation of 58,331 primary caretakers of 0-24 months chill will be reached through Key MIYCN and WASH messages.

2 - Project activities

The proposed response will focus on addressing the immediate nutritional need and addressing the underlined cases through integrated response. The response mainly emphasized on four strategic approaches1.Increase accessibility and provision of quality nutrition treatment service : This will be achieved through: Established strong community mobilization screening and referral mechanism: Community outreach activities as the backbone of OTP/SC and TSFP interventions. Family MUAC will be given priority and at least 45 mothers per each OTP site will be trained. The MtMSG, CHW and CNV will be trained in MUAC screening.Establish and strengthen static OTP and sites: To ensure the accessibility, timely provision of appropriate nutrition therapeutic care and quality nutrition service 68 OTP/TSFP sites in all counties (AE and AS:10PAnyi=18 and RE;23) will be strengthened and also ensure increase in nutrition service coverage to new OTP in Aweil East will be established. The three SC will also be supported to ensure they provide 24/7 for SAM children with medical complication in need of inpatient services. All nutrition sites will have at least two CNWs. All SAM and MAM benefactrices will be admitted using MUAC and Oedema (for SAM) only in adherence with the guidance of nutrition intervention in the context of COVID-19. Establish rapid response mechanism team to ensure that the nutrition service is accessible and increase service coverage, there was a need to establish RRM-mobile team, hence IRC will establish six mobile team with two in each county of Aweil Eats, Aweil South and Panyijar. 2.Capacity building and nutrition surveillance: Capacity building of human resource is the key strategic response mechanism in emergency situation that has significant contribution on ensuring the quality of nutrition service and to ensure sustainability of the program. The primary focus of this project will be in building the capacity of the MOH, State MOH and CHD as well as IRC nutrition team staff. Training on CMAM, MIYCN and promotion of hygiene and sanitation, GBV and child protection will be provided for 80 the health and nutrition team, quarterly joint supportive supervision will be held with SMOH and CHD beside human rescore. 3.Promote and support of safe and appropriate feeding for MIYCN: The MIYCN will focus on the prevention and support of safe and appropriate which will be addressed through provision of BCC focusing on Optimal MIYCN with 340 MTMSG that will be supported. IEC material including counseling care for each group. Kitchen garden and cooking demonstration will be also held at each OTP sites to ensure MDD for children are optimized and practiced, hence a total of 3,060 mothers at each MTMSG will have kitchen gardening and cooking demonstration done. A total of 58,331 primary care takers of children of 0-24 months will be reached through Key IYCF and IRC will engage in NID with an estimate of 372,204 children reached through vitamin A supplementation (195,897) and deworming (176,307) across the targeted counties.4. Integrated nutrition response with others sectors to address the underlined cases of malnutrition: IRC will establish kitchen gardens at the nutrition centers for demonstrating to the nutrition site users on the production and utilization of nutritious locally available food for MIYC with collaboration of FSL. IRC will conduct cooking demonstration to ensure the primary caregivers of 6-24 months understand how to prepare nutritious food harvested from the kitchen gardens. referral linkage will be established from TSFP to GFD to ensure beneficiaries discharged from TSFP are linked to GFD. Similarly, the team will ensure all entry point at the PHHCC/U have used specially on screening of under-five and PLW for malnutrition at MCH, EPI at OPD with strong referral linkage as well as malaria test and ANC follow up.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Project interventions will incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity at all times. Prevention from harm, in particular will ensure that women and girls are not put at risk as a result of our interventions. In identifying vulnerable women and girl beneficiaries for different aspects of the program, IRC will proactively seek out those who are the most marginalized including female-headed households, GBV survivors, persons with disability to ensure they can access our the services provided through the multi-sector project. Women and girls will be empowered to participate in all interventions, and their views listened to and taken into account. The IRC will further ensure that during service provision at all levels, including service provision at the facilities and service provision at communities (house-to-house visits, community gatherings and referrals from communities to facilities) will not expose beneficiaries to further risks. Staff will be reminded and trained on the IRC code of conduct and required to sign an acknowledgment to uphold stands of prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA).

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The IRC will ensure gender equality is at the core of its nutrition program by collecting sex and age disaggregated data, ensuring nutrition facilities have gender segregated latrines, and services can be accessed at appropriate times for the traditional division of labor between men and women in the county and among others. IRC also prioritizes highly vulnerable groups including children and women to improve their access to services. Caregivers will be encouraged to bring boys and girls under five for screening and enrolment, should they meet the admissions criteria, unaccompanied children of both genders will be linked to protection services appropriately. Community and facility-level health education sessions will be provided to both genders to ensure each is aware of the health services available. Furthermore, PLW and their male partners will be engaged in counselling sessions on optimal feeding practices in IYCF to promote positive behavior and health relations at home.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

IRC has developed a mechanism of client responsiveness to ensure that the target population is involved in the whole project cycle. Community and beneficiary feedback mechanisms will be employed as part of accountability to the affected population and that will be mainly realized through various approaches including country leadership, emphasis on transparency, feedback and complaints mechanisms and participation in project implementation, monitoring and evaluation. Through the client responsiveness approach, the IRC will further provide and manage context-appropriate communication and feedback channels that are safe, accessible, transparent and confidential for clients to provide feedback and complaints on IRC services as well as seeking information and receiving timely, appropriate responses about IRC’s actions and decisions. IRC will conduct community sensitization and information-sharing sessions on project activities with girls, boys, women, and men, together with community and government leaders at the onset of implementation to ensure communities are well informed about the project, receive feedback from beneficiaries and communities throughout the project implementation phase. The SMOH staff will be engaged after the patient exit interviews to enhance learning and ensure the sustainable improvement of the health system. At the time of the project implementation, the IRC will ensure minimum essential lifesaving packages and functional community feedback mechanisms such as beneficiary complaint desk, client exit interviews and direct feedback from communities being served both at the facilities level and community level and quarterly feedback surveys.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Based on the benefactrices feedback, IRC will design the project and address the needed assistance. If the response is ongoing already based on the feedback appropriately and timely, action will be taken to ensure the beneficiaries satisfaction. The design will be held in the consultation of the community elders, leaders and the benefactrices will be informed on the action taken and feedback will be collected on the revised or corrected action to ensure the assistance is addressing the affected population.
