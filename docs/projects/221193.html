Project Summary


Bui and Donga Mantung Divisions in the North West of Cameroon have been severely impacted by the Anglophone Crisis since 2016, resulting in widespread food insecurity. The crisis has disrupted livelihoods, particularly in Bui, a key center of hostilities, and Donga Mantung, a relative safe haven for IDPs. The majority of inhabitants, including internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees, heavily rely on agriculture, and over 60% of women-led IDP households lack a sustainable source of income.
Current Situation:
Humanitarian needs have escalated, with IDPs and vulnerable populations experiencing higher levels of food insecurity compared to regions frequently exposed to food crises. The ongoing conflict has depleted household stocks, restricted access to fields, and lowered purchasing power due to increased prices. Rural communities, like Mbam, Nseh, Njare, and Birnju, face severe disruptions in agriculture, impacting essential crop production such as cassava, cocoyam, maize, and beans. The Russian-Ukrainian war has further exacerbated the situation by inflating prices of farm inputs, especially chemical fertilizers.
Small-scale farmers, constituting 90% of arable land cultivators, lack access to cost-effective financial services. Closure of financial institutions due to the war, has left them vulnerable to exploitation by unscrupulous money lenders, resulting in exorbitant interest rates and unfavorable selling conditions during harvest.
A March 2020 assessment by the UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) highlighted the urgent need for humanitarian action to prevent severe malnutrition and death, especially among women and children. The World Bank's predictions indicate a potential 9% drop in GDP by 2025, with long-term devastating effects by 2030, including a 60% decline in regional GDP and more than a 5% drop in national household welfare.
Proposed Response - ACOHOF Initiative:
ACOHOF aims to enhance the productivity and resilience of 2000 HH ( farmers) including, IDPs, returnees, and vulnerable host community members in Bui and Donga Mantung Divisions. The response focuses on addressing key needs identified in the Food Security Cluster (FSC) 2024 and the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) 2024.
Interventions:
Critical Farm Inputs: ACOHOF will provide timely and targeted assistance during cultivation periods, offering farm inputs such as fertilizers, hoes, cutlasses, files, and insecticides, reducing the reliance on exploitative loans.
Market Access Support: During harvest, ACOHOF will provide market information, valuable advice, and assistance in selling non-perishable crops. Cooperatives, Common Initiative groups and other Farming groups shall be assisted to sell in major towns, ensuring fair prices and preventing exploitation.
The project will prioritize the protection needs of vulnerable populations, particularly women-led households, girls, boys, adolescent girls, and boys. Special attention will be given to displaced individuals engaged in farming, unaccompanied and separated children, survivors of sexual and gender-based violence, at-risk girls and young women, pregnant/lactating mothers, people with disabilities, and women facing other protection concerns.
Outcome:
The initiative aims to break the cycle of poverty, improve food security, and enhance the resilience of farmers, contributing to overall community well-being. By addressing immediate agricultural challenges and protecting the most vulnerable, ACOHOF strives to mitigate the long-term economic impact of the conflict on the region.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The ACOHOF Agricultural Resilience and Market Access Initiative for Bui and Donga Mantung Divisions in Cameroon aims to address a range of assessed needs through its activities. The identified needs are primarily a consequence of the Anglophone Crisis, exacerbated by the Russian-Ukrainian war, leading to widespread food insecurity and economic challenges. The project will focus on the following assessed needs: Food Insecurity: The initiative addresses the immediate need for food security among vulnerable populations, including internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees. The ongoing conflict has disrupted livelihoods and agriculture, leading to depleted household stocks and restricted access to fields. Farmers are poor and do not have enough income to purchase farm inputs whose prices are sadly on the rise. Providing critical farm inputs such as fertilizers, hoes, cutlasses, files, and insecticides aims to enhance productivity and mitigate food insecurity. Livelihood Disruptions:The conflict has disrupted the livelihoods of rural communities heavily reliant on agriculture, impacting essential crop production such as cassava, cocoyam, maize, and beans.Small-scale farmers, constituting 90% of arable land cultivators, lack access to cost-effective financial services, leaving them vulnerable to exploitation by unscrupulous money lenders. Financial Vulnerability: The closure of financial institutions due to the war has left small-scale farmers vulnerable to exploitation by money lenders, resulting in exorbitant interest rates and unfavorable selling conditions during harvest.By providing timely and targeted assistance, the initiative aims to reduce the reliance on exploitative loans, thereby addressing the financial vulnerability of farmers. Market Access Challenges:Market access challenges have been intensified by the conflict, limiting the ability of farmers to sell their produce at fair prices.ACOHOF will provide market information, valuable advice, and assistance in selling non-perishable crops, like corn, beans and soya beans for small holder farmers ( in groups) to ensure fair prices and prevent exploitation during the selling process. Protection Needs of Vulnerable Populations: The project prioritizes the protection needs of vulnerable populations, including women-led households, girls, boys, adolescent girls, and boys.Special attention is given to displaced individuals engaged in farming, unaccompanied and separated children, survivors of sexual and gender-based violence, at-risk girls and young women, pregnant/lactating mothers, people with disabilities, and women facing other protection concerns.Long-Term Economic Impact Mitigation: The initiative aims to mitigate the long-term economic impact of the conflict on the region, as predicted by the World Bank, including a potential 9% drop in GDP by 2025 and a 60% decline in regional GDP by 2030.In summary, the ACOHOF initiative comprehensively addresses the assessed needs by providing support for agriculture, financial resilience, market access, and protection of vulnerable populations, with the overarching goal of breaking the cycle of poverty and improving overall community well-being in the affected regions.
