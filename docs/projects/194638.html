Project Summary

Impact Health Organization (IHO) proposes emergency WASH assistance to IDPs, returnees and host communities in Canal Pigi Jonglei, Twic, Warrap, and Aweil East county northern state. Bahr el. Ghazal, South Sudan in 2023. This project will provide essential life-saving WASH services including access to an adequate amount of water, hygiene awareness, access to an improved water source, access to a functioning and improved sanitation facility, and access to WASH NFIs ( undamaged canister/bucket with lid, access to soap) at Severity 4 Aweil East and Twic. IHO conducted a needs assessment in Aweil East and Twic County, Northern. Bahr el. Ghazal and concluded that most households did not have access to latrines, most households reported using less than 15 liters of water per day per person due to the lack of containers and the water source being too remote . According to the IPC Acute Food Insecurity and Acute Malnutrition Analysis, February July 2022, issued April 9, 2022, classified Pigi County as an Emergency (IPC Phase 4),Aweil East County as an Emergency (IPC Phase 4), and Twic Counties as Critical (IPC AMN phase 4) on. Food insecurity worsened is mainly driven by conflict, destruction of crops by floods, insufficient crop production and restricted freedom of movement due to Covid-19. To address the underlying causes of malnutrition, morbidity, and mortality from WASH-related diseases, IHO targets communities with poor access to safe water supplies, unsafe sanitation and hygiene practices, areas with poor sanitation, and supports health, education, and nutrition centers Rehabilitation of WASH systems. IHO conducted a multi-sector rapid assessment in Kanal/Pigi County, Severity 5, from July 12 to July 15, 2022, which showed that the county has experienced continuous displacement due to flooding. Wunlith Payam hosts 10,400 displaced people and Khorkulus Payam 10,300 returnees. People use 13L of water per day and travel 5km to get water from the Nile, Sobat River and Jonglei Canal. Open stools, no latrine, poor hand washing practices, limited access to soap. Jari canisters, water purification supplies, and high morbidity from diarrhea and vomiting are among the biggest challenges. Therefore, this project will meet the WASH needs of 25,000 people (Aweil East 10,000 Individual, 5,000 Twic and 10,000 Pigi Canal) During the implementation process, IHO will work closely with communities to identify the issue affecting them and the community will be heavily involved in the development and implementation of activities and provide feedback on the progress of the interventions. The project will build community capacity through training and awareness campaigns to stop the spread of WASH-related diseases. IHO will work closely with health, nutrition, protection clusters and relevant government ministries to ensure the timely delivery of life-saving WASH services.

1 - Needs

Following the outbreak of conflict, the December 2013, the Republic of South Sudan continues to face considerable humanitarian problems, which have increased substantially. Among the most affected Counties is Aweil East , Pigi Canal and Twic having figures way off thresholds in the thematic areas of WASH. The situation has been deteriorating due to outbreak of Covid19, floods, internal conflicts, in flow of returnees and internal displacements.. According to the IPC Acute Food Insecurity and Acute Malnutrition Analysis, February July 2022, issued April 9, 2022, classified Pigi County as an Emergency (IPC Phase 4),Aweil East County as an Emergency (IPC Phase 4), and Twic Counties as Critical (IPC AMN phase 4) on. Food insecurity worsened is mainly driven by conflict, destruction of crops by floods, insufficient crop production and restricted freedom of movement due to Covid-19. To address the underlying causes of malnutrition, morbidity, and mortality from WASH-related diseases, IHO conducted a needs assessment in, Aweil East, Twic and canal Pigi County concluded that most households had no access to latrines, most households reported to consume less than 15 Liters of water per day per person due to lack of containers and because the water source was too far. In response to the above indicators, IHO plans to undertake WASH interventions to meet the needs of 25000 individuals in Aweil East, Twic and Pigi Canal.

2 - Project activities

IHO will align the project Activities in line to the WASH cluster 2022 objectives and severity 5 counties Canal Pigi and Priority 4 counties Aweil East and Twic will undertake the following activities to ensure that women, men, girls and boys have safe access to quality basic, gender-responsive, ability-challenged accessible water, sanitation and hygiene services: • IHO will reach people through household water treatment, IHO will obtain Water treatment supplies from the WASH cluster and conduct household distribution, Households will be trained on water treatment and conduct monitoring to ensure people are treating water. In the same regard, IHO will adopt local methods of household water treatment using boiling, filtration and ozone. • In the same regard, IHO will conduct water point construction and rehabilitation to ensure people access safe water other than household water treatment. The construction/Rehabilitation will also target Health facility and Nutrition sites.• IHO will reach people with hygiene promotion awareness both at house hold using hygiene promoters and community through hygiene campaign and in the same regard IHO will promote access to WASH NFIs (unbroken jerrycan/bucket with lids, access to soap etc) and will promote good hygiene behaviors • IHO will in addition support people with access to sanitation facilities through rehabilitation/construction of community and institutional (Including Health facility and Nutrition sites) latrine. In the same regard, IHO will promote access to household latrines by working closely with household members to construct household latrine. • IHO will promote protection community member from exposure to environmental hazards such as floods by ensuring that water points rehabilitated/constructed and as well as Latrine’s stances rehabilitated/constructed with a flood-resilient design.• IHO will integrate WASH activities with GBV mitigation, were IHO will undertake safety audit / consultation for latrine and water points rehabilitation/ Construction, awareness of GBV risks, training WASH staff and community WASH groups on the safe design and construction of WASH facilities that mitigate GBV risk and women provided with job skills training on operation and maintenance of water supply and sanitation. In addition, IHO will create referral pathways for Survivors by working closely with GBV sub cluster partners and empower IHO WASH staff to have the basic skills to provide survivors with information on where they can obtain support. IHO will create GBV messages (including where to report risk and how to access care) and incorporate into hygiene promotion and other WASH related community outreach activities using multiple formats to ensure accessibility.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

IHO will ensure the project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls) by creating different channels that allows them to share concerns anonymously. Examples of feedback and reporting channels may include telephone hotlines, satisfaction focus group discussions, community feedback committees, voice recorders and feedback boxes While conducting discussions with general topics and IHO move towards more sensitive topics as the participants become more comfortable. Guide group discussions towards general, rather than personal conversation, so people do not feel pressure to disclose their own experiences of violence. IHO will ensure that staff member are trained to speak privately with participants who require additional psychosocial/emotional support and/or referrals to other services and will have experience working with survivors of GBV. IHO will be flexible when and where consultations take place.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

IHO will facilitate the provision of feedback from affected people on the services provided through feedback meeting which will be held Monthly basis and upon completion of the project. IHO will administer a perception questionnaire to beneficiaries. Team will ask beneficiaries to give feedback on the services received and suggest way to improve where necessary. Finally, IHO will also conduct evaluation exercise involving men, women, girls and boys. The feedback Information will be available to local communities in local languages were possible.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

IHO has a policy of zero tolerance of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) by IHO staff members and the employees or any other persons engaged and controlled by IHO Contractors .All IHO staff members are trained on PSEA Awareness-Raising. The organization staff or contractors or suppliers are prohibited from exploiting beneficiaries for exchange of services for sex or any form of abuse. All IHO staff members have a duty to report in good faith any SEA allegations, concerns or suspicions, or retaliation related to SEA and SEA of affected populations constitutes gross misconduct and can result indisciplinary action, including summary dismissal and referral for criminal prosecution, where appropriate. IHO will ensure all service provision points are accessed in safe and secure place.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

IHO will use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance to promote accountability, this will allows beneficiaries to hold IHO and its staff to account and reduces the possibility of mistakes, abuse and corruption. IHO will also be able to assess and improve the quality of work, which benefits both the IHO and the people it shall reaches. IHO will also use beneficiaries’ feedback to respond to their issues in real time and act upon it before the project ends and lastly IHO will use use beneficiaries’ feedback Empower communities and promote project sustainability.
