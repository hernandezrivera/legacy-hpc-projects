Project Summary

CASS will be directly targeting about 108,836 people. Children under 5 years are 18,502 children (9,795 boys and 8,707 girls), children above 5 years to 18 years of age are 40,269 children (18,502 boys and 21,767 girls) and adult 18 years above to 60 years are 37,004 people (17,414 men and 19,590 women) and persons 60 years and above are 13,060 people (3,265 men and 9,735 women). Among them are 2,176 persons with disability (1,175 males and 1001 women).
CASS will conduct 78,867 consultations in static facilities/mobile clinics, facilitate 4,353 normal deliveries attended by skilled birth attendants, submit 52 timely and weekly EWARS/IDSR reports from each of the 4 focus Counties, facilitate 11,500 children (5,920 boys and 5,520 girls) with 3 doses of PENTA-3 vaccine, provide maternal healthcare services to 4,353 women of child bearing age of IDPs, returnees and host community including FP , ANC, PNC, IPT 1 and 2, de-worming and TT immunization, provide child healthcare services (routine immunization, Vit A supplementation and child growth monitoring) to 16,734 children (7,815 girls and 8,919 boys), including accelerated mass measles and polio campaigns and provide psychiatric treatment and psychosocial support to 175 mentally ill persons
In addition, CASS will provide community-based level 2 and level 3 (non- specialized) MHPSS to 11,200 persons living with disabilities (PLWD's), including referrals for specialized services from other partners, manage 8,300 uncomplicated diarrhea cases with ORS and Zinc, 14,700 uncomplicated malaria cases with ACT and 7,800 uncomplicated respiratory infection (pneumonia) treated with Amoxicillin. About 78,867 people will reached with health education and promotion to prevent disease outbreaks and awareness raising community engagements on GBV prevention, prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, drug abuse, peace building and peaceful coexistence.
CASS will conduct 12 monthly community meetings in each target county to provide accountability to the communities (AAP strategy), screen 11,500 pregnant women, lactating mother and children under 5s for malnutrition and refer them to for treatment/management, facilitate 12 monthly joint supportive supervision visits to increase quality of services, strengthen linkages with Boma Health Initiatives (Boma Health Workers) for community-based disease surveillance and referral, provide care to physical injuries and CMR including level 2 and level 3 MHPSS to survivors of GBV, provide active epidemic disease surveillance, prevention and control including outbreak detection, investigation and respond within 48 hours including ensuring IPC in health facilities through regular assessment using score cards, provision of supplies and protocols and continuous monitoring of WASH in 31 target health facilities and capacity building of front-line health workers.

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to face overlapping crises, including floods, conflict, dry spells and food insecurity all of which adversely affect the resilience and vulnerability of people across the country. The ongoing Sudan crisis has had a dramatic negative impact on the already fragile humanitarian situation. Disruptions in cross-border trade and humanitarian corridors have led to increased prices of food and commodities, particularly in the northern half of the country where most of the new arrivals are settling. According to South Sudan FEWS NET Food Security Outlook June 2023 - January 2024, warns that South Sudan will continue to face extremely high levels of acute food insecurity, with over 60% of the population anticipated to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes. The South Sudan Humanitarian Snapshot September 2023 shows that 9.4 million people are in dire need of humanitarian aid, 2 million people are living as IDPs in the country. About 293,000 persons have cross into the country along the borders with Sudan and additional 30,000 people have also returned from Ethiopia into the country. In addition, according to Acute Food Insecurity Projection Situation Map and Population Table (Jan- July 2024) (IPC) projected that Guit, Mayendit, Manyo and Fashoda Counties at some point all be in Level 4 (Emergency levels) between January to July in 2024. According to health cluster People Need Analysis, the PIN in Guit County is 55,539 people and target is 36,314 people. In Mayendit County, the PIN is 73,441 with a target of 75,464 people. Similarly, Manyo County, PIN is 75,915 people and targeting 49,535 and in Fashoda County, the PIN is 73,970 people and targeting 56,358 people. The total of 278,865 people as PIN in the four (4) targeted Counties. The total cluster target in the four (4) Counties is 217,671 people. CASS will be directly targeting about 108,836 people. Children under 5 years are 18,502 children (9,795 boys and 8,707 girls), children above 5 years to 18 years of age are 40,269 children (18,502 boys and 21,767 girls) and adult 18 years above to 60 years are 37,004 people (17,414 men and 19,590 women) and persons 60 years and above are 13,060 people (3,265 men and 9,735 women). Among them are 2,176 persons with disability (1,175 males and 1,001 women). IRNA reports from Mayendit, Interangecy Rapid Need Assessment (IRNA) Report Mayendit County, Unity State, 03 - 08 September 2022, IRNA Report: Manyo County, Pen Boma, Ayat Boma, Akurua Boma, Kaka Payam, Athidwe Payam, Demot Boma, Dela-Ajak Payam, Kwoc Boma (27th September to 1st October 2022) and IRNA Report: Kodok, Lul, Dethwok of Fashoda County Flood assessment, Upper Nile State (28 September 2022) all suggest that CASS HRP target locations are in dire needs of health services as most health facilities have been heavily affected by flooding from 2021. The reports shows that malaria, diarrheal diseases and acute respiratory tract infections including increasing cases of snake bites are the main causes of morbidity and mortality. Therefore a dire need to provide integrated health services to the areas isolated by floods in term of regular mobile clinics and strengthens provision of same in the functional static health facilities. The plan response will provide quality health services to people affected by health events, including maternal, child, adolescent, sexual and reproductive health, common and chronic diseases, neglected tropical diseases, MHPSS (Mental Health and Psychosocial support) and GBV (Gender Based Violence). The response will also improve sexual reproductive health services of BEmONC Basic (emergency newborn and obstetric care), family planning, PMTCT (prevention of mother to child transmission), Clinical Management of Rape and supplies for vulnerable women and girls including capacity building of front line health workers.

2. Project activities

The programmatic activities are designed to align with the Strategic Objective SO 1 and Strategic Objective SO 2 and Strategic Objective SO 3. It is further aligned to Specific Objective 1.1, Specific Objective 1.3, Specific Objective 2.2, Specific Objective 3.1 and Specific Objective 3.2. The activities are also in line with Cluster Objective 1, Cluster Objective 2 and Cluster Objective 3. The key activities are: Activity 1: Conduct 78,867 consultations in static facilities/mobile clinics Activity 2: Provide maternal healthcare services to 7,250 women of child bearing age of IDPs, returnees and host community including FP , ANC, PNC, IPT 1 and 2, de-worming and TT immunization and facilitating 3,900 normal deliveries attended by skilled birth attendants. Activity 3: Provide child healthcare services (routine immunization, Vit A supplementation and child growth monitoring) to 16,734 children (7,815 girls and 8,919 boys), including accelerated mass measles and polio campaigns and provide 8,500 children with 3 doses of PENTA-3 vaccine Activity 4: Provide community-based level 2 and level 3 (non- specialized) MHPSS to 11,200 persons living with disabilities (PLWD's), including referrals for specialized services from other partners and provision of psychiatric treatment and psychosocial support to 175 mentally ill persons. Activity 5: Reach 65,500 with awareness raising community engagements on GBV prevention, prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, drug abuse, peace building and peaceful coexistence including provision of care to physical injuries and CMR including level 2 and level 3 MHPSS to survivors of GBV
