Project Summary

SC has designed this project intervention based on the WASH cluster priorities and findings of different WASH and health assessments in North Gaza. All these show a sharp deterioration of hygiene conditions among vulnerable HHs, which is increasing their exposure to health risks.
The proposed intervention is in line with WASH Cluster Objective 2 (Ensure equitable, affordable, and sustainable safely-managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinians). The project aims to improve access to sanitation and hygiene for 900 vulnerable HHs in North Gaza in two levels:
1- HH level :
SC and project partner Biet Lahia Development Association (BLDA)
will support 200 HHs (at least 30 headed by women and 10 with PwD) to improve the hygiene conditions through the rehabilitation of household sanitation facilities, provision of HH hygiene items, and the delivery of hygiene awareness messages. SC will select the beneficiaries based on vulnerability criteria such as HHs with malnourished children, HH income, and family size, prioritizing HHs headed by women, HHs with PwD or chronic diseases.
SC, in partnership with BLDA, will provide conditional cash assistance to 200 HHs. They will receive an amount of $1000 (in two installments) to rehabilitate the HH WASH facilities. In addition, SC will provide the selected HHs with 2 top-ups of hygiene items E-Voucher. This is in line with the WASH guidance provided to partners to ensure that households use the cash and voucher assistance provided for its intended purposes.
In coordination with the WASH cluster,SC and our partner will develop suitable hygiene messages such as hand washing , tooth brushing and how to clean effectively their the HH sanitation facilities to avoid health risks. BLDA will deliver hygiene awareness key message through conducting home visits.
2-Community level:
In coordination with Coastal Municipality Water Utility and Palestian Water Authority , SC will develop the plan to extend the sewer pipelines in 15 streets in Biet Lahia and Biet Hanoun to connect 700 HHs to the public sewer network. SC will implement the infrastructure work in close coordination with CMWU.

1 - Needs

Inability of households to cover basic items due to poverty;The 25 year old, long lasting blockade on Gaza and the recurrent hostilities in 2022 are worsening the humanitarian situation in the strip. The high unemployment rates and the blockage are pushing households into extreme poverty and relying on humanitarian assistance to meet their basic needs. The Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) 2022 indicates that 81% of Gaza households reported challenges to being able to afford their household’s basic needs. This is affecting their ability to purchase basic hygiene items and maintain their sanitation and water facilities at home. Women headed households are poorer than their male counterparts and those headed by people with disbailities are poorer than those without. Lack of adequate and waste management systems;In addition, the lack of a safe drainage system in many neighborhoods in Gaza is increasing houdeholds health risk exposure, especially children, as they are frequently playing in the direct vicinity of overflowing cesspits and the open ‘grey-water’ channels. The MSNA-2023 indicated that approximately 13.5% of Gaza’s HHs are not connected to the public drainage system. Many of these HHs are located in densely populated neighborhoods which increases the vulnerability to health risks such as diarrhea, skin diseases and worms. In September 2022, SC and BLDA conducted field visits to the streets and households that are not connected to the sewage networks in Biet Lahia and Biet Hanoun and conducted Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) with 20 residents (16 Males ,4 Females) including 2 People with Disabilities (PwD). The assessment revealed that there are approximately 2,000 HHs that are not connected to the public sewer networks and other 1,000 HHs are connected randomly. The participants in the FGDs stated that the absence of a sewer network and the use of cesspits causes frequent sewage flooding in the streets. This in turn increases the financial burden on the already poor families, as the average monthly cost of emptying the cesspit is approximately 50 ILS. Women speficically stated that they suffer from the spread of mosquitoes and rodents in their homes as a result of the sewage flooding. Women noted that due to the poor drainage system in their homes, their ability to use water for cleaning purposes is limited. This is causing additional workload to women, who already have to care for children and older people. In addition, the participants said that due to the poor hygiene conditions in and around their homes, their children are often exposed to diseases such as parasites and diarrhea, which increases the financial burden on families, as well as the burdens on childcare for women. Poor and unsafe sanitation facilities at household level; In November 2021, SC and BLDA conducted field visits to assess the need to improve the hygienic condition of 1248 vulnerable HHs in North and Eastern Gaza city. The results revealed that 1,048 HHs needed to rehabilitate and upgrade their WASH facilities. The assessment also indicated that many of the visited HHs suffered a lack of water storage tanks. In many homes, bathrooms and toilets are unsafe and difficult to clean which increases the burdens of household chores on women. In some homes, women and girls suffer from limited privacy while using their WASH facilities, which makes them feel unsafe and in some cases, limit their use, especially at nigh. This is a serious concern for mothers with regards to their daughters. They said they had to stand in front of the door until they are finished.To assess the urgent needs of vulnerable female HHs, BLDA conducted FGDs in September 2022 with 13 vulnerable women in North Gaza. The participants stated that the low income led to deterioration of the hygiene conditions in their homes due to the inability to afford hygiene items and inadequate WASH facilities in their homes. In addition, the findings of needs assessment conducted for 1,000 vulneable HH in North Gaza by BLDA in August 2022 showed poor hygiene practices in the visited HHs. 65.5 % of children said that they don’t wash their hands after eating or playing and they are not used to brushing their teeth. In addition, the outreach workers observed flys in 18.5% of visited homes and 12.5 % HHs don’t clean their drinking water tanks. To assess the impact of poverity in North Gaza, BLDA conducted home visits to 600 vulnerable HHs.The results indicated that 586 ( 97.5% ) of HHs are suffering from shortage of cleaning materials such as soap, dishwashing liquid, shampoo or aanitary pads, among others.Child malnutrition;Malnutrition is strongly linked to recurrent infections such as gastroenteritis and respiratory tract infections.The SC health partner’s records revealed an strong correlation between poor WASH conditions (e.g. quantity and quality water availability, WASH facilities) and malnutrition in children. The records showed that 30% of 220 malnourished children in North Gaza who received medical treatment in the AEI clinic were suffering from water borne diseases. In addition, the results of the home visits to 1,520 malnourished children, which were conducted under the SC-ongoing project in 8 localities, showed that 67% (1021) of them live in homes with substandard WASH facilities.

2 - Activities

SC designed the intervention in line with the WASH cluster priority, notably to improve access to sanitation and hygiene. The project aims to improve access to sanitation and hygiene for 850 vulnerable HHs in North Gaza at community and households level. SCI, in partnership with BLDA, will improve hygiene conditions for 200 vulnerable families, including at least 30 HHs headed by women and 10 HHs with PwD, through the rehabilitation and upgrading of HH WASH facilities, provision of hygiene items and the delivery of hygiene awareness messages. The target 200 HHs distributed in the following localities : Biet Lahia : Al Amal, Al Atatra, Assiafa,Aslan,Al MashiahBiet Hanoun : Izabit Biet Hanoun( Alzaiton Alska ), Al Amal, Activity 1.1: Selection of beneficiaries:SC and BLDA will develop the selection criteria based on the vulnerability and the level of risk to water borne diseases, including malnutrition. The selection criteria includes HH income, and family size prioritizing HHs head by women, HHs with PwD or chronic diseases and HHs with malnourished children.BLDA will publicize the project among the target communities to apply to benefit from the rehabilitation of WASH facilities. BLDA will use its’ database to encourage families for applying. BLDA outreach workers will conduct verification visits to applicants’ HHs to assess the level of vulnerability based on the agreed criteria . BLDA in coordination with SC will screen the applications. SC will cross-check the list of potential beneficiaries with MoSD to ensure the vulnerability. Activity 1.2; Technical Assessment of household sanitation facilities: SC and BLDA engineers and outreach workers will conduct home visits to assess the needs for rehabilitation of sanitation facilities, considering the needs of different family members (boys, girls, men, women). Based on the assessment results, SC will develop the lists of WASH needs for each HH considering the needs of all family members including PwD when need. In coordination with the HH , After consultation, with the target families, BLDA will develop the rehabilitation plan including the cost of implementation for each HHs. Activity 1.3; Improvement of household sanitation facilities:SC will support 200 HHs with conditional cash support to improve their sanitation facilities based on the technical assessment conducted in activity 1.2. The technical assessment will also consider accessibility issues for those HH that have members with a disability. Each HHs will receive an amount of $1,000 (in two installments). SC will manage the provision of cash assistance, reviewing the financial documents, while the partners’ teams will follow up the implementation of rehabilitation plans by conducting home visits. Activity 1.4; Provision of hygiene items for the target families :Through the home visits to the selected HHs, BLDA will conduct meetings with boys, girls, men, and women to identify the specific hygiene items they each need. SC will also draw from WASH cluster’s recommended list of hygiene items. SC will discuss the results with the WASH cluster to prepare a list of allowed hygiene items in the E-voucher system. SC will provide the required hygiene items to the target families through 2 top up of E-vouchers and this will run in parallel with the hygiene awareness raising messages delivered through the home visits. Activity 1.5; WASH awareness raising;SC, in coordination with the WASH cluster and BLDA, will develop suitable hygiene awareness messages. BLDA will print the hygiene educational material and use them as aids during the home visits. SC health team will train BLDA’s outreach workers to deliver the hygiene awareness messages. The outreach workers will conduct home visits to the selected HHs and meet children, women, and men to deliver the hygiene awareness messages. These messages will also include information about an improvement in hygiene practices will improve nutrition outcomes among children and what small steps households can take to ensure this. This activity will be integrated with the ongoing SC health project, which focuses on improving the nutrition awareness for vulnerable HHs. Activity 2.1: Connecting public sewage networks;SC, in coordination with PWA and CMWU, will connect 650 HHs to the public sewage network The work will include the construction of 6,422 meters of sewage pipelines (10,8,6 inches) to extend the sewer pipelines in 15 streets in North Gaza as follows : Biet Lahia: ( Alzen , Al Barawi, Al Salateen, Al Qasem,Ghben,Teka,Aslan, Al zamieh, Al tanneh, Al zawari, M’roof ). Biet Hanoun :(Zemmo, Shram and Hamad Industrial area ) Activity 2.1: Construction of the new pipeline:In coordination with CMWU, SC will prepare the engineering design, BoQ, and other bid documents for the construction of the new pipeline. 2.2 Recruitment of contractors:SC will select a qualified contractor to implement the work through a public bid. 2.3 Technical follow up:SC will follow up the implementation in coordination with CMWU. SC site engineer will ensure the quality of work, safety conditions, including safeguarding considerations. SC project officer will follow up the coordination with the target communities. 2.4 Handover of the construction; A technical committee from SC , CMWU, PWA, and the contractor will hand over the work to the service provider CMWU. CMWU will be responsible for the maintenance of the new pipeline. Monitoring, Accountability, Evaluation and Learning (MEAL): SC team with review the rehabilitation plan for each HHs this will include, ensure that the plan considers all family members’ needs, the prices of rehabilitation items and the the expenses based on the market prices. After signing the contract, BLDA engineers will conduct home visits to verify that the beneficiaries implement the work within the required specification based on the agreed plans.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

SC is committed to being accountable to communities by sharing appropriate and timely information, by supporting them to influence processes and decisions that affect them and by enabling them to provide feedback and share complaints about our work. Feedback and complaint mechanisms will be in place and disseminated at the start of the project, including SC’s toll-free hotline (1800-724-726), complaint boxes (placed at the CBO offices) and community sessions. SC pays special attention to ensure that beneficiaries are encouraged to raise any concerns and make use of their preferred communication channel. Therefore, the sensitizations sessions will be utilized to raise the awareness about the rights and responsibilities of the beneficiaries, as well as about the FCM and how we identify and respond to feedback. SC team will also train providers in SC Child Safeguarding, SC Gender Policy and SC financial policies with the focus on preventing fraud and corruption. A specific session on PSEA, with a focus on identification and reporting, will be delivered to partners and providers, ensuring these incidents are also reported through the cluster system. SCI PSEA mechanism is integrated into our broader feedback and complains mechanism.SC has Community Protection Committees (CPCs) and other youth groups in target localities that will participate and be engaged in the planning and implementation of the intervention. Moreover, they will participate and support the process of the selection of the beneficiaries. They will be also the key linkages with their respective communities and represent them in raising any concern or complaint to SC and partners. SC has different ongoing projects managed by specialized protection and health team and will make sure to establish the linkages for the needed referrals using the case management system, including the child protection and malnutrition cases.
