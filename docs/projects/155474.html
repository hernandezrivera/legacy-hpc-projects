Project Summary

Zimbabwe is experiencing an acute on chronic shortage of medicines and medical sundries due to unavailability of foreign currency. The level of medicines in both the public and private sector is a serious cause of concern, characterised by stock-outs of certain commodities and less than 2 weeks cover for others. Availability of medicines is at overall 26% against a target of 60%; Vital is 30% against a target of 100% and Essential is 24% against a target of 80%. Availability of medical sundries is 22% against a set target of 80%.
JUSTIFICATION
There is an acute shortage of essential medicine, diagnostics, supplies and consumables at all levels of the health system, making access to health care out- of- reach for most of the poor and vulnerable population. The current stock levels indicate less than two weeks cover for essential medicines. Although financial support has been received from various donors in the form of the Health Development Fund, Global Fund, the US government through its various implementing partners, the support has been directed at primary health services as well as programmes for specific conditions like malaria, HIV, TB, Family and Child Health.
The shortage of medicines affects the poor in the country who cannot afford alternative care from private providers and leads to poor utilisation of services in general and increased preventable deaths, some of which may not be reported to the health service. It is noteworthy that the above prevailing condition exacerbates the vulnerability of communities especially in the rural settings where communities cannot access foreign currency that is demanded by most pharmacies. The cost of health services provided by the private sector is not regulated and could be prohibitive for vulnerable populations. The private not-for-profit is also faced with economic constraints which impacts negatively on their ability to import adequate medicines and supplies.
THE REQUEST
This appeal requests for support to provide medicines and medical supplies to cover 9, 170 000 million vulnerable population to mitigate against preventable illnesses, disabilities and deaths. The medicines and medical supplies will cover the essential requirements of 1 568 primary health care facilities in 80 districts for a period of 6 months.
Prioritisation
The prioritisation took into consideration the vulnerability and proportion of the population to be reached. In this regard, consideration was made for rural residence where 67% of the population live largely served by primary health care facilities.

Needs

The economic situation in Zimbabwe continues to limit the ability of the Government to support healthcare provision in the country. The stagnant economic situation has adversely affected both the productive sectors and the social sectors, and health has not been spared. The Gross national income per capita is $890 (2016) and the Life expectancy is 59 years for men and 63 years for women. Unemployment levels remain high with the majority of the people now in informal employment. The labour report highlights that Zimbabwe’s unemployment levels rose from 4.8% in 2011 to 11.3% in 2014. Poverty has been on the increase since 2014.The country has experienced a drought since 2015/2016 due to Elnino effect. Meteorological services predict a rainy patter for 2018/19 which could trigger severe drought and/or floods with consequences of food and water shortage worsening vulnerability to diseases.The country has a total population of 13 591 578 of which 15.1% are under five years old and 3% are pregnant women which are among the vulnerable population groups. Sixty seven percent (67%) of the population live in rural areas. About 50% of the population have access to adequate sanitation and only 55% of the population have access to safe water. VULNERABILITYVulnerable population in Zimbabwe include women, children, the elderly, the poor, displaced people (refugees), those living with HIV, those with other chronic health conditions. These people are at risk of poor health outcomes due to limited access to health services and more likely to incur impoverishing health expenditures. The rural population are another vulnerable category more likely to experience geographical isolation, have higher poverty rates and limited job opportunities which could all challenge access to health care and increase disease burden and adverse health consequences.
