Project Summary

NRCs is responding to the humanitarian needs of people affected by displacement in 10 African Countries (Burkina Faso, Central African Republic, Ethiopia, Kenya, Mali, Mozambique, Niger, Somalia, Sudan and Uganda), in order to reduce vulnerabilities amongst Internally Displaced People (IDPs), Refugees, Returnees and Host Communities (HC). In total, NRC will reach 594.033 people (350.013 women and 244.020 men). In Ethiopia interventions are aligned with the two main planning tools for humanitarian response and refugee response; Ethiopia Country Refugee Response Plan 2021 and Humanitarian Response Plan 2021 and assessments. NRC has identified the following groups as the ones that require special support and/or protection. The current COVID-19 pandemic intensifies those vulnerabilities. These groups are; girls/ women, youth and in particular young men, people with disabilities (PWD), elderly and people without legal documents.
This project is a multisectoral response with focused interventions of; Food security and Livelihood plus MPC, WASH, Shelter and ICLA (Information Communication and Legal Assistance). Focusing in providing assistance to refugees, IDPs, returnees and the hosting communities.

1. - Projects activities

LFS and MPC: Displacement-affected and/ or other vulnerable households within the host communities have enhanced access to food, food production inputs, skills and information and MPC. The LFS and MPC component of this project will mainly follow 3 approaches. 1) immediate food assistance through cash/ voucher/ in-kind assistance to cover immediate needs 2) the diversification of livelihoods and 3) MPC The multipurpose cash assistance will be issued to meet a range of household needs, mitigate the use of negative coping mechanisms and prevent, mitigate or respond to any protection concerns raised. Targeting criteria will be identified through various processes as described above and beneficiaries will be selected and registered accordingly., Interventions and values will be closely coordinated with the Cash Working Groups in the respective countries. Irrespective of the modality/ies used, Post-Distribution Monitoring (PDMs) will be conducted after assistance has been distributed to the affected people. WASH: Displacement-affected and/ or other vulnerable households within the host communities have better access to safe water, adequate sanitation facilities and perform increased safety and hygiene practices. NRC WASH response in Tigray region will reach up to 20,000 IDPs, returnees and hosting communities through improving access to safe water through rehabilitation or development of new water sources and monitoring water quality. Works will be preceded by technical assessments including topographical surveys to be undertaken by consultant. In addition, the affected population will have improved access to sanitation facilities to reduce open defecation and practise increased safety and hygiene practices. The main activities in WASH are the construction/maintenance of sanitation facilities including handwashing stations. Works for WASH facilities will be undertaken by contractors engaged through a competitive bidding process and will be supervised by NRC’s engineers. Hygiene promotion will focus on behaviour change through combination of various approaches like WASH’em, design for behaviour change (DBC), Community led total sanitation (CLTS) and community led hygiene campaigns. To enhance hygiene practices, hygiene (NFIs) kits (incl. dignity-kits for women) will be distributed to the affected population. Based on the availability on the local markets, NFI-Kits will be either distributed through cash/ vouchers or in-kind, depending on the cash feasibility and beneficiary preference. Shelter: People affected by displacement have enhanced access to safe and adequate shelter or settlement infrastructure.This intervention is in line with the NRC's strategy of protecting the rights of displaced and vulnerable people affected by the conflict, particularly in hard-to-reach areas. Through the shelter interventions, targeted communities will have access to safe and adequate shelter (shelter solutions), including essential household items kits. The main activities include contribution to transitional shelter solution in kind and technical support, emergency Shelter kits (temporary, transitional and in few cases permanent) and tool's kit for construction, contribution to dignified shelter solutions through light retrofitting material for housing along with the latrines/bathing facilities/handwashing stations, distribution of household. The modality (cash/ voucher and/or in-kind) depends on cash feasibility and beneficiary preferences. ICLA: People affected by displacement receive Information, Counselling and Legal Assistance NRC’s main ICLA objective is to ensure that persons affected by conflict and displacement have increased opportunities and capacities to claim and exercise refugee rights, legal identity, IDP registration and HLP-related rights to achieve durable solutions by preventing statelessness and conflict around access to land, as well as facilitating access to other human rights (like education)

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

NRC will be working with local partner organisations in Ethiopia. NRC will work with two partners to ensure continuity in the support provided to the most vulnerable population affected by displacement in Benishangul Gumuz (BG) and Tigray region. In BG region, NRC will continue to partner with Development Association Network (BGDAN), a community-based organization based in the region. BGDAN is currently supporting in the implementation of the GFFO funded project (S09-70-321.50 ETH 01/20). NRC will continue to collaborate with and build the capacity of BGDAN under the proposed project. Similarly, in Tigray region, NRC plans to work with a local organization based in the region. Mapping of potential community-based organizations (CBO) for partnership is in progress. The partner will engage with NRC for the planned project within 12 months in year 2 and year 3 of the project. Once the partnership agreement is signed with the selected CBO, NRC will initiate capacity development activities as the selected partner would be an important part of the project’s exit strategy.
