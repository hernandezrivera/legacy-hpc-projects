Project Summary

1. The project aims to mobilise resources to improve access to integrated emergency health care services and shall be focusing on the major causes of morbidity and mortality affecting the most vulnerable communities including malaria, Acute watery diarrhoea, Acute bloody diarrhoea, Acute Respiratory Tract infection (ARTI) and neonatal and maternal health conditions.
2. It aims to reduce inequity; it will also focus on health promotion and prevention, screening and early intervention; and improve quality, safety, performance and accountability.
3. As a strategy to detect, prevent and response to epidemics prone diseases outbreaks and in line with the current cluster strategy and objectives, this project will use multidisciplinary approaches such working with WASH hygiene promoters, Boma Health Committees, Religious leaders, extension workers and Mother to Mother support groups to widen the surveillance network.
4. Promote healthcare services integration with nutrition, WASH, FSL, and protection especially Sexual Gender Based Violence (SGBV) to enhance intervention effectiveness and its impact on the most vulnerable communities.
5. The project will integrate the ongoing development focused intervention to narrow the huge gaps in the health care provision and to ensure outbreaks and other emergencies impacting on health are timely and effectively prevented, addressed and contained.

1 - Needs

IPC, May 2019, issued, June 14 2019; in Jonglei State, food insecurity remains high with an estimated 1.33 million (73% of the population) facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. Coincidentally, it is also the most populated and the most food insecure State in South Sudan during the May-July projection period. Six Counties are in Emergency (IPC Phase 4), namely Canal/Pigi, Twic East, Uror, Nyirol, Bor South and Akobo. Canal/Pigi is the worst affected, with an estimated 10,000 people (10% of the population) in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5. Food insecurity in Jonglei State is largely driven by low harvests that have been depleted for majority of the households, floods, lack of markets, or disrupted ones as a consequence of the rainy season, high food prices, limited livelihood opportunities, depleted assets and reduced livestock holdings occasioned by increased incidents of cattle raiding. Risk factors to monitor closely during the projection period include progression of the agricultural season and likely impacts of the FAW, flooding, disruptions to delivery of humanitarian assistance, increased incidents of waterborne diseases, cattle raiding, and the impact of returnees. Moreover, Akobo, Uror, Nyirol and Pibor are allocated north eastern Jonglei State alongside southern bank of sobat and Pibor river, the clashes between community of greater Akobo and Pibor made a lot of displacement towards, Denjock in Akobo East, Buong in Akobo West and Dini in Nyirol, while in Pibor, displacement towards Lilkuangole. However, ongoing conflict in greater Akobo and Pibor continue to the surrounding areas, means that displacement dynamic in and around the area will remain in the near future, with the strong potential for secondary and tertiary population displacement. At the same context of floods displacement, flooding is ongoing with serious devastation of the shelters and other property, which result into suffering of the vulnerable communities especially the children, elderly, pregnant and lactating mothers. Analysis of the affected population in Nyirol, Uror, Akobo and Pibor shows that over 350,000 people have been displaced, the data shows that 41% population out of 845,716 of total greater Akobo and Pibor population were displaced. The population profile, the majority household encountered composed of women, children and elderly persons, 65% (227, 500) the displaced persons are women and children, 35% (122, 500) are men, who are currently settled in the churches and highlands within Nyirol, Uror, Akobo and Pibor. The main health concerns in Akobo, Uror, Nyirol and Pibor were reported to be malaria, diarrheal diseases, Acute respiratory Tract infection, pregnancy related cases, TB, and cholera and its thought to be induced by rampant flooding displacement.
