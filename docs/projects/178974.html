Project Summary

A leader in the fight against malnutrition, AAH implements gender-sensitive life-saving WASH interventions and provide communities with access to safe water and sanitation as sustainable mitigation for hunger causes. After decade of conflicts, drought, economic stagnation and the recent pandemic, basic services infrastructures in the country are facing unprecedented stress and deterioration. AAH continuously assess the basic needs of different communities across the country. Access to water, adequate sanitation and proper access to health and education were found as top priorities. AAH mandate consists of supporting the functional authorities in responding to crises and regaining resilience and preparedness capabilities, in this scope AAH prioritizes sustainable resilience WASH projects, however, provides life-saving rapid WASH (Water trucking, distribution of water storage containers and hygiene items) when access to the basic WASH needs becomes severe or critical.
AAH operates through three field offices in Damascus, Aleppo and Hassakeh, and provides wide range of WASH services. AAH keeps a close contact with SARC and Ministry of Water resources in addition to other governmental institutions (Ministries of Local Administration and Environment, Education and Health, Governorates and local administrations) in order to coordinate efforts and involve the functional authorities in program design, implementation, capacity building and monitoring. In NES, AAH coordinates with UNICEF and NES WWG for its interventions in Hassakeh, and Deir El Zor.
WASH resilience response includes rehabilitation of more than 21 water and 6 sewage systems (networks, pumping stations and treatment plants). AAH also targets public institutions and intends to intervene in 75 schools and 7 public health centers with rehabilitation of WASH facilities, hygiene promotion campaigns and COVID19 response. AAH will provide water trucking in Hassakeh city, rural Idlib and rural Aleppo (areas under GoS) and Daraa, and provide comprehensive WASH intervention in two IDP camps (Areesha and Abo khashab) through direct support to beneficiaries using remote operation modalities.
Rehabilitation works will be coupled with capacity building on operation and maintenance, and provision of water safety plans. While distribution will be paralleled with behavioral change promotion sessions and messaging. All activities will respect the COVID19 guidelines.
AAH intervention will focus on the locations with WASH severity 3 and higher as per the HNO, and the identification of potential projects will be done through MEAL and technical needs assessments, as well as through focus group discussion, Key informant interviews, referrals and requests from relevant authorities and sectors. However, the identification of WASH interventions in public institutions will be based on the severity scores of the relevant sectors being Health and Education (i.e. rehabilitation of schools and PHCs in Hama). AAH WASH will target 1,321,900 beneficiaries in total of which 674,174 females (>18 y.o. 310,308; <18 y.o. 363,866) and 647,726 males (>18 y.o. 298,140; <18 y.o. 349,586). AAH will reach 29,600 students in mixed-gender and gender-disaggregated schools of which 15,096 are females, and around 24K in emergency response of which 12,240 are females.
Community engagement throughout the program design and implementation will be encouraged, and consultations with stakeholders will be carried out in order to avoid duplication and to target the most vulnerable communities. Protection, GBV, PSEA, Climate change and green technologies will be mainstreamed, and a functional feedback mechanism will be maintained to collect complaints and suggestions feeding the monitoring and learning process.
Post intervention monitoring, and satisfaction surveys will measure the accomplishment of the project indicator, impact and acceptance of the aid provided.

4 - Needs

General AAH needs assessments across different governorates found that water supply, adequate sanitation and proliferation of solid waste, mainly in post conflict areas, were the most cited challenges faced by IDPs, returnees and local communities. AAH also relies on nation-wide assessments and referrals submitted by the WASH sector and partner organizations, and also from public institutions. AAH will prioritize locations with WASH severity score of 3 or higher; integrated interventions will also look at the respective severity scores as per other sectors. AAH conducts MEAL (KIIs) and WASH needs assessments, at admin 3 and admin 4 levels. AAH have identified specific WASH needs in terms of water supply and sanitation system rehabilitation in Rural Damascus (Qatana, Jirud, Sahnaya, Sarghaya), Daraa (Ash-Shajara, As-Sanamayn, Daraa Albalad), Hasskeh (Tal Hamis, Tal Tamer) Raqqa (Maadan, Sabka), Hassakeh (Darbasiya, Ras El Ain, Quamishli), Aleppo (Maskana, Tal Ed-Daman Jebel Saman, Haritan, Al Khafsa), and Idleb (Sanjar, Saraqeb); in terms of WASH rehabilitation in public institutions and hygiene in Rural Damascus (Sarghaya, Ghizlaniya), Idleb (Abul Thohor), Hama (Hama), Hassakeh (Ras El Ain, Quamishli, Tal Hamis). AAH WASH project will target around 1.3 m beneficiaries. AAH is also responding to calls for intervention, form NES WWG and camp management in the camps of Areesha and Abo Khashab, and in Hassakeh city for water trucking and sanitation support. MoWR commuicates with the WASH department recurrent requests for support in terms of rehabilitation of different water and sanitation system in Rural Damascus (Qatana, Saasaa, Yabrud), Daraa (Daraa, As Sanamayn), Aleppo (Jebel Samaan).

5 - Implementation of activities

Emergency WASH: AAH provides rapid WASH interventions such as life-saving water trucking, HK distributions, installation of communal water tanks, and desludging in camps, hard to reach and urban areas with acute water and sanitation shortage. Rehabilitation of Water Systems: AAH targets the rehabilitation of damaged and malfunctioning water and sanitation systems. Provision of water through public networks enables greater control on both quality and quantity. Rehabilitation of sewage systems including wastewater plants and sewage networks, aiming to prevent public health risks and contamination of groundwater and soils. Rehabilitation of WASH facilities in public health centres and schools, in the scope of COVID19 aiming to enhance the public hygiene behavior and access to safe sanitation. AAH couples every hand-over of rehabilitated project with specific operation and maintenance training packages to DoW/Sanitation companies, local authorities, and communities. Despite high initial cost of such interventions, rehabilitations can be justified in the mid-term, while comparing the overall cost of water trucking and public health risk associated with it. Provision of Sanitation systems in camps AAH will work with Camp Management to guarantee minimum standards of sanitation in camps through installation of latrines, with specific attention to AGD. Where applicable, AAH will prioritize sustainable and environmentally friendly solution such as communal septic tanks, infiltration wetlands and natural sewage treatment technologies. Hygiene Promotion AAH is providing emergency hygiene and COVID19 kits in collaboration with SARC in most areas with high ratio of PiN. HKs are tailored based on gender, age and cultural norms.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Inadequate quantity or quality of supplied water through trucking: AAH will maintain a network of field monitors to supervise the water supply from source to end-user, monitoring the quality of the water and daily supply of minimum 15lpcd. AAH team will distribute communal tanks and keep a schedule of cleaning. Limited access to toilets and GBV risks: AAH will abide by SPHERE standards and consult with girls and women prior to the installation of latrines/toilets, and will provide user-friendly feedback mechanism for monitoring and learning purposes. Empowering armed groups through inequal distribution of rehabilitation services AAH signed several MoU with authorities and continuously coordinates with SARC, MoWR, MoLAE and involve the relevant technical departments in projects; design and monitoring. The same MoUs guarantee the hand-over of operation and maintenance responsibility to authorities, mitigating the risk of Theft, improper storage and misuse of water and sanitation systems, equipment and materials. Child Labour: AAH will ensure the proper registration system for staff, consultants and contractors, and include child protection charters within contracts and HR procedures. No-tolerance on child-labour, GBV or harassment will be in place. General notes: AAH WASH activities will be sensitive to gender, culture and protection risks, and designed according to prior consultation with beneficiary communities.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

The WASH sector identified PSEA risks as physical and psychological harm arising from violence and coercion due to inadequacy in distribution or inappropriate timing and process of distribution which put women and girls at higher risk of GBV. Likelihood: high, Impact: high. As mitigation measure, AAH will coordinate distributions with SARC and require clear and transparent distribution lists to be ready on site and perform duly communication with the target communities, as their involvement will guarantee the best distribution modality and timing. The risk of sexual abuse/coercion by AAH staff, likelihood: unlikely, impact: high. All AAH staff are trained on PSEA as part of their mandatory induction. Field activities are monitored through continuous MEAL procedures, in addition to transparent and user-friendly feedback and complaint mechanism. Risk of sexual abuse/coercion by AAH contractor staff: likelihood: unlikely, impact: high. All AAH contractor are requested to sign anti-harassment, PSEA, child protection policies as a mandatory part of their contracts. Field activities are monitored through continuous MEAL procedures, in addition to transparent and user-friendly feedback and complaint mechanism.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk of spread of COVID-19 at distribution sites, staff/partner sickness. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigation: social distancing; safe hand-washing area; PPE distribution; COVID-19 protection guidance implemented. Risk of unstable security situation; access to targeted locations prevented; additional administrative procedures on DINGOs, lengthy processes to obtain approvals. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: use local staff and SARC. Advocacy, coordination with local authorities/Ministries, to monitor situation, negotiate access. Adhere to SOPs/security systems, Conflict-Sensitivity approach. Risk of substantive devaluation of the Syrian pound; international bank transfers to Syria are not fluid and timely. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: situation closely monitored with communication to donors with options defined. Risk of aid diversion, fraud, and corruption. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: diversification of supplier, triangulation of information, standard monitoring procedures in place, security plan and risks assessments tables updated and followed. Risk of data collection hampered. Likelihood: low. Impact: medium. Mitigation: transparency, trust-building, developing relationships with municipalities, district authorities and facilities managers. Ethical risk. Likelihood: low. Impact: high. Mitigation: provide compulsory trainings staff member on organizational ethical policies. Follow the internal policies relating to fraud, anti-corruption and abuse of power and whistle-blower mechanisms. The Code of Conduct is aligned with IASC principles. Employees sign a Code of Conduct. AAH has a Policy for PSEA with an implementation plan.
