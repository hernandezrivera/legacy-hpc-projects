Project Summary

The protracted conflict in the east of Ukraine continues to affect the standard of living of citizens. The shelling of settlements located near the demarcation line led to the closure of enterprises, social institutions and medical centers. High unemployment is a reflection of the plight of the economy. The poverty rate is growing along with the increase in living expenses. The problems are compounded by depriving many people of access to social benefits, which for many are the only source of income. At the same time, the wages of employed persons do not correspond to a decent standard of living. People in rural areas have limited access to livelihoods and are more dependent on additional funds. They are more likely to suffer from high unemployment and poor transport infrastructure connecting them with the nearest villages and cities. Farming and cattle breeding are common to satisfy basic needs and create conditions for further survival, but the necessary equipment is not always enough for this. For example, unstable irrigation, due to lack of equipment, leads to poor harvests, which increases the risk of food vulnerability. And if the population of working age, due to their physical capabilities, can independently receive the minimum means of subsistence, then elderly people are forced to rely only on social or humanitarian assistance. Due to the migration of young and middle-aged people, many seniors were left alone, without financial and psychological support, which led to an increase in morbidity and mortality among elderly people living on the NGCA. According to HRP 2019 data of 3.5 million citizens, in need of help, 30 percent are people over 60 years of age. This is the largest proportion of older people, affected by conflict, in the world. 2020 HRP Population Dataset , agreed on inter cluster level for the operational planning purposes, says that in the Donetsk region, 1 950,000 people live on NGCA, of which 515,000 are elderly, which is 26.4%. A third of these people are deprived of the opportunity to receive payments to the GCA, many cannot cross the line of demarcation for health reasons, Some retirees live on the minimum allowance, which since July 1, 2019. amounts to 4000 RUB. (1524 UAH (at the NBU rate). Per month. Payment of housing and communal services after increasing local tariffs is at least 650 rubles (247.65 UAH), an approximate basic grocery set (5 kg of meat, 10 kg of cereals, 10 kg of vegetables, 10 loaves of bread, 4 liters of milk, 2 packs of butter, 1 liter of vegetable oil) - at least 2200 rubles (864 UAH). The vast majority of older people need regular intake of essential medicines, their acquisition is an obligatory expense item, and amounts to at least 1000 rubles (369 UAH). Thus, minimum expenses of an elderly person 3200 rubles (1219.2 UAH) per month for independent satisfaction of basic needs (excluding expenses on hygiene products, clothing and shoes), which is 80% of income.
The implementation of the first project activity was called upon to contribute to improving welfare and to provide 250 households that have resources (land, premises for livestock or crop production, etc.) for agricultural activities, the necessary equipment and tools based on them needs and requests. Additional opportunities for obtaining independent income will increase their sustainability and reduce their dependence on traditional humanitarian assistance. The second project activity is designed to assist 1,000 single elderly people in the form of food packages for two months of residence. This period will be enough for the beneficiaries to be able to spend the saved money on the purchase of expensive medicines or essential goods.

1 - Needs

The socio-economic conditions at NGCA continue to deteriorate, which threatens the life and well-being of the population. There is a clear correlation between food shortages, vulnerability and socio-economic trends. The increase in poverty and un-employment as a result of the conflict affects the standard of living of the population and poses a danger to human health. As a result of active hostilities, the problem of access to food has arisen, especially for families living near the “demarcation line” on the NGCA. According to official data from local social institutions at the beginning of 2019, the rural population is about 108 thousand people. (excluding citizens living in the private sector) and maintains a private subsidiary farm in order to pro-vide their own households with food. Women-headed households and large families experience the greatest difficulties in the processing of household plots and livestock breeding. The conflict in eastern Ukraine has a special impact on older people. These people face serious difficulties in accessing basic services due to their disability and limited mobility, and they are often separated from their families. They are also more vulnerable to abuse and lack of custody, have special medical and nutritional needs, and are most economically vulnerable. Available data indicate that more than half of people who are lacking in food are older people, and for 9 out of 10 older people on NGCA, pension is the main source of income. At the same time, this income is not enough to meet basic needs, since a quarter of the pension is spent on medicines, and more than half on food.

2 - Activities & outcomes

As a result of the project, it is planned to implement the following activities and achieve the following results: 1. Implementation of a set of measures to improve the standard of living by providing agricultural equipment and tools for the 250 most vulnerable households located along the demarcation line, which will help individual households to receive assistance at a new level of quality. The decision to develop measures and a means of intervention at all stages of the activity will be planned based on the needs and requests of the beneficiaries, which will ensure the implementation of an appropriate form of intervention. 2. Purchase and distribution of food packages for elderly people. This form of intervention is intended for 1000 people with a frequency of once every two months during the year. Priority - single, handicapped and persons with disabilities. The grocery set will be compiled taking into account the need for food for the above age group and their physical capabilities. During the implementation of project activities, all activities will be carried out in compliance with the basic humanitarian standards of quality and accountability to the affected population. Actions and approaches will allow local communities to participate in key decisions at all stages of the program: evaluation, analysis, planning, implementation, monitoring and review. To this end, communicative approaches will be used, including the existence of a transparent and constant two-way communication, through which information on the risks, needs and opportunities of the communities will be collected. Gender and age analysis indicates that the needs for this type of assistance for both gender and age groups are almost the same. However, the intervention will take into account a greater number of women, as one of the goals of the project is to maintain the reproductive health of women and adolescent girls and older people due to their low mobility and special needs for medical care.At all stages of the development and implementation of the project, gender analysis will be an integral part of project activities. For this, age and gender data and vulnerability criteria will be taken into account. All events will adhere to the principles of equal access, as well as appropriate solutions for people with special needs. For a clear understanding of how the program is being implemented and the appropriateness of providing this type of assistance, all project activities will be monitored and evaluated throughout the implementation period. Inclusion of the local population from the very beginning, by explaining the criteria of vulnerability and participation in the targeted impact, will significantly strengthen gender and age equality in supported communities. A survey of beneficiaries after receiving assistance will be used to analyze the relevance of humanitarian assistance. The data obtained will be used for feedback from the beneficiaries.Gender and age characteristics will also be taken into account in the monitoring process to provide equal opportunities for women and men and different age groups to give feedback on the project and improve future intervention.In addition, the Foundation will apply, if necessary, the practice of interaction through social networks. Persons affected by the conflict (men, women, boys and girls) will have equal access to feedback mechanisms to provide feedback on the assistance received and its appropriateness.

3 - Indicators & targets

During the implementation of the project it is planned to achieve the following goals1. 250 of the most vulnerable households located along the demarcation line will receive assistance in the form of agricultural equipment and tools, which will increase the living standards of the vulnerable population in the target agricultural sector, create conditions to help reduce dependence on humani-tarian support, as well as additional conditions for diversifying sources of income2. 6,000 food sets purchased and distributed. It is expected that the number of elderly people who will receive food packages will be 1,000 people with a frequency of 1 time in 2 months during the year. As a result of this activity, the saved money can actually be used to purchase medicines, hygiene products and necessary goods, turn into additional rescue intervention and will improve the life of the affected population.The total number of direct beneficiaries is 1250 people.
