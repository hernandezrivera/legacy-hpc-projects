Project Summary

AKF proposes a community-based approach to early childhood programming to ensure children, especially the most marginalized in Syria, have a good start in life. The purpose of the project is to scale-up community-based ECD as a complementary programmatic vehicle to traditional KG models – while at the same time strengthening the institutional measures (within the UN system and government) required to coordinate and support community-based ECD. The specific objectives of the project are: 1) to increase equitable access to quality education opportunities, with a particular focus on quality, holistic early childhood development for children age 0-6, and 2) to strengthen institutional capacity at the national and governorate levels to ensure emergency response in education, with a focus on key government decision makers and institutions in ECD. To achieve this, AKF will increase the capacity of communities, caregivers, and families through the establishment of community driven ECD models, and implementation of a national system strengthening programme to reach a total of 12,776 children and adults. Furthermore, this project will promote community and family-based ECD through a nation-wide awareness campaign, targeting 400,000 parents and caregivers, and more than 500,000 Children in the 6 governorates of Hama, Homs, Tartous, Aleppo, Damascus and Rural Damascus. Different dissemination platforms including homes, health centres, culture centres, schools, KGs, public institutions, religious entities, SMS, and social media will be used to strengthen and improve parental awareness and provide them with the required skills and tools to be able to deliver stimulating activities for their children.

4 - Needs

During the crisis, the system has failed to protect/promote the rights of young children to access quality and safe ECD. ECE registration reduced from 12% in 2010 to 8% in 2016; the total number of public and private kindergartens (KGs) dropped from 2,147 in 2012 to 1,490 KG in 2017 (out of which only 202 public KGs are still functioning according to the 2018 National Education Statistical Report from the Ministry of Education. 73% of formal ECE services are provided by private KGs. When coupled with the fact that 83% - 89% percent of the population lives below the poverty line (HNO 2019), very few, if any, vulnerable and poor households have access to these services. Parents interviewed in AKF’s Quality of Life Study (2017) reported challenges with accessing quality and affordable ECE services, and poor quality of teaching and learning in KGs (AKF Syria, 2017). The lack of qualified caregivers and teachers is mirrored at the national level alongside a lack of quality educational materials, out of date teaching techniques, lack of academic specialists, and limited ability to develop the administrative systems related to ECD according to the National ECD Strategy 2014-2020, published by the Syrian Commission for Family Affairs and Population. More dramatically: in order to reach 100% coverage to ECE, it is estimated that an additional 15,000 KG’s are be required just to provide physical access, and a herculean effort would be required to ensure that families have sufficient income to afford access / or governments provide access at little or no cost. Globally, there is a shortage of men in the ECE sector often driven by stereotypes and societal norms which construct that it is a women’s role to work with young children. AKF’s evaluations of its education programmes indicated that less than 10% of men were engaged in ECD activities and programming.

5 - Implementation of activities

To strengthen the capacity of key government decision makers and institutions in ECD, AKF will work in close partnership with the Ministry of Education and Regional Early Childhood Development Resource Center (ECDRC). Key activities include: (1) ECD training for a team of 16 Master Trainers (MTs) at MoE and ECDRC for education personnel; (2) capacity building of 14 newly established Teachers Recourse Centers (TRC) in Hama, Homs, Tartous, Damascus, rural Damascus and Aleppo to conduct training and workshops for formal KG teachers; (3) training for 650 pre-primary education personnel, incorporating active learning, child protection, and gender responsive care by the Ministry MTs; (4) workshop on “ECD for policy makers” for 40 nominated key decision makers from relevant ministries to deepen understanding of ECD and develop an action plan to contribute to the National ECD Strategy; and (5) conducting studies and research to inform the National ECD Strategy.To scale community-based ECD platforms for the most marginalized children in all targeted geographies, key activities include: (1) establishment of 36 Family Centers (FCs) (6 in each location) to offer a basic and scalable package of child development programmes with the objective of promoting ECD practices among parents and communities; (2) establishment of one Community Based Child Centre (CBCC) in each governorate to provide enhanced non-formal education for ECD aged children and support newly-formed home-based ECD spaces; and (3) promotion of community and family-based ECD through a nation-wide awareness campaign targeting 400,000 parents and caregivers and 500,000 children. A total of 126F teachers and facilitators will be provided with teaching materials in formal and non-formal settings. Process and outcome monitoring activities under the project will include baseline and endline assessments of the target group, ongoing feedback with project beneficiaries and FGDs with project stakeholders.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

A. CBCC facilitators selection committee might face pressure or even might be criticized or accused of bias in selecting the facilitators. likelihood: medium, impact: medium. Mitigation measures: AKF will consider various selection criteria to consider both qualification and social representation. AKF will expand reach to communities as much as possible, to increase the probability to find a qualified and agreed upon facilitators to lead the CBCC. B. Gender related cultural and traditional norms and roles that consider females are prime caregiver for children may discriminate/limit men participation in ECD initiatives. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Medium. Mitigation measures: AKF will conduct awareness session for fathers in addition to selecting a suitable time and space for fathers to participate. Also, AKF will aim to recruit male facilitators which might encourage fathers to participate. Besides, AKF will promote for the participation of both male and female parents in the project activities.C. Safety and Security concerns to find community places that are safe and easily accessible all community members especially females. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Medium. Mitigation measures: AKF will ensure safe and secure environment along with the availability of WASH facilities that is gender-sensitive when community mobilization activities such as SMS, campaigns, community meetings, and open days.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Delays in getting the governmental approvals and prolonged procedures to conduct MTs training at MoE especially that all departments of MoE will be occupied with the national schools’ exams for four months. Likelihood: Low. Impact: high. Mitigation: AKF has already start negotiations with MoE to get prior required approvals.Risk 2: Private KGs teachers already trained under the project might not continue to work at targeted KGs. Likelihood: High. Impact: high. Mitigation: AKF will sign a prior agreement with the Private KGs stating that they have to keep their staff who will be trained by AKF for at least 2 years.Risk 3: Low level of coordination for planned trainings on the level of administrative and logistic level. Likelihood: High. Impact: low. Mitigation: AKF will conduct the training at available spaces belong to the MoE. Also, AKF will coordinate with the ECDRC to use their centers in the governorates to conduct the trainings, along with the possibility to use one of the KGs to conduct the trainings.Risk 4: Lack of private KGs commitment to the application of the training in their teaching environment. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation: AKF will ensure that the signed agreement will include a commitment from KGs to apply the learning from the training. The national team will be responsible to follow up and monitor the application.Risk 5: Lack of interest and participation of the community in ECD. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation: AKF will conduct a number of community mobilization and awareness raising activities such as SMS, campaigns and community meetings.
