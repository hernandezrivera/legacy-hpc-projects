Project Summary

Summary Zimbabwe closed schools on March 24, 2020 as a precautionary measure to contain the spread of COVID-19 epidemic. The continued closure of schools has disrupted the education of more than 4.6 million children, with potentially detrimental effects not only on the hard-won educational achievements to date, but also on the physical, social and mental health and well-being of young people in primary and secondary schools. School closures are likely to exacerbate existing vulnerabilities and inequalities among children, especially girls, children with disabilities, those in rural areas, orphans and vulnerable children, as well as those from poor households and fragile families. Against this background, UNICEF plans to support continued access to quality learning opportunities through alternative learning programmes and mitigate against the adverse impact of the COVID-19 epidemic on learning and on learners, teachers and school communities. UNICEF plans to reach a total of 3.5 million teachers and learners, through the following programmatic interventions across the country’s 72 districts:
1. Development and implementation of radio programming as part of Alternative Learning Approaches.
2. Pre-positioning, development and distribution of supplementary learning materials and textbooks, including early childhood development (ECD) level story books for home learning.
3. Provision of Water, Sanitation and Hygiene kits, including (handwashing soap) to learners, teachers and staff in schools.
4. Provision of complimentary materials and supplies to ensure safe preparation and provision of food to learners.
5. Teacher capacitation to strengthen the technical and adaptive capacities necessary to deliver alternative learning programmes.
6. Development and dissemination of appropriate and targeted information, education and communication materials to enhance awareness and disease prevention efforts targeting learners, teachers and school communities, including persons with disabilities

Underlying each of these interventions is recognition of and respect for the right to quality education for all children. In keeping with the human rights-based approach to programming, UNICEF recognizes that a robust, resilient and efficient education system that can provide sustained, quality services- even in humanitarian situations- is critical for Zimbabwe to respond meaningfully to this pandemic and any future epidemic. For this reason, UNICEF will continue to use existing Ministry of Primary and secondary Education (MoPSE) structures to deliver programmes as part of its efforts to strengthen the education system and the capacities of duty bearers, enabling them to promote and protect the right to quality education for all children.

1 - Activities

Working as a co-lead of the Education in Emergencies cluster, UNICEF will contribute to sector priorities outlined in the Education Sector COVID-19 Preparedness and Response Strategy. Specifically, UNICEF will: 1. Preposition, develop and distribute supplementary learning materials and textbooks, including early childhood development (ECD) level story books for home learning for marginalized children who may not have access to radio lessons in hard to reach areas. Most of these children are in poor areas and bookless households and have increased risk of losing the literacy skills they have made over the years due to lack of access to quality reading materials. UNICEF will target 1,365,028 learners (680,318 females and 684,710 males) in 3,733 satellite and special primary and secondary schools. The materials will be translated to Braille and local languages.2. Provide technical and financial support to the MoPSE’s Curriculum Development Unit and the Technical Services (CDTS) department to develop and implement Alternative Learning options. Specifically, UNICEF will support the development of radio content, augment existing radio facilities and disseminate radio lessons to learners across the country. UNICEF intends to reach 2,638,390 learners (1,327,491 male and 1,310,899 female) in (P3) primary schools with radio lessons. UNICEF will also procure 3,000 radios for children in the most marginalized areas, especially those in rural areas, ensuring that they have learning opportunities and do not fall behind academically. 3. Provide technical and financial support to train teachers and enhance their capacities to deliver alternative learning options and implement safe hygiene practices to mitigate against the transmission of the epidemic. UNICEF will provide support to train teachers on Open and Distance Learning modalities and the development of radio lessons. UNICEF will implement trainings in person at cluster level and virtually, to enhance their capacities to deliver radio programming and behavior changes. 4. Provide age and disability appropriate Water, Sanitation and Hygiene kits, including (handwashing soap) to learners, teachers and staff in schools. The provision of kits and materials will ensure that children and teachers have consumables to practice safe hygiene practices such as handwashing to prevent the transmission of both COVID-19 and other water- and sanitation-related diseases such as Cholera, Typhoid and Dysentery. UNICEF will provide a total of 3,018 kits targeting 1,346,390 learners and 48,048 staff, including 42,031 teachers and 6,017 non-teaching staff. The provision of clean drinking water and basic sanitation for children will also augment efforts to improve nutrition outcomes. 5. Provide complimentary materials and supplies to ensure safe preparation and provision of food to learners. UNICEF will provide financial support for schools to procure additional materials and utensils to facilitate the safe storage and provision of food to children. These materials, including utensils such as spoons and folks, will enable schools to ensure safe distance between the children as they queue to get the food, in the eating areas, and discourage children from sharing utensils.6. Support communication, stakeholder engagement and buy-in through the development and dissemination of appropriate and targeted information. UNICEF will support efforts to communicate effectively with various key stakeholders, including teachers, school staff, parents, learners, communities and MoPSE personnel, to enhance awareness and disease prevention efforts. To that end, will provide materials such as posters and fliers. Further, UNICEF will support communication activities designed to create demand for COVID-19 education services such as radio lessons, engage stakeholders, and promote buy-in among key stakeholders including teachers, school staff, parents, learners, communities and national and sub-national MoPSE personnel.
