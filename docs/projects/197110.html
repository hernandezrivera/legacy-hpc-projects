Project Summary

After 11 years of crisis, Syria’s public health system and infrastructure remains fragile, which hinders access to quality healthcare and services all across Syria. The latter is worsened by the economic situation, coupled with the covid-19 and cholera outbreaks.
According to the Sector PiN, Homs, Hama and Idleb contain among the highest people in need of health assistance, whereby around 2,1 million are in need of health assistance in Idleb, around 700K in Homs and around 622k in Hama (HNO 2022).
Furthermore, SSSD aims to provide primary healthcare services to people in need through 3 mobile clinics in 3 governorates, namely Homs (Qusayr), Hama (Soran), and Idleb (Sanjar) for people in need of health assistance.
The starting point would be from SSSD’s health points, whereby the mobile clinics will go to hard-to-reach areas, where beneficiaries cannot access health facilities due to difficulties in transportation. Furthermore, primary healthcare services will be provided, including consultations, medicinal treatments, health screening, RH services, ECHO testing, health and GBV awareness-raising, through 3 mobile clinics (1 mobile clinic in each governorate), targeting 21,400 beneficiaries from consultations and preliminary screening, 12,000 beneficiaries from medicines_ including NCD treatments, and 32,400 beneficiaries from health and GBV awareness sessions. Each mobile clinic will have one internist, one gynecologist, one nurse, one midwife, one health educator, one GBV educator, 2 facilitators and one team leader. As for the visits, they will be conducted 6 days per week, from 9 a.m till 3 p.m.

3 - Needs

The fragile health system in Syria continues to face concurrent emergencies and chronic challenges which affect the availability and quality of health services across Syria, as well as the physical and mental wellbeing of the population.As per the 2022 HNO, a total of 12.23 million people is in need of health services in 2022, including 4.4 million displaced persons, 1.33 million children under 5 years, and 3.38 million women of reproductive age. Just over 500,000 older people will require inclusive health services, as well as those with early onset non-communicable diseases (NCDs) which are estimated to account for 45 per cent of all mortality in Syria. Regarding the areas of intervention, Al-Qusayr falls within a severity scale of 3, while Souran and Sanjar fall within a severity scale of 4 according to the 2023 health sector severity scale.Furthermore, through SSSD’s database from the health points, the following was deduced:The most common health complaints observed in the areas of intervention by the doctors at the health points were cardiovascular (mainly arterial hypertension, diabetes), and GU/RH followed by respiratory, gastrointestinal and musculoskeletal. Also, based on interviews with stakeholders conducted in mid-November, the team was told that a significant number of people are in need of health services, but cannot get the latter due to difficulties in transportation, as well as customs that do not allow women to go outside alone.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk1: Women and children do not have access to healthcare services due to the prevailing norms and traditions in the areas, whereby women are not allowed to take transportation alone. Mitigation measures: the services provided will be through mobile clinics, in order to reach women who cannot go to health facilities aloneRisk2: high cases of GBV. Mitigation measures: GBV awareness sessions will be conducted to decrease GBV casesRisk3: identity of beneficiaries might be exposed. Mitigation measures: SSSD de-identifies all data of beneficiaries in order to preserve their confidentiality and protect the beneficiaries.Risk 4: women are uncomfortable to get examined due to lack of privacy. Mitigation: in the areas of intervention, a room will be used to examine the women by a female gynecologist and midwife, in order to ensure the women are comfortable


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risks of SEA when the beneficiaries get the health services. Likelihood: medium. Mitigation measures: all staff will undergo a PSEA training. Furthermore, awareness sessions will be conducted to the beneficiaries, whereby they will be informed about the reporting mechanisms in case any incident occurs, and posters will be put up with the details of the reporting mechanisms (hotline, email, website to report any complaint).Also, women will be examined by a female gynecologist and midwife, in a separate room that will be rented in the areas of interventions, so that they won't feel uncomfortable or exposed.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk1: delay in obtaining approvals. Mitigation: taking into consideration the delay in obtaining official approvals in the implementation workplan, and prior coordination with the appropriate authorities to get the approval on time. Risk2: fluctuation in prices. Mitigations: the team will keep monitoring the change in prices, and procurement processes will be prepared early in order to make the procurements at the right time. Also, close coordination will take place with the donors in case any major fluctuations occur. Risk3: the areas may not be accessed. Mitigations: SSSD has access to the intervention areas. Risk4: another outbreak in covid or cholera takes place. Mitigations: conduct awareness sessions on IPC, as well as taking the precautionary measures while in contact with the beneficiaries
