Project Summary

The Logistics Cluster as a service-based cluster and provider of last resort aims to facilitate the timely delivery of humanitarian assistance to vulnerable populations through its logistics coordination support, information management, preparedness and provision of common services to the humanitarian community in South Sudan.

1 - Needs

In 2021, South Sudan will continue to suffer from the compounded shocks (flooding, escalations in subnational and localized violence and COVID-19 pandemic) in accumulation to the impacts caused by years of conflict, violence and limited development investment. 8.3 million people, over 65 percent of the total population, will require humanitarian assistance in 2021. Almost all counties have important needs related to nutrition, protection, and water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH). Additionally, uncertainties with regards to the evolution of the COVID-19 pandemic remain an important risk. South Sudan is one of the most logistically challenging countries requiring a coordinated logistics response to ensure timely and efficient delivery of relief items. Only 192 km of the country’s estimated 17,000 km of roads are paved, less than 2 percent. Two thirds of these roads become impassable during the rainy season, cutting people off from markets and basic services and delaying the delivery of humanitarian assistance due to increased reliance on costly air transport. The situation is further exacerbated with insecurity in many parts of the country. River transport along the White Nile, Bahr El-Jebel, Bahr Az Zeraf and the Sobat rivers carries a great potential for South Sudan, even though it is heavily dependent on seasonality and in dire need of improvements (majority of docking sites are hard to access and have limited capacity). As roads and ports significantly deteriorate during the rainy season, humanitarian organizations become reliant on costly air transport to reach populations in need and the quality of airstrips also remains low. The weak logistics landscape and lack of commercial service providers particularly in remote areas of the country as well as the numerous bureaucratic impediments faced by logisticians in South Sudan necessitates the presence of the Logistics Cluster to bridge logistical gaps and ensure the unimpeded flow of humanitarian supplies. The need for common logistics services is illustrated by the increasing amount of organizations supported over the past few years. Expansion of river and road transport modalities over the recent years have enabled the Logistics Cluster to reduce its reliance on air operations, including through the pre-positioning of relief items in strategic locations to ensure an uninterrupted supply chain throughout the year. However, roads have been massively affected (especially in Jonglei and Unity states) by both flooding emergencies in 2019 and 2020, shortening the pre-positioning window for humanitarian actors in 2021. As such, efforts to plan ahead, adjust pre-positioning strategies and improve logistics and cost efficiencies of the overall humanitarian response are still to be made to ensure a decreased reliance on costly air operations.

2 - Project activities

This project will facilitate the delivery of humanitarian assistance to affected persons by augmenting the capacities of the wider humanitarian community through logistics coordination, information management (IM), preparedness and provision of common services, where necessary, to respond to the crisis. The Logistics Cluster serves as a coordination body for humanitarian actors to identify logistical gaps and agree on the priorities for augmented logistical capacity. The cluster holds bi-monthly coordination meetings in Juba and monthly in its dispatch/reception hub locations (held online in 2020 due to the COVID-19 context), while also supporting partners in the deep field for more localized coordination (to be expanded with a dedicated field coordination position in 2021). Additionally, the Logistics Cluster participates in a total of seven coordination forums which include the ICCG, Civil Military Advisory Group, Humanitarian Access Working Group (chaired by OCHA), etc. The Logistics Cluster also works closely with other clusters/sectors to ensure logistics and cost efficiencies are considered by all humanitarian actors operating in South Sudan. The Logistics Cluster in South Sudan participates in the National Steering Committee on COVID-19, co-leading the Operations Support and Logistics pillar, coordinating closely with the government, WHO and other partners, as well as sharing information and guidance with partners. In terms of Information Management, the Logistics Cluster will support by collecting and sharing operational information. IM products are aimed at supporting operational decision making to improve the efficiency of the logistics response. Upon request, a Geographic Information System (GIS) specialist will support partners with mapping and other tailored GIS needs. The Logistics Cluster also identifies and consolidates information related to logistics bottlenecks/gaps and constraints such as tax exemptions, customs clearance, etc. The Logistics Cluster disseminates information both through a dedicated webpage where all its IM products are published and through its mailing list.The Logistics Cluster will also provide logistics preparedness services to the humanitarian partners. The cluster will aim at rolling out a digital data tool, the Preparedness Platform log:ie. In order to successful design and implement preparedness project in South Sudan, the Logistics Cluster will engage with key stakeholders in order re-launch the Preparedness Working Group, which will aim to look at systemic supply chain issues by bringing together stakeholders from the humanitarian community, government, private sector and academia. As part of preparedness efforts, the Logistics Cluster will continue to provide capacity building and training services to the entire humanitarian community operating in South Sudan, with a special focus on national actorsThe Logistics Cluster facilitates common logistics services, including storage and air, road, and river transport across South Sudan. The cluster will avail common warehousing space in its operational hubs and maintain a stock of Mobile Storage Units for deployment to augment storage capacities in deep-field locations. Based on the demand from its users, the Logistics Cluster will maintain a fleet of aircraft to transport cargo to remote areas inaccessible by road or river. As an alternative to air transport, the Logistics Cluster will also expand its river transportation services with regular barge trips between Bor and Renk and will utilize boats on the El Zharaf and Sobat Rivers. The cluster, in conjunction with IOM’s Common Transport Service project to truck supplies to locations surrounding Bentiu, Malakal, and Wau via the Beyond Responses, further reducing the reliance on air operations to locations reachable by road.

3 - Project indicators

The Logistics Cluster will measure its performance by reporting monthly to OCHA on the following indicators:• Number of organizations utilizing logistics and coordination services;• Number of information products shared with partners;• Number of cluster coordination meetings conducted• Annual performance survey for Logistics Cluster and UNHAS by National and International users• Quantity (mt) of humanitarian cargo moved through Logistics Cluster common services;• Percentage of humanitarian relief cargo moved by river and road versus by air;• Amount of common storage space made available by the Logistics Cluster to the humanitarian community.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

The Logistics Cluster is a Service Cluster and does not have populations as the direct beneficiaries of its services/support, but rather the humanitarian community itself. Due to the indirect relationship with affected populations, and lack of population programmes, the Logistics Cluster’s activities only indirectly impact beneficiaries and affect cross cutting issues.
