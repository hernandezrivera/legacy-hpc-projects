Project Summary

The project aims to improve food security and enhance the food production of vulnerable populations in the targeted counties. This will be achieved by providing Cash-Based Transfers (CBT) to address the food needs of vulnerable communities during the lean season and providing timely and appropriate complementary vegetable, crop and fishing kits coupled with training on improved climate-resilient agronomic and fishing practices. In line with the South Sudan FSL cluster HRP objectives and priorities, NPA will support vulnerable populations in IPC 4 and 5 locations and some IPC 3 locations. Specifically, NPA will target Canal/Pigi, Fangak and Bor South Counties of Jonglei State; Rumbek East, Rumbek Centre, Cueibet, Yirol East, Yirol West, Wulu and Awerial Counties of Lakes State; Terekeka County of Central Equatoria State and Kapoeta North and Budi Counties of Eastern Equatoria State.
NPA seeks to support 111,148 HHs (569,866 individuals) by providing timely and appropriate complementary vegetable, crop and fishing kits. Some kits will be provided by FAO (core pipeline) and NPA partnership projects, while others will be procured and distributed directly by NPA to enhance food production. NPA will build the capacity of the HHs supported with the kits through training on group cohesion and group dynamics, improved climate-resilient agronomic and fishing practices, post-harvest processing, value addition and marketing. A Training of Trainers (ToT) approach will be utilized whereby lead farmers and Lead fisher folk will be trained by NPA. Through farmer field schools, they will share their knowledge and skills with other farmers and fisher folk in their communities. The kits provided through this project and the knowledge shared through the training are expected to improve the agricultural and fishing practices of the target households, thereby contributing to food security and enhancing climate-resilient sustainable food production.
CBT interventions will target 3,000 HHs (18,000 individuals) in Rumbek East, Awerial, Cuiebet, Wulu, Yirol East and Yirol West Counties in Lakes State and 400 HHs (2,400 individuals) in Fangak, Canal / Pigi and Bor South Counties in Jonglei State, to improve access and consumption of diversified food. The planned interventions are aligned with the FSL cluster objectives 1 and 2. They will provide emergency assistance and livelihood support to save lives and avert the total collapse of livelihoods in the target counties. NPA will integrate humanitarian, development-oriented and conflict-sensitivity activities in this project to enhance the resilience of vulnerable populations in line with the Triple Nexus. Therefore, 2,125 vulnerable households will be supported with small-scale cash grants to establish Income Generating Activities. The target HHs will be sensitized to set VSLAs for easy loan access and enhanced saving skills among the VSLA group members. The IGAs will contribute to increasing and diversifying income and livelihood opportunities while building the resilience of the target households and strengthening market functionality.
NPA will employ a community-based beneficiary selection approach, which will entail establishing Project Management Committees (PMCs) to lead the targeting of eligible beneficiaries based on the beneficiary selection criteria and targeting guidelines. Vulnerable households facing severe food insecurity, including female-headed households, child-headed households, people living with disabilities, chronically ill-headed households and the elderly, will be targeted in this project. NPA will ensure meaningful engagement of the target communities throughout the project life-cycle through holding regular meetings with the PMCs and community members and conducting Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) to get their feedback on the project. NPA will ensure that the feedback is incorporated into the implementation of this project and the design of future projects.

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to face unprecedented and persistent humanitarian challenges. An estimated 2.2 million people are internally displaced and in need; at least 148,000 returnees remain displaced within the country and unable to reach their homes. An increased influx of returnees is being experienced in 2023 due to the conflict in Sudan. Multiple intersecting crises currently being experienced in South Sudan, including insecurity, subnational violence, emerging regional and national socioeconomic challenges exacerbated by the global economic slump and the effects of climate change, have a profound and adverse impact on food security in the country . South Sudan remains one of the most food insecure countries globally and in the East Africa region, with 60 percent of the population experiencing significant food consumption shortfalls, indicative of a crisis (IPC Phase 3) or an emergency (IPC Phase 4) . This challenging situation is further aggravated by the increasing prices of essential food items, influenced by high regional prices and disruptions in cross-border trade. In Fangak, Canal Pigi and Bor South counties of Jonglei State, persistent floods fuelled by climate change have intensified vulnerability and increased the risk of food insecurity, malnutrition, and SGBV against vulnerable women, men, boys, and girls. Following the fighting in Fangak between SPLM IO and Agwelek forces from August to September 2022, resulting in massive displacements and population movements, the humanitarian situation has deteriorated further, with needs increasing. Fighting and threats of attacks continue to disrupt the local population's capacity to engage in productive activities to sustain themselves. NPA monitoring reports reveal that poverty induced by recurrent widespread loss of productive assets caused by many years of conflict and flooding continues to be one of the major underlying causes of vulnerability to food insecurity for women, men, boys and girls. Furthermore, revenge killings, the proliferation of small arms and light weapons, and the lack of infrastructure continue to affect Fangak, Canal Pigi and Bor South counties. Interviews conducted in Old and New Fangak counties by NPA in October 2023 with The Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC) confirmed that 3,222 individual returnees are in Fangak and many families have been internally displaced from their fertile lands due to recurrent conflicts, drought and floods, resulting in widespread food insecurity and loss of livelihoods. Fangak and Canal Pigi counties are classified under the emergency food insecurity class (IPC level 4) . The increased food insecurity and loss of livelihoods have affected household income levels and intensified domestic tension, which in most cases is mitigated by resorting to harmful coping mechanisms to ensure the family's survival. These include reducing food portions and the number of meals, limiting food to older people to feed the children, and resorting to less preferred wild foods. Child labour, early/forced marriage, prostitution and unplanned selling of productive assets are also reported in Fangak and Canal Pigi counties . In Lakes State, persistent floods and droughts fuelled by climate change have intensified vulnerability and increased the risk of food insecurity, malnutrition, and SGBV against vulnerable women, men, boys, and girls. Conflict and threats of violence continue to disrupt the local population's capacity to engage in productive activities to sustain themselves. Rumbek North, Rumbek Centre and Cueibet counties are projected at Emergency level (IPC Phase 4) despite the harvest beginning in October, mainly as a result of marginal crop production, high staple food prices, and a lack of resources to support the high numbers of returnees. 2. Project activities

Activity 1 - Project launch and community mobilization: NPA will meet with project staff, local authorities, key stakeholders and community representatives to discuss the project objectives, implementation strategies approach and plan, distribution plans, etc. Activity 2 - Formation and training of Project Management Committees (PMCs): NPA will facilitate the formation of Boma-level PMCs. Each PMC will comprise 10 (5 women, 5 men) members who will be trained and empowered to be a voice to and for the targeted beneficiaries and participate in beneficiary selection and monitoring of the project implementation.Activity 3 - Beneficiary selection and sensitization on the project activities: Under the guidance of NPA based on selection criteria and targeting guidelines, the PMCs will identify and register the beneficiaries targeting food-insecure households with a focus on women/child/chronically ill-headed households, People Living with Disabilities (PLWD), widows, elderly, pregnant/lactating women, orphaned etc. This will ensure the prioritization of assistance towards those who need it most. The selected beneficiaries will be sensitized on the project implementation modalities, entitlements, duration of assistance, etc. At least 60% of the target HHs will be female-headed. Activity 4 -Provision of unconditional Cash-Based Transfers (CBT) to 3000 severely or highly vulnerable households to meet their immediate food needs during the lean season: The cash transfer values will be based on the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) as established by the South Sudan JMMI at the time of the transfer coordinated by REACH South Sudan. Cash transfer will be done by a Financial Service Provider (FSP) who will disburse cash according to the schedule provided by NPA and report to NPA upon completion of the activities. Activity 5 - Distribution of complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs and fishing kits: NPA will distribute Crop kits (Maize, Sorghum, Groundnuts and Cowpea), vegetable kits and tools (Amaranth, Collard, Okra, Tomato, Onion, Watermelon, Pumpkin and Maloda) and Fishing kits (hooks, monofilament and spools of twine) to the 111,148 targeted households. FAO (core pipeline) will provide some of the kits, while the rest will be procured and distributed directly by NPA to enhance food production. Activity 6 - Formation and training of farmers and fisher folk groups: NPA will encourage and facilitate the formation of farmer and fisher folk groups to promote teamwork and peer-to-peer exchange. ToT training on improved agronomic practices, fish preservation, marketing and group dynamics will be provided to 3,700 model farmers and fisher folk. The trained farmers and fisherfolk will then sensitize and build the capacity of the other farmers and fisherfolk in their communities. Activity 7 - Provision of cash grants to 150 Income Generating Activity (IGA): The groups will be trained on business plan development and supported with cash grants for investment in business enterprises. The amount of cash grants to be disbursed will be informed by the nature and type of businesses based on their business plans. Activity 8 - Monitoring and reporting: NPA will conduct routine monitoring activities to gather project progress data and submit the required reports. NPA will also conduct PDM after every distribution of cash, vegetable and crop livelihood inputs and fishing kits to ascertain the appropriateness of the response. Outcome monitoring will be done periodically to assess the changes in the target communities due to the project. Feedback from the beneficiaries, lessons learnt and best practices will be used to inform subsequent project activities and design future projects.
