Project Name

Start Date

01/01/2021

31/12/2021

Project Summary

Building on MdM experience implementing life-saving humanitarian interventions since 2015, MdM will target 85,724 beneﬁciaries through the provision of multi-disciplinary quality healthcare services for the most vulnerable people affected by the crisis and exacerbated by COVID-19 pandemic in Eastern Ukraine (Luhanska and Donetska Oblasts, GCA and NGCA). The project will particularly target vulnerable women and girls affected by on-going conﬂict by ensuring access to Sexual and Reproductive Health services and access to GBV prevention and response services. The project will also contribute to addressing the high level of mental health problems, as well as COVID-19 related additional mental and psychological burden on the population and healthcare workers, among internally displaced persons and host populations in conﬂict affected areas.
In order to achieve this objective, MdM will target the most vulnerable communities and categories of people affected by the conﬂict (such as women and girls, elders, people with disabilities, people exposed to conﬂict -related stressors).
The outreach approach in remote locations of Luhanska and Donetska GCA close to the line of contact will ensure access to PHC, treatment and referral to specialized care with due respect to the COVID-19 prevention measures. Outreach teams will promote a comprehensive approach by providing PHC, MHPSS and SRH services and will be integrated within the existing health facilities.
The local health system will also be supported through targeted donations of supplies and equipment, including life-saving COVID-19 equipment and consumables, and health staff will be trained (with a speciﬁc focus on SRH and MHPSS) to face the current lack of capacity building opportunities in conflict-affected oblasts. The training plan will be developed in line with the ongoing Health Reform and by creating synergies with public training institutes and/or hospitals. Modalities and operational design of the capacity building events will fit the reality based on the current epidemiological situation in the area.
Building up on MdM experience in the area MdM will continue supporting local capacities to provide a comprehensive package of services to survivors of GBV, which was dramatically increased in the conflict affected areas, including the most vulnerable populations, and raising awareness on health and mental health consequences of GBV, promoting the development of a sustainable referral pathway.
MdM mental health and psychosocial interventions based on evidence-based methodology will be expanded and disseminated to support the needs of persons who deal with psychological problems such as stress, fear, feelings of helplessness, as well as practical readjustment challenges related to conﬂicts within families, issues with communities and livelihood diﬃculties. Moreover, MDM will provide crucial mental health and PSS assistance for health workers who faced COVID-19 related stress, etc.
MdM advocacy efforts will continue to ensure the integration of MHPSS into PHC in the framework of current Health Reform. In Luhanska NGCA, where MdM has been registered since 2017, MdM will continue to support PHC facilities close to the contact line, which have been particularly affected by the conﬂict. MdM will pursue donations of life-saving medical equipment to Emergency departments of hospitals with a focus on Children’s health and SRH. The distribution of maternity and new-born kits initiated in 2019 will be expended. In addition, through COVID-19 related donations of medical equipment, supply and consumables MDM will support the health system in Donetska and Luhanska NGCA.
To ensure the response and considering the relatively costly actions (donation of medical equipment, etc), MdM has an efficient operational presence in Donetska and Luhanska GCA, and Luhanska NGCA. For 2021 and strategically, MdM is in the process of negotiations to have access to and presence in Donetska NGCA.

1 - Needs

Despite the conﬂict being now in a protracted state with peaks of violence and ceasefire agreements and violations, humanitarian needs remain at a high level on areas bordering the LoC and are exacerbated by the COVID-19 impact. The lack of health care providers in areas close to the LoC (0-20 km) is a major factor reducing the availability of health care in these areas: there is a shortage of 40% of doctors in Luhansk and 25 % in Donetsk GCA (HC, 7/2019). The conﬂict has increased distances and travel times to primary health and maternity facilities particularly in rural areas. Previously accessible healthcare facilities in Donetsk and Luhansk city areas are now inaccessible to populations west of the LoC. Inaccessibility and unaffordability of health care and medications lead to the worsening of the health conditions and increased acute health events, for patients with chronic diseases. Lack of treatment is life threatening for patients suffering from NCDs which is extremely common among people older than 60 years-old (>65% of beneﬁciaries of MdM outreach teams). In the current setting of conﬂict and displacement, isolation due to COVID-19 restrictions, aggression, violence, disrupted support social networks and incessant exposure to danger contribute to psychological stress and mental health disorders and deteriorate social resilience. Older people are often socially isolated and also need psychosocial support. Stigma associated with mental disorders often deters people from seeking help. Those who do seek treatment may not know where to turn and may seek help from family doctors who do not have the knowledge and skills to help or take medications without consulting with a professional. Access to secondary and tertiary health facilities for curative or diagnostic purposes are limited due to the inability of patients to cover the ﬁnancial cost of long travel distance, immobility of some clients and lack of ﬁnancial opportunities to cover specialized services. Older people are in need of more health services due to health issues, but due to different reasons, they have limited ability to use them. Through the existing program, MdM found out that 60+ with disabilities experience substantial barriers to accessing health care. Based on the COVID-19 Needs Assessment (March 2020) conducted by MDM in 5 reference hospitals for COVID-19 response there were recorded specific needs. The main findings of the assessment are critical shortage and limited supplies of PPE and disinfectants; the insufficient number of health workers and vulnerability of the health staff (about 70% aged 60+); the insufficient quantity of emergency medical equipment, poor capacities of labs for testing on COVID-19, including lack of reagents, rapid test systems, and materials for PCR samples collection and storage, shortage of medications, especially for the treatment of acute states caused by COVID-19. Specifically to explore the situation with contact tracing, MdM conducted several interviews with PHC staff. The detected problems were similar for both rural and city PHC centers: 1. A total shortage of PPE, which significantly reduces the capacity of PHC staff to conduct home visits to the patients in self-isolation or suspected of COVID-19. 2. Lack of rapid tests does not allow to conduct required testing at patients’ home as the testing responsibility is on the Secondary health care level. 3. A critical issue is a gap of interlevel coordination in the health care system when two different structures are involved into the process of contact tracing. 4. Lack of PHC staff motivation in COVID-19 response. 5. Poor transport capacity of PHC reduces the number of home visits, especially in rural areas. 6. The number of existing PHC staff will be unable to duly conduct the tracing in case of high scale outbreak. This issue is extremely critical for rural areas due to the general deficit of the health staff.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

Considering age and gender proﬁles, access to healthcare is essential to save lives and reduce the use of negative coping strategies and exposure to risk among the local population, MdM will reach out to most vulnerable communities affected by the conﬂict via its community health outreach teams, composed of a doctor, a midwife, a nurse and a psychologist with PHC, SRH, and MHPSS services (around 20 remote locations in Popasna and Stanitsa-Luhanska districts, Luhansk GCA, and in around 12 locations in Bakhmut district, Donetsk GCA, within 20 km from the LoC). Outcome 1. Increased access to quality PHC, including SRH and MHPSS services for conflict-affected population in Donbass region, Eastern Ukraine (GCA and NGCA). A1: Deliver direct gender responsive healthcare services through community outreach, including primary healthcare, SRH and MHPSS services in communities with little or no access to healthcare, provide free-of-charge medications and facilitate referrals for patients facing barriers in accessing relevant specialized healthcare services (Luhansk GCA, Donetsk GCA) A2: Implementation of gender-appropriate awareness sessions on PHC, SRH and MHPSS at the supported health facilities and communities close to the LOC (GCA) A3: Distribution of maternity and new-born kits targeting mothers and new-borns residing close to the LoC in Luhansk NGCA Outcome 2. Improved capacities of the communities and local health system in the provision of comprehensive healthcare services (including SRH and MHPSS) for the vulnerable population in conﬂict affected areas of Donbass Region (GCA and NGCA) A1: Capacity building and technical supervision of primary health care staff and of community outreach workers on PHC, SRH, MHPSS and GBV topics (incl. GBV Response Focal Points and community representatives who are conducting home visits to disabled/elderly persons) A2. Support health and social facilities affected by the conﬂict through donations of medical supplies and equipment (GCA and NGCA) A3: Strengthening the local health system in the implementation of the health reform (GCA) A4: Provide community based MHPSS evidence-based services (GCA, NGCA) A5: Conduct rehabilitation works in community based health facilities in Donetsk GCA A6: Community-based MHPSS activities in locations near the LoC targeting the local population in Luhansk GCA and visitors from the NGCA A7: Reduce the transmission of COVID-19 by supporting health authorities to implement and carry out contact tracing of confirmed cases as well as their contacts A8: Provision of life-saving medical equipment for intensive care services (oxygenators, ventilators etc.). A9: Provision of refresher training on proper use of PPE for health workers at PHC and secondary level & refresher training for health staff on Infection Prevention and Control (MdM) + social facilities, and MHPSS aspects of COVID-19 response A10: Provision of Personal protective Equipment (PPE) and infection prevention and control (IPC) supplies for PHC, secondary health facilities and social institutions A11: Establish a system of community messaging through community health focal points to engage local communities in hand washing, respiratory hygiene, physical distancing practices, and psychological self-help Outcome 3. The awareness of national and regional stakeholders is raised through advocacy and evidence-based communication A1: Organize advocacy events at national and local levels, to ensure that the implementation of the health reform is in line with the needs of conﬂict-affected population A2: Participate in national and regional working groups to engage and contribute to the coordination efforts in Donbass Region A3: Support advocacy activities to advocate for the needs in MHPSS, SRH, GBV related services and comprehensive healthcare services for conﬂict affected populations to be addressed through raising awareness and informing target audiences on these issues
