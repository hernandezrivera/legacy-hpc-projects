Project Summary

A comprehensive approach to improving educational services in remote rural (Amvrosiivskyi, Boikivskyi, Marinskyi, Novoazovskyi, Shakhtarskyi, Starobeshivskyi, Volnovaskyi) schools including:
• Providing school uniforms to first-graders;
• Vocational guidance for ninth, tenth and eleventh grade students;
• Organization of first aid courses;
• Providing access to distance education to rural schools;
• Providing transport for transportation of children to rural schools.
"Donbass Development Сenter" uses the accumulated experience in the implementation of education cluster programs.

1 - Needs

The military conflict in the Donbas continues for five years, accompanying by a humanitarian crisis. The region lost many professionals in the field of education due to the increased migration rates, which negatively affected the staff training and functioning of entire industries, especially remote state-financed organizations. Many schools building were destroyed as a result of shelling: there were 31 attacks of educational institutions in the ten months of 2019. The region is unable to support its own infrastructure and depends on external assistance. In case of its absence, the number of refugees and IDPs from NGCA will increase due to the desire of parents to provide acceptable educational conditions to their children. Young people, who got poor basic education will not be either able, not interested to find a more suitable specialization, but will be forced to engage in the ongoing military conflict in the region and will be recruited into armed groups, thereby contributing to the escalation of violence in the region and further aggravating its humanitarian situation. Provision of humanitarian assistance in the field of education allows reducing the consequences of conflict negative impact, reducing the needs for further assistance, socializing and demarginalizing the inhabitants of NGCA. The education cluster estimates that about 143,000 people are in need of better educational services at the NGCA in Donetska oblast — information in the public domain. Based on objective socio-geographical factors, the most vulnerable social subgroup are the inhabitants of rural settlements, including those locates in the 20km area along the ‘contact line’. At the moment, the number of rural schools located on the NGCA of Donetska oblast is estimated at 70 institutions, in which study about 7200 students according to data of de facto authorities. Thus, the indirect beneficiaries of the project will be about 25 000 people from 110 000 living in rural areas: children, their guardians, educational staff. These people are not provided with the necessary equipment for quality education and even learning materials. The introduction of infrastructure and practices of distance education are of utmost importance. They allow to form the competence of students in remote education, allowing them to get education without necessity to leave the place of their permanent residence, in this way reduce the risks of movement in the areas of shelling, mining and remote rural areas. Due to the damaged road infrastructure and lack of school transport, children are forced to reach schools on their own, which negatively affects their health and attendance, especially in winter and during periods of intensification of the attacks. Schools that have transport do not have the means to repair and maintain it in a timely manner, causing the above those transport fractures. Graduates of rural schools do not have an understanding of their future fate, the mechanics of admission to post-secondary education institutions. TVET`s and universities are either concentrated in the cities of the NGCA, or are located on the GCA across the ‘contact line’. Due to their small age, they are not able to cross the ‘contact line’; many of them do not have identity documents of the Ukrainian sample. Based on this, potential applicants from rural areas of the NGCA face many obstacles in their entrance, including lack of consultations that are necessary to plan their future actions in the labor market. This situation impacts negatively on realization of young people`s potential in NGCA. Its resolution will create conditions for sustainable improvement of the socio-economic situation thereby reducing the long-term humanitarian needs of the population. This project is aimed at providing sustainable humanitarian assistance to these educational institutions. Its development were taken into account data published in the Education Cluster Strategy, Cluster Survey and in the public domain.

2 - Activities & outcomes

Within the project it is planned: Activity 1: To reduce social inequality by providing humanitarian aid in the composition of school uniform for first-grade students in rural schools. To contribute to sustainable development of children living in rural areas of the NGCA through their timely vocational guidance. Give them (and their pedagogical and parent communities) the knowledge necessary for safe living in the conflict zone as part of the first aid courses Activity 2: To monitor the availability of distance education in rural areas, publish its results in the public domain. To facilitate young people in their transition from school to the labor market by preparing and publishing a report with identified needs based on the results of the vocational guidance. Activity 3: To improve the quality and security of educational services by providing transport, equipment for the Internet access to the schools in remote rural areas. Through an integrated approach, significantly improve the quality of education in rural areas.

3 - Indicators & targets

Objective 1: Provide school uniforms to first-graders from rural areas. Number of beneficiaries: 450 (#of girls and boys benefiting from supplied education, ECD and recreation kits or learning materials or related cash interventions) minors, including 229 girls and 221 boys in 70 educational institutions. Objective 2: Improving the quality and security of education in rural areas through first aid courses and vocational guidance, allowing more effective transition to the labor market. Number of beneficiaries: 1,050 (#of girls and boys benefiting from provision of life skills and SEL) minors, including 535 girls and 515 boys in 70 educational institutions. Objective 3: Support distance education programs and methods by providing trainings, consultations and equipment to access the Internet. Number of beneficiaries: 1350 (#of girls and boys benefiting from distance learning programs/modalities supported by Education Cluster partners) minors, including 689 girls and 661 boys in 15 educational institutions. Objective 4: Provide transport to attend school to children from remote settlements of NGCA. Number of beneficiaries: 380 (#of girls and boys benefiting from provision of equipment to education facilities), including 92 girls and 88 boys in 5 educational institutions.
