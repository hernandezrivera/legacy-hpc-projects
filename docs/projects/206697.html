Project Summary

UNHCR, as the protection lead agency of the protection cluster, will continue implementing core protection activities in northern Mozambique (while adapting them to the changing context), including providing civil documentation; legal assistance; housing, land and property (HLP) assistance; GBV prevention and response; child protection; community-based protection (CBP); protection capacity-building of duty- and rights-bearers; and protection monitoring, while ensuring accountability to affected populations, PSEA safeguards, localization, and age/gender/diversity approach that provides specialized responses for the most vulnerable groups, including women and girls, older people, people with disabilities, and other people with specific needs.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

This project seeks to address the protection needs and risks of displaced, returned, and host communities in northern Mozambique, and seek durable solutions for them, with accountability to affected populations (AAP), an inclusive age/gender/diversity (AGD) approach, and localization mainstreamed throughout. The project consists of the following components: GPGeneral protection activities include civil documentation assistance, legal assistance, community-based protection (CBP), protection capacity-building, protection monitoring, and accountability to affected populations. HLPHLP activities include HLP legal assistance, land tenure security assistance, HLP due diligence, HLP legal advocacy, and HLP capacity-building of duty- and rights-bearers. GBV:Activities aim to establish vital GBV responses, focusing on risk mitigation and prevention in IDP sites, transit, urban, and hard-to-reach contexts. Adopting a mobile approach, GBV services will be flexible and widespread, conducting safety audits to tailor responses to each community's needs. Capacity-building, following the Community-Based Protection (CBP) and Age, Gender, and Diversity (AGD) approach, will empower and build capacity of Protection Focal Points (PFP) and community volunteers. CP:The project aims to strengthen child protection in the provinces of Nampula and Niassa, specifically focusing on effective identification, prioritization, assessment, and referral of children at risk integrating child protection services and prioritize the registration and issuance of civil documentation, including birth certificates for unregistered children. The project also emphasizes targeting separated and unaccompanied children, with a tailored approach for different age groups, UNHCR aims to improve overall well-being through life skills support and community engagement pf the targeted populations. The initiative reinforces community-based child protection mechanisms for prevention and response, prioritizing adolescents and caregivers. Gender-sensitive strategies are employed, and host community needs are also acknowledged for inclusivity and cohesion.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

CROSS-CUTTING:Protection is at the core of every UNHCR intervention in Mozambique and globally. Through its community-based and AAP approaches, which involve regular community engagement (CE), two-way communication with communities, and the implementation of complaint and feedback mechanisms (CFM), and through its leadership of the Cabo Delgado CE/AAP Working Group, UNHCR ensures people are at the center of its interventions, providing feedback before, during, and after, mitigating the risk of acting in a way that causes harm or is not in the best interest of the population. Risks are identified through various means, including protection monitoring, participatory assessments, GBV safety audits, complaints and feedback mechanisms, partners and community activists trained on identification and referrals of people with specific needs and facing protection risks, consultations with affected population, two-way communication with communities and community engagement. ___________________________________________________Risks addressed by General Protection response:• Lack of civil documentation• Lack of access to justice• Conflict/tensions between communities• Lack of knowledge of rights• Lack of access to services and assistance by vulnerable groups______________________________________________Risks addressed by HLP response:• Forced eviction• Illegal occupation• Illegal expropriation• Land conflicts between communities• Lack of community knowledge of HLP rights___________________________________________________Risks addressed by GBV response:• Rape• Sexual Assault• Physical Violence (including Intimate partner/former partner violence) • Forced Marriage• Denial of Resources, Opportunities and Services• Psychological/Emotional violence In addition to categorizing the above six classes, this project has also recognized risks associated with Trafficking and Exploitation, as well as LGBTQ+ Discrimination and Violence, extending its focus to other vulnerable populations, including men, women, girls, and boys who are at risk of experiencing GBV.____________________________________________________Risks addressed by child protection response:• Unaccompanied and separated children• Child Abuse and neglect• Exploitation and Child labour• Trafficking• Child Marriage • Family separation• Lack of access to education• Psychosocial distress/traumatic Experience• Lack of access to civil documentation/basic needs

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Cross-cutting:UNHCR, with a dedicated GBV Unit of national and international staff, has institutional expertise and GBV technical capacity supporting the project (across all its components) to ensure that the safe and inclusive participation and empowerment of women and girls is mainstreamed across all project activities. Women and girls, as well as older people, people with disabilities, and other people with specific needs, will be consulted via participatory methodologies, ensuring that an age, gender and diversity (AGD) lens is applied across all activities according to UNHCR’s AGD Policy. Furthermore, all project staff and partners are trained on GBV/PSEA core concepts and referrals. GBV risk mitigation is mainstreamed in all UNHCR interventions across all sectors/clusters, as required by UNHCR’s Policy on the Prevention of, Risk Mitigation and Response to Gender-based Violence (2020).___________________________________________General Protection:• Through its CBP component, UNHCR dedicated programs for assisting people with disabilities (PwDs) and older people; chairs the Cabo Delgado Disability Working Group; and provides of capacity-building to duty- and right-bearers for protection and inclusion of PwDs and older people.• Through its legal component, it ensures inclusion of PwDs and older people in legal assistance, civil documentation assistance, and protection monitoring. ______________________________________________HLP:Through its HLP capacity-building of duty- and rights-bearers, HLP advocacy, land tenure security assistance, HLP legal assistance, and HLP due diligence, all community members benefit equally, regardless of age, gender, or vulnerability. HLP advocacy and capacity-building in particular, count on a specific module regarding women’s HLP rights, in an attempt to counter harmful, gender-biased customary land practices._______________________________________________GBV:The proposed GBV project ensures meaningful access to assistance by employing a survivor-centered approach, emphasizing the identification of heightened protection risks across diverse demographics, with a focus on women and elderly individuals. The 'do no harm' principle and an age, gender, and diversity approach guarantee inclusive engagement. Mainstreaming risk mitigation, in line with the UNHCR Policy on the Prevention of, Risk Mitigation and Response to GBV, 2020, recognizes GBV services as lifesaving and further ensures accessible assistance. Through extensive training and collaboration with clusters, the project integrates GBV considerations into various sectors, fostering participation and empowerment. This comprehensive strategy exemplifies UNHCR's commitment to robust protection measures, specifically addressing the unique needs of women and the elderly.__________________________________________________Child protection:The project ensures meaningful access to assistance, with a focus on promoting participation and empowerment, particularly for women and elderly people. By prioritizing the identification and mitigation of risks faced by children, including unaccompanied and separated minors, the project employs a comprehensive approach. It emphasizes collaboration with communities to foster engagement and empowerment, ensuring that the specific needs of women and elderly individuals are addressed. Through initiatives such as family reunification, psychosocial support, and education programs, the project aims to create a protective environment that actively involves and empowers individuals, fostering their well-being and development.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Cross-cutting:UNHCR aims at engaging and consulting people with disabilities through an AGD approach in order to identify the specific barriers they face and ensure disability inclusion in the provision of life-saving assistance, including the provision of assistive devices and rehabilitation services, in addition to referring them to specialized services. People with disabilities are given a distinct voice in UNHCR’s participatory assessments and protection monitoring, ensuring their influence over decisions that affect their lives._________________________________________________General protection:Through its CBP component, the project includes specialized assistance for people with disabilities (including assistive devices and rehabilitation services), and capacity-building of rights- and duty-bearers for inclusion of people with disabilities in community decision-making processes and protection and assistance mechanisms, respectively. UNHCR also chairs the Cabo Delgado Disability Working Group. Protection monitoring and participatory assessments also organize dedicated FGDs with people with disabilities to understand their specific protection needs, and ensure they are mainstreamed in all protection responses._________________________________________________HLP:Through its HLP capacity-building of duty- and rights-bearers, HLP advocacy, land tenure security assistance, HLP legal assistance, and HLP due diligence, all community members benefit equally, regardless of age, gender, or vulnerability, including people with disabilities, ensuring their secure access to land.____________________________________________GBV:The initiative collaborates with communities to involve persons with disabilities in GBV program design. UNHCR co-chairs the Persons with Disabilities Working Group, coordinates Cabo Delgado responses, ensuring safe spaces and specialized GBV services for accessibility. Current services for survivors with disabilities include distributing mobility aids, with the proposed project enhancing GBV support through case worker training.______________________________________________Child protection:The Child Protection (CP) project emphasizes collaboration with affected communities, actively engaging persons with disabilities in program design and implementation. Safe spaces will prioritize accessibility, and CP referral pathways will include specialized services for persons with disabilities, ensuring enhanced access. UNHCR provides training for staff and community volunteers to actively support persons with disabilities, fostering community-led assistance.The proposed CP project will expand training for case workers to better support children with disabilities, adapting established training packages to ensure inclusivity and comprehensive assistance.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

UNHCR ensures environmental considerations across the project. The project is not material assistance or infrastructure based so the risks are less significant. However, materials used for safe spaces are local, natural, and sustainable, the work on the spaces is conducted directly within the community. Also, the project tries to ensure sustainability and localization of all inputs including furnishings for safe spaces and re-usable material masks for project participants.
