Project Summary

Integrated WASH service delivery is essential in alleviating the immerse WASH needs of the vulnerable and affected populations in South Sudan. The IDPS, returnees and host communities suffer lack of WASH services and that can be associated with flooding and adverse climatic conditions in some counties in South Sudan.
Tonj East and Wau Counties are having Immerse WASH needs and are in need of urgent which needs integrated WASH services to alleviate the suffering of the vulnerable populations.
The two counties are in IPC phase 4 and 5 therefore require urgent WASH intervention to ensure that the vulnerable and affected populations have access to safe drinking water, sanitation facilities, protection mainstreaming is observed and malnourished children in the counties have access to clean water for consumption.
Community Advocacy and Relief Organization will proposes to cater for WASH needs of the vulnerable population in the two counties by integrating WASH in nutrition by provision of clean and safe water in the nutrition centers and supplying WASH NFIs and WASH kits to Couples with Malnourished children (Caretaker/child). This is because it has been proven that WASH needs aggravate or contribute to malnutrition.
Community advocacy and Relief Organization will also ensure access to sanitation facilities by offering temporally sanitation facilities to the vulnerable and affected populations in the target counties. Temporary water solutions will be offered by light rehabilitation of the water points and formation of water management committees.
Community Advocacy and Relief Organization will offer integrated WASH and protection needs by conducting safe audits during rehabilitation of the Water and sanitation facilities as well as distribution of MHM kits and ensure that the sanitation facilities are safe for women and girls by having locks that will ensure their safety during use of the sanitation facilities.
CARO will ensure that there are safety audits done to mitigate protection risks for women and girls due to distance for WASH and sanitation facilities and also ensure that distributions are done on time and brought as near as possible to ensure that beneficiaries are not exposed to SGBV during survive delivery.
By provision of clean and safe water for drinking and for domestic use in the nutrition centers we shall be reducing the risk of getting infections related to poor WASH conditions thus improving the recovery of the children with nutrition.
By rehabilitation of Water and sanitation facilities for the affected populations, affected populations will be enabled to concentrate on food security and livelihood activities that will improve the food security situation in the affected counties.
The benefits expected from the activities is aim at providing equality and safe WASH services regard less of gender, age or disability and conductive environment for all the beneficiaries of the project. The project will ensure safe access to the facilities by ensuring that there are safety audits done before the project implementation and also establish referral pathways for survivors of SGBV.
All the WASH facilities will be disability inclusive to ensure that people with disabilities are able to access the facilities without much trouble.
Project will target 6864 vulnerable and affected populations in the three counties with of which 1442 will be children under five with malnutrition, 2657 women and 2765 men. 1029 will be people with disabilities and other special needs to ensure that the project is disability inclusive.

1 - Needs

South Sudan has been having high WASH needs. This can be attributed to years of political unrest and conflict among communities that have destroyed WASH infrastructure in the community. WASH needs continue to increase over the years as signing of peace agreement in 2018 and the formation of transitional government of national unity have seen influx of returnees in the country. The increased population has strained the available WASH facilities.The country was devastated by ravaging floods caused by abnormally high rainfall that has also contributed immensely in destruction of WASH facilities contamination of water and thus exposure to Water related infections as reported by health partners. (South Sudan Flood Snapshot issue 3 2021) The country is experiencing severe food insecurity with all the three target counties being in IPC 4 an emergency situation (South Sudan IPC Report Oct 2020) and high GAM rates of above WHO emergency threshold of 15% apart from Kajokeji which has been experiencing internal conflict and displacements (Nutrition Cluster Proxy GAM rates Jan 2021). This places the vulnerable children who are malnourished to dangers of high morbidity and mortality from WASH related infections like cholera and Acute Watery DiarrheaWomen and school girls are exposed to protection threats as they walk long distances in search of water and as they look for sanitation facilities. They also lack essential menstrual hygiene management kits that are very important for safety and dignity of women and girls. This has led to school dropouts among the vulnerable and affected girls as well as unhygienic practices by childbearing women that can expose them to infections.COVID 19 pandemic has further strained the WASH needs in the two counties as prevention of infection protocol requiring quality WASH infrastructure and commodities to support hygienic living and practices.Water points are dilapidated and competition for water resource has often led to increased conflict among the communities calling for the need to urgently rehabilitate the available water points and point water committees that can manage the waterpoints leading to peaceful coexistence among conflicting CARO will therefore implement integrated WASH activities to ensure that the vulnerable and affected populations are able to get enough water for drinking and domestic use as per the sphere standards, sanitation facilities and safe menstrual hygiene management for women and girls.

2 - Project activities

1. Integrate WASH and Nutrition In the three counties which are having High GAM rates and highly food insecure by doing the following activities:• Rehabilitation of water points in the Nutrition centers• Hygiene promotion for caretakers of children admitted with SAM and MAM in Nutrition facilities• Distribution of WASH NFIs and Hygiene kits to couples(Caregiver/child)admitted with SAM and MAM in nutrition centers2. Integrate WASH in health facilities in the three counties and building resilience to flood affected populations by doing the following activities• Rehabilitation of semi-permanent water points after safety audits in health facilities and in the community affected by floods.• Rehabilitation of sanitation facilities in health facilities and communities affected by floods• Hygiene promotion in the communities• Training health workers on minimum WASH activity package3. Mitigation of gender based violence through WASH interventions by doing the following activities• Safety audits before rehabilitation of latrines and water points.• Distribution of MHM kits to vulnerable and affected women and girls.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

To ensure effective records of feedback/complaints from the beneficiaries for follow-up, CARO team will establish accessible CRFMs (complaint response feedback mechanisms) such as the use of complaint forms, complaint desks, hotlines, complaint boxes and complaint committees based on the area of applicability. CARO will also organize project performance and quality review meetings with staff, beneficiaries, and partners to assess progress towards the set objectives and work plans, to monitor project performance and quality of activities based on monitoring and beneficiary feedback data, to identify risks and set risk management strategies during implementation. A project evaluation will be carried out at the end of the project and the process used to measure the achievement of project outputs and outcomes then documented for the record. CARO will take immediate and appropriate action upon receipt of any report or complaint about SEA allegations, CAROs or suspicions, or retaliation related to SEA. Complaints and feedback will be registered and followed up with the communities on ongoing bases and will be used to adjust project activities

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

For the people with special needs, whose access to feedback and complaint mechanism is limited, CARO will adjust the community-based complaints and feedback mechanism paths to provide a platform for beneficiaries with special needs to voice any issues of CAROs and feedback on the project activities. CARO will set up help desks and encourage vulnerable community members to share any feedback they have on the project through face to face meetings. The most vulnerable members will form part of the PDM sample to ensure that their voice is heard. Help desks will be erected in Mobile Clinics sites to provide a good opportunity for two-way dialogue. In case of beneficiaries with disabilities with limited mobility, CARO will visit households with the help of the Home health promotors and the community health workers to ensure that their ideas are heard and considered during an assessment at the time of intervention. For the illiterate, home health promoters and community health workers will be encouraged to document the complaints and feedbacks since they are selected from within the community and are familiar with the local language. Beneficiaries will be made aware of the complaints and feedback mechanism, as well having the opportunity to provide feedback directly to CARO program staff or monitoring team.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CARO staff will be responsible for championing good practices and maintaining an organizational culture that prioritizes and safeguards all against SEA. Managers at all levels (Both Juba and Field level) have responsibilities to support and develop systems that maintain an environment that facilitates implementation of this Policy and which prevents SEA. During the community mobilization and sensitization meetings, CARO will provide beneficiaries with information on how to safely and confidentially report SGBV/SEA if and when it occurs. CARO will use a community-based complaints and feedback mechanism to provide a platform for beneficiaries to voice any issues of CAROs and feedback on the project activities. In line with CARO's Safeguarding Policy on PSEA, CARO will inform the target communities about CARO's staff responsibilities and the expected code of conduct. At the start of the project, CARO will develop a plan on how information about PSEA will be disseminated. In all its communication activities, CARO will re-emphasize that all the beneficiary entitlements are provided freely and that they should immediately report if and when they’re asked to do or provide anything in exchange of the project’s entitlements. CARO will work closely with community leaders and stakeholders and will sensitize them on how to report any SEA cases that they come across.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Through community mobilization and sensitization activities, CARO will endeavor to provide accurate, timely and accessible information to beneficiaries about every aspect of the project including the planned activities, beneficiary entitlements, timelines and how to share feedback or report complains to CARO. Community mobilization will take the form of regular community and bilateral meetings. CARO will also place banners and signposts with project details at strategic points in the community. CARO will actively involve beneficiaries in identifying their priorities and solutions to their challenges to ensure that the voices and CARO of all groups are considered during the project’s design, planning and implementation. Data for assessment, baseline and monitoring will be collected through institutions and HH survey, focus group discussions with representatives of various groups disaggregated by gender and age, people with disabilities, interviews with key informants, authorities and other NGOs present in the area. Each time CARO staff/or enumerators will inform about the scope and aim of data collection. Collected data will be a base for designing activities, adjusting to the needs or external circumstances. Special attention will be given to women and girls’ voices, PWD and members of minority groups. Regular meetings with the community representatives during intervention implementation will be organized to present plans of the activities and receive feedback on the implementation process, as well as suggestions for improvement. At all phases of the intervention, CARO staff will always consult and engage with the beneficiaries and local authorities to ensure that the decisions are made jointly with their prior knowledge.
