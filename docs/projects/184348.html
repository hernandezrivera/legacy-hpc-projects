Project Summary

Conflict and insecurity have forced hundreds of thousands of people to flee their homes in 2021 and are expected to remain key drivers of displacement in 2022. Conflict-induced shocks exacerbate the humanitarian situation of both IDP and host communities, with increased numbers of children married earlier as a coping strategy. Without humanitarian assistance, nearly 3.5 million are expected to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes through the end of 2021.
Delivery of education services has been severely in adequate across Somalia over the years. Enrolment of school-age children is very low in the whole country. Reports show that enrolment is lower in rural areas compared to urban areas. It is estimated that only 12% of children enrolled in primary schools are from rural areas, rural population makes 50% of the national total. Lower Shabelle Region, one of our target regions, has illiteracy rate of 67% of its rural population (2022 HNO). This is attributed to lack of accessible learning centres and poverty related issues. Households living with extreme poverty cannot have their children access available learning centres as they can’t afford to pay school fees.
Enrolment of children in primary schools among IDP populations is also very low. Poverty related issues are major barriers and challenges hindering IDPs’ children in accessing education. Many of IDP households cannot afford to pay fees charged by schools. Some of the children contribute to the households’ living by working as shoe shiners, house-helps and doing some other domestic works where they are under paid and often subjected to abuses. Early marriage is also a major setback to girls’ education. Lower Shabelle Region has IDP population of over 96000 while Galgudud (Dhusamareb and Guriel towns) has over 92000. (FSNAU report of October 2021)
Children with disabilities are also poorly enrolled in the formal primary schools. Most of the existing school structures are not designed to the comfort of some disability cases while some are discouraged by the bullying of other children in the schools. Walking distance is also a significant challenge for some of the children living with disabilities. Number of teachers and protection staff trained to incorporate children with disabilities in classroom learning is very small.
The project aims to intervene the situation through provision of life saving quality education to school children from IDP, returnees and vulnerable host communities living in Galgadud and L/Shabelle regions. The project targets to enroll 13000 (5720 girls and 7280 boys) out of school children into 20 operational public schools in the districts of Marka, Qoryoley, Barawe and Dhusamareb. Among the target children will be 1950 children living with disabilities. Disadvantaged Children including girls, children with disabilities, children from poor families and minority groups will have increased access to appropriate, participatory and affordable primary education in schools managed by the communities in the two regions .The project will conduct “Go to school” campaign aimed at enrolling these vulnerable children, whose parents cannot afford to pay school fees, in existing operational public schools. These children will not be required to pay school fees as the project will provide emergency teacher incentives to 80 (50 male and 30 female) teachers who will be teaching learners in 20 selected schools. The project plans to support children in scholastic materials, WASH related services and provision of teacher incentives. For delivery of quality education to the target children teachers and school managements’ capacity will be built. Communities’ awareness on children’s rights and importance of sending children to school will be built through establishing community dialogue to raise awareness on mitigating risks and all kinds of child abuse practices to enhance children’s access and retention in education. 20 latrine designed to suit disabilities.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

The project and beneficiaries might encounter which can negatively impact the project out come. Below are possible risks and their proposed mitigation: a) Risks that beneficiaries are exposed to violence 1. Set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Eg. feedback box at learning centres, phone lines 2. Consider the gender balance of education staff - ensure that there are enough women available as a contact point for female teachers and students. 3. Ensure that all staff and committee representatives receive protection mainstreaming training. 4. Work closely with authorities and protection service providers to help refer children with protection issues. b) Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual abuse 1. Set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions c) Marginalized groups not accessing consultation meetings 1. The project will involve and consult all categories and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to education needs. Different criteria may affect the power dynamics. 2. Consultation will be made with women, men, boys, girls, adolescents, children with disabilities, teachers and parents to understand their needs and preferences for school location, design and education services. 3. Engage the community and committee representative to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response 4. Talk to girls and boys separately about the barriers they face and hold discussions with groups of children that could face added difficulties. 5. Establish participatory student associations and parent-teacher committees and engage them in the design of education programs. Such associations can boost the confidence and self-protection capacities of those involved whilst benefiting others. 6. Ensure student, teacher, parent and school management committees are democratic and inclusive and members represent all groups within the community (e.g. gender, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, persons with disabilities). 7. Reinforce the capacity of parents and teachers to act as a protective layer for children against violations. d) Beneficiaries not accessing complaint/feedback mechanism 1. Set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Eg. feedback box at learning centres, phone lines 2. Consider the gender balance of education staff - ensure that there are enough women available as a contact point for female teachers and students. 3. Ensure that all staff and committee representatives receive protection mainstreaming training. 4. In cooperation with protection actors, enhance the capacity of teachers to monitor, report and refer protection cases (such as abuse and exploitation) in accordance with standard operating procedures. e) Explosive risks in the project area for school children 1: Avoidance of placing education facilities near military installations and confict front-lines 2. Considering risks associated with land mines, explosive remnants of war, busy roads, exposed wires and dangerous materials on the ground. 3. Keeping in mind the distance children have to travel and their mode of travel. 4. Ensuring the timing of classes is safe for children; classes will be set for the day time with sufficient time to reach home before nightfall. 5. Situating play areas in clearly visible and safe locations.
