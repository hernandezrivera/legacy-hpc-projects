Project Summary

In response to the immediate need to support IDPs in northwest Syria , who were affected by protracted displacement and the negative impact of COVID-19 infection , two well equipped mobile clinics ( provided by IHH) to INSAN have been rehabilitated and reactivated to work again to provide medical services to IDPs in the camps who face challenges to access to health services due to distance and transportation difficulties .
The project of mobile clinics with HERAMS codes: G0700149 - G0700150 , started on 1/11/2020 and will end on 31/7/2021. These mobile clinics provide primary health care to IDPS in the camps in Maaret Tamasrin sub-district in the communities ( Kelly , Kafr – Kafrehmul , Maaret Elekhwan , Haranbush , Hazano, Kaftin, Maaret Tamasrin) .
Where the medical services are provided according to Essential Health Services Package (EHSP) which was developed by WHO and includes:
1.Integrated management of childhood illness (IMCI)
2.Reproductive health (RH) through the gynecology clinic.
3.Management of non-communicable diseases (NCD) through the GP clinic where all referred chronic cases (elderly, women, and children) can be treated.
4.Management of communicable diseases (CD) through both clinics that provide preventable and curative treatment as well as services that have great roles in the life-saving of children.
Each mobile clinic contains medical team consists of: GP, midwife, lab technician, nurse , pharmacist in addition to Data Entry .
Each mobile clinic has lab room includes the following equipment (Hematology analyzer, CBC (Complete blood counter), Hematology Centrifuge Table top centrifuge, Microscope, Auto clave and Refrigerator &Freezer) which will provide blood and urine analyses.
The mobile clinics will operate 8 hours during 5 days per week ,serving about 50,000 population during 5 months.
INSAN will ensure the proper application of CRM mechanism as it will be informed with the targeted community along with hotline, WhatsApp number, Facebook page, complaints box and field monitor to keep track of the services provided and response to the complaints effectively. The project will be monitored throughout the project through field visits, checklists of the beneficiaries, staff, work methodology, and tools and equipment of the mobile clinics.
To avoid any spread of COVID-19 pandemic ,INSAN will apply IPC protocol which includes.
-provide PPE equipment and IPC supplies.
- follow up triage protocol at the entry of the facility.
- refer any suspected cases according to the health cluster guidelines.
INSAN will coordinate regularly with the IHD, Health cluster, submitting 4Ws and coordinate with the HFs and NGOs operating in the area.

4 - Needs

Compared to 2020, the health sector PiN has increased 3% with the largest absolute change seen in Idleb governorate where the PiN increased more than 200,000. A larger health sector PiN reflects the concurrent stressors of the protracted crisis, the COVID-19 pandemic and the economic downturn in Syria. As the COVID-19 pandemic has revealed, a weakened health system poses a risk to the entire population and therefore efforts must be undertaken to increase the responsiveness and resilience of the health system as well as its capacity to protect vulnerable groups. Emergency, stop-gap measures such as task-shifting and training must be complemented by longer-term efforts to address retention and shortages of health care workers such as training and production of clinical staff. Barriers to health access – particularly cost of care must beaddressed through a combined strategy of mobile and outreach interventions in access constrained areas, strengthening public systems, and investing in private partnerships wheregaps in services exist.Negative trends in determinants of health, such as disrupted water networks and waste management, displacement status, insufficient shelter solutions, and food insecurity, converge to leave populations vulnerable to epidemic-prone diseases. In the context of dramatic increases in food insecurity, diarrheal disease can exacerbate the risk of severe acute malnutrition in children under 5. Food insecurity will also worsen already-elevated levels of anemia in women of reproductive age , posing a risk to adverse health outcomes for both mother and child. HNO (2021)
