Project Summary

This project aims at restoring agricultural and urban livelihood opportunities in South Syria in rural and urban areas. In rural areas, this will be achieved through a combination of action at the individual and the community level. At the individual level, agricultural vouchers will be provided to 1,200 mid-size farmers. Vouchers will be redeemed in exchange of seeds, fertilizers and tools to resume their agricultural production. Farmers will also take part to training on climate-smart agriculture and best agricultural practices. At the community level, the project will focus on the rehabilitation of communal agricultural assets which serve the communities living in the area. These will include public or community-based irrigation channels, wells, and other small agricultural facilities which have fallen into disrepair or been damaged by the crisis. In urban areas, 36 existing businesses will be supported with cash grants and training which will enable them to restore or expand their business. An estimated 9,416 individuals will benefit from the project.

Needs

In 2019, the food security severity ranking remains high in South Syria. In 2018, the food security situation in Dar’a governorate was classified as severe in 11 out of the 17 sub-districts, revealing widespread consumption gaps but also a significant depletion of livelihoods assets by the population, in order to meet their food consumption needs. (Food security situation in Syria, Expanded version of the Food security sector Humanitarian Needs overview 2018). In 2019, the whole governorate of Dar’a is characterized as having a major food security severity ranking. Out of the 984,057 households living in the three districts in Dar’a governorate (Da’ra, As-Sanamayin, Izra) in August 2018, 355,693 were estimated to be food insecure, while 189,943 were considered at risk. 40% of the population is represented by internally displaced persons (IDPs). (HNO PIN Severity 2019). Prolonged displacement, lack of financial and physical access to food, loss and lack of livelihood and reduced production capacity remain the key factors of people’s food insecurity. A nation-wide assessment conducted in 2018 has found that an estimated 40 percent of Syrians spend more than 65 percent of their expenditure on food, 57.2 percent of food insecure households reported to have outstanding debt and 65 percent of food insecure people are adopting crisis or emergency negative coping strategies. The agricultural sector has been particularly affected by the crisis: farmers have lost access to productive land due to displacement, as well as the financial capacity to purchase good quality seeds, fertilizers and tools to cultivate the land. Moreover, the damaged endured by communal assets, including irrigation channels and wells, have further worsened the situation for farmers, who often have difficulties in accessing water for irrigation. (Food security sector narrative – HNO version). In Dar’a governorate, the situation was aggravated by the instability faced in summer 2018, when the Government of Syria regained control on the area previously under opposition control. As a result of the military operations, 270,000 people were displaced between June and July 2018, and this represented the largest displacement in the area since the beginning of the Syrian crisis in 2011 (United Nations).

Implementation of activities

The proposed action consists of assistance provided both at the household and the community level. In rural areas, individual support will be provided to 1,200 farmers will receive a restricted commodity voucher which they will be able to redeem in exchange of seeds, fertilizers, and tools. Farmers will be selected based on a series of eligibility criteria and taking also into account standard vulnerability criteria. This activity will benefit equally host community members, internally displaced people and returnees, based on their needs. In addition to receiving the voucher, they will take part in training on agricultural best practices and climate-smart agriculture. In the same areas where farmers will be supported in restoring their livelihood, 4 communal agricultural assets will be rehabilitated. This community-based intervention will target irrigation canals which have fallen into disrepair, in order to ensure farmers have access to water for irrigation. Assets will be identified in a participatory manner, in consultation with the local authorities and communities, based on the catchment area and expected impact. In addition, under the proposed intervention, support will be provided to small and medium-sized enterprise, which will receive a cash grants and training to restore their functioning and/or expand. Beneficiaries will be identified based on a series of eligibility criteria including being an established business and facing difficulties in expansion. Selected businesses owners will participate in a business training on financial management and planning. Upon completion of the training, each business will develop an action plan to ensure the grant will be used according to an expenditure plan pre-approved. A cash grant will then be provided in different instalments.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: interference with the beneficiaries targeting Likelihood: lowImpact: highMitigation measure: In order to reduce this risk, a strong process of beneficiaries’ selection is in place. This includes a combination of self-application and reliance on list provided by the local authorities. A1 100% verification by Monitoring and Evaluation Department will ensure only eligible households will receive assistance. A Complaints and Response mechanism will also be available for community members to raise a complaint or ask for clarification. Risk: aid diversionLikelihood: mediumImpact: high Mitigation measure: In order to mitigate this risk, a strong community mobilisation will be in place. The project will be carefully explained to community leaders, local authorities and residents. In addition, distribution will be monitored by the independent Monitoring and Evaluation Unit, which will also conduct a Post Distribution Monitoring. Through the Complaints and Response mechanism, beneficiaries will be able to give their feedback and report any alleged attempt of interference. Risk: sexual exploitation and violence during distribution Likelihood: mediumImpact: high Mitigation measure: Distributions to be conducted through locally set-up distribution points. One dedicated female distribution kiosk will be staffed by at least one female staff member to ensure women feel comfortable when receiving the vouchers or kits, and are able to ask questions on the project. Sites will be identified in a full participatory manner with men and women from the communities. For households with too severe movement restraints or protection concerns related to coming to distribution points, distributions will be conducted individually at a household level.

Refusal of activities by the local authorities and community leaders or violence erupting during activities implementation due to lack of acceptance of the project c.
