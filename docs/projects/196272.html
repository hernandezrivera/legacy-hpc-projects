Project Summary

UOSSM seeks to contribute to achieving the following objectives:
Increase access to life-saving and life-sustaining coordinated, equitable humanitarian health services for those most vulnerable and in need.
The proposed project is to keep running the acute mental health unit in Sarmada/Idleb with the two mental health mobile units. The main goal of the project is to promote specialized mental health services to address more severe mental health disorders.
Upon the need of the affected people for specialized MH services which are not sufficiently available in the area, UOSSM contributed to the response by establishing an acute MH inpatient and outpatient unit where services are provided for the most urgent presentations of severe mental health disorders, like schizophrenia (acute psychotic episodes), bipolar disorder (manic episode), severe depression, suicidal risk, severe substance misuse disorder and severe behavioral disorder related to mental health problems which cannot be managed and addressed in the community, in addition to providing multidisciplinary mental health service in the community (outpatient psychiatric units, psychiatric supervision, follow up and case management, individual, couple and group psychotherapy, and the mental health supervision), and receiving referrals of more severe and complicated psychiatric beneficiaries from other agencies and health providers.
Also, to cover as many areas as possible, the acute MH unit is linked with two MH mobile clinics, where MH interventions are provided by doctors trained in MHGAP program for beneficiaries of children and adults with signs and symptoms of mild and moderate mental health disorders, in addition to psychosocial support activities for children, which are: self-recovery techniques, coping skills with different situation skills, emotional expression techniques, PFA and PFA for children. Also, to facilitate awareness campaigns on mental health and protection concerns, such as (the detection of signs and symptoms of mental disorders, early marriage, GBV prevention. etc.). Not only the detection and referral of the serious and severe beneficiaries to the acute MH unit in Sarmada, but also the referral of beneficiaries or families to community resources to help in recovery from mental or physical illnesses, and to facilitate access to services provided by other humanitarian actors.
Objectives of the Project:
•To respond to the most urgent acute and serious mental health problems of the affected populations in the area
•To facilitate access of the affected populations to long-term specialized MH services
•To enhance and promote the affected communities’ healthy coping mechanisms for both locals and IDPs
•To facilitate access for the affected populations to the available services in the area through an active referral pathway.

3 - Needs

More than eleven years of the Syrian crisis have led to a critical situation in various aspects. Overall, it is estimated that 14.6 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance in 2022; 22.8% of them are classified as people in extreme and catastrophic need More than a decade of combats has resulted in a high change in population and residency in Syria; more than 6.6 million people are Internally Displaced People (IDPs). The worsening economic conditions in the northwest have eroded people’s ability to secure their basic needs, contributing further to increased stress levels. Children are particularly affected by the mental trauma of living in a warzone and being displaced for their whole lives. Across Syria, signs of psychological distress in boys and girls under the age of 18 are reported by one-third of households. There is also a link between humanitarian needs and mental health as children of households with lower income and poor food security status show higher levels of distress than children from households not experiencing these problems.More than nine in ten people have heard of suicides in their communities. The reported reasons included depression, domestic violence, particularly against women, financial hardship, and/or loss of property, among other different reasons related to family conflicts.

4 - Implementation of activities

Inpatient unit: specialized psychiatric interventions, including full assessment, and diagnosis with prescription of the appropriate psychotropic medications, will be provided for beneficiaries from both communities IDPs and host, women and men i.e. adults above 18 years old with the most urgent presentations of severe mental health disorders by a professional psychiatrist, which cannot be managed and addressed in the community, in addition to the provision of group PSS sessions by a team of PSWs for beneficiaries. Two MH Mobile Clinics: MH interventions are provided by doctors trained in the MHGAP program for beneficiaries with signs and symptoms of mild and moderate mental health disorders including referral of acute or serious cases of beneficiaries to the psychiatrist in the acute MH unit in Sarmada. provision of evidence-based individual consultations, by the PSWs, for beneficiaries. In addition, the PSWs will provide beneficiaries with group awareness-raising sessions on MH concerns and PSS activities for children, and create, and develop a service mapping for services and service providers.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1-Risk-based violence, gender, and sexual exploitation as well as the exploitation of staff to patients in exchange for service delivery Mitigating : the wards of the beneficiaries in the acute unit will be separated, part for men and part for women. In addition, baths and toilets will be also isolated and separated for men and women and also for the staff. The MH unit will be also provided with a complaint box reached by beneficiaries, all the staff members will sign a code of conduct. the role of M&E will be activated during the project cycle. Also, there will be a client satisfaction survey conducted every three months by the PSWs for beneficiaries. 2- Neglecting the most vulnerableMitigating Taking into consideration the context and the environment roles, traditions, and customs, the MH unit staff will be mixed of two genders, males and females. Also, the working hours in the outpatient unit will be during daylight which enables women to reach the services available. In addition, the building will be equipped with facilities for disabled beneficiaries such as a ramp, wheelchair, and modified toilets. Above all, the outreach PSWs will facilitate PSS activities targeting children and adolescents in schools and community centers in the area on a weekly or biweekly basis. 3- Discrimination in service deliveryMitigating All the services provided will be based on humanitarian perspectives, and many things will be taken into account; the location and place of the MH


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk1: Sexual Harassment: among employees, volunteers and in work environment contextLevel: LowMitigation : All staff have been trained on PSEA and above all has signed a TOR and aware of the internal policy of PSEA which has been monitored by both the HR & MEAL teamsRisk2:Sexual Exploitation and Abuse: between service delivery personnel, towards beneficiariesLevel: LowMitigation : All staff (employees and volunteers) signed on a code of conduct that includes of refers to the commitment to PSEA, that is issued by the UN Secretary General Bulletin for the PSEA procedure. In addition, UOSSM has a PSEA focal point in addition to a dedicated hotline to receive complaints from beneficiaries in case of PSEA incidents. Risk3:Gender Based Violence: targeting the vulnerable groups with communities (i.e., women, elderly, children, special needs …)level: Low Mitigation : The project design is built on robust need assessment and is also targeting the most vulnerable groups in the community without discrimination or exclusion. Above, the project is a multi-sectoral one where all sectors are aware and acknowledged of detection and referral of GBV either internally to the protection department or externally to other service providers.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:- UOSSM will coordinate closely with the health clusters, as well as local authorities and humanitarian actors inside Syria throughout the proposed intervention, informing the cluster if and when adjustments are needed to the intervention.- Location remains accessible for the team and the beneficiaries.- Inflation and currency exchange rates remain within an acceptable range that does not affect planned deliverable quality and quantity.- Local qualified staff required for project activities are available and adequate.Risk:- The conflict prevents beneficiaries and team access to facilities.- The close of borders prevents the shipments of supplies and medications to access facilities.Mitigation:- Develop a strong risk management strategy, (preparing an emergency plan /plan B/ according to the expected scenarios in conflicted areas.- Capacity building plan for the staff.
