Project Summary

The project in line with the education sector response framework in Syria 2020, aiming to contribute to the improvement of access and remain in basic educational services for the most vulnerable girls, boys, and PWDs in the targeted areas targeting 5040 persons (4800 girls and boys & 240 female and male teachers and education personal) following two main pillars 1) Access: Increased safe and equitable access to formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children, 2) Systems: the capacity of the education system and communities to deliver a timely, coordinated and evidence-based education response strengthened, the project is targeting the most vulnerable areas based on the last updated severity scale from education sector (100% from targeted areas having the severity scale of 4,5, or 6), Rural of Damascus Al Nashabyah, Dara'a, Hrak, Hama Mhardeh, Aleppo Jabal Samman, and the project will target the mentioned areas under the pillar (Access) with the following activities and targets;
o Establish, expand and rehabilitate new classrooms: the project will target 8 primary schools in the mentioned areas, as an average of 2 schools in each area with light to medium rehabilitation work for the schools aiming to increase the accessibility and the safety of the schools and make it more school-friendly spaces and reduce any kind of hazard could be affecting the children safety and their learning environment, such as (fixing the electricity, windows, doors, painting, providing schools desks and chairs, boards, and shaded areas, and light rehabilitation for the lightly destroyed walls, etc.) the schools will be chosen based on MoE suggestions and evaluation and in line with the MoE strategy
o Rehabilitate, improve or construct gender-sensitive and disability - sensitive WASH facilities: in addition to the mentioned work in each school , project activities will ensure that the schools will become more sensitive regarding Gender, Disability, and Wash Facilities through the main following actions:
- All the schools will be accessible for girls and boys and children with disabilities (equipping the entrances, stairs, ramps, Wash facilities, classes) to be accessible for children with disabilities.
- In each school the project will equip a resources room, to be used for the additional support for the disable children.
- All the Wash facilities will be renewed and each school will have separated facilities for girls and boys.
o Conduct Back-to-Learning (BTL) campaigns, and after finishing the rehabilitation work in each school awareness events will be conducted to increase the awareness of the community members (children and caregivers) about the importance of education.
o Provide students with school supplies: the project will provide all the children in each school with school bags includes the basic stationery (Based on MoE standards)
o Conditional Voucher for educational support (stationary, school clothes, hygiene materials) for the most vulnerable families of registered children in school-aged between (12-17) aiming to reduce the risk of dropping - out of schools and to protect the children from the risk of child labor and early marriage.
The project will target the mentioned areas under the pillar (Systems) with the following activities and targets;
• Build the capacity of teachers and education personal:
- the project will conduct eight main capacity building training for 200 teachers (the training will be covering policies, planning, data collection, INEE MS, main CP principals and basics of inclusive education)
- the project will conduct 2 advanced training on inclusive education for 40 special education teachers from the eight schools, in Lebanon/ or Jordan and the training will be handled by TDH regional inclusive approach .

4 - Needs

According to OCHA 2020, a total of 13.6 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance, including 73% that are living with 4/5 severity needs. 5.7 million children are in acute need due to a convergence of vulnerabilities resulting from displacement and crisis, exposure to hostilities and limited access to basic goods and basic services, with 4.34 million children in age school (5-17). In 2019, a total of 96,253 refugees have returned, although these refugee return figures are likely to be higher. Moving UNHCR’s operational focus from emergency response to durable solutions continues to be a key priority for the operation throughout the year. People are returning despite challenging circumstances, and the operation aims to provide initial support to returnees and vulnerable populations through an area-based approach. It includes immediate shelter repair, legal aid/documentation, community-based protection, primary health, livelihoods, and education. Increasing needs for support for returnees also require wider and more predictable humanitarian space for all actors. According to UNICEF, a total of 2.1 million children in Syria are estimated to be out of school, with a further 1.3 million at risk of dropping out. Displaced communities remain significantly undeserved, with most IDP camps having insufficient or non-existent education services, and more than one in three schools are damaged or destroyed, while others are used for purposes not related to education such as shelter for displaced people.According to last education need assessment in January 2020 in Aleppo conducted by TDH, the results showed that the total percentage of children (6-17 years) who dropped out of schools was 36% out of the total number of children (6-17 years), many reasons of dropping out, the highest percentage was 40% while the reason was; the child is working meanwhile 17% Cannot afford school costs,17% due to Customs/tradition/no interest in family, and 15% have disability.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk (1): Exclusion of Children/Youth with disabilities who cannot physically access education centers and/or are unable to use relevant education tools. Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation Measures: 1) Learning facilities are improved to ensure ease of access to Children/Youth with disabilities. 2) Staff and teachers are trained to be able to accommodate for Children/Youth with disabilities. 3) Coordination with specialized actors and training to teams on inclusion. 4) Complaints/Feedback and Feedback mechanisms are put in place for students to voice their recommendations, suggestions or concerns. Protection Risk (2): Children and Female Youth might be exposed to the risk of, or experience, physical harm, bullying or harassment by teachers, facilitators, staff or other students. Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation Measures: 1) PSEA policies are in place. 2) Staff and teachers are aware of PSEA policies and Code of Conduct. 3) Children/Youth have access to reporting systems. 4) Field monitoring systems are in place. 5) Children/Youth learning sessions are divided based on different age groups. 6) Youth learning sessions are divided as culturally appropriate. 7) Female Youth attend morning sessions. 8) Complaints/Feedback and Feedback mechanisms are put in place for students to voice their recommendations, suggestions or concerns. Protection Risk (3): Learning facilities and surroundings, including sanitation facilities, might be structurally unsafe or do not provide sufficient protection. Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation Measures: 1) Facilities are improved by specialized technical staff (e.g. Shelter) to ensure that buildings and grounds are safe and protected. 2) Sufficient WASH facilities are provided and maintained, and are gender and age segregated. 3) Complaints/Feedback and Feedback mechanisms are put in place for students to voice their recommendations, suggestions or concerns.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R1: Local partners lose access to the project locations and are unable to implement activities, M1: SARC and SCFPA have displayed an outstanding ability to implement humanitarian projects despite constraints and limitations put onto them. Currently they are already accessing the mentioned locations with funding from various entities for several months. However, if they lost the access to project locations, Should they, TdH would either identify other suitable partners or, in coordination with them and with MoE, identify other locations with same high needs, where the programming could continue. R2: TdH are unable to timely contract technically skilled employee, M2:TdH has solid recruitment/human resources processes and regulations to ensure the best qualified candidates are hired in a timely manner. Early launching of the process is key. Vacancies will be publicly announced soon after the approval is received .R3: bureaucracy and delay in getting needed approvals from local authorities, M3: TdH already obtained approvals to work in Northern and southern area of Syria, in addition, TdH will advocate through the local partner to have the needed approvals based on the signed MOU, R4: delay in receiving the fund due to the sanctions on the banking system in Syria, M4: TdH has different banking accounts in Syria with different pipelines to receive the fund from different sources. R5: Security risks : TdH and Local partner’s staff are exposed to threats, including kidnapping, detention, intimidation, and serious injury, M5: the security situation has been considered relatively calm but unpredictable and partners are able to conduct their work in relative safety, TdH Security person and Security Focal points in the project locations are regularly analyze the operating environment and have established safeguards to prevent and respond to risks of insecurity which may arise, addressed through location-specific safety and security management plans
