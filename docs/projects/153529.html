Project Summary

Première Urgence Internationale develops an integrated approach, combining health, nutrition, and FSL services to effectively tackle the needs of the populations: this allows to respond to urgent needs and to foster recovery and improve the resilience of the communities, for a more sustainable program.
PUI will provide livelihood support to 35,000 food insecure households in Aweil North (Northern Bahr el Ghazal) and Twic (Warrap State). The vegetable and fishing intervention planned will help increase food production, improving dietary diversity and coping strategies for the most vulnerable populations.
The project will revolve around 3 key pillars.
The targeted households will receive vegetable and fishing kits, with seeds, tools (ie, hoes) and fishing equipment (ie, twines, hooks) to improve production means and thus reinforce the accessibility, availability and diversity of food at household and local market levels. The project targets 16,000 households in Aweil North, where PUI already supports 15 nutrition sites and has implemented a FAO funded Emergency Response Livelihoods Project (ERLP) as an integrated approach to complement its nutrition and health programs –meaning that households with pregnant and lactating women and children under 5 suffering from malnutrition will be prioritized. In Twic, 16,000 households will also be targeted and receive kits, as PUI will take over and expand activities started under the previous FAO ERLP in the area by other FAO-funded partners.
PUI also proposes to implement Conditional General Distribution for 3,000 households, providing food to vulnerable people in Aweil North in exchange for their involvement in an activity chosen by the community. The chosen activity will create an added value for the household or the community at large, while reinforcing ownership. PUI will indeed discuss their needs with each community through its Program Management Committees and other possible focused group discussions.
Farmer and fishing groups will be established to manage demonstration plots, provide a forum for PUI trainings and ensure peer-to-peer experiential learning: 1,067 households in Aweil North and 533 households in Twic will be part of farmer groups and fishing groups. The groups will collectively fence the group gardens, prepare the land and make conventional seed beds. PUI will provide pilot pond fishing farming trainings in some of the Payams with more persistent and localized swamps as well. Households will be trained on conservation agriculture techniques, including post-harvest and handling techniques to enhance conservation and preservation of the vegetables. They will also learn about Integrated Pest and Disease Management (IPDM), using local resources as a way to build resilience within the communities. Fishing groups will furthermore benefit from trainings on good, safe and hygienic fishing practices, including learning about fish preservation with smoke kilns and salting techniques and will be trained on fishing-net making.
Through the vegetable gardens, the availability of food for consumption and the dietary diversity will be increased, while the training in conservation agriculture, good agricultural and fishing practices will improve coping strategies. Through garden groups’ establishment and trainings in conservation agriculture, food processing and preservation, rural livelihoods will be protected, safeguarded and stabilized all while improving food availability and reducing the food gap. As PUI links beneficiaries in the nutrition program to the FSL program, PUI aims to shift interventions, lowering dependence on the nutrition program and focus more on fostering resilience and communities’ future self-reliance.

Needs

Food insecurity continues to be the main cause for acute malnutrition. According to the IPC findings released in September 2018, 53.9% of the population in Northern Bahr el Ghazal is considered to be in IPC phases 3 & 4 for food insecurity in September: 495,000 people in Phase 3! (crisis – approximately 35%) and 260,000 in Phase 4 (emergency – approximately 19% of the State’s population). Food insecurity is the main cause of malnutrition, related to, inadequate resources and production.Première Urgence Internationale implemented in 2018 a FAO funded Emergency Response Livelihoods Project (ERLP) in Aweil North in a total of 18 bomas, located in 5 Payams: Malual Center, Malual West, Malual East, Malual North and Ariath as an integrated approach to complement its Nutrition and Health programs. Under this FAO ELRP, PUI directly distributed crops kits, fishing kits, vegetable kits and tools to 3,500 beneficiaries. However, the crop assessment reports sampling 5% of the beneficiaries show that households mainly grew 5 crop types which are: Sorghum 29%, Maize 24%, Cowpeas 19%, Simsim 6% and Groundnuts 5%. With these results, it can be clearly seen that household dietary diversity scores are low; the lack of food diversity reflects food insecurity and has a direct impact on their nutrition and ultimately, health status. The crop assessment also shows that 74% of households weeded only twice and 25% only once. According to an assessment done by PUI in 2017, weed infestation, especially Striga parasitic weed invasion, has reduced yields up to 66% compared to what it was in the 1990s. From the baseline information collected on registration forms, most households produced a total average of 200kg of cereals for the whole year in 2017; their average needs per year are around 1,022kg. Moreover, the looming yield gap for the end of 2018 and onwards is reflected in the September released IPC for South Sudan. For September 2018, Aweil North is set to experience a crisis (IPC Phase 3!), which could be worse if not for the intervention of humanitarian partners in the area. In Twic, the IPC also warns of a food crisis (IPC phase 3) for September 2018. This was evidenced during a rapid FSL assessment conducted by PUI in August 2018. From the assessment, it was determined that during the 2018 main season, 4 out of the 6 payams in Twic county (Turalei, Aweng, Wunrock and Panyok) have been badly affected by floods, directly reducing crop yields up to approximately 65%. From the focus group discussions conducted with community members, it was emphasized that the 2018 main season harvest will not adequately provide for a majority of households (60% to 70%) beyond December 2018. Observations and focus group discussions also showed that despite the abundance of water caused by flooding, most households do not have fishing kits and resort to using wooden pole spears to fish (ie, mud fish, Claria s. anguiliaris).Communities also lack cold storage facilities or ignore proper techniques of fish preservation. Fish processing is still traditional, as most households use traditional fermentation methods. The process is often not well controlled, involves poor and unhygienic handling and storage practices, culminating in accumulation of microorganisms, jeopardizing consumer safety. This has impeded developing fishing as major livelihood activity despite its potential given the abundance of swamps and other water bodies.Livelihood support in Aweil North and Twic will thus compensate the looming yield gap in 2018 as well as between the potential yield and required yield and ensure food security for the most vulnerable households.

Activities

- Distribution of vegetable kits for cultivation and agricultural training; - Distribution of fishing kits to increase the availability of animal protein and for fishing training;- Conditional General Distribution and activities decided with the community members.- Establishment of farmer and fishing groups targeting the fishing and vegetable kits beneficiaries to increase food production and beneficiaries’ resilience; - Training on conservation agriculture techniques, including post-harvest and handling techniques through the use of demonstration plots;- Training on good, safe and hygienic fishing practices, including fish preservation techniques;- Organization of garden field days and fairs for community mobilization; - Distribution of appropriate tools.

Consultations with affected people

Yes

Provide breakdown by women, men, boys and girls

To ensure quality programming and good services, PUI prioritizes needs of the communities. They have the opportunity to influence decisions in the different phases of the program cycle management, especially during assessments and needs identification, as well as strategy planning and monitoring and evaluation. All views of the different targeted groups are taken into account. Première Urgence Internationale concretely involves local communities through the Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs), Mother-to-Mother Support Groups and Farmers Groups. In September 2018, PUI organized its strategic workshop in Aweil North and invited community members for discussions on PUI’s current program and future activities. The groups varied from one village to the other, as some involved women part of the Mother to Mother Support Groups, mainly aged from 15 years old to 40 years old; other groups had been set up for PUI’s FSL activities in 2018, including the farmers groups so they included men and women heads of households. In total, PUI has consulted around 300 members of the community in Aweil North. In Twic, PUI conducted a rapid FSL assessment and largely relied on discussions with Key Informants (21 people involved – ranging from traditional community leaders to local authorities and NGOs) and 4 focus group discussions – thus consulting around 100 people in 4 days. These discussions are instrumental to assess local perspectives of community capacities and vulnerabilities as well as to outline local priorities in addressing their vulnerabilities. Such consultations will not only help PUI to fine-tune and improve its programs, it will also reinforce the community’s acceptance and ownership of future activities.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

The community members have the opportunity to influence the whole project cycle management. They are indeed consulted for feedback on the current services and give some recommendations to PUI for new activities in 2019. Then, once a project is starting or about to start, PUI shall organize a kick-off day to celebrate the project and inform the community members on the forthcoming activities. During the project launch phase, appropriate sessions will be organised with all stakeholders to provide information on the project's objectives, the implementation methodology, the planned activities, the selection criteria of the beneficiaries, etc. The Community Nutrition Volunteers, the Mother to Mother Support Groups and the Farmers Groups are all groups on which PUI can rely on to provide the beneficiaries with information. While implementing the project, a number of feedback mechanisms will be put in place to acquire the relevant data which will enable PUI to tailor its activities to the needs of the populations. Some of these mechanisms include monitoring visits, community leaders meetings and key informant interviews.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

A key aspect of PUI’s program is its integrated approach, which underscores the importance of awareness-raising on primary health care, nutrition and hygiene. Discussions are facilitated with the Mother-to-Mother Support Groups –PUI has established 56 groups-, who raise issues related to safe nutrition and hygiene practices with their communities. They spread basic health and nutrition messages, aiming to improve the general nutrition status of the households. These mother to mother support groups have already showed great results, as mothers are increasingly open to information, eager to share experience and show the positive impact of MIYCN practices; the linkages with the health facilities have also improved. Moreover, PUI has painted sensitization messages on the walls of its nutrition sites in the villages to reach as many community members as possible, showing safe child feeding practices, mothers using mosquito nets and washing hands with their children before a meal. Furthermore, if messages are shared on paper, they are written both in English and in Thuangjang, a Dinka dialect. This was the case for PUI's FSL activities in 2018, with messages on soil fertility and early planting, such as "early planting allows the cops to escape weeds, pests and diseases attack. The early planted crops have high yields." Throughout the years, these actions have helped foster a greater acceptance of the activities and greater proximity between PUI and community members – which helps adapt messages accordingly.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

PUI employees are all bound by PUI’s policies, including the Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse Policy (PSEA). Staff are trained and informed on the protection policies and sign a certificate to agree with the protection policies. Beneficiaries in the communities are informed of PUI’s protection policies and are asked to report any violation. PUI has also endeavoured to recruit more female staff but the challenges of low literacy, especially for women, is a hindrance, especially when qualified staff is needed to provide quality life services. Moreover, awareness activities related to basic human rights and Gender based violence will be integrated into PUI programs and target both men and women adjusted to their specific roles in the community. PUI integrates basic (S)GBV awareness activities in its nutrition and health programs, during health promotion sessions through story-telling, which has shown very positive results and fostered engaging discussions. While it is true that women in general are more vulnerable to (S)GBV, PUI engages both men and women in the promotion of gender equality, streamlining inclusivity in all its activities. In order to break the cycle of silence and stigma, different group sessions are held to allow more space for women and men to freely express themselves. As the most vulnerable beneficiaries of the nutrition activities are linked with the FSL activities, participants in the FSL activities will also benefit from these awareness raising sessions. Given PUI has been working for 3 years with the communities in Aweil North and is well respected for the given services and the NGO’s reliability, PUI also plans to do an initial assessment to have greater visibility on needs, practices, attitudes and knowledge of the communities on (S)GBV matters. Depending on the outcome of the assessment, (S)GBV awareness activities will be readjusted to the needs of the community.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

PUI aims to include all relevant stakeholders in its consultations: traditional community leaders, local authorities (ie, the State Minister of Agriculture in Twic, Commissioners, Agriculture County Directors, etc.) and community members. The latter are represented through the Community Volunteers, the Mother to Mother Support Groups and the farmers groups: the aim is to regularly consult the populations to adjust services and ensure PUI responds to their needs.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

PUI will discuss and decide the feedback mechanism adapted to the needs of each Boma, taking into consideration relations within the community, gender and modes of communication.Options considered will include:- Feedback through a specific committee; - Feedback through community Nutrition Volunteers and PUI staff; - Evaluations through focused group discussions.As an example, when implementing its FSL activities in 2018, beneficiaries expressed in a Rapid Post Distribution Monitoring their satisfaction with the services but hope, in the future, to benefit from more trainings and receive more diversity and quantity in the items distributed. Premire Urgence Internationale is also expected to respect and safeguard the dignity of the beneficiaries. PUI employees are all bound by PUI’s policies, including the Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse Policy (PSEA), the Child Protection Policy and the Anti-Fraud and Corruption Policy. Staff are trained and informed on the protection policies and sign a certificate to agree with the protection policies. Beneficiaries in the communities are informed of PUI’s protection policies and are asked to report any violation.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

In 2019, PUI plans to establish Project Monitoring Committees in all its sites of intervention, aiming to enhance ownership of the program in the community. PUI will start with an initial discussion and explanation of the role of the project committees and discuss with the community and local authorities the selection process of its members. The selection will consider specific criteria to provide equal opportunities for all and ensure equal participation of women and men. The community members involved in the Project Monitoring Committees will then ensure that activities run smoothly and that the services are provided as planned, all while providing feedback to PUI’s staff. This system will be paramount to improving capacity building and increasing ownership of the activities.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

When implementing FSL activities in Aweil North and Twic, PUI will consider the most vulnerable populations. The needs analysis is based on September IPC results (both counties are respectively in IPC3! and IPC3), discussions with key informants and focus groups. In Twic, the FSL intervention will target payams and households that have suffered from floods. Moreover, beneficiaries will be selected through committees which will establish a list of vulnerability criteria and will collectively select the most vulnerable households. For instance, in Aweil North where PUI already implements FSL activities, the most vulnerable households targeted for PUI’s nutrition activities have been directly considered to receive emergency livelihood kits, as well as single-mother headed households.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

PUI is aware that some areas in Aweil North and Twic are hard-to-reach and often necessitate several hours of trekking by foot for the beneficiaries. To ensure there are fewer barriers to access services, PUI has already decided to increase the number of distribution sites and will continue to do so, to reduce the distance walked by vulnerable beneficiaries to collect their input kits. Beneficiaries are also warned at least two days prior to each distribution even; each distribution will last for a maximum of 4 hours and will be completed at the very least 3 hours before sunset. Distributions to beneficiaries will also be prioritized according to the distance from the beneficiary’s village to the distribution site: villages further away will be served first, while those closest will be served last.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The work of PUI, the services provided and the behaviors of the staff are always guided by PUI's Protection Policies as well as the project monitoring mechanisms, which strive to ensure that beneficiaries are protected and that their safety is taken into account during the projects' implementation. Beneficiaries are selected based on a set list of vulnerabilities.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

For the FSL program, PUI will address vulnerabilities and will consider the most vulnerable groups, which include: - Households with PLW and CU5 who are enrolled in the nutrition program;- HHs with disabled women and men, - Child headed families,- Single women / female headed households, - HHs with IDPs and Returnees - HH affected by floods. AAP committees are established at the start of the program: members then look at the list of vulnerability criteria and select the community members most in need. Regarding protection, PUI always informs its beneficiaries on the services provided, what supplies they will receive and about the timing of the services. PUI's work and the behaviour of the staff are always guided by the organization's Protection Policies, to ensure protection for its staff members and the beneficiaries: staff is indeed not allowed to ask for money or is expected to provide services equally, for all those in need (no favors), in order to prevent coercion or exploitation. There are complaint and feedback mechanisms in place and community members know to whom they can address their concerns, as PUI has been involved in the community since 2015 and is well accepted. Feedback can be provided through staff or PUI’s team of volunteers, as well as PUI's supervisors and managers, individually or during group discussions.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project monitoring shall take into account the access of the beneficiaries to the services. This includes assessing the distance to and fro the distribution sites, the modes of transportation to reach distribution areas and bring the distributed items to the beneficiaries’ homes, the time to access the services as well as the time each beneficiary can spare in their daily routines to access the services.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

For its new projects, PUI shall endeavour to learn about the perceptions of safety and dignity of the beneficiaries. The aim is to know whether they feel safe when coming to the distribution sites or during the distributions; PUI shall also try to learn how the beneficiaries feel regarding their dignity, whether the sites are clean and welcoming for instance. Feedback will be gathered during community meetings (during assessments, focus group discussions, Farmers Groups Meeting) and through the Project Monitoring Committees.
