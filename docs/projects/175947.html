Project Summary

RI’s proposed 2021 Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) intervention is designed to contribute to improved health and nutrition outcomes, addressing key factors of wider
community disease outbreaks, vicious circle of malnutrition, timely and equitable access
to safe WASH and mitigation of related GBV, amongst 250, 000 IDPs, returnees and
vulnerable host communities in Upper Nile state in South Sudan.
The proposed WASH interventions will be implemented in close coordination with RI’s
health , nutrition , protection and FSL programming and teams, applying an integrated approach to improve people’s lives and well-being, thus contributing to South Sudan WASH Cluster objectives and the 2021 Humanitarian Response. Recognizing WASH services to be key drivers of health and malnutrition outcomes and women and girls to be the primary users of WASH
services, the project aims to reduce GBV risk by ensuring adequate and needs based
provision of WASH services that address key protection concerns. RI’s integrated approach will further address high levels of malnutrition by targeting SAM and MAM children and families and contribute to controlling disease outbreak.
Additionally, RI will ensure equitable, timely and sustainable access to improved safe and adequate water and sanitation facilities that address the vital WASH needs, public health and protection issues, respect sphere standards; engage communities in design and ownership, improve knowledge on appropriate hygiene practices to reduce and prevent WASH-related diseases including cholera, AWD, typhoid, skin diseases etc. RI will construct gender-sensitive water supply systems, mainly solar powered, boreholes through manual drilling, extensions to bring water points closer to vulnerable households, schools, health and nutrition facilities, rehabilitate damaged / nonfunctional boreholes, increase access to safe water and mitigate the risk of GBV at WASH facilities. Comprehensive training will be provided to female and male members of Water User Committees (WUCs) and Health & Nutrition facility staff. RI will work with existing WUCs to reinforce technical skills for water point O&M (operations and maintenance), establishing new WUCs where relevant. Solid waste management training will be provided to solid waste/hygiene committees to implement community-driven solid waste collection and disposal campaigns. RI will also construct incinerators for medical waste disposal at health facilities.
At the household level, RI will construct 1,500 household/family shared latrines, with owner contribution, amongst displaced, returnee, and conflict-affected communities to ensure that immediate and long-term sanitation needs are adequately met and facilities are gender and culturally appropriate. Latrine construction will adhere to protection standards as elaborated in the guidelines for WASH Cluster partners in South Sudan. RI will also promote hygiene awareness aimed to support behavior change in hygiene practices through house-to-house hygiene education sessions, training of hygiene volunteers, and distribution of household package for famine prevention which will include: one 20-liter bucket with tap for proper water storage, one 20-liter bucket without tap or jerry can for water collection, filter cloth, water purifier (PuR), and soap for three months consumption.

1 - Needs

South Sudan continues to experience the impact of years of war, violence and resultant economic crisis and food insecurity. The formation of government of national unity has led to a decrease in armed conflict. Although communities continue to experience the impact of years of war, violence, and resultant economic crisis and food insecurity, as violence decreases and confidence gradually grows, large numbers of spontaneous returnees are coming back to South Sudan. The people who flee to Ethiopia and other neighboring counties have started to return to their original places in Greater Upper Nile region especially in Longechuk, Nasir , Ulang and Maiwut counties.. The WASH cluster 2021 severity / Vulnerability mapping revealed that; total of 5.5 m people are in need across South Sudan where 3.0 m people are targeted for WASH interventions mainly in severity level 4 & 5 areas. The main objectives of WASH-2021 HRP is to reduce the morbidly and mortality in severity level 4 & 5 areas and Ensure safe, equitable and dignified critical basic services in severity level 4 and 5 areas. Thus, RI proposed Longechuk , Ulang and Maiwut counties are classified as severity level 5 where Nasir county classified level 4 for WASH needs. Access to water and sanitation is projected to remain a major issue in 2021 , further aggravating the risk of malnutrition. The lack of safe drinking water, in combination with poor hygiene practices leaves a large proportion of South Sudan’s population at persistent risk of preventable waterborne diseases including cholera, AWD,etc. Moreover; RI has lead and participated several integrated Rapid Needs assessments (IRNA) in Upper Nile State later part of 2020, including an interagency rapid multi-sector assessments conducted in Oct 2020 in Ulang county , Sept 2020 in Nasir county & Dec 2020 in Maiwut counties. All these Assessments are mainly revealed the below findings: • Water points were flooded and contaminated during the flooding; people are forced to drink and use contaminated river, swamp and stagnant water close to homes. • There has been no any water treatment practices were observed and people drink water directly from unsafe sources.• The primary water collectors are women and girls have to walk long distance to fetch water and are exposed to GBV and harassments.• open defecation is prevalent, and latrines are not functional, especially in rural areas, except in households where latrine construction was supported by INGOs. Approx. 80%-85% people practice open defecation close to their homes.• unsafe hygiene practices prevail as does lack of handwashing facilities in homes and institutions. Children are particularly prone to water borne diseases since they play in open areas and bathe in stagnant water.• sanitation facilities in health and schools are not functional as they lack regular maintenance, resulting in deteriorating sanitation and environmental health amongst patients, health workers and pupils. • Poor hygiene practices further aggravate health issues and heighten the risk of disease outbreak (including cholera and typhoid). Moreover, frequent flooding, displacement, lack of access due to poor road conditions and conflict have prevented rehabilitation of existing water supply structures. • Lack of awareness, prevention and control measures for prevailing Covid-19 and no control measures or screen processes at the board crossing points to Ethiopia. Through proposed WASH interventions in water supply (water point rehabilitation, construction and water point disinfection), sanitation (latrine construction, waste removal) and hygiene promotion. RI further aims to improve and strengthen ongoing WASH initiatives and expand into further hard to reach locations in Greater Upper region.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

All RI staff and volunteers who are based in the four locations are trained on RI's PSEA and safeguarding policies, and ways to report incidents. Each RI field office has safeguarding champions who are given more intensive training on RI's safeguarding and PSEA approach. During project kick-off meetings with communities RI will inform communities of the PSEA policy, who the safeguarding champions are, what constitutes PSEA, emphasize that all project activities are delivered free of charge and that beneficiaries should not be asked to accept anything in exchange for any goods or services. RI will place posters and visuals outlining RI's policy and where community members can report at distribution sites. Additionally RI will have a help desk manned by RI staff at each distribution site, to collect complaints and feedback and to oversee distributions. Finally, RI's independent MEAL team and project management staff from Juba will conduct ad-hoc monitoring visits to project sites, meeting with community members and will reiterate the training and RI's position on PSEA.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

As highlighted above, the majority of RI proposed locations prefer to give feedback verbally, either directly to RI staff or through community leaders. RI MEAL and WASH staff will conduct monthly monitoring visits and will ensure they meet in person all aspects of the community to gather feedback verbally. Additionally, Water User Committees will receive feedback and complaints directly through the community including marginalized and vulnerable people, RI staff will additionally conduct monitoring visits to field sites to consult with beneficiaries on their satisfaction on RI programming, ways of working and ways to improve. Additionally, at all distribution sites, a complaint desk is present (including membership from local authorities) to address issues raised immediately. Moreover, RI will conduct house to house visit during the hygiene promotion session and other WASH implementation such as registration and verification of beneficiaries for WASH interventions, monitoring household latrine construction, conduct post distribution monitoring and obtain feedback and perception on RI interventions from marginalized and vulnerable disabled and elderly people so as to inform 2021 WASH programing

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

RI will conduct sensitization and mobilization prior to start of project, through consultations with community leaders, both formal and informal. Chiefs, project management committees, water user’scommittees and women/youth groups will be provided with full details of assistance including water, sanitation and hygiene promotion activities. The project introductory meetings in all project areas will include explanation and orientation on how to make feedback and complaints and how the feedback and complaints will be analyzed, and when feedback on complaints and requests received will be given back to the community. Moreover; RI staff and local authority will be trained on how to orient, collect, record beneficiaries’ feedback and complaints received, as well as how and when to respond. all staff will be trained on core data protection and confidentiality principals also. RI has already conducted a AAP study in each location proposed which asked a statistically significant sample size what the preferred means of both receiving information and giving feedback were. Overall, individuals felt more comfortable giving feedback verbally through RI staff or community leaders. Interestingly, females preferred female community leaders or direct to RI staff. Further, all participants stated they preferred to have information shared verbally from RI staff, and then community leaders. A significantly fewer cohortt stated they preferred giving feedback to local authorities. The verbal nature of feedback and information was emphasized throughout and is unsurprising given the literacy levels of the wider population in all counties, and the limited/no access to electricity and mobile phone connection for more advanced feedback mechanisms e.g SMS, hotlines or email. RI will ensure to have the following community engagement throughout the project lifecycle:- Project kickoff and close meetings with Community Leaders, and womens leaders, local authorities, - Monthly FGDs with disaggregated cohorts of the target population- baseline and endline KAP surveys- in-depth interviews with sample beneficiaries- case studies. - PDM for cash and NFI distributions- complaints hot-desk and AAP champions at trainings and distribution activities

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

RI will use the beneficiaries’ feedbacks to shape and adapt into project design and implementation. RI will conduct WASH-NFIs and Hygiene kits post distribution monitoring to get beneficiaries feedback on beneficiaries’ satisfaction on content, quantity and quality of the kits and will change / adjust the kit content, quantity and quality based on the feedback. RI will gather beneficiaries feedback on all WASH hardware activities through face to face meetings on a monthly basis (Rehab and construction of water points, construction of HH and institutional latrines and Waste management and disposal facilities ) and software hygiene promotion activities to shape and adapt beneficiaries feedback in future program design and implementation. Additionally, RI's MEAL team will conduct quarterly FGDs with community members to gain feedback on RI's WASH project activities. RI's response to feedback will be shared back with community leader. Action plans will be developed with RI's project team and where appropriate actions will be followed up to improve the implementation of the project.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

RI will conduct safety audits of all locations, interviewing women and girls and other particularly at risk groups in the community, as well as community leaders to identify particular locations, activities and services where protection violations occur. this mapping will identify where RI's assistance will make the most impact and reduce protection violations from occurring. RI has already completed a comprehensive protection baseline in all proposed locations which emphasized a huge intersection between access to basic WASH facilities and items and prevalence of GBV and other forms of protection violations. Risk 8.1 and 8.2 - These two were identified as a key issued affecting women and girls in RI's locations proposed under this action during RIs protection baseline in September 2020, particularly related to violence/sexual violence occurring during transit to access basic services, and sexual assault/violence when defecting or traveling to bathe. Provision of safe sanitation is expected to reduce exposure of women and girls to potential violence and insecurity while walking long distances for open defecation. RI female staff and volunteers will conduct registration, verification and HP session at the HH level to avoid that women and girls travel to distance venues for WASH assistance. RI will also establish multiple distribution points for WASH NFIs and HKs distribution to ensure women and girls can easily access without harassment or violence. Risk 8.5: RI will make sure all beneficiaries are consulted and informed about RI interventions through meetings, especially through FGDs with women and girls conducted by RI trained female staff and volunteers to get their perception and contribute to WASH design, site selection and implementation. RI will also explain implementation modalities and intervention timelines to target beneficiaries, specifically those designed to mitigate exclusion of women and girls from consultation and decision making process. Risk 8.8 There might be chances of impartial and need based selection of beneficiaries by influences of external stakeholders / local authority directly or indirectly by local authority on humanitarian assistance. RI will use clear selection criteria will be explained to local authority and beneficiaries before the selection process and registration and verification of beneficiaries will also be conducted at house to house to avoid any impartial and double assistances of same beneficiaries. RI's international staff will review the criteria and locations and ensure that there is equity in the distribution list amongst Payams, and that there are no cultural/local bias involved in the decision making. As part of accountability to affected communities, RI will employ two different types of complainant and feedback mechanisms - responsive and proactive, permitting direct and indirect beneficiaries to provide feedback and/or lodge complaints. Moreover, PDM will be conducted to allow beneficiaries to provide their feedback on satisfaction with quality and quantity of goods and items included in the kit. All beneficiary feedbacks and ideas are taken into account to adjust and realign ongoing and future WASH interventions.
