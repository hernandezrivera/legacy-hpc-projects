Project Summary

This project aims to meet the immediate, medium and long-term food security needs of the target communities in Eastern Equatoria, Lakes, Western Bahr el Ghazal, Unity, Jonglei and
Central Equatoria states that were affected by armed conflict, insecurities,
climate change and displacement. This will be achieved by:
a) Access to cereal seeds’ kits: to ensure the target communities recover from hunger, the distribution of cereal seeds’ kits and tools will be conducted. Accompanied by agricultural extension activities, focusing on good agronomic practices (such as season of planting, seedbed preparation, planting and spacing, weed management, irrigation, integrated pest and production management, harvesting and post-harvest management). This intervention will be delivered using lead farmers and farmer field school approaches with demonstration plots.
b) Access to vegetables’ kits: in areas with access to suﬃcient water for vegetable growing, beneficiaries will be provided with vegetables’ seeds’ kits containing a variety of species. The distribution will be conducted with extension training on good agronomic practices including seed and nursery bed preparation, seed planting and spacing, irrigation, integrated pest and production management, harvesting and post-harvesting management.
c) Access to fishing kits: in areas with access to swamps, rivers or lakes, non-food kits with tools for fishery will be provided. Fishing has the potential to immediately address food security needs. It enables people to immediately harvest fish from the rivers and swamps in the areas.
d) Improving the capacity of farmer field schools: lead farmers and extension workers will be trained as trainers for community farmers. The lead farmers and extension workers will then facilitate discussions and demonstration on good agronomic practices for the vegetable and cereal crops producer groups.
e) Setting up irrigation systems: Farmers who grow vegetables will be provided with treadle pumps, watering cans at household levels. Drip irrigation systems will be established at farmer field school gardens or demonstration plots. Drip irrigation systems together with community training on their adequate and sustainable use will enhance the copying mechanism during the dry season.
f) School feeding program: Food for education and school feeding programme will be considered in the project areas where PAH carries food security and livelihood activities with seeds distribution and vegetable and cereal crops production.
g) Conditional General food distribution: as a modality is already being developed and submitted awaiting approval. This will target the most vulnerable food insecure population with limited access to markets and having a poor road network. h) Cash Programming: will be based on initial market and community assessments, considering cash vouchers or cash for work. Food for asset/cash will be conducted in the areas where there are the need and local markets can support the project targeting the establishment of community structures and facilities like schools and infrastructures like community road projects and communal farms establishment.
i) Integration: For more a holistic approach, PAH will integrate interventions with sectors of WASH,
nutrition, education, protection and S/NFI through combined hygiene and nutrition promotion in addition to screening and referral to nutrition partners. Education and health will be integrated through training of school hygiene clubs and school gardening and awareness at the schools, nutrition and health centres. PAH will work with nutrition and health partners like Johanniter in Wau, RMF in Boma while the PAH ERT will integrate the WASH, S/NFI and FSL as in the projects in Magwi, Boma, Koch and Jur River counties since 2017. Protection will be mainstreamed across all sectors and additional protection team will be supporting the community where PAH implements FSL and others

1 - Needs

In August 2019, an estimated 6.35 million people (54% of the population) are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, among whom an estimated 1.7 million people are facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and 10,000 people are in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). Compared to the same period in 2018, there is a slight reduction in the proportion of people facing Phases 3-5 by an estimated 5%. However, high levels of acute food insecurity persist in the country. In both periods, these estimates are in the presence of humanitarian food assistance. In the projection period of September to December 2019, the food security situation is expected to improve as seasonal harvests become available. During this period, an estimated 4.54 million people (39% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse. In the post-harvest period of January to April 2020, the food security situation will deteriorate as household food stocks start depleting and an estimated 5.5 million people (47% of the total population) are likely to face Crisis (IPCPhase 3) or worse. The projection analyses have both factored in the presence of likely humanitarian food assistance. In August 2019, Yirol East of former Lakes The state had an estimated 10,000 people in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). Former Jonglei State has the highest number of people estimated to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 1.25 million people, followed by former Upper Nile State with 845,000 people. In total, 28 counties are classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity in August 2019. In the projection period of September to December 2019, Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity is expected to persist in the four counties of Duk county in former Jonglei State, and Longochuk, Maiwut and Ulang counties in former Upper Nile State. Between January and April 2020, 14 counties are projected to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Former Jonglei State is expected to have the highest number of people estimated to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity at 1.18 million, followed by former Upper Nile State, with 765,000.

2 - Activities

a) Support to beneficiaries through fishery activities. Fishing kits’ (2 x boxes of hooks, 1 x monofilament, 2 x twines), distribution and demonstration on proper use, training on preparation and preservation of fish. b) Support on vegetable production for beneficiaries. Distribution of vegetable kits with seeds from 20g to 100g depends on preference. Training the beneficiaries in good agronomic practices. c) To ensure the success of vegetable production, irrigation systems and adequate training on their use will be provided. Vegetable farmers will be provided with treadle pumps and 10 to 15-litre watering cans. Drip irrigation systems together with community training on their adequate and sustainable use will enhance the copying mechanism during the dry season d) Support for cereal crop production for beneficiaries. Distribution of cereal kits (2-5Kg depending on preference) and culturally acceptable tools. Training the beneficiaries in good agronomic practices. e) Training for lead farmers/extension workers and setting up Lead Farmer Field Schools for a practical demonstration of farming practices. The lead farmers/extension workers will then facilitate discussions and demonstration on good agronomic practices for the vegetable and cereal crops producer groups. Lead farmers will be conducting training sessions for farmers in their fields. f) Food for Education and school feeding programme will be considered in the project areas where PAH carries food security and livelihood activities with seeds distribution and vegetable and cereal crops production. g) Conditional General food distribution: Conditional general food distribution will target the most vulnerable food insecure population with limited access to markets and having a poor road network. h) Cash programming will be based on the initial market and community assessments, considering cash vouchers or cash for work. Cash programming strategies for various sectors are currently developed with the involvement of PAH staff in Cash Working Group. Food for Asset/cash will be conducted in the areas where there is the need, local markets can support the project. This will target the establishment of community structures and facilities like schools and infrastructures like community road projects. i) Mainstreaming cross-cutting issues across all activities with a particular focus on gender mainstreaming, GBV, protection, accountability to the affected population and committing to improving the effectiveness of cooperation with communities. j) Integrated projects: PAH will have an integrated approach with FSL and WASH, NFI and nutrition, specifically community hygiene and nutrition promotion on food hygiene and safe water management to break the chain of contamination at the household level; nutrition screening and referral of SAM/MAM cases to nutrition centres. This will also be integrated with education and health through training of school hygiene clubs and school gardening and awareness at the schools, nutrition and health centres. PAH ERT will continue to integrate the WASH component as PAH WASH and FSL have already been working in integration in Magwi, Boma, Koch and Jur River, Wau counties since 2017. PAH NFI team will work together with other sectors in identifying S/NFI and Protection gaps and respond accordingly. k. Post-distribution (PDM), post-intervention (PIM) monitoring will be done across the project done by independent PAH M&E team, as long as the location is still accessible and secure. PDMs/PIMs gives a clear view of whether beneficiaries have profited from the provided assistance and whether they are satisfied with the quality and safety of carried activities. These reports will provide clear lessons learnt and recommendations and will be used to improve the overall quality of PAH’s projects.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

PAH has high standards of conducting assessments and distributions, always concerning rights of beneficiaries and in full accountability. Before the activities are conducted, the beneficiaries are mobilized and made aware of the project activities, of their rights and selection criteria, what they will get and what is not in the project. Local authorities, chiefs and the entire community are enlightened of the selection criteria and how the registration is done. Beneficiaries will be asked to sign on the forms as a confirmation for receiving assistance. Distributions and other activities are conducted with high sensitivity to the special needs of women, children, elderly and disabled. PAH put strong attention to cultural norms and local law, acts always within the law of South Sudan, while humanitarian standards and international human rights are our highest guidepost. "Do no harm" principle is fully respected. All beneficiaries and affected population are informed about compliant mechanisms, there are multiple ways of asking questions, introducing queries and complaints, which are always considered and answered by our staff. Personal data of beneficiaries are protected according to European standards (GDPR). PAH professional Monitoring Team pays special attention during post-intervention monitoring to check the respect of rights of beneficiaries.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Focus group discussions with various groups of people, including IDPs, returnees and host community, will be conducted to collect information on the needs. FGD take into account the identification of challenges of displaced communities. This information is gathered from the different categories of the target groups and analyzed for challenges. Recommended solutions for effectiveness are put in place for each category of beneficiaries including displaced populations. IDPs are always taken into consideration in assessment, planning, implementation and monitoring of the programs, as often they have special needs, which make their situation more vulnerable. PAH has the capacity for multisectoral programming and will use a wide variety of tools and a broad web of partnerships to comprehensively address the needs of all groups of the affected population.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

PAH will work in close relationship with local communities focusing on traditional structures and mechanisms in coordination, strengthening relations between different stakeholders at the local level with local leaders and community leaders like the Boma chiefs, Payam administrators among others ensuring proper information sharing. During assessment, PAH will involve the community in identifying their needs and coming up with recommendations. Intervention terms of terms of references will be shared with the community leaders and the general community like women groups in FGDs to ensure the community take part in decision making and implementation.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

This project is aligned to the national FSL cluster objectives - Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations(in IPC 5, 4 and 3);Enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing and livestock support (in IPC 5, 4 and 3);Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The protection environment is challenging, due to high needs in the affected population. There are big groups of people requiring professional assistance in terms of healing traumas, GBV case management, child-protection, disabled protection etc. PAH, as very experienced in work across South Sudan, has good capacity in the management of activities in such an environment. The monitoring team has protection-related questionnaires, developed by protection partners, approved by protection cluster. The project measures the safety of beneficiaries by establishing a complaint mechanism, consultative problem identification and solving through dialogue. The tools gather key feedback from beneficiaries on safety related to assistance delivery. Where distributions are conducted, demonstration on the use of items is done and safe disposal of packaging materials is encouraging during the demonstrations. PAH mainstreams protection throughout the project ensuring that activities target the most vulnerable, enhance safety, dignity, and promote and protect the human rights of the beneficiaries without contributing to or perpetuating discrimination, abuse, violence.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

PAH takes steps towards building resilience and reducing future risks for targeted communities. This project will have a component of working in collaboration with National NGO partner like CISDA, having joint activities including using the same resources when going to the field and having joint training. Community farmer groups and extension workers will be trained to enhance self-management and skills to build the capacity to keep on practising proper agronomic practices, PAH will focus on re-establishing traditional structures and mechanisms in coordination, strengthening relations between different stakeholders at the local level and work with local authorities and community leaders. PAH cooperates with local county Agriculture Departments in the different counties and payams thereby building their capacity. Community training will be focused on increasing knowledge about proper agronomic practices as well as training on signs and symptoms of EVD, referrals, treatment, dangers and risks associated which they will continue using this knowledge after the end of the project. Particular focus will be put on women as active members of the water committees to participate in spreading proper hygiene messages in their houses and communities

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

PAH will conduct protection assessments and risk analysis, stakeholders mapping, and needs verification with clear AGD data to understand context dynamics, protection risks, vulnerability level. This will be done through focus group discussions, key informant interviews, meetings and HH surveys with women, men, children, institution workers, community leaders, within IDPs, host community and returnee population. The project has clear risk analysis and mitigation related to access, this includes logistic and security challenges. Ongoing coordination and communication with log cluster is done to support delivery of project items. NGO forum and OCHA is focal point for all discussion regarding access and permission. Support from Access Working group discussed where needed. In addition, PAH will prioritize needs and design all activities with the project interventions in line with the identified and verified needs. Protection assessments and analysis capturing information on access, safety, dignity, participation and power dynamics will be the key methodology where needs are analysed, prioritized with clear recommendations captured from the beneficiaries and later utilized to influence the intervention modalities.
