Project Summary

This project seeks a 12-month period starting March 2022 to provide Critical and Lifesaving Basic Services to Vulnerable Populations in hard to reach areas of Northern Bar el Gazal, and Lakes State
The project will aim to improve the quality of gender and disability-inclusive education services and teachers' capacity to protect the wellbeing of all children and prevent the negative impact of the emergencies on learning environments. In order to ensure increased availability and accessibility of learning opportunities the project will support learning environment, improve infrastructure and also increase WASH activities. The project will also support education in emergencies as well as teacher training and provision of incentives. This project is designed to reduce vulnerabilities and risk associated with poor WASH conditions in schools to reduce the risk of Covid 19, and it also will complement existing long-term Protection of children while in schools
The project will provide equitable and uninterrupted access to safe, protective and inclusive learning, improving cognitive skills and coping abilities of male and female children, adolescents and school aged youths through education in emergency ECD, basic education, youth education and ALP, and ensuring girls and boys are protected through increased access to psychosocial support, life skills and referral pathways
This project is designed to provide intervention in improving equitable access to safe and protective education services targeting 5,778 emergency affected IDPs(400), returnees(200), spontaneous refugees(40), and 5,138 host community children including children with disabilities and youth (girls and boys) with education and integrated WASH minimum package aiming at breaking WASH-related diseases transmission cycle by improving access to safe water and promote safe hygiene practices in communities showing a high prevalence of GAM and high WASH vulnerabilities Under Health and education component.HACT will work to Reducing the prevalence of WASH-related diseases and risk of the outbreak by transmission contexts upon epidemiological investigation working in coordination to the Health partner in Aweil ,Rumbek North and Rumbek centre counties.
HACT response will involve integrated specific activities for GBV mitigation including safety audits on existing and new infrastructure alongside referral services to survivors and provision of lifesaving GBV, Child Protection, Psychosocial support, and Trauma healing services.HACT joins the strategic orientation of WASH, and Education clusters for scaled-up response to multi-faceted needs in Aweil center, Rumbek North and Rumbek centre
The project will be implemented in three Counties where HACT has established presence Aweil center,Rumbek North and Rumbek Center with huge Education and WASH gaps and prioritized, which directly answers the prioritized needs listed in the allocation strategy in terms of education, Health, Nutrition, WASH, Food Security, and Protection. The project will relate to sartorial objective #1(Improve equitable access to safe and protective education services for 866,000 emergency affected IDPs, returnees, spontaneous refugees, and host community children including children with disabilities and youth (girls and boys), #2(Improve the quality of gender and disability-inclusive education services and teachers' capacity to protect the wellbeing of all children and prevent the negative impact of the emergencies on learning environments and #3(Strengthen/enhance the education system’s response capacity of the school community and national/local education actors to mitigate the impact of existing emergencies on learners, teachers, and communities)
Focusing on the Strategic Objective #1:Vulnerable people who experience multi-sectoral severity levels of 4 and 5 have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral essential services to meet their needs.

1 - Needs

People in South Sudan continue to face deteriorating humanitarian conditions. Their situation is worsened by endemic violence, conflict, access constraints and operational interference, public health challenges such as direct and indirect effects of COVID-19 (WHO Report march 2022).Climatic shocks resulting in the dual phenomena of extraordinary flooding and localized drought, which have a severe impact on people’s livelihoods, hampers access to education and water, sanitation and hygiene and health services. Less than 30% of the communities assessed reported their main source of drinking water is an improved source (borehole, tap stand & water-yard) and available in under 30 minutes, with over 70% of the proportion of assessed settlements reporting the main source of drinking water is an unimproved source (river, swamp, unprotected well), (REACH, 2021). South Sudan’s education system is characterized as a low investment, low capacity, but high demand system. The state building and peace building efforts of the national plans put high demand on the education system to expand fast, reduce inequity, and provide appropriate teaching.UNICEF HAC report of march 2022 also highlights that that 4.5 million children need humanitarian assistance in South Sudan. According to IOM report November 2021, about 60 per cent of IDPs and returnees live in settlements where no more than half of the children are attending primary education, mainly in Northern Bahr el Ghazal, and Lakes state.Torrential rains in the months of August – October 2021 have resulted in massive flooding thereby affecting access to basic needs and education. Remoteness and access issues into locations of,Awei centrel and Rumbek centre and North have further worsened the already dire situation with majority of the schools assessed lacking essential WASH infrastructure, children and teachers reliant on unsafe water due to inadequate safe water sources/points and these makes them more vulnerable to Covid 19 and also unqualifying for school feeding programme. Continued conflict and instability in the country combined with flooding have resulted in large-scale internal and cross-border displacement. At the same time, limited improvements in some areas have prompted some people to spontaneously return. In addition, the government, with the support of some humanitarian agencies - has facilitated returns to certain areas. Due to compounding shocks, both in areas of displacement and return, populations have been forced to keep displacing time and again. South Sudan’s education system is characterized as a low investment, low capacity, but high demand system. The state building and peace building efforts of the national plans put high demand on the education system to expand fast, reduce inequity, and provide appropriate teaching. But public expenditure is one of the world’s lowest for education. UNESCO October report states that some 2.4 million children are out of school in 2021. The majority of them are girls. Children in rural areas and IDP, refugee and returnee children are most vulnerable. About 60 per cent of IDPs and returnees live in settlements where no more than half of the children are attending primary education, mainly in Northern Bahr el Ghazal, and Lakes state. . The situation further worsened countrywide with the temporary closure of schools due to COVID-19, disrupting education and limiting children’s access to essential services like school feeding programmes, information on disease prevention and access to water and sanitation.COVID-19 response activities are entirely new and require special arrangements with additional resources to implement education in emergencies’ activities in order to bring back children to schools. Support to learning environment, teaching and learning, psychosocial support programming, infrastructure improvement, school WASH activities, teacher training and provision of more resources to respond to education needs.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

HACT will work to mitigate beneficiaries’ exposer to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services through AAP committee information sharing that seeks to reduce such associated risks including PSEA. This response includes Community Response and feedback Mechanism handled by trained staff with a clear referral pathway working in coordination with existing humanitarian actors in the project area. 90% of the project staff will be from project location who understand project area and security situation and impending desputes. HACT gives a main concern on applying and committing to Do No Harm principles through all project design, activities and results. HACT will save no efforts on following these principles and mainstreaming the protection through this project life cycle, including consulting with the stockholders, coordinating with partners and cluster, ensure safe and productive environment, providing equitable access to project services without discrimination and promoting the social cohesion. HACT will therefore apply the following principles during risk protection and application of mitigation measures. 1.Prioritizing the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and avoid causing harm: HACT takes great concern for the safety and dignity of individuals and avoiding causing further harm is central to mainstreaming protection. The safety of beneficiaries should be the primary consideration in humanitarian action. But being safe is not enough if people do not have their dignity: people need to feel valued, have a sense of self-respect and feel they have some control over their lives. Prioritizing safety and dignity can also help to prevent or minimize the possible unintended negative effects that our interventions can cause for beneficiaries. 2. Ensuring meaningful access to assistance and services: Recognized as a crucial component of all humanitarian interventions, HACT will seek to ensure that people have a meaningful opportunity to attain their rights by accessing available services and assistance. Conflict and divisions within society often emerge from discrimination resulting in individuals or groups being unable to access services. Barriers to access can exist in different forms, including logistical and social/cultural factors, insecurity and a lack of information on available assistance and services. 3. Accountability: HACT will set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints. Mechanisms for collecting feedback will use a combination of channels. Channels include active channels, where project beneficiaries perspectives are deliberately and systematically solicited (such as focus groups); reactive channels, where project beneficiaries have more control over when and how they share perspectives (such as complaint lines); and finally, ‘open channels’ where feedback is solicited in an ad hoc manner, through ongoing interaction during implementation. 4. Participation and empowerment: HACT would strive to put affected populations at the center of decision-making processes that affect their lives so that services are choice-driven and beneficiary focused. Special attention will also be paid to ensure the participation of and consultation with, the most at risk and/or marginalized members of society whose voices may not otherwise be heard. Humanitarian programming should also seek to support the development of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights, including (not exclusively) the rights to shelter, food, water and sanitation, health, and education
