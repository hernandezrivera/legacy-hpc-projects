Project Summary

On 9th July 2011 South Sudan attained her independence as a newly formed nation and was struggling to rebuild after its social-political and economic structures were destroyed by the war. While the country was towards development though with some challenges there erupted another conflict in December 2013 which totally distorted everything. Communities have suffered because of this conflict, war and disorder which resulted into destruction of livelihoods and property, displacement, hunger and diseases, widespread rape (GBV) incidences, torture of innocent individuals especially women, children and the elderly, inter tribal fighting, cattle raids and revenge and forced conscription of child soldiers. This disorder in the community and the whole of South Sudan worsened the situation that was already on recovery after independence. This situation needs redress so that people can realize and enjoy freedom, equality, justice, peace, access to basic services, livelihoods and economic opportunities. Redress is only possible by reconciliation, tolerance and peace building, transforming from violence to peace for all. Humane Aid for Community Organization (HACO) believes that peace building is part of humanitarian assistance and that communities have capacity and can be supported to break cycles of violence and conflict to build sustainable peace for their development.
HACO works with vulnerable communities to alleviate suffering caused by war and conflict and bring back hope of peace and life. HACO through its offices in Rumbek center and Yirol East counties in Lakes State, seeks to promote 'Peace building and peaceful coexistence among the communities in Yirol East and Yirol West. The cattle raids and revenge killings/attacks in the area have resulted into continued displacements, communal violence, denying individuals access to basic services (health, water, education) provided in the community, destruction of social structures, property and infrastructure in majority of the locations. The project seeks to provide awareness and dialogue to access peaceful reconciliation, negotiations and mediation among households/families and different communities for peaceful co-existence in Yirol East and Yirol West Counties; awareness and mass campaigns with all existing community structures and institutions (Governmental and non-governmental) that provide services.
The project targets to reach 40, 000 individuals (17,248 women, 5,152 men, 13,552 girls and 4,048 boys) in both Yirol East and Yirol West from host communities, IDPs and returnees.
The project through traditional leaders, the local government and other institutions will conduct dialogues and awareness sessions on peace building with the communities (men, women, boys and girls (youth and children) in the area. Will look into traditional and cultural practices that trigger and encourage violence and revenge for individual/family/clan wealth acquisition at the expense of other community members' property, discrimination and marginalization on basis of ethnicity and rationally look for remedial measures to be exercised by the communities for durable peace building.

1 - Needs

Conflict and war have made people suffer and feel unsafe even in their own homes. Violence, cattle raiding and revenge, killings and attacks among the people in Lakes State extend to areas of Terekeka Central Equatoria and Juru county . There has been serious conflict between the pastoral community and the farming community because of, pasturing on crop farms in areas other than of the pastoral communities. Cattle raiding and revenge among the youth and communities to get heads of cattle for bride price continued as a cultural norm however this worsened instability, created hostility and hatred among the communities in Lakes State. The 2013 and 2015 conflicts that spread to many parts of South Sudan made the situation worse. Violent cattle raiding went on to the extent of warring among cattle owning clans/tribes and even spilling over to the communities in Terekeka and Mundri East. The cattle raiding combined with conflict/war situation that spilled into the area affecting the women, children, the elderly and other vulnerable people; destruction of property went from that of individuals to public/communal property like schools, health facilities, water points; security and physical protection of the community was disturbed as women and girls were raped, forcefully married and young men forcibly recruited to serve with the army and armed groups. Government and agencies worked together communities to reconciliation through dialogue for durable peace to promote development in the area. Humane Aid for Community Organisation (HACO) with UNDP from September 2015 -March 2017 worked on "Peace building and coexistence" program in Yirol East. The situation is still fragile it needs to be addressed with communities for extended protection. Communities need awareness to be educated more on tolerance of each other in order to coexist, how to mitigate protection risks, respect law and order and be aware that sustainable peace building requires ideological transformation of the society from violence to dialogue as a means of resolving conflict. The project targets to reach 40, 000 individuals (17,248 women, 5,152 men, 13,552 girls and 4,048 boys) in both Yirol East and Yirol West. HACO with an office in Yirol East currently, will be able to undertake the project and extend its operation to Yirol west to serve in the area promoting long lasting peace restoration and peaceful coexistence among the conflicting communities. It has been noted that during the conflict there have been incidences of rape, forced early marriage and the related GBV cases which also precipitate conflict. HACO in efforts of peace building with the communities will therefore consult the GBV cluster for technical guidance on how to conduct awareness to the community on GBV, the GBV referral pathway for the survivors, train case workers and explain on available and accessible medical and psycho-social support services for case management. PSS first aid will be provided at the women/girls friendly space or any community social facility in the area designated by the community leaders and protection committee. In consultation with the GBV cluster and Health Cluster medical services for the GBV survivors (Clinical Management of Rape) will be as a priority integrated at the nearby health facility to ease medical referral. The GBV cluster will also advise on how the survivor can proceed for legal redress as a protection measure on the violated rights and regain dignity for peaceful coexistence in future.The aforementioned Communities have lived under threat and fear of attacks, there is a need to restore peace for every one to live in safety, without fear or threat of violence; people have to know that there is no form of violence tolerated by law. This state of peace evolved will reinstate the integrity of the communities, transform their lives and give room for every one in Lakes State without discrimination to be part of the social and economic development.

2 - Activities

Instability after war, conflict and violence frequently reveals the reality that grievances or other causes of conflict have not been addressed and may erupt again. To effect the peace building and coexistence, HACO with the community and other protection stakeholders in Yirol East and Yirol West counties in Lakes State will undertake the following activities under the project:Activity 1: Community dialogue and awareness raising on legal awareness on human rights, peace building and coexistence.Activity 2: Engagement of neighboring counties/payams in dialogue on good neighborhood, peaceful coexistence and reconciliation than violence.Activity 3: Work with the other existing community based committees e.g GBV , Protection, Child Protection, WASH and Health etc. to have dialogue and an integrated approach to address issues that perpetuate conflict and misunderstanding in the community. Activity 4: In consultation with GBV cluster conduct awareness on GBV, referral pathway, available and accessible medical and psycho-social support services to the survivors for healing, recovery and reintegration in the community.Activity 5: Awareness and training with government law enforcement organs (police, prisons, judiciary, customary courts) on peaceful coexistence as agents of change since everyone is equal before the law, the systems for justice are to be trusted, fair and effect laws by protecting people’s rights (the government is accountable to the people).Activity 6: Engage traditional/customary leaders/chiefs in dialogue among communities if still in rivalry, local government, religious leaders trained on transitional justice. Activity 7 : Conduct peace building awareness sessions and awareness on human rights among the warring communitiesActivity 8: Conduct joint awareness with other partners and stakeholders to integrate the delivery of services to demonstrate to the community that everyone has fair and equal access to the basic needs for their well being – such as food, clean water, shelter, education, healthcare and a decent living environmentActivity 9: Conduct peace building awareness training in schools, awareness on "Rights of the child" and "Obligations of the child" to the family and the community. Together with education sector devise special peace education for the children to grow knowing more of the importance of peaceful environment than violence.Activity 10: Integrate psycho-social support and mental health services to GBV survivors, children and youth released from armed forces by referring them to the nearby referral Health center.Activity 11: Innovate sports activities for the youth (boys and girls) to encourage friendly interaction and peace education for the youth who are prone to violence.Activity 12: With the community consultation and involvement set up a community peace center which is user friendly to everybody in the community without segregation or discrimination.Activity 13: Undertake protection assessment with other stakeholders to understand the situation and the impact of the project among the community.Stability means the absence of violence, when people can get on with their lives after the fighting stops. The current lull, dubbed as ‘negative peace’, should be built upon and not be allowed to degenerate to another crisis. Temporary stops in fighting without sustained peace building efforts help to explain why a third or more of peace agreements in South Sudan break down.The challenge as of now should be to use periods of stability to build longer-term, ‘positive’ peace' where everybody should have in their mind that what happened should not be repeated and let everyone be a change agent to ensure that people are safe from violence and conflict (harm), have access to law and justice (and their human rights are not violated), are included in the political decisions that affect them, have access to better economic opportunities, and enjoy better social services and livelihoods.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes. The project is linked to the government and community systems that is why from the beginning the community structures and the government will be involved to ensure they accept and accommodate the project for the benefit of the affected communities. The project intends to give hand to the government in its initiative to bring lasting peace to the citizens. The project will be implemented with support from the government organs/sectors that will ensure safe environment for the project to take off and continue for the benefit of the people in the area. Peace building starts in the household to the community and to the nation.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes. The project is aligned with the national objectives of peace building in the communities and supporting peaceful coexistence and security for the citizens to leave protected and in harmony. The National objective is to bring long lasting peace, the project is for peace building and coexistence of all the people without discrimination which is in line with the national objectives.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes. The overall security situation is still subject to wait and see, however with the on going implementation of the peace negotiations at the country level it is anticipated that the situation will continue improving to allow humanitarian and development activities continue as planned. Working together with the government security organs and the protection committees and the community will equip the project team with all the necessary information needed to ensure implementation is done in a protection environment which is sufficiently safe and stable.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes. The project includes sustainability as it is looking into long term peace building and coexistence. The communities in the areas have been looking for sustainable peace because they are tired of war, conflict and tension. The project involving all levels of people in the community with support of the government to implement the project is with the intention to have the community own the project from its inception dealing with the problem. Understanding that humanity lasts than everything achieved through engagement in peace building will make the peace and coexistence long lasting achievement. The handover of the project will be handing over people responsibility over their own people and their own lives because peace means life.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

The project will ensure from its offset members of the minority groups and the vulnerable are consulted and their views incorporated in the plan. Women and girls together with men and youth will all be consulted and given opportunity to continue being part of the project so as to be able to build sustainable peace. Through mass awareness campaigns the community will be informed of where to access services e.g. GBV, Child protection and other services from other integrated sectors. Through engagement of community protection and security committees beneficiaries will be secured and protected as they go for meetings, distributions and accessing other basic services . Any activity undertaken will ensure to include the most vulnerable households without favor to community or tribe which can intensify tension and lead to conflict. Teams will always complete a stakeholder analysis before entering the field and will adjust to the situation on the ground. HACO will ensure a risk assessment, stake holder analysis and protection analysis is carried out before any intervention. This is to ensure that the teams are fully aware of relationship dynamics on the ground and have mitigated against any potential harm, however where intervention will do more harm better hold the intervention. HACO will ensure beneficiaries have access to the complaint box and can freely write, talk in meetings and FDGs to express their opinion as this is for the advantage of the project. HACO will complete a protection risk/mitigation analysis and safety audit to assess any risks for beneficiaries while attending activities. HACO will mitigate against the risks that will be identified and make sure the implementation of activities does not compromise with the safety and security of the beneficiaries at all.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The project team/s will work with the community, protection committees and protection actors to identify the most vulnerable from the time of project inception. In integration with other sectors, assistance will be given to those who need it. Through focused group discussion, home visits by the community outreach volunteers and meetings during the implementation the vulnerable will be able to give feedback on the project, if anything confidential they will be able to give it in confidentiality, if something needs correction will be addressed immediately and the people will be timely informed. A suggestion box will also be fixed where it can be easily accessed for individual to write their complaint, suggestions and feed back on the overall project implementation. At the end of the project, exit interviews will be conducted with the most vulnerable community members and their caretakers.
