Project Summary

Centre for Emergency and Development Support (CEDS) proposes to reach a total of 30,974 households (185,842) women, men, girls, boys including people with disabilities) in Aweil East, Aweil North and Aweil South Counties in Northern Bahr el Ghazal state, Chueibet, Rumbek Centre and Aweirial Counties of lakes State and Twic County of Warrap state with essential life-saving food security and livelihoods assistance to improve the food security situation of the IDPs, Returnees, flood, drought and conflict affected households in the three states. To achieve this, CEDS plans to train the affected households on best sustainable farming practices and fishing techniques as well as support the farming and fishing households with assorted seeds for crop and vegetable production during the main planting season and dry season. CEDS will also distribute assorted farm and fishing tools to facilitate farming and fishing activities, provide monitoring support visits to provide technical support and guidance to assisted households and as well in marketing their produce to generate household income. As a means of ensuring accountability to affected communities, CEDS intends to ensure all targeted beneficiaries are able to receive relevant and timely information about the project and are also give an opportunity to give feedback about the project implementation and the assistance they are receiving. Feedback obtained will be used to shape the project implementation and improve programming. CEDS will also put in place concrete mitigation measures to ensure that vulnerable community members (people with disabilities, the illiterate persons, women and girls) are included in the project activities and their feedback is taken into consideration. To ascertain beneficiary safety, CEDS will make sure all project activities, personnel and organization operations do not in any way cause harm to the beneficiaries through putting in place concrete, measures to mitigate any PSEA and safeguarding concerns. CEDS will seek to uphold the core humanitarian standards (CHS) and protection principles throughout the project implementation in an effort to ensure, relevant and timely assistance is provided and, in a fair, transparent, participatory and inclusive manner. As a consequence of this project, it is anticipated that the food security situation for supported households will improve, and their resilience built to withstand future shocks.

1. Needs

The food insecurity levels remain elevated in South Sudan with the situation expected to worsen further in 2024. The recent IPC Acute Food Insecurity report (November 2023) projects that an estimated 5.78 million women, men, girls and boys in the country in the period between December 2023 - March 2024 will face crisis (IPC phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity compared to 7.10 million (April – July 2024) expected to face crisis IPC phase 3 acute food insecurity, a rise of 10.2% from March 2024. Northern Bahr El Ghazal, Greater Pibor Administrative area, lakes state, Warrap Upper Nile and Jonglei states have been projected to be the most food insecure states with at least 655,000 people in Aweil East, Aweil North, Aweil South and Aweil West counties anticipated to face Crisis and Emergency (IPC phase 3 and 4) acute food insecurity compared to 827,000 people in the neighboring Twic, Tonj North, Tonj South, Gogrial East, Gogrial West counties and 444,000 people in Cueibet, Awerial, Rumbek East, Rumbek Centre, Rumbek North, Wulu, Yirol East and Yirol West counties. The deterioration in the food security situation is generally attributed to: recurrent flooding, influx of returnees and IDPs of Sudan conflict, long dry spell and conflicts and insecurity triggered by cattle raiding and intercommunal conflicts that results in destruction and disruption of crop production and livelihoods as well as massive displacements; long dry spells; high food prices in the markets triggered by road inaccessibility, a result of the poor road conditions especially during heavy rains; and a reduced household incomes that limits household purchasing power. The ongoing widespread food insecurity has forced majority of the affected households to resort to negative coping strategies like eating smaller portions of food, consuming one meal per day in most instances a less diverse diet consisting of only staple foods, borrowing from others and sale of productive assets to obtain food. High levels of food insecurity, IPC phase 3 and beyond will also contribute to acute malnutrition in most of the counties. In fact, based on the analysis and estimations from the results of the SMART nutrition surveys, FSNMS and program admission trends approximately 1.4 million children under the age of 5 years between January – December 2023 are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition. Of the total, 26% account for severely and moderately acutely malnourished (SAM and MAM) children. The food insecurity situation is foreseen to become worse due to un-receding floods, prolonged conflicts and insecurity and poor harvests experienced in 2023. By April 2024, a large proportion of the household food reserves will be depleted therefore prompting most households to be dependent on the markets. Continued soaring food prices will make it much more difficult for a significant proportion of the affected households (most of whom have low to no income) to access food supplies. In regard to this, CEDS proposes to implement food security and livelihood activities that will contribute to an increase in crop production and improved livelihoods to households thereby improving their nutrition status and their living standards.

2. Project activities

- Identification, registration and verification of farmer households for vegetable and cereal crop production for the main planting season and vegetable production for dry season.- Secure and distribute assorted vegetable and crop seeds such as amaranth, okra, eggplant, kales, tomato onions, sorghum, groundnut, cassava and maize to registered farmer households.- Secure and distribute assorted farming tools such as hoes, rakes and watering cans to registered farmer households.- Carry out an onsite and post distribution monitoring of assorted farming tools, vegetable and crop seeds distributed.- Group the registered farmer households to groups each consisting of 25 members and identify and select lead farmers.- Establish farm demonstration plots to facilitate practical learning and replication to individual farm households.- Train selected lead farmers on best agricultural practices and post-harvest management.- Through WASH and Nutrition partners, identify lead mother-to-mother support group members and lead community water user committee members.- Train lead mother to mother support group members and lead water user committee members on kitchen gardening techniques.- Train community leaders and stakeholders on community-based disaster risk reduction.- Support mother to mother support groups and water user committee groups to set up kitchen gardens near the nutrition centres and near water source points that can be used for food supply and demonstration to mothers and caregivers of children receiving nutrition support and community members accessing water from water points.- Mapping of the fishing areas and validation of needs.- Identify, register and verify fishermen households.- Secure and distribute fishing kits to registered fishermen.- Training of fisherfolks on fishing techniques and fish preservation methods.- Carry out an onsite and post distribution monitoring on fishing tools distributed.- Conduct routine onsite supervision, trainings and monitoring of farming and fishing activities.- Form poultry producer and seed producer groups each consisting of 20 members.- Train poultry producer groups and seed producer groups on poultry farming and seed multiplication respectively as well as marketing and support in linkages to buyers and markets.- Conduct regular monitoring of group activities, providing technical support and guidance.- Form Village Savings Loans Associations (VSLAs) and train them on entrepreneurship, financial literacy and loan disbursements.- Support VSLA meetings and activities and carry out routine monitoring and supervision support.- Carry out routine community information sharing and feedback collection meetings
