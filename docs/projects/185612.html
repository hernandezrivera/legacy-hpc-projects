Project Summary

IOM’s Child Protection Project in 2022 aims to continue to prevent and respond to child protection issues and empower vulnerable girls, boys, parents and caregivers in camps by strengthening the protective environment through a multi-sectoral approach and community-based interventions.
To achieve its objectives, IOM’s prevention and response programme will focus on enhancing community-based protection with an age and gender-appropriate participatory approach to foster social cohesion.
IOM will strengthen Community-based Protection (CBP) Mechanism in the camp to enhance the capacity of communities to protect children with the participation and inclusion of children and youth through groups of different ages and gender (including adolescents, girls and women-led groups). IOM will focus on child protection issues and risks including potential consequences of prolonged displacement in order to foster resilience through community-led activities and empower the wider community to proactively identify and address protection issues.
Child protection system strengthening will focus on capacity building of the Community Safety Forum, community-based protection groups of different ages and gender, community leaders, religious leaders as well as community volunteers.
IOM will ensure gender equity for menstrual health education, building on IOM research and developing an evidence-based curriculum with other complementary outreach tools focusing on life skills and male engagement in cooperation with the IOM GBV Team and Research Unit.
IOM will also provide specialised child protection services, including case management, structured static and mobile psycho-social support (PSS) to reach out to boys and girls, including adolescents, without dignified and safe access to services and facilities, with special focus on children with disabilities. IOM will continue to support child survivors of GBV in the camps and in IOM safe shelter.
IOM PSS will build on cultural education to nurture Rohingya roots and identity and promote intergenerational dialogue. Child Friendly Corners within the Women and Girls Safe Spaces will provide PSS for children, whose caregivers are participating in WGSS activities, as part of IOM's integrated protection services. Aiming to increase engagement with boys, IOM plans to provide traditional livelihood skills training. In addition, parents and caregivers will be provided PSS and positive parenting support. IOM programming incorporates age, gender, and diversity needs of the affected population in line with COVID-19 protective measures and mainstreaming to respond to specific needs, especially to enable girls and boys, including adolescents and children with disabilities to be instrumental in improving the quality of their life and mitigating risks and dangers in their community and living environment.
IOM's integrated protection services include General Protection, GBV and Counter-trafficking, interweaving child protection capacity building and awareness raising throughout their programmes in the camps and in host communities, with the technical support of the Child Protection team.
With a cross-cutting approach, IOM closely cooperates with other sectors and IOM units such as Health/MHPSS (including the Rohingya Cultural Memory Centre), Shelter, WASH, Site Management/Communication with Communities and Social Cohesion to mainstream child protection, child safeguarding, age, gender and disability throughout their programme and to coordinate disaster preparedness and emergency response.
IOM will mainstream and expand PSEA Champion component to strengthen PSEA reporting, awareness and response effectively to the IOM Community Feedback Mechanisms.
IOM is committed to child protection inter-agency efforts and is an active member to the CP Sub-Sector, CP Case Management Technical Working Group, Anti-Trafficking Working Group, MHPSS Working Group and PSEA Network as well as field level coordination.

1 - Identified Needs

For the over 900,000 Rohingya in Bangladesh and affected host communities, 2022 marks the fifth year of the humanitarian response with the needs still in a dire situation. The environment remains congested, coupled with the socio-economic disadvantages, restriction of movement, lack of livelihood, educational opportunities, escalating violence, environmental hazards including cyclone and monsoon conditions, and the COVID-19 pandemic have all contributed to a rapid increase in vulnerability of children to violence, neglect, exploitation and abuse. Children represent 51 per cent of the refugee population. Due to the prolonged period of uncertainty about the future of the Rohingya community, the physically and mentally challenging living environment, disastrous fire incidents, monsoon floods, the disorienting security situation due to violence and threats of criminal groups, relocation activities of the government to different camps and Bhasan Char, as well as the different restrictions imposed by the Government of Bangladesh to limit protection activities as prevention measures to contain COVID-19, new protection risks have emerged and further needs has been identified. According to an IMMAP survey, the impact of COVID-19 on children, boys and girls, adolescents and caregivers (including elderly caregivers) was heavily impacted by the closure of Child Protection and Educational facilities- which only re-opened after 18 months in September 2021 in limited capacity. Child wellbeing was affected due to loss of daily routine and psychosocial distress, as well as limited access to basic needs and services, leading to negative impact on children’s development. IOM protection monitoring indicates that children are increasingly engaging in child labour, drug dealing and gambling, peer violence and there has been an increasing trend in violence against children, child marriage, child trafficking and GBV incidents. Due to economic struggles, families send their children to work in or outside the camps, where they are often are exploited and at risk or being trafficked. Based on the findings of a recent Child Protection Assessment (CPSS), the increasing violence against children are perceived by the community as a result of lockdown, lack of cultural acceptance, parental separation and parental stress. As a long-term impact for boys and girls not having access to education and PSS/life skills activities for 18 months, IOM observed that children are less motivated and interested in rejoining activities since they reopened in Sept 2021. Instead, children continue to earn income or stay in the shelter doing household chores, caring for sick and older relatives and getting married earlier. According to a REACH Report on Age and Disability Inclusion, children with disabilities were disproportionately affected as they had less or no access to any services during the COVID-19 pandemic.For prevention and responding to child protection concerns, community-based child protection needs to be strengthened to promote inclusion, participation, meaningful access to services in a dignified way, especially for children with disabilities and persons with diverse sexual orientations, gender identities and expressions, and sex characteristics (SOGIESC) for a safe transition of sustainable community protection mechanisms. Empowerment of the refugee community, religious leaders, volunteers as well as children/adolescents through capacity building and awareness to enable them for a greater participation in decision making concerning their life situations; community-led activities and delivering key messages through community members targets a wider audience to reach high-risk adolescents and their families. Life-saving individual case management services and MHPSS activities need to be increased to address the current high volume of protection concerns.

2 - Activities or outputs

1- Capacity building of Community-based Protection Committees- groups of Adolescents (aged 12 - 17), parents, caregivers, families of children/persons with disabilities to learn to identify risks, gaps, barriers to access to services, promoting positive social and behavioural change. Groups will be formed at block-level separately for boys and girls and ensure equal participation, including for children with disabilities.2- Support groups (Community-based Protection Committees, groups of Adolescents (aged 12 - 17), parents, caregivers, families of children/persons with disabilities) to develop 20 community-led initiatives to raise awareness, mitigate or address child protection issues.3-Capacity building to Majis, Imams, teachers, and humanitarian partners on Child Protection, prevention and response, principles, identification and referral mechanism to support CP system strengthening.4- Provide PSEA ToT training on the obligatory SEA reporting system for humanitarian actors, community leaders and community volunteers to support raising awareness in the community.5- PSS activities through mobile child-friendly spaces/adolescent groups to ensure safe access to age-appropriate, disability-inclusive and gender-sensitive structured psychosocial, recreational and cultural activities for boys and girls.6- Provide menstrual health education through peer-based models building on the findings of IOM´s summary report Ma Boinor Rosom or “Mother and Sister´s Ways” and its related curriculum. 7- Traditional skills training for boys and girls in cooperation with the Rohingya Cultural Memory Centre to nurture the Rohingya identity of children and adolescents such as basketry, embroidery design, micro gardening, music through Rohingya tutors. Caregivers meeting the criteria will be linked up with IOM's Social Cohesion Unit's skills trainings projects to enhance greater self-reliance.8- Provision of PSS support and positive parenting sessions for caregivers, including caregivers of children with disabilities.9- CP case management services to children at risk and survivors of child abuse, neglect, violence and exploitation, including alternative care, considering age and gender-appropriate solutions, family tracing and reunification, comprehensive support to boys and girls with disabilities, their carers and families, services for female and male child survivors of sexual abuse.10- Provision of temporary Safe Shelter for child survivors.11- Continue CP mainstreaming and safeguarding through a multi-sectoral and participatory approach, involving the community, children and caregivers, also promoting “Do no harm" principles in all IOM programming to prevent further harm to the community. Safeguarding visits and sessions in Health facilities and distribution points to prevent child labour and other exploitation in cooperation with Site Management, Site Development, Shelter and WASH to support the community to develop risk mapping and mitigation plans, inclusive to disaster risk reduction in monsoon and cyclone season, and COVID-19 mitigation measures.12- Emergency Preparedness and Response- the CP team (part of PERU team) and Rohingya volunteers will reach out to vulnerable groups and disseminate information for risk mitigation. CP will coordinate with other agencies and units to re-establish meaningful access to humanitarian services and monitor CP trends for new or emerging threats and risks and apply mitigation measures.
