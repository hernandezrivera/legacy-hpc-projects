Project Summary

Unemployment rate has continued to deteriorate over the past several years and remains high at 21.9% according to the latest official statistics. Even if a worker has a job, majority of economic activities are estimated to take place in the informal sector, where workers are exposed to decent work deficits such as in terms of lack of access to appropriate wages, social protection coverage, OSH, career development opportunities and prospects, to name but a few.
Accordingly, ILO proposes to implement two-year employment-intensive infrastructure recovery works with the dual objective of decent job creation and community infrastructure rehabilitation. This approach maximizes the wages rate as percentage of the total expenditures for infrastructure works through alternative engineering solutions. It also promotes decent work principles throughout project lifecycle. The project although it will support the people to meet their immediate needs however, as ILO we do encourage our target groups to be productive, self-reliance and experience good exposure to the labour market especially for youth and women through providing decent employment.
This can be achieved through short-term jobs (3 months) through the community works, roads rehabilitation, and cultural heritage site maintenance. In addition, formal apprenticeship programmes will be implemented with the aim of increase the long-term employability by supporting skills development through on the job training in partnership with local enterprises. The estimated budget is USD 11,614,498 for two years implementation with the aim of creating 4,498 decent short term decent jobs with 323,820 workdays, and 900 beneficiaries enrolled in the apprenticeship programme, generating 93,600 workdays. The proposed interventions will be implemented in Aleppo, Homs, Hama, Lattakia, and Rural Damascus governorates based on the following criteria: number of people in need and target as set by ERL sector; high severity of needs; actual needs for infrastructure improvement for accessibility to basic services; and the presence of ILO and its partners in the targeted areas.
Protection risk 1: There is a risk of biased selection of community members (workers) who engage in the proposed infrastructure works and apprenticeship programmes, at the exclusion of others. Such a skewed selection does more harm than good in terms of social cohesion.(Likelihood: low, Impact: high).
Mitigation plan 1: The project will establish a local steering committee for each area with the equal representation of female, male, youth, people with disability, internally displaced persons, and other concerned parties, to give voices to all in an inclusive manner. Recruitment advertisement will be publicly announced, with an established and agreed upon quota for the particularly vulnerable categories, based on vulnerability assessment.
Protection risk 2: Occupational injuries of workers at worksites due to exposure to biological, chemical and physical occupational hazards without control measures put in place.(Likelihood: High, Impact: High)
Mitigation plan 2: Occupational safety and health (OSH) training will be conducted prior to start of any worksite activities for infrastructure works, for both workers and employers. Safety managers will be recruited to ensure the safety of the works, while all workers will be covered by injury and health insurance. Targeted training and labour inspection will be conducted at factories for apprenticeship programmes to ensure the compliance of OSH measures by employers.
GBV Risk – Female workers may be exposed to worksite harassment by co-workers or employers during the activities’ execution.(Likelihood: medium, Impact: high).
Mitigation plan – GBV training will be conducted for each worksite to raise awareness in prevention of violence and harassment at work. Assign Female team leaders as a focal point. A help line will be established to receive complaints and provide necessary measures.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The project has two outputs: decent short-term jobs and long-term employment. The first output will be achieved through local community works with workers recruited with a formal employment contract for 3 months with minimum wage of 5 USD/day to rehabilitate the basic infrastructure. The components and activities of Output 1 are i) Community Works – debris removal, material recycling, sewage manholes clearing, rain manholes cleaning, parks rehabilitation, roads potholes repairing , green works (e.g. irrigation works, soil/water conservation, flood protection, etc.), ii) Roads Rehabilitation -maintenance of the secondary and feeder roads in rural area in terms of pothole and cracks repair, side drain maintenance, culvert maintenance, surface spot improvement and levelling, and maintenance of irrigation channels, iii) Cultural Heritage Sites Reservation - the activities will be implemented in three sites within Aleppo city, namely, the Citadel, old city, and religious buildings. The proposed activities are stabilization the separated parts of soil, rebuilding of the collapse parts of the walls, and dismantling and rehabilitating unstable parts the walls. Cultural works were selected with a view to enhancing social cohesion through revitalizing commonly shared identity. For this output, the target for 2024 in terms of decent short-term jobs creation is 1,799 jobs generating 161,910 workdays. These jobs will be distributed among the targeted governorates as follows; Hama 322, Homs 210, Lattakia 250, Aleppo rural and urban 1,017 jobs. The second output is long-term employment, the ILO will refer selected workers from the infrastructure works beneficiaries based on skillsets, aspirations and labour market demands to suitable skills training courses for occupations that are in demand. They will be provided with career counselling and supported for job placement. These complementary skills development courses will be offered through Work-Based Learning (WBL) in partnership with local TVET and the branches of the Chamber of Industry as well as its SMEs networks interested in introducing apprenticeship for the semi-skilled occupations needed in their enterprises, beneficiaries will be paid USD 5/day. Four months of theoretical and on-the-job training under ILO’s supervision with the principal objective of formalizing the jobs for workers beyond the temporary nature of the short-term livelihood support. The targeted governorates are Aleppo (rural and urban), Hama, Homs, Lattakia, and Rural Damascus (only for output 2). For this output, the target in 2024 in terms of the number of beneficiaries enrolled in the WBL programme with the aim of long-term employment is 450, generating 46,800 workdays distributed among the targeted governorates. Female workers percentages for decent short-term jobs and WBL program are 20 and 50 respectively while the people with disability percentage is 3. The total estimated budget for 2024 for the two outputs is USD 5,807,249.
