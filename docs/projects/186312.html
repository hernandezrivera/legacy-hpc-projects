Project Summary

The five year Anglophone crisis which has deteriorated into a civil war has destroyed once a self-sufficient in food region of the South West Cameroon to a low productivity of crops, livestock and fish resulting into nutritional maladies. An assessment of the prevailing situation on food and nutrition security reveals that the main nutrition problems affecting Cameroon’s South West and North West regions include inadequate intake of foods containing micronutrients, protein and to a lesser extent energy. These are reflected in the prevalence of nutritionally-related maladies and diseases, such as protein-energy malnutrition, Vitamin A deficiency, iodine deficiency disorders, malaria and HIV/AIDS. The situation on the ground is a cause of a renewed concern to stepping up intervention in Integrated and specialized Nutrition services among the IDPs, Returnees, and Host communities to scale-up nutrition intervention in Manyu, Lebialem and part of Kupe-Manenguba divisions of the South West regions of Cameroon.
October 2019 report showed a proxy GAM rate of 7.1% and SAM prevalence rate of 3.0% in the South West region of Cameroon according to emergency food security assessment. This therefore, implies that there is need for scaling up of SAM treatment programme in the SW region. In the 2021 North West and South West situation report on Nutrition No 35, only 8% of the total caseload of 8,000 were reached. This means MAM and SAM admission match caseload rates are still low.
The objective of this intervention, therefore, is to achieve an Integrated and specialized Nutrition services in Emergency through supplementary feeding program to treat MAM, reduce rising SAM cases, create a paradigm shift to a prevention oriented program, household food supplement, social and behavioral change through community training and promoting IYCF-E.
The Nutrition response shall be implemented in six health districts in Manyu, Lebialem and Kepe-Manenguba divisions. The activities shall include; active screening of all children from 6-24 months and 6-59 months to prevent malnutrition and to treat uncomplicated MAM cases where referral services are not available, for acute malnutrition and referral of children with severely acute malnutrition for treatment, Sensitisation of infant and young child feeding in Emergency and capacity building in communities for village health care teams (community health workers), training of care givers on MUAC/mother MUAC. There shall be integrated basic nutritional care into the care of all expectant and new mothers with specialize attention given to high-risk Women and children.
The nutrition response will be in the context of COVID-19 preventive and treatment measures, conscious of the principle of “do no harm” the intervention will also be in line with the national nutrition protocol.
In order to improve on nutritional status of the population, the response shall integrate activities to promote household food security and healthier eating habits with special attention on children, pregnant and lactating mothers. Special education and other measures will be undertaken to protect the population against micro nutrient deficiencies, obesity and other nutrition related diseases as the intervention will target to; Reduce stunting and underweight in the under 5 year old children, Increase exclusive breastfeeding at 6 months, Increase and sustain vitamin A supplementation coverage for children 6 – 59 months, and Increase community awareness on appropriate nutrition practices.

Plan fields

1 - Besoins / Needs

Through the previous nutrition needs assessment carried out, it was found that there is the problem of food security due to the interruption of farming activities caused by the anglophone war in Cameroon hence, there is need to promote household food security and healthier eating habits with special attention on children, pregnant and lactating mothers. Special education and other measures needs to be undertaken to protect the population against micro nutrient deficiencies, obesity and other nutrition related diseases to target and reduce stunting and underweight in the under 5 year old children, Increase exclusive breastfeeding at 6 months, Increase and sustain vitamin A supplementation coverage for children 6 – 59 months, and Increase community awareness on appropriate nutrition practices. Through the cooperation and coordination with the nutrition cluster and other sectors like the health districts and ministry of public health, the response shall embark on identification, treatment and follow-up of cases of malnutrition in the communities through nutritional activities carried out in the targeted communities These activities shall be carried out mindful of the needs of the people with disabilities and taking into consideration the necessary COVID-19 prevention measures. The needs and feedback from the affected population shall be integrated into the project. The beneficiaries shall be educated about their duty to report suspected, perceived, or identified case of sexual exploitation and abuse in the field.
