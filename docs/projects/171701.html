Project Summary

Though, there have been efforts made by the humanitarian organizations including UN agencies, INGOs, and LNGOs as well as Somali Government to improve and sustain food security and lifesaving for people in acute food insecurity in Somalia. However, 2.1 million people across Somalia are also expected to face Crisis or worse (IPC Phase 3 or higher) outcomes between now and December without sustained humanitarian assistance, additionally, 3 million people are expected to be Stressed (IPC Phase 2), bringing the total number of people facing acute food insecurity to 5.1 million in general. Therefore, Livelihoods support is required for people that are Stressed or worse (IPC Phase 2 or higher). according to FSNAU-FEWS September-2020 report. This came as the result of collective effects of highly infestation Desert Locust hugely impacted to Somaliland and Puntland and central regions as well, widespread and severe flooding in riverine regions, socioeconomic impacts of COVID-19, and the cumulative impacts of previous shocks the entire country. FSNAU report September 2020, added.
The magnitude and severity of acute food insecurity in rural areas expected to increase significantly in 2021, due to the likely impacts of forecast below average 2020 Deyr seasonal rainfall and possible delay and/or poor performance of rainfall during the 2021 Gu season. According to 2020 Post-Gu Assessment findings.
In response to the aforementioned food security gaps and urgently required lifesaving due to significantly increments of acute food insecurity, ADO targeting in Sanaag and Sool regions which are the worst situation of food insecurity as indicating FSNAU FET report September 2020, where projection stating substantially escalation towards emergency (IPC4) and proposing this project that will provide immediate life-saving relief assistance through unconditional in-kind/ cash-based transfer to 650 vulnerable households in crisis and emergency (IPC 3 and 4) to access essential food items for four months as an immediate response. Likewise, the project provides restocking for 250 vulnerable pastoral HHs for 11 herds per household that make 2,750 herds. Water trucking for additional 42,000 animals benefited 600 pastoral households in target areas that will be supported by the project. Complementary cash programming (e.g. “Cash+”) will enable households to cover food consumption gaps while avoiding the distress sale of livestock to afford food and non-food items
Furthermore, the project will provide emergency agricultural support to 560 agro-pastoral households to secure harvest in the upcoming cropping seasons (Gu and Deyr rainy seasons in 2020). This includes provision of quality drought-tolerant seed varieties to replenish depleted seed stocks, farm tools, land preparation, rehabilitation, and construction of irrigation canals and extension services. Complementary cash programming will cover immediate food needs during the lean season (i.e. before the harvest) while contributing to preventing further displacement. Cash transfer is highly crucial in order for people to access food and non-food items immediately.
Since this project will give special consideration to the very vulnerable community members including Disabled persons, the preliminary activities in the project preparation and initiation stages, disabled persons shall be involved, the community leaders themselves will be sensitized to the significance of disability inclusion to the beneficiaries and there will be beneficiaries selection criteria to make sure inclusion of disabled people. People with disabilities always can not take part in the cash for work activities, for this scenario they will be given a chance to send representatives.
As the WHO declared a global outbreak of COVID19 in late 2019, the alert of covid19 transmission became mandatory to anyone, likewise, this project will be implemented along with practicing of COVID19 Protocols and guidance notes for each activity.

Project Activities

1. Provision of 2,750 healthy and young goats will be purchased and distributed for 250 target pastoral households those not yet fully recovered from the extended cumulative impact of previous droughts. 130 will be female pastoral HH’s while 120 will be male in Ainabo District of Sool. Priority will be provided to disabled people, women, and elder people. 2. Provision of refresh CAHWs training to 15 previously trained CAHWs 10 male and 5 females in 5 target villages of in Ainabo district of Sool. 3. Conducting animal treatment campaign to be treated 9070 of livestock suffered pneumonia, PPR disease, malnutrition caused by ecto-parasite and diarrhea (Sheep, goat) by using multi-vitamins, Anti-bacteria, Ivermectin and sulfa/sulpha in the villages in Ainabo districts of Sool 4. Provision of food/cash voucher for 650 vulnerable households are already in IPC Phase 3 or higher through the voucher value of USD 105 for the period of 4 months. Food items will consist 50 kgs of rice, 25 kgs of flour, 5 kgs of cooking oil, 12.5 kgs of sugar, 2 kgs of dates, 1 tin of milk powder. 2,268 will be female while 1,932 will be male beneficiaries. Priority will be provided to disabled people, women, and elder people in target villages of Ainabo and Erigavo district.5. Supply 3,560 water voucher system to 42,000 domestic livestock population owned by 600 HHs (average 70 heads/HH) in Ainabo districts of Sool . Priority will be provided disable people, women, and elder people.6. Distribution of Agricultural input packages consisting of land preparation through the provision of tractor hours, certified seeds, rehabilitation of flood irrigation system for 560 HH’s in target villages of Erigavo district. 310 are male while 250 are women-headed households’ beneficiaries. Priority will be provided to disabled people, women, and elder people. 7. Construction of 70,000 linear meters of soil contour bunds in agricultural Land through cash for work approach in target villages of Erigavo District. 333 casual labors will get employment opportunities for a period of 3 months. 190 workers will be female while the 143 workers will be male. Priority will be provided to disabled people, women, and elder people. 8. Rehabilitation and construction of 3000 M of irrigation canals in the target villages of the Erigavo district. 9. 2 earth dams will be rehabilitated by removing deposited sands in the dams and installing fence through cash for work approach engaged 120 casual labors for the period of 90 days. 52 casual labor will be male while 68 will be female workers. Priority will be provided disable people, women, and elder people 10. Conduct GAP training workshops attending by 100 farmers’ 40 of them will female while 60 will be male trainees in project target villages areas of Erigavo district and In Ainabo district. Priority will be provided to disabled people, women, and elder people.

Mitigation Measures

A. Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services. 1 • Identify central locations - beneficiaries should not have to traverse long distances or pass-through conflict zones to access distribution points. 2• Avoid placing distribution points near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets,crossfire, or violence. Weapon bearers should not be present inside distribution points. 3• Distributions should take place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel homebefore nightfall. 4• use a few police members with community leaders keep the protocol and security of the place of distribution B. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities 1 • Making sure the full participation of marginalized groups in designing and planning of the project and as well as registration of beneficiaries by verifying the presence of marginalized people 2 • Consult with women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, chronically ill, older persons, pregnant and lactating women, and marginalized persons to collect accurate information about their specific food and nutritional requirements and preferences for food distribution points and livelihood services. 3 • Engage the community and committee representative to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response 4 • Applying a systematic outreach process in registering beneficiaries – otherwise vulnerable persons, such as older people, may easily be excluded as other more mobile and vocal population groups are registered. C. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms 1• Setting up mechanisms for submitting feedback that do not require the beneficiary to expose themselves to project staff. 2 •Responding to complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can/need to be put in place. 3 •Organize awareness-raising sessions so that people know how it works. 4 •Consider a joint feedback mechanism with other sectors to minimize confusion.
