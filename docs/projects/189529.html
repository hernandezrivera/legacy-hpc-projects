Project Summary

A total of 171,581 are people in severe need for health services in Kajo-Keji county, including 27,453 IDPs and 24,021 returnees according to the HNO report of February 2022.. The main objectives of the project are aligned with the HC strategic and sectoral objectives as indicated below:
Strategic objectives:
Decrease excess morbidity and mortality rates from illnesses, such as malaria, diarrhea, acute respiratory infections, and vaccine preventable illnesses, such as measles, among Host community, IDPs and returnees
Provide access to life-saving essential healthcare, including mental health to women, men, girls and boys, including ability challenged persons
Reduce the suffering of girls, boys, women, men, older persons, persons with disabilities, and other persons with specific needs at risk of, or who experienced violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect, including gender-based violence, through the provision of specialized protection and multi-sectoral services.
Reduce the vulnerability of women, men, girls and boys at increased risk of mortality and morbidity and protection incidents/threats in priority areas through protection monitoring, advocacy, awareness-raising and risk mitigation.
Ensure that population groups targeted to receive assistance are consulted throughout the entire cycle of the response, and their needs are taken into account in decision-making by humanitarians.
Strengthen coordination and contextual analysis of needs conducted at national and sub-national levels.
Sectoral objectives:
Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases
Improve equitable access to life-saving essential quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations
Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms

The project will work closely with the County Health department and other partners to support health service delivery through supply of essential medicines and technologies, capacity building, technical support, Human resources support, documentation & reporting as well as demand creation for essential health and nutrition services. The project will be implemented in a duration of 8 months in Kajo-Keji County.

1 - Needs

A total of 171,581 people in severe need for health services in Kajo-Keji county, including 27453 IDPs and 24,021 returnees according to the HNO report February 2022. Due to the ongoing conflict in the County, the vulnerable people have limited access to life saving primary and secondary curative and preventive health services which has contributed to high morbidity and mortality amongst the host, IDP and returnees population especially in women and children. The inter-agency IRNA report dated October 2021 indicates that only 14 (28%) out of the 50 recognized health facilities are functional but lack adequate essential medicines, equipment and qualified staff to offer quality health services. Some facilities reported essential medicine stock outs for more two weeks at the time of the assessment. Child health care services such EPI are inadequate and the national EPI administrative data (January -June 2021) shows that Kajo-Keji County has one of the highest numbers of zero dose children (94%). Reproductive health services are also inadequate with facilities lacking adequate equipment and skilled birth attendant to provide these services.Access to secondary care is also limited since the only hospital available is only functioning as PHCC and still below the minimum recommended national Basic Package for Health and nutrition services (BPHNS).Consequently, all patients who require secondary care are referred either to Midigo Hospital in Uganda or Juba teaching hospital. There is only one functional old ambulance serving the whole county and this often breaks down causing delays in referral. Although some partners are supporting provision of health services, the resources are not adequate to meet the immense and huge needs of the population and huge gaps exit.Due to the protracted conflict, cases of SGBV have been on the rise as indicated in the IRNA report of October 2021.These,by their nature also contribute to mental health issues especially amongst the survivors. Unfortunately, SGBV and MHPSS services are limited in the County with only 2 PHCC and i hospital able to provide these services.This project, therefore seeks to bridge the existing gaps in order to improve access to life saving curative and preventive health care services to reduce morbidity and mortality amongst the vulnerable population.

2 - Project activities

The activities are aligned with the health cluster response plan, objectives and indicators as illustrated below.Conduct Epidemic disease surveillance, prevention and control including outbreak detection, investigation and response Conduct Immunization against vaccine preventable diseases and procurement of outbreak response commodities and emergency antigens for immunization and cold chain commodities.Ensure IPC in health facilities through regular assessment using score cards, provision of supplies and protocols and continuous monitoring of WASH in health facilitiesProvide life-saving essential healthcare services including management of non-communicable diseases through static (Hospitals, PHCC, and PHCU) and mobile healthcare services, procurement of essential medical kits and training of health workers.Provide technical and operational assistance to heath facilities through supportive supervision visits to ensure quality provision of health services and accountability to the affected populationConducting community consultative meetings within the community regularly and implementing feedback mechanism and ensure compliancePromote humanitarian-peace-development nexus bringing humanitarian, peace and development actors together to promote synergies and optimize use of available resources.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

This information will provided to the beneficiaries through their leaders and the home health promoters using simple pictorial IEC posters. All project staff and health workers will be oriented on the GOAL PSEA policy and will be required to sign on it and thereafter regularly inform the beneficiaries bout it through the routine health education sessions at the health facilities and awareness sessions in the communities.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will continuously engage the beneficiaries to provide vital information about the availability of the feedback mechanism through regular scheduled meetings with community leaders, health facility/Boma health committees and the home health promoters. These will then be facilitated to pass on the information to the wider community about the project and feedback mechanism. A project inception meeting will also be conducted with representatives from all priority groups and stakeholders in attendance. In addition, quarterly review meetings will be conducted for all stakeholders to jointly review progress of the project.
