Project Summary

ROHI will enhance the protection of 10,000 conflict-affected children, through the provision of targeted case management, PSS services, and COVID-19 key messages, as well as strengthening community-based protection mechanisms. Key Child protection needs and gaps across high-priority LGAs in Borno and Adamawa States will be targeted, through a holistic and integrated approach. Specific focus will be on the most at-risk children, including unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), children associated with armed groups (CAAG), children with special needs and children at-risk of gender-based violence (GBV). ROHI will seek to address immediate needs as well as work with parents, caregivers, communities and stakeholders to address deeper root causes of child protection risks and strengthen the child protection system. To prevent and mitigate the impact of COVID-19 spread, children and communities will be equipped with key age appropriate life-saving knowledge.
In Adamawa, the targeted LGAs will be Mubi, Michika and Madagali, in Borno MMC, Shani Gwoza, Damboa, Monguno, Biu,

1 - Needs

An estimated 1.8 million children and adolescents in North East Nigeria are in need of child protection services. At least 6,000 children (boys and girls) have been recruited, abducted or victims of violent attacks since the start of the conflict, and at least 15000 children (Boys and girls) have been affected by risks including abuse, exploitation, family separation, neglect and violence. Girls and boys may be exposed to different types of risks, with girls more likely to face SGBV and boys more likely to be at risk of recruitment in to armed groups. The risks faced are also age-specific; particular risks are heightened for adolescents, such as underage recruitment into armed groups, sexual exploitation, child labor, early pregnancy, early marriage and other reproductive health issues. There is therefore a need to design more gender-specific initiatives which meet to the different risks and capacities of girls, boys, women and men. Case management of acutely at-risk children, including UASC and CAAG, has been identified as a significant gap in the current response in the north-east Nigeria. There is a need to improve FTR services, inter-sectorial referrals and follow-up, and the provision of alternative care. Cases at the sector level has indicated that children living with vulnerable caregivers are amongst the most likely to face protection risks. An estimated 110,700 parents and caregivers are in need of support to be able to care appropriately for their children and avoid negative coping mechanisms. Certain groups face heightened risks and are more in need of support than others: female-headed households, for example, are amongst the most vulnerable. The severity of needs is highest in Borno state, with the needs in 12 LGAs rated as 'severe'. This includes the LGAs of MMC, Jere, Gwoza, Damboa, Shani, Biu and Bama. In Adamawa state, the needs in Michika and Madagali LGA are rated as 'severe' protection partners, particularly in providing quality case management services and timely referrals. Linked to this, there is a need to strengthen Field-level coordination and build LGA level capacity Adamawa and Borno State.

2 - Activities

ROHI will provide psychosocial support (PSS) to 15 thousand children through afterschoolRecreational activities and peer clubs facilitated by trained PSS facilitators. The Activities will be tailored to the different gendered and age-specific needs of different Children and will address topics such as health and hygiene, COVID- 19, GBV prevention and child rights. Caregivers will also be engaged in PSS activities to promote positive Parenting skills. Child Help Desks will run alongside PSS activities, providing more tailored support for children identified as at-risk (including referral for specialized services as needed) and supported by trained clinical psychologists. PSS facilitators will be trained community members to build community capacity and improve sustainability. 10,000 children affected by protection risks (including GBV survivors, CAAG and UASC) will be targeted for tailored case management, including a tailored care plan and regular follow up from dedicated case workers. ROHI services include mental health and psychosocial support, UASC, socio- economic reintegration of CAAG, the provision of protection kits and GBV case management. In Addition, referrals will be made for specialized services including medical support and legal support and follow-up conducted for all referrals made. Capacity building to local partners and actors will include the strengthening of field-level referral pathways and developing LGA capacity to monitor and respond to needs. In addition to traditional case management services, CAAG will receive tailored socio-economic reintegration support, including vocational skills training and resettlement grants or education and life skills training, as appropriate. ROHI will also work with communities through adolescent and community activities to advocate for reintegration and acceptance of CAAG, and to reduce stigmatization. As well as providing services to UASC, a focus will be on improving care providing by alternative or interim caregivers, including foster parents. Positive parenting skills training will target these caregivers (both male and female), and the situation of children in alternative care arrangements will be monitored at least once a month, with the aim of identifying any challenges and providing foster parents with Support as needed. Key actors and stakeholders (such as MoWASD) will be engaged to Screen and monitor foster parents as well as supported to develop sector-wide guidelines on alternative care, building capacity and strengthening the system as a whole. Parents and caregivers of the most at-risk children will also be supported with livelihood activities, which aim to build sustainable income-generating opportunities and ensure that increased household income improves the wellbeing of their children (including through enrolment in school). In all target communities, ROHI will build community-based protection and response mechanisms, establishing or strengthening community-based child protection committees (CBCPCs) and mapping referral pathways to empower community members to be able to identify child protection concerns and respond. CBCPCs will also be the entry point for working with communities for disaster risk reduction (DRR), identifying and mitigating major risks and dangers to children's safety and security. ROHI is committed to localization and will work through local to implement project activities and provide tailored mentoring and capacity building to strengthen technical and organizational capacity.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

ROHI is committed to ensuring that protection is central to all of its activities and projects, acknowledging that prioritizing protection, safety, dignity, accountability and minimizing any unintended negative effects of our activities are crucial to meaningful, dignified access. To achieve this in this proposed project, specific risks at all project stages have been identified and mitigation strategies integrated into the project design, clear protection reporting and response mechanisms have been included, and protection mainstreaming indicators have been included in the monitoring and evaluation. In order to ensure participation and minimize inter-group tensions, targets have been set toInclude different communities (IDP, host community and returnee) as well as children, both men and women. Community leaders and members will be informed of the selection process. Sensitization on these topics through regular community visits and feedback sessions has proven successful during past and current project at various locations. As part of our commitment to accountability and protection, ROHI commits to work towards the elimination of exploitation and abuse in the communities where we works and in our engagement with vulnerable populations. Sensitization during activities through community forums and through the use of visuals such as posters also seeks to empower community members to better understand their rights, and to know how to report any concerns they may have.Finally, ROHI is an active participant of CPWG,EiE and GBV working group in Borno and Adamawa State, as an extra level of accountability, and follows the best practices and lessons learned shared through this working groups.
