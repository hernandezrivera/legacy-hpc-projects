Project Summary

This project aims to ensuring that there is increased equitable access to health services at health facility, at community level and through integrated outreach activities conducted by mobile teams among IDP and Host (rural and urban) populations in South Central Somalia. The project design takes on a targeted approach with a focus on:
1. Control of communicable childhood illnesses (diarrhea, acute respiratory illnesses malaria and measles).
2. Integrated management of common childhood illnesses (Eye, ear, skin, worm infections, anaemia and chronic childhood diseases)
3. Expand access to EPI, contributing to control of vaccine preventable diseases
4. Expanded access to reproductive healthcare services for women of reproductive age.
4. Community Disease surveillance and response - An emergency Rapid response component is built in to allow quick scale up of lifesaving health services ( eg through CTCs/ORT corners/mobile ORT and isolation wards) during rapid onset emergencies.

Needs

The Health System in Somalia remains fragile due to insecurity, massive displacement and limited access to safe water and sanitation infrastructure. According to September 2018 UNOCHA estimates, nearly half the Somali population (5.4 million people) are currently in need of emergency health services. Communicable diseases such as acute respiratory tract infections, diarrhoea and cholera continue to cause significant morbidity and mortality particularly among children aged 5 years and below. In 2018, WHO estimates that 40% of AWD caseload has been in children aged 5 years and below. (WHO Sitrep, September 2018). Jowhar, Mogadishu and Kismayo are the worst affected districts. All are considered chronic AWD/Cholera hotspots. The burden of vaccine prventable diseases such as measles is alarmingly high. WHO estimates that only 30-40% of children in Somalia are immunised against 6 of the major childhood diseases (WHO Country statistical profile, 2017). In 2017, Somalia experienced the worst measles outbreak in half a decade. In 2018, the disease burden among children aged under 5 remains high underscoring the lack of sufficient immunisation coverage to ensure herd immunity (90%). The worst affected areas include Galgaduud, Bay, Mudug and Banadir regions, all of which have a high concentration of IDP populations living in crowded settlements. The low coverage and utilisation of quality reproductive health services contributes to the high maternal mortality rates recorded for the Country. Even in the absence of devastating outbreaks frequently experienced in the Country, there is urgent need to continue to provision of lifesaving health services to mitigate morbidity and mortality and improve health outcomes.

Activities

1. Provide diagnosis and treatment and necessary follow-up of common illnesses, in women, men, boys and girls form both IDP and host populations.2. Ensure access to skilled birth attendance through quality Basic Emergency Obstetric Services (BEmONC) for women of reproductive age. 3. Provide training and ongoing supportive supervision to male and female healthcare workers at facility and community level on integrated management of childhood illnesses. 4. Expand coverage of vaccination in children aged 5 years and below. 5. Deploy male and female CHWs to facilitate health education/promotion at health facilities and FHWs at community level. 6. Improve access to vital ANC and PNC services at community level to improve early access to maternal and reproductive healthcare. 7. Form representative (gender, clan, IDP and host balanced) community health committees/health centre committees to provide oversight of implementation activities. 8. Respond to disease outbreaks through stand-by emergency rapid response teams and mobilise community to respond to mitigate impact of disease outbreaks.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

This project links directly to Strategic objective 5 of the Somalia Resilience and Recovery framework to improve basic service delivery. Sustainability is built in to the project through integration of community health committees and health centre committees who provide community oversight of health activities implemented.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Risk: Cost of seeking healthcare servicesLikelihood: Medium/High - Impact: HighMitigation measure:- Avail high quality healthcare services free of charge- Utilise mobile health clinics to eliminate distance as a barrier to access to healthcare. Risk: Low utilisation of healthcare services by marginalised groups.Likelihood: Medium/High - Impact: High- Provide health information in various forms: audio, visual/pictoral and through mass media eg radio.- Recruit staff representative of the community served, incouding persons with disability, IDPs and other marginalised groupings.- Form representative community health committees/health centre committees.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Risk: Mistrust of formal healthcare servicesLikelihood: High - Impact: HighMitigation measure:-Ensure gender balance among staff to enable same-sex consultations.- Ensure outreach messaging considers social and cultural preferences of the community.- Collect implementation data disaggregated by sex and age.- Train all staff on gender equity considerations in service provision.- Form gender balanced community health committees/health centre committees.
