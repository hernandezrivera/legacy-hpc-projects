Project Summary

RI aims to provide prevention and response GBV services as well as empowerment activities through standalone and integrated approach with the health services RI providing in the targeted area. RI will target the most vulnerable women and girls including: people with disability , widowed women living in widows camps , women and girls headed household , adolescent girls and older women
RI will support functioning 4 WGSS in the following subdistricts: Darkosh , Maaret Tamsrin in Idleb , Jandairis , Aghtrin in Aleppo and will establish two new WGSS in Azaz in Aleppo targeting widows camps as RI has already access to the camps through health team including CHWs and PSWs and aiming to ensure permanent present for protection activities in the camps through coordination with LCs and camp management in addition to local humanitarian actors to ensure smooth establishment for the WGSSs and safe implementation for staff and clients. In addition, following the integrated approach with its health services, RI will deploy GBV teams in ten RI health facilities to provide prevention and response services for women and girls visiting the health facilities in many areas in Idlib and Aleppo. RI is working on strengthening the referral for women and girls at risk between RI health and GBV services in the targeted area either by establishing a WGSS near to RI health facility or through deploying GBV team in the health facility to provide prevention and response services
In line with the GBV Minimum Standards in Emergencies, the services provided within RI WGSS under the present proposal are case management following the GBV case management SOPs developed by GBV SC, community based and focus PSS services including psychoeducation sessions , psychological first aid , individual and group counselling through trained and supervised PSS team as well as awareness raising and empowerment activities including skills development, life skills and vocational training . Moreover, in order to strengthen the community approach RI will support the establishment of women’s committees to foster participation of women in decision making structures as well as build on community protective mechanisms already in place. in addition RI will conduct safety audits to services and facilities, risk mapping, contextualize participatory GBV key messages, integrated awareness raising and psychoeducation sessions for men and boys as well as psychosocial workshops to address behavioral problems such as anger, violence to enhance their engagement in the GBV program.
RI will provide integration approach within its supported health facilities by providing awareness raising and information sessions for women , men girls and boys on GBV topics in addition to providing focus on strengthening the referral pathway for GBV survivors and women and girls visiting RI health facilities , in addition to provide case management and individual and group counselling through GBV team in the health facilities. All activities will be implemented while adhering to strict COVID 19 prevention measures. Hotline service will be used additionally to provide online information sharing, Psychological first aid , individual counselling and case management for women and girls who don’t have access to the center.in addition , health workers from RI filed staff and other health actors will be trained on GBV core concepts , survivor centered approach safe referrals and how to deal upon disclosure considering the health service as entry point for GBV survivors to ensure safe access to more specialized services and adherence to GBV guiding principles. Finally, MEAL approach will be followed to ensure consistent and accurate flow of information and data in relation to Gender, age and disability. RI will use the tools included in GBV SC M&E Toolkit to ensure adherence to survivor centered approach through the MEAL process.

4 - Needs

According to VOICES FROM SYRIA 2021, the most common form of violence mentioned by women and girls in Idleb was intimate partner violence (IPV), family violence against adolescent girls and young women by male family members. Sexual harassment and sexual violence within the context of work were mentioned frequently in relation to girls engaged in child labor and early marriage was reported as a very common practice affecting adolescent girls between 12-17 by 30% of communities in Idleb, with 82% of assessed communities claiming they had witnessed a girl this age getting married in the past three months. Adolescent girls, on the other hand, were more likely to reflect on the reduction of sexual violence within and on the way to school since education was interrupted in this period. Meanwhile in Aleppo, women and adolescent girls expressed concerns over and awareness of early marriage as a risk of violence against girls, and linked it to ongoing instability. Girls use early marriage as a negative coping mechanism to escape family violence, while women and girls in Aleppo continue to report sexual harassment in the street, camps, crowded places, distribution points, and at work. Displacement was also identified as a key factor contributing to increasing the risk of facing GBV. Displaced adolescent girls were particularly concerned about being denied education due to their status. Women linked psychological distress to the experience of displacement and increased GBV against women and girls. In both Idleb and Aleppo governorates, women and girls also perceived the COVID-19 pandemic to have increased GBV against them at home.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will support functioning 4 WGSS in addition to establishing two new WGSS , prevention and response services in addition to empowerment services will be provided by trained and supervised team , the services will be provided through static WGSS facilities in addition to 12 mobile teams .the activities will be awareness raising utilizing the GBV SC toolkit in addition to provide skills development activities , vocational training after conducting consultation with women and girls on their preference and wishes in addition RI will coordinate with Livelihood actors to figure out the opportunity for linking the clients with the job market with consideration to their wishes and skills . humanitarian life-saving specialized quality GBV services, including case management, psychosocial support and establish referral pathways will be provided through trained and supervised staff and following the GBV case management SOPs including GBV case management in addition to provide focused psychosocial support service for women and girls at risk and GBV survivors. Ri will deploy GBV team in nine RI health facilities to provide GBV prevention and response services through securing private space within the health facilities. In the health facilities a private space will be secured for the GBV team to be able to provide GBV services for women and girls visiting the health facilities. Transportation will be provided for women and girls to access the services in the WGSS or during the referral outside the WGSS. GBV training to field health workers from RI and other health actors will be provided. the project will be monitored, measured, and evaluated with recommendation to improve the services delivered in the targeted areas.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

First risk : Lack of quality services and referral expose survivors to harm, enhancing risk of stigmatization reappraisal and further trauma in addition to possibility of breach of confidentiality putting at risk SGBV survivors due to lack of capacity on information management of service providers. likelihood: High, the impact High . however RI will implement the following mitigation measures: 1.Case management with survivors to be conducted by trained personnel only 2. Service mapping including only those that can provide survivor centered services 3. Mainstream messaging on the risk of the available legal systems which is likely to cause harm to survivors, 4. Self-care strategies as part of programmers and organization HR policy .Second risk : Resistance or backlash from sections of the community and community leaders on GBV messaging ,backlash from family or community on women participating in recreational, vocational or empowerment activities finally Community and family stigmatization for participating in programming, family resistance to girls’ mobility and low participation of girls because of family and community resistance. Likelihood L medium. Impact: high . Ri will implement the following mitigation measures: 1. Community engagement from onset of project, from assessment phase and throughout all project cycle. It includes meetings with religious and community leaders 2. Adaptation of GBV messaging to ensure sensitivity to the context and culture 3. Thorough gender analysis prior to start of programming 4. Targeting adolescent girls with socially accepted activities like life skills as safe entry point.5. Engage with the community and local authorities and clear communication on project objectives

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: The WGSSs and are located in relatively safe areas. Risk: Armed Clashes, arrest and detention, shelling/artillery, VBIEDs/IEDs. mitigation: staff to contact the security focal point and alert all staff to be prepared for immediate evacuation/hibernation if possible. Activate an emergency plan, previously designed and explained to all staff members. Being neutral and Avoid political action Suspend/Hibernate and/or relocate as per the severity of the risk and based on a contingency plan and in line with “Protection Cluster Guidance: Protection Principles and Minimum Standards for Emergency Relocation Support in Northwest Syria, May 2020”. Staff to Stay away from convoys of military improvisation during the movement Following the steps for ethical closure of the program and ensure continuous updating of the plan to close the program as needed. Assumption : The new WGSS will be established in widows camps smoothly and activities will be implemented as planned.Risk: Resistance or backlash from sections of the community and community leaders on WGSS establishment in widows’ camps. Mitigation: RI will use their already access through health services in the area to strengthen community engagement from onset of project, from assessment phase and throughout all project cycle including meetings with religious and community leaders and Engagement with the community and local authorities and clear communication on project objectives to ensure ability to establish the WGSSs. RI will target all surrounding camps not only the widow’s camps to avoid any stigmatization for the project clients.
