Project Summary

Ensure access to and continuity of essential high impact nutrition services, including prevention, early identification, and treatment of acute malnutrition, emergency referrals, capacity building, procurement of equipment and medicines, service delivery, response operations for vulnerable displaced people and host communities targeting children under five, adolescent girls, women of reproductive age, particularly pregnant and lactating women and people living with disabilities. This will be implemented through an integrated health and nutrition community-based approach in Northern Mozambique and through the deployment of IOM-operated mobile clinics, temporary clinics and support to government mobile brigades. Further, IOM will build government capacity for humanitarian interventions through training of healthcare workers and emergency infrastructure support.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Through this project IOM proposes to deliver community-based nutrition support, to hard-to-reach populations and improve access to nutrition services in Northern Mozambique, which continues to be affected by pre-existing social issues and ongoing violence and displacement. IOM will provide primary health care and nutrition services through the staffing and operation of mobile and temporary clinics in hard-to-reach areas, support integrated mobile brigades, and provide trainings and mentorship for nutritionists, to meet the needs of displaced persons and host communities. IOM will conduct Community awareness-raising on malnutrition preventative practices, optimal breastfeeding and complementary feeding practices, and Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) best practices messages for caregivers by community activists. IOM will Implement preventive measures to prevent the increase of acute malnutrition and micronutrient deficiencies in children under-five and pregnant/lactating women, including vulnerable groups through active Nutrition screenings, wasting management, vitamin A supplementation and deworming services. IOM will support to patient follow up and referrals at community level by community health activists and bolster Emergency medical and specialized referrals of patients to next level of care Furthermore, IOM will provide medical equipment, essential medicines and supplies such as Vitamin A, Deworming drugs) and ensure the distribution of nutritional supplements based on coordination with nutrition cluster partners, health authorities and an assessment of gaps, and support health facility inventory management. IOM will ensure coordination with DPS, SPS, SDSMAS, MISAU, and the health cluster partners at national level and Cabo Delgado.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

IOM has a mandatory instruction, which binds staff members to standards of behavior at all times and especially when working with beneficiaries of assistance, including during emergency response. Sexual exploitation and abuse of affected populations constitutes gross misconduct and is grounds for disciplinary action, including summary dismissal and referral for criminal prosecution, where appropriate. In 2019, IOM launched the We Are All In platform, which includes a publicly available reporting form and an internal space with awareness-raising and training materials. IOM has also made PSEA commitments that include inter-agency coordination to prevent and address SEA incidents, including active participation at cluster levels in Mozambique and engagement with the PSEA focal point. IOM provides humanitarian health services free of charge and all IOM staff attend an obligatory training on PSEA and health projects are required to outline necessary actions for the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse. There will be regular reminders advocating for all health responders to undergo training on PSEA, and to facilitate training for their target populations. As much as possible, health teams will consider gender balance in staffing, so that women and girls feel safe when they access services.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

IOM has an integrated protection-MHPSS approach which ensures intersectional considerations of protection and MHPSS needs as well linkages with health programming in a whole-of-community approach. IOM Health Teams who support mobile clinics and community based nutrition activities and referrals are trained on protection principles. IOM identifies individual and structural-level protection risks by conducting the protection/safety audits in the districts. Also, the individual cases are identified and referred to specialized protection services in order not to cause further harm. IOM follows strict data protection rules and assistance standards in order to ensure meaningful access, not to cause further harm and empower individuals and communities assisted. IOM conducts community based rapid assessments in targeted communities and settlements, to map and assess populations main needs, vulnerabilities, resources, expectations and capacities, including key informant interviews and focus group discussions. This approach ensures that the project activities are tailored and respond to the needs of the communities and that community members can participate in project design and implementation. IOM also conducts satisfaction surveys and lessons learned workshops with affected populations to ensure accountability and for services to be appropriately tailored to meet needs. IOM’s community health workers are identified from the communities they serve, ensuring local involvement and accountability to affected populations, while enhancing the impact of interventions through their contextual knowledge and tailored approaches. This localized strategy has ensured community-level trust and holds benefits for public health-related social and behavior change communication. Moreover, IOM regularly assesses knowledge of community activists and health workers to ensure the quality of services. IOM has trained and deployed clinicians in coordination with provincial and district health authorities. These healthcare workers (HCWs) are presently employed by IOM, compensated at government salary scales and are supervised jointly by IOM medical staff and district medical chiefs. All HCWs have been identified and recruited from Cabo Delgado, including recent graduates and those waiting absorption in the health system, as part of IOM’s localization strategy. IOM has also actively worked with provincial and district authorities to integrate HCWs into public health system.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

IOM Mobile Teams identify people with special needs including individuals with physical and mental disabilities under the integrated health-protection-MHPSS activities. Also, IOM uses a diverse range of health information dissemination materials and channels (including door-to-door, community theatre groups, radio, and other community-based channels) to reach individuals with impairments. IOM also conducts accessibility audits of its health service delivery points for improvements to ensure greater accessibility and disability inclusion.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The proposed project will include some construction work/upgrades on temporary clinics, with minimal environmental impact. IOM’s community-based health and MHPSS activities strive to use environmentally friendly, recycled or reusable materials, and respect for the environment is included in some awareness-raising sessions with communities. IOM also ensures appropriate waste management procedures are in place at all health service delivery points.
