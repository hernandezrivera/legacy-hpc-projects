Project Summary

RENHW uses an integrated, multisector approach to improve nutrition outcomes in Jonglei State. The project objectives are: 1) increase access to and use of quality primary health services, 2) improve access to quality and standardized nutrition services for pregnant and lactating women (PLW) and children under 5 (CU5) and 3) improve WASH services in health facilities and communities. Ayod, Duk, Pochalla, Twic East, and Uror are the target counties for the RENHW project.

1 - Needs

Nutrition: The 2020 HNO estimates that 2 million people in South Sudan are in need of humanitarian assistance due to malnutrition. An estimated 1.3 million children and 350,000 women were acutely malnourished in 2020. Among children aged 6-59 months, Jonglei measured the highest rates of GAM, MAM, and SAM in South Sudan in both 2018 and 2019. While the effects of comorbidity with COVID-19 are not yet known, there is an expectation of increased vulnerability.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Safeguarding and protection are mainstreamed fully in all activities and approaches. Information on PSEA is shared with beneficiaries through IEC materials posted in health and nutrition facilities as well as through Care Group structure. Within the nutrition and health facilities, staff conduct health education sessions during which PSEA messages are shared. There is also a Protection Partner in each county that conducts trainings on safeguarding for all staff, partners, and volunteers.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

There are different literacy rates and limited access to technology and assets, such as radios and phones, which can contribute to the exclusion of the most vulnerable from accessing information that is in written form. Given differential access and barriers to information, CRS has tailored communication approaches to help meet the different information needs of various groups. For example, RENHW leverages Care Groups structures, engages female and male volunteers and health workers, uses picture messages, and shares information in spaces where women and girls are likely to frequent to ensure feedback is captured from the most vulnerable community members. In addition, the project also ensures that project participants are provided with the option of providing feedback in their local languages either through direct engagement with project staff or through local stakeholders. The project’s FRM has been popularized at the community level and participants are aware of the numerous channels through which they can provide feedback.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Information is provided through health facilities’ healthy spaces, CHWs, and the Care Group structure. IEC materials, translated into local languages with clear and culturally appropriate illustrations and images that are easily understood, are used to provide information on the project goals, services offered, and context-specific feedback and response mechanisms for target communities.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Quarterly review meetings with project stakeholders are held to beneficiary feedback, review lessons learned and coverage data, evaluate behavior change adoption, and adaptively manage mobile unit activities. These meetings are also used to monitor evolving protection risks and review established mitigating measures. In addition to quarterly meetings, participant feedback is reviewed during Project Steering Committee monthly meetings and action plans developed and tracked to ensure that feedback received is adequately addressed. In accordance with COVID-19 protocols, the project team also conducts community engagement events during which project participants and community stakeholders are encouraged to share feedback.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

CRS will continually monitor current affairs and the national and local safety and security environment and take all necessary precautions and safety measures to ensure the safety and security of project participants, CRS staff, partners, and consultants. CRS will look for ways to adapt to rapidly changing situations and minimize disruptions to planned activities. CRS will undertake an integrated protection risk analysis across all proposed sectors. CRS and consortium partners will mainstream protection in the provision of services proposed in sectors and sub-sectors. In addition, context-appropriate complaint channels for safe and confidential reporting will be established with fair and impartial feedback and response mechanisms set up in collaboration with the communities. A KAP survey and community consultations will inform materials and supplies needed for communities to practice promoted nutrition and hygiene behaviors. WASH NFI and Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) kits will be distributed alongside culturally appropriate education and messaging on nutrition and hygiene through Care Groups and ‘Health Spaces’. CRS will consult with community members, especially adolescent girls and women, on preferred materials, supplies, and distribution approaches, including safety considerations, to inform the provision of health and nutrition services and WASH NFI/MHM kits, mitigation of any potential protection risks related to the activities, and barriers to access. In addition, all CRS staff and partners are CRS trained on the code of conduct and PSEA policy. All partners that CRS works with are also required to have an organization code of conduct and PSEA policy.
