Project Summary

Somalia’s public health system has significantly underperformed due to longstanding conflict and instability. In addition to this, the combined effects of widespread and severe flooding, desert Locust infestation, socioeconomic impacts of COVID-19 and the cumulative impacts of previous shocks have contributed towards the deterioration of production outputs and social economics increasing vulnerabilities of households. These cumulative factors have significantly impacted the state of nutrition in children, women and men.
The median Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence has remained Serious (10–14.9%) for the past three consecutive seasons (11.8% in 2020 Gu, 13.1% in 2019 Deyr and 13.8% in 2019 Gu). Based on the nutrition survey analysis and projections conducted by FSNAU and partners in June and July 2019, High levels of acute malnutrition continue to persist. Urgent treatment and nutrition support are required for approximately 849,900 children under the age of five years who will likely face acute malnutrition through August 2021, including 143,400 who are likely to be severely malnourished.
For population groups that registered a high prevalence of acute malnutrition, contributing factors include high morbidity, low immunization and vitamin-A supplementation, poor care practices and food insecurity. One contributing non-food security factor to consistently poor acute malnutrition is the incidence of measles as well as Acute watery diarrhea(AWD)/cholera, due to unprotected water use and poor WASH practices. According to joint WHO and Somalia Federal Ministry of Health reports, nearly 1,257 measles cases were reported from January to August 2019, keeping the outbreak at epidemic levels. In the same period, 1,909 cases of AWD/cholera were reported, an increase of 48% from the number of cases reported in June to August 2018, but 31% lower than same time in 2017 drought year. More than 250 cases were reported in August 2019. There is concern for increased waterborne disease in October due to ongoing flooding and contamination of water sources.
In Lower and Middle Juba, falling livestock prices limiting household income. Given ongoing dry conditions through September, livestock body conditions have further deteriorated and income from sales is even lower than reflected in August prices. Poor households are unable to access adequate food to meet basic needs and are expected to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3)
Population groups classified as Crisis (IPC Phase 3) in our project area of interest are 5,460 in badhadhe, 32,459 in Afmadow, 120,352 in Kismayo, 34,905 in Buaale, 34,905 in JiIlib ,14932 in bardheere, 60,471 IN Garbaharay and 6760 In Ceel waaq require interventions aimed at reducing food consumption gaps, eradicating acute malnutrition, saving lives, and protecting and saving livelihoods. In the Integrated interventions we intend to provide activities to support recovery and prevent deterioration in the nutrition situation.
ADA is well positioned to implement the management of severe Acute Malnutrition both with and without medical complication since it already has well established four health facilities which will host static nutrition centers and also the outreach nutrition teams. ADA actively participates in nutrition cluster coordination meetings at regional level and closely co-ordinates with individual nutrition partners in referring the active cases. ADA also has currently been working closely with the community through community health workers who will take lead in ensuring timely identification and referral of Malnourished cases from the IDP camps.
The project will target 18,299 direct beneficiaries from the community who will benefit indirectly from the project from improved services and improved nutrition and hygiene practices.

Mitigation Measures

Volatile risks in Somalia context with service delivery has been very fragile. ADA will focus on the humanitarian response and provision of protection services in areas that have been inaccessible thus forcing IDPs to move to find assistance inaccessible, less conflict-prone areas. ADA will engage further in conflict affected areas through various actions including but not limited to engaging the various gatekeepers and the local authorities and the IMAMs Our mitigation strategies will include Community engagement of all groups of people especially marginalized groups to ensure a joint transparent process in the response mobilization phase. strengthening of gender inclusive community-based protection mechanisms. Information campaigns will be boosted to ensure impartial and timely access to GBV protection services, especially in situations of displacement. Staff training of PSEA will be conducted to minimise the risk. Protection monitoring will be used for both referrals and evidence-based advocacy, especially with authorities responsible for the implementation of durable solutions for displacement affected communities. ADA will build trust in the efficiency of our Complain and feedback mechanism to identify any situation of exclusion, and to condition implementation to be an equal and ensure direct access to all communities. We will ensure that complaints are followed up immediately and corrective measures are implemented on time. In site planning by provision of solar lights around water and sanitation facilities will reduce risk. ADA will work with communities in order to increase access to the disabled persons and ensure that persons with disability will receive necessary intervention without discrimination and stigma.
