Project Summary

The main objective will be to address critical gaps previously existing and made dire by the ongoing 4 year humanitarian crisis in maintaining good coverage of critical life saving interventions for treatment of acute malnutrition and prevention of under nutrition through preventive activities in rural areas in the North West Region especially in three divisions: Mezam, Ngoketunjia and Menchum.
Activities include
1. Active screening for malnutrition and referral and follow up of children for treatment of Severe Acute Malnutrition treatment
2. Sensitization of caregivers on infant and young child feeding in Emergency and capacity building in rural communities
3. Training of nurses, CHWs and caregivers on family MUAC approach on early idenfication of SAM
4. Boasting of Health facilities with essential drugs for the management of SAM cases
Involving CHWs through sensitization, active screening, referrals for treatment and follow up; involving caregivers through Family MUAC approach in nutrition screening activities recognizes the fact that they are best placed to identify early signs of malnutrition and reinforces their role as primary guardians in protecting and promoting child health especially in emergencies

1 - Besoins / Needs

The 2019 Emergency Food Security Assessment (EFSA) showed proxy GAM rates of 4.8% and 1.5% in the SW and NW Regions (the regions that have been hit by the ongoing crisis) respectively. This emergency food security assessment data showed proof to a grave humanitarian need especially for the rural population particularly those now as IDPs or Host local population most hit and whose routine livelihood had been affected by the crisis. No doubt then, the SAM rates were reported at 3% and 2% for the two regions respectively. Two years ongoing the crisis is still ongoing and hitting rural areas and enclaved areas most. It is not an exaggeration to now witness increase numbers and even increase number of deaths in children aged 6-59months. The high SAM, (reflected in the 2019 EFSA data, a data made more dire by the fact that the crisis deepens) prevalence necessitates the scaling of malnutrition treatment centers in the North West and South West especially in hard to reach rural areas due to access and security constraints like Furawa and Menchum Valley in Menchum Division, Bali and Lower Bafut in Mezam and Bum and Belo in Boyo and the Wum and Ndop Health districts which are highly affected by the crisis. The current coverage of the nutrition interventions and especially SAM treatment in both regions is low and so far in December 2020, 9% (230) out of the total estimated caseload for 2020 (2500) assessed treatment for SAM highlighting the need to scale up and increase coverage of this life saving intervention for children who are one of the most dependent and vulnerable persons hit by this crisis. This scale up intervention will include boasting health facilities to be able to treat uncomplicated SAM and providing means including financial and access for caregivers of children with complicated SAM to be able to reach SAM treatment centers.The nutrition situation in NWSW regions remains unclear due to absence of a comprehensive nutritionanthropometric survey using SMART methodology. Proxy data from rapid emergency food securityassessments (EFSA) conducted in October 2019 revealed a precarious situation with a proxy GAM of5.1% and 7.1% in North West (NW) and South West (SW) regions respectively. Analysis from MidUpper Arm Circumference (MUAC) Screening data conducted by nutrition partners between Januaryand August 2020, showed a proxy GAM prevalence of 4.8% and 1.5% in NW and SW regionsrespectively. However, these findings are not conclusive of the nutrition in NW and SW regions asthere could pockets of high nutrition vulnerability that are not yet identified. Prior to the crisis, SWand NW regions presented vulnerabilities to malnutrition with high rates of stunting of 36% and 28%in NW and SW respectively. Furthermore, poor indicators on Infant and young child feeding practiceswere reported with 42.5% and 58.3% of exclusive breast-feeding rates for NW and SW regionrespectively. Proportions of children 6 to 23 months who met Minimum Dietary Diversity (MDD) wasreported to be low at 31.8% and 20.7% NW & SW respectively. Approximately 40% (44.3% NW and41.9% SW) of children aged 6-59months were reported to have anemia and with the ongoinghumanitarian crisis, it is very likely that the poor situation of MDD has worsened.
