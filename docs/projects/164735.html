Project Summary

Syria Immunization Group (SIG) is co-chaired by WHO, UNICEF, and consists of all NGOs implementing partners working in the field of immunization in Northern Syria. SIG is responsible for planning, coordination, implementation, monitoring, supervision, and assessment of vaccination activities (EPI, SIAs & AIRIs) in N. Syria. EPI immunization activities inside N. of Syria are implemented through vaccination teams, which administratively belong to NGOs implementers, and technically to SIG technical committee. In Idleb governorate, and in absence of government, a supervisory team is formed to manage the technical issues of the program ( governorate supervisor, in addition to logistic, social mobilizer, data officer, and finance officer), in addition to governorate level there are 5 supervisory teams at district level ( district supervisor, logistic, social mobilizer, and data entry ), these supervisory teams manage the technical aspects of EPI in the field under supervision and support of SIG technical committee in Gaziantep in Turkey. This work is technically supervised and supported directly by WHO experts. World Health Organization has supported these supervisory teams on governorate and district level since 2017, the end of the last project for this support on 31 December 2019.
The project will be closely monitored and managed by the project officer who will work directly from the Turkey office with the field’s staff to ensure projects are being correctly implemented, bottlenecks are relieved and challenges are addressed in a timely manner. Furthermore, the project officer will prepare and submit monthly report on the progress of the project which includes detailed activities executed including total children vaccinated at fixed sites and out-reach sessions by each EPI center on prescribed format, Vaccines supply, and any other data required by SIG and that will be according to WHO guideline and standard.
Before the project implementation, M&E team will make a regular collecting the required data that help in making enlightened decisions. Monitoring and evaluation of the quality of the service provision through analyzing the collected data based on the Log-Frame, the project will be monitored and evaluated through three levels:
1) The operational, for which the project manager is responsible.
2) The technical, for which the technical team are responsible.
3) The objectives, for which the MEAL team is responsible.
Monitoring tools:
•Performance Indicator Tracking Tables (PITTs), issued in monthly basis.
•Satisfaction survey among beneficiaries and stakeholders will be analyzed and reported to stakeholders.
•Project Evaluation Report, by the end of the project-term.

4 - Needs

Routine immunization activities and campaigns are the most important health program at emergency and war conditions to decrease morbidity and mortality from vaccine-preventable diseases. EPI in the north of Syria has been relaunched in 2017, to improve and maintain herd immunity among children against vaccine-preventable diseases, the program should continue chronologically and geographically to cover newborns and reach each community. One of the most important elements of success of the program is the existence of a technical body that is responsible for the technical management, supervision, on-the-job training, and evaluation to improve the performance of the program continuously. The project activities will be coordinated with other actors including partners through the Technical meetings and health cluster meetings, in addition to the regularly meetings with SIG and IHD, so we have a full coordination with all parties working in the same field, and for the community within project areas we are coordinating with the HDs through regular visits will be conducted by the project manager.

5 - Implementation of activities

Al-Sham Humanitarian Foundation will identify and recruit the required technical experts to provide the mentioned support to the immunization program in Idleb. Al-Sham Humanitarian Foundation will ensure that the mentioned technical experts will contribute to the mentioned activities through capacity building, technical supervision, data management, and effective monitoring. AHF will contract the same personnel that AHF contracted in the previous projects to do these tasks as they have gained sufficient experience in the work in terms of proper planning and implementing of vaccination activities, supervision, and monitoring & evaluation, to reach as many eligible children as possible; and provide the service in a proper way. They will work as communication channels between the central office and field staff and partners. 7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

AHF will coordinate closely with the health cluster, as well as local authorities and humanitarian actors inside Syria throughout the proposed intervention, informing the cluster if and when adjustments are needed to the intervention. the staff are available the required supplies and vaccines.Risk 1: The close of borders which prevent the shipments of supplies and medications to access to facilities. mitigation: Develop a strong risk management strategy, (preparing an emergency plan /plan B/ according to the expected scenarios in conflicted areas)Risk 2: Conflict related threats including aerial bombardment, advancement of hostile actors and shifting alliances. . Mitigation: If any unseen circumstances happened, we will take the following actions: Postponing the services till safety is back or relocation if the project's activities can't be provided in the same locations.Risk 3: Wrong vaccination can occur Probability: Low Impact: High Mitigation measures: train the staff well Risk 4: The spread of false rumors about the harm of vaccines or causing harm to some children Probability: Average Impact: High Mitigation measures: 1- Conduct awareness campaigns to emphasize the importance of the vaccine 2- Publish articles that deny these rumors and that these words are untrue.
