Project Summary

The project will provide access to essential primary healthcare services for the Rohingya refugees through 2 RPN (Reaching People in Need) operated health posts located in two different camps (camp 8E and 19). The health facilities will provide healthcare services according to the essential services packages (ESP) with special focus on sexual reproductive health (SRH), neonatal, child and adolescent health, non-communicable diseases and mental health and psychosocial services in addition to the regular and routine services such as outpatient general medical consultation, immunizations, referral services, information management, waste management and IPC, laboratory services including ECG facility and medicine dispense. The project will also focus on improving health seeking behavior of Rohingya community by providing health related counseling at household level. At the community level 20 community health volunteers will be engage under the project.
On the other hand, we will provide eye care service for the host as well as Rohingya community people through mobile eye care clinic. Well-equipped medical van will facilitate the service. The proposed project aims to reduce blindness of the Rohingya and host community through organized eye camp. Through conducting eye camp, cataract surgery for intervention of developing and improving of both community’s clear vision during its implementing period. It also will include eye patient screening, consultation, drugs and spectacles distribution.

1 - Identified Needs

Over 860,000 Rohingyas (including 39,048 previously registered refugees) are currently living in 34 camps under Ukhiya and Teknaf upazilas with limited access to basic services like food, water, sanitation, protection, and health care. This community as a whole is a vulnerable group, however women, children, elderly, and persons with disabilities are more vulnerable and at risk of harm. The majority of the Rohingya population are women and children (52% are women and girls; 55% children under 18). As Rohingya community stance a patriarchal culture, movements of women and girls are restricted by male and they have limited access to healthcare services, especially during pregnancy and delivery. In a Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (J-MSNA) during July-August 2020 it was found that, 12% of households have pregnant women and among them only 51% of pregnant women were enrolled in an antenatal care program. As per JRP 2020-midterm review, only 53.2% deliveries are being conducted in the health facilities. As such, a large percentage of pregnant women are without adequate ANC care and deliver at home, increasing their risk for maternal mortality and morbidity. In addition, critical gaps exit in family planning services. Long-acting reversible contraceptives are not widely available, with IUD devices offered in only 43%of facilities. In addition, services for gender-based violence are not yet offered at all facilities; less than 50% of primary healthcare centers offer comprehensive GBV medical services. Coverage of Pentavalent 3 is only 67% among children under 1 year in the Rohingya community, whereas the target is >95% and coverage of measles-2 vaccination is only 25%. Furthermore, the J-MSNA found that 42% of households reported that they had at least one person with an illness serious enough to require medical treatment.With the onset of COVID-19, the already weakened healthcare system is more at risk of widespread infection transmission in the camps and host community and women, children, transgender persons and persons with disabilities will face more barriers in accessing health services particularly sexual, reproductive and maternal health services. Many literacies shown that before coming from Myanmar the Rohingya people were living in unhygienic with nutritional deficient condition that is why they are more infected in eye diseases. During Myanmar living period most of the refugees were unable to face eye doctor due to lack of available eye infrastructure in Rakhine State. Due to lack accessibility and opportunity to eye care service provider the Rohingya Refugee are deprive from eye care service in camp life. Scenario showing that, they are slowly going to blind. So, for reducing total blindness from the camp and host society, comprehensive eye care service will need for Rohingya as well as host people. The project has been providing limited primary healthcare services for the Rohingya refugees through 2 RPN (Reaching People in Need) operated health posts located in two different camps (camp 8E and 19) funded by the Humanity Rises, USA and Yeryuzu Doctorlari, Turkiye through April 2021. Now we are re-programming to implement the new activities beside the existing activities. Considering the above facts, we are planning to expand the program. "
