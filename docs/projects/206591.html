Project Summary

The signing of the Revitalized Peace Agreement by former protagonists in South Sudan on 22nd February 2020 has led to slow but continuous flow of returnees to their homes. This led to the decision by the UN mission in South Sudan (UNMISS) to withdraw its peacekeepers from South Sudan’s Protection of Civilians sites (PoCs) and reclassify them as Internally Displaced Peoples (IDP) camps. The result has been a slow but continuous flow of returnees to their homes. This has raised the need for MTT to alert communities in Jonglei State to beware of the remnants of war and unexploded landmines to places they are returning to. With the outbreak of conflict in Sudan, many people returned as refugees into South Sudan, most in dilapidated state escaping for dear life. The signing of the Revitalized Peace Agreement increased the number of returnees. The influx of the new refugees has made it necessary to conduct EORE which ended two years ago. COVID-19 protocols will be observed in the processes in data gathering and information dissemination about threats existing explosive remnants of war (ERW) within Canal/Pigi. In the project, the targeted populations are actively involved in peer education across all gender and age groups. The reconstituted team in RENK will focus on the two main approaches to reach people with key MRE messages in all areas of operation. These will namely be primary and secondary approaches. Primary approaches will be used for groups identified as the most-at-risk in the community – those living in or close to contaminated areas or other suspected hazardous areas. They will benefit through direct presentations. Secondary approaches will involve the most influential stakeholders. These are people regarded as very close to their population, who act as agencies of change in their community. Religious leaders, teachers, group leaders or representatives fall into this category. They will be educated in Explosive Ordnance Risks to pass on other members of the population who might not be reached directly with the EORE messages. They will be provided with EORE information to pass out to other members of the community not directly reachable. They will also be directed to channel the information to local authorities, NMAA and UNMAS. MTT will coordinate with other clusters such as Education, WASH, Nutrition, Child Protection and Health to pass on key EORE information. The project aims to educate the community on how to stay securely in their environments.

1. Needs

Renk is one of counties in South Sudan that has been a major battleground in the South Sudan civil war, both before and after independence. All along the population has borne the brunt of the risks of war. One incident was on January 25th 2018 and another on February 2nd 2019 when civilians came across two land mines in Pigi town. According to EORE, civilians alerted the authorities who called G4S – the clearing group to clear them from the town. Fresh returnees need to be educated of the need to beware of unfamiliar objects they may come across in their day to day activities. The risks cut across all community members of all ages and genders. There is need to reeducate hosts, IDPs and returnees about the hazardous post-war environment. Targeted community members have been disaggregated into six categories, namely women, girls, boys, orphans, the elderly, the disabled, widows and widowers from Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) the hosts and the Returnees. The war front gave women greater leeway to move about to perform their traditional chores of feeding their households. Their trek through long distances, however, exposes them to risk of EOREs and being attacked by wild animals and sexual molestation.
