Project Summary

INTERSOS Action tackles identified problems and unmet needs through an EiE response targeting the most vulnerable population in areas with high severity conditions in Hama, RD, Idleb Governorates where INTERSOS is already present with protection and health interventions. In line with 2022-2023 Syria Education Sector Response Plan, the Action aims to improve 1) safe and equitable availability and access to formal and NFE services; 2) enhance the quality of formal and Non Formal teaching and learning; 3) enhance education system and communities’ to support a timely, coordinated and evidence based education response. During 2022-2023 INTERSOS aims at outreaching a total number of 18070 beneficiaries (B7502; G8128; M1220; W1220). The programme is starting in January 2022 and will be continuing throughout 2022-2023. INTERSOS is building on evidence-based support of the formal school system in Syria provided in the 2nd half of 2021 and on its extensive expertise in the delivery of NFE in the Middle East region. INTERSOS is currently working under a bilateral MoU signed with MoE in June 2021 and under the broader MoU with SARC. INTERSOS carries out integrated actions where Protection and CP principles are mainstreamed along with GBV preventive messages. INTERSOS work through multiple approaches promoting boys and girls ACCESS to formal and NFE through: hard components (school rehabilitations, provision of emergency prefab classrooms, furniture), SLP, remedial classes (after school for children at risk of drop out and in learning center for OOSC), adolescents' clubs. INTERSOS promotes an inclusive environment enhancing access and structural accessibility (including WASH) of CwDs (1250) and providing the community with the skills to support CwDs inclusion in the learning environment. Education and hygiene kits are delivered to all the children enrolling in the classes (stationary and SLP). INTERSOS is focusing on girls’ continuation of learning preventing adolescent girls drop out also through the distribution of adolescents’ dignity kits. Classes are arranged based on a flexible schedule to accommodate the needs of children supporting the family with work/house chores. Regular classes are alternated with PSS group sessions and individual counselling is provided in order to promote boys and girls’ wellbeing and retention. QUALITY of teaching and learning is promoted through training to formal and NFE personnel on Active Learning, Inclusive Education, Curriculum and Planning, Pedagogy, Teachers’ role and well-being, CP principles (last 4 subjects are already included in INEE standard TiCC training). INTERSOS has developed a response strategy based on a strong COMMUNITY-BASED APPROACH coupled with a robust capacity building programme to enhance the quality of services delivered to the affected individuals. PTAs are established and/or supported and their members trained on: CP elements, PSEA, Safe identification and referral, education outreach and monitoring, gender mainstreaming in education. Once trained they become the first initiators of: i. PTAs led initiatives and ii. Back to Learning (B2L). Through consultations with the community and HHs assessments, the education outreach volunteers identify OOSC and individual causes for being OOS. Such methodology promotes the definition of individual pathways for re-enrolment through NFE. Finally, INTERSOS is fully integrating health and hygiene promotion and infection prevention and control in its action providing holistic knowledge and capacities to the targeted schools through training and awareness and promoting the knowledge and respect of the school Health Protocol. INTERSOS targets the most vulnerable boys and girls at risk of drop out and OOSC privileging overage students, adolescents’ girls and boys, IDPs and CwDs based on clear vulnerability criteria with an holistically integrated response with Protection, Health (MHUs school visit) and ERL.

4 - Needs

2022-2023 Education HNO draft estimates 7M PiN in the Whole of Syria, where 6.3M are children in need of education assistance. Self-reported attendance at national level is 82% with similar rates between boys and girls but 12-17 yo children are less likely to attend. Economic factors are a key reason for not attending school pushing boys into child labor and exposing girls at higher risk of early marriage. The formal system is unable to absorb and retain all school age children. More than 11 years of conflict left schools destroyed and/or damaged, with consequent overcrowded classrooms, lacking supplies, equipment and adequate wash facilities. Overage students are at high risk of not re-entering education also because of the scarcity of secondary schools. The quality of the learning system has been deeply impacted facing today an insufficient number of teaching personnel in need of professional development. Adults and children have been differently affected reporting signs of PTSD, depression and isolation, mainly girls, anger and violence behaviour usually among boys. Teachers themselves show signs of distress and are in need of PSS, peer support groups and of an improved referral system. CwDs are particularly affected by this complex crises and continue to be excluded. Half of children with a reported health condition, injury or disability reported school attendance, compared to 84% of children without these issues.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

R: INTERSOS/implementing partners (IP) staff, suppliers (etc.) have prior conviction for SEA related offenses; IP, staff member, suppliers, do not comply/accept PSEA policy; IP does not have a strong PSEA system in place; SEA perpetrated by staff/service providers; PSEA allegations are not timely/effectively addressed; survivors have limited access to safe assistance/remedies; lack of PSEA reporting mechanisms; communities not aware of PSEA risks/reporting channels. M: INTERSOS has a Global PSEA/CP Policy that applies to all personnel in addition to a country level Strategy to ensure SEA risks/mitigation strategies/procedures are tailored to the context. INTERSOS staff must sign a PSEA/CP Policy Declaration, attend a PSEA/CP Policy induction and one refresher training per year. One PSEA Referent and two PSEA Focal Points are appointed to receive complaints/reports about alleged acts of SEA and follow up in compliance with relevant guiding principles, ensuring safety of survivors. Complaints are referred to the in-country PSEA Referent and are subject to investigation. An assessment about IP commitment and capacity to prevent/respond to SEA is conducted; efforts are made with IP to harmonize PSEA strategies/reporting procedures to the best extent possible. Training is offered to partners to strengthen the identification of SEA risks/adoption of related SEA mitigation measures; when possible awareness raising on PSEA risks/reporting channels is conducted at community level.
