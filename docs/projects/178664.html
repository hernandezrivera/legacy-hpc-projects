Project Summary

The proposed project is designed to support health facilities in Aleppo and Idleb Governorates to ensure the communities residing in target areas have adequate access to quality life-saving health and nutrition services. The planned support is the continuation of previous projects the NGO has been supporting in Northwest Syria to improve access to quality health services for vulnerable populations including IDPs. Under the project, the NGO seeks to continue service provision through the supported health facilities in line with the essential care packages recommended by the Health Cluster. The NGO will provide primary and secondary healthcare of communicable and non-communicable diseases, reproductive health care through inpatients and outpatients services, furthermore, expanded program on immunization, care for victims of gender-based violence, and basic mental health and psychosocial support services.

4 - Needs

As of January 2021, 13.4 million people in Syria are in need of humanitarian assistance, an estimated 12.4 million people are specifically in need of health services, of which 5.6 million are children, while the health system remains heavily disrupted, according to the HNO 2021According to the REACH Northwest Syria Situation overview of Nov 2020, interviewees from the communities experienced high prices for healthcare, scarcity of medical supplies, and overcrowding in facilities. Transportation to hospitals and clinics was unavailable or unaffordable in more than half of the assessed communities, and 40% HHs reportedly could not afford health services. In nearly half of the assessed communities, key informants also indicated that overcrowding was a barrier to accessing health care, which may complicate efforts by public health officials to promote physical distancing measures required for COVID-19 control. In February 2021, the most commonly reported first priority need for interviewed residents of Northwest Syria was healthcare.The scarcity of resources is further reflected in infrastructure and staffing availability, The population in Northwest Syria remains vulnerable to outbreaks of diarrheal diseases, pneumonia, meningitis, measles, typhoid fever, leishmaniasis, cholera, viral hepatitis B and C, tuberculosis and other CDs. According to the Northwest Syria Health Cluster, epidemic-prone diseases remain a risk in IDP camps and overburdened host communitiesNCDs remain the most prevalent health issue in the Syrian context. The WHO estimates that approximately 12% of the population has diabetes, while 20% has hypertension.Estimates for the prevalence of chronic respiratory diseases and cancers are around 6%

5 - Implementation of activities

The NGO provides comprehensive health services integrated with WASH and Protection. The NGO will provide primary and secondary healthcare services in 24 health facilities, including 8 general and specialized hospitals, 8 PHCs, and 8 MCs. The target HFs provide the following services: reproductive health services (ante-natal care, post-natal care, family planning, normal deliveries, gynecological consultations, and clinical management of rape), integrated management for child illness, vaccines, prevention and control for communicable and non-communicable diseases, nutrition, mhGAP, health awareness, and basic laboratory and radiology services.The NGO also provides medical staff with capacity building on management of communicable diseases, pharmaceutical supply management, standard patient registration, and record-keeping, family planning and counseling, gynecological and obstetrical care, and newborn care, and COVID-19. In addition, The NGO trains them on the standardization of treatment protocol in line with WHO standards.The activities under this project are:•Prevention and Management of Communicable Diseases•Management of Non-Communicable Diseases•Reproductive healthcare services•Community health, nutrition, and hygiene promotion•Malnutrition prevention activities at both facility and community level for Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) and children aged 5-59 months•WASH monitoring, medical waste management, and general rehabilitation.The NGO conducts regular M&E activities in the targeted health facilities, a complaints & feedback mechanism is in place, in addition to coordinated activities with Idlib and Aleppo health directorates, TPM activities are also organized through external firms.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Regardless of age and gender, the NGO ensures that targeted communities have equal access to health and protection services corresponding to their needs. Cultural sensitivity and respect for the social norms encourage more female patients to seek health services from the NGO run HFs. The NGO also collects and monitors gender desegregated data from all target facilities. The decrease in female staff and the prioritization of trauma and critical care due to the increased violence have contributed to the reduced access for women to healthcare services. In Idleb and western Aleppo, the overstretched health system and the continued risk of attacks against HFs have challenged the delivery of Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) services, and the risk of GBV remains acute. Women and girls in Syria have recurrently reported that they seldom feel safe due to the risk of violence, emphasizing the need for prevention services and the necessity of delivering consistent, quality services to survivors. In Northwest Syria, protection risks have increased considerably in line with recent displacement, with the lack of shelter options and dire living conditions in the areas of displacement putting women and their families in vulnerable conditions. Overcrowding and the lack of privacy and suitable gender-segregated WASH facilities place women and girls at particular risk of GBV.

The NGO is an active member of the Protection Cluster, GBV and Child Protection Sub-clusters, and the PSEA network for Northwest Syria. The NGO regularly attends meetings, sharing information and providing updates on its program activities through 4Ws, service mapping, and referral pathway information.At the field level, there is also strong coordination between Protection (Women and Girls Safe Spaces) and Health, where HFs can play a key role as an entry point for survivors to reach specialized protection services. The Syria GBV sub-cluster has reported several cases of rape, exploitation, and abuse of displaced women and girls by men in positions of power. The existing vulnerabilities of the communities residing in Northwest Syria further increase their risk of exposure to sexual exploitation.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The presence of GOS has been increasing with the attempt to seize control of more territories north of the M4 and M5 highways. To mitigate safety and security risks, the NGO provides essential security equipment and conducts security and risk assessments to provide efficient mitigation measures. Staff in the supported health facilities and in NWS are receiving continuous security trainings on the SOPs and risk mitigation plans. SOPs in place could prove high efficiency for different occasions that cover airstrikes, GoS capture of territory or individual related incidents. SOPs currently cover incidents relating to loss of resources through attempts to influence recruitment, impose taxes, control access to services and supplies, and use humanitarian activities to cover their operations.To prevent fraud and corruption at the supported HFs, the NGO has developed a robust, context-specific fraud mitigation plan and continues to reassess and improve operations based on lessons learned, involving field staff from HR, finance, operations, and security.The NGO puts the safety of personnel and beneficiaries as its highest priority, so there will be no visibility for the proposed program in Syria, keeping the high level of coordination with the stakeholders at both Turkey and NWS levels. Needed protocols and MOUs are signed to clarify the roles and the responsibilities of the involved parties. Maintaining the presence in Turkey has provided stabilization for the cross-border operations supported by an open ending registration in Turkey.
