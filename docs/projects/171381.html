Project Summary

The project will contribute to two strategic objectives of the health cluster:
SO1: Ensure the availability of acceptable and quality essential healthcare services to vulnerable communities in oPt; and,
SO2: Strengthen the healthcare system's capacity to respond to emergencies and build community resilience to cope with the impact of current and future crises.
The project will serve a total of approximately 78,750 people including; 5,000 children under five, including 500 neonates; 9,000 high risk pregnant and lactating women; 14,750 men; 500 health professionals; 50,000 adults suspected of the COVID-19 infection.
UNICEF ensured the coordination with relevant authorities in the assessment of medical supplies and equipment needed at targeted health facilities in H2.

1 - Needs

The State of Palestine continues to be beset by a protracted protection crisis and suffers from chronic humanitarian concerns. More than 1.45 million Palestinians, including refugees, across the State of Palestine have been identified as requiring health-related humanitarian assistance in 2021.This project will address the health cluster objective to ensure the availability of acceptable and quality essential health care services to vulnerable communities. The focus will be to address critical gaps in the health sector response for women and children in the West Bank, especially in Hebron (H2 and Area C) communities, including the need for maternal and child health and neonatal services. In addition, the proposed activities are aligned with Joint United Nations Strategy for Hebron (UNSCO, 2016) and responds to the latest Multisectoral Household Assessment of Essential Services in H2 (OCHA, 2019), and the neonatal services assessment recently completed by WHO (2018-2019). The project will address the gaps through the provision of critically needed equipment, medical consumables and drugs. The total number of women, children and men that will benefit from this project will be 28,750 across the H2 area and two localities in Area C (Qilqis, Khalet Ad Dar) in Hebron district over a twelve-month period. It is estimated that at least 9,000 high-risk pregnant and lactating women and 5,000 children under the age of five (2,500 boys and 2,500 girls) including 500 neonates, will benefit from urgently needed lifesaving health interventions. Some 14,750 men will benefit from the provision of essential healthcare services. The existing health sector challenges in the SoP are being exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic, and the territories are classified as a high-risk setting with limited response capacity. In 2015, a Joint Assessment on International Health Regulations was conducted by WHO and the Norwegian Public Health Institute. The assessment identified: limited financing for preparedness, limited human resource capacities; health facilities that do not meet standards for infection prevention and control; lack of personal protective equipment (PPE), inadequate stockpiles of pharmaceuticals and diagnostics for an outbreak situation, equipment and training shortages. Thus, the COVID-19 outbreak in the SoP is likely to add burdens onto already strained health system capacities, such as availability and access to health care services and availability of medical equipment, and supplies. The impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on the lives of newborns, young children and pregnant, lactating women and adolescent mothers in Palestine might be severe due to the scale down of Maternal, Newborn, Child Health (MNCH) services delivered by all key health providers (MOH, UNWRA, NGO). Beyond direct illness, around 30,000 newborns and 210,000 pregnant women could have their health impacted as health systems are overwhelmed and routine services, have been reduced or suspended. UNICEF is particularly concerned about the impact of COVID-19 on all children and women in Gaza and West Bank already affected by inequality, poverty, and social exclusion. Especially children who are living in families at risk as they tend to live in overcrowded settings, without adequate nutrition and WASH services and the possibility to practice physical distancing. UNICEF will continue to work in close coordination with WHO, relevant authorities and other health and nutrition partners in its immediate interventions to protect children and their families from exposure to COVID-19 and to minimize the risk of mortality. UNICEF will contribute to the efforts strengthening the healthcare system's capacity to respond to emergencies and build community resilience to cope with the impact of current crises through provision of medical supplies including diagnostic kits and PPEs, benefiting some 50,000 people and 500 health professionals.

2 - Activities

Activity 1.1 Provide access to healthcare and nutrition that prevents maternal and newborn morbidity and mortality by ensuring the availability of clean and safe delivery, essential newborn care and emergency obstetric and newborn care services. Under this activity, UNICEF will work with relevant authorities to identify the needs of targeted health facilities in H2 and procure the necessary medical equipment; drugs and consumables and other medical supplies to support the provision of services for neonate children, children under-five and women including COVID-19 response related supplies; diagnostic kits and PPEs. This will also include capacity building on emergency and life-saving skills for neonate care and children care; maternal care. UNICEF will also ensure leveraging resources through other partners to address issues related to maternal care such as GBV and referral of cases to relevant services. Further activities with relevant authorities and nutrition technical partners will involve infant and young child feeding practices awareness, including training on responsive feeding.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

UNICEF safeguards beneficiaries’ rights to safe and dignified interventions. Consent of beneficiaries is usually considered and obtained through partners and implementing agencies joining training and community interventions. UNICEF ensures applying international standards, and WHO standards for medical equipment and medical supplies procured. UNICEF developed feedback mechanisms from beneficiaries to ensure accountability toward affected populations and to mitigate any risks identified by end users.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNICEF conducts independent need assessments prior distribution of supplies or materials. UNICEF verify needs through WHO and cluster members to ensure targeting the most vulnerable. UNICEF applies a multi-vulnerability targeting criteria to ensure equity and impartial interventions are designed.
