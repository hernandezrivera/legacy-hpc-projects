Project Summary

The proposed intervention will be jointly implemented by Welthungerhilfe (WHH) and its partner Shafak.
It aims to address the basic emergency needs of 60,970 of the most vulnerable Internally Displaced
Persons (IDPs) across 4 districts in north west Syria (NWS) through provision of lifesaving WASH services and supplies which will improve the hygiene situation. The conceptualization of this project has
been with engagement of the targeted communities and relevant stakeholders, following a
multi-stakeholder approach that enables ownership, complementarity, sustainability and integration of the
project interventions.
The targeted locations are highly vulnerable to a range of natural disasters, intensifying conflict, and forced
displacement, exhibiting critical multi-sectoral needs particularly relevant to this allocation. The living
conditions, including clean drinking water, hygiene items, and livelihood opportunities have
all degraded due to the protracted situation in the targeted areas, particularly for women and children.
Food security, livelihood and protection are identified as the top priorities in Northern Aleppo in WHH
assessments. Furthermore, there is an urgent need for WASH services in the overcrowded
camps in Idleb. In this extremely fragile context, the project will deliver appropriate, lifesaving support. The project aligns with and contributes to the HRP strategy by providing safe humanitarian aid responding
to the context, minimizing the spread of the coronavirus, and adapting different modalities in the
implementation of interventions.

4 - Needs

WHH and Shafak conducted a needs assessment survey for life-saving WASH supplies and servicesin Idleb and Western Aleppo districts, to determine the urgent needs and appropriate responses. The datacollection tool was designed by Shafak team. Data was collected by four male field officers throughinterviewing with 32 Key Informants (one KI from each targeted camp) where selection was based on thecriteria of knowledge about the camp situation, local context, and the technical ability to understand theWASH needs. The data collection process took two days from the 6 to 7 December 2021. The collecteddata provided camp-level analysis of the humanitarian situation of camps in Bab El Hawa, Babisqa,Burdaqly, Deir Hassan, Hezreh, Kafr Deryan, Sarmada and Tal Elkarameh communities. It analyzed theavailability and needs of both WASH services, and NFI items. . Of the 32 camps in Dana, 16% report not having any latrines. While in the 84% of the camps with latrines, the average number of individuals per latrine is far below Sphere standards. Furthermore, 96% of existing latrine units need maintenance and 97% of targeted camps are not connected to the sewer networks but instead are using septic tanks. The assessed IDP sites are critically lacking access to clean water, handwashing facilities and soap. According to assessment, 25% of camps do not have handwashing points at all, and 46% of those which have handwashing points are not sufficient to cover the camp.

5 - Implementation of activities

When looking at the assessment conducted by Ihsan in Idlib and western-Aleppo, it becomes very clearthat purchasing water from trucks as a supplementary source of water (as is necessary for 97% of therespondents) puts a huge financial burden on families. According to KI-interviews the living condition of thepeople in the camps is far below the poverty line, while for example 80% of families in Hezreh community’scamps and 77% of families in Sarmada community’s camps reported to have no income. Besidesthe virtually non-existent purchasing power of IDPs in camps, the situation concerning latrines and solidwaste is catastrophic with 16% reported to have no latrines and/or waste containers in thecamp. Furthermore, camp managers responded, that 96% of latrine units need maintenance andperiodic desludging. When considering the developing COVID-19 situation in NWS, it is concerningthat 25% of camps do not have handwashing points at all, and 46% of those who have handwashingpoints reported, that the number is not sufficient to cover the camp, 88% of existing points in thecamps need maintenance while 4% of them are out of service. Therefore, Ihsan RD will be providings afe water supply (water trucking and repair of existing water supply services), sanitation anddistribution of hygiene kits (in emergency situations, including COVID-19) to the targeted camps.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

WHH focuses on applying protection mainstreaming (both through the principled approach and in the technical areas of operations), primarily following the guidance of the Global Protection Cluster (GPC). While implementing protection mainstreaming throughout project cycle, WHH is pursuing four elements: prioritizing safety & dignity and avoiding or at least mitigating the effects of (internal or external) physical and psychological threats faced by affected population when accessing humanitarian services, arranging for people’s access to assistance and services – in proportion to need and without any barriers (e. g. discrimination), setting up appropriate accessible mechanisms, through which affected populations can evaluate the adequacy of interventions and address concerns and complaints in a participatory or involving way and finally supporting the development of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights, including – not exclusively – the rights to shelter, food, water and sanitation, health, and education. WHH adheres to the Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS), the Sphere Technical Standards, the Code of Conduct of International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement and NGOs in Disaster Relief and to other international standards and commitments. The aim is to improve the quality and effectiveness of its humanitarian assistance and to support greater accountability to communities and people affected by crisis. WHH's mandate is to end hunger.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) and Sexual Harassment (SH) are unacceptable breaches of fundamental human rights and a deep betrayal of WHH’s core values. The sexual exploitation and abuse of those who depend on WHH for assistance runs counter to all our personal and organisational values. It is unconscionable, it is intolerable, and it is often criminal. Equally intolerable is the sexual harassment of our fellow aid workers. Working closely with partners and communities, WHH has significantly stepped up its fight against SEA and SH. This work is informed by, and taking place in tandem with, the work of related actors including the broader UN system, donors, and civil society organizations. Strategy covers both SEA and SH, bringing them together in a way that is mutually reinforcing and that creates opportunities to leverage practices between the two. The Strategy also acknowledges the complexity and limitation to WHH’s authority in relation to SEA perpetrated by implementing partner personnel, including Government personnel. However, it does include measures to address and prevent these violations including supporting partners to fully integrate PSEA into programmes in both development and humanitarian contexts, creating a joined-up accountability culture that abhors, sanctions and prevents SEA and protects victims.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The security situation in Afrin is characterized by a high risk of explosive devices and presence of armed groups. WHH employs a balance of individuals recruited from the target area as well as other parts of Syria. WHH generally works on the basis of the acceptance strategy (acceptance by the local population through high-quality and rapid assistance) and good relations with the target groups. The work of WHH strictly follows the principles of neutrality and impartiality in relation to political, religious, military or other interest groups. In general, a low profile is pursued. The project team coordinates with the regional security advisor and local security officer. They are in constant contact with responsible information services (UN, Turkish border officials, AFAD, camp managers, local councils etc.) and exchange with local staff, Syrian NGOs and community members. The information is communicated promptly to personnel using a predefined communication system. All posted WHH employees are expected to complete security training, COVID-19 training, Code of Conducts training and all personnel are expected to attend first aid course. WHH regularly participates in the INSO security briefings, NGO Forum and the Access Working Group. WHH uses SOPs and chains of communication, and personnel movement in the project area is monitored. WHH has a duty of care policy and C19 contingency plan. Covid-19 stands as a big risk, therefore, necessary COVID-19 precautionary measures
