Needs

In 2019, the humanitarian community plans to respond to the most pressing needs of 1.1 million of the 2.9 million people in need. Under the humanitarian component of the Tribal Districts Transition Plan, 1.1 million IDPs in host and return areas will be provided health services, 1 million IDPs in host and return areas will be provided with food, 0.27 million will benefit from improved access to drinking water, sanitation or hygiene, and 0.1 million IDP children will be provided with access to education. The humanitarian community aims to ensure that all IDPs in host and return areas have access to primary healthcare, with special emphasis on mother and child health. Principled returns and access to protection services for IDPs are central to the Humanitarian Transition Plan. The humanitarian community will also support the Government in preparing to respond in the case of a natural disaster

Activities

OCHA will support the Humanitarian Coordinator and Humanitarian Country Team to develop and implement the humanitarian component of the Tribal Districts Transition Plan for 2019. This involves managing the Humanitarian Programme Cycle and supporting coordination structures at national and provincial levels. OCHA aims to coordinate and promote the efficient and coherent delivery of humanitarian assistance to people in need. OCHA will advocate with the Government on behalf of the humanitarian community for improved access, resource mobilization, principled and effective humanitarian programmes, and also support the Government in smooth transition from humanitarian response to development operations by providing secretariat support to the coordination. Emergency Response Preparedness (ERP) planning coordinated by OCHA will help in enhancing the HCT’s readiness for potential large-scale emergencies. OCHA will ensure an enhanced coordination between humanitarian and development planning processes to link preparedness and resilience efforts. OCHA will ensure that the HCT’s preparedness planning is harmonized with government contingency planning and national DRR priorities. OCHA will work with National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA) on developing the Disaster Response Framework which will provide guidance on HCT/NDMA collaboration, and relevant government bodies. It aims to ensure ‘operational readiness’ of HCT for supporting the Government of Pakistan in responding to the natural disaster when international humanitarian assistance is requested. • OCHA will support humanitarian financing by administering the Pakistan Humanitarian Pooled Fund (PHPF) and coordinating the Central Emergency Response Fund (CERF).• OCHA develops and produces information products for the humanitarian community, which aid in coordination and decision making.• OCHA will advocate for the inclusion of gender distinct needs in all humanitarian programmes including gender balanced assessments, gender analysis, adherence to HCT gender commitments and reporting gender distinct gains in monitoring. OCHA will provide coordination support on cash transfer programming in emergencies to the Government and humanitarian community including the Inter sectoral cash working group. In case of a humanitarian emergency or significant change in the situation, OCHA will conduct/facilitate rapid assessment and provide technical support to cluster/sectors for detail assessment. Transition to Development: OCHA will also continue to coordinate closely with various Government Disaster Management Authorities and other relevant bodies, to harmonize humanitarian response and transition activities. Technical Working Groups will assume the responsibility for coordination of relief and long term development activities and also determine modalities and timeframes for transition between cluster/relief activities and development activities.
