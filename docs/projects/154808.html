Project Summary

Provision of basic education and child protection services in IDPs and host communities in Ninewa Governorate: The IDPs are in need of basic education and child protection services through targeting of 2100 conflict affected children, adolescents and youth (3-17) that will be receiving teaching and learning materials; rehabilitation of 6 classrooms, rehabilitation of WASH facilities in 2 other schools. In addition, the project will aim to enhance capacity of targeted 80 teachers through training of teachers in gender sensitive and inclusive pedagogy, life-skills, Psycho-social Support and on PSEA, gender based violence. In addition, specialized child protection services will be provided for 50 at risk children and 1440 children will receive structured psycho social support through the child life skill and resilience. Integration with livelihood component of the existing WV Iraq project will enable to sustainable reduce the child protection concerns of out of school children.

Needs

The humanitarian crisis response in Iraq has now shifted its focus from relief into recovery support. As per UNOCHA humanitarian situation report as of July 2018, 8.7 million people are in need while 4.1 million of them are children. As of mid-2017 internally displaced families started returning home mainly to liberated areas of Ninewa, Anbar, Salah al Din, Kirkuk, Diyala, Baghdad and Erbil. The Ninewa Governorate the second largest province which was under Daesh control for up to four years suffered extensive destruction in the battles for liberation. Since then approximately 60,000 – 80,000 people have returned as per the rapid overview assessment conducted by REACH where it was highlighted that this around 30 – 40 percent of the pre-June 2014 population in Telafar. Telafar is a strategically located city in west Mosul where most communities from surrounding towns and villages to access basic services and other amenities. The communities in Telafar are predominantly home to ethnic Turkmen, where the majority of whom are Shi’a while the remainder is Sunni. In addition the area is also home to Kurdish, Christian and Sunni Arab communities. Rapid Overview assessments conducted by REACH on areas of return such as Telafar highlighted that there was a considerable gap in basic service provision across which is a pre-requisite for durable and safe return of IDP populations. As per the assessment a considerable reason for return was the improvement in the security conditions. Currently access to services in the area are improving although rapid overview assessments conducted by REACH on areas of return such as Telafar highlighted that there was a considerable gap in basic service provision across which is a pre-requisite for durable and safe return of IDP populations. As highlighted in the assessment, reasons for IDPs for not returning included property damage, lack of schools opened for children to continue their education and lack of job opportunities. Although schools were reported to have opened, there is a significant shortage of teachers, educational material and classroom equipment. In Ninewa, Telefar included, protection concerns remain high and the returning population, especially children, face challenges including voluntary returns, dangers posed by explosive remnants of war, rights violations by military or security actors, and safety risks caused by intercommunal tensions. In 2018, protection needs in newly liberated areas are likely to remain a priority concern since detention of women and children who surrendered with IS fighters has become protection concern, as is the potential for retributive action against them. The project targeted a governorate where very high gaps are indicated by Multi-Cluster Need Assessment (MCNA) and IOM displacement trend reports. Further guidance has been sought from the Ninewah child protection and education sub-clusters to complement the gaps from the existing efforts of World Vision Iraq and other implementing partners.

Activities

Child Protection: The project aims to provide specialized protection assistance that enhance the resilience for displaced and vulnerable communities. In addition, the project contributes to improved quality and inclusive learning opportunities for boys and girls in Telafar. Based on the different assessment results and existing livelihood, education and child protection projects in Telafar and other areas of Ninewa, World Vision International-Iraq is planning to further extend its support in both education and child protection In Telafar. The Project aims to advance the impact on the lives of children and their families by integrating the proposed protection assistance with the existing livelihood interventions. In order to promote the psycho social well being of boys and girls, the project will also build resilience and psycho social well being through life skill sessions at the mobile multi-purpose center. The project will adapt and integrate the existing Child Protection Cluster recommended Child resilience in creating awareness among the children on the psycho social well being and life skill development. The sessions will be conducted for one month for 6 cohort that will consist of 25 to 30 children over the course 6 months. In addition the project will encourage the boys and girls who completed the sessions to share with their peers in the neighborhood as a mode of transferring the knowledge and skill. Follow up sessions are planned after the first three months to support and record the learning and challenges for further impact. Through the life skill and resilience module, the project will reach 1440 boys and girls. The project will also support boys and girls who are at risk and survivor through case management services. The project will reach 50 children with complicated protection concerns. Families and communities will be aware on the identification and referral systems of child protection concerns. Integrating with the existing interventions of livelihood, the project will further empower families to support psychosocial wellbeing. Education: In addition to the specialized protection assistance and community based support, the project will contribute to improving access to quality and inclusive formal education. In order to increase access to quality formal learning opportunities, the project will provide learning material for displaced and vulnerable boys and girls who are at risk of dropping out due to financial difficulty to cover the school materials. In addition, transportation support will be provided for displaced and vulnerable community whose children are unable to attend due to distance to school. The families of these children will also be targeted either in the existing or new project interventions of World Vision. Since teachers play a crucial role in quality learning, the project will conduct training on learner centered, life skills, psycho social support, PSEA, inclusive and gender sensitive teaching approaches. The project will rehabilitate 6 classrooms and other two schools with inclusive WASH facilities.

Mitigation Measures

1. Fraud and Corruption Risks - Systemization of WV financial, logistics/supply chain and security procedures. Movement of large amounts of cash is (and will be) kept to the minimum and all the other transactions are done through banks. Staff and partner briefing and training on Code of Conduct and anti-Fraud/Corruption. Independent auditing process.2. Child Protection Risks - As a child based organization focusing on the most vulnerable children World Vision will ensure that the project implementation will not only adhere to WV 2017 Child Protection Policy but Cluster recommended Child Protection Policy and other associated standards, procedures and tools in ensuring our commitment to child protection through field oversight mechanisms, risk monitoring, incident reporting and other community feedback mechanisms ensuring the escalation of feedback and complaints.3. Counter-Terrorism Risks - Reduce frequency and exposure to at-risk potential targets (i.e. areas, situations and personnel). Situational awareness and coordination with UN, other INGO and Civil Community. Contextualized communications plan, and emergency warning to other staff. Profile Management Plan, JOPs Protocol. Emergency and medical SOPs. Journey management SOPs. Trauma-level First-aid kits. Staff trained in Advanced First-aid. Incident reporting procedure. Crisis management and Evacuation plans completed and staff are briefed appropriately.4. Health and Safety - Provide medical insurance for staff. ERW, Mines and IED Risk awareness for all staff. Access and movement SOPs. Local networks to monitor security situation and trends. Security Briefing for staff and partners. First aid equipment and training. Fleet safety and driver training. Online - Personal Security training for all staff and partners. Communication and advocacy plan that establishes WV neutrality and impartiality. Developing local acceptance. Hardening of WV locations. Access and Asset protection SOPs. Limit handling cash. Maintain low profile.5. Partnering Risks - Partner briefing and capacity building. Partner monitoring and conducting Blocked Party Screening. Partners’ evaluation before signing the MoU and committing. Supervising the implementation of the projects. 6. Contextual Risks - Access and movement SOPs. Local networks to monitor political and security situation as well as trends. Communication plan and local acceptance. Security Briefing for staff and partners. First aid equipment in vehicle and training for staff and partners. Communication and advocacy plan that establishes WV neutrality and impartiality. Developing local acceptance. WV supported facilities, activities and stores are chosen in areas at a distance from frontlines and armed combatant locations. Developing local networks to provide warnings. Contingency plans for WV staff.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Throughout the course of the project, WV will ensure that the targeted communities will be consulted on project activities through community meetings and program assessments each quarter. Beneficiaries and communities will also contribute to project inputs, such as labor and skills. Communities will also be provided with relevant progress and evaluation reports/updates.WV has used its experience and information from accountability mechanisms in similar intervention to amplify the voices and perspective of beneficiaries in the project design. World Vision will also continuously receive the input of beneficiaries throughout the project implementation to continue to integrate beneficiary feedback into the implementation of the project design.Steps for consultation with beneficiaries on design inputs involve:a) Scoping visit by the Accountability team to understand the power dynamics of theCommunities and document other observationsb) Summary report of the scoping visit to provide insights and direction on the consultationProcessc) Identification of key community leaders (men and women and groups) for participation inThe community consultation.d) FGD guidelines to be developed for standardizatione) The FGD guidelines translated into appropriate local language for easier usef) The consultations are recorded and analyzed for later useWV International has developed a partnership-wide Program Accountability Framework which seeks to define the minimum accountability standards for World Vision programs and how we engage with communities. This framework is relevant to all World Vision programming activities and in line with the fulfilment of Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP) principle # 2 and benchmark # 1, both which require setting of accountability standards and establishment of a quality management system. WV is also a HAP certified organization, with a strong commitment to accountability and the promotion and adherence to humanitarian principles and committed to ensure that persons of concern are well informed about the project activities and the existence and importance of a community-based complaints and response mechanism. WV is following the Sphere and UNHCR standards to implement, monitor and evaluate the project. As a matter of policy, every staff member is obliged to sign the WV Code of Conduct regarding abuse of power and sexual exploitation, fraud and corruption upon signing of employment contracts.Under the 4 pillars of Program Accountability framework (PAF) and based on the Core Humanitarian standards (CHS) commitment 4&5 the below activities will be conducted:-Information Provision: - WV will provide information to beneficiaries using face to face engagements. Furthermore the community member’s awareness on PSEA including informing them on the appropriate reporting mechanisms through face to face inter action and hanging banners at all the centers. Through this channel they will be informed on available feedback and complaints mechanisms.Consultation: - WVI is committed to the principle of informed consent and ensuring that communities are aware of, understand and agree with key decisions relating to our intervention. Participation: - MEAL team will ensure to communicate with the most vulnerable people (uneducated, elderly, people with disability, etc.) to raise their issues.Complaints and feedback mechanism: - WVI has an existing robust complaint and feedback mechanism. This mechanism used to receive information, complaints, and feedback through face to face meetings, hotline and Suggestions & complaints boxes. This information is classified 5 categories namely- Sensitive complaints, non-sensitive complaints, suggestions, inquiries, and feedback. All the inputs received will be addressed and followed up appropriately.
