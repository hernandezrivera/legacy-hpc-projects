Project Summary

Needs
After 12 years of hostilities and persistent economic crisis, people in
Syria saw their situation worsen when a series of earthquakes hit
northern Syria and Türkiye on 6 and 20 February 2023 Damaged housing in Syria remains a critical issue due to ongoing conflict and natural disasters, leaving numerous homes uninhabitable and displacing families. according to HNO 2024 6.2 million of people need shelter most of them live in places unvalid to live.
Activities:
Activities are rehabilitate damaged housing in project rehabilitation essential living spaces such as rooms, bathrooms, and key facilities like plumbing, electrical systems, doors, and windows. (Syrian syndicate in deir ezzor)
protection risks
Security Risks : Physical harm to workers/residents, hindrance to rehabilitation efforts.
Property Rights: Violations, Forced evictions, further marginalization of vulnerable populations.
Lack of Access to Basic Services, Threats to health and well-being of returning residents.
Gender-Based Violence (GBV): Increased risk of sexual harassment, assault, and exploitation, particularly for women and girls.
mitigation:
- Conduct security assessments, ensure mine clearance, provide safety training.
- Implement clear property laws, dispute resolution mechanisms, and ensure legal support.
- Coordinate with service providers to ensure access to water, health, and education services before return.
- Implement GBV prevention and response programs, provide secure and private living conditions.
operation risks
Risks related to housing rehabilitation in Syria encompass security concerns, funding shortfalls, and logistical challenges in conflict zones. Mitigations include coordinating with local authorities, securing dedicated funding sources, and employing community-based approaches to ensure project success and sustainability.
PSEA Risks
takatof Integrity Framework has policies that encompass fraud, corruption, PSEA, complaints and child protection. As a matter of fact takatof has zero tolerance to PSEA.. PSEA Risk: Exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers when accessing assistance and services. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation Measures: 1. Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and for whom they are intended, 2.Reinforce the message that humanitarian services are FREE OF CHARGE and signal the importance to report abuse and exploitation to the service providers ensuring confidentiality of the reporting, 3. Consult periodically with the population receiving the services/assistance, through regular and targeted sessions, including separate sessions with different groups that may be more at risk, ensuring standards of data protection and confidentiality, 4. Implement specific complaint mechanism and assign adequate staff to follow up on complaints, ensuring standards of data protection and confidentiality, 5. Provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA, 6. Perform post-provide services monitoring.
