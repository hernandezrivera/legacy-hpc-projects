Project Summary

The counties of Ayod, Pigi, Fangak, Maiwut, and Nasir in Jonglei and Upper Nile respectively display some of the lowest standards in WASH parameters according to the most recent WASH cluster severity mapping.
Gains in service provision over the years have been eroded by adverse weather effects resulting in prolonged flooding, leading to the destruction of WASH infrastructure, displacement of communities, deprivation of markets, and access to most parts of the states in focus. Flooding has further increased WASH-related mortality and morbidity affecting mainly under 5s, pregnant and lactating women, persons with special needs as well as persons living with HIV/TB. Lack of adequate WASH services directly affects the well-being of persons and communities, contributing to health, nutrition, food security, education, and protection indicators within affected communities.
Community Action Organization (CAO) works in Jonglei and Upper Nile States in South Sudan and plans a robust WASH response aimed at improving community well-being while integrating and working closely with other thematic areas.
The project will focus on fostering community-driven WASH responses to triggers. The project will further seek to enhance the reduction of WASH-related morbidities and mortality, through the provision of adequate, safe, and clean water for both domestic and livelihoods purposes, mitigating WASH-related GBV, and prevention of livelihood deprivation associated with inadequate WASH standards.
The project will reach a total of 75,000 persons in the counties of Ayod, Pigi, Fangak, Maiwut, and Nasir in Jonglei and Upper Nile respectively, 60% of these females, 5% persons with special needs.
CAO further will roll out an integrated response modality, seeking to implement jointly WASH, Health, Nutrition, FSL, Education, and Protection projects in the aforementioned counties.

1 - Needs

The protracted humanitarian situation persists in Greater Upper Nile due to the effects of conflict and current flood challenges, access, service delivery, and adverse weather conditions. Communities in the locations of Ayod, Canal/Pigi, Fangak, Maiwut, and Nasir in Jonglei and Upper Nile continually experience unacceptable living conditions due to poor access to clean and safe water, sanitation, and hygiene services; Greater Upper Nile has the highest number of people estimated to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3, 4) or worse acute food insecurity, with over 1.25 million people food insecure. With joint populations exceeding 1,427,399, more than 50% of these communities fall under IPC 3 and 4. Poor WASH conditions contribute to high levels of disease, malnutrition, and food insecurity affecting mostly under 5s, pregnant and lactating women, elderly, and PWDs. And high GAM rates are linked to poor access to safe and clean water, sanitation services hence leading to high cases of water-related diseases. GAM prevalence exceeded the WHO emergency threshold in Greater Upper Nile. REACH, Cluster Severity surveys indicate that over 78% of the communities in the locations lack access to a secure source of water, with over 86% using surface water for both domestic and personal use. Open water sources are most times contaminated due to poor fecal disposal as a result of a lack of latrines. Latrine usage is below 20% in the locations, with the majority of those with no access to safe and secure latrine being women and girls. Latrine HH surveys indicate a low latrine coverage, especially in Greater Upper Nile. Over 80% of the communities reported practicing open defecation. The insufficiency of water points has further been worsened by increased concentration of IDPs in remote secure locations, increase in returnees due to stability, breakdown due to over usage, and lack of access by humanitarian actors to carry out routine rehabilitation and repairs. An analysis of the Food Consumption Score (FCS) indicates gaps in dietary intakes among HHs, with low dietary diversification, coupled with grim figures on the household hunger scale. Poor WASH standards continue to contribute to high levels of malnutrition in Jonglei – 21.5% GAM; leading to increased incidences in child mortality. Torrential rains in the latter part of 2021, leading to sustained floods have made access to water points difficult and contributed to the destruction of latrines and bathing shelters. OCHA reports indicate over 1,000,000 people are affected by floods, exposing entire villages to water-related diseases. Protection issues are closely linked to access to basic needs such as water, sanitation, and hygiene. Women bear the brunt of collecting water, the majority from distant water points thereby exposing them to gender-based violence and SEA, with girls having less time for school and other livelihood activities. More time spent at water points reduces opportunities for women and girls to access other services. Women and girls lack access to dignity and hygiene kits, resulting in protection issues.

2 - Project activities

In line with the WASH Cluster Objectives Integrate WASH response with Nutrition/(Food Security) through; WASH service provision in nutrition facilities (WASH NFI kits and hygiene promotion to SAM children) and access to safe water to facilities in IPC 4/5 locations and provision of sustained water to communities showing high GAM prevalence and poor access to water. This will be mainly through WASH integrated intervention starting at OTP/SC targeting the couple “mother/care-taker – severely malnourished child” until HH level aiming at breaking WASH-related diseases transmission cycle aimed at improving access to safe water and promote safe hygiene practices in communities showing a high prevalence of GAM. 1. Provide timely rehabilitation of 40 boreholes and upgrading of 8 motorized water yards with the high production yield to 25000(women 6,425, girls 7,350, boys 7,150, and men 4,075) 2. Train on maintenance of sustainable water points WASHCOM and mechanized water yards 200 (Men 100, women 100) 3. Train 150 (women 75 and men 75) WASH Staff on protection and gender guidelines.4. Construction of 100 gender-appropriate and secured sanitation facilities to (men 1000, Women 1500, Girls 2000 and boys 2500 respectively).5. Conduct community consultation meetings that target leaders, representatives of stakeholders groups targeting all the project sites to introduce the project to the beneficiaries (men 500, women 1000, girls 600, and boys 400).6. Provide key hygiene promotion messages/sessions to (Men 5,000, women 11000, girls 12500 and boys 10,500) that include in community, health facilities, schools, OTPs/SC, market places, schools, and other public gatherings Facilitate gender risk analysis through focus groups discussion with women and girls by female WASH Staff (Women 1500 and girls 2300).7. Conduct focus group discussion on safe sanitation and hygiene facilities access in a secure location agreed after a GBV risk analysis for women 1000 and girls 2000 respectively.8. Provide hygiene WASH hygiene kits to children admitted for SAM/MAM treated and discharged from functional OTPs/SC (Girls 2100 and boys 1400).9. Provide safe water to children admitted for SAM/MAM through the construction of hand-dug or drilling of new boreholes and repair of the existing water points at the OTPs/SC facilities (Girls 1500 and 1000 boys)10. Train nutrition, health, protection, and gender volunteers on WASH-related topics (Men 100 and men 110) Provide a point of use household water treatment to the vulnerable population with safe drinking /chlorinated Water (Men 1600, women 2200, girls 1800 and boys 1700).11. Provide vulnerable population with soaps to functional handwashing facilities to reduce WASH-related diseases such as cholera and AWD with active chlorine 0.05 %(men women 1400, men 900, girls 1100 and boys 1200).12. Provide vulnerable population with WASH minimum package of activities adapted to their vulnerabilities (men 400. women 1360, girls 1200 & boys 1500).
