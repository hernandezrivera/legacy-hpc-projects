Project Summary

This project initiated by GREENCODE is aimed at promoting inclusiveness and peaceful co-existence among IDPs in Host Communities, Returnees, and Host communities through activities that enable displaced communities to assess conditions in their home areas and to plan their return together with receiving communities and support the return and reintegration process, promote livelihood, capacity building and community-based responses;
Promoting the return and reintegration or local integration of IDPs and returning populations within local communities and ensuring their integration into local/area recovery and development plans and interventions;
It will also ensure that displaced and returnee populations and women in particular are not victims of discrimination in relation to their rights e.g. access to land and property.
It will be targeting all the gender and ages in the communities of Askira Uba nd Chibok LGAs

Needs

The multi-year Early Recovery and Livelihoods Sector strategy is aligned to the 2016 Buhari Plan developed by the President Committee for the North East Initiative (PCNI) by contributing to its priority intervention on Rehabilitation, Relocation & Resettlement. The ability to access basic services, produce goods and participate in the local economy has been significantly hampered. This has resulted in a loss of livelihoods and increased hardship for millions of people across the BAY States. Moving away from geographically and thematically fragmented interventions, the overarching goal of this multi-year Early Recovery response plan is to stabilize local communities through the provision of support in four inter-related areas: livelihoods, security, basic services, and emerging local governance. This will in turn lay the foundations for sustainable development. Data from recent multi-sector needs assessments and Livelihoods and Economic Recovery Assessments report on inadequate levels of access to basic services and public infrastructure relative to the size of the population, limited income and livelihood opportunities, and increased resource demands, leading to societal tensions between IDPs, returnees and host communities. To address the affected people’s needs, targeted early recovery interventions that contribute to short, medium and towards longer-term development interventions aimed at strengthening the self-reliance of affected populations, especially girls, youth, women, and the elderly against future vulnerabilities will be prioritized. These are divided into four main areas of i. Improving access to Basic Services and Infrastructure; ii. Economic Recovery; iii. Peace Building and Social Cohesion; and iv. Local Governance Restoration.
