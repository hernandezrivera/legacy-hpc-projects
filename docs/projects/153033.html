Project Summary

This project intends to support communities, IDPS, returnees and host communities. It will target total beneficiaries of 10,000 with health care services in static and mobile clinics around five strategic health centers in Leer and Mayendit County. The project aims at assisting and alleviating the suffering of mix of beneficiaries comprising of returnees, IDPS and host communities to prevent diseases in the two counties of Leer and Mayendit. The project will provide medical support to vulnerable members of the community with 5 primary health care centers, 6 primary health care units and 12 mobile clinics services in order to increase health services accessibilities in remote and unreachable part of the two counties. This support is believed to minimize and control the spread of diseases in the communities affected by the conflict that started in December,2013.
According to Coalition for Humanity assessment, which was done in October 2018 on health specific needs in targeted locations in both Leer and Mayendit counties, the report finding confirm that over 60% of the population are in need of health services more so the maternal health and mother child health. In 2019 it is estimated that 50% of the population in the targeted locations in Leer and Mayendit Counties shall have access to health care through this intervention complementing the work UNIDORS is doing across the two counties.
The two Counties of Leer and Mayendit have been the epic centres of the conflict with population completely uprooted from their homes hence unable to access to basic health care services. Few functioning health centers are situated far away from population especially in areas targeted by this project. During the heat of war, health facilities were targeted and destroyed or occupied by the warring parties, which has made things to be started from scratch. The insecurity have further pushed community to more remote villages which were not serviced by partners before hence averagely a mother or child walking distant of three to four hours to access the nearest health center.
The IPC food security report indicates catastrophe phase 5 of the two counties with likelihood of less diet or general eating deficiency caused by limited food.This has health complications especially for children U5, PLW and the elderly. The source of income especially cattle in these two counties were taken which led to the high rate of malnutrition in children and elderly people. Any minimal health intervention shall integrate basic nutrition and general public health messaging including WASH and Food security and livelihoods to support the vulnerable population in the counties.
The population in these two counties has gone through highest level of insecurity and conflict, displaced severally from 2016 to late 2018. These forced the communities to live in hard to reach areas or islands for safety. With recently signed peace, community shall return to their homes and influx expected from neighboring counties, POCs and neighboring countries over stretching on the available health services. Therefore it is in this understanding that Coalition for Humanity will focus on PHCC, PHCU and mobile clinics at the targeting locations with highest needs; the project will also give training to local health personnel and community health workers to building on the volunteers that are willing to support or run these centers. Services shall be provided to vulnerable communities in the selected IDPS setting while extending the same to returnees in their new settlements.
The project is expected to address urgent lifesaving medical support in term of supporting the existing facilities or do temporally structure in the host communities concentrating on IDPS sites such as in islands and other hard to reach areas as well as providing basic health service to those started return to their homes.

Needs

According to the recent coalition for humanity findings assessment conducted in October 2018 within targeted locations in leer(Pakuem,Kom and Pading) and Mayendit (Kertuok,Dhorleek) Counties, it was found that majority of the health facilities were burned burned down in both Leer and Mayendit Counties. Most of the community assessed averagely trek for 2-3 hours to get to the nearest medical center to get support. This is very inhumane considering that most of the distances are covered in swamps or several islands to cross or insecurity along the way. Usually if a child is sick, the mother is forced to take her/him to the nearest facility that could be 2 to 3 hours walk away. As result women and girls have been exposed to high level of sexual violence along the way including when walking after dark.Patients most times arrive after their medical condition get worse especially for maternal cases which need urgent midwife or medical attention. The planned health intervention in the two counties targets a total of 10,000 persons comprising of IDPS, returnees and host communities. This estimate could increase with the relative peace (R-ARCSS) resulting in high demand for medical services in 2019 period and beyond. Due to protracted conflict in the two-targeted counties of Leer and Mayendit there exist serious health gaps, which are further compounded by worsening insecurity lack of infrastructure. According Reach report in April to June 2018 approximately 90% of health facilities in Mayendit and Leer Counties were reportedly burned down or where community structure exists, there are no drugs supplies and personnel. The report further pointed out that numbers of health-trained professionals are not easily found locally apart from those already working in NGOS partners on the ground. This results in the community health workers inability to manage difficult cases. The gap created as result of absence of humanitarian assistance due to insecurity were confirmed by an internal Coalition for Humanity assessment conducted in Leer and Mayendit Counties in September-October 2018, which found that the general health status in those counties is completely broken down and further worse in particular locations where we planned this intervention across the two counties. Most population have fled their homes and currently living in IDPS settings especially in islands where they sheltered for safety and overstretched the already limited services offered by leading partners on the ground such as UNIDORS in case of health and Nile Hope and Medair in case of nutrition. Those islands does have very limited access to health facilities both static and mobile clinics.With recently signed R-ARCSS in Addis Ababa and observed cessation of hostilities in effective, it is expected that local population will start moving from those hard to reach areas closer to their homes where they will start having access to health service from their nearest health facility. It is therefore imperative that having an organized and planned intervention can therefore cover such gaps. This intervention shall provide Lifesaving emergency healthcare and nutrition access to vulnerable populations focusing on the major causes of morbidity and mortality.

Activities

Coalition for Humanity (CH) will implement the following essential health care packages (as per health cluster guidance): • Establish 5 primary health care centers, 6 primary health care units and 12 mobile clinics services in order to increase health services at all levels in targeted locations in Leer and in Mayendit to minimize and control the spread of diseases in the communities affected by the conflict. • Provide Ante and postnatal checkup, normal deliveries in the 5 PHCC• Conduct Family planning activities in all the 11 facilities in order to creat awareness among the community members about the importance of child spacing, prevention of unwanted pregnancies.• Provide EPI activities against the preventable childhood diseases• Establish 5 cold chain system for the vaccines in the PHCCs• Provide Basic management of SGBV survivors (PEP, CMR, treatment for STI, wound care etc)• Minimum Initial response for HIV• Management of TB• Provision of MHPSS for people in needs • Provided training to 40 health workers on Psychological Support and Psychological First Aid for people affected by the conflict (Children, women, and people of special needs).• Establish 12 Mobile units both in Leer and Mayendit counties.• Conduct RRT Mobilized Training for 40 health workers on integrated Health, WASH.Protection and nutrition response• Train 40 health workers on emergency preparedness and response.
