Project Summary

UNICEF recognizes cash transfers as the most effective first response in humanitarian crises, providing immediate relief and empowering affected populations to meet their urgent needs with dignity and flexibility, and is looking to increase its humanitarian cash response in 2025.
UNICEF is targeting 15,000 people with a multi-purpose cash response in the NW, SW and Far North regions in 2025. Cash response will be only implemented in locations where cash was identified as a feasible response option. UNICEF will target the most vulnerable households using vulnerability criteria include factors such as displacement, income and family size, and household make-up (single-parent households, child-headed households, households with persons living with disabilities, households with malnourished children/or at riskI, etc.). Furthermore, vulnerability criteria are contextualized to the intervention region together with the affected communities to improve accuracy and acceptance. UNICEF will use the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) transfer value as established by the Cash WG and validated by the Inter-Sector WG and the HCT.
The selection of beneficiaries will be preceded by a targeted community sensitization on the scope and purpose of the humanitarian cash transfers. The cash transfers will be accompanied by complementary social behaviour change (SBC) (cash-plus) activities, on the needs of children and risk communication, and community engagement (RCCE), gender equality, gender-based violence (GBV) in emergencies, including sexual exploitation and abuse prevention and response measures and flood related disease mitigation.

1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The populations in the Far North, NW and SW continue to be deeply affected by violence, natural disasters and epidemics. The Lake Chad basin conflict and the North-West and South-West crisis continue to impact the lives of women, girls, men and boys in affected areas. The various shocks have led to a decrease in income, making it difficult for crisis-affected populations to access basic social services.
