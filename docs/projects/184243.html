Project Summary

Many parts of Somalia are experiencing prolonged drought conditions and water shortages since June, with thousands of people affected, according to the Somalia WASH Cluster. According WASH cluster In South West State approximately 17,263 households (103,578 people) are facing devastating water shortages in several districts including Ceel Berde, Rabdhure, Hudur in Bakool region, Qansax Dhere, Dinsoor, Berdale, Burhakaba and Baidoa in Bay region Wanla Weyne, Barawe and Merka in Lower Shabelle district. Most of those locations solely depend on rainfed earth dams or shallow wells which at risk of running dry.Forecasts indicate an increased likelihood of below-average rainfall in October to December, which will adversely affect food security and nutrition outcomes. In the absence of humanitarian assistance, the FSNAU estimates that nearly 3.5 million people across Somalia will face food consumption gaps or depletion of livelihood assets indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes through the end of 2021. In addition, approximately 1.2 million children under the age of five are likely to be acutely malnourished, including nearly 213,400 who are likely to be severely malnourished.In September, the FSANU indicated that a highest water price increases observed compared to the five-year average (42-53 percent) in most parts of the northwest (i.e. Toghdeer, Sool, and Sanaag, 12-16 percent), most parts of Bari and Nugaal (20-64 percent), Glakacyo district of Mudug (48 percent), Marka district of Lower Shabelle (44 percent), Xudur of Bakool (21 percent), and Qansax Dheere of Bay (54 percent). According to HNO and HRP 2021 projection an estimated 6.61 million people, about 42 per cent of the Somali population are in need of emergency life-saving WASH assistance of which 4.2 million will need safe drinking water, 3.2 million sanitation services, and 5.2 million hygiene supplies. 16 per cent of these are females, 64% children and the elderly for 4 per cent
The proposed project is intended to respond to the WASH needs identified in terms of providimg emergency water services, appropriately designed and managed emergency sanitation services, critical hygiene items and key hygiene message to vulnerable settlements and communities in Bakool Bay and lower Shabelle regions of Somalia. These interventions will include but limited to drilling of one borehole in Qansaxdhere to provide sustainable access to water for 5,000 beneficiaries, rehabilitation of 2 boreholes by installation of hybrid solar systems in one Qansaxdhere, one Baidoa and Elbarde to service a population of 10,000, 9 rehabilitation/restoration of shallow wells with hand pumps to serve a population of 4500 beneficiaries. Provision of emergency water trucking/voucher to 10,000 beneficiaries for drought hardest 3 months of the year and targeted locations will be project targeted districts. Conduct water management committee training for 60 participants selected for community water points created/rehabilitated.GRRN proposes to construct 400 gender , disability and protection sensitive communal latrines for displaced population 12,000. GRRN will conduct massive hygiene promotion activities targeted 75,000 beneficiaries and proposes to distribute 4000 hygiene kits targeting 24,000 beneficiaries with basic hygiene kits within proposed project targeted districts based on vulnerability of the targeted communities. GRRN will ensure people with disabilities inclusion through assessment prior to implementation to identify people with disability, consult them in WASH infrastructure designing, location and their desired designs. All WASH project activities will be done with protection lenses. The targeted communities women, men, girls and boys will be engaged in all steps of project implementation. People with disabilities, women, children, minorities , elderly people will be given special attention to ensure inclusivity and diversity of the project service delivery.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

As per WASH cluster recommendation this project will have some component of Market based WASH programming to enable local markets and capacity of local communities to respond to future emergencies. Most south West state districts are sieged/have access constraint for both supplies and humanitarian persons therefore cash transfer programming will be appropriate fastest ways of reaching disaster affected communities. For example latrine construction and hygiene promotion incentives will be through cash transfer modality. To ensure accountability and transparency GRRN will used mobile cash transfer through and service service providers through competitive process.Beneficiaries of hygiene kits, construction materials will be given a voucher which they will redeem their entitlement from local stores.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

GRRN will conduct baseline survey in all operational district to assessed availability of goods and services of concern in the local market, local capacity to main supply chain in terms of quantity and quality. All locations where market based WASH programming is feasible and appropriate GRRN will implement through market based WASH programming. This project activity will be implemented through market based programming where GRRN will identify local artisan who have experience in well rehabilitation as well as local stores who have the capacity to provide good quality WASH services and goods. In order to capacitate well operators and beneficiaries on well operation and maintenance they will be trained by GRRN water technician and artisans identified during shallow well rehabs, latrines constructions, pipeline extension who will provide casual labor and they will be paid for the service provided. This will boost local market and will ensure sustainability of supplies since local stores will be motivated to provide supplies required for well rehabilitation and maintenance. The beneficiaries will be empowered through their participation of wells rehabilitation.Latrines will be constructed at least 30 meters away from any groundwater source, and the bottom of any latrine will be at least 1.5 meters above the water table. The latrines construction will be implemented through Market Based Programme whereby latrine beneficiaries, suitable stores/hardware within the area of implementation and local artisan or skilled men and women in latrine construction are identified by GRRN logisticsdepartment following the organization standard procedures. The identified artisan and HHs will be trained on latrines construction standards by building prototype first with the help and guidance of the WASH project engineer and then GRRN will provide all construction materials to the beneficiaries through voucher system that can be redeemed from local stores dealing with construction materials that is pre-assessed by GRRN technical team, local artisans will be identified capacitate on the designs of the latrines requirements and will do the construction of the latrines with help of GRRN hired engineers supervision and guidance.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Deteriorating security situation - In order to overcome the security risks that may arise, GRRN will work closely with United Nations Department of Security and Safety (UNDSS), Somali National Security Agency (SNSA), and the clan elders. GRRN has been working in those location for more than 6 years and knows the terrain, clan dynamics, security and access challenges and to mitigate risk to staff and project implementation GRRN will avoid anything that leads to conflict of interest, poor targeting or bias in beneficiaries selections. Instability of prices and availability of the needed resources on the local market as a result of blockage and access constraint in some locations – GRRN will undertake competitive and wide sourcing of construction materials to ensure efficient utilization of available funds. Lack of acceptance by the local leaders of the participatory approach for the beneficiary selection process – GRRN will undertake initial dialogue, information sharing with the relevant stakeholders to ensure inclusion, understanding and acceptance of planned programming. Lack of access for monitoring purposes – GRRN will have in its employment staff from the local community who will have access, even during challenging situations Security in Somalia is dynamic and the districts security focal point will be analyzing security situation and advice the front-line staff the situation every morning. It is anticipated that the security will remain calm and allow smooth implementation of activities within the project time-frame. GRRN will also employ market based WASH programming to avoid risk of delays arising from supplies delay due to blockage or access issues in the region as part of mitigation measure.Design and construction of WASH facilities must preserve the safety and dignity of its users. If an individual does not feel safe or dignified in using the facility, (s)he may go elsewhere, potentially exposing themselves or others to harm. Build separate toilet and bathing facilities for males and females. Make sure they are clearly marked in pictorial form for illiterate users and work with community to ensure they are used by the indicated sex. For privacy, provide secondary enclosures around facilities or put locks on the doors to latrines and bathing houses.
