Project Summary

The explosion in Beirut on August 4 came as Lebanon had been facing a multi-layered crisis.
The disaster has negatively impacted the healthcare system capacities in Lebanon, which were already diminished due to the economic crisis, devaluation of the national currency, and the ongoing Covid-19 pandemic.
The needs assessment highlighted key concerns related to the health sector to be considered at first stage, indicating the importance of preparing and implementing actions in the targeted areas at the fastest possible interval. Reducing some of the needs of the vulnerable population could provide a relief either short or long term and encourage people to move forward with their lives.
Immediate risk factors for mortality and morbidity in post crisis include:
 disruption to health services and other key health system functions
 decreased access to health services
 exposure to violence and other traumatic events
 insufficient vaccination coverage
 change in behavior following crises
Condition which could increase with the Beirut Blast:
 Minor injuries from glass or debris
 Trauma from fall, heavy object clash and accident
 COVID-19 infections
 Mental health disorders (Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder)
The disaster has further fuelled the COVID-19 outbreak with daily confirmed infection rates ; and ICUs were already near capacity prior the Beirut port explosion.
After disasters, women could have specific needs related to health, specially pregnancy-related issues. Risks of sexual violence are also experienced differently based on gender and gender roles, with an estimated one in five refugees or displaced women in complex humanitarian settings experiencing some form of sexual violence.
The project will improve the access to quality essential health services (primary healthcare services, including mental health) to victims of the blast in Caritas Lebanon (CL) health facilities, provide health outreach services to the affected populations and link them to the health centers, and strengthen the infection prevention and control measures to prevent a bigger increase in Covid-19 cases by distributing PPEs to households and individuals, and conducting awareness programs.
The project will focus on promoting gender equality in all responses in order to ensure that women, girls, can access assistance safely, and a special attention will be given as well to other vulnerable groups including children, persons with disabilities; single heads of households, displaced persons, refugees and migrants.
To do so, the following will be implemented in Caritas Lebanon health centers:
- Female doctor availability for women who do not want to interact with opposite gender service providers,
- Security in front of centers to ensure safety and wellbeing of all patients,
- Priority and reservations given to women, and especially pregnant and lactating ones, to ensure they receive primary health care services first,
- Advocate the provision that free services are being provided at the health centers to break economic barriers,
- Provide primary health care service through home visit if the patient does not have access to the health care center (elderly, PWD...)
The project will be implemented in the two following centers:
CL Sin El Fil primary healthcare center
CL St Michel (Sid El Bauchrieh) primary healthcare center (even if this center is outside the directly affected area, many referrals are done to this center, and a big number of affected persons are approaching the center ; also, a team from the PHCC is conducting door to door visits to provide health services in the directly affected areas)

1 - Needs

The explosion has negatively impacted public healthcare capacities in Lebanon, which were already diminished due to the economic crisis, devaluation of the national currency, and the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic. Due to the explosion, at least three hospitals and 12 primary health care centres have been severely damaged, rendered partially or fully inoperable, which used to previously serve around 120,000 people.Also, as per the recent assessment by WHO following the blast, thousands of persons from Lebanese and non-Lebanese communities are in need of immediate mental health and psycho social support interventions and referrals to specialized services.Also, the disaster has further fuelled the COVID-19 outbreak with daily confirmed infection rates reaching more than 600 cases.

2 - Activities

The activities that will be implemented under the project are the following:1- Provide primary healthcare services PHC services will be provided to 5,000 beneficiaries (750 boys, 750 girls, 2,000 women, 1500 men) via CL’s 2 PHCCs, and through door to door visits for any patient who can't access the centerThrough CL health facilities, the affected population will be provided with free essential health services by a multidisciplinary health team. Primary healthcare services will include medical consultations, provision of medicines, nursing care, diagnostic exams, vaccination with a special focus on women, children, elderly, persons with disabilities, migrant workers.All the recommendations and guidelines in regards to the Infection prevention and Control of Covid-19 will be applied (entry point, triage, limited number of beneficiaries at the same time, use of PPEs, physical distancing)2- Conduct Psychological First Aid and individual Mental health and psycho social supportPsychological first aid will be provided in CL facilities, and also through door to door visits to any individual not being able to access the center.Mental health follow up will be delivered by a team of psychologists and psychiatrists, via individual sessions or group therapies. A close coordination takes place between the health and social teams of CL, based on a referral system. The mental health and psycho social support that will be offered is based on the Intervention pyramid for mental health and psycho social support in emergencies, as described in the mhGAP Humanitarian Intervention Guide.PFA and MHPSS will be provided to 1,500 individuals (200 boys, 200 girls, 700 women, 400 men)3- Conduct awareness programs on infection control and prevention measures against Covid-19 Awareness programs will be conducted in CL health facilities and through outreach workers to 9,000 individuals (1,500 girls 1,500 boys 3,000 men 3,000 women).The main goals of these sessions will be the following:• Raising awareness/prevention, symptoms, action to take in case of suspected case• Fighting stigma arising from the pandemic• Explaining the importance of PPEs4- Distribute PPEsPPEs will be distributed in CL health facilities and via door to door visits to 9,000 individuals (1,500 girls 1,500 boys 3,000 men 3,000 women). This distribution will take place in parallel with the awareness programs on Covid-19, where people will be informed on how and when to use the PPEs
