Project Summary

The main objective of the project is to improve protection, safety, security and enhance access to justice and peaceful coexistence between refugees and host communities in additional 8 camps in Cox’s Bazar, with particular attention to vulnerable groups such as youth, women, elderly and persons with disabilities.
The project will provide a forum through which refugees and host communities will have improved access to protection services and conflict resolution mechanisms, as well as access to legal assistance and victim support services. The expected results will reinforce the capacities of actors that are currently working on community safety and security, justice and protection in both refugee and host communities. This project will focus on addressing crime, promoting women’s empowerment, prevention and responding to SGBV in a more inclusive and sustainable manner in accordance with national legal and local administrative structures and refugee protection standards. The interventions proposed are based on the findings of a community safety pilot project carried out in two camps in 2020 and 2021, which emphasized the need for increased police presence in the camps and training on humanitarian law, protection principles and community safety. The pilot project also established community safety forums in the two camps to enable partnership and dialogue leading to local solutions to problems identified jointly by refugee and host communities while strengthening different forms of social accountability. This project will be jointly implemented by UNDP, UNHCR and IOM in close collaboration with the Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commissioner (RRRC) and Camp in Charge officials, Armed Police Battalion (APBn), District Police and local government institutions in Cox’s Bazar. Further to this, the project will also work closely with UNHCR and IOM Protection sector and staff in the camps, Sexual and Gender Based Violence (SGBV) staff and Camp Management.
The multi-stakeholder approach will achieve refugee protection through more predictable and sustained support to both refugees and the communities hosting them. Through the project, efforts will be made alongside law enforcement agencies and the judiciary to ensure refugees and host communities live with reduced fear of crime and to ensure accessible, safe reporting mechanisms are in place to allow refugees to report incidents of crime and violence, and to do so confidentially and without fear of reprisal. In terms of participation and empowerment, this project combines top-down processes and institutions, including supporting police and justice actors, good governance, strengthening accountability and social justice instruments, with a bottom-up focus in which inclusive and participatory processes support the important roles of individuals and communities as actors in defining and implementing their essential freedoms. For disaster preparedness, agencies will work with a range of partners, including other Sectors, community-based protection networks in the camps, and relevant authorities as appropriate to the situation to identify the threats and support the people in need. Agencies will verify the needs of EVIs, coordinate services, and support other partners to mitigate harm during disaster response. The agencies will work closely with protection staff and partners to support outreach sessions for Rohingya and host community members, camp authorities and partners to raise awareness on COVID-19 and impact on available protection service.

1 - Identified Needs

Nearly all Rohingya refugees living in the camps in Cox’s Bazar depend on humanitarian services for their survival. They are confronted with limited opportunities for livelihoods and complex and intersecting financial pressures despite efforts by the Government of Bangladesh and humanitarian agencies to provide for their basic needs. They also face a range of safety issues including theft, lack of lighting, drug use, drug trafficking, human smuggling, trafficking in persons, disputes over aid distribution and gender-based violence. The Bangladesh Government has made concerted efforts to curtail the spread of COVID-19, yet the negative economic and social impacts of the pandemic reverberate across the camps, as is the case globally. The Government of Bangladesh has enhanced security measures in the camps since 2019, however gang violence in the camps and throughout Cox’s Bazar district, particularly that related to drugs trafficking, continues unabated. The Cox’s Bazar Analysis and Research Unit (CARU) of UNDP reported high numbers of homicides in 2020 and 2021. The violent attacks and murders continued in 2021 involving two and more different religious groups of Rohingya attacking populations inside Cox Bazar camps. Police also report regular arrest of Rohingya involved in drug trafficking and various crimes including smuggling, human trafficking and prostitution. Through the pilot project on Community Safety in 2020 and 2021 three agencies conducted Community safety baseline focus FGDs in 2 camps. The FGD findings indicated the need for 24/7 presence of the police in the camps, varying perceptions of safety, security, risks and mitigation measures between refugee men, women, boys, girls, camp leaders and camp administration. In addition, the FGD findings pointed that women and girls face additional layers of violence as a result of socio-cultural practices and lack of adequate response and crime report mechanisms in their communities.Based on the assessment by Centre for Peace and Justice and The Asia Foundation in February 2021, refugees stated that they need better access to civil justice for cases involving interpersonal disputes, divorce, domestic violence, polygamy, among other issues, and in dealing with challenges around birth and marriage registration. The lack of access to civil justice experienced by refugees is connected to a web of interrelated variables centered around social issues, personal safety, security, and broader human rights issues. One-third of respondents said they had been able to obtain legal support and found that support satisfactory. This is a sign of the effectiveness of efforts already undertaken by the NGOs and other stakeholders to strengthen refugees’ access to justice. But improvements and expansion of services are needed, as two-thirds of respondents said they were unable to access information, justice-related services, and expert help in the camps when needed, and many who did receive support and services found them inadequate to resolve problems. The lack of an adequate camp dispute resolution system and the absence of a national policy framework outlining the rights and responsibilities of refugees and displaced persons in Bangladesh are the two key areas impeding progress toward access to justice for Rohingya.In parallel, host communities in Cox’s Bazar are also facing socioeconomic and safety pressures. Host communities indicated a feeling of being ignored by humanitarian organizations, rising labor competition, deforestation, price increases and damage to physical and natural resources. GBV is on the rise in the host communities, including intimate partner violence, physical assault, emotional abuse, and denial of resources. There is rising anxiety among locals about being outnumbered, and a widespread perception that kidnapping, thefts and armed robbery have increased since the 2017 influx. Access to justice for vulnerable and poor host communities remains a challenge.

2 - Activities or outputs

1- IOM, UNHCR, UNDP will continue capacity building of law enforcement authorities, particularly APBn, in humanitarian law, refugee protection and community safety through a trainer of Trainee (ToT) curriculum in collaboration with partners within the Protection Sector. In parallel, UNDP will continue with capacity development of District Police in the area of criminal investigation, gender sensitive policing and community policing, while IOM will cover trainings on anti-trafficking and link police capacity development with Countertrafficking Committees established in the host communities through awareness raising activities . Training platform for police on humanitarian law, refugee protection and community safety will be developed and integrated into the curriculum of the Police Academy. 2- UNHCR, IOM, UNDP in cooperation with General Protection, Gender Based Violence, Child Protection will support establishment and capacity building of Community Safety Forums in selected camps. The agencies will apply gender, age and diversity markers when establishing Forums and enable inclusive participation. Capacity development will focus on community safety, conflict resolution and mediation, promoting peaceful coexistence. Skills gained through training programs will be utilized for development of safe community plans jointly with police and CiCs, recognizing different gender and age safety and risk perceptions and addressing the vulnerabilities facing women and youth. UNDP will revamp existing Community Policing Forums (CPFs) in host communities adjacent to the camps to contribute to overall peaceful co-existence. 3- Capacity development of community leaders in conflict resolution, and youth peer-to-peer education on violence and crime prevention, including SGBV and Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA). Activity will promote positive attitudes and behaviours and offer skills and knowledge to promote peaceful coexistence, protection from violence and crime, sexual exploitation and abuse, and minimize potential secondary victimization. 4- Improving the reporting of crimes and police response, to SGBV , SEA, trafficking in human beings and other crimes affecting women, children and other vulnerable groups, and expanding legal assistance services, including targeted awareness raising activities within the refugee communities on access to formal and informal conflict resolution mechanisms. The Agencies will apply an integrated approach to Protection, and increase coordination with GP, GBV, CP different Protection sector groups. 5- Provide targeted legal and social awareness trainings to women in camps to capacitate them on their legal rights and access to legal remedies. UNDP will work on enhancing the capacity of Union Parishad Legal Aid Committees in host communities through trainings and awareness raising on case referral mechanisms to support the most vulnerable and poor, especially rural women and girls. 6- UNHCR and IOM will build capacities of refugee community structures (women, men, youth, elderly, people with disabilities) and camp leadership committees on aspects of alternative dispute resolution and mediation through trainings to support peaceful coexistence. In parallel, UNDP will support local level host -communities mediation forums in Ukhiya and Teknaf through skills development and awareness raising. Good practices identified through this mechanism in local communities can be extended to mediation mechanisms within the camps.
