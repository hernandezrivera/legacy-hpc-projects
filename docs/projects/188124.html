Project Summary

11 years of conﬂict in Syria has not only led to destruction and reduced functionality of infrastructure, but also affected socio-economic patterns, and diminished Syria’s population’s ability to sustain living standards, to beneﬁt from decent and gainful livelihood opportunities and to access essential services, in addition to COVID19, which add a further burden of socio-economical destruction on Syrian communities. Consequently, the protracted nature of the crisis has put an additional strain on social cohesion, community security and peace building.
Needs assessments through various sources indicate that currently PIN for ERL Support increased from 11.6 M to 13.8 M, which means the growing need for job opportunities after the massive loss of livelihoods and increased pressure on essential services and infrastructure.
Based on that, the ERL sector’s objective framework needs, have been categorized under the three interrelated components: Support the maintenance and improve equitable access to basic services, focusing on electricity as an important service has an impact on other sectors, such as a productive and social infrastructure, markets, health canters, WASH and education facilities. The other objective focuses on increasing the job opportunities, providing vocational skills. The third objective to strengthen social cohesion by empowering communities to lead the recovery process, focus on joint context analysis with communities, build their capacity and facilitate inclusive community led and participatory early recovery plans and priorities’ areas.
Through the area-based and community engagement approach, considering the context sensitivity in participatory planning and designing with community, UN-Habitat aims to boost sustainable access to basic and social services to displaced, host and returnee populations in selected neighbourhoods in (Rural Damascus, Daraa, Deir-Ezzor, Hama, Homs and Aleppo) and help to reduce risks of leaving vulnerable groups behinds. Since, the area-based approach ensures more equitable access to essential services using multi-sector efforts, access to electricity is one of the most critical service underpinning to address the wider sector needs. By doing so, the project will achieve an immediate response to COVID-19 affected communities by enabling a source for livelihood generation and promoting social cohesion among different affected communities.
In practice, the approach consists of the rehabilitation of market areas, public spaces, roads, sidewalks, providing solar lighting. Also, it includes debris removal where needs are presented in damage-affected neighborhoods and are prioritized through ‘Neighborhood Recovery Action Plans”, which have been developed through a municipality and community-engaged program supported by UN-HABITAT. The restoration of urban service and infrastructure (e.g. restoration of market area and its surrounding roads) will contribute to enhance self-reliance of communities and lead to economic recovery. The rehabilitated infrastructures will revive the value chain and urban-rural linkages. Through this process the livelihoods opportunity for urban and rural communities will also increase. Also, this will be supported by COVID19 response plan to creating healthy market and places. This plan will contribute to livelihoods activities.
In addition to the criteria in HRP, the selection of targeted locations is based on:
- Being in line with the ERL Sector and HRP 2022 strategic priorities and objectives, as well as severity of needs
- Being priority interventions and locations identiﬁed through area-based recovery plan with detailed ﬁeld assessments and consultation with local communities and municipalities
- Complementarity with ongoing projects already through UNHABITAT resources and donor commitments including UN-Habitat projects under HRP Shelter and WASH sectors
- High numbers of people in need, vulnerable and expected returnees in target locations.

4 - Needs

Acknowledging the core ERL objectives, it is in critical need to provide essential services and to facilitate socio-economic recovery not only for 6.9 million internally displaced persons, but also for host communities and returnees. Livelihood: According to sector estimates, 48% that, support to livelihood alone has proven insufficient, as well as the provision of quality services alone. Therefore, only a multi-livelihood and services approach can provide the needed support. Besides to the lack of getting access to employment, the high likelihood of youth to adopt negative coping mechanisms such as joining extremist organizations, crime, traﬃcking etc. is an important consideration. Social cohesion and community security: Prolonged conﬂict and the challenges of COVID-19 have weakened the social fabric in ways which equally affects host communities and IDPs. This situation is further pressured by returnee dynamics, adding strain to already overburdened systems.The community-lead planning is a continuous and established methodology seeks to engage target communities in all phases of the early recovery process. In terms of the analysis and planning stage, local authorities and community representatives are engaged in participatory assessments and planning workshops, which result in identified early recovery priorities. The approach lends itself to wider partner prioritization of activities, since the ERL plans are endorsed by communities and local authorities.

5 - Implementation of activities

In the selected locations, the project will implement the following activities:a) Updating/or creating the recovery action plan using the area-based approach, engaging target communities in the context analysis to identify the real needs and priorities.b) Restoration of and access to basic social services: 1. Cleaning the neighborhood from debris and waste and sterilizing the roads and streets. 2. Rehabilitation of market area, public/open spaces, roads, and footpaths and provide street lighting3. Creating a gender friendly learning open spaces for women, youth and children to conduct awareness campaigns and sessions4. Livelihood support to Female headed households (e.g. access to urban agriculture activities and link them to rehabilitated market)c) Improve effective local public services delivery: 1. Support the communities at the neighborhoods' level to identify the specific needs in terms of rapid COVID risks’ assessment and response plans with focus on ERL. 2. Provision of required training to engage the communities in monitoring the humanitarian support being provided. Following the respective ministries’ procedural requirements, projects will be implemented through local resources – communities, municipal technical departments, private sector entities, and workforce engaged from affected populations, in particular unemployed youth will be an important to provide short-term employment opportunities.The volume of activity will be balanced between 2022 and 2023

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Discrimination towards potential beneﬁciaries from ERL assistance on the basis of ethnicity, religion, age, gender etc. Likelihood: H Impact: H Mitigation Measures:  Adopt interactive, stakeholders’ participatory transparent process for criteria deﬁnition and selection. Strengthening local Authorities capacity to restore and update relevant records to develop area-and situation speciﬁc action plans for all residents regardless of documentary status.  Constant advocacy on all levels for inclusion of all possible beneﬁciaries in temporary assistance and durable solutions. Protection Risk/Threat: Exclusion of female beneﬁciaries and female headed households from ERL assistance due to absence of registered male family leaders Likelihood: H Impact: H Mitigation Measures:  Transparent process for criteria of beneﬁciary selection.  Project design to prioritize vulnerable beneﬁciaries and at-risk groups  Collaborative, stakeholder inclusive process to identify priority areas to design speciﬁc interventions. Risk: Young people risk exploitation through child labor in ERL projects due to absence of working age male family members, family's lack of other revenues and/to lack of ﬁnancial resources Likelihood: L Impact: M Mitigation Measures: Ensure proper registration system for staff/partners/contractors’ laborer.  Add zero tolerance on child labour in the contracts & and proceed random check of personnel’s civil documentation on project sites.

Risk/Assumption: Delayed assistance to vulnerable and people in need due to the processes to approve programs, projects, locations Likelihood: H Impact: H Mitigation:  Close coordination with the speciﬁc ministries and other related public departments to expedite processes  Consideration of anticipated time in project planning processes.  Selection of project locations with high concentration of vulnerable / at-risk groups but with little likelihood of being contested
