Project Summary

Since 2019, Sudan is in a transition period and despite the recent political efforts from international community and local community lead initiatives to bring the conflicting parties into agreements and to stabilize the situation, the humanitarian health situation remains complicated and fragile. The main drivers of the humanitarian health crisis in Sudan include armed conflict, displacement, disease outbreaks, and floods as much as lack of staff, medicines, medical supplies, equipment, testing capacities and operational costs. Disease outbreaks are mostly water-borne, vector-borne and vaccine preventable. The reporting of recent 18 MPX confirmed cases from 6 states, importation of 2 suspected cases of EVD in Khartoum and identification of invasive vectors for transmission of dengue fever and urban malaria has highlighted low preparedness capacities, weak cross border surveillance mechanisms and added further stress to already strained health system due to risk of importation and further spread of highly fatal zoonotic diseases. The humanitarian health response project aims to address major risk factors, to provide free of-charge essential and life-saving health services to vulnerable populations and to strengthen mitigation, early detection and intersectoral multi-hazard preparedness and response. WHO’s mitigation, detection, preparedness and response to health emergencies is in line with the national health sector and health cluster strategies and follows national and international standards. WHO will provide technical and operational support to strengthen the coordination mechanisms on all levels of the health emergency preparedness and response, will strengthen and support the collection, analysis and dissemination of health related information and data in close collaboration with partners and other stakeholders. WHO will work closely with both Federal and SMoH at subnational levels by providing technical assistance through WHO country office and hubs across the country with the aim to increase the capacities of early detection, verification, risk assessment, confirmation and timely response to public health threats for enhancing the national health security. WHO will focus on all essential primary health care components to achieve universal health covering through providing integrated health services for meeting people’s health needs throughout their lives; addressing broader determinants of health through multisectoral policy and action and empowering individuals, families and communities to take charge of their own health.
WHO, as provider of last resort, will procure and distribute free of charge medicines, medical supplies, medical equipment, personal protective equipment (PPEs) and laboratory supplies to support the humanitarian health care provided by NGOs and MOH. WHO will also engage in capacity building of health workers on health emergency related subjects such as disease surveillance, rapid response, case management, infection prevention and control and risk communication among others. The project will also include small repair and rehabilitation of health facilities and supporting operational costs for humanitarian health services. Access to health services for all age groups and gender with their specific health needs will be adapted to the local cultural customs, including advocacy and synergic linkages with ongoing recovery-development interventions. 6.4 million people residing in 75 localities with severity ranking level of 4 and 5 for humanitarian health response. As custodian of IHR, the activities related to infectious diseases outbreak control will be extended to address the health security and protection needs of affected population wherever needed. WHO will continue to mobilize all potential resources to address all-hazards emergency risk management including mitigation, detection, preparedness and response measures.

1 - Needs

Humanitarian needs across Sudan are at record levels one year after a military coup, with unclear prospects of how the transition to a new Sudan that started three years ago will evolve. Protracted and new displacement induced by inter-communal localized conflict often driven by political agendas, increasing protection of civilians' risks and threats due to the rise in criminality and insecurity in parts of Darfur, BN, WK and other conflict-affected areas, unprecedented persistent disease outbreaks have increased numbers of people in need of humanitarian assistance third year in a row. The most significant risks posed to Sudan are conflicts, disasters associated with natural hazards, infectious disease outbreaks, economic deterioration, lack of staff with high turnover rates, lack of medicines, medical supplies, equipment and operational costs. In 2023 HNO, humanitarian partners estimate that about 15.8 M people, roughly (32%) of the population – will need humanitarian assistance in 2023. This is a 1.5 M increase compared to 2022 and is the highest since 2011. Of the 15.8M people in need, about 11M need emergency assistance for life-threatening needs related to critical physical and mental well-being. This is a (21%) increase compared to the previous year. Meanwhile, all the people require life-sustaining support to meet minimum living standards. The health cluster estimated that 10.1 M people are in need. There are 3.7 M (IDPs) in Sudan after the International Organization for Migration (IOM) did the latest round of verification of displacement tracking across 801 locations in Sudan. Humanitarian partners estimate that with various challenges and factors that underpin conflict and displacement not resolved, there can be more civilian displacement in 2023 and the number of people who need assistance is likely to increase further. The disease surveillance system is fragmented with only 2,168 out of 6,300 total health facilities (34.4%) representing sentinel surveillance and 70% of health facilities lack essential lifesaving medicines. Sudan continues to suffer from the brunt of (VBDs), despite of a long history in the fight against these diseases through vector control. Sudan shares 56% of morbidity due to malaria in the EMR, and is also endemic for the arboviruses including Chikungunya, Dengue and YF. These arboviruses are causing outbreaks annually with observed rapid geospatial expansion of the infestation due to the huge population movement, and existence of the disease vectors in almost all the states. All 18 states of the country suffer two or more VBD outbreaks annually. Dengue Fever reached 1,126 including 8 deaths with an AR and CFR of 1.3/10,000 and 0.7% respectively. 13 localities in 5 states are currently reporting dengue cases. 17/18 States have reported either lab confirmed, or epi linked cases of measles. There are ongoing measles outbreaks in Kassala, Gedaref and North Kordofan. A total of 2,614 suspected cases and 1,059 laboratory confirmed/epi linked measles cases have been reported so far this year. Malaria cumulative reported cases reached 1,899,124 including 167 associated deaths and crossing of the epidemic threshold in 14 states. Inter communal conflict in BN state resulted in 69,550 IDPs within BN and other states. Conflicts in Darfur and Kordofan states have resulted in additional 146,360 IDPs. From Jan-Sep 2022 (OCHA) reports of about 348,724 people affected by torrential rains and flash floods across Sudan with 73,109 houses destroyed/ damaged by floods in 16 states. FMOH reports 18 States, 123 localities and 1,568 geographical areas affected by flooding including 181,778 houses affected/ destroyed, 24,135 latrines destroyed/damaged along with 107 deaths and 206 injuries reported.

2 - Activities/Outputs

To increase equitable access to health services for vulnerable communities, the project will implement the following. Activities 1) support provision of primary and secondary health care services including trauma care, EmONC, and referral services through health partners and government counterparts as appropriate. The project will support procurement and propositioning of essential medicines, medical supplies, laboratory reagents, medical kits and equipment and provide training for health workers on diagnosis, case management and IPC. A total of 3.5 million people including 1.7 million women,1.77 million men including 347,700 people living with disabilities, in settled communities and IDP camps, returnees and refugees will be reached with PHC services. 750,000 children (including zero dose) < 5 years of age will be vaccinated against measles and other VPDs. 200 primary health facilities will be equipped with essential medical supplies including IEHK and TESK. Regional laboratories will be strengthened. At least 20 emergency mobile clinics will be deployed. Referral pathways will be strengthened with special attention to vulnerable groups such as women, children, IDPs. Activities 2) 189 locality level and 18 states level surveillance officers will be trained on notifiable diseases case definition, importance of immediate and weekly reporting and to be supported to submit reports using mobile phones. 205 EWARS reporting sites in 9 states will receive receive training on alert detection and reporting using EWARN reporting tools. 2,500 community volunteers in 18 states will receive refresh and basic trainings on CBS. 2 RRTs per state and 1 RRT per locality will be trained on timely alert investigation and response in the 18 states and their missions will be operationally supported. ONE HEALTH joint investigations will be operationally and technically supported. POE and cross border surveillance will be enhanced. Multi Hazard risk planning exercise will be conducted in consultation with States and National level. Necessary support will be provided to National level for advancement in digitalization of Surveillance and response system through ICT and technical support. Sample shipment cost to WHO reference laboratories will be covered. 7.5 million people will receive key messages for disease prevention from community health workers and community leaders. 1,200 CHWs will receive training on RCCE. At least 10 biological hazard IEC materials will be printed and disseminated. Rehabilitation support will be provided for health facilities in affected areas. Activities 3) As interagency health cluster lead agency, WHO, will also support coordination and leadership in health emergency preparedness and response efforts; foster interagency collaboration, support establishment for task forces, activation of Public Health Emergency Operation Centers (PHEOCs) with ONE HEATH concept, co-lead health cluster coordination at national and sub-national level with FMOH/SMOH; monitoring of emergency health response through health cluster tools such as HeRAMS, 4Ws matrix, State’s vulnerability, Cluster bulletin and WHE-HIM bulletins updates regularly. The project will aim to strengthen health systems capacity to enhance vulnerable groups and communities (including IDPs, refugees, and people living with disability) resilience to absorb and respond to shocks. Support will be provided to conduct 4 workshops on the concept of PHEOC for the government authorities at national and subnational level to improve coordination capacity.16 subnational PHEOC will be supported with IT and furniture to run smooth coordination. WHO will implement these activities in close coordination with MOH, UN agencies and health cluster partners to ensure no one left behind. Activities 4) The project will support improving access to health services for vulnerable communities through mobile and outreach services when appropriate; strengthen MHPSS delivery.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project focus is to improve access of affected population regardless of their gender and sex to essential health care services, putting the services closer to the affected communities protects the most vulnerable entering in damaging coping strategies that increases their vulnerability to exploitation and abuses (especially women and children). The need for improving the access to free-of-charge health care for the communities affected by humanitarian crisis is even greater in Sudan where the out-of-pocket expenditure is more than 70%. During monitoring of the health situation, WHO coordinates with community leaders' committees which set to accommodate the affected communities’ concerns and satisfaction while early identification and response to ongoing outbreaks as mitigation and control measures for public health threats/outbreaks is the most efficient way to prevent emergenciesbecame disasters, protecting not only the well-being of affected group but also the host communities, both IDPs and host communities will benefit getting access to the supported clinics as do no harm act. Proper medical waste management (incinerators, pit sharps) is included into the sector and MOH standards for the protection of staff, patients, communities and environment. All the activities that will be implemented in this project will use environmentally friendly procedures and equipment. The mitigation procedures will be in line with the concepts and understanding of the beneficiaries in the way that will keep their dignity especially in the indoor vector breeding sites treatment and adult flies control using products approved by the WHO and ensuring proper disposal of chemical waste. Reducing the burden of diseases for the vulnerable household, would reduce the expenditure for health protecting the families entering damaging coping strategies that will make them more vulnerable to abuses and exploitation
