Needs

The conflict has created legal needs of great magnitude due to large number of rights violations. According to the recent OHCHR report (16 May to 15 August 2018), new wave of hostilities in May-June 2018 worsened the immediate and long-term human rights protection of people living in the affected areas. As a result, conflict directly affects more than 600,000 people, who live within the range of five km. on both sides of the contact line. The surge in protection needs resulted in increased demand for state run social benefits and other protection services. The 1.5 million IDPs have mainly become dependent on state-run IDP social benefits including rent subsidy. Arbitrary denial of social benefits and pensions has proved to be a major legal issue for the IDPs in the last one year. Referring to Pension Fund statistics, OHCHR notes that from April to June 2018 only, the number of IDPs receiving pensions fell by 15 per cent, from approximately 562,000 to 477,000. Lack of civil documentation, including death certificates, birth certificates and national passports have also been proved as a vital factor for lack of access to social benefits and services, albeit for a small section of IDPs. Existing state mechanisms and civil society resources and facilities for legal aid have proved to be inadequate in addressing the massive legal aid needs created for 3,3 million people in need. In addition to the shortage of resources and facilities, there are systematic issues that continue to affect IDP and conflict affected people’s access to justice. Impediments to IDP and conflict affected people’s access to justice can be classified into four categories. First, the loss of or non-access to judicial and administrative documents and archives has caused visible dysfunctionality within the judicial and administrative system where judicial and administrative bodies’ capacity to dispense justice to IDP and conflict affected people is substantially limited or restricted. Instances of the dysfunctionality is reflected in the challenges in registration of death, birth and marriage, enforcement of judgment, resolution of pending cases, serving judicial processes, notarisation of document, restoration of pension and social benefits, and production of witness and evidence. Secondly, lack of physical access to the judicial and administrative bodies, and lack of access to archive documents are severing right to access to justice for 3,3 million (according to 2017 HRP) of conflict affected people and grey zone IDPs. According to UN, an estimated 57% of children born in Donetsk and Luhansk NGCA do not have a birth certificate issued by the Government of Ukraine. Thirdly, the vast unmet legal aid needs have yet not been met by corresponding provisioning of legal assistance services by state and civil society legal aid actors. The imbalance in demand and supply has debarred many vulnerable IDPs, even in the GCA, from their right to access to justice. The situation is occasionally compounded by the propensity of the State of creating unwarranted legal aid case volumes through structural violation of IDP rights. The most recent example is the apparent unlawful action of the government to verify IDP registration and suspend social and pension benefits. The Supreme Court found this unconstitutional, but at the time of this writing verifications continue. This particular act of the State alone has created tens of thousands of new legal aid cases, adding further pressure on the available legal aid resources in the country. Finally, there are important areas where access to justice for IDPs and conflict-affected populations is dependent on financial resource availability of the State. The area of housing, land and property (HLP) rights is a prime example. It is believed that tens of thousands of IDPs may have legitimate claim for compensation for movable and immovable properties damaged in the conflict.

Activities & outcomes

Between 12-18 January 2017 DRC undertook a legal aid needs assessment survey in the buffer zone. The survey team interviewed 199 heads of households, selected randomly, using a questionnaire containing 28 questions. Among the survey participants 72% were female and 28% male which may be explained by the fact that men, viewed as main breadwinners, tend to leave buffer zone areas to find employment opportunities. The survey found the following most pressing legal assistance needs in the buffer zone. • Civil documentation including missing passport and expired photo in the passport• Land documentation, including land privatization document • Legal needs of unaccompanied children, including guardianship and access to schooling and healthcare services• Enforcement of restitution and compensation right of residents with damaged and destroyed houses• Inheritance• Access to social benefit and pension• Issues relating to livelihoods and employment rights• Freedom of movement • Legal rights of the victims of mine and other conflict related casualties 49% of the survey participants belonged to 56-85 age group, while 29% belonged to 41-55 age group. Such figures reflect the exodus of younger generations who have high mobility thus able to leave buffer zone areas in search of safer habitation and employment opportunities. Individual Assistance versus Policy Responses: While many of the IDPs’ legal needs are solvable through individual legal assistance, structural interventions are warranted in the areas of HLP, civil registry, court fees, releasing pension for conflict affected, reconstructing court and civil documents and redefining court jurisdiction. Systems advocacy aiming at influencing legislative and policy changes has broadly remained ad hoc, uncoordinated and reactive. National NGOs, with the larger number of advocacy initiatives so far, are often constrained by resources, analytical expertise, and lack of exposure to comparative global knowledge and good practices. With this in mind, the following activities are proposed for this intervention: (a) Rights Awareness: Methods will include organising group information sessions, and dissemination of information through printed leaflets and brochures.(b) One-on-One Legal Counselling: Individual IDPs will be empowered to deal with his/her legal issue through the provision of one-on-one legal counselling. Counselling will be provided during mobile clinic, at office for walk in clients, as well as through mobile phone calls and Skype calls.(c) Administrative and Documentation Assistance: Administrative and paralegal assistance will be provided in order to secure relevant registrations, obtaining civil documentation and obtaining social benefit and services. (d) Court Cases: Assistance will include drafting of plaints and petition and representation of beneficiaries in court proceedings. (e) Systems Advocacy: The project will contribute towards bringing changes in policies and legislations through enhancing collaboration between civil society and other relevant stakeholders. Methods will include legal analysis of national and international law, compilation of comparative global best practices, classification of legal aid trends and practices, drafting of position papers, organizing roundtable discussions, and presentation of advocacy agenda to stakeholders. Advocacy initiatives will also include publication of monthly legal alert, and periodic holding of roundtable discussions.

Indicators & targets

The project will be implemented in compliance with the AAP guidelines 2012. DRC legal assistance program is built on solid evidenced based findings accumulated through stakeholder consultations, past experiences in service delivery and post service monitoring reports. DRC also maintains a complaint mechanism which provides the affected population the opportunity to provide feedback on the services received. DRC regularly carries on baseline surveys involving beneficiaries, and post distribution monitoring surveys. Awareness raising - 30,000 beneficiaries (65% female) will have awareness of rights through group information sessions and dissemination of printed materials. 15,000 beneficiaries (65% female) will be provided one-on-one legal counseling on legal issues affecting their durable solutions. 1,500 beneficiaries (65% female) will receive administrative assistance with regards to accessing state sponsored benefit and services and other legal matters. 1000 beneficiaries (65% female) will be assisted in obtaining civil and other documentations. 700 beneficiaries (65% female) will be assisted in court cases. 12 monthly legal alerts will be published Legal Alerts.
