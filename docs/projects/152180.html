Project Summary

Project will build the capacity of relevant service providers and traditional structures for enabling prevention, identification, and response to protection concerns of displacement-affected communities; will promote the rights and support protection needs; and will provide emergency and early recovery assistance to returnees, IDPs and host community in North East Nigeria

Needs

Since 2009, northeast Nigeria has been characterized by complex and mixed displacements due to the ongoing conflict. According to OCHA (August 2017), unpredictable population movements and access continue to be major challenges for humanitarian agencies to respond in a timely and targeted manner to the humanitarian crisis in the northeast. 1.63 million people are currently displaced by the insurgency in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe states, with the majority residing in Borno (77%). Protracted displacement has compounded vulnerabilities and increased dependence on aid from government and humanitarian actors to meet most of IDPs’ basic needs, which IDPs themselves reported perceiving as a negative aspect of their displacement. It has been observed during various needs assessments that children are the most affected due to displacement, whereby the fatigue caused by displacement has put them at high protection risks and they are increasingly becoming vulnerable to suffering from violence, exploitation, neglect, and abuse. This suggests a need for prioritizing resilience-building activities and long-term support, particularly in sectors essential to successful long-term integration. There is also a need for adopting a community- based approach whereby the capacity of community members is increased to respond to the child protection concerns within the community and refer other child protection concerns which they cannot address themselves to relevant actors. Field visit reports have indicated that subject to displacement and lack of available services, a number of refugee and IDP returnees do not have identity documents especially birth certificates for children. NRC has noted that children born during displacement are at risk of statelessness due to ignorance and lack of information on how to obtain birth certificates. This is particularly the case for children born during displacement abroad, i.e., when their parents were refugees in neighboring countries such as Niger or Cameroon. With the disruption or lack of service providers in some LGAs, target populations lack information on where or how to access identity and civil documentation especially birth registration, which would enable their children to access basic rights and services. In terms of HLP rights, cases of destroyed or occupied properties and cases of land grabbing have been reported in the return areas. Loss of title or other documentation during the move gives little hope of land reclamation. As is often the case, women are the most vulnerable to land rights abuses. The Housing, Land and Property (HLP) sub-cluster in Nigeria reports several cases of IDPs renting houses without a formal document to secure tenancy, placing them in a vulnerable position in the face of arbitrary eviction. The evaluation carried out by the ICLA team in Nigeria shows that the lack of knowledge of the rights and administrative procedures of displaced populations was the main cause of this fragility. For CDR, IDPs and host populations have greater confidence in customary dispute resolution structures than the statutory legal system. In Nigeria, local leaders bring the parties together, collect evidence and make a decision on a dispute which the disputants may choose to accept or not for reconciliation. If either party is unsatisfied, there is an appeal process. Moreover there is an interaction between the statutory and customary systems, where courts give weight to the decisions of the customary justice actors. The dispute resolution structures are interconnected in Nigeria, and it offers an important route to ensuring that more options to solve disputes are available to complainants and disputants to serve their interests consistent with their rights. One of the advantages of customary system is that local leaders are readily accessible and do not demand fees, which is a contrast to the statutory system. However the services are not directly accessible to women.

Activities

1. Train authorities involved in civil registration and identity management, as well as humanitarian actors and displacement-affected communities, on legal identity, CDR and HLP2. Organise advocacy events with decentralized and national authorities responsible for civil registration to facilitate civil registration procedures.3. Conduct awareness-raising campaigns and group information sessions targeting displacement-affected communities on legal identity, including on the prevention of statelessness, as well as HLP rights and CDR4. Provide counselling to displacement-affected individuals on access to civil and identity documents, and HLP rights and issues5. Provide legal assistance to adults and children to obtain LCD and HLP related documentation6. Organise advocacy trainings targeting humanitarian partners, government authorities and displaced populations 7. Develop and disseminate area-specific information packages for displaced populations on legal identity and access to essential services.8. Support the authorities in the coordination of HLP working groups9. Provide protection referrals within NRC and to other humanitarian partners
