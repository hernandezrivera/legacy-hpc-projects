Project Summary

Protracted crisis in South Sudan, including climactic shocks and continued conflict, have resulted in 9 million people projected to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2024.On top of these multilayered shocks, the Sudan crisis has adversely impacted South Sudan with more than 375,000 people having crossed the countries northern borders, predominantly from Renk. As a result of the shocks, such as flooding, inter-communal violence (ICV) and ongoing armed clashes, the majority of the population are facing increased food insecurity, lack of access to basic services, protection risks and mass displacement. As per the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2023, lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices, lack of shelter and survival means, will negatively impact the most vulnerable South Sudanese.
Since 2006, Solidarités International has become a key humanitarian actor in South Sudan, implementing emergency lifesaving responses across the country and providing integrated WASH services to crisis-affected populations in areas of static presence in Upper Nile and Unity States. Since 2012, SI has played a key role in the Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) strategy in South Sudan, implementing multi-sectoral assessments and delivering emergency responses to crisis-affected populations all over the country. In 2023, SI became member of the ERRM country response and was able to deploy emergency response team in Upper Nile following new conflict and or displacement alerts.
In 2024, SI will continue to provide emergency live-saving WASH, FSL and Shelter responses across South Sudan to restore basic access to safe water, hygiene, and sanitation while ensuring equitable access, safety, and accountability to supported populations. Through its Emergency Rapid Response Mechanism team, SI will deploy to emergency-affected locations to provide first phase and second phase responses. It will include WaSH, Shelter and as well FSL quick response under cash or in-kind distribution to tackle emergency food insecurity situations. Fishing and agricultural support will be as well provided in second phase response to improve coping mechanism as well as livelihood economic opportunities for long term recovery for the population.
At the same time, SI will expand its presence in Upper Nile, and Unity to provide basic WASH, FSL and Shelter services that target the most vulnerable, including those suffering malnutrition, food insecurity and protection threats (including GBV survivors).
Static programs will focus on providing community-based solutions for second phase response with the assistance of the population in agricultural and fishing sector with specific designed kits and trainings to allow durable response and economic activities that can be maintained independently by communities. Engagement and joint programming with Protection and Nutrition actors will ensure a multisectoral response of SI that can be accessed by the most vulnerable and will have a tangible impact on reducing overall priority humanitarian needs. The increased integration of protection, inclusion, and conflict sensitivity analysis in all programming will ensure that WASH services and resources can be safely accessed by all in the community. Greater engagement of communities and local markets will support the design of long term and flood designed solutions starting from the supply chains to implement the activities to ensure that interventions produce durable results for communities.

1. Needs

In Unity and Upper Nile, where SI has a static presence, flooding and conflict result in displacement of thousands of households, it reduces their access to basic WASH services, food access as well as increased protection risks. Water points and sanitation infrastructure have been submerged, contaminated, and damaged. Assessments in Fashoda, Manyo, and Melut found that SWAT systems had been submerged by flooding. Even in areas with normally better water supply, flooding and displacement have had significant effects. Flooding is seasonal in South Sudan, the raining season being delayed this year will highly affect the households in the upcoming months according to the latest forecast of humanitarian agencies. This situation in addition of the increased pressure over resources (e.g. water, land, etc.) since April in link with the influx of migrants from the Sudan crisis will further impact the already affected-population. For instance, Unity State (Bentiu, Rubkona, Leer etc), one the two county that received the greatest number of refugees, flooding has caused huge loss in land for farming and livestock. In November 2023, 46% of the overall population was facing high levels of acute food insecurity. Among children, 1.65 million are experiencing moderate to acute malnutrition situation. IPC projections for 2024 expect to have 7.1 million people facing acute food insecurity with a peak of vulnerability for the population during the lean season. On top of the needs identified, major clashes have been reported in SI area of intervention in 2022 and 2023 (namely Fashoda and Panyikang/Malakal, Tunga, Aburoc counties), with around 40,000 people displaced by the clashes. WASH and food security related needs are huge, as newly displaced people are completely lacking access to basic services. This lack of access to safe water, improved sanitation, and hygiene and continuing shocks has increased the risk to vulnerable populations of water-borne diseases, in turn influencing rates of malnutrition (affecting especially children under 5and pregnant and lactating women). People with specific vulnerabilities, such as People with Disability, also face many additional challenges to accessing basic services.

2. Project activities

SI’s 2023 strategy will focus on 1) Short-term emergency response to reduce mortality and morbidity for the most vulnerable population in priority and WSC level 4&5 locations and in IPC4+ counties through static and emergency responses (corresponding to WASH objectives CO1, CO2.1, CO2.2) 2) Ensuring medium term safe, equitable and dignified access to essential WASH services to enable populations to meet their basic needs in locations of WSC 4&5; (corresponding to WASH objectives CO2.1, CO2.2) 3) Identify and implement long term solutions for sustainable community-based WASH, FSL services through piloting innovative solutions and engagement with communities and local markets, and increased flood resilience of infrastructure (corresponding to WASH objectives CO2.1, CO2.2, CO3) -Assessment of and support for local markets to supply FSL items and materials for activities. -Distribution of emergency one-off Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) - Distribution of Cash for food assistance to household with Pregnant and Lactating Women and under 5 children-Provision of agricultural inputs and technical trainings to farmers to restore local food production. -Support to kitchen gardening and fishing activities, including distributing seeds, gardening tools and capacity building on theoretical and practical climate-smart farming practices through trainings. -Provision of fishing kits, trainings and transportation means to fishermen groups While specific high priority counties have been identified by SI as key locations for intervention, SI will have the flexibility to respond to emergencies in other locations through the ERRM team.
