Project Summary

The humanitarian crisis in Northeastern Nigerian States of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) is driven by hostilities between government security forces and non-state armed insurgency groups over the past 10 years. Civilians bear the brunt of the conflict that has resulted in widespread displacement, destruction of infrastructure and collapse of basic services including nutrition. With an estimated total population of 13.04 million, in three NE states – the state hardest-hit by the conflict - remains the epicenter of the humanitarian crisis. Conflict and the consequent displacement have resulted in disrupted livelihoods, food insecurity and population overcrowding, which in turn increase the risk of malnutrition and mortality in affected populations.
The poor nutrition situation is compounded by weak healthcare, limited access to safe drinking water and sanitation services, and poor infant and young child feeding practices. According to the NE Nigeria Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance (NENFSS), October 2019, the levels of global acute malnutrition (GAM) have deteriorated from an average of 7% in 2017 to 11% across the three BAY states. The 2020 HRP, estimates approximately 1 million children and women in need of immediate nutrition services.
On 27th February 2020 the Federal Ministry of Health, Nigeria announced the first COVID-19 case in Lagos State. In less than 3 months, Nigeria has reached more than 981 cases across the country with 31 deaths. On 30th March, the Nigeria President declared COVID-19 as a dangerous infectious disease and put in place emergency measures to control its spread. Borno has 9 confirmed cases, with reported community transmission. While there is currently no data showing the impact of COVID-19 in malnourished patients, since these patients are immuno-compromised, they may be more likely to become critically ill with complications and die in higher numbers from the disease than less vulnerable patients. Current data shows that patients with non-communicable diseases, as well as older patients, are at higher risk of both severe disease and death from COVID-19. Patients who are immune-suppressed such as those with HIV, and those with a TB infection can also be assumed to be vulnerable
Due to this, malnourished patients should be treated as a vulnerable cohort and should have minimal contact with health services at this time to reduce their chance of exposure to COVID-19. Malnourished patients and their caregivers with suspected or confirmed symptoms of COVID-19 that do not result in medical complications and loss of appetite should be managed at home. This will require adaptations to the way of delivering services to beneficiaries, including changing program mode of delivery and changing the timing of the activities, such as daily OTP to bi-weekly OTP or even monthly.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian crisis in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY) states, in northeast Nigerian, is worsening after more than a decade of armed conflict and its devastating effects on the life and livelihood of some 7.9 million of people. There are over 900,000 cases of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) and 288,299 Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). The threat of the COVID-19 pandemic in the BAY states is high considering that around 35 per cent of health facilities have been destroyed or partially damaged due to the conflict. With 1.6 million IDPs living in congested camps, decongestion of camps is an immediate and urgent priority to accommodate 424,515 individuals who are having a surface area less than 19 sqm per person for living space. The minimum recommended sphere standard is 45 sqm per person, making the current living conditions high risk areas for transmission of COVID-19. While the COVID-19 pandemic’s long- and short-term consequences on affected populations in BAY states remains to be fully understood, some immediate effects are certain to take hold on humanitarian aid flows due to restrictions. Significant disruptions of food, nutrition, health, water and sanitation supply will impact people’s well-being. The nutrition sector is likely to be impacted by the COVID-19 pandemic and likely to see an increased SAM burden in already fragile high risk populations; children under five are likely to be at risk associated of contracting the virus due to the already compromised immunity which will lead to high morbidity and mortality. The nutrition sector developed a business continuity plan which prioritizes life saving PC1 and PC2 activities, which will focus on maintaining the 24 hours stabilization centers (SCs) and adapting scenario planning on how the outpatient therapeutic programme (OTP) will continue to function to reduce the exposure of both staffs and the beneficiaries. While there is currently no data showing the impact of COVID-19 on malnourished patients, since these patients are immuno-compromised, they may be more likely to become critically ill with complications and die in higher numbers from the disease than less vulnerable patients. The current capacity of MoH to manage and treat suspected/confirmed COVID-19 cases is also limited. Although the MoH has instructed all confirmed/suspected cases to be referred to MoH treatment centers, it is expected that these treatment centers will soon get inundated with patients as numbers increase, creating a backlog of patients that will need to be isolated within SCs waiting referral. Additionally, these MOH run treatment centers would be designed to provide treatment to the general population; however, malnourished children with complicated SAM will need appropriate treatment and care for their underlying SAM complication, as well as COVID treatment/care in order to survive. Current data show that patients with non-communicable diseases, as well as older patients, are at higher risk of both severe disease and death from COVID-19. Patients who are immune-suppressed such as those with HIV, and those with a TB infection can also be assumed to be vulnerable Due to this, malnourished patients should be treated as a vulnerable cohort and should have minimal contact with health services at this time to reduce their chance of exposure to COVID-19. Malnourished patients and their caregivers with suspected or confirmed symptoms of COVID-19 that do not result in medical complications and loss of appetite should be managed at home. This will require adaptations to the way of delivering services to beneficiaries including establishing an isolation ward at all SCs, setting up accessible triage areas in outreach clinics for SAM cases, recruiting additional emergency staffs and modifying the current OTP working hours from daily to bi-weekly or monthly OTP services. The adaptation will also enhance strengthening of Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE) through CHVs',MTMSG.

2 - Activities

The programme criticality assessment identifies the most critical UN programme output and task to be maintained during the disruptive impact of COVID-19 in the BAY states for the next 9 months. It is linked to humanitarian strategic response plan 2019-2021 with focus on two strategic objectives SO1 and SO2 which focus on saving lives by providing timely and integrated multi-sector nutrition assistance and mainstream protection interventions to the most vulnerable. The IRC proposes to:• Maintain two 24 hours SCs in two LGAs and put up mitigation measures to reduce the risk of COVID-19 to both beneficiaries and health workers through IPC measures including providing personal protective equipment (PPE) to staffs and beneficiaries accessing the stabilization centres. The IRC, with HPC funds, will also create isolation units in all the SC to minimize transmission of COVID-19.The isolation units will have dedicated doctors and nurses available 24 hours per day to ensure coverage, as well as additional dedicated cleaners on staff.• Set up a triage area in outreach clinics for malnourished children referred from the community/OTP sites to separate those with suspect COVID symptoms. Accessible triage will be done at the SC as well, where COVID-19 suspected/confirmed cases will be separated in isolation units while non cases will continue treatment in the non-COVID SC unit. The triage will operate 24 hrs/day and will be equipped with handwashing facilities. The triage and isolation units will be designed based on IRC triage and isolation unit design guidelines (see attached) The IRC will also display COVID-19 related IEC materials at all sites.• Procure additional hospital beds for the triage to accommodate more patients and ensure the centre is well equipped with basic IPC supplies for cleaning/disinfection, hand hygiene materials, and PPE such as masks, face shields, gowns, aprons, hand gloves, boots and hand sanitizers for both staffs and beneficiaries • Recruit additional emergency staffs (medical doctors and nurses) for the isolation areas to adopt the recommendation of two dedicated teams, one for non-suspected COVID -19 patients and one for the isolation ward. In the isolation ward, maintain ratio of one nurse to 8 to 12 patients, and task nurses with activities previously held by “help mothers” including measurement and milk preparation and distribution, to minimize number of staff accessing the SCs. • Hire additional 24 hours ambulance services for the two SCs and outreach sites to ensure timely referral of SAM cases with suspected COVID-19 symptoms to government treatment centers • Provide IPC training and PPE to SC and ambulatory staff. • Procure additional pharmaceutical and non–pharmaceutical medical supplies and equipment as per WHO priority medical devices (see attached) for the isolation units. This includes essential drugs, medical equipment such as oxygen concentrators, stethoscopes for clinical staffs, infra-red thermometers, air extractor, generator, ventilators, patient monitors and other essential supplies for the treatment and prevention of COVID-19 The IRC will also procure laboratory and diagnostic service equipment (haemacue machines, full haemogram machines and laboratory reagents) for the Mashamari SC.• Provide mobile phones and mobile phone credit to staff for coordination of referrals and follow-up of cases. • Construct isolation unit at Mashamari SC and upgrade the WASH facilities at Mashamari SC. • In addition, mothers with SAM children in SCs and OTPs will be trained on use of mid-upper arm circumference (MUAC) tape at home to minimize community health volunteer (CHV) home visits and improve early referrals of SAM children to the nearest OTP and SC. All mothers trained will be issued with MUAC tapes and follow will be done by CHV to ensure accuracy of MUAC measurements

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will integrate client responsive feedback mechanisms designed to be accessible to all groups, including women, girls, and people with disabilities. These will include regular focus group discussions (FGDs) and listening sessions that include sessions specifically with women, girls, and persons with disabilities, the elderly, and other vulnerable groups. It will additionally make use of the existing suggestion boxes and community discussion forums to collect beneficiary feedback on quality emergency response actions. The IRC also has a toll-free hotline with dedicated hotline handlers who record and refer complaints to the appropriate staff. The number for the hotline is advertised on IRC banners at program implementation sites. The IRC’s hotline, COVID response WhatsApp group will ensure that community voices are inclusive of the implementation process and furthermore serve as reporting mechanisms in case of maltreatment or abuse. Client exit interviews will be conducted via telephone to receive feedback directly from beneficiaries attending clinics, which will allow the IRC to make rapid and informed adjustments to the project. Safety audits will also be conducted each month with women and girls in the IDP camp to understand the nutrition protection related risks. Advocacy strategies will be developed together with other partners, community structures, and female beneficiaries, to respond to identified needs.Phone interviews, text messaging or other virtual communication channel will be done as a form of remote monitoring and client feedback to ensure protection of staff and clients. If possible, feedback will be collected from community leaders/representatives when direct services users are not reachable or accessible.
