Project Summary

• Since August 2017, ADRA has been providing internally displaced people, returnees and the host communities in LGA Konduga, Borno, and LGA Hong, Adamawa, with food and multi sectorial cash with 75% focused on food assistance.
• Despite the humanitarian intervention, the people in Konduga LGA in the Borno State remain precariously in need for assistance especially in food sector reflected in the ADRA food security assessments published in April 2018. https://reliefweb.int/sites/reliefweb.int/files/resources
• ADRA therefore plans to expand the intervention in the state of Borno with the following goals:
 Ensure availability and access to food assistance for 1000 vulnerable households through e-cash transfers.
 Ensure that 250 households have developed strategies for an early reconstruction of their livelihoods with e-vouchers and enhanced training in micro-gardening and back yard gardening enterprises that link improvement to food security sector and improved nutrition.
 Will ensure that 1800 HHs (about 10,800 beneficiaries) will be trained and equipped with protection information, GBV prevention mechanisms, PSEA and SRHR knowledge attain increased implementation of protection measures focusing on gender-based violence in their context.
 This will also cover the hygiene and sanitation, elementary nutrition and dietary improvement through balanced diet training, breast-feeding and child-care plus environment management through livelihoods activities that promote improved agricultural practices.
* The targeted population shall consist of 58% women, 10% men, 20% girls and 12% boys. This will also comprise of 65% IDP, 25% Returnees and 15% Host community members.
The identification of the beneficiaries for Cash for food will depend on the poor food consumption score, women headed household, Children headed HHs, Elderly, People with disability, Households run by vulnerable men and Households hosting IDPs.
For Livelihood component, the selection will be focused on households with minimum agricultural knowledge,vulnerable and unsecured household among host communities and returnees, households with many family members, high malnutrition household with one or more relatives and households with minimum assets. The selection of such beneficiaries will entirely be made through collaboration with local leaders, food security sector guidance and other stakeholders. The focus will be on women, young people with vulnerable households and other interest groups.
ADRA complies with the humanitarian standards in all its interventions and has put in place guiding policies like human resources policies, GBV and protection, Anti-fraud policy and all ADRA staff sign Code of Conduct at recruitment time.

Needs

The project will address the needs identified in the target area as follows:  Numbers of households who depend on farming as their primary source of income and livelihood depreciated from 73%, prior to the conflict, to 56% presently due to limited access to farmlands because of military restriction and fear of attack by insurgents ❖ Present average household spending on food during the time of the assessment is 10,000 NGN (27.7USD) ❖ Only 12% of the people have received food assistance from different humanitarian agency ❖ Food Consumption Scores recorded 75% of households within the poor food consumption category in Jakana ❖ All assessed communities have functional markets with an average of 57 retailers doing business in the community and the respondents believe that the market has capacity to respond to increased demand ❖ Lack of capital is the major challenge affecting the functionality of the market despite other challenges like insecurity, transportation, etc. ❖ There have been significant changes upwards in the prices of food and non-food items in the market compared to the same period last year but this is not a major factor affecting market functionality ❖ 54% of the respondents admitted that there were no formal complaints mechanisms for them to register their dissatisfaction with Humanitarian services in their communities ❖ Domestic violence is the most re-occurring gender-based violence in the assessed communities, which is mostly associated with idleness of the population in camps and meager resources Humanitarian intervention was still predominately “response and assistance” rather than mitigation and/or early warning and recovery ❖ The report indicated a need for humanitarian agencies to deliver timely assistance and also design the most appropriate assistance for the people in need including early recovery and livelihoods programs among others in areas that have relative security and stability ❖ By visual observation, the malnutrition rate among the children between 0-5 years is high and needs immediate attention ❖The humanitarian situation in Jakana is worsened by the arrival of new IDPs almost on a daily basis there by stretching further the needed community with food security, shelter, health services, education needs and other social services

Activities

The project activities that will address the identified needs are as follow:Activity 1: 1.1 Recruitment of staff1.2 Carry out Baseline survey1.3 Sensitization and mobilization of communities1.4 Identification of beneficiaries1.5 Identification and selection of Financial Service Provider in coordination with the sector1.6 Set up of multifunctional support for electronic cash transfer for 1000 HH beneficiaries for food assistance1.7 Installation of ICT and data protection systems1.8 Training beneficiaries, community leader and stakeholders on the new transfer modalities1.9 Process inspection and ICT methodology testing1.10 E-smart card distribution to registered beneficiaries and training on usage1.11 Cash Transfer through merchant payouts based on electronic transmission (Offline-NFC) based on 70% MEB1.12 Carry out PDMs and household visits1.13 Carry out monthly price monitoring and market assessmentsActivity 2: 2.1 Identification and registration of 250HHs with e-vouchers2.2 Training of beneficiaries on utilization of e-vouchers2.3 Training of beneficiaries on micro-gardening and small scale animal husbandry in collaboration with the LG and community leaders2.4 Training of TOTs on Micro-gardening and small scale farming (Vegetable farming, Good Agriculture Practices, Use of fertilizers, vital family and hygiene health care2.5 Distribution of livelihood input E-vouchers2.6 Carry out PDMs, Household visits and M&E, the project management will carry out weekly, monthly and quarterly monitoring of activities. The results will be shared with the sectors and other stakeholders for consolidation. The programme monitoring unit will give technical M&E support to the project implementation based on outcome-output-activity plan. Joint monitoring will be encouraged throughout the implementation Activity 3:3.1 Establishment of complaints and feedback mechanism including a referral system3.2 Organizing community consultation 3.3 Organize PSEA events to raise awareness on protection challenges and GBV issues3.4 Carry out protection GBV surveys among the beneficiaries in collaboration with the protection stakeholders3.5 Training for 1,800 HHs in protection and GBV aspects3.6 Training for 1,800 HHs in Hygiene, Sanitation and health care (Boiling water, environmental cleanliness, safe food storage, garbage disposal and Nutrition and balance diet
