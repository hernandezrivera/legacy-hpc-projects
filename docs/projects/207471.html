Project Summary

CARE SMSD aims to improve the living conditions of the refugees and host communities living in camps 13 and 16 through strengthening community participation and ownership, reinforcing accountability mechanisms, reducing safety risks, and enhancing coordination, while ensuring access to adequate humanitarian assistance and protection. The project will prioritize the most vulnerable households including women-headed households and people with disabilities. Disaster management and mitigation initiatives will be critical across both camps, especially during major fire incidents, heavy cyclone, and monsoon seasons.
As a Site Management Support (SMS) agency, CARE intends to support the SMSD Sector, the RRRC and the Camp in Charge (CiC) in the conduct of Site Management Support activities at site level. CARE will reach participants across camps 13 and 16 to improve coordination and information sharing between camp actors to address critical gaps and needs; strengthen community resilience; and reinforce participation of and accountability towards the community.
CARE's integrated programming approach has enabled Site Management Support, Site Development and gender and protection teams to work jointly to provide holistic assistance to both refugees and host communities. Representing a protection mainstreaming vision, paired with a gender lens, CARE, through a participatory approach, will continue to meaningfully engage communities in this project, with focus on women and girls, to promote social cohesion and women empowerment.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

CARE's Site Management and Site Development (SMSD) proposal is founded on the multi-sectoral need assessment. The MSNA identified that approximately 52% of households are aware of feedback or complaint mechanisms enabling them to communicate with aid providers regarding community needs, assistance, and aid-related issues. However, a significant 44% are unaware of these crucial mechanisms, and an additional 4% express uncertainty about their existence. This underscores the imperative for robust communication channels and expanded outreach efforts to empower community members in voicing their concerns effectively.Moreover, when it comes to lodging complaints about living conditions or assistance received, a substantial 77% of households refrained from doing so. Conversely, only 22% attempted to voice their concerns, while 1% remained uncertain about the procedure. This highlights the need to streamline feedback mechanisms and enhance community engagement to enable residents to articulate their needs and grievances confidently.Satisfaction with the existing complaints/feedback mechanisms presents a mixed picture, with only 36% expressing contentment. Concerningly, 41% reported that their complaints had never been addressed, and an additional 23% experienced excessive delays in receiving responses. Additionally, 11% perceived that staff did not prioritize their concerns, while 2% found the process complicated. These findings emphasize the critical importance of enhancing the effectiveness of feedback mechanisms and reducing response times to better serve the community.In the realm of disaster preparedness and awareness, 88% of respondents felt that humanitarian agencies provided sufficient information on natural hazards and preventive measures. However, specific needs were identified, with 8% requiring more information/training about fires, 6% about landslides, 6% about flooding, 5% about cyclones, 3% about waterlogging, and 1% expressing uncertainty about their requirements. This underscores the necessity of tailored disaster preparedness programs to bridge these gaps effectively.Satisfaction with humanitarian agencies' responses to natural hazards was generally high, with 96% expressing satisfaction, 3% dissatisfied, and 1% declining to provide a response. This satisfaction reflects the effectiveness of current efforts while highlighting the importance of building upon these achievements.Protection referral needs among surveyed households revealed significant demands, with 84% requiring general protection referrals and 19% needing child protection referrals. Additionally, 8% sought referrals for Gender-Based Violence (GBV), 7% required referrals for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) committed by humanitarian actors, and 2% chose not to respond. These statistics underscore the necessity of providing comprehensive support and referrals for various protection needs, acknowledging the diverse challenges faced by the community.In conclusion, the needs assessment identified a range of pressing concerns and areas requiring intervention. Addressing these needs, including improving feedback mechanisms, disaster preparedness, shelter conditions, and core NFIs, is paramount for enhancing the overall well-being and resilience of the affected population. The proposed interventions aim to address these challenges comprehensively, ensuring a more secure and supportive environment for those we serve. CARE remains committed to working hand in hand with the community to create lasting positive change.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

CARE's SMSD project is committed to embedding protection mainstreaming in every aspect of its operations. The project adheres to Sphere standards, ensuring that delivery of goods and services by all sectors follows these guidelines. A key focus is on providing equitable access to services, ensuring that assistance reaches those in need without discrimination. Regular accessibility audits are a vital part of this process.Central to our approach is the implementation of the 'Do No Harm' principle, which is ingrained in the project through effective joint planning with various sectors and continuous monitoring during service delivery. This includes the establishment of dignified, gender-segregated lines during distributions and complaint desks to address community concerns. Additionally, protection principles are integrated into all stages of program delivery, ensuring that every action taken is aligned with these core values.CARE adopts an intersectoral approach to protection mainstreaming, facilitated by training in areas such as the GBV pocket guide and safe referral, in coordination with CARE’s Gender and Protection sector teams. In the site selection process, special attention is given to ensure safe accessibility, with a focus on the needs of girls, boys, women, Persons with Disabilities, and individuals with diverse sexual orientations and gender identities.The project places a strong emphasis on gender-inclusive programming. Staff training in gender-inclusive programming, gender mainstreaming, and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse is a critical component of this initiative. The established feedback and complaint mechanisms are regularly reviewed and enhanced through a gender lens to ensure they are responsive and effective. This comprehensive training and approach aim to reach and support women, girls, persons with disabilities, and gender-diverse individuals, upholding the dignity and rights of all individuals in CARE's project areas.

3.3 - Age & Disability

CARE's project targets diverse age groups, including youth, adolescents, and the elderly, avoiding overlap. CARE will initiate community-led projects tailored to the specific needs of each age group through community consultations. CARE has established relevant committees in both camps to ensure comprehensive support. This includes:• Ensuring persons with disabilities and older people have access to information on humanitarian services. This is achieved through door-to-door consultations and dedicated information sharing efforts.• Facilitating introductions of persons with disabilities to different service providers to promote neutral treatment. Advocating for equal and dignified access for persons with disabilities and the elderly in participatory processes, contributing to the design and delivery of inclusive humanitarian aid.• Conducting accessibility audits to enhance physical accessibility in project areas and promoting disability-friendly universal design principles in infrastructure development. Gender mainstreaming, under this project, involves collecting and analyzing sex-segregated data and carrying out rapid gender analyses to address specific gender concerns. The project emphasizes finding solutions to overcome gender barriers for efficient reach and implementation, through collaboration with the Gender and Protection sector.CARE will work with disability support groups and ensure service accessibility through safe referral mechanisms. The teams will collaborate with other stakeholders in service provision at the camps, upgrading facilities such as Women and Girls Safe Spaces and roads to be disability friendly. In partnership with the WASH team, the project will establish disability-friendly WASH facilities and conduct surveys for better identification of persons with disabilities.CARE is committed to ensuring that women and persons with disabilities actively participate in the project, including constituting 33% of cash-for-work participants.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

In the Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) section of the project, CARE is dedicated to establishing a robust and responsive Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM). This mechanism is designed to empower the affected community by providing a platform where they can voice their complaints or concerns. The process involves registering their issues and ensuring they receive timely feedback within a predetermined timeframe. This system is pivotal in maintaining transparency and accountability, allowing community members to feel heard and respected.Beyond the CFRM, CARE is actively engaged in fostering community involvement. This is achieved through the establishment of various committees, including youth, porter, persons with disabilities, women, and adolescent committees. These committees play a crucial role in facilitating regular meetings and activities throughout the project period. During these gatherings, community members have the opportunity to engage in meaningful dialogue, where they can express their concerns, share their ideas, and contribute to the project's development. This participatory approach not only empowers the community but also ensures that the project's initiatives are aligned with their needs and aspirations.In addition to these committee meetings, CARE is introducing an innovative approach to community engagement through sub-block level radio listening sessions. These sessions are designed to cater to small groups of 10 to 20 community members. The format involves gathering these individuals to listen to various contemporary informational programs and radio broadcasts. Following these sessions, participants are encouraged to provide their feedback. This interactive approach keeps the community informed and allows them to voice their opinions and thoughts on the content they are exposed to. It serves as an additional channel for community members to stay connected, informed, and engaged in the project's endeavors.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

CARE's Safeguarding and Child Protection Policy is aligned with international rules, regulations, guidelines, standards, and policies. CARE maintains a zero-tolerance stance towards sexual harassment, exploitation, and abuse, adhering to the minimum Standard for Child Protection in Humanitarian Action and the Inter-Agency Guideline for Case Management.CARE has instituted a comprehensive training and orientation framework. All staff, including partners and volunteers, are systematically oriented on PSEA and Child Safeguarding policies. New staff members undergo mandatory training to ensure they are fully aware of these critical policies. CARE also provides refresher courses for all staff to maintain a high level of vigilance and awareness.Adherence to the PSEA and Child Protection policy is mandatory for all staff and volunteers, who are required to sign and commit to these principles. This formalizes their understanding and agreement to uphold standards set by CARE. Integration of PSEA messages throughout the project's lifecycle is a key strategy, ensuring consistent awareness and adherence at all levels of project implementation.CARE's approach to PSEA is sensitive to local cultural norms, gender dynamics, and age-related considerations, recognizing the importance of these factors in identifying and mitigating risks associated with sexual exploitation and abuse. The organization fosters a culture of accountability, empowering staff to report any abuses or policy violations. Clear reporting channels are established, emphasizing the responsibility of staff in reporting and responding to safeguarding concerns.CARE is an active participant in PSEA networking groups, demonstrating its commitment to collaborative efforts in this area. Dedicated safeguarding focal points in the CBDO and Cox’s Bazar Regional Office oversee the implementation of safeguarding policies, providing guidance, and ensuring compliance across all activities and operations.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

This project takes a comprehensive approach, focusing on DRR and Disaster Risk Management (DRM) activities, which encompass both Emergency Preparedness and Response. One of the critical functions of SMS within this framework is acting as the first responder to incidents or emergencies in the camp, playing a pivotal role in immediate crisis management.To enhance the emergency preparedness and response capabilities in its targeted areas, the project emphasizes the capacity building of the community. This involves not only training and empowering local members but also the effective dissemination of vital information that can aid in early warning and quick response during emergencies. An integral part of this process is the mapping of areas at risk of floods and landslides, which is crucial for planning and implementing mitigation strategies.The project ensures the functionality of essential emergency response equipment, like firefighting tools, to be readily available and operational. This preparedness is key in minimizing damage and protecting lives during unforeseen events.CARE's role extends to acting as Member Secretary of the Disaster Management Committee in its project areas. This position involves coordinating with DMC members to plan and strengthen preparedness and response activities, particularly concerning multi-hazard scenarios. The project advocates for a collaborative approach, bringing together different stakeholders to enhance the efficacy of these measures.The project is dedicated to ensuring that an Emergency Preparedness and Response Plan is developed and updated regularly. This keeps the plan relevant and effective, considering the evolving nature of risks and the environment.Given that the camps are exposed to multiple hazards, a singular approach is insufficient for comprehensive risk reduction. Recognizing this, the project advocates for a holistic approach that involves integrating initiatives from various sectors and projects.
