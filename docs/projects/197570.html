Project Summary

This project will enhance life-saving humanitarian health assistance to address critical needs of trauma cases, and support people living with disabilities
Trauma and disability are considered public health concerns and remain significant challenges due to the ongoing Syrian crisis. Today, millions of people reside in areas contaminated by explosive hazards, with unexploded ordnance (UXO) continuing to claim civilian lives, causing serious injury and posing barriers to freedom of movement and access to health services and livelihoods across Syria.
WHO will continue providing technical support, capacity building, and direction to the health sector in the event of a Mass Casualty Incident (MCI) occurring in the country. In addition to strengthening trauma response capacity, WHO will also endeavor, through the physical rehabilitation working group to provide continuum of care to persons with disability, including those affected by trauma. Provision of physical rehabilitation services and prosthetic devices will be complemented by existing mental health and psychosocial support services (MHPSS) as described under a separate project submitted by WHO.
In NWS, WHO, through trauma the technical working group (TWG), will update the MCI management emergency preparedness and response plan and regularly will assessment current prehospital and emergency trauma care services at the district, subdistrict, and community level, including in mapping the services coverage, availability, HR and equipment, and trauma stockpile. Moreover, the WHO will continue to conduct hospital and per-hospital MCIM training, prepositioning trauma kits to identified PHCs, ambulances, and hospitals under the MCIM plan. Meanwhile, WHO will continue leading the disability and rehabilitation TWG ensuring well coordination and partnership with stakeholders intersectoral partners. TWG will maintain regular services mapping, need assessment, developing and implementing specific interventions to improve the quality of life for persons with disability, and exercising those plans. Under the leadership of the health cluster and technical support of the WHO and health sector partners, the Trauma and disability technical working group in NWS will conduct a workshop aiming to endorse and contextualize the WHO technical guidance for trauma and disability for NWS. this includes definition, standardization of services provision, implementation framework, integration of health information system (HIS) for trauma and disability inclusion. The Trauma and disability TWG will use developed assessment tools to conduct regular services mapping, need assessment, developing and implementing specific interventions, and endorsement of HIS for monitoring and evaluating the implementation progress and ensuring improve the quality of life for persons with disability, and exercising those plans. On the other hand, MCIM's emergency preparedness and response plan, and regularly will assessment current prehospital and emergency trauma care services at the district, subdistrict, and community level including mapping the services coverage, availability, HR and equipment, and monitoring trauma kit stockpile.

3 - Needs

More than 15.3 million people are estimated to be in need of life-saving and life-sustaining health services, including trauma and physical rehabilitation services, with 25% estimated to be living with disability (HNO 2022). Due to the heavily disrupted health system in Syria the provision of trauma services, as well as the provision of disability and rehabilitation services declined. In addition, the protracted conflict has severely disrupted referral services across Syria. Both the public and private health sectors have been adversely affected and have limited capacity to treat referred patients and emergency cases.Identified needs are:-Poor access to health services -Injuries as a direct result of bombing, fighting and mines/UXO. -Lack of proper and secured referral system for treatments of injuries and other emergencies and surgeries (including NES).-Disruption in medicines and medical equipment supply chains for trauma, -Urgent need to ensure safe and sufficient blood transfusion services at health facilities level in Ar-Raqqa, Al-Hassakeh, and Deir-ez-Zor.-Support for trauma interventions cover the needs for life-saving medicines, surgical supplies, trauma and burn kits and equipment, blood safety kits, manufacturing equipment and accessories for prosthetic devices, assistive devices, physical rehabilitation services and the need for strengthening the referral system by providing fully equipped ambulances, in addition to rehabilitating emergency departments.

4 - Implementation of activities

Key activities under this project include:• Procure essential life-saving medicines, medical supplies, equipment and trauma kits and anesthesia medicine.• Strengthening the capacity of mas causalities for trauma referral hospitals in NWS.• Rehabilitate the infrastructure of departments and emergency units in health facilities providing Trauma care and burn services.• Procure and distribute Blood safety testing kits to the blood bank centers• Procure First aid kits to health partners.• Procurement of advanced ambulance vehicles.• Conduct training courses on trauma care management and physical rehabilitation.• Conduct assessments related to hospitals' needs of medical supplies, the hospitals' preparedness/capacity to provide trauma care and the existing referral pathway • Procuring and distributing prosthetics and orthotics devices, supplies and equipment to centers• MOU with NGOs to provide surgical interventions and physical rehabilitation services• Establishing a software, conducting the needed training and provide the necessary equipment for establishment of national register for PWDs• Support coordination meeting with partners involved in trauma and physical rehabilitation provision.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1- Attacks on health facilities, ambulances and first responders2- Lack of safe referral pathway for the wounded patients especially in NES to level 1 or 2 hospitals which can provide proper medical interventions. Taking into consideration the volatile and fragile situation and the widespread lands of mines provoking higher vulnerability to trauma injuries in northern eastern part.3- staff burn out. 4--Staff exposure to infection Mitigation measures:1- Reporting on and advocating against attacks on health facilities and personnel.2- Increase the capacity building activities in areas of high severity scale (>3 such as NES and south of Syria) on specialized trauma care management protocols including response to mass casualty, chemical hazards and burn management . Maintain capacity building activities for health staff including training of trainers.3- Expand partnerships with NGOs in order to strengthen referral services and provide surgical and emergency interventions to IDPs and host communities living in high severity scale areas.4- Promoting Work/Life Balance, to Monitor Workloads & Scheduling, to monitor Workloads & Scheduling.5- Ensure that PPEs supplies are provided through the usual supply line within Syria so that HCWs are safe and have access to adequate quality PPEs


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Yes.FPs in WCO are selected and designated to address PSEA activities, guidelines and reporting mechanisms are shared with implanting partners in addition to implementing PSEA awareness sessions to health partners. Mechanisms to facilitate feedback from beneficiaries are in place in WCO, providing opportunities to identify the extent to the delivered services are supporting their health and protection needs, and for people to be able to give feedback to the organization so that they feel confident their concerns will be taken seriously and in confidential way. WHO makes available immediate and confidential mechanisms to report SEA for WHO staff members and WHO collaborators or any other person who may have been a victim of SEA or may have witnessed or otherwise been informed of a case of SEA involving WHO.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1- Lack of reliable data regarding people with physical disabilities including amputees which may affect planning for a proper response according to the real needs.2- Lack of proper and reliable data related to the current health country profile which reflect the real needs and help guiding the level and type of interventions needed (technical support, provision of materials, raising awareness… etc.) especially in the fields of physical rehabilitation, trauma care management and reproductive health. 3- Constant IDPs movements and continuous demographical changes affect the sustainable of a proper response especially for patients who need continuous supervision and non-interrupted treatment.4- Lack of flexibility by the concerned authorities for conducting large-scale assessments and field visits to the newly accessible areas and to the northern part of the country. Mitigation measures:1- Advocating with health authorities the importance of conducting large scale assessment for better planning and response as mobilization of resources.2- Improve the exchange of information and coordination among health sector partners for better responding to the identified needs.3- Support MoH in improving data collection mechanism ad establish national registry for people with disabilities.4- Expand partnerships with NGOs in order to strengthen referral services and provide physical rehabilitation services to IDPs and host communities living in high severity scale areas.
