Project Summary

ARC is implementing nutrition interventions in Aweil West and Kajo-Keji Counties; they include Community Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) through Outpatient Therapeutic Programme (OTP) and Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programme (TSFP), and Maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN).

Assessments have shown high prevalence of acute malnutrition and/or poor food security in the respective targeted locations. In Aweil West County, the SMART survey carried out in November 2017, reported a Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) of 15.2%, which is above the WHO emergency thresholds for acute malnutrition. Furthermore, the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) analysis report released in September 2018, further predicted an estimated population of 54.6% in NBeG State would face acute food insecurity between January and March 2019. On the other hand, though the September’s IPC report and program data in Kajo-Keji County reported low prevalence of malnutrition within the acceptable thresholds for acute malnutrition, the situation is unpredictable due to poor food security combined with other aggravating factors such as lack of adequate humanitarian assistance due to accessibility challenges, water and poor sanitation practices common during displacements. This is consistent with the findings of the IPC report where 32% of the population in Central Equatoria State is projected to experience acute food insecurity between January and March 2019. From the foregoing, there is need to strengthen and improve access to the existing nutrition interventions to prevent further deterioration of the nutritional status of children under five years and women of reproductive age (15-46 years).
This project therefore seeks to improve the nutritional wellbeing of the most vulnerable members of the community mainly children under five years as well as pregnant and lactating women aimed at contributing to the reduction of morbidity and mortality as well as improved maternal and child wellbeing. This will be achieved through delivery of timely quality lifesaving management of acute malnutrition and increased access to MIYCN interventions to prevent under-nutrition among the targeted beneficiaries as well as integrated nutrition, health, WASH, FSL and protection programming. The project aims to reach 5,647 beneficiaries i.e. 961 children (471 males & 490 females) with Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) without medical complications, 2,658 children (1,302 males & 1,356 females) with MAM and 2,028 PLW.
ARC therefore plans to implement the following activities:
• Operate OTP to treat children aged 6 to 59 months with SAM without medical complications. SAM cases with medical complications will be referred to Stabilization centres for inpatient care.
• Operate TSFP to treat children aged 6 to 59 months with Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) and to ensure continuum of care for children discharged from OTP as well as treat PLW with acute malnutrition.
• Promote and support optimal MIYCN practices through individual and group counselling in the nutrition sites and in the community including mother-to-mother support groups. The messages will also include key messages on appropriate hygiene and sanitation practices and primary health care.
• Coordinate with other sectors/partners such as WASH, GBV, Early Childhood Development, Protection, Food Security and Livelihoods, and Health at the County level to enhance integration of services with the goal of preventing acute malnutrition.
• Build the capacity of the nutrition workers on CMAM and MIYCN through interactive classroom trainings and supportive supervisions to identify capacity gaps that will inform on areas to focus during on-the-job trainings and mentorship, which will subsequently result to quality service delivery to the targeted beneficiaries.

Needs

Malnutrition in South Sudan remains a public health problem resulting from unsustainable livelihoods, limited access to health and nutrition services and inadequate social support mechanisms for maternal and childcare, among other factors highly attributed to many years of civil conflict. Northern Bahr el Ghazal (NBeG) State has had relatively better political stability as compared to other parts of the Country; however, it has been significantly affected by consequences of protracted conflict in the Country. This has led to severe economic crisis and subsequently high inflation affecting household food security, as well as erratic weather resulting in poor harvests leading to high prevalence of acute malnutrition. This is consistent with the results of the SMART survey conducted in Aweil West in November 2017, which revealed a Global Acute Malnutrition rate of 15.2% and a SAM rate of 2.7%, which are both above the critical thresholds. The Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) analysis report released in September 2018, predicts the lean period will start earlier than usual in 2019 and will result in an estimated 510,000 people in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and 285,000 people in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) or worse acute food insecurity in NBeG State. Kajo-Keji County in Central Equatoria State is among the worst affected locations due to the ongoing political instability and fighting in the Country that has led to displacement of the population to refugee camps in Uganda and other locations. Insecurity in Kajo-Keji has aggravated the food insecurity situation as a result of disrupted normal livelihood activities creating an economic crisis with a high cost of living, limited markets that limits food accessibility, and continued population displacement caused by recurrent armed clashes. Furthermore, insecurity has limited accessibility to deliver humanitarian assistance to the affected population in the area. ARC has been providing health and nutrition services to the internally displaced persons (IDPs) living along the Uganda-South Sudan border in Kajo-Keji County. Although the nutrition program data indicates a low prevalence of malnutrition, the situation is unpredictable due to poor food security combined with other aggravating factors such as lack of adequate water and poor sanitation practices common during displacements. Moreover, the IPC analysis report released in September 2018 has predicted that the lean period will start earlier than usual in 2019 and will result in an estimated 155,000 people in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and 10,000 people in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) or worse acute food insecurity in Central Equatoria State. The capacity of the government to respond to the high level of malnutrition is already overwhelmed and the existing infrastructure and systems are inadequate or non-existent in many of the areas. In the light of the above, treatment and prevention of acute malnutrition among children under five years and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) remains a significant need in Aweil West and Kajo-Keji Counties. ARC therefore plans to increase coverage and access of both preventive and curative interventions by: • Managing cases of SAM without medical complications among children 6 to 59 months through OTP. • Managing cases of MAM among children 6 to 59 months and PLW through TSFP. • Promoting optimal MIYCN practices through group education and individual counselling in the community (house-to-house counselling and mother-to-mother support groups) and in the health facilities/nutrition sites including during World Breastfeeding Week.• Promoting dietary diversity through the support of FAO by establishing kitchen gardens in nutrition sites for demonstration and linking up beneficiaries discharged from OTP and TSFP with FSL programmes.• Building the capacity of the nutrition workers on CMAM and MIYCN through interactive classroom training, supportive supervisions and on-the job training.

Activities

• Treat severe acute malnutrition among children aged 6 to 59 months without medical complications through outpatient therapeutic programme.• Treat children 6 to 59 months and, pregnant and lactating women with moderate acute malnutrition through targeted supplementary feeding programme.• Refer cases of severe acute malnutrition with medical complications to the Stabilization Centre for inpatient management.• Conduct nutrition screening at the health facilities/nutrition sites and in the community to enable timely identification and treatment of children under five years as well as pregnant women and lactating mothers with acute malnutrition.• Conduct MIYCN individual and group counselling targeting pregnant and lactating mothers and caregivers of children below 23 months in the health facilities/nutrition sites and in the community.• Establish and facilitate mother-to-mother support groups in the community.• Train nutrition and health workers as well as community nutrition volunteers on MIYCN and CMAM. • Test and treat malaria among children aged 6 to 59 months with severe acute malnutrition in OTP sites.• Provide WASH NFI kits to children with severe acute malnutrition in nutrition sites.• Establish kitchen garden at nutrition sites for demonstration and link up beneficiaries discharged from OTP and TSFP with food security and livelihood programmes.• Integrate child stimulation and ECD in the provision of care for children with SAM.• Conduct safety audit/assessment at nutrition sites to identify and address women and children’s protection/safety needs.• Sensitize nutrition and health staff based at the facility and community on GBV and safe referral ways for GBV survivors as well as on child protection so that they are able to identify and refer survivors for support.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

ARC will build strong relationships with the community to allow community feedback to shape and improve programming, ensuring that interventions are relevant and appropriate. ARC will therefore continue to organize consultations with the local authorities, community leaders and beneficiaries outlining the aims, objectives, activities, expected results, funding level and beneficiary selection criteria for each intervention. Other methods of communicating with communities will include open forums, key informant interviews and focus group discussions.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

ARC will ensure the messages provided to beneficiaries and the entire community are relevant to the context of the respective communities as well as upholds the local culture, norms and traditional customs. In addition, where necessary, messages will be designed to address the specific needs of the particular gender and age-groups.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

ARC condemns and prohibits any behavior on the part of an ARC employee or volunteer which constitutes any form of sexual misconduct, including sexual harassment, sexual exploitation, and sexual violence towards any other staff member, client, patient, beneficiary, or other individual participating in an ARC program or activity. All new employees and volunteers will receive an initial orientation and periodic refresher training on the policy. Subsequently all employees, volunteers, and beneficiaries will be made aware of the policy and how to report complaints of sexual exploitation and abuse. Employees and volunteers will also sign a document verifying their understanding of the policy. Moreover, ARC will investigate all allegations of SEA and violations of ARC’s SEA policy will lead to immediate termination of employment.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Apart from the local community members and leaders, ARC will coordinate with other relevant stakeholders such as the local authorities, Ministry of Health at National, State and County level, other nutrition partners within the area of operation and sectors such as WASH, GBV, Health, Protection, and FSL to enhance integration of services with the ultimate goal of preventing acute malnutrition.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

ARC values complaints and feedback because it helps to improve programming by identifying problems early, maintains trust, increases transparency, encourages respectful and ethical behavior by staff and assists communities in taking on challenges. To avoid marginalization of vulnerable community members, more than one channel will be available, focusing primarily on verbal rather than written communication and allowing confidentiality and safety. Examples include one-on-one meetings at nutrition sites and office, and discussions with a staff member during community events. Mechanisms will be designed to minimize physical barriers due to age, disability, or distance, and ensure discretion and confidentiality. In addition, focal points will be identified and oriented at all sites to lead on community complaints/accountability response mechanisms, including listening to and reporting feedback and complaints, ensuring timely response, and sensitizing communities on use and purpose of these mechanisms. Furthermore, ARC through the Project Management Committees in each nutrition site, will provide a means for beneficiaries to voice complaints and provide feedback on areas relevant to operations in a safe and dignified manner.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

ARC will conduct gender-segregated focus groups discussions with women and men aimed at inviting regular community feedback on current program progress, as well as inputs for future programming. This will also contribute to lessons learnt in the monitoring and evaluation of the program.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

ARC recognizes the need for protection risk analysis that considers the specific needs of different population so as to develop mitigation measures to either eliminate the risks altogether or to reduce the intensity of it. The protection of all persons affected and at risk must inform humanitarian decision-making and response, including engagement with States and non-State parties to conflict. ARC will contribute to an improved protection environment by aiming to achieve collective outcomes on priority issues. Through collective protection and gender risk analysis, ARC will ensure that humanitarian responses do not exacerbate risks and reinforce patterns of violations. However, this will require specialized and integrated programmes, protection mainstreaming across sectors, and promoting an age-gender-diversity needs-based and conflict-sensitive response.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Access constraints in South Sudan encompasses a wide range of dimensions (from bureaucratic to physical) and each of them must be taken into consideration to address the broader challenge of access in the Country. ARC will focus on building a general holistic access advocacy approach to mitigate a silos approach that is still too prevalent and creates competition and narrows the focus for a problem that must be addressed by engaging all actors. Key to this will be adopting an inclusive approach with all type of actors including engaging decentralized actors. ARC and partners will be vigilant in identifying and where possible negotiating with the leadership of the day as pertains the bureaucratic impediments and will share such information for advocacy purposes. ARC will use available channels of communication to escalate to Juba level such issues for advocacy. Access barriers related to limited services will require mobilization of resources to expand the services to areas not reached with services. ARC will share such information accordingly through the cluster for advocacy and prioritization during funds allocation.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

The project activities seek to address inequalities or protection threats. Protection threats continue to intensify in scale and scope, and the population is exposed to repeated deliberate attacks on civilians and other human rights violations. Through the various activities under this project, ARC will be addressing issues of violence, coercion, exploitation, deprivation or neglect.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project activities seek to address inequalities or protection threats. Women and children face specific protection risks, including gender-based violence, forced recruitment, family separation, forced and early marriage. Through this project, ARC will advance gender equality between men and women by challenging socio-cultural norms, practices and beliefs that promote GBV through community sensitization efforts.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The target beneficiaries will be informed and involved in the process of project monitoring and evaluation. Also, beneficiaries will be made aware about the Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) system which ARC will analyse from time to time to measure beneficiaries’ access barriers to services. Through period program activity reports, ARC will documents the bureaucratic and physical access impediments, its magnitude, scope and impact on programming. All the information will be documented in the Complaints and Response Mechanism database held by the MEAL Coordinator at a central location in Juba. Appropriate action will be taken accordingly. However, sensitive issues will be channeled through the channels provided by ARC.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

All the information collected that contains details of beneficiaries will be kept confidential to the organization and utilized for project purposes only. This information will be kept within ARC office in lockable cupboards under the custody of the MEAL department. The MEAL Coordinator will develop tools for MEAL staff to periodically conduct Post Distribution Monitoring (PMD)/Post Service Surveys with beneficiaries to collect and analyze information on various issues including beneficiary-safety. This information will be analyzed and reported accordingly to the project management team.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

ARC will measure the dignity of beneficiaries through organized focused group discussions and interviews with beneficiaries to evaluate the nature and quality of services provided including if the services are provided in a dignified manner and identify areas for improvement. The M&E team will assist to collect and analyze information shared through the feedback mechanisms and Post Service Surveys and to develop a report which will be shared with the program teams for action. Issues that will require the attention of the senior management will be escalated accordingly.
