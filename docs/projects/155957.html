Project Summary

Bonyan designed its Food Security interventions to meets the actual food needs in Syria and comply with the strategic Objectives of both the HRP and Education Cluster guided by an analysis of the severity of needs in accordance to assessment findings and key indicators. Bonyan 2019 Food Security program will focus on time critical interventions which lay the foundations for sustainable recovery and a speedy return to longer term development are also imperative. integrating cluster 3 objectives in order to provide inclusive respond to the specific needs in each targeted locations by improving farming livelihoods, crops production and productivity, food security and food availability targeting at both geographical and household level and then providing needs-based assistance to meet the needs that vulnerable households (prioritizes vulnerable children including adolescents/youth, girls and boys living with disabilities) who cannot meet for themselves ~ 100,000 individual (50% females, 50,900 children <18 )
Obj.1: Improve the food security status of assessed food insecure people through life-saving and life sustaining food assistance targeting 12,400 HHs ~ 74,400 individuals (12,400 Males, 12,400 Females – 37,200 children <18)
Bonyan will provide 10,000 Food basket response (following GFR standards) for IDP influxes for 10,000 HHs ~ 60000 individuals (30000 male, 30000 female) in order to improve food security score. On the other hand, Bread in the middle east’s culture, Syria particularly, is an essential part of food the daily meals and because of high ratio of carbohydrates which provides body with as energy and give them the feeling of satiety, Bonyan will participate in Bread supply chain through supporting local wheat farmers, wheat millers, and bakeries to distribute bread with less prices enabling access to 2,400 HHs to bread rations ~ 14400 (7200 male, 7200 female, 7,200 children<18) The distribution will last for 90 days and will be subject to vulnerability criteria.
Obj.2: Support self-reliance of affected households by protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income generating opportunities to save and sustain lives targeting 2,600 HHs ~ 15,600 individuals (7,800 Males, 7,800 Females)
The project will provide critically needed life-saving farming inputs, including seeds, fertilizers, farming tools and introduce farmers to modern agriculture methods to protect their crops from various natural hazards. Targeting 2000 HHs of small farmers ~ 12000 individuals (6000 male, 6000 female)
Another 600 of vulnerable HHs ~ 3600 individuals (1800 male, 1800 female) will receive livestock along with feed for 4 months
Bonyan will provide training, supervision, and consultations for the beneficiary’s households by expert agronomists and veterinarians aiming to prevent crop, animal losses and increase the production.

Registration of beneficiaries in all activities will be through outreach campaign by announcing in accessible points for all groups. Selection and prioritization criteria will also be published clearly. Transparency of shortlisting of beneficiaries will be achieved by establishing a committee in each community consist of one member from LC, one community leader and team of Bonyan. This mechanism ensure accountability and promote trust between Bonyan and beneficiaries
Obj.3: Improve communities' capacity to sustain households’ livelihoods targeting 10,000 individuals (6,000 Males, 6,000 Females)
by improving linkages with value chain through the rehabilitation/ building of 3 productive infrastructure such as (Canals, irrigation systems, markets, storage facilities, bakeries, etc.)

Needs

An estimated 6.5m Syrians are food insecure and an additional 2.5m people are at risk of food insecurity. From the assessed households, an estimated 40% of Syrians spend more than 65% of their expenditure on food, 57% of food insecure households reported to have outstanding debt and 65% of food insecure people are adopting crisis or emergency negative coping strategies. The humanitarian needs in Syria are expected to remain high in 2019 and multiple actions are needed to ensure that all four pillars of food security related to access, availability, utilization and stability of food are adequately addressed through lifesaving, life sustaining, appropriate resilience, and early recovery interventions. Emergency response at the early onset of any sudden population movement as well as targeted food assistance and lifesaving emergency agricultural assistance to meet the nutritional and kilocalorie deficits of the most vulnerable population as per food security indicators is crucial across Syria. Agriculture sector is a major contributor to GDP with more than 30% of Syria’s GDP reliant on this sector, and it is also producing food for more than half of the population. Therefore, saving, restoring and protecting agricultural and related livelihoods and value chains will be critical to increase the self-reliance and strengthen resilience of Syrians 600,000 farming households are suffering from the lack of agricultural inputs (cereals, vegetables, fertilizers, irrigation kits, etc.) to support their own production, 200,000 breeders’ households are suffering from the loss of productive assets (livestock) and damaged infrastructure (land, irrigation infrastructure, facilities, etc.) which is affecting both farmers and breeders. The greatest need is the fertilizers due to the large restrictions on their circulation, followed by seeds, which were affected by several factors, such as the failure of the government foundations works concerned with the multiplication of seeds and its development researches. Then comes the pesticides and the preparations of the equipment needed to irrigate crops as sprinklers, pumps, hoses and so on. There are small differences between these areas in terms of crops, nature of soil and water resources, which require different requirements to improve the reality of agriculture. The study showed that the greatest need in the assessment areas is the fertilizers due to the large restrictions on their circulation, then followed by seeds, which were affected by several factors, such as the failure of the government foundations works concerned with the multiplication of seeds and its development researches, then comes the pesticides and the preparations of the equipment needed to irrigate crops as sprinklers, pumps, hoses and so on. Food insecurity contributes to the risk of Gender Based Violence in situations of crisis. Lack of information or access to food security related can cause household tensions leading to negative coping mechanisms including tensions and early marriage. Qualitative and quantitative data provides support to the vulnerability of female-headed households as prevalence of food insecurity from the assessed households indicate that 28.1% of the male-headed households are food insecure, compared to 32.4% of the female headed households. Furthermore, from the assessed households, 44.2% of the male-headed households adopted crisis or emergency livelihood coping strategies compared to 47.3% of the female-headed households, who are often placed in a situation where they are at risk of being exploited due to their status. Furthermore, divorcees or widows have difficulties to access assistance because of customs and traditions at times and harassment may occur at distribution points. In some areas, females’ salaries working as agricultural labor are lower than males’ earnings (CFSAM). Child labor and early dropouts of school continues to be a major coping strategy of farmers and displaced household

Implementation of activities

All the activities will be coordinated with the clusters upon implementing in order to follow the guidance and standers of FSS CLFOOD/CA1: Emergency response to crisis affected vulnerable people with short term food assistance through appropriate modalitiesCLFOOD/CA6: Provision of protection Food Rations to most vulnerable farming household Targeting 10,000 HHs (60,000 persons - 10,000 Men 10,000 Women, 20,000 boys, 20,000 girls ~ 40%IDP, ~25% farmers HHs) by distributing 10,000 baskets in the emergency cases.The process will be depending on RNA according to vulnerability criteria followed up with a direct distribution and PDM activities. These baskets will be taking into account minimum nutrition standards and local customs and will be easy to be prepared The food baskets are in line with GFR standards and include locally available and culturally acceptable foods in order to add nutrients, taste and variety to basic foods, to increase the palatability, familiarity and acceptability of prepared foods and for the preparation of cultural/traditional foods and dishes. Populations generally will not consume a monotonous diet of three commodities. CLFOOD/CA4: Provision of flour or bread directly to households or to bakeriestargeting 2400 households (14400 individual - 2400 men, 2400 women, 4800 boys, 4800 girls) for 90 days (1 pack of bread for each 3 persons) This activity will contribute in the value chain as follows a) Purchasing the wheat directly from the framers including transferring and providing storing place if neededb) Providing the millers with the wheat in order to produce flour including delivering wheat to the bakery and providing storing place if neededc) Producing a supported bread after suppling bakeries with needed materials0. CLFOOD/CA5: Distribution of agricultural inputs, such as seeds, fertilizer, pesticide and equipment; and related training.Targeting 2000 vulnerable household-level small farmers ~ 12000 individuals (2,000 Men 2,000 Women, 4,000 boys, 4,000 girls), Bonyan will provide critically needed life-saving farming inputs, including legumes, vegetable seeds, liquid fertilizers and basic farming tools. following standard agricultural inputs kits in addition to introduce farmers to modern agriculture methods and how to protect their crops from various natural hazards.Families will have to meet certain criteria such as have previous experience in agriculture and have access (ownership, renting or other agreement with the landlord) of minimum space of land and a water resources system and infrastructure to enable sowing crops. CLFOOD/CA7: Support to small-scale food production Targeting 300 households ~ 1800 individuals (300 men, 300 women, 600 girls, 600 boys) will benefits from the distributing of (horticulture, poultry-egg laying hens, market gardens) CLFOOD/CA9: Support to asset building and asset protection (small livestock and animal feed distribution) including winterization activities; and related training.Targeting 300 households ~ 1800 individuals (300 men, 300 women, 600 girls, 600 boys) will benefits from the distributing of 4 animals of small livestock and distributing feed that suffice for 4 months. The activity will be accompanied with basic veterinary services as well. For CA5 – CA7 – CA9: Bonyan will provide training, supervision, and consultations for the beneficiary’s by agronomists. The technical consultancy aims to prevent crop losses and increase the production. CLFOOD/CA12: Support rehabilitation of relevant economic/productive infrastructures through appropriate modalities. 3 infrastructures will be rehabilitated such as (Canals, irrigation systems, markets, storage facilities, bakeries, etc.). the infrastructure will be assessed by Bonyan engineering team, prepared BOQ, and rehabilitation plan, then the rehabilitation is conducted. ~ benefiting 10,000 individuals (2000 men, 2000 women, 4000 girls, 4000 boys)
