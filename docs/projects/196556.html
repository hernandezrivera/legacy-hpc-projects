Project Summary

The project aims to ensure the survival and to improve the living conditions of IDPs and vulnerable host communities in rural areas in Idlib and Aleppo, Syria
the planned activities are intended to enable internally displaced persons and host communities in the Idlib region, as well as internally displaced persons in informal camps, to survive in dignity and contribute to self-determination. By providing free bread ,and food parcels,
3 Round per year 2022 Each round is for three months

3 - Needs

food security crisis in Syria is indeed ”a protracted crisis”, and remains characterized by very complex drivers and contributing factors, from economic worsening and weakened local currency, to drought-like conditions and severe agro-climatic fluctuations, water scarcity and limited energy supply and population movements. According to WFP RBC’s remote food security monitoring, in 2022, (especially female-headed) reported facing food shortages and engaging in some sort of food coping mechanism to maximize the use of little available resources. for instance, households adopting IPC crisis or beyond food coping alike mechanisms [rCSI 19+] reached 50 per cent, although 15 per cent of households had to withdraw children from school , increasing risks for child labor and related protection concerns, , 63 per cent households reported reducing the number of usually accustomed meals households borrowing money to buy food reached as high as 58 per cent. As many as 90 per cent of households reported relying on markets as main sources of consumed foods. High food price increases, hyperinflation, and underperforming agriculture seasons resonated with reported coping mechanisms. Lastly, ongoing electricity and fuel rationing compound with declining economies affected several businesses, resulting in reduced income opportunities for Syrians. For example, in April 2022, as many as 46 per cent of households reported having experienced income loss or reduction

4 - Implementation of activities

Activity Distribution of a total of 9,675 food packagesContents: Rice, bulgur, red and brown lintel, sunflower cooking oil, margarine, baking peas, shairya, sugar, zaatar, canned tuna, halwa, tomato paste, tea.Recipients: needy households in 19 communities/informal settlements Period: Beneficiaries will receive a food parcel once a month for a period of 3 months. After this period, new beneficiaries will be selected. A total of three such 3-month cycles are planned per year. In the interest of the enormous needs, the distribution period was chosen to be short, in order to reach as many people in need as possible and to provide some relief.Activity Establishment of a traditional bread oven, production and distribution of at least 500 loaves of bread per day, 6 days a week (pilot project)Recipients: 470 IDP families daily in 2-3 small informal settlements Description:1st phase: construction of a room with a traditional oven for conventional bread-making. Providing all the materials needed for bread making (flour + fuel + yeast + water + salt + salary) for 30 days and distributing the bread packs among the residents of the informal IDP settlement.2nd phase: Reduction of the materials needed for bread production by half. The other half of the ingredients are self-funded by the committee of the informal IDP settlement for 30 days.3rd phase: conclusion of a supply contract with the committee responsible for the bread production for the selected beneficiaries
