Project Summary

based on HNO 2022 and the primary need assessment conducted by Women support association (WSA) in conjunction with Nasaem Khair organization (NKO) in Idleb, Bennsh, Ghandorah, Jarablus (Marma Elhajar,Jamel) sub-districts and Azaz .
this project is developed to:
Frist: support Wheat Value Chain (WVC) in 1200 hectares, which are expected to benefit 2400 smallholder households (12000 individuals. 3240 B, 3360 G, 3500 W, and 1900 M) in 2022 ,and 1000 hectares, which are expected to benefit 2000 smallholder households (10,000 individuals. 2700 B, 2800 G, 2920 W, and 1580 M) in 2023 , 70% of targeted populations are female head households (FHH), and 30% are IDPs
1- first group. 800 households 400 Ha will be supported with Urea (Azote) fertilizers, supplementary irrigation and harvesting cost, for the 2021-2022 season.
2-Collect seeds from first group of farmers. The expected amount to be collected is 200 MT.These seeds will be distributed for 1600 farmers in the next season
2- Second group: 1600 households (800 Ha) will receive seeds and NKP fertilizers for the 2022 .
3- Same as the second group 1600 households 800 Ha will be supported with Urea (Azote) ,irrigation and harvesting for the 2023 season.
4- third group: 400 households new (200 Ha) will be supported with Urea (Azote) fertilizers, supplementary irrigation and harvesting cost, for the 2022-2023 season.
5- Collect seeds from first group 2+3 of farmers. The expected amount to be collected is 400 MT for grinding in the summer 2023
Second: contribute towards addressing critical food insecurity aspects through enhancing access to bread in 2023.
1-WSA will conduct a detailed food security need assessment at household level to identify 2000 families ( 10,000 individuals. 2700 B, 2800 G, 2920 W, and 1580 M) (50 % host community and 50% IDPs) with focusing on most vulnerable segments of the population including( Female-HH caring for children, elderly, pregnant and lactating women, persons with disabilities, family with no source of income, as well as families with the largest number of women and girl.
2-Daily free bread distribution for period of 5 months, targeting 2000 of the most vulnerable household 1 kg/day/HH This activity will be materialized through supporting one bakery in Idlib and one in Jurablus with 60 MT flour/month and associated materials for bread production (yeast, salt), as well as covering the operational cost (fuel and labor).
the project will adhere to a Do No Harm approach (DNH), and Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) to ensure quality livelihood services without causing any harm to the existing social system. WSA proposed program is cost effective, and leverages existing operations and program resources, to maximize efficiency. As part of exit strategy, WSA will inform all relevant stakeholders of the exact project dates including closure from the onset of the project. WSA M&E department will complete an end line survey to gauge the outcome and output level results.
WSA designed the project complement with early recovery project at household level (XNP-188140-1),via supporting business assets and vocational training for 800 households (using business group approach) that will be selected from the targeted household member in the wheat value chain activities.
WSA has been approved funding for planned activities in 2022 from the SCHF (TUR-21/3559/SA2/FS-ER/NGO/20842 ) in the amount of 325,000 $ for 2022.

4 - Needs

Syria continues to suffer from multiple and complex socioeconomic difficulties, the protracted crisis, now heading into its 11th year,is exacerbating food security and livelihood needs for many families across the country, further eroding the resilience and recovery prospects. The overall estimated sector PiN for 2022 - of food insecure plus those at risk of food insecurity - has increased from 14.2 million to 14.6 million, with 12.8 million facing acute food insecurity and 1.8 million at risk of sliding into food insecurity. Furthermore,1.8 million people living in camps are deemed to be 100 per cent food insecure, with an increase of 470,000 people facing acute food insecurity in 2022 compared to 2021. This alarming and worsening food insecurity situation is concerning, especially in the backdrop of significantly low wheat production in 2021. FAO preliminary projections indicate that wheat yield dropped to 50 per cent of last year’s output and is only a quarter of pre-crises levels HNO-2022WSA and NKO conducted primary assessment in Idleb, Bennsh, Ghandorah,Jarablus sub-districts,and Azaz using KIIs methodology to figure out the poor people's livelihood priorities. The findings indicate the economic downturn has reversed on all life dimensions; commerce is disrupted, fuel prices and costs for other commodities shot up, with costs of food and other essentials inflated by 150% and beyond the reach of most populations in these locations. 5 - Implementation of activities

WSA will consult men, women, boys, girls, the elderly, and persons with disabilities to understand their needs and preferences for location, design, and methodology of assistance. This project will be directly implemented by WSA in coordination and cooperation with the local councils of the selected target districts. The community mobilizers will be in charge of beneficiary selection from both IDPs and host community, based on the following criteria: most vulnerable households, households headed by women, household with elderly, disabled, households with sick care takers. WSA and NKO will implement full market assessment and identify traders and evaluate their capacity and reputation, to sign the MOU with selected traders. many awareness sessions will implement with traders and beneficiaries to identify vouchers and how benefices redeem it. Voucher will provide with many information (ID, code, singe of .. etc) to avoid fraud. The beneficiaries will be verified and integrate IDPs with the host community through the establishment of clear mechanisms in cooperation with the local councils to distribute bread and identify the HHs and the establishment of mechanisms for monitoring and supervision by coordination with local authorities. The project will be managed by a qualified FSL technical adviser and oversee the local staff by providing technical advice and guidance. 9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Conflict between the beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries To prevent the conflict between the beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries, WSA will ask the local councils/actors and the residents of these areas to nominate a community representative to represent the people in the area. WSA, in cooperation with the Community Representative will choose the beneficiaries according to specific criteria to serve this community and will adopt a clear and transparent policy in the implementation of the project. Risk 2: Security concerns WSA team will make continuous assessment on the accessible safe routes before the movement of the bread. Appropriate time of truck movement will be assessed and chosen. Risk 3: Diversion of support by the belligerent parties. WSA field team assesses any area before commencing any type of activities. If there is any probability of aid diversion in a certain area, WSA refrains from working in it. Additionally, WSA has good networks with local communities and stakeholders, which enables WSA to know about any potential risks of such type and take the necessary precautions. Risk 4: Movement restriction to basic services due to the high increase in the number of COVID cases and the high level of risk. Mitigation Measure: adopt all COVID preventive measures. Follow the guidance for FSL cluster for distribution in COVID context. Risk 5 : Persons with disabilities, the elderly, and widows have difficulty accessing to distribution points. 10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Based on WSA needs assessment conducted within the targeted locations, WSA will priorities the most vulnerable persons to receive services during this project. However, they may face risks of exploitation by service providers or by another actor in the community and WSA will work on mitigation measures through the following: 1. Ensure participants know services were to disclose if violence occurs 2. Raise awareness on PSEA and other types of exploitation and how to report 3. Establish good relations with local authorities, clarify humanitarian principles, and stick to them. 4. ensure the compliant and feedback mechanisms are accessible for all. 5. train all staff and contractors on PSEA CoD. Also, the project is designed to enable equal and impartial access through community consultation and participation across age, gender and diversity in how to reach the most vulnerable groups, not to exclude any groups and provide the identified services with dignity and impartiality. Starting with the project's first needs assessments and during the whole project period, WSA will focus on using the "Do No Harm" approach to integrate conflict sensitivity wherever the feedback (complaint) mechanisms will be in place and all beneficiaries must be aware of them. It will be confirmed that the service prevision process does not generate any Sexual Exploitation and Abuse against beneficiaries. Apply the principle of “different needs equal opportunities.”


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

WSA is based on a scientific basis through the development of a risk matrix during project design that includes all possible risks as well as risk mitigation mechanisms, The military factions' interventions in humanitarian action are possible, so WSA always mitigate this risk through community acceptance. WSA will make continuous assessment on the accessible safe routes before the movement of the bread and other commodities. Diversion of support by the belligerent parties, if there is any probability of aid diversion in a certain area, WSA refrains from working in it. WSA has good networks with local communities and stakeholders, which enables us to know about any potential risks of such type and take the necessary precautions. The existence of many checkpoints inside Syria, which may limit the access to the target area. WSA will coordinate with local partners and local committees who will help in facilitating the movement of the items to the target areas. The abduction of humanitarian workers is a possibility and part of the risk management is the commitment to humanitarian principles, especially neutrality towards the parties to the conflict in addition to the movement clearance for field staff. As part of the Security of Information management, the field team daily basis scans the financial documents and sends them to the main office in Turkey, with all employees signing the information security policies.
