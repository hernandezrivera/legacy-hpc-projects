Project Summary

Recovery and the pursuit of durable solutions remain a daunting prospect as local communities across Somalia continue to grapple with the legacy of decades of war. The challenges confronting displaced communities in Somalia are entrenched and cumulative. While meaningful strides are being made by the government and international partners to address the displacement situation, these efforts are being continually reversed and/or undermined by generalized inadequate protection of HLP rights, including widespread evictions. A little over 200,000 individuals have been evicted across Somalia in 2019 alone (HLP Subcluster). Forced or otherwise, evictions have an enormous impact on the victims’ dignity, physical security, livelihoods, and most importantly, their search for durable solutions to displacement. In addition to the enormous cost in humanitarian investments, the consequences range from secondary displacements and total destruction of livelihood strategies and assets to the disruption of established life routines and social networks.
Widespread tenure insecurity and general inadequate legal and policy frameworks remain the primary underpinning factors. Still, development-induced evictions, inability to pay rent and dispute over ownership between competing landowners are among the reasons most cited for evictions. Eviction as a problematic
phenomenon remains a foundational imperative to addressing the issue of displacement in Somalia in a realistic, comprehensive, and sustainable way. It suffices to conclude that efforts to strengthen protection and contribute to durable solutions to displacement in Somalia will be incomplete and/or undermined without addressing the issue of eviction concurrently and systematically.
It is within this broader strategic framework that the current project, which seeks to facilitate enhanced protection of displaced communities from evictions, has been conceptualized. Project services and customized assistance packages will benefit 196,100 individuals in 21 districts across 15 regions. In terms of delivery modalities and outputs, the overarching objectives of the project will be achieved through a combination of complementarily reinforcing set of actions that are framed around the following eight core pillars.
1. Strengthening ongoing eviction monitoring systems to enhance analysis, data availability, and usability.
2. Scaling up and consolidating eviction risk mapping to inform advocacy and response.
3. Supporting proactive engagements to negotiate improved tenure arrangements and/or facilitating dignified relocations for populations of concern.
4. Mitigating protection risks exacerbated by evictions by providing different forms of tenure security support to displaced populations, including facilitating the issuance of documentation at both household and group levels. Documentation will also seek to reduce risks to HLP assets on which local livelihood initiatives are dependent.
5. Supporting extremely vulnerable victims of evictions cope with post-eviction stress (PES) through the provision of multi-sectoral cash assistance, specialized counseling, and legal assistance services.
6. Mobilizing and supporting the government’s effort to address evictions, including robust engagement with landlords, gatekeepers, and community leaders.
7. Facilitating access to information designed to assist IDPs access services, navigate complex procedures, and utilize existing remedies in areas of displacement to enhance their protection.
8. Strengthen coordination, as well as the functional and operational capacity of the HLP sub-cluster.

Project Activities

While contributing toward the realization of the principal objectives set out under this project, activities will also aim, as part of the overall conceptual and technical design, to consolidate ongoing humanitarian efforts by expanding critically essential protection services and strengthening operational synergies between mainstream Protection and other sector areas in ways that promote integration. The following activities have been prioritized: Activity 1: HLP dispute resolution: Initiatives will be geared toward facilitating the resolution of HLP and other civil disputes, and strengthening local dispute resolution capacities as part of efforts to promote social cohesion. Activity 2: Advocacy: efforts aimed at influencing decisions and policies to advance protections, and to trigger appropriate protection responses to specific problems. Activity 3: Capacity development: This broad activity will consist of three different training types – HLP, CDR and Eviction. These trainings will target a mixture of duty bearers, humanitarian service providers, and community stakeholders. Capacity development will also include technical and material support to state agencies with statutory responsibilities linked to the promotion and protection of HLP rights, as well as some of protection competencies. Activity 4: Eviction: Prevention and response: Monitoring, documentation and reporting of eviction incidentsas well as interventions to prevent the occurrence fall under this activity category. A parallel component of this project will encompass negotiating improved tenure arrangements, access to alternative lands, and/or facilitating dignified relocations. Participants of the meetings and consultations necessary to achieve this deliverable will be counted. Activity 5: Information services: Facilitating access to information necessary to navigate administrative and procedural obstacles linked to the enjoyment and exercise of HLP rights Activity 6: Legal aid: This will include the provision of specialized counselling and legal assistance services through fixed centres and mobile clinics. Access to legal aid services will be unrestricted due to the nature of the service. Activity 7: Coordination of the HLP AoR: In collaboration with UN-Habitat, NRC will take steps necessary to ensure the smooth functioning of the HLP sub-cluster across Somalia. Among a broad set of activities, this will include strategic and operational planning, organizing and conducting regular coordination meetings, documenting and reporting of HLP activities across the country, management of and technical support to various HLP working groups, conducting periodic and ad-hoc assessments, as well as organizingnational-level HLP forums. Activity 8: Tenure security and documents: Project teams will liaise with relevant state institutions to facilitate the issuance of tenure security documents intended to increase protection against forced evictions. Cash as service and modality: The cash component of this project will take on two forms, both as a service component and a delivery modality. As a service component, cash will be provided to victims of evictions to help them address post-eviction complications, including relocation. On the other hand, project teams will explore the feasibility and contextual appropriateness of using cash to deliver project deliverables such as legal aid services, tenure security support, and material support to state institutions. The project takes into Persons with Disability (PWD) as among the most vulnerable in target communities. In ensuring that the project addresses their concerns, NRC will work with disability-focused organisations to identify and target them with specific tenure security support. At the same time, project teams will place particular emphasis on identifying PWDs among populations affected by or threatened with forced eviction and provide them post-incident cash assistance and appropriate referral services.
