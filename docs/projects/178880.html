Project Summary

The impact of the crisis on the provision of trauma services has been evident, aggravated by the high demand to respond to COVID 19 over the last one an half years. It is estimated that the provision of trauma services has declined by 13% in 2020 compared with 2019. Similar trend, but sharper, was observed in the provision of disability and rehabilitation services where it declined by 53%. According to HNO 2021, 25% of population have disabilities, that is, approximately five million people which is a significantly higher figure than the global reference figure of 15%.
WHO in Syria is the leading technical organization in disability and rehabilitation sector. It plays an essential role in coordination and partnership with stakeholders such as MOH, UN agencies and NGOs in developing and implementing specific interventions to improve quality of life for persons with disabilities.
A workplan for trauma project has been designed based on the need-driven approach taking into consideration that 12.43 million people in need of health services including 3.34 m suffering from disability. The aim of this project is to enhance life-saving humanitarian health assistance to address critical needs of trauma cases, and support people living with disabilities. National health authorities (MOH,MOHE), UN agencies, national and international NGOs, SARC, and Private Sector are genuine partners project. The project aims to strengthen referral services and provide physical rehabilitation services to IDPs and host communities living in high severity scale areas. The activities of this project will be monitored through different modalities with active involvement of project partners.
It is important here to mention that there is an increase int the project cost compared to last year’s estimates. This is mainly due to sharp increase in prices made by the devaluation of the SYP and high shipping cost.

4 - Needs

The disability forms a great challenge in the ongoing Syrian crises. According to HNO 2021, 25% significantly higher figure than the global reference figure. Both the public and private health sectors have been adversely affected and have limited capacity to treat referred patients and emergency cases. The provision of trauma services declined 13% in 2020 compared with 2019. Similar trend, but sharper, was observed in the provision of disability and rehabilitation services where it declined by 53% (was 94046 in 2019 and 53106 in 2020). WHO in Syria is the leading technical organization in disability and rehabilitation sector. It plays an essential role in coordination and partnership with stakeholders such as MOH, UN agencies and NGOs in developing and implementing specific interventions to improve quality of life for persons with disabilities. Identified needs are as follow:• Poor access to health services due to lack of functioning facilities, shortage of health staff and of medicines and medical supplies, insecurity and unaffordability.• Millions of people reside in areas contaminated by explosive hazards, with unexploded ordnance continuing to claim civilian lives, causing serious injury and posing barriers to freedom of movement and access to health services and livelihoods across Syria.• Lack of proper and secured referral system for treatments of injuries and other emergencies and surgeries (including NES).• pre-positioning of emergency supply stocks for trauma and emergency surgical kits; • There is an urgent need to ensure safe and sufficient blood transfusion services at health facilities level in Ar-Raqqa, Al-Hassakeh, and Deir-ez-Zor.• Contingency planning for expected use of chemical weapons

5 - Implementation of activities

1. Procuring and distributing medicines, trauma and surgical kits, and equipment to main emergency-providing health facilities.2. build capacity of health workers on management of burn, chemical exposure and trauma management in addition to COVID identification, triage and referrals3. procuring and distributing blood safety kits.4. rehabilitate the infrastructure in health facilities/emergency units providing trauma care and burn services (the Burn unit in Al Razi hospital in Aleppo, the National hospital in Lattakia, and the Emergency department in Duma hospital in Rural Damascus).5. strengthening the provision of physical rehabilitation services through distribution of prosthetic and orthotic supplies. 6. Procuring and delivering of equipped ambulances to the Ministry of health and Syrian Arab Red crescent. As well as working with other partners to strengthen and systemize the referral system. Emergency medical situations, are refereed to Secondary and tertiary hospital. In Syria, any patient can be admitted, if needed, to Secondary and tertiary public hospitals directly without being refereed medical doctor in addition, to referral through the Directorate of Emergency and Ambulances of MoH to health facilities.8. Improving access to trauma and physical rehabilitation services through supporting health actions conducted by national NGOs. Survivors of trauma served with different type of integrated MHPSS services. Basically, through trained health and community workers, counselling and/or stress management/problem management plus programs, and consultation by doctor to manage mental, neurological and substance use. Referral could also be advised if indicated. 9. monitoring and evaluation.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1- Attacks on health facilities, ambulances and first responders2- Lack of safe referral pathway for the wounded patients especially in NES to level 1 or 2 hospitals which can provide proper medical interventions. Taking into consideration the volatile and fragile situation and the widespread lands of mines provoking higher vulnerability to trauma injuries in northern eastern part.3- staff burn out. 4--Staff exposure to infectionMitigation measures:1- Reporting on and advocating against attacks on health facilities and personnel.2- Increase the capacity building activities in areas of high severity scale (>3 such as NES and south of Syria) on specialized trauma care management protocols including response to mass casualty, chemical hazards and burn management . Maintain capacity building activities for health staff including training of trainers.3- Expand partnerships with NGOs in order to strengthen referral services and provide surgical and emergency interventions to IDPs and host communities living in high severity scale areas.4- Promoting Work/Life Balance, to Monitor Workloads & Scheduling, to monitor Workloads & Scheduling.5- Ensure that PPEs supplies are provided through the usual supply line within Syria so that HCWs are safe and have access to adequate quality PPEs

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

FPs in WCO are selected and designated to address PSEA activities, guidelines and reporting mechanisms are shared with implanting partners in addition to implementing PSEA awareness sessions to health partners. WHO makes available immediate and confidential mechanisms to report SEA for WHO staff members and WHO collaborators or any other person who may have been a victim of SEA or may have witnessed or otherwise been informed of a case of SEA involving WHO.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1- Lack of reliable data regarding people with physical disabilities including amputees which may affect planning for a proper response according to the real needs.2- Lack of proper and reliable data related to the current health country profile which reflect the real needs and help guiding the level and type of interventions needed (technical support, provision of materials, raising awareness… etc.) especially in the fields of physical rehabilitation, trauma care management and reproductive health. 3- Constant IDPs movements and continuous demographical changes affect the sustainable of a proper response especially for patients who need continuous supervision and non-interrupted treatment.4- Lack of flexibility by the concerned authorities for conducting large-scale assessments and field visits to the newly accessible areas and to the northern part of the country. Mitigation measures:1- Advocating with health authorities the importance of conducting large scale assessment for better planning and response as mobilization of resources.2- Improve the exchange of information and coordination among health sector partners for better responding to the identified needs.3- Support MoH in improving data collection mechanism ad establish national registry for people with disabilities.4- Expand partnerships with NGOs in order to strengthen referral services and provide physical rehabilitation services to IDPs and host communities living in high severity scale areas.
