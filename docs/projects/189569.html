Project Summary

The burden of disease is caused by unsafe water, sanitation, and hygiene, as such in this 2022 Response plan, CIDO intends to enable access to safe, clean drinking-water and basic sanitation and hygiene and enhance survival of the affected population to address morbidity and mortality related to WASH through integration of WASH intervention with FSL, Protection, NFI, Health and Nutrition
The response targets 50,872 (Akobo-8155, Canal Pigi- 3622, Tonj East-2800, Tonj North-7164 and Tambura – 2086) persons with much needed WASH services in line with the following WASH specific objectives for the 2022: The locations targeted falls under Priority 1, 2 and 3 as established by the cluster targeting guidance 2022. This response attracts a budget of 500,000$ to support 5 counties within a period of 1 year.
In this response, CIDO intends to address SAM children and their care takers as well as Pregnant and Lactating women through nutrition facilities with WASH services (WASH Kits) to reduce disease transmission at household level. The Nutrition facilities such as OTP sites, TSFP and SC including health facilities will be reached with basic water and sanitation package to reduce risk of HAIs transmission due to poor WASH conditions. In addition, communities showing high malnutrition prevalence combined with limited access to safe water will be targeted with permanent solutions for water supply and hygiene
Throughout the response, CIDO will as well mainstream protection and mitigate WASH Gender Based Violence to guarantee that beneficiaries of WASH services, in particular women and children will access facilities in a safe manner. In some specific contexts, CIDO will also ensure that water points are not a trigger for conflict by ensuring that water user management committees are well trained to discharge their mandate without biasness
With experience in implementation of multisector project including Health, Nutrition, WASH, NFI, Protection, and FSL. CIDO intends to provide inter sectoral aid for the most vulnerable population with WASH services. Recognizing the cross-cutting nature of WASH, CIDO will combine the described activities with other sectors by providing dignity kits and NFIs, CIDO works to improve protection status of women and girls. While improving access to water CIDO takes care of irrigation needs for farmers and water for livestock, which contributes to improvement of food production and contain elements of diseases prevention. By improving sanitation and water access in nutrition sites, health centers and schools, CIDO contributes to work in those sectors.

1 - Needs

According to the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview it is exactly Ten (10) years after independence and three years after the signing of the revitalized peace agreement, people in South Sudan continue to face deteriorating humanitarian conditions. Their situation is worsened by endemic violence, conflict, access constraints and operational interference, public health challenges such as direct and indirect effects of COVID-19 and climatic shocks resulting in the dual phenomena of extraordinary flooding and localized drought, which have a severe impact on people’s livelihoods, hampers access to education and water, sanitation and hygiene and health services. Protection concerns remain high, with people impacted by violence having limited access to justice and the rule of law. In 2022, the humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that more than two-thirds of South Sudan's population, 8.9 million people, are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 since 2021. Continued conflict and instability in the country combined with flooding have resulted in large-scale internal and cross-border displacement. At the same time, limited improvements in some areas have prompted some people to spontaneously return. In addition, the government, with the support of some humanitarian agencies - has facilitated returns to certain areas. Due to compounding shocks, both in areas of displacement and return, populations have been forced to keep displacing time and again. At least 90,000 returnees from abroad remained displaced within the country, unable to reach their homes. In 2021, there were 2 million IDPs in the country (55 per cent of whom are women and girls), as compared to 1.7 million in 2020. An additional 2.3 million South Sudanese remain refugees hosted in neighboring countries. Humanitarian access to essential services, including health care, education, water and sanitation, as well as protection and legal services, remains a challenge in an already complex context. Between January and December 2021, 591 reported humanitarian access incidents were recorded. These ranged from violence against humanitarian personnel and assets to operational interference.More people are likely to experience severe food insecurity in 2022. An estimated 8.3 million people, including refugees, are expected to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the 2022 lean season (May-July). This represents a 7 per cent increase from the 7.7 million in 2021. The counties of most concern are in the states of Jonglei, Lakes, Unity and Warrap (specifically Greater Tonj), with the most extreme case being Fangak County in Jonglei State, where the situation is comparable to Pibor County in 2021. Food consumption gaps are extreme, and people have largely exhausted their emergency coping strategies. In 2022, the precarious situation is expected to be further exacerbated by the country’s increasing humanitarian caseload, the expected increase in the duration of needs in the most affected areas and the projected further reduction in humanitarian assistance across the country. Food assistance is expected to decrease by 10 to 20 per cent from the already reduced levels in 2021. The current economic crisis is impacting people’s ability to access markets, while conflict, flooding and seasonal effects are limiting the flow of both commercial and humanitarian goods and services.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

In consultation with the beneficiaries an appropriate, safe and inclusive community based feedback and complaints handling mechanism will be established and implemented to provide platforms for beneficiary’s active involvement in project and avenues to voice any issues or concerns about the project. This will also optimize existing community structures and ensure or establish committees where they do not exist. Focus Group Discussion and individual conversations as part of getting feedback from the beneficiaries and suggestion boxes stationed in the strategic places within the community and CIDO offices that the beneciaries who are able to read write use this means to share their feedback will be explored

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

These project activities are designed to give priority to safety and dignity of all men, women boys and girls and prevent negative coping strategies and mitigate risks including issues of discrimination in regards to accessing services. Through regular coordination meetings, CIDO will work with other partners to train the frontline workers on disability mainstreaming and also report and share protection concerns for persons with specific needs.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CIDO has a functional policy on PSEA. All staffs will be obligated to sign against the policy in respect to the 6 core principles of PSEA. The respective project staff leading this project will be guided by the standard operating Procedures (SOP) of PSEA and where applicable will use the community based complaint Mechanism to address cases of PSEA. CIDO will ensure that PSEAIEC materials are used and are visible in all designated areas of service delivery. In collaboration with other partners and recognition that CIDO is an active protection partner, efforts will be consolidated to conduct capacity building of front line workers on PSEA and extend applicability of SOP to other NGOs at grassroots level

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

An integral part of this project delivery will be participatory Learning and Continuous Improvement. CIDO will organize regular program performance and quality review meetings with staff, beneficiaries and partners to assess progress towards objectives and work plans, monitor project performance and quality based on monitoring and beneficiary feedback, identified risks and risk management strategies during implementation. At the end of the project CIDO will carry out a project evaluation and document the process used to achieve project outputs and outcomes. The report will also be shared with the beneficiaries
