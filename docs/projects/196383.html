Project Summary

The Rohingya community people from Myanmar fled their country of origin, sought as refuge. Some pre-existing social reasons (i.e. discrimination, violence etc.) put the Rohingya community in a vulnerable situation. However, children, adolescent, women, elderly person and person with disabilities from any community are always more vulnerable than others and are at risk of any kind of harm and violence. The main goal of this project will be to strengthen and expand the community mobilization. To strengthen the capacity of the FDMNs, information of various services in the camp areas will be disseminated and the process will be done through the established community centers, forming several groups segregated by age, gender, vulnerability and diversity. Community centers will be established in an inclusive and person friendly form where person with disabilities, adolescent girls and victim of any form of violence can get their required services. Developing and strengthening the capacity of staff, Rohingya community volunteers and community based protection committee (CBPC) member will be conducted through training, supervision session and monthly meeting. FDMNs will have awareness raising session on anti-trafficking, emergency response (i.e. fire safety, land slide) and preparedness, gender based violence prevention, benefit of small family, positive parenting etc. To facilitate these sessions, to play the role as identifying the protection and psycho-social needs of the community volunteer will be have training on session specific facilitation skill, need assessment process, protection mainstreaming and process of community people engagement and referral. To identify the protection risk and concerns and to get immediate response, the FDMNs will perform as the key person, where CBPC will be formed by ensuring the participation of each group of community. They will be trained up on developing action plan to identify different the forms of violence and protection concern. Case management services to the victim of violence and Mental health and psycho-social (MHPSS) services (as per IASC intervention pyramid) will be provide to build the resilience and develop the positive coping mechanism. To build the project staff capacity training on- advance PFA, PSS, community based protection GBVIMS etc. will be conducted. To integration with other sector, training on protection mainstreaming, MHPSS integration, safeguarding, PSEA will be conducted to other sector staff of CB. As part of reaching the vulnerable people or hard to reach people, proactive engagement in empowerment activities such as life skill or vocational training (i.e. solar repair, tailoring and jute product making) initiative will be taken, where the participants will be the victims of any violence and person with psychological issues. To engage the children and adolescents, play therapy will be provided to build early childhood development. To support the person with disabilities, 50 assistive device will be provided after proper screening. Women led and male led focus group discussion will be conducted to identify the barriers, needs and way of mitigation to any protection concerns. For addressing the needs of diverse groups (i.e. persons with disabilities, marginalized groups, members of LGBTQ etc.), CB will ensure their participation in awareness sessions, case management service, PSS services, community volunteer selection, CBPC meetings, day observations (i.e. World mental health day, 16 days of activism, International Women day etc.) and so on as part of gender mainstreaming and disability inclusion. This specific project will also focus on the establishment of community based complain mechanism. CFRM will be collected from the CFRM mechanism and response accordingly to improve the quality of the project activity. These activities will have impact of community based protection and helps when the FDMNs will be repatriate to their country of origin.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Rohingya refugees of different age group, gender and diverity are neither fully aware of the available services at camps nor how to access them. Specially the FDMNs of block A of camp 19 and FDMNs of block G in camp 4ext. are hard to reach. Due to frequent child marriage, FDMNs are facing protection hazards. During any protection risk, accessing to service is key challenges due to the lack of information about the service facility. They do not possess the agency to participate in decision making due to age, gender and diversity (AGD)-related challenges.Assistive device requiredfor the person with disability. Lack of awareness on key lifesaving and protection issues also pose as a key binding constraint on the demand side. Also giving feedback about service or complaint mechanism is not fully functional in camp areas. To improve the quality-of-service participation in decision making, advocacy with government stakeholders and other actors required capacity of community people to identify the protection concern and mitigation of protection for all community people segregated by age, gender and diversity. To repatriate in Myanmar with maintain quality of life, advocacy require from the community people. Thus, capacity building activity/training is part of the requirements for the sustainability.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

From the beginning of the Rohingya influx in Bangladesh since 2017, Caritas Bangladesh (CB) is one of the first responders and has been working in the Rohingya refugee camps. As part of humanitarian crisis response activities, Gender based violence (GBV) program has been operating since 2018. Same as before, CB is going to implement GBV activities in line with the JRP objectives as well as maintaining the IASG, IASC, Protection, GBVSS, child protection along with MHPSS WG, GiHA WG and ADWG guidelines. This project will be implement in camp 4ext, 19 and newly in 20 ext. CB already implementing community based protection in camp 19 and 4 ext. and already have integrated protection facilities in those camps, it will be helpful to get involve in the community people. As per the needs of FDMNs, to provide the service, activities might overlap with other actors, but in practical context, communication with actors of same sectors will make to continue the smooth process of accessing protection services. CB always prioritize on the quality of services. Expert personnel or consultant will work to develop the quality and to contextualize all the protection services of CB. CB has been working for sustainable capacity development for the community people. In this regard, in protection program, CB implement the community-based protection (CBP) approach. CB is an active member of PSEA Network and have PSEA, Safeguarding focal within the organization. At camp level inter-sectoral coordination and networking & camp collaboration with CiC Office. In the mentioned camps CB has Child protection, GBV, Shelter & camp, Site Improvement and WASH programs. CB has been implementing the activities with the good support and approval from RRRC & CiC office and never found any objection so far from any Government authorities. CB operates all activities through Ukhia and Cox’s Bazar office and is able to share resources across different programs as needed.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Caritas concerns with the unique and immense risks of the safety and dignity of Rohingya persons, especially those who have elevated vulnerability like women, girls, boys, persons with disabilities, elderly persons, etc. Learning facilities are in a safe location, free from dark footpaths, risky slopes and isolated areas. Separate latrines there for girls and boys in the facilities with labeled clearly. PSEA and Safeguarding is the major concern areas of humanitarian response. Consultation with stakeholders planned to ensure access to all to the services. Community centered approach is following to ensure participation, empowering and strengthening communities. Community-based planning reduces risks and hazards and promotes resilience. AAP and responsiveness to the communities will maintained through MEAL structure and CFRM mechanism. Conducting real-time monitoring to inform project adaptation and build flexibility into this project considering the fluidity of the situation.

CB follows comprehensive Gender Policy in operation of humanitarian responses and gender is mainstreamed here. In the proposed project, it will continue services to ensure female participation in all committees and decision-making process, trained equally both female and male staff, gender segregated WASH facilities including MHM. Focusing on gender sensitive activity such as developing women forum, separate female and male leader group is part of this project. Deploy female volunteers for girls only sessions, especially for adolescent girls and young adult women and adapting services culturally responsive and following gender principals. All project staff will be trained and equipped on gender sensitive programming. The program will also be emphasized to enhance girls friendly and gender-equal environment in the learning facilities. 3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

CB follows a comprehensive Child and Vulnerable Adult Safeguarding Policy to this humanitarian context. CB has Safeguarding Committee and one dedicated Safeguarding Focal in all offices, including Cox’s Bazar office. Staff, volunteers are trained and Safeguarding, Code of Conduct, PSEA orientation organize each year for all. Staff and volunteers must sign a safeguarding, Code of Conduct and self-declaration form during joining and reference checked before offering job. Beneficiaries are oriented on safeguarding policy through providing orientation before any service delivery, distribution. Agenda on safeguarding keeps in all meeting, training and workshops. Bengali version of Safeguarding policy and one pager distributed among the staff and host volunteers. All visitors, advisors and vendors read and sign the safeguarding form before visiting the camps. All above discussion and obligation are applicable for the staff, Facilitators and volunteers under the proposed project.

3.4 - Diversity

Community people includes the children, adolescent girls and boys, men, women, old age people and persons with disabilities and other diverse groups, receive effective remedial development, protection and comprehensive services to help recover losses and improve overall welfare. Children with disabilities are systematically targeted and their inclusivity is consistently mainstreamed in the program. Individual Improvement Plan will be taken for person with disabilities for promoting inclusive services.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Systemic MEAL mechanism ensures AAP principles, CFRM and stakeholder consultation, and periodic meetings with stakeholders are designed in the project towards improving the quality of protection. CB aims at capacitating staff and volunteers to facilitate cultural programming with a perspective of sustainability and potential replication and to promote empowerment of community. Involvement of community members in needs assessment through Participatory Action Research (PAR) methods. Sharing impact findings with beneficiaries, organizes monthly Community based protection committee meeting to take decision on the ways to ensure the quality protection to the community people. Children’s including adolescent and youths of diverse population group’s feedback will be considered/addressed importantly.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes. Emergency preparedness and response activities are in place in the proposed project. CB will continue to provide services so that each facilities has DRR contingency plan and continue following plan accordingly with community people. Capacity building of Facilitators, Community Volunteers, CMC members, CBCPC, community stakeholders, and their active engagement in PERU, DRR program management is the crucial part of the proposed project to prevent and mitigate the crisis. Their knowledge and skills contribute to the decision-making process and implementation, ultimately help the community to be sustainable and resilient. Facility-based EPR plan will be developed in 2023.
