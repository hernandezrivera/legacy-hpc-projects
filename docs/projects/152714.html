Project Summary

This project is about prevention and mitigation of the risks of gender based violence in the society and direct integration of GBV Survivors into their communities through education and training on fuel efficient stoves and briquettes. Gender Based Violence (GBV) is a complex issue that requires a collective responsibility to solve the problem. There are many ways and approaches to designing, implementing and monitoring gender based violence (GBV) projects at disposal of various organizations. Titi Foundation has designed to prevent and reduce the risk of exposure to GBV through a Fuel Energy Saving Stove and briquettes project to empower 14000 women, girls, boys, elderly and disabled who may be highly exposed to undignified sexual abuse and harassment, assaults and abductions as they go doing their daily chores in the community.
The Fuel Energy Saving Stoves (FESS) and briquettes project aims at providing life-saving training skills and knowledge to selected vulnerable members of the communities (beneficiaries) on making fuel efficient stoves (FES) and briquettes. This project will help both the targeted GBV Survivors and other highly exposed vulnerable members of the communities who are at risk of being exposed to gender based violence (GBV) in regaining and maintaining their dignity in the society.
The project will target 14000 beneficiaries in Kajokeji, Yei, Lainya Counties in Central Equatoria and Magwi and Lafon Counties in Eastern Equatoria State.
The project will be fully implemented and managed by Titi Foundation staff with support from the leadership selected from the beneficiaries and local authorities in all the areas of operations.

Needs

Gender based violence is a complex problem that requires a multi sectoral approach, however there are limited availability of appropriate information for the various actors to ensure a coherent and effective response to gender based violence in South Sudan that effectively combines both female and male participants. This project is unique because it is inclusive and multi directional in nature to increase collective responsibility of the key stakeholders.Addressing GBV is a shared responsibility as its negative effects cuts across all sectors and levels. Governments, NGOs, civil societies, political leaders and community members need to strengthen collaboration and establish systems to prevent GBV from occurring, and to appropriately respond to the needs of the GBV survivors. Titi Foundation is one of the many agencies implementing GBV projects in South Sudan through Fuel efficiency Stoves and briquettes training for GBV survivors and the vulnerable members of the communities. However, under this programme, the project will target both women and men which will be implemented in the context of the newly signed Khartoum Peace Agreement signed on the 12 September 2018. It is important to put in mind that SGBV is not only an issue for women; it is also an issue for men as it is intrinsic and affects the entire households and society at large, (Fatherhood Programme, 2003-2004, South Africa). Following the peace agreement it is expected that demand for fuel for cooking which usually takes large percentage of the vulnerable groups’ income will be at higher rates, especially for the vulnerable women and men who could be prone to assaults and rapes in the forests. This is because of new relocations back home or to new homes; and the only source of major supply being the ‘forest resources’. Based on HNO 2018, needs population estimates were at 110,000 vulnerable people. The Fuel efficient Stoves (FES) and briquettes project aims at training selected vulnerable members of the communities (beneficiaries) on making fuel efficient stoves (FES) and briquettes. The project has in the past proven to reduce on the frequency, time and distance traveled during firewood collection, hence reducing incidences and exposure to SGBV among the beneficiaries. This project will help the target GBV Survivors and other vulnerable GBV beneficiaries in regaining and maintaining their dignity in the society and less time will be spent in food preparation . With a reduction in time spent, frequency of trips and distance traveled by women and girls for firewood collection, it is assumed that there will be a significant reduction of gender based violence ranging from assaults, abductions, rape, domestic violence and engaging in transactional sex in exchange for fuel or money. Additionally, the fuel-efficient stoves and briquettes are more efficient as compared to the traditional three-stone fire and the potential time saved may translate into time spent on safer and more productive activities and allow for more caring capacity.To ensure effectiveness and efficiency of the project, Titi Foundation will also establish WGFS providing psycho social support for women and girls. Other key stakeholders in the communities and local partners from other sectors will also be involved and consulted to ensure that the project is beneficiary based and is accountable to the affected populations. The model project will employ community engagement, capacity building and minimum standards for GBV programming.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Titi Foundation has several methods of passing information to communities and beneficiaries that are targeted by our interventions. These approaches include:• information passed to target groups during initial rapid assessments• information through community leaders sensitization meetings• information through beneficiary identification, registration and verifications exercises• information through beneficiary trainings and workshops relevant to the benefits of the project

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Titi Foundation messages especially for SGBV are context specific and tailored to the design of the project. Sometimes there are generalized messages which seek to inform all highly vulnerable groups highly exposed to risk of GBV. This kind of information may target all the women involved in a project. While on the other hand, context-specific information may be prepared for SGBV Survivors. These messages may be delivered by an expert on GBV and a psychologist who counsels the concerned persons.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

Titi Foundation is committed to the principles of quality of service delivery and responsibilities of the beneficiaries on a project that concerns them. This means beneficiaries are fully consulted and informed about the benefits of such projects. They are also allowed room to discuss, speak and analyse what short and long term benefits such a project may offer them. Therefore, all Titi Foundation humanitarian funded projects are guided by the humanitarian principles of ‘Do No-Harm’ and This allows Titi foundation to use its Human Resource policies guiding staff behaviour, conduct at work and towards the target populations.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Yes, all Titi Foundation projects have broad consultative base which goes beyond the local government leadership. For example, in GBV Project Cycle, before implementation, we have to carry out wide consultations with RRC, Local County, Payam and Boma Authorities (Chiefs, Elders), and Women, Men, Girls’ and Boys’ Groups (18 – 59) and complimentary NGO partners in the area. The consultations are in part to disseminate the purpose, aims, objectives and what activities the project will do. It is intended to seek approval from a broad audiences who may be directly and indirectly affected by the project.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

The GBV project has an in-built feedback or complaint mechanisms put in place right in the proposal. For example, Titi Foundation appoints GBV Group Leaders from the beneficiaries who link with the staff implementing the project indirectly. In other cases, the Project Development Officer who is a Counselor also organizes clinics to hear to GBV cases and such clinics also allow only specific affected persons to report their cases individually.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Besides, on a broader base, participatory evaluations are used through project implementation phases to collect complaints, advises and resentments which can be used for accounting to the grassroots and staff learning new ways to improve the project implementation process.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project is based on protection risk analysis which takes into account the different and wider needs of the community into consideration. Titi Foundation uses, Protection Cluster Flash Assessment Reports, Joint IRNA protection reports and also we also carry out our own Safety Audit Reports based on tools prepared by the cluster. All these take into account internal, and external risks that may cause problems for the project management.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Kajokeji right after the December, 2013 internal fighting, became a very difficult place to access for most humanitarian actors. In 2016, Titi Foundation on its own made Local Access arrangements with local authorities in SPLA-IG and SPLA-IO controlled areas by involving key figures from Kajokeji, Diocese of Kajokeji, and Uganda District Local Authorities bordering Kajokeji. This was based on a catastrophe which was looming over Kajokeji for the civilians trapped there. This method worked for us and until date this has attracted more NGOs to work in the area .

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The GBV project aims to address the issues of violence, coercion, exploitation, deprivation and neglect through education of women, men, boys and girls against Violent Against Women and Children. Including men into GBV projects for the first time in Titi Foundation project is a testimony that GBV is not only concerning women but also men and boys to care for women, girls and children. This also doesn’t portray all men and boys as perpetrators of GBV because there are few of them who actually do this. Using empowering projects like Fuel Energy Saving Stoves and briquettes to empower women and girls aim at economic empowerment and equality in community.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes. Titi Foundation provides equal access for all affected target beneficiaries to GBV services by showing all of them the availability of service delivery sites, contact addresses of service delivery people and inform them about availability of what projects for what category of people are being channeled for them. Titi Foundation also targets the most vulnerable groups such as Women Groups, Youth Groups and Disability Groups. Such a list may also allow us to measure number of highly vulnerable people accessing services.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes. Titi Foundation is conscious of security of beneficiaries in the operations we carry out. As a result we try to find out from the community leaders which sites would be best for relief distribution, training of GBV groups and how received items are used at family levels. Resources often expose women to risk of family violence. GBV projects also ensure that all key stakeholders are involved in keeping local peace through Local Committees for Peace.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

The dignity of beneficiaries is key. Offering beneficiaries to be responsible for implementing parts of the project allows them feel respected, honorable and gain ownership of the project. Dignity is about allowing people achieve their rights and make duty bearers to make sure beneficiaries receive their rights.
