Project Summary

Transitional shelter assistance will be provided to households who previously received upgrade shelter kits (USK). This will include in-kind core items, a voucher system for optional items, and technical assistance to provide resilience against adverse weather shocks. The majority of the refugee caseload will be targeted with transitional shelter assistance.
With this project HEKS/EPER plans to extend the ongoing intervention to cover entire Camp 14 & camp 15 with TSA, TSA-II and provision of NFIs through comprehensive voucher system. This intervention will be implemented in close cooperation with IOM, that will provide required shelter materials as an in-kind contribution, whereas HEKS/EPER will ensure the whole implementation of the project activities. The approach includes the distribution of essential items, a voucher system for optional items taking individual needs into consideration. Households will be technically assisted on construction and DRR, and cash for work is implemented to support households with increased vulnerabilities.

Needs

The Shelter/NFI Sector Working Paper 2020 – 2022 reconfirms the principle that all shelters should be safe, constructed from materials that are resistant to short-term deterioration and located on sites that provide protection against natural and man-made hazard. The results of the Multi-Sector Needs Assessment 2019 (MSNA 2019) showed that the refugees identify rotten bamboo, damaged shelters, leaking roofs and low-quality materials as their main issues of concern. Despite the positive impact on living conditions arising from implementation of the Upgrade Shelter Kit (USK), Transitional Shelter Assistance (TSA) and Mid-Term Shelter (MTS) programmes, the MSNA 2019 results indicate that the shelter response must keep evolving to avoid entropy.

Activities or outputs

1. Catchment Focal Point (CFP) for camp 14 and 15: HEKS will start its new status as catchment focal point for camp 14 and 15. The CFP will coordinate with all relevant agencies in the camp, CiCs, Shelter/NFI Sector and IOM as and when required. The CFP will also take the lead of the rapid damage verification and ensure appropriate support to the affected families. The support will be ensured through the IOM common pipeline system. 2. TSA I (Transitional Shelter Assistance 1st phase); 6,000 HHs in camp-15 and 1000 HHs in camp-14: HEKS will continue TSA-I with the pipeline support from IOM and will ensure the operational cost to achieve the target. HEKS has its dedicated field team including sufficient engineers, community mobilizers and volunteers to support TSA-I activities. HEKS team has already received the technical training from IOM. In the budget, HEKS will charge the operational cost only, not the materials cost. Treated bamboo will be used from IOM pipeline system. This support will be implemented as blanket coverage. However, special attention and care will be provided to the extreme vulnerable individuals. EVI will receive full shelter construction support from HEKS. EVI will be identified through a door to door assessment. All the targeted families will receive a training on TSA-I. There are some CfW opportunities in the construction and/or HH settlement works where both men and women members will be engaged. 3. TSA II (Transitional Shelter Assistance 2nd phase); 3,500 HHs in camp-15: HEKS will start TSA-II with the pipeline support from IOM and will ensure the operational cost to achieve the target. HEKS has its dedicated field team including sufficient engineers, community mobilizers and volunteers to support TSA-II activities. HEKS team has already received the technical training from IOM. In the budget, HEKS will charge the operational cost only, not the materials cost. Treated bamboo will be used from IOM pipeline system. EVI support will be ensured as per actual need in the field. This support will be implemented as blanket coverage. However, special attention and care will be provided to the extreme vulnerable individuals. EVI will receive full support from HEKS. 4. Provision of NFIs through comprehensive voucher system; 4,000 HHs in camp-15 and 4,000 HHs in camp-14: HEKS will follow the Shelter/NFI sector developed modality to distribute the NFI packages. In the budget, HEKS will charge the operational cost only, not the materials cost. Individual family members need will be assessed through an assessment conducted by HEKS team. According to the need of the family, package will be designed and distributed. Post distribution monitoring: HEKS will implement one post distribution monitoring (PDM) exercise after each TSA/NFI distribution round. TSA minimum performance standards and desired standards will be assessed during PDM. Compliant raising and handling mechanism: Complaints will be collected by a dedicated person during each distribution and during beneficiary selection. Beneficiaries can also raise concerns to the program staff or directly in the HEKS/EPER camp office. The system will be explained to the community prior to implementation. Complaints will be recorded and submitted to the HEKS MEAL and Grant Coordinator, who will handle the cases, request the field team for action or initiate an investigation.
