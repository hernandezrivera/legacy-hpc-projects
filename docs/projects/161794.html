Project Summary

This proposed project will focus on enhancing the capacity of 250 community volunteers to deliver structured: age appropriate psycho-social support, recreational activities, life skill services and livelihood support to conflict affected adolescent boys and girls and their caregivers in MMC, Jere, Konduga, Bama, Mafa and Gwoza LGAs.
GOALPrime will use the UNICEF Adolescent kits for Expression and Innovation in Emergency and the iDEAL module to build the life-skills capacity of a minimum of 5,000 adolescent boys and girls through the support of the trained community volunteers. GOALPrime shall also engage the trained community volunteers to deliver basic, quality, art based, creative and indigenous context psychosocial support engagement and will target another 5,000 conflict affected young boys and girls (under 10yrs) identified to be at risk across camps and communities in MMC Jere, Konduga, Bama, Mafa and Gwoza LGAs.
GOALPrime will work with UNHCR to provide livelihood skills training and support to 500 vulnerable caregivers who lack the capacity to care for their children. The Organization shall also train caregivers on protection principles and minimum child care standards and strategies.
GOALPrime shall integrate GBV into this project in MMC Jere, Konduga, Bama and Gwoza LGAs by using the adolescent safety and well-being for girls, showing different scenarios and response strategies for GBV related situations for adolescent boys and girls. This will serve as an opportunity to monitor GBV violations perpetrated against children especially girls, which will be reported to the protection monitoring dedicated desk in collaboration with other protection actors working in the locations.
The project also focuses on strengthening the capacity of existing Community Based Child Protection Committee (CBCPC) and create more if necessary; and build their capacities to be able to identify child protection and GBV concerns and respond accordingly and/or report such cases to the child protection monitoring desk that will be set up at the CFS in IDP camps and communities for response and preventive action.
GOALPrime shall also in collaboration with government stakeholders and the child protection sub-sector work towards strengthening the referral pathway for child Protection in MMC, Jere, Konduga, Bama and Gwoza LGAs. GOALPrime shall also establish two community based child protection monitoring and advocacy desk in each of the target LGAs. Through the community based child protection monitoring and advocacy desk that will be established in the LGAs, child protection and GBV concerns shall be identified and GOALPrime shall be intentional about addressing the identified concerns directly and through revitalized partnership with all relevant community, humanitarian and government stakeholders operational in MMC Jere, Konduga, Bama and Gwoza LGAs.
GOALPrime shall train community volunteers and child protection committees on the protection and prevention from sexual exploitations and abuse (PSEA) and they shall be intentional about sensitizing the community on SEA and its prevention. Child protection committees shall work closely with the ministry of Women Affairs Social workers to monitor and report SEA related concerns.
GOALPrime Organization Nigeria will use her existing bugs bunny cartoon character strategy to continuously sensitize children, caregivers and the community and increase their knowledge on protection and prevention from GBV, PSEA, and to mitigate child protection risks in MMC Jere, Konduga, Kala/Balge and Damboa LGAs.
GOALPrime shall establish functioning complaints and feedback mechanisms to receive feedback from the community members and the beneficiaries on the impact of this project on the children, their parents and the community.

1 - Needs

Emergencies such as we have in Northeast Nigeria has severe consequences on children and their families as well as affect their ability to build back better. According to Nigeria’s HRS 2019/2021 https://reliefweb.int/report/nigeria/nigeria-2019-2021, 7.1 million people (2.3 million girls, 1.9 million boys, 1.6 million women and 1.3 million men) are in need of humanitarian assistance in north-east Nigeria as a result of a crisis that is now in its tenth year. The projections of the Child Protection Subsector in the 2019 Nigeria HRP estimates that 2.7 million young children and adolescents (1.5 million girls and 1.2 million boys) in the BAY States are in need of protection and cross-sectoral services. Threats and risks to the well-being of girls and boys include family separation, explosive remnants of war, gender-based violence including child marriage and conflict-related sexual violence, recruitment and use of children, abduction and other grave child rights violations. According to the Child Protection Guidance Tool for the 2019 HRP, it is projected that at least 6,000 children (more than 50 per cent girls) are estimated to have experienced sexual violence by the Non-State Armed Groups. The crisis, evidently has caused severe psychological distress amongst over 2 million young children and adolescents; Borno State being the epicenter. The conflict has not only disrupted the social fabric but has also negatively affected the ability of parents and communities to appropriately care for their children. Also, according to the 2019 Humanitarian Response Plan, At least 150,000 caregivers of vulnerable children have suffered from severe psychosocial distress. The social relations disruption, the anxiety and fears that accompany the event before, during and after, and uncertainty for the future have rendered young children, adolescents and their families helpless thus preventing them from taking care of themselves and their environment. As a result, they are evidently loosing hope for the future hence limiting their active participation in the realization of national priorities. If the situation is left unaddressed, and this proposed programme is not implemented timely, the psycho-social effects of the conflict may lead to continuous suffering, indifference, depression, frustration, negative coping mechanism, violent behavior and other antisocial dispositions, thereby increasing social tension, prolonging the recovery process and weakening the resilience of children in the long-term. It is therefore top priority that we provide these conflict affected young children, adolescents as well as their caregivers’ continuous access to psychosocial support services by strengthening the capacity of Community Volunteers to deliver quality Life-skills and Art Therapy to enhance beneficiaries’ reintegration and resilience.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

GOALPrime shall establish functioning complaints and feedback mechanisms to receive feedback from the community members and the beneficiaries on the impact of this project on the children, their parents and the community. Complaints boxes shall be kept at strategic positions within the project environment for beneficiaries and their parents to drop their complaints. GOALPrime shall also hold bi-weekly focus group discussions with target adolescents’ boys and girls, caregivers and other community stakeholders to receive feedback on the project impact. GOALPrime shall provide feedback of complaints to the beneficiaries, parents and other relevant community stakeholders on weekly basis. Effective and timely actions shall be taken to address concerns received through our established feedback mechanism in close collaboration and coordination with other actors working in the area. We shall also provide complaints and feedback hotlines in the LGAs with the available communications network providers. The identity of complainants shall remain confidential except otherwise requested by the complainant or feedback provider.
