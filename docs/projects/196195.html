Project Summary

The overall goal of the War Child programme is to increase the resilience and wellbeing among vulnerable children and youth in North East and North West Syria. Under this action, War Child would respond to urgent Child Protection needs in Idleb and Aleppo, and expand the service provision for highly vulnerable children in Raqqa and Deir ez-Zor through CFS, structured PSS, awareness raising, and developing specialized service capacity within local partner organizations, so that they can deliver a quality case management service. This will target 8,750 in NW hub, and 8,750 in NE hub over the one-year period. The budget is $1,125,000 for NW and $1,125,000 for NE. This prevention and response approach will address immediate and urgent identified child protection risks while contributing to long-term change. The project aims to reach parents and caregivers as well as local leaders and communities to build a more protective environment for vulnerable children affected by conflict. The project will contribute to multi-sector initiatives (specifically in Education) to support the mainstreaming of Protection outcomes. It will also focus on local organisations to build sustainable technical CP capacity and enhance the quality of the overall response.

3 - Needs

Children comprise almost half of the affected population in Syria and continue to be exposed to multiple protection risks and grave child rights violations, including in areas where hostilities have declined. Deepening poverty have worsened harmful coping mechanisms such as child labor and child marriage. 96% of children live in communities where child labor is reported. Another 84% of children live in communities where child marriage is an issue for girls (HNO 2023). According to WCH Needs Assessment (Apr 2022), children in NWS and NES experience multiple protection and wellbeing challenges. Whilst girls and boys showed similar wellbeing levels, children with disabilities have particularly low levels of wellbeing. In both areas children reported suffering from nightmares due to war trauma, bombing or displacement. Younger girls reported nightmares due to fear of theft or being kidnapped. Both girls and boys reported that they sometimes felt inexplicable tiredness or pain. Among girls aged 14-17 and boys aged 10-13 this was reported more frequently in NES. In terms of negative coping mechanisms, child labor is increasingly evident and, according to some respondents, somewhat normalised. This affects boys disproportionately (68% of surveyed working children were boys). Caregivers reported that male adolescents are at risk of engaging with armed groups due to peer pressure or opportunities to generate income. Early marriage was rated as the greatest risk to girls by caregivers.

4 - Implementation of activities

The Action will use local engagement as an entry point for integrated community-based CP, supporting informal settlements and host communities to strengthen their resources. Based on regular service mapping and clear referral pathways, these community approaches will be linked to more specialized support. The Action will build the internal technical resources of local partners to identify and respond to Protection issues through CP case management (CM). Recognizing the severe lack of specialized CM services, WCH will assess current organizational capacity, build caseworker competencies, and provide intensive CP CM training based on the inter-agency standards and information standard procedures. Children with specific CP concerns will be assigned to dedicated caseworkers in each location, supervised by WCH technical specialists, and in coordination with the CP sub-working group. The Action will increase attention to the specific needs of adolescent girls by providing comprehensive PSS interventions and implementing structured age-/gender-appropriate PSS activities (Deals/6-18 years). M&E processes will use global tools (Kiddy Kindl, Warwick), contextualised to the area, and collaboratively with partners. Data collection methods (surveys, pre-post-tests) are tailored to indicators; data are collected digitally (Kobo). TPME will also be used to support outcome-level indicators. Reporting to the clusters will be via monthly 4Ws, CM reporting, and supporting WG initiatives.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk: Unsafe CFS spaces Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigation measures: Each of the project locations will be identified and set up using War Child CFS guidelines, which will also be used to train partners on minimum standards for assessing the risk and establishing safe spaces. The spaces will be monitored with contextualized tools, including assessment using the War Child CFS checklist. In all cases War Child will ensure the sites and their surrounding entry and egress points for families and children alike are cleared of ERW. Protection risk: No inclusion and/or participation of disadvantaged groups Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation measures: To increase equitable access to the facilities, an inclusion checklist will be applied, so that the needs of disadvantaged groups are considered into program design (IDPs, CwDs, adolescents, girls). The checklist will assess accessibility and meaningful participation in the needs assessment; outreach activities and inclusive development of activities; engagement of marginalised groups; and representation in monitoring and evaluation activities. Protection Risk: Changing dynamics and sudden displacement may not allow War Child to continue delivering case management services to the same beneficiaries due to loss of communication or inability to access the cases in their new destination Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation measure: continue following up with the cases remotely


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Protection risk: Sexual abuse, or attempt of sexual abuse of a childLikelihood: Low Impact: High Mitigation measures: (1) Train partners to review or set anew a child safeguarding (CSG) policy for their respective organisations (2) WCH program team monitors partners and general media postings on activities and alerts child protection/safety focal point at WCH whenever there are concerns (directly discussed with partners and action plan agreed upon) (3) Staff of partners on the ground have one CSG focal point who reports to WCH any breach (4) Child safety concerns identified through internal monitoring and evaluation are discussed and followed up with partners For all field staff (including facilitators and teachers) and before activities begin, WCH will provide trainings on CSG/PSEA. Trainings will be provided on WCH Safeguarding policy, to ensure awareness, response and compliance across all partners. WCH has developed child-friendly PSEA materials that can be displayed in classrooms and CFS, as well as child-friendly identification and reporting mechanisms, to ensure the safety of children. These are constantly monitored through the project lifecycle by the Child Protection team at WCH, and regular training and updates are conducted with partner field staff and management teams. To avoid inefficient referrals that do not result in service provision, referral pathways will be mapped for all project locations and regularly updated throughout the lifecycle of the project.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Protection risk: Security incidents directly affecting WCH program and/or partners (staff, facilities, beneficiaries) Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation measures: Partner analyses local conflict dynamics and can anticipate developments impacting humanitarian response. Follow up by WCH on security situation (trends) and measures; security fund included in budget to support cost of material damage to facilities or cost of measure to increase safety; Development of a Duty of Care Policy for partners in coordination with other INGOs; Train CBO partners in security management and analysis of security threats; Ensure contingency measures are in place and agreed upon. Protection risk: Financial mismanagement or fraud by partners Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation measures: Closely follow implementation of financial monitoring procedures agreed with partners; Ensure monthly reporting of all financial expenditure with multiple forms of verification (videos and photos of activities and material with dates, talking to people on the ground). Verify monthly financial report (by programs and financial staff) before next monthly funding transfer.
