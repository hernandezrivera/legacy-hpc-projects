Project Summary

The ongoing war between the SAF and RSF and the increased hostilities continue to be reported across Khartoum, Darfur, Kordofan and Blue Nile States. The reverberations of hostilities have permeated every facet of education, culminating in the closure of 10,400 schools and the suspension of educational services for a staggering 6.5 million children, with a predominant majority being girls. According to the 2024 HRP, an estimated two million school-aged children are internally displaced, over 5.5 million children are awaiting the official re-opening of schools, and an additional 6.9 million children, including children with disabilities, are out-of-school.
The need to urgently address this crisis is underscored by the staggering numbers: as of November 7, 2023, 19 million children are out of school, with only 107 thousand having received basic school kits and materials.
In response to this crisis, the proposed project aims to reach 14,000 children (5,600 boys and 8,400 girls, 1,400 disabled), 500 teachers, PTAs, and education officials with essential education services. The newly displaced children have exacerbated the strain on already inadequate education services in gathering and return areas, which have suffered severely from a lack of educational resources and infrastructure. The overarching objective is to ensure that marginalized children, aged 6 to 18, gain access to safe and protective learning environments, especially in overcrowded and deprived locations.
In responding to the unique challenges faced by the education sector in post-conflict Sudan, UPO's proposed activities are strategically designed to address critical needs.
With a significant portion of schools forcefully closed and communities overwhelmed, UPO's intervention aims to provide immediate relief by restoring functional learning spaces by rehabilitating 80 classrooms and 20 temporary learning spaces, disaggregated by the severity of needs in localities. Furthermore, UPO recognizes that in times of crisis, recreation becomes a powerful tool for normalizing the learning environment and promoting social interaction. Hence, recreational materials are integrated to promote holistic child development, in which the project not only nurtures the emotional well-being of the children but also contributes to the overall normalization and stability of the educational experience. As the crisis has resulted in psychological distress among children, risking long-term impacts on their mental health and educational progress, the provision of psychosocial support to the 14,000 targeted children will not only address the urgent need for mental health support but also lay the foundation for sustained emotional well-being, ensuring a conducive environment for effective learning.

In crowded and vulnerable areas, access to clean water and sanitation is often limited, making the risk of disease outbreaks a constant threat. By addressing this basic necessity, the project aims to not only promote hygiene but also create a healthier learning environment, mitigating potential health crises. UPO has a WASH project in the HRP and plans to coordinate its project locations under the different sectors to harmonize and better serve the targeted population in need. Additionally, the project includes the provision of 8,400 dignity kits and Menstrual Hygiene management (MHM) education. This targeted support not only preserves the dignity of female students but also actively breaks down gender barriers, promoting inclusivity and equality within the education system.
UPO places a strong emphasis on community involvement through the comprehensive training of Parent Teachers Association (PTA) members in various critical areas such as school management, gender-based violence (GBV), child protection, and school safety. This approach aligns with our commitment to empowering local communities by cultivating a profound sense of ownership and active participation in the educational process.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

The project recognizes the significance of community feedback as a crucial mechanism for continuous improvement and adaptation of activities. Several strategies will be implemented to ensure effective utilization of community feedback:1/ Regular Consultative Meetings:UPO will establish regular forums, such as community meetings or focus group discussions, to gather feedback directly from the affected communities. UPO will also encourage open and transparent communication to understand community perceptions, concerns, and suggestions regarding the ongoing education initiatives. 2/ Surveys and Questionnaires:UPO will develop and administer surveys or questionnaires to assess community satisfaction, gather specific insights on program effectiveness, and identify areas for improvement.UPO will also ensure the surveys are culturally sensitive and accessible to all members of the community, including children and parents. 3/Community-Driven Monitoring and Evaluation:UPO will develop mechanisms for community-driven monitoring and evaluation, empowering local stakeholders to assess the impact of the project on their education environment.Additionally, UPO will collaborate with community leaders to establish criteria for success and jointly monitor progress.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

UPO regularly informs the inter-agency coordination mechanism through information sharing via emails, phone calls, and meetings, including best practices and lessons learned. Quarterly reports that include, but are not limited to, activities conducted, concerns raised, issues identified, and mechanisms established to address the issues will be prepared and submitted to the sector coordinators both at the field and Khartoum. At the field level, the organization will establish a committee to meet on a monthly basis to review the complaints and feedback received from the communities. The reports will be shared widely with other agencies working in the same areas.
