Project Summary

The education sector in the Gaza Strip has been negatively affected by the accumulated humanitarian crises due to the 14-year siege, high rates of poverty and unemployment, the power outages, the COVID-19 pandemic, and recently the escalation of hostilities in May 2021. These problems worsened the situation and increased Gaza's economic, social, and psychological problems, including Khan Yunis Governorate. The pandemic and the escalation of hostilities exhausted the education system, the students lost months of schooling, and their educational achievement was significantly lowered. In response, the MoE and the UNRWA were forced to adopt the e-learning program during the peak of COVID-19. However, due to poverty and the lack of livelihoods for many vulnerable families cannot meet the emerging needs of the internet, electricity, and supporting educational materials, these families could not catch up with this new mechanism.
CWDs, one of the most vulnerable groups; have a lower chance of joining formal education. The PCBS indicated that 46% of CWDs in Palestine are not enrolled in formal education. Moreover, the lack of funding makes it difficult for CSOs/NGOs to respond to the educational needs of children with disabilities.
These crises negatively affected the psychological well-being of children. e.g., the recent escalation of hostilities and the fear of infection with the COVID-19 left the children with anxiety, depression, and stress. The lack of PSS for children in schools and parents' preoccupation in managing their affairs daily may cause chronic mental health problems in the long run.
Thus, the project responds to the educational and psychological needs of children in Khan Yunis through three main components: remedial education services, specialized services for students with learning difficulties, and psychological support services for both groups.
The remedial education services will serve, each semester 360 children from 1st to 4th grade, including 120 CWDs learning difficulties from Al-Basma School. In total, 720 students will be served during the project duration. The one-year project is located at the headquarter of the Emaar Association in Khan Younis, targeting the most vulnerable and needy children. There will be 3 weekly classes for basic subjects: Arabic, English, and Mathematics. Each class consists of 2 lessons for 1 hour per each.
Special education services will be taken place in the resource room for 150 CWDs/semester, for a total of 300 by the end of the project. Groups of 5 will be created, between 4 and 12 years old; each group will have 2 weekly sessions.
Weekly psychological support sessions will be delivered to improve the psychological situation of children from both targeted groups in the above activities. Each session will be 40 minutes.
For the maximum protection, children hygiene kits will be distributed to them, and health procedures will be followed in the Emaar Association facility according to the health protocol approved by the WHO and the MoH. Parents’ awareness will be raised on protecting their children from the COVID-19 and SEA by distributing awareness brochures to them. SMS containing advice and how to submit complaints to the association will be sent to them. Project workers will also be trained on PSEA and included within their contracts.
To achieve the principle of transparency and access to information, the activities of the project will be announced through funded ads on the Facebook page of the Emaar Association.
To achieve the principle of community participation, the local community has been involved in identifying its most problems, needs, and priorities during this period by holding a needs assessment targeting children aged 6-9 years and their families. 50 parents will be contacted in the middle of each semester. At the end of the project, 280 questionnaires will be distributed to the families to measure their satisfaction with the services provided.

1 - Needs

The living conditions in Gaza Strip are more and more wretched due it its 14 years of blockade, the internal Palestinian division, and the repeated escalations with Israel. Gaza continues to suffer from high poverty and unemployment rates, reaching 64% and 49%, respectively, according to the OCHA,2021 and UNRWA,2021 . In addition, the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic made their living condition more challenging. In the HNO 2022 report, 74% of the Palestinian children, at school age and who need humanitarian assistance to access education, live in Gaza Strip. Among these, 13,398 children are CWDs.These aspects negatively impacted the children's learning; those with disabilities could not afford their educational needs or cover their costs. The HNO mentioned that 31% of interviewed households in Gaza have difficulties meeting essential education needs such as tuition fees and books. The parents of these children are one of the most vulnerable groups in the Gaza Strip, as they are suffering from extreme poverty and a high rate of unemployment. They are also unable to purchase remedial education materials for their children. Unfortunately, some of the poorest parents may force their children to drop out of school to help to support their families financially. The (UNICEF,2018) indicated that 5% of children, aged between 10 and 15 years, are out of school; either they left it or did not enroll in it.The measures that were imposed by the local authorities to limit the spread of COVID-19, such as imposing closures and restricting movements, had a direct impact on the unemployment and poverty rates. According to (PCBS,2020), 68.4% of workers' income in Khan Yunis was reduced during the lockdown. So, many could not provide e-learning tools for their children, such as tablets, the internet, or other self-learning materials. As a result, some of these students could not access education. HNO 2022 also confirmed that more than 1.3 million Palestinian children lost at least 3 months of education in the school year of 2020-2021. In the previous period, the frequent interruption of learning and the movement between face-to-face and online learning confused the students. It weakened the link between the student and the education process.The last escalation of hostilities on Gaza Strip, May 2021, also impacted education. The escalation of hostilities damaged more than 150 private and public schools (HNO 2022). Moreover, the UNRWA also allocated 63 of its schools as shelters for the displaced people, which affected the students enrolled in the UNRWA schools. It should be noted that about 65% of the UNRWA schools work as double shifts.All the above humanitarian crises in Gaza Strip directly affected the children psychologically. They had various forms of anxiety, depression, involuntary urination, and the spread of the phenomenon of violence. The needs assessment report by Emaar Association, Sep 2021, that studies violence rates among the elementary school, indicated that the percentage of school violence is increased significantly in the Khan Yunis. Moreover, The HNO 2022 confirmed that the interruption in school hours and the weak psychological support services provided in schools threaten children with long-term mental and psychological disorders.The HNO 2022 report indicated that 71% of the families who participated in the MSNA that their disabled children face challenges accessing education services, and 25% indicated that at least one CWD is not enrolled in school. Children, who are enrolled in schools, may face a higher risk of dropout due to gaps in adapted facilities and assistive devices at schools, adequate transportation means, and staff capacity to accommodate their needs.Adapting the belief of the importance of the society in participation in identifying its needs and problems, Emaar Association conducted a need assessment study. This study targeted 4 FGDs of parents and their children, of both sexes, aged between 6 and 9 years, including children with disabilities. This study aims to know the impact of humanitarian crises, including the COVID-19 pandemic and the 2021 war on the Gaza Strip, on education in Khan Younis. The majority of children indicated that they face problems understanding the vocabulary of the basic subjects (Arabic, mathematics, and English) due to the interruption of learning. Also, 80% of children's families confirmed that the escalation of hostilities and the pandemic had negatively affected their psychological well-being, including those with disabilities. These last cannot integrate into an educational program adopted by the UNRWA and the MoE due to their difficult living. The families of these children stressed that the educational services provided are not compatible with the needs of their children, especially with the difficult situations that these families are facing. All these aspects led to the incapability to reach their educational needs. The children's mothers also stated that they could not follow up their children's academic achievement, especially concerning e-learning, due to the increased burdens on them in providing for the needs of their homes, which increased the psychological pressure on them.Therefore, the idea of the project is to respond to those needs through 3 main components: providing adapted education for marginalized and vulnerable groups, providing appropriate educational services for children with disabilities, and contributing to psychological support for both groups.To achieve the principle of protection for the project's beneficiaries, methodologies and systems to prevent the transmission of COVID-19 will be followed according to the MoH and the WHO in the facilities of the Emaar Association. Also, children hygiene kits will be distributed to them and the project staff, and awareness will be provided about preventing the COVID-19 virus during the project period. PSEA training will also be included for the project crew.

2 - Activities

The project activities are designed to respond to the needs of the target groups while ensuring the implementation of these activities in line with preserving the dignity and safety of the beneficiaries and responding to their needs according to the nature of the problems they face: 1- Conducting a training course on PSEA, providing educational services remotely, complaints system, and child protection in Emaar Association for the project staff (12 teachers of the project, psychologist, and special educator). The training will be 18 HRs for 3 days (6 HRs/day). 2- Contracting with consultants to prepare supporting educational material (remedial education) for the basic courses (Arabic , English, and Mathematics) for the basic education levels (1st – 4th grades). this activity will be in coordination with the MoE and UNRWA to ensure the quality of the prepared materials and to be aligned with standards of the MoE and UNRWA. 3- Providing remedial education classes in Arabic, English, and Mathematics for 360 children from both sexes in the basic education levels (1st – 4th grades) during the semester and with a total number of 720 during the project period (2 semesters). Each child will receive 3 classes per week, and there will be 3 groups of each education level. One of them will be for CWDs from the students attending Al-Basma School affiliated to Emaar Association, so there will be 6 groups of each level per week (3 groups on Sat, Mon, Wed and the other 3 groups on Sun, Tue, Thu). Each group will consist of 15 students to ensure the quality of learning and their protection against COVID-19 infection. The classes will be held in well- equipped rooms/classes at Emaar Association. Students will be provided with necessary stationery and hospitality (biscuits, juice, etc.). The selection process of the students will be in full coordination with the ministry of education and UNRWA to prevent the duplication of services and guarantee the selection of the neediest students in terms of educational achievement, poverty severity, disability, and psychological well-being. For children who do not have the money for transportation and are far away from the Emaar association, a bus will also be adapted to CWDs will transfer them in order to ensure their safety when they come to take their lessons. 4- Providing emergency educational supplies at the resource room at Emaar Association to target the CWDs. 5- A special educator will provide education sessions for the CWDs (with learning difficulties) at Al-Basma School at the resource room. This activity will target 150 CWDs aged from 4- 12 years old. Each CWD will receive 2 sessions per week, and each session will target 5 CWDs. There will be 300 CWDs during the project duration. 6- Providing group Psychological Support Services (group PSS) for the children in the remedial and resource room activities. The children will receive 1 session per week for 40 minutes to alleviate the stress and support them psychologically (16 sessions/semester, and 32 sessions/project duration) . Additionally, there will be a final recreational activity at the end of each semester. This activity will be implemented in the green area of Ro'ya school affiliated to Emaar Association and it will be with the participation of the parents of the children. The activity will be implemented by the animators under the supervision of the psychologist. 7- Parents' awareness will be raised on protecting their children from COVID-19 infection and Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA), and the mechanisms of filing complaints. This will be done by distributing a leaflet to the students to be delivered to their parents. Furthermore, Emaar will build a simple database containing their contact information to provide them with health education messages, complaints mechanisms, and their children's educational achievements. Moreover, a coordination will be made with MoE and UNRWA to present the children's acheivement by sending their records to their formal schools. 8- 8 Facebook sponsored ads will be published on Emaar Association's page containing awareness messages on how the parents can protect their children from COVID-19, SEA and file a complaint within the Emaar Association complaints system (1 ad/month). 9- In all project activities and within the protection framework, children hygiene kits will be distributed to them and the project staff to ensure their safety from COVID-19 infection.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Within the policies and the principles of Emaar Association in involving the local community to participate in all stages of its programs and projects that are implemented, Emaar Association adopts complaints and feedback system that is also suitable for PWDs. The system collects the data, complaints and any suggestions from the beneficiaries and the community members through using the following tools: 1- The complaints boxes, which are located in all Emaar Association's facilities.2- Generic email3- Paper-based or electronic questionnaires.Complaints are managed through the following mechanism:- Enable receiving complaints from affected population.- Responding to complaints.- Accountability and lessons learned.Emaar Association announces complaints mechanisms and tools through social media to engage the local community and encourage them to file complaints. Moreover, the receptionists in each of Emaar Association's inform the public face-to-face about the complaints mechanisms. Moreover the beneficiaries can use special e-mail to file more sensitive complaints about sexual exploitation while receiving services. Special committee from Emaar Association manages these complaints. The committee sends reports about the beneficiaries' complaints to the Executive Director and the board of directors to solve them and design future intervention strategies. Through these methods, the beneficiaries' satisfaction can be measured in addition to the most important problems they face while receiving services from the Emaar Association’s facilities. The suggestions and feedback are used to draw future intervention strategies, including:• Contribute to strengthening the protection policy the principles of humanitarian action.• Contributing to the dissemination of the societal accountability approach.• Strengthening the adaptive management approach in Emaar Association.• Adding and developing the services. • Developing the performance of employees.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The project idea, services, and implementation mechanisms start from respecting the principles of protection stipulated in the humanitarian charter, including the right to live with dignity, receive assistance and protection. Moreover, these mechanisms are part of the Emaar Association organizational values, where Emaar Association ensure the safety and dignity of beneficiaries through:• To prevent SEA during the provision of the project services, the project staff will attend PSEA training. As well as their contracts will include a standard clause on PSEA following IASC principles and guidance.• In order not to expose the beneficiaries (children) to more harm, their parents will be educated about SEA and the complaints mechanism.• Equal access and non-discrimination in the provision of the services.• Beneficiary consultations and documentation are private and confidential.• The project services will be provided to the beneficiaries who suffer from poverty and vulnerability totally free of charge under the human rights-based approach.• All necessary information for the beneficiaries related to their educational levels and mechanisms of intervention will be provided to them and their parents.• Gender needs will be taken into account through the presence of a female psychologist among the work team to respond to the gender needs of girls.• The facilities of the Emaar Association were designed to suit people with physical disabilities to ensure easy access to the services.• Providing work accident insurance for the project staff.• The project services will be provided to the beneficiaries regardless of their color, race, religious or political affiliation. The proposed project will enable equal and impartial access to health services to marginalized and vulnerable people.• For the children's safety, the facilities of Emaar Association are designed to ensure the protection of them including stairs rails, first aids, firefighting equipment, etc.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The Emaar Association adopts the principle of equality and impartially in providing its services to beneficiaries, especially marginalized groups that need humanitarian interventions to improve their humanitarian status. Emaar Association provides its services without discrimination based on age, sex, religion, ethic, race, or political affiliation. Knowing that through this project and to achieve the principle of impartiality in the provision of services, Emaar Association will provide the services to the targeted people on the basis that each beneficiary has its individual characteristics and special needs, especially individuals with disabilities as the most vulnerable in the society and who need special attention and protection depend on Human dignity, confidentiality, and non-discrimination. Access to the project services will be accessible according to the following: 1. Accessibility: The Emaar Association will provide the project services through its facility in KhanYounis governorate. The geographical location of the facility is characterized by its presence in the middle of the governorate, which is easily accessible and has been known to the public since its establishment. The project services will be provided to the target groups, including PWD, in a safe and dignified manner and without any discrimination. In order to o ensure the access of all the targeted children to the project services, especially the children who don't have the money for transportation and are far away from the Emaar association, there will be a bus adopted to CWDs to transfer them. 2. Availability: services are provided in the facility of Emaar Association after the end of the school schedule, while the Emaar Association is open for any inquiry, complaints, and services from 8:00 am to 2:00 pm from Sat to Thu. 4.
