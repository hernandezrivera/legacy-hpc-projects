Project Summary

This intervention aims to support in and out of school girls and boys aged 3-17, whose education have been disrupted as result of conflict to have access to safe, quality protective and inclusive education and vocational skills opportunities in formal and non-formal schools. GOALPrime intends to ensure quality learning through accelerated learning and teaching at the right level methodology.
GOALPrime intends to target a total of 7440 individuals in Damboa, kala balge, Geidam, Gujba, Madagali and Michika LGAs of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states. out of which 7200 (50% girls and 50% boys are children (learners aged 3-17) including those with disabilities in schools, camps, and host communities.
The adult target will be 120 (50% women and 50% men) teachers who will be trained on improved teaching and learning approaches using the ABEP curriculum and TaRL teaching methodology including the creation of child-friendly, gender-sensitive, SEL/PSS sensitive and inclusive learning spaces.
Also, 120 SBMC members (48 men, 48 women, 12 boys & 12 girls) will be trained on safe schools and the creation of child-friendly, gender-sensitive, SEL/PSS-sensitive, and inclusive learning environments.
A target of 5% of the overall individuals to be reached will be persons living with a disability.
Out-of-School Children (OOSCs) will make up 3600 of the total learner targets. In collaboration with community members, they will be identified, profiled, and enrolled in the age-appropriate Accelerated Education Programme (AEP). Following successful AEPs, profiled OOSCs will be mainstreamed into formal schools in partnership with LEAs, SAME, and SUBEB. Age- and class-appropriate educational resources will be provided to the 3600 learners targeted, and teaching materials will be provided for the targeted 120 teachers. Back-to-school enrolment kits will be provided for the 3600 children that will be mainstreamed into formal schools.
Moreover, caregivers of targeted learners and OOSCs will be reached with positive parenting sessions through a door-to-door approach by the Community volunteer teachers.
Targeted adolescent girls in school will be reached with Menstrual hygiene management life-skills sessions after which Menstrual Hygiene Management Kits will be distributed. The Pads in the kit will be reusable sanitary pads.
GOALPrime intends to establish a textbook lending program in targeted schools to address immediate educational needs, reduce barriers to learning, empower teachers, and contribute to the long-term development and resilience of conflict-affected girls and boys.
GOALPrime will collaborate with relevant sectors such as WASH, CP, and GBV to address issues of Mental health through training on psychosocial support activities using local and low-cost PSS Materials, the improvement of school WASH disease outbreak prevention through health, hygiene, sanitation awareness, and provision of WASH in school items, establish a complaint response mechanism to identify and address child protection concerns, create awareness on prevention, response and reporting of Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), Gender Based Violence (GBV ) and Violence Against Children (VAC) and back to school enrolment drives.
GOALPrime will Identify and support the retrofitting and renovation of dilapidated TLS in target LGAs and provide target schools with 3-seater classroom desks.
GOALPrime will coordinate with the State Agency for Education (SAME) to carry out routine monitoring and supervision support visits across AEP centers for quality assurance, policy alignment, and provision of targeted support by SAME geared towards the sustainability of the program.

1 - Needs

According to the HNO 2024, 1.55 million children aged 3-17, affected by crises have been identified to need critical educational assistance across the BAY states. Access to education has been obstructed by a range of complex challenges, including poor and unsafe school infrastructure, lack of proper water and sanitation facilities, insecurity, attacks on educational facilities, shortage of teachers, lack of premises adapted for children with disabilities and other protection concerns. These challenges have varying effects on different population subsets, for instance, girls are likely to face the risk of early marriage, while boys may force to recruitment by armed groups. The crisis has exacerbated the situation for children with disabilities, who constitute 15% of the population (8% of whom are girls), depriving them of essential, needs-based educational services. In fact, 56 percent of all public primary classrooms are deemed inadequate and nearly 1,400 schools have been destroyed since the onset of the crisis in the North-east. The 2022 and 2023 flooding and climate change affected 132 schools depriving 50,490 girls, 48,510 boys and 9,000 children (4,860 girls) with disabilities of access to educational facilities. Climate change related crises will continue as a significant threat to children and causing disruptions in educational services. Access to safe, protective quality and inclusive education remains unsustainable across the BAY states which can potentially give rise to child protection issues such as child labor, child marriage, early pregnancy, GBV and recruitment into armed groups. Furthermore, in the BAY states, an increasing number of children persistently encounter gender-based violence, including instances of sexual exploitation and abuse, particularly when they are denied access to education services. An estimated 24 per cent of the GBV survivors who sought assistance from GBV service providers were children, adolescents, young girls and boys, ranging from 1- 18 years of age.The extended period during which displaced children were denied access to education services has resulted in noticeable learning gaps. Children living in the extremely remote and hard to reach locations have the pronounced needs of education opportunities. The escalating levels of insecurity and food scarcity are expected to drive further displacement in 2024, necessitating alternative learning opportunities for affected school-age and over-aged children, especially for those involved in livelihood activities. The sudden closure of camps and camp-like settings potentially lead to a significant relocation of IDP and returnee children, potentially to areas that are inaccessible to humanitarian organizations.GOALPrime via this intervention will enhance and sustain access to safe, protective quality and inclusive education and vocational skills opportunities within a safe environment to 7440 people via integrating the teaching at the right level methodology in the implementation of accelerated basic education program, establishment of textbook lending programs in upper primary, lower and senior secondary schools, retrofitting and renovation of temporary learning spaces to be disability friendly, provision of classroom desk, awareness and sensitization on prevention, response and reporting of PSEA, GBV, VAC and back to school enrolment drives, teachers training on the creation of child friendly, gender sensitive, SEL/PSS sensitive and inclusive learning spaces, positive parenting sessions to facilitate continued learning in home in case of loss of access, awareness on health, hygiene and sanitation to prevent disease outbreak in schools and maintain a clean, safe and healthy environment across 6 LGAs in the BAY states

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Identify, profile and enroll 3600 OOSc for 9 months accelerated basic education program. Rationale: The ABEP will bridge the educational gap for children who have missed out on formal education as a result of displacement or disruption, Providing them the opportunity to be mainstreamed back to formal schools to complete their basic education. Activity 2: Identify and engage 120 (Borno=40, Adamawa=40, Yobe=40) community bacsed volunteer teachers for AEP centers.Rationale: The community-based volunteer teachers will facilitate learning across the AEP centers. Community-based volunteer teachers possess a deep understanding of the local culture, languages, and specific needs of the community children. This localized expertise can enhance the relevance and effectiveness of education programs, making them more responsive to the unique challenges. It is cost effective, culturally sensitive and breeds trust and acceptance Activity 3: Train CVTs on ABEP curriculum and (TaRL) teaching methodologyRationale: Training teachers in these methodologies enables them to better understand and address the diverse learning levels within a classroom. The training is intended to enhance the confidence and competence in delivering effective instruction. This is especially important considering that ABEP and TaRL methodologies requires a nuanced understanding of foundational concepts and teaching techniques. It will also give the teachers a wider perspective to understanding the ABEP pathways. Activity 4: Provide scholastic materials to 3600 ABEP learnersRationale: supplying scholastic materials to learners will promote active learning, enhance educational outcomes, address learning gaps, and creating a supportive and inclusive learning environment. It is a strategic investment in the academic success and overall development of the learners participating in the ABEP Activity 5: Provide enrollment kits for 3600 OOSC ABEP learners mainstreamed to formal schools.Rationale: This will address barriers to learning, promote inclusivity, and contribute a positive and supportive learning environment. Activity 6: Construction/renovation of Temporary learning spaces in 6 LGAs of the BAY states.Rationale: Constructing/ renovating temporary learning spaces in conflict-affected communities is a multifaceted approach that addresses immediate educational needs, promotes community resilience, and lays the groundwork for long-term development and stability. It reflects a commitment to the fundamental right to education even in challenging circumstances. It will also provide an opportunity to have disability friendly TLSs in place and provide alternative learning spaces. Activity 7: Construct/rehabilitate gender segregated latrines and water points.Rationale: Gender segregated facilities will provide children with privacy and dignity. In conflict-affected areas of NE Nigeria, where living conditions are challenging, ensuring personal privacy becomes especially important, particularly for women and girls. Gender-segregated facilities will contribute to a safer and more secure environment, reducing the risk of harassment or violence. Additionally, Providing designated spaces with water and sanitation facilities supports the hygiene and well-being of girls during their menstrual cycle and ensure girls do not stay out of school during their menstrual cycles. Activity 8: Awareness on prevention, response and reporting of PSEA, GBV and VAC.Rationale: This awareness is essential for creating safer, more accountable, and supportive environments. By empowering parents/caregivers, community members, girls and boys, building capacity, and fostering a culture of openness, these campaigns will contribute to the protection and well-being of vulnerable groups. Activity 9: Provision of teacher training on the creation of child friendly, gender sensitive, SEL/PSS sensitive and inclusive learning spaces.Activity 10: conduct parenting session meetings

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

By integrating every strategy with the protection principles, GOALPrime will mainstream protection (GBV & PSEA) throughout the execution of this project and advance meaningful access, safety, and dignity of beneficiaries, employers, and volunteers. Safety, decency, and avoiding any kind of harm to both beneficiaries and employees are priorities for GOALPrime. Additionally, GOALPrime will also make sure that the target population has equitable access to the service, free from impediments and prejudice to their needs. GOALPrime will pay attention to people and groups who could be especially vulnerable or have a hard time obtaining help and resources, including those with disabilities and GBV survivors who have been identified. GOALPrime will integrate GBV Risk Mitigation efforts to reduce or decrease GBV risk to beneficiaries. GOALPrime will work consciously in compliance with the SphereHumanitarian Core Standards and Imperatives. All approaches will align with the principles of protection as we remain committed to ensuring that this project does not expose beneficiaries to more risk in compliance with the DO NO HARM Protection Principle. Also, GOALPrime will be intentional about maintaining an environment where there is zero Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA). All staff, volunteers, contractors, and vendors engaged directly or indirectly to implement this project will be given PSEA orientation. They will be familiarized with GOALPrime's policy on PSEA, and they MUST express their unwavering commitment by signing GOALPrime's Zero Tolerance to SEA Code of Conduct. Also, GOALPrime will lay down reporting protocols for the reporting of PSEA cases if unfortunately, they occur. GOALPrime will also ensure that the identity of the reporter(s) is kept very confidential. When convenient, GOALPrime will encourage reports to be made on anonymous grounds. Also, being guided by the Inter-Agency mechanism for reporting SEA, GOALPrime will ensure a systematized engagement with affected populations including confidentialcomplaints and investigations procedures at both the system and individual agency levels. As recommended by the IASC Task Force on PSEA which identified interagency cooperation in the creation and maintenance of community-based complaintsmechanisms (CBCMs) as a key component in the prevention and response to SEA. GOALPrime will continue to raise awareness to the target population that humanitarian assistance is free and never conditioned on sexual favours as well as to be informed on how to access the appropriate complaints mechanisms, and support to set up in the community if SEA occurs. Furthermore, GOALPrime will ensure that established CBCMs in target locations are effective that there is inter-agency coordination to ensure consistent messaging and that access to the mechanism is asbroad and straightforward as possible for potential complainants. GOALPrime will also be intentional about the environment and safety of the communities where we implement by ensuring that our approaches and tools do not contribute to the degradation of the environment.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

GOALPrime will be consolidating its established community-based project accountability mechanism through this project to improve community stakeholders and project beneficiary participation both in the project decision-making and implementation. This project was designed in consultation with the community stakeholders and children based on lessons learned from our previous implementation in the target LGAs. GPON uses a people-centered feedback mechanism to gather opinions, and complaints of the community members and beneficiaries on its project to inform decision-making for quality project implementation. However, lessons learned from the approach include, forming an LGA-level project committee that will include all community groups' representatives and stakeholders to improve the participation of the affected community in the project implementation process. GPON has also made provision for anonymous feedback complaint/feedback boxes, SMSs, and direct calls to the Compliance and Accountability team. GPON project team will conduct an LGA inception workshop with the established community-based project committee to discuss the project deliverables and the community-level needs and how this project will contribute to resolving the needs. Also, the LGA project committee will work with the project team to define project beneficiaries’ vulnerability based on the context of the LGA, agree on vulnerability criteria, and provide a recommended approach on how the project can be implemented to better reach and serve the project targeted groups. Also, the project team will conduct quarterly review meetings with the community-based project committee at the LGA level to inform how each quarter can consolidate with the previous quarter's achievement to improve the quality of services rendered.
