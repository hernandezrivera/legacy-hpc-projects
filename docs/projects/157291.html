Project Summary

In close coordination with the Government of Sudan, Clusters, and other local and international organizations, RI will fill critical gaps in basic service systems in water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) targeting vulnerable IDP and host community members in North Darfur. Almost 3.5 million people in Sudan require some form of WASH assistance, with acute need present within the Zamzam IDP Camp and the rural localities in North Darfur.
In the targeted localities, RI is the primary implementer of WASH goods and services programming. With more than 17 years in North Darfur, RI develops programs systematically based off community and other local stakeholder input to ensure activities are implemented in a manner that is sensitive to the different localized needs of men, women, boys, girls, and people with disabilities. It spans both infrastructure activities to increase access, and behavior change and capacity building to ensure sustained services. Activities will be integrated into existing RI Health and Nutrition sector activities through WASH improvements at health facilities, and inclusion of WASH hygiene messaging through Community Health Workers and Community Nutrition Volunteers.
To note, this project is intended to be together with RI's proposed multi-sector project to integrate with the health, nutrition, and livestock components. RI will work with local partner, ANHAR for Peace, Development and Humanitarian Work; they are primarily involved in health and nutrition interventions, but will assist with health facility WASH facilities and hygiene promotion.

Needs

Almost 3.5 million people in Sudan require some form of WASH assistance, including access to improved drinking water, access to safe means of excreta disposal and hygiene sensitization. In relation to the minimum SPHERE standards, the major gaps in WASH services within Zamzam Camp for internally displaced peoples (IDP) include: - Piped water systems showed low or no free residual chlorine (FRC) levels at point of distribution; Long queues and long waiting times (approximately 90 minutes) at water collection points; - Communities lack sanitation facilities in the new arrival areas; Competition for water at available sources for both people and animals; Water collection points and distribution tap stands are not protected from animals resulting in degraded sanitary conditions, which are a public health risk; - Lack of sanitation facilities in schools with an average ratio of 1:300 for schools; - Lack of access to sanitation facilities in health facilities with an average ratio of 1:100 for health facilities; and Poor solid waste management practices within the camp. Under current and past programs, RI has made strides in improving water and sanitation infrastructure and improving hygiene behavior. Up to the end of 2017, RI supported: 6,333 households to collect water for drinking, cooking, and hygiene from improved water sources; and rehabilitation of 23 hand pumps – eight in Zamzam IDP Camp and 17 hand pumps in Sayah completed.However, there still remain significant coverage gaps in Zamzam IDP Camp due to poor management of WASH facilities, a continued influx of IDPs and recent returnees, and a scarcity of implementing WASH partners. These factors are also contributing to WASH coverage gaps in rural localities of North Darfur outside of Zamzam Camp. According to the North Darfur WASH Cluster 4W Matrix, more than 90% of communities in Al-Malha use unprotected sources of water, 50% of existing hand pumps are non-functional, and 70% have no access to sanitation facilities. Findings in Al Sayah (population 205,000) also revealed a lack of adequate WASH facilities with 50% of the population using unprotected sources of water, and 80% having no access to latrines. Based on communications with UNICEF, there are additional coverage gaps for unmet WASH needs in El Sireat and Kebkabiya Localities, which RI also proposes to address.

Activities

The proposed geographic area for WASH infrastructure activities will be communities in the Zamzam IDP Camp and the rural areas of El Sireat, Kebkabiya, and Al Mellit Localities. Proposed activities are: - In line with the GoS CLTS behavior change messaging, will train 83 community health workers (CHWs) and 63 community nutrition volunteers (CNVs) on hygiene awareness. The CHWs and CNWs will sensitize 5,080 households (25,400 individuals) in Zamzam Camp and 2,317 new households (13,901 individuals, 6 by family by house) in 19 communities in El Sireat, Kebkabiya and Al Mellit through hygiene promotion sessions at the community level. In addition to general hygiene messages, women and adolescent girls will be targeted with menstrual hygiene management (MHM) messages.- Hygiene kits will be distributed to new arrivals/returnees in Zamzam IDP Camp. Given the significant challenges in accessing cash in Sudan, RI will explore options for integrating cash-based interventions (e.g. vouchers for hygiene kits). - Construct 500 household latrines in Zamzam Camp.- Expand WASH services at the five RI-supported PHCCs through construction of 5 incinerators; improvement of water storage capacity, extraction, and distribution systems (including protective fencing against animal access and contamination); & construct 7 latrines in the in Zamzam El Sireaf and Kebkabiya.- Rehabilitate 18 community hand pumps (7 in Zamzam IDP Camp, 2 in El Sireaf, 8 Kebkabiya and 1 in Mellit); upgrade 3 community hand-dug open wells with solar-hybrid powered submersible pumps in mini water yards, 2 in Kebkabiya and 1 in Al Mellit; upgrade 3 water yards to solar-hybrid systems and construct 3 water tanks in Kebkabiya, and Zamzam. It will include protective fencing against animal access and contamination.- RI will establish Water User Committees (WUCs) and train 100 minimum members on water management. Conduct regular water quality and depth monitoring jointly with WHO & SMoH.

Impact on environment/natural resources

According to Sudan's 2016 National Adaptation Plan (NAP), North Darfur has a semi-desert prone to drought, low rainfall, and geological system that is unfavorable for groundwater storage. Climate scenarios predict increased temperatures, decreased rain, and decreased groundwater recharge over time. In line with the NAP, RI will ensure water facilities are dug in areas safe from pollution and with strong management and monitoring systems for both quality and quantity. Regarding sanitation, RI recognizes that improper sanitation can lead to public health threats through fecal contamination in the environment. Therefore, RI will provide a minimum of Basic sanitation services as per the Sustainable Development Goal's sanitation ladder. As part of hygiene (including MHM), households will be sensitized on proper disposal of sanitation waste.
