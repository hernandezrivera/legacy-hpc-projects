Project Summary

As a result of the crisis affecting Syria for over ten years now, the conflicts have left several areas around the country with considerable damages in the infrastructures, housing, and services.. causing a large number of the population to flee their areas of residence & seek refuge in neighboring areas. & after the military actions have ceased, IDPs are eager to return to their houses in their original areas of residence.
The Syrian Society for Social Development is planning to provide basic necessary life-saving services, through rehabilitation of Damaged houses for Returnees & Host Communities in various locations in the governorates of Rural Damascus and Idleb (150 houses in 2022 & 150 houses in 2023).
SSSD has previously implemented a similar project in the area of Az-Zabdani / governorate of Rural Damascus, rehabilitating Damaged houses, Schools, and the main sewerage pipe serving the area, in addition to community centers providing services to beneficiaries of the host community & returning population in the area, & official approvals are already obtained for over 180 houses.
In 2022 SSSD has implemented a rehabilitation of:
- 85 Damaged Houses in Az-Zabdani / Rural Damascus
- 35 Damaged Houses in Harasta / Rural Damascus
- 40 Damaged Houses in Khan Shaykun / Idleb
SSSD is planning for a similar project in the year 2023 in the same locations of the previous project in 2022. The planned number of the houses is expected to reach /150/ houses in Az-Zabadani, Harasta & Khan Shaykun & Expecting to host /750/ beneficiaries, with around 69% of returnees, selected following the agreed selection criteria, among the most vulnerable of children, women, men, people with special needs and elderly among the affected IDPs.
- 75 Damaged Houses in Az-Zabdani / Rural Damascus with 375 Beneficiaries
- 35 Damaged Houses in Harasta / Rural Damascus with 175 Beneficiaries
- 40 Damaged Houses in Khan Shaykun / Idleb with 200 Beneficiaries.

3 - Needs

As per the "Syria Humanitarian Response Plan 2023" 2.8 M people are in need in the Shelter sector. As per the 2023 HNO The country still has the largest number of IDPs in the world: 6.8 million people, & the highest number of PIN since the beginning of the crisis.15.3 million people will require humanitarian assistance, including 2.1 million IDPs living in last-resort IDP sites, with needs that keep increasing across all sectors.& Following information by SSSD staff in the suggested sites, communications with Municipality, Region Mukhtar, neighborhood committees, & coordination with SSSD community centers, staff has conducted a search of Damaged houses & expected number of returnees, & a technical assessment of houses with average /no structural damages. over 6000 families of returnees in Az-Zabdani, 13000 in Harasta, with weak financial condition have started doing random repairs, eager to get back to their residences. Over 1600 families in Khan Shaykun, where public administrations have moved from Idleb. Over /60/ houses assessed in April 2022, through site visits, information collected on building structural situation, damages, needed winterization items, kitchen & sanitary fittings, personal information of inhabitants. A major part of Official approvals is obtained in Az-Zabdani & the project is ready for direct implementation. approvals for Harasta & Khan Shaykun are to be applied for with the beginning of the project activities.

4 - Implementation of activities

After searching for houses with average, non-structural damages in the selected areas of returning population, the modality adopted by SSSD in implementing the project activities is: - Applying for official approvals for the selected project locations & houses (Harasta & Khan Shaykun, as approvals for Az-Zabadani are already obtained). - Conducting a detailed technical study of assessment in selected houses, & needs of residing beneficiaries. - Announcement of tenders as per the adopted SSSD policy. - Implementation of the project activities in adherence to shelter sector technical guidelines, through local contractors & encouraging the participation of skilled beneficiaries in the implementation of works. - Procurement of material from the local market. - Supervision & tracking of the activities by SSSD staff in Rural Damascus & Idleb. - Collecting needed data for periodic reporting as per the agreed templates. - Handing over of the implemented houses to beneficiaries, in presence of the municipality representative.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

- Physical risk due to structural integrity of buildings &/or presence of ERWs. L: High / I: High Mitigating Measure: "1. Structural assessment by qualified personnel. 2. Repair/rehabilitate buildings only if structurally safe. 3. Work closely with authorities involved in ERW clearance to ensure building/area is free from ERW. 4. Orient contractors/workers on risks of ERW and ask them to report immediately if suspcious object is found and suspend the work until suspected object is removed safely. - Increased community tension due to different assistance packages, unclear beneficiary selection criteria leading to a feeling of discrimination. - Selection criteria is influence by local authorities may contribute to nepotism, tension in the community and risk of most vulnerable being left out. L: High / I: Medium Mitigating Measure: "1. Standardise assistance package with guidelines. 2. 8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SSSD, Since 2018, has adopted a PSEA policy, all the humanitarian staff is trained on applying this policy and asked to sign it. As well as training the beneficiaries on established complaints mechanisms in case of offence or abuse, the mechanisms include a hotline in all governorates, as well as through Social Media, in addition to providing activities of protection awareness sessions to beneficiaries. As well as preventing potential GBV, exploitation or harassment, by providing safe, well lit & well equipped spaces in the project area. and providing easy & safe access for elderly & people with special needs to houses, bathrooms & toilets.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: inflation prevents the implementation of activities within the allocated budget as per the agreed BoQs Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigation measure: partial alteration of the work items: Eliminating of non- essential work items and/or decreasing the number of rehabilitated units in order to stay within the allocated budget & provide the agreed standard of services. Risk: potential shortage of needed materials for activities to be provided by suppliers in the local market Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation measure: procurement of all needed material ahead of the start of implementation: procurement of the totality of needed material prior to the start of implementation of the project in agreement with suppliers to supply gradually following the phases of the project. Risk: Imposed restrictions to humanitarian actors to respond to Covid-19 Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigation measure: Adopting preventive measures against Covid-19: providing all SSSD working staff with Covid-19 preventive/protective equipment such as masks, gloves & applying imposed restrictions on the working site. Risk: Logistic risks such as security risks, operational risks, delays of approvals.. Likelihood: Low Impact: High Mitigation measure: - No security risks are expected in the project areas in Rural Damascus, as military actions have stopped & inhabitants are returning to their area of residence - Risk of explosive remnants of war (ERW) in the area of Khan Shaykun / Idleb
