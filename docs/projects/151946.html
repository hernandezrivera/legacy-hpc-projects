Needs

Since August 2014, PIN has been responding to the humanitarian crisis in the Donetsk and Luhansk Oblasts and is one of the largest humanitarian NGOs operating in Ukraine. Building on its 15-years activity in Ukraine, PIN established a significant presence and operational capacity in GCA and Non-Government Controlled Areas (NGCA) The on-going conflict; high numbers of IDPs; many thousands caught in the conflict zone; significant disruption of basic services; and large damages to housing, combined with a lack of means to cover essential costs have cultivated a significant humanitarian crisis. Food Security & Socio-Economic Trend Analysis – Eastern Ukraine (March 2018, Food Security and Livelihoods Cluster (FSLC)) highlights the continued correlation between food insecurity, vulnerability and socio-economic trends, which led to drastically increased levels of poverty as of 2017 compared with the start of the conflict in 2013, both in Luhansk GCA (74% from 20%) and Donetsk GCA (66% from 22%). Since the humanitarian crisis is not expected to be over in the near future, various kinds of humanitarian assistance are needed. Therefore, the food voucher and agro voucher intervention will ensure the minimum household food security of vulnerable population and create opportunities for income generation. Alongside with this, the reconstruction of communal infrastructure objects, suffered from war-related damages, is also an area of concern, thus making a “cash for work” (CFW) intervention timely sensitive. Furthermore, provision of agriculture grants, employment generation grants and tailored trainings is an additional response to facilitate improvement of business and production practices thus leading to poverty reduction and strengthening food security on the household level. Regarding vulnerability criteria and beneficiary selection, generally, PIN will follow recommendations of the FSL Cluster. PIN will continue to prioritize individuals in vulnerable categories, like those with limited access to income opportunities. The following vulnerability list is only indicative: Households/ persons without any income; women-headed households with children, households/persons that do not receive appropriate social benefits for different reasons; chronically ill people and people with disabilities; people over 60 years old; IDPs; and single-headed households/families. All activities described below are to be carried out in close coordination with the FSL Cluster, and other humanitarian organizations operating in eastern Ukraine. PIN is an active member of various coordination structures in Ukraine including several clusters, the Humanitarian Country Team, and the NGO Forum.

Activities & outcomes

Objective 1: Activity 1 Distribution of food vouchers to the conflict affected population to ensure their ability to cover basic needs. This modality will cover 3700 beneficiaries living along the contact line in NGCA (3700 beneficiaries), focusing on settlements in Slavianoserbskiy district of Luhansk region. This activity will be implemented through local vendors capable to provide respective services. The assistance will be delivered in four rounds, three months each, covering 925 beneficiaries per round. Activity 2 Cash for work (GCA) to provide emergency cash injection to 400 beneficiaries in exchange for their engagement in basic works for the benefit of the local community. This activity will involve the work-age beneficiaries from vulnerable groups, falling out of standard humanitarian aid criteria, such as young couples/parents with 1-2 children or people of pre-pension age whose employment opportunities are few or limited due to the conflict. Selected beneficiaries, both IDPs and the local residents (many of whom are returnees), will be organized in work teams and work on restoring the communal infrastructure objects that are of priority for the local community. Objective 2:Activity 1: Provision of agricultural inputs through in kind or voucher mechanism for 200 beneficiaries who live close to the contact line and have basic assets for homestead agriculture activities (the agricultural seasonality is applied while detailed planning of provision). Activity 2: Agricultural trainings for representatives of 200 beneficiary households/families, who live in rural areas and are involved in homestead agriculture to increase their agriculture skills, using new technologies. (implementation period MARCH-DEC) Objective 3: Activity 1: Business and vocational trainings for 300 beneficiaries who live in urban areas to improve efficiency of their self employment activities or job opportunities. This engages on-site trainings, webinars and class trainings to be conducted in JAN-DEC period. Activity 2: Promotion and technical support for collective action by farmers for the joint sale of agricultural products to 80 beneficiaries. (peak in demand observed in SEPTEMBER-OCTOBER) Monitoring and evaluation: The M&E teams of PIN will ensure the applicability, accuracy and efficiency of the activities implemented over the course of the project and in the post intervention/post distribution period. The following M&E processes are sought:Beneficiary Verification: Beneficiaries will be cross-referenced with other humanitarian actors to avoid any risk of overlap. The project will ensure that female headed households, esp. single mothers, elderly (especially elderly isolated women) and households with 3 or more children are prioritized. In NGCA, initial beneficiary lists are provided by de facto authorities, followed by PIN’s needs’ assessmentMonitoring: Regular monitoring activities to be conducted by both management and M&E staff with random beneficiary interviews. Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM), baselines and endline CSI : M&E Units of PIN will carry out PDM surveys reviewing beneficiary experiences on the whole aid process.Accountability and Complaints Mechanism: Pre-distribution information sessions with beneficiaries will be organized in particular on how to use the dedicated hotline and e-mail address that have been established by PIN. A complaints database will be maintained, where all complaints will be categorized on the basis of urgency, relevance and sensitivity/seriousness.
