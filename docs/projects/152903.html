Project Summary

The project will:
1. Improve access to and responsiveness of integrated frontline health services, focusing on the major causes of morbidity and mortality affecting the most vulnerable groups.
2.Prevent, detect and respond to epidemic prone diseases outbreaks through an integrated and multidisciplinary action
3.Promote health services integration with Nutrition, WASH and Protection to enhance intervention effectiveness and its impact on the most vulnerable, strengthening their
resilience by increasing referral to specific services (as those for mental health and disabilities) and offering comprehensive care (combining health and psychosocial services, as in the case of SGBV victims)
The project will be integrated into the ongoing intervention to narrow the gaps in health care provision and to ensure outbreaks and other emergencies impacting on health to be timely and effectively prevented, addressed and contained. The project will therefore contribute to enhancing the resilience of the targeted communities and their capacity to cope with current and future crises

Needs

In Lakes, response to health/nutrition needs is challenged by -internal conflicts, affecting services provision/accessibility, even in the areas closed to town (as shown byJuly 2018 measles outbreak in Rumbek) -IDPs burden over the families they join, whose resources are limited. In Yirol East, IDPs influx from Rumbek North has been followed by measles outbreak (August-September 2018) and GAM increment (above 45% as per June 2018 IRNA)-Inadequate HFs number/distribution (Wide gap for emergency management) -low relevance in Humanitarian Agenda MCH indicators are behind desired threshold, especially for services utilization and quality: ANC4 29% (vs ANC1 74%), DPT3 53% (36% drop out), institutional delivery 28% (47% of which SBA), MDOC attended 12%, HIV test in ANC1 43%. HFs U5 OPD lack of qualified staff. Curative consultation rate is 0.79 (1.48 in U5). Malaria/Diarrhea/Pneumonia are U5 main diseases. Amongst reported diagnosis, 60% Malaria, 22% Diarrhea, 18% Pneumonia. Only 46% malaria cases confirmed. Malaria prevention is improving but more has to be done to have a real impact: 62% ANC clients receive IPT2, 67% LLTN. Nutrition conditions have worsen. In Sept 2018 It has been estimated 73.20% of Lakes State population to be in crises/emergency/catastrophe situation. This % is expected to decrease by March 2019, but remaining above 50% (IPC Sept2018-March2019). Particularly concerning Yirol East and Yirol West Counties (affected by IDPs influx and recurrent outbreaks). In YW, January 2018 SMART survey found GAM 13%. Nutrition services are being scaled up at HFs, but lack of staff and supplies delay affect quality attendance. Growth monitoring is not integrated, resulting in missed opportunities for SAM/MAM identification. CNVs are helping in the HFs, limiting their impact in term on prevention and timely referral at village level. In Western Equatoria, services availability and quality are affected by local conflict. Although many HFs have been reactivated, their full functionality is penalized by the impact looting and damages had on their infrastructures and equipment and part of the staff is still reluctant to come back on duty. This is the case especially for Greater Mundri, where also mobile services are being limited because of the insecurity. The demand for services is increasing, both on preventive (79% ANC1, 64% DPT1) and curative (2.4 OPD Utilization Rate in U5) side, but the risk is the system not being adequate to sustain and enhanced it further. Actually, services utilization is still low (40% ANC4, 51% DPT3), as well as institutional deliveries coverage (30%), with 62% of delivering mothers attended by qualified personnel and therefore concentrated in Hospital and PHCCs, with little assistance provided in the periphery. The area is classified at Crisis, in terms of food security (IPC Sept2018-March2019), but the fragmented and vertical nutrition intervention hamper the response to malnutrition and eventual health complications. In Maridi County, the overall situation is slightly better and all existing health facilities have been reactivated. However, they are facing the same problems in terms of infrastructures, equipment, staffing adequateness and this makes them not really effective in preventing and containing eventual outbreaks. This is particularly concerning, as Maridi is bordering with other Countries, where serious epidemic prone diseases are endemic (ie EVF).Unity State is one of the conflict areas. Panyijar County is safer compared to others and for this reason is particularly affected by IDPs influx, being Nyal the main destination sites. One of the main reasons for people to migrate to Nyal is looking for health services, which are not adequate to cope with keeping increasing demand. This is concerning as 60% of the IDPs are women and children, 40% of coming households are including at least one PLW, 10% a malnourished child and 5% a disabled member (REACH Nyal Port Monitoring Factsheet).

Activities

PHCC/PHCU/County Hospital Package will be provided, reactivating/maintaining existing LKS and WES HFs (selected in line with Cluster priorities), ensuring essential lifesaving services to the most vulnerable groups. Mobile Team package will complement/replace static care, increasing preventive services coverage, offering healthcare when HFs functioning/accessibility will be compromised by insecurity/adverse environment, responding to outbreak/displacements... In Panyijar County (Unity State), Mobile Team package will be preferred, complemented by ICCM/CHW package, bringing key curative supplies at beneficiaries’ closest level where care accessibility is complicated by adverse environment. This all will be realized by-hiring/rewarding/training/supervising local health staff and community based operators-procuring and distributing essential drugs and supplies buffer stock, complementing what provided in kind by government and other agencies-replacing old/ looted equipment and repairing damaged infrastructures, to restore enabling conditions for services provision-ensuring transport means availability and running, to support movements to the fieldThe Focus will be on most common infectious diseases (Malaria, Diarrhea, and Pneumonia) prevention and treatment, EPI coverage boosting, obstetric emergencies prompt referral and attendance. The project will ensure all sites to be adequately provided of supplies needed for preventing, diagnosing and treating the above mentioned diseases and the staff will be trained and supervised on that. EPI will be ensured both at HF level and in the villages, being these latest covered through routine outreaches conducted by health staff and field volunteers, moving by motorbikes. In case of outbreak risk, mass campaigns will be organized. County wide free of charge ambulance service will be permanently guarantee, prioritizing the obstetric cases needing higher level assistance. PHCCs will be put in conditions of operating as BEmONC centers.Supervision/coordination for effective prevention, timely identification of and integrated response to outbreaks through multi-disciplinary approach will be promoted, especially in the bordering areas. In this sense, it will be fundamental the contribution of WASH sector. Local authorities’ surveillance staff and activities will be technically and materially supported, to ensure them to be continuously and consistently conducted. Coordination mechanisms will be put in place, involving all concerned stakeholders and defining roles and responsibilities for concerted actionsFrontline health care will be integrated to prevention, diagnosis and treatment of TB/HIV and health services will complement the nutrition package, ensuring severe acute malnutrition complications being adequately treated. Staff training and sensitization as well as advocacy for all needed supplies constant availability will be ensuredAttention will be given to SGBV cases, to be promptly identified and carefully handled, considering not only physical but also other aspects. Patients will be given adequate clinical attendance and then referred to specialized centers for psychosocial and legal support and follow up. Network with facilities treating mental health and offering specialized care to individuals with disabilities will be strengthen to ensure adequate attendance to concerned people. HWs will be trained and sensitized to respect the dignity and to address the specific needs of these groups of patients.

Messages are tailored for the specific population they are referring to and they are usually defined together with local authorities/community representatives, in order to be adequate for the expected audience. Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

CUAMM always applies a principle of zero tolerance to all forms of exploitation and abuse, especially of a sexual nature, while finalizing a document on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, to be adopted and implemented by all its staff on ground. While waiting for the document to be completed, all CUAMM members are being sensitized and alerted on this matter. All the activities included in this project, in addition to those already implemented, will be informed by the provisions of another project on protection implemented by CUAMM in consortium with another NGO which seeks among other things to address the issues of exploitation and abuse of vulnerable people.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

CUAMM is used to maintain a constant dialogue with local health authorities (State and County level) and with communities representatives. These latest belong to the Health Facilities related Health Committees but also to other community based associations, which are invited to take part to the Quarterly Review Meetings held at County level and which ensure a wider perspective on community needs and expectations. The recently introduced Client Satisfaction Survey offered the opportunity to get feedback on provided services and therefore elements to understand the extent they are considered responsiveness directly from the beneficiaries, representing a small sample of the targeted communities

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

CUAMM has recently introduced a Client Satisfaction Survey Tools to be used to monitor services responsiveness to patients needs and expectations from the beneficiaries point of view. The same tool will be used to get a feedback concerning the services sustained by this project

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

CUAMM is used to involve the Health Committees in the monitoring and evaluation of the Health Facilities they are related to. Health Committees members belong to the communities served by the concerned Health Facilities and they are invited to be part of the monthly supervision conducted by County Health Department and CUAMM. During supervision briefing meeting, the HC members can report about experienced staff attendance and performance, drugs presence and management, equipment and infrastructures adequateness, cleaning, use… During the Quarterly Review Meetings held at County level, community based organization representatives can add their own perception on the same issue, enriching the overall picture CUAMM and local health authorities can get on the provided services, informing following measures and strategy to address the identified problems

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project is designed taking into account the main barriers affecting access to services for certain groups (as women, children, displaced). These barriers have been identified in the past through some qualitative studies conducted in certain counties. Although each County has its own peculiarities, the general findings are considered to be common to various context. Ensuring frontline services are provided free of charge and at the closest possible level to the communities is expected to minimize economical and geographical obstacles, usually affecting the most vulnerable when willing to access the needed assistance. This is realized through the integration of mobile and static health care and the implementation of County wide referral system, activated not only by the HFs but also by the patient. Services integration, not only in terms of different health care component (for instance prevention and curative ones) but also of different sectors (as health with nutrition) reduce the missing opportunities and maximize the impact of a single access to service. Continuous community representatives’ involvement in services planning, implementation and monitoring contributes to improve both services responsiveness/acceptability and people awareness on their importance, reducing fear, suspect and cultural resistance towards institutional care. Women inclusion in the process guarantee its gender sensitiveness and create a window of opportunity for women and men to discuss on health care provision and to jointly realize the reciprocal benefit of promoting women and children access to proper assistance.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project does not directly address inequalities or protection threats, however it will be implemented paying particular attention in ensuring the provided services will be accessible to everyone, without discrimination, especially to the most vulnerable and usually neglected as women, children, displaced… Bringing services closer to the people by adopting mobile team strategy or sustaining facilities in the remote areas will be one of the way to minimize the barriers certain categories of people are facing when looking for assistance. Special cases as patients victims of SGBV or patients with disabilities will be carefully handled, to ensure their dignity and privacy will be respected and to facilitate their access to adequate and comprehensive care, provided with the required expertise and competencies
