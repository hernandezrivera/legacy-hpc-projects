Project Summary

PASERREL II will continue to support both urgent food needs in Mopti region - meeting immediate needs - while supporting the broader recovery of communities through agro-pastoral support. Household requiring urgent food will receive 4-6 months of coverage for the full MEB. Communities will supported with a broad package of agricultural and pastoral support, designed to help increase food self sufficiency. The activities will cover the target cercles of Bankass, Koro, Djenne, Bandiagara and Mopti cercle.

1 - Contexte

According to the HNO, food security remains the highest need across all of Mali, with an estimated 2.3 million persons in need in 2020. Households in eastern Mopti region are no exception to this, as they are increasingly vulnerable to food insecurity due to ongoing conflict, flooding and record levels of regional displacement. As a result of deteriorating livelihoods and an early lean season, it is expected that poor households in Bankass, Koro and Bandiagara, and parts of Djenne will be classified as integrated phase classification (IPC) Phase 2 (Stressed) in the remaining months of 2020, with 456,000 people in total facing Phase 3 (Crisis) conditions. Conflict and worsening food security also exacerbate existing malnutrition and stunting; Mopti region reported the highest prevalence of stunting in 2019, at 34.6%, primarily resulting from inadequate diet and a low score for dietary consumption and diversification. The ongoing conflict and intercommunal violence across the Mopti region is likely to continue this pattern by triggering additional displacement across 2021-22, and is expected to continue to prevent IPDs from returning to areas of origin. Depleted assets and savings as a result of prolonged displacement coupled with a conflict-induced drop in cultivated land will lead to continued food needs.Regarding the latter point, both IDPs and host communities are facing a worsening food security situation in 2020 due to the widespread abandonment or destruction of cultivated land. Recent Geospatial analysis conducted by the Food Security Cluster comparing cultivated land in 2018-9 to past growing seasons found widespread evidence of a reduction of cultivated land, especially in the conflict-affected cercles of Koro, Bankass, and Bandiagara. The satellite-based mapping can be further triangulated with direct reports from Mercy Corps’ field teams, as well as authorities such as Mopti’s Regional Ministry of Agriculture, who reports over 50,000 hectares to have been abandoned or destroyed over the course of 2019 due to conflict. As a result, regular patterns of agricultural labor have been disturbed due to deteriorating ethnic relationships and work has slowed, been relocated closer to the community center, or been taken on increasingly by women. The satellite-based mapping found over one hundred localities in Mopti where cultivated land had significantly shrunk, in that land that had previously covered a radius of up to 10 kilometers from the village in 2016 and 2017, was now only cultivated within 500 meters to 2 kilometers. As shown in the map below, cercles most affected by such losses in 2019 are Koro (25% of villages), Bankass (22%) and Bandiagara (35%). This has continued through 2020, with the increase in security incidents having limited the level of cultivated land by 15-20% in the cercles of Bankass, Koro, and Bandiagara.The conflict-linked reduced production impacts all residents of the target zones for this program, both host and displaced families, and key livelihoods such as livestock rearing. The PASERREL baseline confirmed that livestock rearing remains a key livelihood activity in Mopti, with 42% of households rearing animals - primarily cattle, goats, sheep and poultry. Livestock rearing in Bankass, Koro and Bandiagara was – and remains – especially constrained in 2019-2020 by insecurity, restricted access to normal grazing land and increasing losses due to theft. In the 2018 Mopti assessment, 39.2% of displaced households and 32% of host community households had lost at least one kind of animal normally in their care, with some reporting having lost hundreds of animals. Through coordination with the Food Security Cluster and other actors in Mopti region, Mercy Corps found that the planned lean season support efforts do not adequately cover host-community pastoralist needs.Working in concert with the newly added urgent food activities found in the Food Assistance Sector, PASERREL II will meet emergency food and non-food needs, support the transition of displaced and other vulnerable households to greater states of food self-reliance, and will support the adaptation and strengthening of communities as a whole to changing agriculture cultivation and livestock rearing requirements
