Project Summary

The program will provide life-saving and life-sustaining NFI and shelter assistance to conflict affected households while contributing to household resilience of the most vulnerable families through sustainable housing repairs and rehabilitation of communal shelters, and the provision of repeat, winter fuel assistance for the coldest three months.
Planned activities under this response are summarised below:

Activity: Delivery of essential, life-saving NFI kits to 1,200 households in Idleb, Aleppo and Hama governorates affected by a sudden shock. The intervention area will be informed by the context given this activity will provide timely response to urgent, unanticipated needs, including conflict-related displacement. Fully funded by ECHO from 01 January through 30 June 2019. Implemented in partnership with a Lead Partner. Needs

According to the Whole of Syria Shelter / NFI Humanitarian Needs Overview 2019 (HNO), 4.7 million people are in need of shelter support in Syria due to a lack of available, adequate and affordable shelters and poor conditions in existing shelters. Significant over-crowding is reported in Idleb and north-west Aleppo (targeted by this action), while shelter capacity in Idleb is considered ‘exhausted’ (HNO 2019). Key factors contributing to a worsening of the situation include finite shelter capacity available, the inadequacy of shelter available and the economic barriers to rehabilitating shelters or maintaining rental payments for affected populations (HNO 2019). Approx, 1,130,815 people are in need of shelter assistance in Aleppo governorate / 16.3% of the total population, while Idleb hosts 847,579 PIN of shelter / 11.73% of the total population (hno 2019). Reference to NFI, Aleppo governorate hosts 789.974 PIN / 16.39 % of the population with a further 1,028,750 PIN in Idleb governorate (HNO 2019). According to the 2019 HNO, the sub-districts targeted for shelter assistance have a combined PIN of (Atareb: 78,704, Darret Azza: 43,745 and Ariha: 30,007). The shelter severity score for Ariha and Atareb is 3, while Darret Azza is ranked as 4 in terms of severity of shelter need (HNO 2019). An estimated 4.4 million people require NFI assistance according to the 2019 HNO. While this represents a decline overall from the previous year, pockets of acute need exist in certain areas including the northwest, and for priority groups including newly displaced households, both targeted by this action (HNO 2019). With reference to planned winterization activities, fuel (for heating and cooking) was referenced as a priority NFI need for households sampled during a multi-sector assessment our team completed in targeted areas during June 2018. Although the assessment was completed during the summer months, fuel for heating was still referenced as a priority NFI need by respondents. The same assessment found that food costs represented between 75% and 100% of expenditure for a quarter of the sample, and between 25% to 50% for the remaining respondents, leaving insufficient resource to meet other non-food needs, including NFI, shelter and heating fuel. . A Multi-Sector Needs Assessment completed by our team in targeted areas during Dec 2018 reinforced the need for the proposed programme of activities. The most frequently reported shelter issues by respondents were damage and a lack of climatic protection. While high rental payments were, unsurprisingly, the primary concern for households living in rented accommodation, these households also reported issues with damage and exposure to the climate. The planned housing and communal centre repairs will respond directly to this need by ensuring moderate repairs are completed allowing increased thermal / climatic protection for highly vulnerable households. The coping capacity of conflict affected households is stretched beyond limit, with 83% of the households surveyed by our team during this assessment reporting that they were in-debt. The majority of debt taken was used to meet fuel and NFI needs in addition to food. Unsurprisingly, 80% of respondents indicated that they needed extra money to meet winter related needs. More worryingly, a significant percentage of the sample size reference using potentially harmful coping strategies, including reducing the number of meals eaten in any given day and purchasing food on credit (76%).

Implementation of activities

Proposed emergency response NFI activities will be implemented by us directly. Current winterization assistance has enabled us to successfully access and initiate operations in Afrin district of northern Aleppo. This in turn expands the geographic area we can access for emergency response. The emergency response component will benefit from well-established systems and monitoring process designed to ensure targeted, timely assistance to populations affected by sudden shock and not targeted by other actors. Proposed shelter assistance by our organization will target Ariha, Daret Azza and Atareb sub-districts which the 2019 HNO ranks highly in terms of severity of need. To address economic barriers to winter proofing homes and communal centres, an owner driven repair process will allow beneficiaries to identify the most urgent priorities and corresponding works to be completed by local contractors under the supervision of our organization's engineers and the beneficiary. Results from satisfaction surveys completed by our team with housing repair beneficiaries under an OFDA funded shelter show that repairs addressed the most urgent needs in relation to shelter conditions for 98% of the 197 beneficiaries sampled. While our organization has not implemented shelter activities during 2018, this successful earlier shelter rehab experience is complemented with extensive rehabilitation activities under 2018 and current WASH and Education programming. This means well-established systems and significant technical expertise can be applied to planned shelter rehabilitation, including development of scope of works, contract management and technical checklists and monitoring processes to ensure technical quality and beneficiary / user satisfaction and relevance to needs. Current winterization activities funded by ECHO and SDC are being implemented in Afrin district, Aleppo at the time of writing. Planned 2019/20 winterization activities will be agreed in coordination with the SNFI cluster and members to address gaps and particular vulnerabilities and to ensure a harmonized and cohesive response. Protection Risk Analysis

The project will follow cross-cutting protection mainstreaming guidelines as per the protection cluster guidelines, including collection of sex and age disaggregation of information, participation, access, and an understanding of how different needs will be met. To reduce risks or harm to vulnerable populations in the face of conflict sensitivity, beneficiaries in Syria will be involved in all stages of the project cycle wherever possible – for example, needs and resource identification, design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. Gender based violence disproportionately affects women and girls, especially those displaced or recently returned. This was highlighted in the draft 2019 HNO. This project will work with female and male leaders and members of the affected community to ensure that the distributions are equitable and gender sensitive, for example having separate lines and ensuring that any clothing is gender appropriate. Wherever possible and culturally acceptable, female beneficiaries meeting the selection criteria will be prioritized for cash programs. As highlighted by the 2019 draft HNO, the loss of documents are a key protection risk. This will be mitigated by using a community council registration and community verification mechanism. Our organization designs its projects with an emphasis on meaningful access, safety, dignity and non-discrimination so that all potential beneficiaries will benefit from inclusive service provision to the greatest extent possible. Moreover, by applying lessons learned from previous interventions, we will engage with local councils, relief committees and other key actors to mitigate risks faced by women and children and to build an environment that guarantees basic human rights.A second critical protection risk highlighted by the draft HNO 2019 was that of housing land and property (HLP) occurring in 47% of survey households. This has an impact on the shelter programme. Within HLP issues, disputed ownership is described as a major concern, reported by 61% of the respondents in the assessed communities. To mitigate these risks a preliminary beneficiary selection list will be compiled and verified through door to door visits to ensure household eligibility and technical criteria for repairs are met. The team will complete door to door visits with all pre-identified beneficiaries. All required assessments will be completed within specifics shelter visits (damage level assessment, HLP assessment, and shelter technical assessment). During this activity, our Shelter staff will assess the land and housing ownership in accordance with HLP guidelines. We will work with local councils to ensure that HLP guidelines are adhered to with regard to the collective centre. Our team will work closely with Local Councils and other administrative bodies to ensure open and accountable feedback mechanisms for the affected population. Our team utilizes face-to-face interviews with the affected population in all areas of operation. All feedback is registered, shared and used to improve programming. If there are any specific issues raised, our staff will follow up with the concerned party and ensure that the feedback loop is completed. A second feedback method is a WhatsApp number for registering any complaints or issues. This is managed by the M&E officer and will provide a direct link with the field to ensure transparency, while also allowing for a way to handle complaints and feedback which is used to improve programming. Our organization also requires that all staff sign the organisation’s code of conduct, which includes the obligation to report abuse by staff or beneficiaries, including misuse of assets or funds. We have strict protocols against any sexual relations with minors or beneficiaries of any age.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Consideration of the highly volatile situation within Syria has been built into program design. Real-time analysis of the security situation will continue to allow for rapid adjustments to planned activities as necessary. This can include temporary suspension of field activities during times of extreme conflict. During such times, our field teams employ various techniques to maintain communication and support to project stakeholders and beneficiaries. In line with well established operating procedures, distributions will be placed on temporary hold during periods of heightened risk to staff and beneficiaries. Our organization employs multiple measures to maintain delivery assistance, which can include door-to-door distributions, or phased distribution times to reduce the risks of large numbers of people gathering. We are in the process of finalizing updated Framework Agreements with NFI suppliers. Reliability and ability to consistently deliver has been considered when assessing bids, which will mitigate against the risk of supply chain breaks delaying assistance delivery. Should supply chain breaks be experienced due to border closures or other, unanticipated events, we hav the ability to immediately transition from in-kind assistance to conditional NFI or fuel vouchers to ensure targeted beneficiaries can maintain access essential NFIs. We continually updating its contingency plans to adapt to the changing context in NWS, these plans detail various scenarios and required actions for mitigating risks, including operational and programmatic changes. Risks beyond our control could include: shifts in administrative powers; heightened insecurity restricting access; targeting of aid organisations or staff. We have dedicated Security staff that closely monitor and advise on the dynamic, continually evolving security situation. Additionally, we utilize an internal Risk Register as a tool to track, analyze, and mitigate organizational risks. Under this project, one of the highest potential risks is violence at distribution points, which will be managed by carefully planning distribution timing and location, as above. All field staff are well trained and incidents trackers are maintained to identify trends and flashpoints. As referenced above, our organization has recently commenced operations in Afrin district, Aleppo. This will allow us to respond to need in these areas should significant incoming displacement be experienced as a result of further, possible conflict in Idleb and western Aleppo. Securing this access also allows us to re-programme planned FSL assistance to areas in northern Aleppo should security prevent ongoing operations in Idleb, Hama or western Aleppo.
