Project Summary

Severe food insecurity has continued to undermine and nearly collapsed Livelihood in areas where the population are classified in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) and has increased over years in Jonglei state; Bor south county, Lakes state; Cuiebet, Rumbek East, Rumbek Center and Rumbek North; Central Equatorial; Yei, Lainya, Kajo keji, Morobo, Budi, Kapoeta South targeting the host vulnerable households, IDPs and returnee’s communities with severe food and nutrition needs in the counties. With heavy local presence, ongoing projects and as a static in those Locations; TITI Foundation aims to target the most vulnerable people with Nutrition, Food security and Livelihood response to increase agricultural production and build community resilience for the vulnerable food insecure households through a multi sector integrated response to prevent catastrophic consequences.
Due to the protracted crisis and recurrent shocks across most of the counties which include: an intensification of conflict that resulted in loss of life and assets, displacement, disruption and destruction of livelihoods; a second consecutive year of exceptional high floods caused livestock death, submerged human settlements, farmlands and pasturelands as well as disrupted markets and delivery of humanitarian assistance to the flood-affected populations; the continuing and worsening macro-economic crisis that is resulting in food price hikes due to the devaluation of the South Sudanese Pound; and the indirect effects of COVID-19 that have disrupted livelihoods as well as slowed and restricted the flow and delivery of both commercial and humanitarian supplies and services, the situation was further exacerbated by reported incidences of fall armyworm and desert locusts that further reduced crop yields, the projected Conflict likely to increase in pastoral areas has increased competition for resources and is expected to start during this period are a threat to Food Security and Livelihood.
TITI Foundation in response to the ongoing emergency will improve the general availability of food and strengthen vulnerable communities’ ability to withstand future threats associated with massive displacements. The project will continue to promote alternative farming techniques through capacity building of contact farmers and farmers groups on conservation agriculture and value chain addition to increase crop production coverage and minimize production costs. Farmers groups will be organized and supported to continue developing community food banks, gunny bags gardening and kitchen garden to enhance food security for the vulnerable households. This will assist them have capacity to process farm produce and identify potential markets for their farm produce.
TITI Foundation will work with strategic partners to provide inputs including early maturing vegetable seeds and tools to farmers in the target locations with an aim to restore food security, production of vegetable variety, fishing to enhance nutritional diversification and production capacity while maintaining high levels of accountability to affected persons through participatory engagement. In addition, TITI Foundation will support FES (fuel efficient stoves and briquettes), support the animal health in the pastoral communities, Provide food assistance, this will limit house hold food expenses and easing the burden. TITI Foundation will also ease pressure on the elderly, women, Girls, PWD to acquire Psychosocial support and child headed household’s protection, GBV.
During the project implementation, TITI Foundation (MEAL) teams will conduct field visits and progress reports in the selected locations. This will involve key informants interviews in the various communities and focus group discussions to determine the efficiency and effectiveness of the intervention.

1 - Needs

October 2020 to July 2021 IPC reported the most severe acute food insecurity conditions in counties where chronic vulnerabilities have been exacerbated by a combination of shocks, including floods, insecurity incidents, the macro-economic crisis, and the effects of COVID-19 measures. Between October and November 2020, 34 counties across the country were classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4. Of the remaining counties, 51 were classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, and 12 were classified in Stressed (IPC Phase 2) acute food insecurity. In the first projection period of December 2020 to March 2021, 22 counties are classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, with the number likely to increase to 33 counties in the second projection period of April to July 2021. About 1.4 million children under five years are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition in 2021 based on same season historical data of food security and nutrition monitoring system, SMART nutrition surveys and admission trends for 2020. 2 - Project activities

• Identification, registration and Verification of vulnerable HHs amongst IDPs, returnees and host communities as primary beneficiaries of the project in Jonglei, Lakes, Central Equatoria and Eastern Equatoria state in consultation with other stakeholders like the community leaders and the local authorities; • Distribution of seeds, tools and fishing gears to vulnerable households based on FAO technical standards. • Rapid training of beneficiaries on vegetable farming in Jonglei, Lakes, Central Equatoria and Eastern Equatoria states; Support Contact farmer’s feed disseminate key production information and submit reports; Training of contact farmers on conservation Agriculture and value addition;• Facilitate establishment of community food banks, introduction of appropriate preservation technologies e.g. hematic storage bags to HH., Support contact farmers and farmers groups trainings on post-harvest handling• Link producer groups with partners supporting storage commodities and initiatives.• Provide livelihood support through improved market access, provision of seeds, fruit tree seedlings and tools (farm inputs) to stimulate production back to surplus levels, particularly in the greenbelt. • Maintain support to small scale subsistence producers in locations with less agricultural potential and include animal health support. This should also include emergency nutrition, especially during the lean season.• In collaboration with SSMoA, Food and Agriculture Organization for United Nation (FAO) and partners support Vulnerable Household to maintain food production through establishment of kitchen gardens and application of other land husbandry method as well as linkages to markets.• self-selected producer groups formed as a platform for transferring improved agricultural technologies and making stronger market linkages. Agricultural training modules include social cohesion, gender, and conflict and nutrition sensitivity.• Recruitment of Field Extension Agents (FEAs) equipped with modern agricultural education and skills.• Training of Community Based trainers (CBTs) as TOT on VSLAs.• Cascade training of community beneficiary groups on VSLAs.• Monitoring of small enterprises and support for small startups to strengthen markets.• Support sensitization for Pregnant, breastfeeding mothers and caregivers living with under 5 to address barriers to good nutrition during the monthly feedback meetings. • The project will support vulnerable households with food supplies and severely malnourished will be linked for/supported with food by prescription.• Setting up of mobile Growth Monitoring out post, Cooking demonstration and education on complementary feeding using commonly available foods. .• Efforts to reduce malnutrition should include the broader goals of improving knowledge related to childhood nutrition and IYCF practices and health seeking behavior. • Sensitize and Link youth, women and adult GBV survivors on Development of IGA’s. • Monitoring and Evaluation, will be conducted throughout the project cycle.Cross cutting activities:• TITI will conduct assessments with support from local community leaders, Local Authorities, women, People with disability and displaced community leaders. • Mainstream GBV and supporting the implementation of PSEA to safeguard the vulnerable groups from any form of abuse throughout all levels of response. Continued implementation of the peace agreement and addressing the root causes of insecurity especially across Jonglei, Lakes. • Scale up and improve access to basic services, including WASH and health service delivery throughout the year

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Besides, on a broader base, participatory evaluations are used by including the various authorities and group representatives through project implementation phases to collect complaints, advises and resentments which can be used for accountability to the grassroots and staff using the FSL cluster AAP checklist is also used to improve on engagements process.Through Titi Foundations already existing code of conduct for the staff on zero tolerance to prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) we are able to prevent staff from indulging in activities that can lead to protection threats during project implementation. Also Titi Foundation has readily available hotlines, help desks, complaint committees which consist of men, women, youth, child headed households, and people living with disabilities and local authorities to address gender equality and through the various means beneficiaries can reach out to higher authorities of the organization to report any possible protection threats in the cause of project implementation. And also for the representatives and Titi Foundation staff to be more accountable to affected populations so as to mitigate any protection threats.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

All of Titi Foundations Local Monitoring Groups are inclusive of different population groups, to ensure that the staff are receiving feedback from even the most vulnerable members of a community. The establishment of the complaint desk with a focal person allows for people who are not able to utilize the suggestions box to also provide feedback. The Local Monitoring Groups also consult with the broader community, and are able to advocate on behalf of their most vulnerable members. Titi Foundation staff also consult with communities to identify vulnerable members to ensure that they have access to the program’s activities and services.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

At the inception of the project Titi will provide sensitisations explaining details of the programme, community involvement in the targeting process, what assistance beneficiaries are entitled to and the CRM. Following beneficiary selection, beneficiaries will be consulted on design of Nutrition and FSL kits to ensure materials are appropriate to the needs. Stakeholders and community feedback meeting and sessions will provide avenues for sharing of the findings and development of solutions that are long lasting, this will be used as learning avenues that will strengthen the relationship of the program at the community level.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Titi Foundation has established Local Monitoring Groups in each of the locations that it works in, to ensure that in addition to M&E mechanisms, community members are able to provide regular feedback on project assistance. Titi Foundation values and takes seriously all of the feedback it receives through these mechanisms, and uses it to inform the design of projects and activities where possible. This may include changing the location of activities to increase accessibility, assisting in the identification of referral services needed, or other mitigating measures to reduce risk or harm to beneficiaries, while also ensuring all projects are inclusive and accessible. Any complaints made against Titi Foundation’s staff or programming will also initiate an internal review and investigation, in alignment with the organizations policies and code of conduct.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

• Risks that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and servicesTiti will conduct protection assessments and risk analysis, stakeholders mapping, and needs verification with clear AGD data to understand context dynamics, protection risks, vulnerability level and inform the WASH, S/NFI, FSL, Nutrition and Education responses plan based on needs, access, safety, and gap findings, this will be done through focus group discussions, key informant interviews, meetings and HH surveys with women, men, children, institution workers, community leaders, within IDPs, host community and returnee population. • Risks of women, girls and boys without and with disabilities exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance.TITI has integrated the SEA in its HR manual and an orientation/ sensitization done to each staff, Volunteers and suppliers as must. Where the rights have been violated an immediate action is taken to ensure the beneficiaries are protected throughout. • Risks that the project does not have sufficient internal mechanisms to combat and prevent child safeguarding and PSEA.Titi Foundation is committed to the principles of quality of service delivery and responsibilities of the beneficiaries on our project. This means beneficiaries are fully consulted and informed about the benefits of such projects. It allows room to discuss, speak and analyses what short and long term benefits such a project may offer them this is encouraged to allow the targeted beneficiaries and communities to be able to own the project throughout the project cycle. Therefore, all Titi Foundation humanitarian funded projects are guided by the humanitarian principles of ‘Do No-Harm’ and This allows Titi foundation to use its Human Resource policies guiding staff behavior, PSEA code of conduct which has been signed by all staff to abide by. This therefore will also guide the employees in their daily interaction with beneficiaries in the field.• Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.).Based on the considerable protection risks in the programme locations, protection will be considered at all stages of the programme cycle. South Sudan ranks 187/189 countries in the 2017 HDI. The country loses 36.3% when the value is discounted for inequality (HDI reduced from 0.388 to 0.247 ). This figure reflects a strong patriarchal society and culture in South Sudan; and traditional gender roles subjected to women play secondary roles in the community. This places them at a disadvantage with household decision-making and this disempowerment exposes women and girls to several risks including early/forced marriage and sexual and domestic violence. While the laws in South Sudan provide for the protection of women and girls against discrimination, abuse and GBV, they are often misinterpreted or ignored. In 2017, the Protection Cluster reported 3,585 GBV cases (95% female, 19% children); an increase of 35% compared to 2016. This in particular was in locations affected by conflict. In PoCs, overcrowding, often up to 3 families living in the same shelter, can lead to increased tension and violence along with lack of privacy for women and children increasing the risk of GBV. There are often large numbers of female headed households where women have to take additional roles and their needs will need to be considered such as whether as per social norms they can access land for farming and markets for products.
