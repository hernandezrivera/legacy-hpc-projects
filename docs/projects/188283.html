Project Summary

As the crisis continues and schools are destroyed or inaccessible to students, COVID 19 has been an added threat to them as the virus tends to spread quickly when in large crowds and so the schools that can be found are not equipped to take in students. The objective of this project is designed to contribute to HRP strategic objective 3 increase resilience and access to services through increased safe and equitable availability and access to quality education reaching total beneficiaries 13,239 (12,920 children and 319 teachers and educational personnel). A focus will be on the IDPs in camps in Idleb and Aleppo governorates( 100 % of targeted children will be in within severity 3, 4 and 5.), through formal and non-formal education programs, and inclusive Support for 8 schools and 5 non-formal education centers. the project will contribute to Help to create a safer and more secure environment for children. The project will focus on children aged 5-7 years in and out of school by providing set of services that targets the gaps and needs of the most vulnerable groups while ensuring the centrality of protection.
MF is keening to achieve the following:
Increase access 12,920 Children (6520 girls & 6400 boys,150 CwD, 5 – 17 years old) affected by conflict in NW Syria to education services, by providing formal and non-formal education programs, divided as following: 10600 children will attend formal education classes in 8 schools and 2320 children will attend non-formal education ( ALp & remedial program) for two cycles in 5 non-formal education centers in line with the distribution of school bags, also operational cost and other needed expenses will be provided, MF will conduct 13 BTL campaigns, focusing on encouraging both students and their parents to get back to schools.
Increase access 12,920 Children (6520 girls & 6400 boys,150 CwD, 5 – 17 years old) to a
clean, sterile and safe environment in 13 schools and education centers in NW Syria, by conducting light-scale rehabilitation 104 classes due to usage during passing last academic year (e.g: one window
in every class, door, desk, etc), ensuring accessibility for CWDs, providing heating materials—Fuel-based heater and fuel— and conducting precautionary measures in schools such as measuring temperature regularly, social distancing and distribution of masks in those targeted schools.
Improve the quality of educational services within a safe and protective learning environment, by conducting a training program for 319 teachers ( 159 female ,160 male). on PSS,EIE,INEE, educational subjects, child-centered and protective pedagogy, and PSEA. Trained teachers will reflect on their in-school attitude and will enhance relations and communications with students. Also, incentives will be provided for teachers and educational personnel.
The inclusion of CWD will take place throughout most of the project activities, for
example, the rehabilitation will take into consideration CWD's needs, as well as training for staff will
be conducted on how to deal with children with disabilities.
MF will ensure the participation of all relevant stakeholders at all stages of the operations
management cycle. MF will be responsible for the design, implementation, monitoring and
evaluation (M&E), and closure phases. MF will manage the project, provide the contractual
services, ensure the required coordination among clusters, and ensure high-quality results
and efficient progress through the provision of capable programmatic specialized staff.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2022, an estimated 981,393 children are PIN (severity 3-5) in the targeted sub-district under this project. With an estimated 18% of school age children not attending any form of learning, out of school children remain one of the most affected groups.School dropout exposes children to protection threats such as child labor (mainly for boys) and early marriage (mainly for girls).The protracted nature of the conflict, economic duress and the Covid-19 pandemic,continue to impact overstretched education services, attacks affecting schools remain high.The formal system is unable to absorb and retain all school age children. Many students continue to learn in unconducive and/or unsafe learning environments.The highest ratios are found in Idleb (1:178) Over a third of schools operate in shifts with Idleb triple and quadruple shifting to address overcrowding and physical distancing.MF through this project aims to address the educational needs by comprehensive education services to Increase safe and equitable availability and access to quality education by: providing NFE for 2320 children who are drop out and at risk of dropping out. As well as provide FE to 10600 children with Schools supplies and operating costs for 13 schools ,provide training and incentives for 319 teachers to ensure quality education, light rehabilitation is needed for 104 classroom to make the safe and qualifiedSchools especially for CwDs

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will launch upon signing a MoU with the ED.10600 children(6520 girls,6400 boys) will be targeted by FE, The face to-face modality will be adopted in this project, given that the precautionary COVID responses which are encouraging social distancing measures, MF field team will limit the hourly classes to be as short as 30 minutes with increasing the frequency of each class to contain small group in each classroom. 1160 children who are out of schools (580 girls& 580 boy) will attend ALp program and 1160 children who are at risk of dropping out (580 girls& 580 boy) will attend remedial classes through two cycles the NFE will work to refer children to FE after completion of cycle.At the beginning of the project a training program will be conducted for 319 teachers (159 females, 160 males) for three days as well as 104 classrooms will be rehabilitated.MF has already designed monitoring and evaluation tools that ensure accountability of the affected populations, also it has provided beneficiaries with these tools. Monitoring and evaluation will be in line with all project phases, starting from initiation until the close-out phase.The monitoring tools that will be used: surveys, on-site observation reports, meetings with community key informants, interviews, and pictures.Besides,M&E staff has established many channels for complaints mechanism.MF will directly implement the activities since MF has full access to the targeted location.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Learning facilities could be attacked.*Mitigation: To reduce the risk of a military-related attack learning facilities can be deconflicted using the deconfliction mechanism administered by UN-OCHA, in addition to ensuring all schools have an evacuation plan in place to be prepared to any unforeseen hostilities.Teachers or other education personnel participate in physical punishment, act violently, bully and neglect students.*Mitigation:Learning facilities should have an enforced code of conduct. The code of conduct shouldcover the conduct of teachers and other education personnel that enables learning spacesto being welcoming, inclusive safe, violence-free and promote well-being and a protectiveenvironment.Children are over-exposed to the risk of, or experience, harm by adults suchas teachers, school personnel, organization staff, other adults or older students.*Mitigation: Child Safeguarding polices and related systems should be part of anyeducation programing and should be regularly monitored and corrective action takenwhen needed. Aid workers or associated people use their power or influence to sexuallyexploit or abuse the people they should be serving, particularly children. The risk may beheightened for children with disabilities, those unaccompanied, orphans and youngerchildren.*Mitigation: Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)policiesshould be in place.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policies took in place, practiced and monitored. Polices cover standards of behavior for the organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and include accountability measures. Polices address risks at the organizational level and the program level.MF will Implement training on PSEA to be mandatory for all personnel. Periodically, with the support of Field Managers, Focal Points organize a training session for recruited staff, ideally within three months of recruitment. MF will incorporate an ‘attitudes and beliefs’ session in the training as many respondents highlighted this as the key obstacle to reporting.MF is a member of PSEA network so when it comes to sensitive complaints they are usually referred to PSEA focal point persons, which are the HR manager and M&E manager, therefore they deal with such cases in high confidentiality.Besides MF conducted trainings on PSEA as for all its personnel.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Target schools are not threatened or occupied by armed groups, always community people are the safe place to go when facing problems with armed groups.The risk of having difficulties in purchasing the needed materials from the local markets,therefore MF will make sure to get the needed materials ready for the required activities in advance, so we could avoid such situations, not to mention our own past experiences inthe field in the same areas, helped us to find new ways and methods to minimize the risks;MF has very good community acceptance. All these conditions will play an important role to pave the way for children (girls, boys) and youth to have access to education;MF coordinates with the education ministry in unsupported school selection. - as the situation is stabilized and no displacement, the parents will not prevent children from school enrollment. Schools are open, and no Covid-19 virus outbreakRisk1:Interference by local authoritiesLikelihood: Possible.
