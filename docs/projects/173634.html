Project Summary

Across Sudan, girls and boys and their families face risks associated with conflict, violence and displacement including family separation, Sexual Gender-based Violence (SGBV), mine risk, psychological distress, and child labour.
Save the Children will ensure the provision of timely and rights-based child protection services to the most deprived and marginalized girls and boys, especially those affected by conflict and displaced from their homes. These services will include Family Tracing and Reunification (FTR) for Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC); prevention and response to the needs of child victims of Sexual and Gender Based violence (SGBV); material support, vocational training, support services, and life skills training for extremely vulnerable children and adolescents affected by conflict; and safe places for play, recreation and the provision of psychosocial support to children displaced or affected by conflict.
To prevent and mitigate child protection risks in communities affected by conflict and displacement, Save the Children will strengthen the capacity of social workers, teachers, and law-enforcement staff, as well as investing in capacity building of the National Council on for Child Welfare and other humanitarian partners. In order to reinforce the resilience of communities and strengthen capacity of child protection institutions, Save the Children will support community based child protection mechanisms and civil society organisations to disseminate information on relevant services and contribute to advocacy and the referral of child protection cases; and to promote safer environments for children.

1 - Needs

Although armed conflict has reduced as a whole in the period from 2019 to 2020, violence against civilians has actually risen. In Darfur region especially, intercommunal conflicts between sedentary farming communities and nomadic-pastoral communities over access to, use of, and management of resources continue to cause insecurity resulting in new displacements. Children affected by fighting, particularly those displaced, remain exposed to various protection risks such as exposure to violence, family separation, deteriorating living conditions and recruitment by armed actors. These risks have a devastating impact on their well-being, physical security and future prospects with girls often bearing the heaviest brunt of the conflict, and more at risk of specific risks such as sexual violence. Displaced populations, whether displaced by conflict or natural disaster, often are forced to flee with little or no belongings, and face challenges in accessing life-saving and life-sustaining services, whilst often suffering from additional protection risks. This is compounded by limited access to income opportunities and prevalence of child labour, early marriage and other child protection issues as well as SGBV risks. Although theoverall security situation in Sudan slightly improved in 2017 with an estimated 386,000IDPs voluntarily returning to their places of origin across the country, an estimated 1.6million IDPs are still living in camps. In Darfur region, despite this improvement, intercommunal conicts between sedentary farming communities and nomadic-pastoralcommunities over access to, use of, and management of resources, especially landcontinue, to cause insecurity resulting in new displacements. This coupled with limitedeconomic opportunities has increased the strain on the available resources to cater for thehost, IDPs and the returnees. In addition to the protracted conflict, Sudan has beenexperiencing an influx of refugees from neighboring countries, poverty and deterioratingeconomic situation. With an estimated 8,500 refugees from Chad living in two sites inCentral Darfur, it is resultantly decreasing the capacity of the host communities to supportthe population needs including the much-needed protection services.

2 - Activities/Outputs

1. Provision of material support (based on the need) to girls and boys identified with protection concerns. Referral of girls and boys with identified needs to medical, legal support and other services.2. Provision of psychosocial support to girls and boys identified with protection concerns or psychosocial distress through regular age-appropriate and gender-sensitive psychosocial activities. Referral of girls and boys to specialized MHPSS support and formal counselling services.3. Training of social workers (from government and other agencies) and interim caregivers on case management, UASC case management and CP information management system (CPIMS).4. Identification and registration of unaccompanied and separated children (UASCs), including conducting Best Interest Assessment and Determination processes to registered UASCs.5. Support to the establishment and functioning of Community-based Child Protection Networks (CBCPNs) including training of members of the CBCPNs on how to prevent, identify and support child survivors of violence and abuse, and on referral mechanisms.6. Training and capacity building support to social workers, law-enforcement staff and health-service providers on child rights, child protection, child safeguarding, and child-appropriate response to SGBV cases.7. Strengthening of the reporting and referral mechanism that is safe and confidential for girls and boys who experienced sexual violence. Provision of gender-sensitive and age-appropriate support, care services and life skills training to girls and boys SGBV survivors.8. Child Protection and SGBV community outreach activities.9. Support the establishment and operation of Child Friendly Spaces (CFS) to work as a safe space for provision of structured MHPSS and recreational activities.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project is designed to consider protection issues and concerns for those that havespecific needs or higher levels of vulnerability. Protection programming aimsspecifically to support access to services, increase safety and uphold the dignity ofidentified vulnerable children and women. Protection mainstreaming is also integratedacross all project activities. To mainstream gender in this project,the IASC Guidelines for integrating Gender-based Violence interventions inHumanitarian Settings are adopted. There will be a reference document for ensuringquality in planning and coordination and staff will be trained on the guidelines. Savethe Children will strive to ensure that the proposed interventions will minimizeunforeseen or unintended negative effects on the targeted population which couldpotentially expose them to further physical and psychosocial risks. Gender, age anddisability sensitive measures will be adopted, including adapting meeting/serviceschedules and meeting venues to ensure equitable participation, using single sexdiscussion groups, considering ways to support the dignity and privacy ofbeneficiaries, and ensuring facilities are physically accessible to all. As appropriate to the specific context, activities to support highly vulnerable individuals or those that may be targeted by members of their communities, will be organised in a way to reduce further stigmatisation or exploitation, e.g. providing services within multi-purpose centres, with internal private spaces, may better serve SGBV survivors as they are less identifiable. The protection awareness-raising and capacity-building activities will enhance thecapacity of communities through formation and training of community-basedstructures to understand the protection risks and differential needs of children andother vulnerable people within the communities. The project considers aims tobe conflict sensitive by analysing the situation, examining how each activity mayinfluence conflict dynamics and working to maximize the positive and minimize thenegative impacts of the proposed interventions. In particular, risks. Save the Children will work closely with multiple stakeholders (community members, government partners, local NGOs) to avoid exacerbating existing community tensions. Effort will be made to consider and respond to existing power dynamics within communities. Protection staff will work alongside teams from other sectors to raise awareness on protections issues and mainstream protection across all programming, for example supporting training on referral pathways, conducting protection audits, and advising on actions to make non-protection specific programming accessible and appropriate for all depending on age, gender and disability. All Save the Children projects are assessed according to a gender and age marker prior to proposal submission, and efforts made to promote projects that are gender transformative.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Stakeholders’ participation and accountability to affected people is part of Save the Children's core values and Save the Children is a signatory of the Core Humanitarian Standards. Save the Children has established mechanisms for ensuring accountability to affected populations, for gaining feedback and for responding to complaints including safeguarding and abuse concerns. Save the Children Sudan will ensure SCI AAP guidelines are followed and applied as one of key quality programming measures. SC will provide regular, timely and accessible information to targeted communities about: who we are and the principles we adhere to; project interventions and what SCI intends to deliver; our staff and how we expect our staff to behave (including our Child Safeguarding policy and Code of Conduct); and how communities can be involved. MEAL and project staff ensure information on the projects are sharedwith beneficiaries and stakeholders to strengthen community participation and enable beneficiaries to provide constructive feedback that will improve performance and quality of implemented interventions, during needs assessment and throughout the intervention period. Information will be communicated in the local language and medium that are easily understood, respectful and culturally appropriate for different members of the targeted communities, especially vulnerable and marginalized groups. Save the Children adapts feedback mechanisms so that they are appropriate for engagement by children of different ages and genders. Save the Children use different means to share information with and gain feedback from beneficiaries including community meetings/ sessions and focus group discussions with specific groups, IEC materials and mass communication modalities (Posters, Banners, Social Media and Radio Messaging), feedback boxes, and complaints desks. Additionally, Save the Children Sudan has a dedicated accountability number for beneficiaries or their representatives to share their feedbackor raise complaints. Complaints may also be received in person directly to staff. All feedback or complaints received through the different accountability mechanisms will be categorized based on SC agreed categories and accordingly shared with relevant staff for resolution. All received cases will be documented on the CO Action Plan Tracker for follow up. Cases on the Action Plan Tracker will not be closed unless feedback is provided to feedback provider or complainant. All feedback from communities is dealt with in confidentiality. Any serious complaints (including child safeguarding concerns, sexual abuse and exploitation concerns and breaches of the Code of Conduct) will be immediately elevated to the designated focal point (e.g. child safeguarding focal point, HR Manager, Director for Program Development & Quality, Country Director). Save the Children will also participate and engage with interagency feedback mechanisms available at local and national levels.
