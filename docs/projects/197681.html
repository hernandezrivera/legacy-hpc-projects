Project Summary

This project aims to improve on the psychosocial wellbeing of conflict-affected teenage mothers and their children (2000 teenage mothers, aged 12 to 19, 2500 children, aged 0 to 5) through life skills and vocational Education to build resilience and provides them with stability, structure, and hope for the future. TeenAlive Association seeks to achieve this through:
a) Case Management for teenage mothers and their children will help strengthen the referral pathway. TeenAlive will ensure the provision of psychological first aid, mental health and psychosocial support, and vocational training to 75 conflict affected teenage mothers and their children in Mezam, Boyo and Fako Divisions; as well as other services provided by TeenAlive. This will be managed by experts in MHPSS, GBV, Child Protection, Case Management and trained social workers, by 2023.
b) Production of civil documents to conflict-affected teenage mothers and children; production of 200 birth certificates to conflict-affected teenage mothers, 200 birth certificate to conflict-affected children of teenage mothers; production of 200 national identity card to conflict-affected teenage mothers, 100 identity card to conflict-affected teenage boys. All this will help the children gain access to nationality, prevent trafficking, prevent arbitrary arrest/ease movement of young persons, and help them access basic services by2023
c) Running of Safe Spaces for conflict-affected teenage mothers, which will lead to the creation of 06 Teenage Mothers Clubs in communities. Through these safe spaces, teenage mothers will enjoy the freedom to express themselves without fear or judgement or harm. This is to build the resilience of 300 teenage mothers through discussion of their life challenges, coping mechanism, skill acquisition and develop a good mental health and wellbeing by 2023.
d) Economic empowerment of teenage mothers and other girls/women through TeenAlive Vocational Training Center to empower 25 teenage mothers and other vulnerable girls into traditional embroidery, beads designing and provision of startup up capital to beneficiaries by 2023.
e) Train 100 teachers on comprehensive sex education where emphasis will be made on sex education, sexual reproductive health right, and menstrual hygiene/sanitation. This will enable them pass on the knowledge to their pupils and students.
f) Train 80 community protection Focal points on Child Protection/Gender Based Violence in Mezam, Boyo and Fako Divisions, where emphasis will be made on the identification, protection mainstreaming, and reporting of child abuse/GBV cases such as rape, sexual assault, domestic violence, etc.
g) Train 300 teenage mothers acquire knowledge on life skills on positive parenting training in Mezam, Boyo and Fako Division. This will help girl mothers benefit from skills needed to rais health, safe and resilient children.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The crisis which has engulfed the North West and South West regions of Cameroon since late 2016 have left many teenage girls aged 13 to 19 vulnerable. Teenage age is a transitional phase of growth and development between childhood and adulthood. It can also be referred to as the stage of maturation in which they undergo physical, psychological and social changes. Teenagers face some common problems which includes; unwanted pregnancy, exposure to substance abuse, peer influence and issues related to child abuse, forced labor and career orientation. There is a constant need for teenagers to be educated on these societal issues and how to mitigate the harms on them. During adolescence skills and information that will inform our entire lives are gained. In times of crisis, development is restricted and decisions made (particularly for teenage girls at risk of child marriage and teenage pregnancy) that will determine young lives. The lack of education, in terms of basic skills, qualifications and vocational opportunities missed, compounded by a lack of access to sexual and reproductive health education and resources will follow them into the future. It is worth noting that the conflict that started in 2016 has forced about 4,400 schools (80%) in NW/SW to close (TeenAlive, 2021). An estimated 5% to 33% of girls aged 15 to 24 years who drop out of school in Cameroon do so because of early pregnancy or marriage. It was observed that the prevalence of adolescent pregnancy is high in rural communities as compared to urban cities (VAHA, 2018). Following the crises in Anglophone Cameroon, teenager girls in these regions are facing high risk of SGBV, unwanted pregnancies especially in rural areas and high mortality rate as a result of abortion or complications in pregnancies. In these crisis regions, children are traumatized as a result of violence that has led to killing, maiming, burning of schools/houses and threat to stay away from school and abuses (PAID-WA, May, 2019). Support systems particularly, Families and social structures have also been disrupted in these regions. Psychosocial support systems in communities are inadequate. Many children and youth have limited opportunities to develop social and emotional life skills such as self-control, empathy, sympathy, negotiation, and joint problem solving, which are normally cultivated at the family level and further developed at schools. Based on an assessment conduction by PAID-WA on May 2019, teenage girls in Cameroon crisis affected regions of the North west and South west especially in the rural communities aged 12 to 19, lack life-skills in areas such as self-control, stress coping skills, problem solving challenges and reproductive health. They suffer from unwanted pregnancy, unsafe abortion and sexually transmitted infections (STIs), including HIV, as well as smoking, drug and substance abuse (PAID-WA 2019). Teenage girls are at risk of sexual and gender-based violence, including early marriage that prevents them from accessing their right to education, livelihood opportunities and other services. Furthermore, dropped out and internally displaced teenage girls (12 to 19 years) (as a result of the crises) in host communities especially in NWSW have limited access to any form of vocational Education talk less of employability skills. There is therefore need to improve on the protection and psychosocial wellbeing of conflict-affected teenage mothers and children through life skills and enhance the employability skills of teenage mothers and pregnant teenagers through vocational Education to build their resilience and provides them with stability, structure, and hope for the future in this region
