Project Summary

FIVDB will undertake certain training programs, awareness sessions, and CFW program under this project aiming to promote self-reliance of men & women in Rohingya refugees and enhance the livelihoods and resilience of vulnerable host communities intending to reduce different identified vulnerabilities. In 2021, FIVDB implements programs such as Agricultural, Non-agricultural and off-farm IGA programs, CFW program in camp & Host community under EFAL and IOM-FCDO transition project funded by DCA and FCDO. During beneficiary selection, household with persons with disabilities (PWDs), female and elderly headed, orphan hosted, household with elderly persons, large sized households will be prioritized according to order. Center based training programs on agricultural, nonagricultural sessions with continuous input distribution will be provided considering SAAD. Providing technical support to the beneficiaries under previous projects will be included. Refugee participant will be selected from Camps 2E,10,14,21,22, and KRC while host participant from Halidiapalong, palongkhali, Hnila and Whykong. All cross-cutting issues will be prioritized. Overall 58% female and 42 % male with other gender from all age groups and 7% households with PWDs are the estimated target for this project. Participants will be provided freedom of choice over selecting nonagricultural and off-farm trades, and further input & other technical support to put their training into practice. DRR training and tree plantation program will also be undertaken under this project. Tailoring has taken place particularly for the agricultural and off-farm trades activities through utilizing community feedback and suggestions regarding culture, convenience and practices collected by community consultation in different form during implementation of the previous projects.
Maximum participation of women and mainstreaming of protection principles will be ensured to make safe participation of all vulnerable gender groups particularly women and youth girls in the project activities. Considering the “Do no harm” principle, initiatives like infrastructure with appropriate facilities for PWDs, lactating mother, elderly persons etc. and trained up the staff will be undertaken to make sure meaningful access for all the most vulnerable groups.
FIVDB has all kinds of relevant policies especially PSEA policy, gender policy, child safeguarding policies that will be introduced to make sure the beneficiaries’ safeguarding. All the staff will be well oriented and reoriented on those policies for practicing throughout the project. FIVDB is not a member of PSEA network yet, but already has taken initiative to be a member of this network.
Key focus areas of AAP guideline will be emphasized in every phase of project like design to implementation to monitoring the performance of the project. During design, consulting and share about the activities will be taken place and their suggestion will be prioritized. During implementation, timely information sharing about all activities and utilization of community feedback will be take into account as the core part of the project. Establishment of structured and essay accessible complain & feedback mechanisms (help desk, complaint box, hot-line) with quick response system and a referral pathway to ensure need-based services for the project participants and other, and quarterly satisfaction survey conduction will be ensured throughout the project.
A M&E framework will be developed and following the DIP, regular process monitoring will be carried out by the MEAL team. A comprehensive target achievement tracking system will be introduced to track the daily, weekly, monthly and quarterly achievements and periodical DQA will be taken place.
A contingency plan has been included in this project for the estimated number of the project participants who may be affected by the devastating natural disaster events like cyclone, heavy rain, land slide etc.

1 - Identified Needs

Since the huge influx in 2017, over 0.89 million FDMNs are residing in 34 congested camps in Ukhia and Teknaf Upazilas in Cox’s Bazar (GoB/UNHCR Joint Registration Exercise - 30 September-2021). Subjected to several vulnerabilities despite various assistance provided by the humanitarian organizations and government, all refugees and more than .37 million populations in host community including men, women & children are reported to be in need of diversified support including food assistance, livelihood and cash assistance support (Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics population census 2011, with 2017 WB projections and FSS-PIN). In the camps, food security & livelihoods LSG severity score is 3 means severe for the 54% households while it is 4 means extreme for 13% households. About 59% of households in camps have demanded “access to food” and at the same time, 30% have made their demand for “access to self-reliance activities” as priority needs. Food Consumption Scores having slightly improved in 2021 compared to 2020 –however, still, only roughly half the households were found to have acceptable Food Consumption Scores. overall, 70% of households reported having adopted livelihoods-based coping strategies due to a lack of money to meet basic needs and the adoption of strategies were spending savings, reducing essential non-food expenditures, selling household goods and selling labour in advance. It also has been found that average monthly per capita expenditure of 60% of refugee households was below SMEB (excluding imputed amount of assistance). However, with limited access to self-reliance activities, the refugee population in camps remains highly reliant on humanitarian assistance, and almost all households have unmet multi-sectoral needs, most commonly related to shelter/NFIs and food security/livelihoods. (J-MSNA Key Findings, 2021)For the Bangladeshi host community, 65% of households have made a requirement of “access to food” while 37% have demanded “access to income-generating activities/employment” as priority needs (J-MSNA Key Findings, 2021). The reasons behind that sort of priority needs were found - households not having recovered from the COVID-19 outbreak and its secondary impacts on food security and livelihoods, with a potential for further deterioration and to reduce purchasing power and increased food prices compared to last year (FAO-WFP Market Monitor, August 2021). It resulted in further reduced proportions of households with acceptable food consumption scores compared to 2020 J-MSNA findings and reported levels of adoption of livelihoods-based coping strategies. It was found that 89%of of households reported engaging in coping mechanisms due to a lack of money to meet basic needs. It was also found that average monthly per capita expenditure of 10% of host community households was below SMEB (excluding imputed amount of assistance). (J-MSNA Key Findings, 2021) All the Refugee population and population in need for the host community with different age and gender groups require appropriate support to meet family food security while specific types of households, such as female-headed households, households without access to self-reliance activities, or households with persons with disabilities may often be more likely to have unmet needs. In certain cases, needs may also differ from camp to camp (J-MSNA Key Findings, 2021). Female headed households in both hosts and Rohingya are more likely to adopt severe coping compared to male-headed households. Female headed households have lower income levels in both Rohingya and hosts and also female unemployment rates within the labor force are also much higher than men (REVA 4, April 2021).

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1: Skill development training with the arrangement of suitable income generating self-reliance activities into camps: the selected female participants will be provided skill development training using the training center inside the camps and they will later engage in the IGA activities using the facilities provided by FIVDB based on demand in the community into the campsActivity 2: Training and input support for making homestead gardens for the targeted Rohingya refugees: Based on a comprehensive module, the participants will be trained up on making and managing the homestead garden, organic manure and pesticide production and after training, as input support, seed, bamboo, organic manure, material for producing organic pesticide will be delivered. Activity 3: Training and input support for creating agicultural and non-agricultural livelihhod opprtunities for the selected host community participants: trainingf and iput support as cash for making homestead garden will be provided to the participants who will be selected for agricultural livelihood progame. For the participants who will select the non-agicultural livelihood options, 72 sessions on finacial literacy including life-skill, simple accounting, business plan preparatino and tecnical issues related to non-agicultural options will be carride out throught the project and after preparing the business plan, they wil be provided cash grant. Activity 4: Support for planning and starting the off-farm income generating livelihood options: The selected participants will attend 72 sessions including life-skill, simple accounting and business plan preparation and vocational training on selected trade. After completing the business plan, they will be provided investment capital to start the planned trade.Activity 5: CFW program in the camps: the selected participants (30% women) will perform tree plantation and taking care, drainage cleaning, road repairing and other suitable activities will be carried out under this program.Activity 6: CFW program in the host community: the selected participants (30% women) will perform tree plantation and taking care, drainage cleaning, road repairing and other suitable activities will be carried out under this program.Activity 7: Tree plantation for reforestation into the campsActivity 8: Tree plantation for reforestation in the selected host areas Activity 9: Technical support providing to existing homestead garden beneficiaries in the camps: trained volunteers and staff will provide need-based support Activity 10: Technical support providing to existing homestead garden and IGA beneficiaries in the host community: trained Community Resource Persons (CRPs) and staff will provide need-based supportActivity 11: DRR training for the Rohingya refugees: the targeted participants who will take part in other program like CFW, homestead garden etc. under this project will receive DRR training with an aim to create awareness among the participants and develop their skill to mitigate the risks created due to hazards and adopt necessary measured during the calamities.Activity 12: DRR training for the host community: the targeted participants who will take part in other program like CFW, homestead garden etc. under this project will receive DRR training with an aim to create awareness among the participants and develop their skill to mitigate the risks created due to hazards and adopt necessary measured during the calamities.
