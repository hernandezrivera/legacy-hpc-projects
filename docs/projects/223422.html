Project Summary

This project addresses the critical child protection needs of 150,000 individuals across Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY) states through community-based reintegration services, preventive programs, and high-quality response services. It aims to safeguard children, including those with disabilities, those at risk of abuse, exploitation, or neglect, and those affected by grave violations of child rights, while empowering communities and strengthening child protection systems through innovative approaches and capacity building. The project will create awareness on child protection, PSEA, and referral pathways, reaching 70,200 individuals, and provide life skills education for 10,000 adolescent boys and girls to build resilience and leadership. Comprehensive child protection case management services will support 900 children with tailored care, while 250 children will benefit from alternative placement options under case management. Family tracing and reunification services will assist 60 unaccompanied and separated children, and 52,000 children aged 9–12 will participate in structured recreational activities that foster safety and creativity. Specialized MHPSS services will be provided to 75 children and adolescents, and 500 children with disabilities affected by protection risks will receive targeted support. Economic, social, and educational reintegration programs will benefit 900 children, including those affected by grave violations of child rights and CAFAAGs, while positive parenting sessions will engage 8,000 parents and caregivers in nurturing practices. Training for 100 child protection partners and social welfare personnel will enhance the capacity of the protection workforce, and 6,500 birth certificates will be issued to ensure children’s identity rights. Counseling sessions will be offered to 325 adolescents and caregivers to provide psychosocial support. The project incorporates innovative community-based initiatives such as Child Protection Community Champions to raise awareness and support referrals, Peer-Led Life Skills Clubs for youth mentoring, and Parent-to-Parent Mentorship programs to foster protective caregiving practices. Community Resilience Hubs will combine child-friendly spaces, recreational activities, MHPSS services, and caregiver training, while digital awareness campaigns using storytelling, and radio will spread child protection messages and strengthen referral pathways. Mobile Birth Registration Units will also be deployed to deliver services to hard-to-reach areas. This comprehensive approach ensures inclusivity and equitable participation, addressing the unique needs of men, women, boys, and girls across all target LGAs, with activities tailored to their specific vulnerabilities and strengths.

1 - Needs

The project will address several critical needs identified in the BAY states, where conflict, insecurity, flooding, displacement, camp closures, and other socioeconomic factors have significantly heightened child protection risks. Approximately 1,044,526 individuals require child protection services due to widespread abuse, neglect, exploitation, and discrimination, exacerbated by the absence of adequate child protection systems and community-based safety nets. The project targets children at risk, including those with disabilities, unaccompanied and separated children (UASCs), children affected by grave violations of child rights, and children exposed to harmful traditional practices. There is a pressing need for family tracing and reunification services for UASCs, comprehensive case management for vulnerable children, and alternative care placements to ensure their safety and well-being. Mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) is urgently needed for children and adolescents coping with trauma and adversity. Structured recreational activities and peer engagement are required to provide safe spaces for learning, socialization, and healing.Many families lack adequate knowledge and resources to provide nurturing and protective environments for their children, highlighting the need for positive parenting programs and economic reintegration support for families impacted by conflict and child recruitment. Capacity gaps within child protection systems and the social welfare workforce limit the quality and reach of response services, underscoring the need for training and capacity building. Additionally, there is a critical need for awareness creation on child protection risks, PSEA, and referral pathways to empower communities to prevent and respond to protection concerns. Birth registration services remain a priority for ensuring children's identity and access to services. Innovative approaches are needed to address protection risks in hard-to-reach areas, leverage community structures, and foster resilience within affected populations. Through its activities, the project will holistically respond to these needs, prioritizing the safety, well-being, and resilience of children while empowering communities and strengthening child protection systems to create a more protective environment across the BAY states.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

To ensure protection mainstreaming, this project will implement several key modalities that integrate protection principles throughout its activities. All activities will be designed with a focus on safety, dignity, and non-discrimination, ensuring that vulnerable children, particularly those with disabilities, are prioritized and receive appropriate support. Community-based reintegration services will be offered in a way that respects the rights and safety of all beneficiaries, including children, caregivers, and community members. We will also establish child protection referral pathways that ensure timely and safe access to services for children at risk of abuse, exploitation, or neglect. To address gender-specific needs, gender-sensitive approaches will be embedded in activities such as life skills education, positive parenting sessions, and community engagement. These will promote equal participation of men and women, boys and girls, in efforts to protect children and transform harmful gender norms. Additionally, training for child protection partners and social welfare personnel will include protection mainstreaming principles, ensuring that all actors involved understand their role in safeguarding and preventing harm. Regular monitoring and feedback mechanisms will be in place to assess the effectiveness of protection measures and ensure continuous improvement. By prioritizing a child-centered, inclusive, and rights-based approach, the project will ensure that protection is mainstreamed across all interventions, fostering a safe and supportive environment for all beneficiaries

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members by utilizing multiple accessible and inclusive methods. This includes leveraging GOALPrime's toll-free hotline (080000701701), which allows beneficiaries to report concerns, provide feedback, and ask questions in a confidential and safe manner. Additionally, the project will establish community-based feedback mechanisms, such as focus group discussions, community meetings, and suggestion boxes, specifically designed to engage vulnerable populations, including women, children, persons with disabilities, and marginalized groups. These methods will ensure that their voices are heard and that any barriers to participation are minimized. Furthermore, community mobilizers and child protection volunteers will be trained to reach out directly to the most vulnerable individuals, ensuring their inclusion in feedback processes.
