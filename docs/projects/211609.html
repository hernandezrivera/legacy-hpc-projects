Project Summary

According to HNO 2024, 16.7 million people (8.4 million female, 8.3 million male) are assessed to need humanitarian assistance across Syria, up from 15.3 million in 2023. Of the 16.7 million people in need, 5.5 million are displaced, including over 2 million who live in last resort sites. From October to December 2023, Northern Syria witnessed a significant escalation of hostilities as attacks targeted critical infrastructure such as electricity, water stations, factories, food production, grain storage, mills, industrial sites, and medical facilities which will worsen the humanitarian crisis for millions of people. In response to these needs, DCA and in collaboration with a local partner will work directly with community-based groups (i.e. youth groups, women’s groups, and agricultural cooperatives), in the development of Community-based response plans (CBRPs) that address each community priority needs. DCA will engage an estimated 5 communities (around 1000 households) Within these schemes' 2-3 community groups/self-groups will be identify will be responsible for researching, populating, and monitoring CBRPs, while DCA will facilitate the planning and learning cycle, and provide monetary and technical support to the execution of schemes and the compilation of lessons learned for potential scaling up. The partner will work directly with community-based groups (i.e. youth groups, women’s groups, and agricultural cooperatives), in the development of Community-based response plans (CBRPs).

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The proposed project will be implemented by a local partner organization with technical oversight, monitoring and support from DCA’s early recovery and protection teams, utilizing their contextual expertise and past experience in delivering humanitarian responses in NES and their good relationships with the local stakeholders and communities in the proposed areas of intervention. The implementation of the SCLR as an integrated approach seeks to build, strengthen and support rightsholders’ and communities’ existing capacities to lead on identifying their needs and jointly as a community design an appropriate response to enhance their protective environment, while simultaneously enabling rightsholders to connect and collaborate with local responses and capacities available in their areas. Through Participatory Action Learning in Crisis (PALC), SCLR recognizes crisis-affected rightsholders as first and last responders and seeks to empower and strengthen their capacities through the provision of demand-led skills training, group microgrants, financial management as well as linking, connecting, and networking local actors and community groups (CGs). This approach directly supports and operationalises DCA’s commitment to Charter-for-Change, Grand Bargain, and Global Localisation Framework 2021/2022, which all seek to enhance local leadership, empower and promote local actors and perspectives, and counter global power imbalances within the sectors of development and humanitarian relief. Based on the context analysis conducted under SCLR, the protection component will support SCLR to work together and guide community groups to respond to any protection threats and risks identified during that process. Rightsholders (men, women, boys and girls) will be engaged through the use of participatory tools (community and resources mapping, safety walks, access and control, etc.) to reflect on the vulnerabilities and risks faced by different gender and age groups within the community for them to be able to design focused projects later to ensure opportunities are equally accessible and available to the rightsholders.
