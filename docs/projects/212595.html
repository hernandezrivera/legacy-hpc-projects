Project Summary

Syria suffers complicated and long-term crisis, characterized by conflict, economic instability, and environmental challenges. This multifaceted crisis has created a serious food security situation, with 65% of the population, approximately 15.5 million people, in urgent need to food or livelihood support according to the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2024 (HNO2024). The severity of the situation has escalated compared to previous years, putting worrying pressure on the population in Syria.
Al-Hasakah Governorate, an area severely affected by the conflict, has had its share of this crisis and is located at the epicenter of this worsening crisis, where food needs are particularly widespread, affecting 74% of the population. Not only does the governorate face the same comprehensive challenges that Syria faces, but it is also marred by the continuing instability of the food security situation, which has been exacerbated by the prevailing economic crisis in which declining agricultural production, especially vital crops such as wheat, falls short of national needs, suffering a deficit exceeding 35%. This lack of agricultural production, along with a myriad of compounding factors, poses a major threat to the food security in Al-Hasakah.
Several factors contribute to the decline in agricultural production, including the deterioration of the economic situation, the decline in the exchange rate and purchasing power of agricultural workers, the rise in prices of production inputs, and the decline in the quality of available seeds. In addition, the region faces adverse weather conditions, water severe shortage, damaged irrigation systems, and shortage in energy supplies, especially fuel and electricity, which hampers agricultural and non-agricultural activities. These challenges have a cascading impact on supply chains and transportation costs, ultimately leading to higher production costs.
The repercussions extend beyond crop cultivation, affecting the health and nutritional status of livestock. Reduced access to pastures, high prices for animal feed, and lack of availability of public veterinary services, and the precarious conditions faced by those who depend on livestock for their livelihoods contribute to the decline in livestock production. . These challenges have a cascading impact on supply chains and transportation costs, ultimately leading to higher production costs. Al-Hasakah Governorate is thus caught in a web of interconnected challenges, creating an urgent need for comprehensive interventions to address the multifaceted issues affecting its population. In this context, Al-KhaboorAssociation designed a project to help 1,550 families affected by the crisis (equivalent to 9,300 people) in the southern countryside of Hasakah in the town of Arisha, by supporting the diversification of their livelihoods and sources of income and improving their food security. This will be achieved by supporting livestock production and agricultural production in the countryside of
Al-Hasakah Governorate.
Intervention and implementation of activities:
Objective: To improve food security for vulnerable families affected by the crisis that depend on agriculture and livestock as a main source of income in the southern countryside of Hasakah in Arisha town.
Through this intervention, Al-KhaboorAssociation will provide an emergency response to stabilize the livestock chain and develop agriculture in the countryside of Al-Hasakah Governorate during the period of high input prices for livestock and agriculture, and the economic crisis. This project will contribute to reviving vulnerable groups that depend for their income on agriculture and livestock as a main source. With a focus on improving livestock chains, which leads to improving the economic and living standard of the region’s residents.
