Project Summary

Continuity of education in emergencies focuses on improving learning outcomes for children in school while increasing the enrollment of children at school through non-formal education and alternative education delivery mode.
The program targets 6000 direct beneficiaries who will improve learning outcome through the Teaching at the Right Level (TaRL) approach and established radio clubs. Non formal education program targets 5000 children and adolescents who will be engaged through accelerated learning programs to achieve completion of 9 months of learning and further mainstream to primary schools and junior secondary schools.

1 - Needs

With the insurgency escalating in different parts of the North East particularly in Borno State, the relocation of the IDPs to their LGAs places them as soft targets if the Armed Opposition Groups (AOGs) decide to conduct attacks at these locations. Children who have resided at relatively safe locations and have enjoyed schooling for some years might be dropped out of school due to the unavailability of educational infrastructures in the LGAs of return. At other locations, children's learning outcome has been reduced due to several barriers to education including COVID-19.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The action recognizes the importance of mainstreaming protection and several deliberate actions have been taken into consideration, for example, the project foresees rehabilitation of gender-segregated latrines with clear marks and directions of the illustrations of FEMALE and MALE, furthermore, the action has also taken in to account training of young parents and teachers as well as the SMBC members on the positive discipline, positive parenting and child and GBV protections. Life skill training will include topics such as mine action, identification of unexploded ordinance and what steps to follow. Using the safe school initiatives, the action foresees engaging with the school-based Management committees, for provision of security to the learners while in school. The project will further establish an identity protected feedback mechanism through the establishment of a feedback mechanism to ensure that beneficiaries are able to freely provide feedback without exposing them to further risk.Internally ROHI has a child safeguarding policy on which every staff is trained and is signed to ensure that project staff and contractors do not cause any harm to the beneficiaries. In addition, the project will ensure that rehabilitations and excavation of pit latrines are done during the time that children are not in school to avoid children getting harmed by debris or construction work. The project through the strict safeguarding policy will further ensure that children are not at any time recruited by contractors to do work in the project sites, externally the project will work and advocate with other consortium partners to ensure that distributions for children are done at safe areas to avoid children getting harmed during the process. ROHI also proposes to train teachers and SBMCs on disaster risk reduction and management in order to ensure that children are safe at schools and in the event of any attack organize an evacuation of the school children. 9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Drawing from the education sector strategy, ROHI will work within the accountability framework of information provision, involvement of the community in decision making process and setting up feedback and complaint mechanism during the implementation of the project. To enhance accountability for results amongst beneficiary groups to communities we are working with, partners, and government, ROHI will use a variety of strategies keeping stakeholders informed, encourage meaningful participation, and adapt programming as needed.At inception stage, a project presentation meeting with state and LGA level stakeholders will be held in the community for easy attendance by the beneficiaries including special interest groups such as persons with disability, women and girls to explain to the beneficiary groups and government partners the project deliverables, their roles and expected results of the project, and the change the project aims to support.At the school level, beneficiaries will be engaged through sensitization sessions with key influential people in their communities and monthly meetings with their leaders and the peer educations in the case of children who will act as agents of change for the fellow children. During these meetings, the participants will be encouraged to express the challenges faced and improvements they would like to see in the programme implementation. This will create continuous communication with communities specially to inform them of about the programme (at inception), gather and respond to feedback on monthly basis and disseminate assessment findings, lessons and data (baseline).
