Project Summary

This project aims at improving the food security and nutrition situation of vulnerable households targeted by the intervention while protecting and gradually restoring their agriculture-based livelihoods. the project will strengthen food production capacities of a total of vulnerable households in the Governorates of Aleppo, and Idleb. Priority will be given to women-headed households.
The proposed interventions will also encourage the targeted households to plant legumes with their cereals to improve soil health and fertility, besides diversifying their incomesources. This practice will minimize the erosion of soil and compensate for the reduced use of fertilizers due to their high prices and/or unavailability.
This intervention will strengthen the production of Wheat and vegetable crops widely used in the diet of Syrian people to address food insecurity while protecting and gradually restoring agricultural-based livelihoods and strengthening the capacity of people to manage and recover from shocks. By integrating immediate relief with livelihood-recovery activities, the proposed action will contribute to the strengthening of resilience of the targeted crisis affected people and their host communities.
SECD Organization will target Atareb,Idleb,Harim,Jisr-Ash-Shugur and Mhambal sub districts in Aleppo and Idlep governate through distribution of 3000 agriculture input kits.
The project will provide some workshops for training on Good Agricultural Practices whose who are interested in receiving these kits.
3000 households will be targeted by this project; some of them are already Farmers, while some will be trained to start in this kind of job for the first time.
In the absence of robust support for livelihoods and agriculture, Syria’s dependence on humanitarian food assistance will continue to worsen in 2020 and beyond. Access to adequate dietary diversity continues to be a key need, especially for persons with specialized needs including children, pregnant/lactating women, and the elderly.
Self-reliance and resilience inside Syria can only be improved through better supporting the core components of food value chains alongside continued humanitarian assistance.
Key areas of value-chain support include provision of productive inputs, creation of income-generating opportunities, and the strengthening of community services and markets.
Family with many children has an advantage because teaching children is a priority too. Finally, we prefer to target more females than males since women in the community stay at home and are more likely to do the house gardening then men.
Briefly, we set conditions as follows:
• Particular attention should be given to women-headed households (or to families in which women play an important role in food production or household food security).
• Beneficiary farmers have been affected, directly/indirectly, by the on-going crisis;
• Beneficiary farmers should be poor/destitute and resident in the areas targeted by this intervention;
• Beneficiary farmers have lost their harvest or cannot afford the agriculture inputs cost to farm their land/vegetable plots due to ongoing crisis and high prices;
• Beneficiary vegetable growers to have a small plot of land in their backyard or greenhouse
• Agriculture is the main source of income for the beneficiary households;
• Beneficiary farmers are not engaged in other income-generating activities;
• Beneficiary farmers are the breadwinners of their households;
• Beneficiary farmers should not be government employees and should be without a steady source of income

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will be implemented on many steps as mentioned in the time frame below.Firstly, we need to conduct a need assessment in target areas for three weeks.Second step is hiring trainers who will give some training courses for the beneficiaries in a suitable place that we will rent for this purpose. At the same time, we will start to procurement the vegetables homestead kits which requires some trucks and workers to achieve the mission. After distributing the items to the beneficiaries, a specialized team will visit the beneficiaries at homes regularly to provide any assistance and respond to any question raised by beneficiaries. Along with the visits, an evaluation team will be conducting a post distribution assessment to fill any gap and fix any problem. Finally, a report will be written and prepared for feedback to donor.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project ensures extensive communication with the community (including leaders,women, youth, and children) to determine the needs that can best be supported by SECD in the implementation period. Additionally, issues such as the hiring of staff and the contracting with vendors will be looked at in a manner that no discriminating against any specific populations will be done (host community populations are intentionally targeted) and by being aware of the existing social dynamics. SECD takes all aspects - gender, and age - into account in its project cycle management: • Assessment: SECD carries out consulting meetings with women and girls on FSL needs to ensure program strategies that mitigate exposure to risk. Implementation: (1) Women and girls are consulted to ensure they feel and are safe, especially when using FSL services. (2) Adequate numbers of women are involved in the formation of focus group discussions. (3) The female staff is recruited for FSL interventions to mobilize women and girls from diverse groups and tofacilitate feedback from these groups. Protection Risk Assessments : Risks and mitigation (Mitigating Measure) : Risk (1): A. Increased the risk of inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages across members. Likelihood: medium, Impact: medium. Mitigating Measure: Use the Cluster guidance on minimum standards and recommended content, Use recommended minimum standards in quality and specification of items, If different to recommended standard, identify and exclude from variance any items particularly prone to create tensions. Risk(2): Risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment) of women and girls. Likelihood: Low, Impact: high. Mitigating Measures : -SECD team follows SECD Sexual Harassment policy. SECD will establish a confidential complaint mechanism also Code of conduct in all staff contracts Address any SEA complaints.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

SECD conducts a security assessment for its projects by receiving information on security situations from its network of local councils and civil society activists, as well as by remaining continuously apprised of security situations through bulletins released by the international community. For emergency relief projects, when a security situation arises that threatens the safety of staff, the staff is redirected to a nearby, safer locationuntil the area is safe enough to conduct the aid or distribution project. For non-relief projects, an assessment of the security situation is conducted in order to determine the location of the project. Risk (1) of overcrowding living conditions and the absence of adequate services might expose to risk vulnerable beneficiaries. Likelihood: medium, Impact: medium. Mitigating Measure: Ensure that adequate space is provided by family, after the completion of the rehabilitation, Raise awareness among the beneficiaries of thedangers of overcrowding, Ensure that a management system is in place for big collective shelters rehabilitation, Consider sphere standards when planning the response, and ensure that management is coordinating services with other actors to cover needs and gaps. Risk (2): Outbreak of conflict prevents access to project locations. Likelihood: low, Impact: high. Mitigating Measure: Lesson learned from 2013 operations found that while conflict occurred, access could be restored within a few days. A cautious approach thatplans for these types of delays is expressed in the project work plan. Risk (3): Airplane shelling . Likelihood: medium. Impact: Very High Mitigating Measure: The risk of Security situation (only heavy shelling) and The risk of the local council buildings being targeted by airstrikes are a very low probability according to The historical information for 9 years ago .
