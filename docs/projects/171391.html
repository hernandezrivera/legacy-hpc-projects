Project Summary

This project is ensuring that protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the COVID-19 pandemic containment measures and deteriorated health, socio-economic conditions targeting 12,680 beneficiaries in Gaza Strip.
Through this Action, Save the Children (SCI), and in partnership with AISHA, seeks to facilitate the access of vulnerable women and children in the quarantine centres and home quarantine to GBV and MHPSS support services. This will directly contribute to improving the level of progress towards recovery in addition to enhancing the resilience of the people during the current and upcoming stage of COVID-19 breakout.
The project will provide case management and support mechanisms using appropriate remote modalities according to the different categories of target groups. This is to ensure continuity of services in providing child-friendly and holistic care for children experiencing violence.
Moreover, the project will provide Child Protection, including MHPSS, Multi sectoral Services and cash assistance to children and adults, focusing on communities affected by quarantine and home isolation.
Additionally, SCI and its partner AISHA will maintain safe family-based alternative care arrangements and other mechanisms using appropriate remote modalities to prevent family separation and identify and refer unaccompanied and separated children. Moreover, the project will provide capacity development for specialized child protection service providers on remote counseling services during COVID-19.

1 - Needs

COVID-19 is a global pandemic which threatens children’s rights in countries around the world and exposes them to potentially massive disruption to their healthcare, education, access to basic needs and services. Further impacts of COVID-19 include heightened risks to child abuse, neglect, violence, exploitation, psychological stress and negative impacts on development through loss of or separation from primary caregivers. They will suffer from loss of connection to protection mechanisms and services, limited access to community support, disruption in livelihoods and family connections, fear and anxiety caused by a pandemic containment measures and the increase of domestic violence in the home setting. Children affected by COVID-19 and their families face various stressors including social isolation, health related fears, and fears about contamination or spreading the disease. Front line staff are confronted with stressful working environments of witnessing considerable suffering and grief among children affected and their families. They have to battle their own fear and concerns about the disease. The mental health and psychosocial impact of quarantine, isolation, stigmatization, and separation from caregivers can take a devastating psychological toll on children if not immediately supported. Additionally, when parents and caregivers become ill, it becomes increasingly difficult to effectively care for their children. Caregivers facing significant loss of income without a family or societal support networks and safety-nets may feel forced to resort to negative coping strategies that further place children at risk of child labour. There is an increased need for Child Protection and MHPSS interventions. Since the start of the pandemic, there have been 44 attempted suicides involving children in Gaza compared to eight in 2019. The Protection Cluster estimates that some 198,000 children in Gaza are in need of structured protection and psychosocial support due to risk of severe and moderate mental health conditions, while nearly 36,000 children require individual case management. Additionally, over 274,000 adults, including caregivers, are also in need of various MHPSS services. Another factsheet commissioned by Atfaluna Society for Deaf Children in March 2020 showed that 84% of respondent families reported increased anxiety and 89% reported that their mental health well-being was negatively affected due to COVID-19 crisis. Moreover, 82% of children reported the negative implication of COVID-19 crisis on their Wellbeing. Children are exposed to increased exposure to violence, including sexual violence, physical and emotional abuse. This may result from caregivers and other adult family members becoming increasingly distressed where Loss of household income due to death or illness of caregiver increases the risk of child labour – and for girls in particular transactional sex. According to a report carried out by AISHA Association published in June 2020 on the impact of COVID-19 crisis on mental health and wellbeing conditions of women, 2 out of 3 respondent women reported experienced violence during home isolation. The report further showed that more than half of women are at risk of violence and have no access to protection services. Aisha recent study published in September 2020 found that more than 40% of children 9-14 years of age meet the criteria for PTSD, and one third meet the criteria for severe depression. In 2019, AISHA community center received 414 children (age 6-18, 58% females) referred from the outreach activities of the organization for mental health assessment. The majority of these children reported exposure to war trauma, and 23% reported exposure to domestic violence and sexual abuse (12%).Forty-three percent (182) of these children received individual and family counseling for stress and behavior related problems. The majority complained of fears (74%), bedwetting (21%), nightmares and several behavioral and emotional symptoms. Twenty percent (88) of these children were found to have a mental illness mainly emotional disorders including depression (17%), PTSD (23%), behavior disorders (18%) and substance abuse (6%). They received trauma focused psychotherapy, family therapy and behavior therapy. A rapid assessment on COVID-19 carried out by the UN Women in April 2020 revealed that domestic violence has increased due to the conditions created by the COVID 19 pandemic, which in most cases resulted in lock down of survivors of violence with their abusers. Additionally, the report concluded that front-liners are working under stressful conditions, putting their emotional and psychological well-being at risk. 53% of service providers reported an increase in domestic violence and psychological and physical violence being target towards both adults and children. During the COVID-19, PWDs who are dependent on receiving support from family members to perform their daily living activities may find themselves isolated and unable to survive during lockdown measures. Frequent hand washing and physical distancing are almost impossible which increases their fears and vulnerability as well. PWDs also continue to face discrimination and other barriers in accessing to health services, WASH facilities, livelihood and income support, and lacking access to the Social Protection Services, participating in e-learning, and seeking protection from violence. (AducAid 2020) With households facing lockdown, financial pressures and anxiety related to the pandemic, experts say there has been a dramatic increase in calls for help. Yet, support for survivors of gender-based violence has been curtailed due to the movement restrictions. Access to safe spaces and in-person counselling have been significantly reduced and, in some cases, totally suspended. Organizations are racing to adapt to these new circumstances, often without adequate funding.

2 - Activities

Act 1: Screening study for 1,000 affected children from home quarantine and quarantine centers affected by COVID-19 in the five governorates of the Gaza Strip (500 boys and 500 girls age of 10-17 years including 20% of CwD, orphans, working boys/girls, street boys/girls, girls victims of violence, at risk of early marriage, sexual abuse, one of their parents have mental illness or addiction) to identify the protection concerns including violence, level of trauma and resulting behavioral problem. The results of the study will classify the targeted children into low, middle, and high trauma/ behavioral problem in need for psycho-social support, specialized MH services and case management using various tools designed and implemented by AISHA. Act 2: Provide remote Psychosocial support to 1,000 conflict and covid-19 affected children (500 boys, 500 girls; age 10-13 & 14-17) who are at the above mentioned risks. Remote group psychological sessions (100 group of 10 children, each group will attend 5 group sessions) considering precaution measures during implementation. Act 3: Provide case management to 200 children (100 boys, 100 girls) at home quarantine and quarantine centers affected from COVID-19. AISHA will provide case management and support mechanisms (using appropriate remote modalities according to categories of target groups) to ensure continuity of services providing child-friendly and holistic care for children experiencing violence (including hygiene materials, cash assistance). AISHA will provide MHPSS services to children and adults, especially in communities affected by quarantine and home isolation. Additionally, AISHA will maintain safe family-based alternative care arrangements and other mechanisms using appropriate remote modalities to prevent family separation and identify and refer unaccompanied and separated children. Providing case management for 200 children (100 boys, 100 girls of Age 10-13, 14-17 yrs.) including specialized MH services, legal aid, individual/family counselling, remedial classes for 50 children (25 girls, 25 boys) financial/cash will be provided to 100 children (50 boys, 50 girls including 10%CwD) to support their commitment to remedial classes and education including transportation to educational center, clothes, shoes, pocket money, etc. of 30$ for 5 months. The selection of those beneficiaries will be in coordinated with the Protection Cluster, CP/MHPSS Sub Cluster, MoSD and UNRWA with whom we have a signed a regional MoU. The selection of the 200 children with severe and medium trauma symptoms as they will be refereed by case mangers through the remote psychosocial support. Act 4: 500 parents, living in most vulnerable areas in GS will be equipped with the skills and techniques to practice Positive Discipline in Everyday Parenting- PDEP, and with knowledge to eliminate violence against children practices and reactions. The PDEP manual will address 8 to 11 different sessions focused on the most important issues that concern parents and instructing them on improving their parenting skills in non-violent discipline, problem solving and conflict resolution.in order to change attitudes and behaviors of parents to decrease the use of violence in disciplining and social raring of their children. Act 5. Provide MHPSS support to 500 women affected of COVID-19 in home quarantine and quarantine centers including remote counselling, individual counseling sessions for 200 women >18, remote group sessions for 300 women>18 of 10 women per group following Hawwa Zahra manual developed by AISHA in 2016 for GBV cases during and post crisis. Act 6. Provide health services to 200 women in need for SRHR and health services. Act 7. Economic support for 30 poor /GBV cases/ mothers >18-59 of the targeted children with poor economic situation including vocational training and financial support/ grants for 15 small, and 15 cash for work opportunities for the rest of the 30 selected women who got the training Act 8. Provide financial support for transitional sheltering to 40 families affected by conflict and COVID-19. The transitional sheltering to 40 FHH (20-59) will focus on those facing serious protection risks and who cannot resort to both available and impacted by covid-19. Each FHH will have 3 months to support transitional sheltering until sustainable/safe solution offered by AISHA and other actors. Act 9. Provide child protection non-specialized service providers with developed capacities in structured PSS, identification of protection cases and referral pathways/measures, protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) according to IASC standards.; 100 service provider (50 women,50 men aged 18-59 yrs), 5 groups of 20 persons will attend 3 days training, each day of 5 training hours. Act 10. Capacity development for specialized child protection service providers on remote counseling services through COVID-19; 40 persons (20 women, 20 men >18-59 yrs) will attend 50 training hours (2 groups of 20 persons, 25 training hrs per group). Act 11. Conduct advocacy and media campaign targeting the community to raise their awareness about alternative to violence and positive parenting during covid-19 outbreak and crisis time. Including: 2 Radio sessions, 1 radio spot, SMS tracking 10,000 community persons, 1 fact sheet studying the reality of affected children in quarantine centers and home quarantine, Positive messages and videos to be share on FB and other social media platforms via paid advertisement.AISHA will follow the PA and WHO official precaution procedures related to the spread of COVID-19. Aisha will take safety and protection measures in implementing the project activities such as large Hall, good ventilation, lighting and Hygiene measures for both place and target group before and after the sessions, and distributing personal Hygiene kits for all children and adults and PPE for partner CBOs.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

SCI has an integrated and systematic approach to monitoring and evaluation ensuring gender and conflict sensitivity, identifying, capturing, evaluating, managing and sharing program knowledge through regular tracking of outputs and outcomes. Monitoring and evaluation will include weekly updates on the number of direct and indirect beneficiaries and achievements, with constant field visits and reports. Gender Advisor will ensure gender mainstreaming in the interventions. SCI’s Field Officer will supervise the implementation in cooperation with the partners. Partners will submit weekly progress reports. SCI has established an accountability mechanism including complaints and suggestion boxes placed at CBOs’ offices as well as a Hotline. SCI staff and partners will make sure that targeted communities are informed on the types of feedback mechanisms available through publicizing materials and information sharing sessions. Any recommendations, suggestions and concerns of the beneficiaries, along with the mitigation measures will be discussed with partners to be properly addressed. SCI in some cases follow an aggressive approach in Accountability by conducting specialized monitoring visits to the very marginalized communities to hear from them and to document any raised concerns, CSG or protection issues.SCI has Community Protection Committees (CPCs) and child protection focal points (CPFPs) that actively participate and are engaged in the planning of the intervention. Moreover, they participate and support in the process of the selection of the beneficiaries; they will be also the key linkages with their respective communities and represent them in raising any concern or complaint. To ensure access of vulnerable groups/individuals, CPCs and CPFPs are also involved in raising awareness of the communities on the different available accountability mechanisms provided by SCI (1800-724-726).

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

SCI will ensure “Do-no-harm” through our project intervention in terms of mitigation ofpotential social tensions in communities resulting from community members feelingexcluded. Interventions are based on needs assessments and transparent selectioncriteria. Appropriate targeting of beneficiaries will be the result of quality assessmentof design and planning processes, coordinated through regular meetings with thepartners and communities during the course of the implementation.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

To ensure the participation of the targeted people and the equitable access to theservices provided, a series of discussion groups and consultation meetings will beconducted at all project stages with girls, boys, women, men, elders and people withdisabilities. This will enable communities to raise their concerns and together come upwith solutions to ensure that all the interventions do not exacerbate risks or create newrisks.To ensure children, youth and adolescent girls’ and boys’ voices are heard and fairly/equally represented, SCI will be facilitating discussion with children and youth/adolescent girls with and without disabilities to hear their perspectives, concerns and suggestions regarding the current situation. SCI will consider using the Basic Needs Assessment tool, which incorporates child participatory methods and child/adolescents-centred approach. While their leaderships in this is crucial for sustained response, participation of communities, including children and adolescent girls, will be carefully considered given the risks and the need for social distancing.
