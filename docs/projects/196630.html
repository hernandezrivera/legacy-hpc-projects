Project Summary

INTERSOS intends to address identified problems and unmet needs through an EiE response targeting the most vulnerable population in areas with high severity conditions in Hama, RD, Idleb governorates. Here, INTERSOS will continue implementing integrated protection (see HSYR23-PRO-197346-1), health (HSYR23-HEA-197010-1) and ERL (HSYR23-ERY-196628-1) interventions. In line with 2022-2023 Syria Education Sector Response Plan, the action aims to improve 1) safe and equitable availability and access to formal and NFE services; 2) enhance the quality of formal and non formal teaching and learning; 3) enhance education system and communities’ to support a timely, coordinated and evidence based education response. In 2023, INTERSOS will continue building on evidence-based support to the formal school system in Syria, while relying on its extensive expertise in the delivery of NFE in the Middle East region. INTERSOS operates in close coordination with MoE, with whom it signed a sub-MoU in June 2021. INTERSOS carries out integrated actions where Protection and CP principles are mainstreamed along with GBV preventive messages. INTERSOS works through multiple approaches promoting boys and girls access to formal and NFE through: hard components (school rehabilitations, provision of emergency prefab classrooms, furniture), SLP, remedial classes (after school for children at risk of drop out and in learning center for OOSC), adolescents' clubs. INTERSOS promotes an inclusive environment enhancing access and structural accessibility (including WASH) of CwDs and providing the community with the skills to support CwDs inclusion in the learning environment. Education kits are delivered to all the children enrolling in the classes (stationary and SLP). INTERSOS is focusing on girls’ continuation of learning preventing adolescent girls drop out also through the distribution of adolescents’ dignity kits, which are complemented by education sessions on menstrual hygiene management. Classes are arranged based on a flexible schedule to accommodate the needs of children supporting the family with work/house chores. Regular classes are alternated with PSS group sessions and individual counseling is provided in order to promote boys and girls’ wellbeing and retention. Quality of teaching and learning is promoted through training to formal and NFE personnel on Active Learning, Inclusive Education, Curriculum and Planning, Pedagogy, Teachers’ role and well-being, CP principles (last 4 subjects are already included in INEE standard TiCC training). INTERSOS has developed a response strategy based on a strong community-based approach coupled with a robust capacity building programme to enhance the quality of services delivered to the affected individuals. PTAs are established and/or supported and their members trained on: CP elements, PSEA, Safe identification and referral, education outreach and monitoring, gender mainstreaming in education. Once trained they become the first initiators of: i. PTAs led initiatives and ii. Back to Learning (B2L). Through consultations with the community and HHs assessments, the education outreach volunteers identify OOSC and individual causes for being OOS. Such methodology promotes the definition of individual pathways for re-enrolment through NFE. Finally, INTERSOS is fully integrating health and hygiene promotion and infection prevention and control in its action providing holistic knowledge and capacities to the targeted schools through training and awareness and promoting the knowledge and respect of the school health protocol. Based on clear vulnerability criteria with an holistically integrated response (protection, health and ERL), INTERSOS targets the most vulnerable boys and girls at risk of drop out and OOSC, prioritizing overage students, adolescents’ girls and boys, IDPs and CwDs.

3 - Needs

The Education HNO 2023 estimates some 6.9M persons in need of education services in the Whole of Syria, representing an increase of almost 0.3M compared to 2022. Self-reported attendance for pre-tertiary education at national level is 88%. Economic factors are a key reason for not attending school pushing boys into child labor and exposing girls at higher risk of early marriage. The formal system is unable to absorb and retain all school-age children. The protracted conflict has left schools partially or entirely damaged, leading to overcrowded classrooms, lacking supplies, equipment and adequate wash facilities. Overage students are at high risk of not re-entering education also because of the scarcity of secondary schools. The quality of the learning system has been deeply impacted facing today an insufficient number of teaching personnel in need of professional development. Adults and children have been differently affected, reporting signs of PTSD, depression and isolation (mainly girls), anger and violent behaviors (mainly boys). Teachers themselves show signs of distress and are in need of PSS, peer support groups and of an improved referral system. CwDs are particularly impacted by the crisis and continue being excluded from education services due to physical and attitudinal barriers (e.g. lack of transportation, non-accessible buildings, stigma and discrimination at community level), as highlighted by INTERSOS RMNA conducted in RD, Hama, Idleb (May/Jun 2022).

4 - Implementation of activities

INTERSOS, in coordination with MoE/relevant DoEs, aims to improve: 1. safe and equitable access; 2. enhance quality of education services; 3. enhance the education system and communities’ capacities. INTERSOS will intervene through light rehabilitations (including WASH in school, accessibility works, provision of solar pann els), emergency prefab classrooms, schools’ furniture-education supplies. Under integration with health, safe schools are ensured by training school personnel/PTAs on IPC, hygiene practices, health and hygiene awareness (under EiE) and PHC consultations (under health). INTERSOS will distribute education, hygiene, winter and adolescent dignity kits to promote enrolment and retention. Remedial classes are provided after school hours/during weekend/summer break. Children’s performance improvement of children is evaluated against the learning outcomes. Carry out B2L to encourage enrolment in formal/NFE. Teachers Professional Capacity Development is conducted through training in EiE, inclusive education, active learning, CoC and positive discipline, CP, PSEA and safe identification and referral. PTAs are established/strengthened to ensure community ownership and active participation in child education. Activities will be monitored and evaluated through rehabilitation reports, field visits, distribution lists/GRNs, PDMs, attendance sheets in awareness sessions and trainings, quality checklists (initial-mid-final), children-friendly feedback mechanism, and FGDs.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Following main protection risks were identified: Risk of physical punishment from teachers in schools; Acts of violence and bullying perpetrated by peer students; Lack of quality services and referral expose children and survivors to harm, enhancing risk of stigmatization and further trauma; Education facilities are not safe for children; Risk faced by children on the way to/from school; Risk of disease outbreaks due to inadequate WASH facilities, lack of awareness, lack of cleaning and hygiene supplies; Community lack of acceptance of GBV/CP messaging, refusing protection activities. To address these risks, the following mitigation actions will be adopted: Training/coaching on code of conduct and positive discipline provided to school personnel; CP awareness raising (incl. bullying); PFA/PSS alternated to regular classes; service mapping developed and regularly updated; Rehabilitate school infrastructures to ensure safe and inclusive access; Activate buddy system for movement to/from school; Rehabilitate school WASH infrastructures; Health and hygiene awareness sessions complemented by distribution of hygiene kits; School personnel trained on IPC/school health protocols; Community engagement to identify protection concerns and define mitigation actions; Community communication SOPs developed and enforced; Age/gender-appropriate capacity building for community volunteers/leaders; Trainings on Protection, CP, GBV, CoC, PSEA, Safe Identification and Referral are mainstreamed.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risks: INTERSOS staff, suppliers/contractors have prior conviction for SEA related offenses; staff member, suppliers, do not comply/accept PSEA policy; SEA perpetrated by staff/service providers; PSEA allegations are not timely/effectively addressed; survivors have limited access to safe assistance/remedies; lack of PSEA reporting mechanisms; communities not aware of PSEA risks/reporting channels. M: INTERSOS has a Global PSEA/CP Policy that applies to all personnel in addition to a country level Strategy to ensure SEA risks/mitigation strategies/procedures are tailored to the context. INTERSOS staff must sign a PSEA/CP Policy Declaration, attend a PSEA/CP Policy induction and one refresher training per year. 1 PSEA Referent and 4 PSEA Focal Points are appointed to receive complaints/reports about alleged acts of SEA and follow up in compliance with relevant guiding principles, ensuring safety of survivors. Complaints are referred to the in-country PSEA Referent and are subject to investigation. An assessment about IP commitment and capacity to prevent/respond to SEA is conducted; efforts are made with IP to harmonize PSEA strategies/reporting procedures to the best extent possible. Training is offered to partners to strengthen the identification of SEA risks/adoption of related SEA mitigation measures; when possible awareness raising on PSEA risks/reporting channels is conducted at community level.
