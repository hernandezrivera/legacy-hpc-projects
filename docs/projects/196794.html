Project Summary


The proposed project aims to address the critical needs of vulnerable conflict-affected populations living in sites, settlements, and surrounding areas in Northern Syria, through camp and coordination support. Specifically, ACTED will implement CCCM responses to coordinate service provision and build local capacity for self-management. ACTED’s proposed assistance, through its static and mobile CCCM teams, will include key information management activities such as service mapping, risks and demographic assessments, running of coordination structures through regular engagement and advocacy and the strengthening of self-management structures through trainings. ACTED will focus on the reinforcement of accountability systems (e.g. Complaints and Feedback Mechanisms). In addition to providing CCCM core activities, ACTED CCCM mobile team will conduct rapid needs assessments throughout Northern Syria which will primarily use to inform the programmatic decision to deploy.
In NES, ACTED will continue operating in sites where it has a static CCCM presence, and continue conducting rapid assessments to identify informal sites most in need of mobile support, which it will select based on the assessed level of need and its own response capacity. ACTED will continue to provide static CCCM support through maintaining the Camp Management function in Areesheh; Washokani camps (Al-Hasakeh governorate) and Abu Khashab Camp (Deir-ez-Zor governorate), its mobile support to Serekaniye camp (the mobile status will be re-assessed regularly and might evolve depending on situation on the field), as well as provide support to sites and settlements in Ar-Raqqa and Deir-ez-Zor governorates. This support includes the coordination of service providers to ensure the coverage of in-camp multi-sectoral needs, site maintenance, and support to camp committee and communication mechanisms. For in-camp livelihood activities not targeting entire resident populations of targeted sites, ACTED will apply standard demographic and vulnerability criteria for beneficiary prioritization.
In NWS, ACTED will continue providing support in sites based on coordination with Cluster partners, assessed needs, and its own capacity. ACTED will target camps located in the Azaz district (Northern Aleppo governorate) through site maintenance and operational assistance, as well as support to camp committee and communication mechanisms.
.
With regards to its general CCCM approach through 2023, ACTED plans to maintain full static presence in camps that do not currently have viable and durable solutions or transitional capacity with a focus on capacity-building and resilience programming. In static sites which do have avenues for durable solutions, in the short term, ACTED will start sensitization of local authorities, service providers, humanitarian actors, and communities and ensure that the transition processes are established in a collaborative, coordinated manner, and abide by key indicators within CCCM minimum standards. In the long term, ACTED will retain some level of CCCM presence through site management support. This project will target 332,068 individuals.

3 - Needs

Across Syria, over 2 million persons are expected to be in need of CCCM assistance in 2023, an increase from 1.7 million in 2022, as per the (unreleased) 2023 HNO. The population in ten camps with a static camp management has increased by 4,320 individuals since the beginning of 2022, while the rate of new arrivals significantly outweighs the departures, with 7,000 individuals on camp waiting list. This is alarming, specifically for women, girls and persons with disabilities who can face increased protection risks. While instances of significant population displacement are rare, they remain a concern with potential displacement events in the area and formal camps already at their limits or are undergoing relocation plans due to floods. Smaller numbers of IDPs are also settled in informal sites around main cities, with very limited assistance coverage despite high level of needs, calling for a continued mobile CCCM response. Across areas, IDPs remain heavily dependent on humanitarian assistance to meet their basic needs due to lack of livelihoods. Priority needs remain food, winterization needs, as the camps and settlements are susceptible to cold weather as well as flooding, bearing a heavy toll on shelter units and access roads, respectively, particularly highlighting needs for CCCM-led camp maintenance works. Chronic underfunding and access constraints have led to severe gaps in camp management. In NWS, 76% of IDP sites do not have a camp management system in place.

4 - Implementation of activities

Through this intervention, ACTED will focus on offering comprehensive responses in protracted underserved displacement sites. ACTED will implement a range of static and mobile CCCM responses that advocate for and coordinate service provision as well as build local capacity for self-management. ACTED’s proposed assistance will include key information management activities such as service mapping, risks and demographic assessments, running of coordination structures through regular engagement and advocacy and the strengthening of self-management structures through trainings. ACTED will focus on the reinforcement of accountability systems (e.g. CRMs). In NES, ACTED will also continue to provide static CCCM support through maintaining the Camp Management function in Areesheh, Washokani camps (Al-Hasakeh governorate), as well as Abu Khashab camp (Deir-ez-Zor governorate) as well as transition its response for Serekaniye camp and provide mobile responses to sites and settlements in Ar-Raqqa and Deir-ez-Zor governorates. In NWS, ACTED will target camps in Azaz district (Aleppo governorate) – where a camp manager entity is already present – with camp committee support and site care and maintenance activities.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Non-inclusion of vulnerable group needs during project design/implementation. Likelihood: Low – Impact: High. Mitigation: The proposed intervention will be implemented in accordance with ACTED’s gender approach, which follows the Gender Handbook for Humanitarian Action reviewed in 2017 by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC). ACTED incorporates gender analysis and considerations into the full project management cycle, from project design to implementation and monitoring. During the community sensitization, ACTED encourages the identification of special needs of most vulnerable groups including women, children, elderly, or people living with disabilities and conducts FGDs and/or community meetings prior to implementation. ACTED addresses where possible gender and protection concerns. In areas out of the scope of programming or expertise, ACTED relies on referral procedures to specialized agencies. Risk 2: Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation/abuse. Likelihood: Low – Impact: High. Mitigation: All ACTED staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct which directly refers to Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Gender Based Violence (GBV). As part of efforts to improve accountability, ACTED is committed to implement Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its projects to allow beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on ACTED work, as well as the work of the contractors, and to relay any arising issue.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

ACTED has developed training packages on the matter which are delivered to all its staff members during the first month of employment and through regular refresher trainings. These trainings will also be delivered to incentive workers, CfW participants, and Camp Committees and Camp Administration members involved in ACTED’s activities under this award. Lastly, these training sessions will also be delivered to all the contractors under this intervention to ensure beneficiaries’ safety during the delivery of the services. ACTED AME Unit will also conduct monitoring of the services delivery through contractors to ensure it is conducted in a safely and timely manner. ACTED AME will also conduct satisfaction surveys with beneficiaries at the end of the services delivery to gather information on the beneficiaries’ satisfaction with these services and on the behavior of ACTED staff and contractors, enabling ACTED to take corrective action in a timely manner.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- Security and Political: Expansion to new areas: in-depth access mission from the ACTED Security team, formal approval required for daily movements and daily sharing of security updates. Conflict escalation: movement suspension, relocation of staff Community acceptance: monitored daily by liaison assistants, regular meetings with local community leaders’ network in order to prevent tensions. Regular dialogue ensured with all relevant authorities, with support from various coordination mechanisms at area level. Risk of interference: low visibility, recruitment and procurement processes, information sharing sensitivity, clear communication with local actors regarding expectations and responsibilities. - Resource availability: Road closures: ACTED diversified its network of suppliers through tender processes and reinforced local suppliers’ capacities to ensure that items are procured regularly and prepositioned. Procurement related risks: 2-week local advertisement in multiple locations and subsequent transparency control and safeguards: analysis of all offers at capital office level, evaluation of technical and financial offers conducted separately between field team and capital. Recruitment related risks: process entails 2-week advertisement with inputs from field, regional office, area coordinators. - Community and cohesion dynamics: CRM inside Syria: stakeholders can safely provide feedback and report any project issues or concerns.
