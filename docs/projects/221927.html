Project Summary

GSB work will contribute to a coordinated life-saving response that promotes IDPs and returnees’ fair access to intersectoral humanitarian assistance and services in Quissanga due to displacement shocks affecting the District. The Administrative Post of Bilibiza will be the main area of intervention, targeting 1,568, returnees and host communities (50% women and 50% men). The project will be covering the 4 HNRP CCCM Cluster Objectives: CO1 Ensure equal access and needs based assistance to improve the quality of integrated services for populations affected by displacement through Coordination and monitoring; CO2 Respond to vulnerable population by addressing protection gaps and strengthen the predictability and effectiveness of multi sectorial interventions through robust community engagement and participation; CO3: Strengthen inclusive community participation to ensure local ownership, self-governance and self-reliance; and CO4: Strengthen Community Cohesion by facilitating the transition of IDP sites into neighbourhoods through capacity building and initiation of women and youth led livelihood projects.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

GSB will facilitate partners’ coordinated lifesaving activities and returnees’ fair access to basic services and dignifying living conditions. Whilst continuing learning and improving its technical capacity. GSB CCCM intervention will adopt the area-based approach to assist IDPs and returnees restart their lives in the areas of origin that they now consider safe, as well as to support them get into former or new food security and livelihoods activities.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The intervention will be targeting Bilibiza, in the localities of 19 de Outubro, Ujama, Maua and Nivico. This CCCM intervention aims at increasing the IDPs and returnees’ access to basic services and humanitarian assistance, therefore also aiming at strengthening the response mechanism, in line with local district administration priorities, communities’ needs, while ensuring inclusive participation with age, gender and diversity representation and promoting community feedback mechanisms (CFM). Activities: Activity 1. Establish a strong coordination system for humanitarian partners’ response and service provision. Identify humanitarian response gaps and share them at Quissanga coordination meetings (led by SDPI/Administrator), to be held biweekly (area level weekly meeting and district level monthly). Support authorities’ overseeing role by developing the 5Ws (considering that in many cases authorities are taken out by other activities); Activity 2. Monitor lifesaving services provided by partners. Use the monitoring tool developed by the CCCM cluster to supervise all the services that partners are offering. This monitoring will be conducted conjointly with authorities, to bring real life stories regarding the provision of services such as WASH, FSL, GBV, hazard mitigation, etc.; Activity 3. Promote community engagement and participation. Facilitate the participation of IDPs and returnees in area-level governance, ensuring their access to essential services, as well as community feedback mechanisms (CFM) that allow them to provide input and raise concerns. This approach strengthens accountability to affected people (AAP) and supports the effective delivery of services and assistance. Making sure that “nothing for the community is done without the community” – improving the effectiveness of humanitarian programs and ensuring that aid reaches those who need it most. In this context, Area Management Committees, Women’ Committees, Youth Committees and Disability Inclusion Committees will be established to ensure these groups can participate and be heard. For instances, Women Led Projects, Community Based protection, Community Led Projects, etc., can be developed in this context, after area mapping and planning, service monitoring to ensure that IDP and returnees can access the provided services and that efficient feedback mechanisms are in place; Activity 4. Promote Care and Maintenance Create awareness, sensitize and mobilize communities to embrace actions to protect the environment, mainly using the concept of sanitation and hygiene, keeping the area safe and reducing the transmission of diseases through hygiene practices. For instances, mobilizing community led initiatives to keep the area clean, mitigate environmental impacts, including prevention of erosion (sandbags, tree planting) or be prepared for the flood risk mitigation in the rainy season (through construction of drainages, ditches and water channels), and provide them with tools (wheelbarrows, shovels, rakes, etc.).

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), security, gender and environment are some of the areas of concern in humanitarian response, including CCCM, that address individual, group, or general vulnerability issues. These crosscutting issues will be integrated in the operation through reinforcement of existing frameworks and policies, conduction of situational analyses and needs assessment; Reinforce the observation of GSB’s policies related to anti-corruption, procurement, conduct code and principles, PSEA, emphasizing zero tolerance to any misconduct in this regard, and held offenders accountable. The Community Feedback Mechanisms (CFM) will be put in place to allow beneficiaries to provide feedback on the services that are being provided as well as to entitle beneficiaries to receive feedback from different clusters, in line with the approach of accountability to affected people (AAP), ensuring that “nothing for the community is done without the community” – improving the effectiveness of humanitarian programs and ensuring that aid reaches those who need it most.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

GSB will promote measures that increase sensitivity to women and girls’ particular safety needs and reduce/prevent their exposure to sexual violence (positioning of water points and other public spaces, lighting, etc.), ensuring that local governance structures are aware of this need of addressing safety issues as well as the referral route of GBV cases for assistance, including the Green Line 1458. The needs of persons with disability, children and the elderly will also be considered, with special focus on ensuring accessibility for temporary infrastructure, WASH facilities, distribution and communication activities. PSEA is also integrated in GSB codes and principles of conduct, binding its staff and any third parties contracting with GSB, that can also use the existing complain mechanism.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The initial phase of the response implementation will always consider a rapid gender analysis to identify and understand the issues of different groups, including disability groups, to ensure that the response is fair to their needs, they are heard and can benefit from the assistance. A Disability Inclusion will be established for this purpose. During project implementation project data disaggregation will also consider disability groups to understand their access to assistance.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The project acknowledges the advent of social and environmental risks, therefore its will consider early warning systems in place, integrate disaster risk reduction in the interventions, map critical periods and places for disaster and inform all staff on this and how to address any situation. In addition, the project considers creating awareness, sensitizing and mobilizing communities to embrace actions to protect the environment, mainly using the concept of sanitation and hygiene, keeping the area safe and reducing the transmission of diseases through hygiene practices.
