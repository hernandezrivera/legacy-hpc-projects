Project Summary

The Nutrition project aims to address urgent and life-saving nutrition needs of mothers and children in Syria, located in high severity areas across the entire country, as depicted by the 2023 HNO.
5.9 million people including 3.75 million children (2.2 million girls and 1.55 million boys) and 2.1 million women are in need of nutritional assistance in Syria, amongst which 0.5 million children are stunted and around 363,556 children below five years of age suffer from acute malnutrition. Mimicking the elevated levels of stunting, disparities in prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) in children was recorded across the entire country, with GAM rates in the North-West of Syria and Damascus approached the 5% threshold, while the north-east recorded a high of 12% ( classified as critical as per the nutrition severity ranking scale) resulting in 287,830 children with MAM. Simultaneously, maternal malnutrition remains an impeding concern, with prevalence of Acute Malnutrition (AM) ranging from 11% in the North-west to 25% in the North-East, resulting in 544,752 PLWs suffering from AM, including 460,124 suffering from anemia. Similarly, an approximate 25 % (721,388) children under five years suffer from anemia.
In line with the nutrition sector priorities, the project aims to provide preventative and curative nutrition services to the most vulnerable groups (namely; children U5 and PLWs) amongst all affected populations, including IDPs, host communities and returnees identified with high severity food insecurity and nutrition indicators. The project will, therefore, focus on:
- Improve access to equitable and quality curative nutrition services through the most appropriate modalities to include early identification, referral, and treatment of moderate and severe wasting among children under five and PLWs in collaboration with the WASH, health and food security sectors to address the drivers of malnutrition. The targets for MAM treatment are: (35,193 CH, PLW:32,586 )in NES and (41,494 CH, PLW: 80,511) in NWS and (109,525 CH, PLW: 231,903) in inside Syria.
− Scale-up Blanket Supplementary Feeding Program (BSFP) to prevent Acute Malnutrition and micronutrient deficiencies in children 6-23 months
− Improve access and utilization to nutrition services, nutritious and age appropriate diets and feeding practices for CU2 and PLWs. Vulnerable PLWs will receive cash assistance of a maximum value of 25$ in NWS and 36$ in the rest of the country for a max of 15 cycles.
WFP aims to target boys and girls aged 6- 23months and PLWs, including those with disability, through its programmes. , the project will adopt a context and severity-based approach to prioritize sub-districts identified with high nutritional and food insecurity severity, mainly those with severity level 3,4 &5. In case of lower funding, priority will be given to locations were severity of food security and nutrition of is more than 4 and to newly accessible areas.
WFP monitors will carry out regular monitoring visits to oversee implementation and collect quantitative and qualitative data through on-site monitoring, Focus group discussions and post-distribution surveys.
To ensure proper and effective implementation of the Nutrition activities, WFP will start the obligation of reporting children and PLWG’s with disability in their prevention and treatment programs if applicable. This will be done through the already established reporting channels (4W’s Considering the high needs and limited resources , 10% of each allocation(that was given by HRP committee) was applied to calculate disability, this might change during the implementation of the programs . WFP will maintain regular coordination with MOH and other nutrition actors through the nutrition sector, and various thematic group.

3 - Needs

The protracted crisis, coupled with recent economic downturn and the deteriorating food security is having a detrimental effect on dietary patterns of individuals, adversely affecting the nutrition status of the most vulnerable segments in Syria. Today, Syria faces a triple burden of malnutrition, highlighted by a deterioration in all forms of undernutrition due to the aggravating factors of the crisis, including food insecurity, inflation, unsafe water, and spread of disease. This has resulted in 5.9 million children and PLWs (3.75 million children and 2.1 million women) in need of lifesaving preventative and curative nutrition services in Syria amongst which 363,556 children below the age of five and more than 0.5 million PLWS are affected by acute malnutrition and in need of immediate treatment, while more than 0.5 million children are at risk of losing their future to stunting, while one in four children and PLWs are estimated to suffer from iron deficiency anemia. As The trends of acute malnutrition in children continues to increase, prevalence amongst PLWs remains an impeding challenge across Syria, with rates exceeding the emergency thresholds in various locations as cited by 2022 JANFSA data and NWS 2022 SMART Survey.

4 - Implementation of activities

WFP will maximize the coverage of its nutrition programmes across Syria by • Scaling-up BSFP to prevent Acute Malnutrition reaching 228,000 children 6 - 23 months of age while implementing the family MUAC approach, for early identification and referrals of acute malnutrition cases to treatment centers. • Improve access and utilization to nutrition services, nutritious and age appropriate diets and feeding practices for CU2 and PLWs targeting 75,000 PLWs while simultaneously incorporating early detection and referral of acute malnutrition cases identified at registration centers • Improve access to equitable and quality curative nutrition services through the most appropriate modalities to include early identification, referral, and treatment of moderate and severe wasting among CU5, PLWs in collaboration with the WASH, health and food security sectors to address the drivers of malnutrition, targeting : 151,11 CU5 and 312,423 PLWs . Support to national stakeholders in responding to the high rates of anemia, by piloting the fortification of bread within the national subsidy programme to increase resilience of the community. WFP’s nutrition programmes remain inclusive to people with disability among the targeted groups and WFP will ensure that distribution and activity points are accessible to them.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

a. Data-protection: Likelihood & impact: Medium in CVA. Mitigation measure: using anonymous identifiers to all beneficiaries when sharing information between CP &WFP. Beneficiary info stored on an online cloud run by a third-party provider (managed by WFP HQ).b. Supply Chain disruptions for BSFP &TSFP: Likelihood & impact: high. Mitigation measures: WFP looks into diversifying LNS-MQ &LNS-LQ sources, prepositioning supplies within WFP warehouses, and ensuring procurement requests are sent with at least 6 months lead time.c. Disruption of services in NWS and NES due to conflict and increasing access challenges: Likelihood and Impact: High. Mitigation measures: in NWS, alternatives are being explored with the nutrition cluster and WFP HQ ,including but not limited to possible local manufacturing of SNF in Turkey, handover of nutrition supplies to INGO within turkey for distribution in NWS . For NES, prepositioning of supplies remains a priority.d. SYP fluctuations &compromise of food basket qualities: Likelihood & impact: High. Mitigation measure: Focus the assistance towards the most vulnerable families, while ensuring ongoing reassessments to reduce exclusion errors, , ensure all livelihoods projects conducted by WFP are nutrition sensitive e.Aid workers use their power or influence to sexually exploit/abuse beneficiaries. Mitigation measures: PSEA policies put in place, practiced & monitored, with targeted communities well aware of policies and reporting platforms.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The existing WFP Helpline has witnessed a significant expansion in 2023 which expanded into complaint boxes and partners’ helpdesks. The line is open to all the communities that are reached through the different WFP activities and an informative flyer is contained in all the WPF food baskets that are distributed. A specific mechanism for SEA related cases has also been established so that allegations of SEA are immediately referred to trained specialized staff. All the Field Offices have an active PSEA focal point who has been trained on how to safely identify and report cases. Focal Points also raise awareness among staff about SEA related issues All WFP partners have been assessed within the PSEA Interagency Network on capacities around PSEA. The partners with low capacities and without clear policies to prevent and respond to SEA cases, will be trained in 2023 and supported in the development of strategies and policies.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

In order to minimize the potential adverse effects to the targeted communities and humanitarian workers while delivering nutrition interventions to the most vulnerable segments of the community, such as girls and boys under five and PLW, the sector carried out a thorough forecasting of likely risks that may be caused while providing nutrition services and developed a framework with mitigation measures. Risks related to activities carried out by the nutrition sector are anticipated to be mainly to all personnel working in nutrition projects including distribution staff, sensitization staff and CHW’s….etc” moving between camps, potential looting of nutrition supplies, attacks on health workers and facilities as well as beneficiaries accessing the facilities, weak follow up of beneficiaries and creating tension when targeting IDPs for distributions in the host community. In order to mitigate these risks, and ensuring adherence to humanitarian principles, the sector continues to adopt a “Do No Harm” strategy while providing nutrition services to vulnerable groups throughout Syria. These mitigation measures include training of nutrition implementing partners on the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, scaling up community-based approaches to ensure quality access to services.
