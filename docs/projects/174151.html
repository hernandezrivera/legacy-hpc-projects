Project Summary

The proposed interventions are for cholera preparedness and response in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) States. SI is the current lead for the Cholera Task Force under the WASH sector. The Task Force preparedness activities will be included as part of preparations for the 2020 cholera season, as well as SI specific preparedness and response activities. SI specific preparedness activities are as follows :
- Chlorination of water points and water quality monitoring at source and point of use for free residual chlorine & turbidity. (This activity is specifically planned August and December 2021) ;
- Communication and mass awareness raising campaigns, including production and dissemination of IEC materials. Household and community level hygiene promotion with focus on Cholera and COVID preventions;
- Desludging of latrines prior to the rainy season in SI areas of operation ;
- Prepositioning of contingency WASH supplies as a preparedness measure (IEC materials, chlorine, consumable hygiene kits and cholera kits) to be able to immediately respond in situations when cholera cases are being detected;
- Environmental clean-up campaigns ;
- In terms of response, SI is proposing to conduct the following activities, through its emergency team, in a 72 hours windows following a case or alert:
o Household disinfection of suspected cholera cases and their radius of 30m ;
o Distribution of consumable Cholera kits for households with suspected cases and immediate surrounding households;
- The Cholera Task Force led by SI will carry out the following activities in BAY states as part of its preparedness plan:
o Mapping of all partners, donors and areas of intervention per LGA, ward and community;
o Mapping of partners’ funding capacity;
o Compilation of available contingency stock for the common pipeline;
o Upgrade the exisitng reporting and monitoring tools;
o Capacity building of partners on WASH SOPs for cholera intervention, including water chlorination, household disinfection and hygiene promotion;
o Preparation and dissemination of dashboard.

1 - Needs

The complex emergency and con􀂧ict in North-East Nigeria started in 2009 and has resulted in the displacement of over 2 million people. Overall, 7.1 million people are in dire need of assistance across Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states. IDPs' situation is critical, as they are living in severely congested temporary camps, prone to regular epidemics, rain, fooding, and fire incidents. Due to increased hostilities and insecurity, the number of registered displacements continues to increase, and is expected to continue, as the Nigerian Government Forces adopted the strategy of establishing “super camps” to strengthen their defenses in population concentration, thereby removing troops from remote areas.As WASH lead agencies in Borno State, SI addressing some of these gaps through different interventions, ranging from emergency response to early recovery where possible. The formal IDP camps are already overcrowded, the huge number of these IDPs are settling down outside these camps within host communities in informal settlements. IDPs and host communities are thus compelled to share the already stretched resources leading to the insufficient quantity and/or unsafe water, inappropriate housing conditions and inadequate access to shelter, sanitation facilities and health care services. Poor living conditions, overcrowded camps and congestion create an unhealthy environment conducive to outbreaks (cholera/AWD, COVID), leading to tensions/conflicts. Under this project, the SI plan provides life-saving support to these beneficiaries by providing safe drinking water, emergency sanitation facilities and hygiene awareness along with COVID preventive measures while specifically targeting the IDP population within the host communities. This funding is addressing the con􀂧ict mitigation strategy and strengthening life-saving services to cope with foreseeable arrivals.In 2019, a number of cholera cases were reported from Adamawa state, and a high number of AWD cases in Borno State were reported, although a cholera outbreak was not declared. At the time of writing this proposal, the outbreak is yet to be contained. The rains in the area can demaged the WASH facilities and which further exacerbates a high likelihood of cholera epidemics. This strongly justifies the need for strong cholera preparedness and response activites. Solidarites International leads the Cholera Task Force which coordinates cholera preparedness and response in BAY states. The need for continued support to partners is required in terms of targeting hot spots, partner mapping, compilation of available contingency stock, training on SOPs and reporting of partner activities. This proposal will include activities that strengthen partner and sector preparedness for cholera response in order to mitigate the impact of cholera in the BAY states in 2021.

2 - Activities

Cholera activities will be conducted in BAY States through support of review of the current cholera outbreak and development of contingency plans for 2021, implementation of preparedness and response activities and continued leading of the Cholera Task Force. SI’s areas of operation and those will be considered for outbreak response through its dedicated emergency team. Preparedness activities will include: - Chlorination of 120 water points and water quality monitoring at source and point of use of free residual chlorine & turbidity. - Communication and mass awareness raising campaigns, including production and dissemination of IEC materials with clear cholera messages as well as Household and community level hygiene promotion sessions ;- Desludging of 1200 latrines prior to the rainy season in SI areas of operation. Male and female latrines will be targeted to improve access to sanitation for both sexes ; - Prepositioning of contingency WASH supplies as preparedness measure (IEC materials, chlorine, consumable hygiene kits and cholera kits) to be able to immediately respond in situations when cholera cases are being detected; - Environmental clean up campaigns every first day of the month from August to December in the targeted areas. - Household disinfection of 1200 suspected cholera cases and their radius of 30m The Cholera Task Force led by SI will carry out the following activities for the three states in Northern Nigeria: - Risk analysis, mapping and development of contingency plans ;- Mapping of all partners, donors and areas of intervention per LGA, ward and community ;- Mapping of partners’ funding capacity ;- Compilation of available contingency stock ;- Upgrade the exisiting reporting and monitoring tools through improving the kobo tool and dashboard which SI assisted the WASH Sector to develop in 2019. - Training of partners on WASH SOPs for cholera interventions, including water chlorination, household disinfection and hygiene promotion. 3 trainings will be conducted in the 3 states prior to the onset of the season.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

In accordance with SI’s core policies, assistance is inclusive and does not discriminate based on ethnicity, age, religion, gender, or sexual orientation. SI will implement these interventions focusing on the cross-cutting principles of gender sensitivity, participatory conflict analysis, community-based planning and Do No Harm. SI regard gender equality as a fundamental principle of their work, recognizing that it is a social, humanitarian concern rather than merely a women’s issue. SI are aware of the importance of including gender equality to increase humanitarian assistance efficiency and recognize that men, boys, girls and women have specific needs and constraints. Ignoring these specificities can have disastrous implications in terms of protection and survival for the victims of humanitarian crises. Participatory methods are used to access and incorporate gender equality and age perspectives in all aspects of the project cycle, focusing on gender during the design phases, with community discussion and assessments mandatorily conducted with groups of men and groups of women. Gender-related issues, including women’s, men’s, boys’, and girls’ roles and specific needs, were included in the assessment.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

SI strives to include all stakeholders throughout the project implementation. Joint planning and review meetings will be held between the project staff and relevant stakeholders including the beneficiaries to capture lessons learnt for better programming. During assessment and monitoring phases, beneficiaries will be interviewed and will thus be able to influence the design and implementation of the project. SI will also involve communities via staff hiring (Daily Workers, CHPs), so that they can benefit from the project interventions, not only as beneficiaries but also actively supporting the implementation of the activity. SI will mainstream Core Humanitarian Standards to ensure quality and effectiveness of assistance and demonstrate greater accountability to affected populations. Throughout the project, SI will strive to respect the five Accountability to Affected Population commitments, as defined by the IASC. The project beneficiaries are systematically included in the decision-making process at each and every step of project cycle. SI is committed to beneficiaries’ involvement to ensure the project fits their expectations and needs and to avoid negative side effects. The methodology followed is inspired by URD guidebook for participation. SI will ensure the respect of the Do No Harm principle through analyzing the possible unintended effect of each step of the project implementation to better mitigate them. In addition, SI will ensure that its existing Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM), managed by the MEAL department, will be accessible to all beneficiaries under this project, and that affected communities have been properly sensitized on how to access and use the CRM.
