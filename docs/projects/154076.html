Needs

The present revision is required to respond to increased needs arising from the deteriorating security situation in the center regions of Mali, notably Mopti and impacting displaced and host populations. The initial WFP planned coverage were defined based on needs identified by available food security and nutrition data, notably the February 2019 National Food and Nutritional Security Survey (ENSAN) and the March 2019 Cadre Harmonisé exercise, which projected that during the 2019 lean season 550,000 women , men, boys and girls would be in need of emergency food assistance and more than half percent of them are located in Mopti and Segou regions. The first half of 2019 has been marked by escalating violence and conflict, leading to a sharp increase in internal displacement. In the eastern part of Mopti region, including Bandiagara, Koro and parts of Douentza, inter-ethnic conflict, originally rooted in competition over land and natural resources, has increasingly taken on the character of a proxy conflict involving Government and extremist groups. This conflict has recently resulted in a series of militia attacks on civilian populations, involving significant loss of life. This has added to already high levels of internal displacement – since mid-2018, roughly 8,000-10,000 women men, boys and girls, per month have been displaced. In addition, while displacement had previously been for relatively short periods, it now appears to be open-ended in duration. In addition to displacement, which is relatively easily observed, constraints on access to land, livelihoods and markets has become an increasingly important threat to food security. While reports of conflict-related population movements enjoy a relatively high degree of acceptance within the humanitarian community, there was initially some scepticism regarding security related constraints to mobility and livelihoods in some areas. The recent use of satellite imagery to monitor agricultural activities have provided a degree of independent corroboration. Since January 2019, the deterioration in the security situation has led to accelerated population movements compared to 2018. From 61,000 in May 2018, the total number of IDPs increased to reach 147 000 in June 2019, of whoch 52% are women and 52% are boys and girls under 18 years old. Around 60% of the displaced persons are located in Mopti (50,000 male and female IDPs vs 10,000 at the same period of last year) and Segou (20,000 male and female IDPs vs 8,000 at the same period of last year) regions. The northern regions (Gao, Timbuktu and Ménaka) also continue to register increased numbers of male and female IDPs specifically in the neighborhood circles with Niger and Burkina Faso. This increase of IDPs creates additional pressure on scarce resources within host communities that are already experiencing chronic poverty and seasonal food insecurity. In Support to the Government and in coordination with other humanitarian actors, WFP will continue to provide food assistance to the most vulnerable households in areas identified as the most affected by food and nutrition insecurity. This assistance will be provided through cash-based transfer (cash or vouchers) and or food distribution depending on the context, beneficiaries preferences, cost-efficiency and operational feasibility. In addition to the 270,000 women, men, boys and girls, already targeted by WFP for the lean season assistance, additional 150 000 conflict-affected women, men, boys and girls including IDPs, vulnerable host communities and populations that have been prevented to rainfall due to insecurity will receive assistance up to end of the year.
