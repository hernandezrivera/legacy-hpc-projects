Project Summary

Aligned with ERP Health Sector Objectives 1 and 2, SAMS will: 1) address the critical hospitalization needs of at least 100 migrants as a result of the pandemic and Lebanon’s socio-economic situation; and 2) procure and distribute PPE to five hospitals within SAMS' referral network, based on assessed needs. Note, hospital selection will be done in coordination with the Health Sector and nominated by Mr. Hisham Fawwaz - MoPH focal point. With regard to provision of critical life-saving hospital care, SAMS will provide financial top-up contributions to support the patient share, diagnostic care, and the non-covered cost (material) to vulnerable migrants, as well as the mandatory PCR tests associated with these hospital admissions. Medical vulnerability criteria will be established in coordination with IOM, ICRC, Caritas and other key stakeholders, to determine which migrant patients will receive financial top-up contributions. With regard to payment modality, there will be no transfer of funds to patients. Rather, contracted hospitals that have been vetted by SAMS will submit invoices to SAMS for review and payment for costs of around $1,000 per patient on average. The cost of $1,000 per patient was determined in coordination with LHF and IOM, based on experience with hospitalization cost coverage for patients. In case patients with catastrophic illness require more support (ie. complications/longer-term support), SAMS will strive to cover the cost in full, negotiating also with contracted hospitals. Only patients that SAMS has pre-approved for treatment will be supported under the project.
The project will decrease protection concerns for patients, including the confiscation of ID and legal documents, the long separation of patients from their caregivers, refusal of the hospital to admit or discharge the patient because of medical debt, and the refusal to release the bodies of deceased patients through MOUs with hospitals that name SAMS as the responsible party for payment rather than the patient. As a secondary outcome, this project sustains hospital functionality during Lebanon’s confluent crisis while strengthening their administrative procedures for future patients. This will contribute to COVID mitigation and the protection of both patients and health workers in hospital settings via PPE provision and PCR testing for admitted patients. All activities will be closely coordinated within key stakeholders within the Health Sector - MOPH, IOM, OCHA, Health Sector WG NGO members, and hospital focal points.
This proposed response addresses the immediate needs of migrants, prioritizing the most vulnerable through temporary solutions, with the aim to mitigate the rapid deterioration of social and economic conditions while contributing financially to strengthening Lebanon’s public health system via income opportunities. As a complementary measure, SAMS will support hospitals with PPE as a means to respond to COVID needs of patients.
As an immediate result: 100 migrant patients experiencing severe economic hardship will access previously unavailable medical care while being alleviated of any protection concerns associated with hospital admissions (such as document confiscation and refusal to admit/detention in hospitals due to a lack of payment). 5 hospitals, selected in coordination with the MOPH based on needs, will receive PPE- 250,000 facemasks, 100,000 gloves, 125,000 headcovers, and 40,000 shoe covers, procurement of which has been hindered due to staff shortages and inability to pay suppliers due to a lack of hard currencies and reduced imports into Lebanon.
As a long term result: Hospitals will benefit from additional income and patient bills paid to avoid further MOPH deficits.
This project is currently 81% funded; the gap remains for provision of PPE support to five hospitals.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Objective 1: In coordination with relevant working groups and key stakeholders to establish medical/vulnerability criteria and raise awareness of available support, SAMS will target 100 migrant workers across Lebanon to support with hospitalization costs, allowing patients who may not otherwise afford the remaining bill to access life saving surgeries and urgent care. Referral sources include: IOM, community outreach volunteers, migrant worker community leaders, and NGOs with established support programs for migrant workers: MSF, Caritas, and ICRC. SAMS will provide up to $1000 per patient for migrant workers, according to the following selection criteria: admission to ICU is needed, intervention will be lifesaving (as opposed to chronic), persons may require multiple admissions, prolonged medical conditions that will drive up the long term cost. SAMS will consider these persons for multiple hospital admissions, provided they have not exceeded the per patient budget of $1000. No funds will be provided to the patient directly; rather, SAMS will contract with pre-vetted hospitals and pay them directly for services. Objective 2: After coordinating with the MOPH focal point to select facilities and finalize their PPE needs lists, SAMS will procure PPE for 5 hospitals (1 per governorate) to mitigate dangers caused by COVID. Hospitals in need with the highest number of health workers will be prioritized for distribution.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

During program design, SAMS consulted with the PSEA Network and SGBV WGs to ensure top-up contributions are equally accessible to men and women, including persons with special needs. The project will decrease protection concerns for patients, including the confiscation of ID and legal documents, the long separation of patients from their caregivers, refusal of the hospital to admit or discharge the patient because of medical debt, and the refusal to release the bodies of deceased patients through MOUs with hospitals that name SAMS as the responsible party for payment rather than the patient. Specifically with regard to PSEA, trained Safeguarding Focal Points will be responsible for ensuring that hospitals display reporting mechanisms, and will be ready to support survivors with consent-based referrals, as needed. All project staff are also trained by a Country Security Officer on SAMS Safety and Security Policy.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

In-keeping with the commitments of the Core Humanitarian Standard on Quality and Accountability, SAMS will welcome feedback from beneficiaries and will react constructively to concerns in a timely manner. Recognizing that individuals have access to different means of communication, SAMS utilizes various methods for receiving confidential feedback/concerns through the following methods:1. Receiving feedback over the phone or through hotline numbers, which are advertised in PHCs, flyers, WGs, and other places SAMS accesses beneficiaries2. Receiving feedback through satisfaction surveys/key informant interviews3. Advertising the SAMS services are free of chargeSAMS will ensure consistent and effective communication with patients through pre-procedurephone calls, post- procedure calls, and satisfaction surveys. A project monitoring plan will be overseen by the MEAL Coordinator, including field visits, patient service verification, monthly quantitative reports, document audit, etc.
