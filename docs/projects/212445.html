Project Summary

Based on 2024 Humanitarian Needs Overview, in 2024, 16.7 million people (8.4 million female, 8.3 million male) are assessed to need humanitarian assistance across Syria, up from 15.3 million in 2023. Of the 16.7 million people in need, 5.5 million are displaced, including over 2 million who live in last resort sites.
Conflict-related casualties, disruptions to essential services, and earthquakes exacerbate protection concerns. The absence of longer-term solutions, ongoing violations of international law, infrastructure destruction, and limited access to services contribute to vulnerability.
Children, more vulnerable to trauma, necessitate immediate child protection and psychosocial support.
The project focuses on improving access to GBV and CP services in the community center established in Idleb (Idleb city). The community center aim to provide a space where men, women, boys, and girls can socialize and strengthen previously fractured social networks to ensure that a greater number of people have access to information related to GBV and CP services in their communities. Women and girls can socialize and gain access to lifesaving GBV services, including case management, PSS for survivors focusing on vulnerable groups such as divorced, widows, females with disabilities. Additionally, they will gain access to other GBV activities such as Life Skills, Free spaces for women and girls, vocational Training, Awareness raising using (GBV-SC toolkit), training of Humanitarian actors (non-specialist) on GBV risk mitigation. In addition to providing direct support service and referrals to other services according to the referral paths that are shared by the sub-clusters and creating a service mapping through coordination with actors in the area, continuously updated. Sadad will also provide Community-based protection services to ensure that IDPs and the host community are effectively involved in the project life cycle from design to implementation to feedback through the already established protected feedback channels to help them report suggestions, complaints, and PSEA concerns (Sadad is a member in PSEA Network).
This project will enhance equitable access to quality Child Protection services including parental skills, MHPSS services, PSS, capacity building, awareness raising, Case management & Referrals for children survivors of violence, exploitation, abuse, neglect, child labor, early marriage, children exposed to severe/extreme psychosocial distress ,13172 beneficiaries including (4700 (Elderly, Adults) and 8472 ( Children, 500 Young Children, and 500 PwD). The proposed intervention will be implemented with particular emphasis on quality services to People with Disabilities (PwDs), PwDs will be targeted through center and community AR program, Who will be identified through the established community committees and field trips of the mobile teams, and supported by transportation from and to the community centers which will be rehabilitated to be inclusive for PwDs. In addition to elderly women and men.
Sadad will establish community committees from the same communities and train them on protection topics. The activities will emphasize equity and non-discrimination, reinforcing the basic social and human rights of participants while taking into consideration the cultural, historical, and social species of targeted populations. The project will consider the environmental situation from the perspective of children/youth with disabilities and the individual needs for all of them. The communities centers will be provided with disabilities facilitation; e.g. ramps, toilets. Sadad will use the M&E tool kit to monitor the GBV activities. To ensure effective coordination is in place, SADAD will continue to attend CP SC monthly coordination meetings and will submit 4Ws reports.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

"The project, centered in Idleb (Idleb city) and Aleppo (Afrin), focuses on enhancing access to GBV and CP services in community centers. These centers serve as spaces for socialization, providing crucial information and support related to GBV and CP services. They offer lifesaving GBV services, including case management and PSS for vulnerable groups like divorced, widowed, and disabled females. Additionally, they provide various GBV activities such as Life Skills, vocational Training, and Awareness raising using the (GBV-SC toolkit). The project also ensures Community-based protection services to engage IDPs and the host community effectively throughout the project life cycle, addressing suggestions, complaints, and PSEA concerns. Children's protection needs are prioritized, especially considering the high number of unaccompanied children. Reports from November 2023 highlight significant humanitarian needs, including internally displaced individuals and conflict-related casualties. To address these concerns, the project will enhance access to quality Child Protection services, including parental skills, MHPSS services, and capacity building. The intervention targets 13172 beneficiaries, including People with Disabilities (PwDs), who will be supported through center and community AR programs. Community involvement is crucial, with community committees being established and trained on protection topics. The project emphasizes equity, non-discrimination, and the preservation of basic human rights while considering cultural and historical contexts. Environmental considerations, particularly for children/youth with disabilities, are also taken into account, with facilities being provided with disabilities facilitation. Sadad will employ the M&E tool kit to monitor GBV activities and ensure effective coordination through regular attendance at CP SC monthly coordination meetings and submission of 4Ws reports."
