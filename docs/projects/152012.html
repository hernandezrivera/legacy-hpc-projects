Project Summary

With financial support from Global Affairs Canada (GAC-MHD), Plan International Nigeria (PI-N) will implement a 24-month intervention aimed at improving resilience and reducing the vulnerability and impact of Gender Based Violence (GBV) on populations affected by conflict in North-East Nigeria. Through this project, PI-N will target 23,000 women, girls, men and boys in 3 Local Government Areas (LGAs) in Borno State with integrated GBV and Livelihood assistance. Project interventions will directly contribute to the key objectives of the Nigeria Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), including increasing program coverage in newly liberated areas and also aligns with Plan International’s regional Lake Chad Initiative. The main targeted outcomes of this project are:
1. Increased knowledge of and capacity for self-protection and recovery from GBV.
2. Strengthened formal and informal community-based protection mechanisms and services.
3. Increased knowledge and skills of survivors and individuals at risk of GBV for generating income.
4. Reduced barriers to income generation opportunities through the provision of cash/material resources.

To achieve these outcomes, the project will focus on actions that will increase access to prevention and response services for GBV survivors and individuals at risk of GBV. 1,000 GBV survivors will receive survivor centred case management services including referrals for special services including clinical care as well as mental health services. Additionally, activities to improve resilience and coping mechanisms of women, girls, men and boys affected by GBV and fostering social cohesion will target 5,000 people through community-based psychosocial support provided in 5 safe spaces that will be supported by the project. Livelihood support in the form of income generation and skills training will reach 900 survivors or individuals at risk of GBV. PI-N will increase local capacity by strengthening community-based mechanisms, increasing awareness on the impact of GBV through outreach to 18,000 individuals and will work with communities to identify GBV risks in the targeted communities and develop action plans to mitigate these risks.

Needs

Now in its eighth year, the Boko Haram crisis is adding to the long history of marginalization and chronic under-development as well as high rates of poverty, illiteracy and un- and underemployment in North-East Nigeria. The North-East has experienced chronic under-development where poverty indicators continue to worsen with each year of the conflict – despite an overall poverty decrease in every other region in the country . Girls and young women are facing increased risk to GBV, including sexual violence, exploitation, early pregnancy and forced marriage in North-East Nigeria. A wide range of sexual violence has taken place and people abducted by Boko Haram, survivors of rape, and children born out of sexual violence face stigmatization and are subsequently ostracized. The culture of silence, stigma and blaming women and girls who have experienced GBV is a factor contributing to the under-reporting of GBV cases; this is further exacerbated by the lack of trust in the current system. Gender norms and socio economic inequalities severely undermine the role of women in the community and society.In many communities, the absence of men and adolescent boys and an increase in female-headed households reveals the prevalence of arrest and forced recruitment of men and boys, which heightens the risk of GBV and other protection risks. Sexual exploitation, rape, assault, and threats of GBV while conducting daily activities such as collecting firewood and water, using latrines, travelling to nearby communities etc. has been reported by women and girls to be a time when they are most at risk to physical and sexual violence. Sites assessed by PI-N confirm these findings with 50% of sites reporting that sexual violence occurs while collecting firewood. Other common responses reported sexual violence while fetching water, while walking to school and to their farm; and at home. Additionally, 75% of sites assessed by PI-N reported that girls are the most affected by sexual violence. Livelihood activities in Borno State have been severely hampered over the last 8 years of the conflict, with commercial and other economic activities becoming paralyzed. Analyses carried out on the rise of the Boko Haram insurgency by the Government of Nigeria (GoN) and other non-governmental bodies within and outside the country have identified unemployment as one of the root causes of the problem. The growing number of unemployed youth in particular has contributed negatively to the insurgency; therefore, as part of the early recovery and durable solution strategy, the affected state governments and humanitarian actors are placing more emphasis on the restoration of livelihood activities through vocational and skills development. The State government has developed strategies to train vulnerable youth groups by adopting livelihood and Income Generating Activities (IGA) training packages for youth affected by the conflict, especially young women. PI-N’s assessment also found that in households that lack access to livelihood or economic opportunities, women and girls adopt negative coping mechanisms such as engaging in transactional sex. To address the increasing needs of women, girls, men and boys who may come forward to report incidences of GBV, the capacity of GBV service providers including community based mechanisms need to be strengthened through training and ongoing supervision and support. Further, survivors of GBV and women and girls at risk of GBV need to be supported to build their resilience through integrated survivor care interventions that address their psychosocial needs, alongside activities that reduce barriers to engage in livelihood activities.

Activities

PI-N is adopting a gender aware approach that provides for immediate response and builds resilience. It targets the immediate protection and food security needs through livelihood and cash intervention for GBV and SEA survivors from both IDPs and host populations. The approach ensures meaningful consultation with civil society organisations, boys, girls, women and men especially GBV and SEA survivors through rapid needs assessments and continuous input through participatory monitoring to inform the direction of intervention. This approach will ensure that specific needs of GBV and SEA survivors are addressed and benefit from the project interventions, while understanding that the root causes of exclusion and gender inequality, SGBV/GBV and protection require longer term interventions. Local community structures will be actively involved in mobilizing and identifying the project beneficiaries, participating in direct implementation, and coordinating with UNICEF, UNOCHA including protection, Early Recovery/Livelihood clusters and the Cash Working Group. PI-N’s strategy focuses on gender participatory programming throughout project cycles and will encourage women and men in communities to take agreed responsibilities within this specific project cycle and beyond. The specific activities proposed in this project—protection, livelihood and cash—will also address PI-N’s broader emergency response plan.
