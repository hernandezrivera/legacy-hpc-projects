Project Summary

The project urgently aims to enhance equitable access to significant and effective utilization of high-quality lifesaving nutrition services. It explicitly targets the early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition in vulnerable populations, including children aged 6 to 59 months and pregnant and breastfeed women (PBWs) of childbearing age. The project has set a target to aid 73,270 individuals in the Counties of Fangak Canal/Pigi, Leer, Ayod, Aweil East, and Twic East.
This includes addressing Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) in a significant number of 23,346 children, composed of 10,573 boys and 12,773 girls, as well as Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM), impacting a substantial 33,468 children, composed of 14,723 boys and 18,745 girls) and supporting 16,456 pregnant and lactating women. The project's comprehensive approach includes providing comprehensive nutritional interventions and treatment for MAM and SAM and implementing mother, infant, and young child nutrition (MIYCN) counseling that emphasizes social behavior change communication,celebrate WBW1Aug
This effort aims to improve household dietary intake, explicitly targeting 52,757 children aged 6-59 months who will receive Vitamin A supplementation. Furthermore, by the end of 2025, around 52,757 children aged 12-59 months will benefit from deworming initiatives, comprising 25,376 boys and 27,381 girls The Healthcare Foundation Organization project seeks to align with the strategic objectives of the Nutrition Cluster’s humanitarian response by the end of 2025, focusing on the early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition of girls and boys under five years of age. In addition, the project will support 16,571 children aged 6-23 months and 23,642 PBWs during the blanket supplementary feeding program (BSFP) distribution. the project will be integrated with health, WASH, FSL, and GBV mainstreaming and work in collaboration with (CHD), and the State Ministry of Health (SMOH), IPs, integrated MIYCN counselling during the distribution of vitamin A deworming services.
Through active community mobilization efforts, community outreach will be integrated with health, WASH, FSL, and GBV mainstreaming strategies, including distributing WASH kits and Long-lasting Insecticidal Nets (LLINs); the specific objectives of this project include:
1. To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services to prevent malnutrition and build resilience among children, adolescents, and women in prioritized locations by the end of 2025 =
2. To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of waste for girls and boys under five years of age and PBW in prioritized locations by the end of 2025
3. To enhance the nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis, and utilization of nutrition information by the end of 2025, the project will implement robust monitoring and evaluation systems, conduct regular data analysis, and use the findings to improve the nutrition situation and program effectiveness.
4. To strengthen coordination & integrated programming with health, WASH, FSL, Education, & Protection sectors for equitable access to services that address the key drivers of malnutrition among vulnerable children and women in prioritized locations
The project plans to recruit and support community nutrition workers, volunteers, and MIYCN counselors to implement these initiatives. The recruitment process will involve transparent consultations with community members and local authorities to ensure community participation in the selection criteria, focusing on gender balance and disability inclusion.
The overall nutrition response will prioritize accountability to affected populations (AAP) at all stages. HFO staff will adhere to the Nutrition Cluster and Global Nutrition Cluster operational framework for AAP, PSEA which include upholding guidelines to prevent harms to beneficiaries.

1. Needs

The malnutrition crisis in South Sudan continues to deteriorate in 2025, driven by multiple, converging humanitarian crises. Approximately 4.3 million children under five, as well as pregnant and breastfeeding mothers, will need life-saving treatment and preventive care. This includes 0.65 million children under five at risk of severe acute malnutrition, 1.4 million at risk of moderate acute malnutrition, and 1.1 million mothers requiring urgent treatment (IPC-AMN 2024). In 49 counties, acute malnutrition rates exceed the emergency threshold of 15%, with one county in Upper Nile projected to reach IPC-AMN phase 5. By mid 2025, eight out of ten children under five are anticipated to be at risk of acute malnutrition, & 7.69 million people, including 1.5 million young children, facing severe food insecurity. Repeated outbreaks of fever, diarrhea, and respiratory infections, coupled with poor nutrition, water, and sanitation access, add to the crisis. The situation is further aggravated by economic challenges, climate impacts, ongoing conflicts, heightened GBV risks, and spillover effects from the Sudan crisis. An estimated 67 percent of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr El Ghazal state, Unity, seven counties in the State (Leer, Mayendit, Panyijiar, Guit, Koch, Mayom, and Rubkona) are currently classified in IPC AMN Phase 4 (Critical), while the other two counties (Abiemnhom and Pariang) are in IPC AMN Phase 3 (Serious). An estimated 702,000 people (60.6 percent of the population) in Unity State are in Crisis (IPC AFI Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity between September and November 2024. The situation will deteriorate in Leer, Mayendit and Panyijiar counties, but remain the same in Guit, Koch, Mayom and Rubkona counties – all 7 of which will remain classified at IPC AMN Phase 4 (Critical). An estimated 748,000 people (65 percent of the state’s population) are likely to be in Crisis (IPC AFI Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, ( Ayod, Canal/Pigi, Fangak, Twic, East,) are all classified in IPC AMN Phase 4 (Critical) An estimated 228,426 cases with acute malnutrition need nutrition services in the State. The State contributes 11 percent of the total burden of acute malnutrition in the country. Acute malnutrition among children is driven by low Minimum Dietary Diversity at 23.2 percent, Aweil East, Aweil North, Aweil South, and Aweil West) are classified in IPC AMN Phase 4 (CrisisThe HFO with support from Nutrition Cluster will enhance coordination, implementation, and localization efforts, especially at sub-national levels state and counties, and strengthen real-time nutrition data systems for faster response and resilience programming. Humanitarian assistance will transition gradually to government support, leveraging platforms like the Health Sector Transformation Project (HSTP), Peace in Health and Humanitarian and Development nexus. Healthcare Foundation Organization (HFO) will continue strengthening robust nutrition information system in the states and counties the nutrition life-saving interventions will target primarily children under 5 years, pregnant, breast-feeding women, and adolescent girls. HFO will target more than 73,270 people in need of nutrition services through community Mid Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC screening, treatment of SAM/MAM cases and referrals for SAM with Medical complications to stabilisation centres, training of mother to mother support groups and fathers to father support group to enhance the prevention of malnutrition at the households by increase knowledge and skills in local food productions in order to reduce hunger and malnutrition. Nutrition information system tools such as the FSNMS, and SMART and other nutrition vulnerability assessments, will continue to be used to monitor the nutrition situation and for real-time prioritisation of population in need for urgent and time-sensitive life-saving nutrition interventions.
