Project Summary

The project aims to addressing the most pressing needs of the most vulnerable individuals. The project will enhance livelihood and food security of 4410 HHs/22,050 HHs members (about 315 HHs/1575 household members per locality (14 localities)) and reduce dependency on aid among IDPs and host communities in South Kordofan state (Abu Jubaiha, Dilling, Kadugli, Rashad, Talodi), West Kordofan (Keilak, Lagawa), East Darfur (El Ferdous, Bahr El Arab, Assalaya, Abu Jabra) and North Darfur state (El Fasher, Mellit, Tawilla).
This will be achieved through diversified livelihoods activities and provision of employment opportunities for youth, to increase food security through capacity building, appropriate production techniques and provision of required tools, equipment and inputs. The project will provide capacity building trainings for national humanitarian actors, farmers and community members including women and youth and disable people on FSL approach, vocational skills training, social development and small business management' and providing necessary inputs. Also will train the most vulnerable individuals on poultry production/veterinary training and distribute chicken/Hens and cages for the most vulnerable families.
The project also designed to increase income through improving access to self-managed loan saving and loan services and capacity enhancing opportunities for diversified sources of income generation.

1 - Needs

The main humanitarian needs in Sudan result from several factors: New and protracted displacement due to conflict, and chronic poverty and under-development including due to economic sanctions, affect access to basic services, disrupts the livelihoods and food security of many people and exposes the displaced to serious risks to their safety and dignity.The 2020 HNO identifies 6.2 million individuals in Sudan that are in need of timely and adequate food and livelihoods security assistance, an increase from 5.7 million individuals in 2019. The rapid deteriorating economic situation, conflict and natural shocks in the country had an overall impact on the agricultural season and the food security of all. As a result, at least 17.7 million individuals, representing 42 percent of the population suffer from some level of food insecurity. Under the Food Security and Livelihoods Sector, 6.2 million people need food and livelihoods related humanitarian assistance. Based on Sudan’s IPC food insecurity August 2019, 5.8 million individuals are estimated in need of food and livelihoods assistance to mitigate food insecurity. Major concerns exist for the States classified under Phase 3 and above, in particular, South Kordofan, Red Sea and the three Darfur States (Central, North and South) which reported a significant increase of food insecure people compared to previous analysis. Overall, more than one third of households reported adopting food-based coping strategies including eating less preferred food, borrowing money to buy food, limiting portion sizes, reducing the number of meals and reducing adult consumption favoring smaller children to maintain minimum food consumption levels. Additionally, 54% of the households resorted to livelihood based coping strategies, depleting their livelihood assets including spending savings, reducing health expenses, and selling last female animals. The main drives of the deterioration of the food security situation are related to the significant macroeconomic challenges still prevailing the country, resulting in high inflation rates, soaring food prices and shortages of foreign currency and cash.In S/WK and E/ND, IDPs, refugees and host communities remain highly vulnerable to food security and livelihood services as ongoing conflicts continue to limited access to income earning opportunities and market access. Primary food prices increased reaching levels of two to three times during the month and discontinuation of wheat and wheat flour subsidies and the continued depreciation of the Sudanese Pound have contributed to increases in staple food prices and reductions to household purchasing power (Sudan- Complex Emergency USAID 2018). Lack of IGA’s project for the families who host unaccompanied children, Lack of awareness about the consequences of early marriage, domestic violence and child labor. As per the latest FSL figures for 2020 (PiN – Targets per localities), this project will prioritize interventions in the most vulnerable localities in:South Kordofan State (Abu Jubaiha, Dilling, Rashad, Kadugli, Talodi) as the verified target out of the people in need is 31,086, 130206, 50828, 322003, 23146 respectively. West Kordofan State targeting Lagawa and Keilak (the FSL sector target is 15,513 in Lagawa & 10,475 in Keilak). East Darfur State (Abu Jabra, Assalaya, Bahr El Arab, El Ferdous) since the verified target among PiN is 12,617, 25,349, 43,655, 24,252 respectively.North Darfur State targeting El Fashir, Millit and Tawilla . The FSL sector target among PiN is 109,292, 29,264, 26,127 respectively.

2 - Activities / Outputs

1. Build the capacity of 420 national humanitarian actors (30/locality) from local CSOs, Local authorities, Line ministries (MoA, MoSA …) etc. on FSL preparedness, response, monitoring and information management in the humanitarian setting.2. Strengthen 280 farmers' organizational capacity (20/locality) to organize and form networks of farmers in each targeted locality for coordination, support each other, exchange lesson learned and experiences 3. Train and support 420 women on self-managed savings (30/locality) to form 70 Village Saving and Loans Associations (VSLA) (5/locality), with 15 members per association/group4. Train 420 women and female youth (30/locality) from the VSLA groups on market oriented diversified income generation activities and marketing skills. 5. 280 youth (140 male & 140 female) receive vocational skills training in market appropriate trades that are consistent with participants own interests (20/locality). 6. Necessary start-up kits to launch their vocational related livelihoods will be provided on successful completion of training 7. Conduct social development training targeting 420 women and youth from girls and boys (30/locality) the most disadvantaged families in the targeted sites and includes: governance, peace-building, democracy, role of law, health awareness, gender awareness, group work, legal awareness, conflict resolution, human rights, violence against women and alcoholism8. 420 youth (male & female) will be trained on small business management' (30/locality) including life skills training; record keeping; small business financial management; savings promotion; marketing and sales; and conflict resolution 9. Train 280 of most vulnerable (orphans, women-headed households, women with special needs) (20/locality) on poultry production/veterinary training (breeding and marketing) and distribution of chicken/Hens and cages (5 chicken per family).10. Facilitate workshops for 420 husbands and male community leaders (30/locality) on gender issues to make men understand gender concepts, change their attitude about gender equity and act as change agents to influence other men within the community

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Environmental mainstreaming will be integrated through the entire project management cycle. Environmental mainstreaming will be integrated in the activities to ensure that environmental harm is reduced or eliminated and environmental benefits are maximized. Key environmental concerns will be built into the project in order to reduce deforestation in Sudan. Almanar will attempt to switch away from fired bricks to alternative construction methods such as stabilized soil blocks (SSB) or concrete blocks (CSB) depending on the prerequisites of the region. Environmental mainstreaming will be included during monitoring and evaluation of the project outcomes of the response to ensure that the effects sought was indeed delivered, and to help identify possible negative environmental effects in time in order to adjust activities accordingly. It is a necessary part of ensuring that the principle of do no harm is fulfilled. Furthermore, to help identify best practices and lessons learnt that offer valuable insight for making improvements in future aid efforts. Almanar will work with local NGOs, CSOs and communities including working with the national and state governmental authorities, since Community consultation and participation is a key part of environmental management. Throughout the project cycle gender will be considered to access to, and management of natural resources to receive consultation from both men and women. The combined information will give more accurate information on the total need for the community and ensure equally assessing.

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

One of the most significant factors, which is increasingly recognized as an integral component of UN programming, is the gender equality and protection of girls and women. Inequalities between the sexes typically reflect, and can increase, vulnerabilities of young girls, teens, may face different protection risks, have different needs and choices, and possess different skills, knowledge and coping strategies. The project activities, all throughout the execution, will aim to ensure community participation through the project cycle, capacity and vulnerability analysis, reduce the level of exploitation to women and promote protection of girls in communities and families with the support of legal and traditional protection systems. The project will use and advocate for Minimum Standards of Protection package amongst the targeted communities all through the project implementation. This project through the referral pathway will ensure that women, girls and boys victims of S/GBV, elderly, disables and their families and guardians are fully aware of the available services around them. Almanar will ensure the services locations are safe and accessible to all without discrimination, and ensure that the beneficiaries receiving services in dignity and confidentiality.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Accountability will be ensured across the activities in this project through establishment of complaints and response mechanisms at project level aimed at ensuring that the voices of the affected populations will be heard. The existing community committees and community leaders will be actively engaged at all levels of the project including management, monitoring and evaluation. Complaints handling mechanisms will be established to ensure that complaints are comprehensively analyzed and responded to with the beneficiaries getting feedback on a monthly basis. These committees are expected to be gender and ability sensitive with the inclusion of both males, females, elderly and people with disabilities to ensure that all voices, including those of the marginalized are heard, equality and equity are realized with regards to access to services that will be provided by the project. Project staff will ensure adequate consultation with the communities to ensure that existing complaints handling mechanisms are incorporated into the complaints and response mechanisms to be established by the project.Almanar staff is trained and signed the sexual exploitation and abuse policy and committed to it with close monitoring by the supervisors
