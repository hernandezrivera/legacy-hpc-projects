Project Summary

Under the first strategic objective of Cameroon HRP 2021, the WASH Sector has planned to provide services as per the agreed standards for immediate, early and short-term response, to address context specific WASH needs of NWSW regions of Cameroon. As per UNICEF’s project, under Cameroon HRP 2021’s Outcome, UNICEF plans to provide lifesaving assistance to people newly displaced by conflict using early recovery approaches to improve the overall access to WASH services, connecting the need for short-term emergency interventions with long term solutions and applying development principles by working directly with local communities to facilitate ownership and accountability. UNICEF seeks to improve and sustain access to safe water in target communities in South West and North West regions, and on improving and sustaining access to safe sanitation facilities and hygiene messages addressing malnutrition related risks and enhance the resilience of affected population to unexpected events or hazards e.g. disease outbreaks
Data collected about the situation through the 2020 MSNA, MIRA and Emergency Tracking Tool (ETT) were used to design the project. According to specific information gathered during assessment visits, IDPs are in urgent need of:
- Water supply systems as a majority have been neglected and in need of repair/rehabilitation to increase community’s resilience to disease outbreaks.
- Knowledge of good water, sanitation and hygiene practices including COVID-19 prevention as basis for survival
- WASH kits (family kits, menstrual kits and water treatment kits)
-Household water treatment techniques
-Open Defecation Free environment
- Adequate personal hygiene for health improvement
Based on the actual situation, UNICEF plans to conduct the following WASH intervention:
- Support hygiene, sanitation and environmental health promotion, as well as awareness about COVID-19 prevention and water quality in affected areas and host communities;
- Increase access to safe drinking water through community training of water management committees, rehabilitation or construction of water sources, small water supply networks and distribution of household water treatment kits;
- Provide WASH kits to ensure a clean environment, maintain contingency stock for cholera and ensure adoption of good water, hygiene and sanitation practices;
- Ensure promotion of menstrual hygiene.
The response targets 380,000 IDPs and host communities. Proposed interventions will be implemented with other key sectors with priority focus on nutritional, protection and health related issues. Other partners will be involved in the WASH response; this includes local and International organizations. UNICEF will make two staff members available for the WASH response coordination and provide technical support to actors involved in carrying out the emergency WASH response during the intervention. Specific needs will receive special attention, as well as beneficiaries’ protection during the emergency WASH intervention, particularly at sites where internally displaced populations are gathered.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The humanitarian situation in the North-West (NW) and South-West (SW) regions of the country continues to appear to be worsening with frequency infighting and incidents reported among the non-State armed groups (NSAGs) and the military. 3.0 million people are estimated to be affected by the crisis with more than 1,600,000 people in need of WASH assistance, out of which 608,000 people will be targeted in 2021 (HRP 2021). UNICEF through this project has targeted The situation has forced people to flee across four regions seeking in villages and bushes that are hard to reach subjecting them to different kinds of suffering e.g. lack of access to basic services like water, health and education In terms of WASH, primary water sources and quality vary greatly, with populations residing in more rural areas seemingly unable to provide clean water for the households. Water treatment is not widely practiced, nor are some primary hygiene practices such as hand-washing Water supply coverage is still low with an overall of more than 53.4% of the population in urban and rural areas lacking access to an improved water source. The most affected divisions in the North West are Menchum (85.6%), Ngo Ketunjia (68.8%), Donga Mantung (68.3%) and Momo (56.1%); Manyu (68.1%), Lebialem (68.1%) and Ndian (56.9%) are the most affected divisions in the south West region. In at least 23% of the assessed villages, most people have access to a functional latrine. In 39% of assessed villages, it takes between 16 and 30 minutes to get to the closest water point. In 8% of the villages, water points are at more than a 60 minutes distance range. Mezam and Donga-Mantung divisions in the North-West have the highest number of assessed villages (corresponding to more than 10% of assessed villages in the North-West) where it takes more than 60 minutes to reach the closest water point. In 71% of the assessed villages, a round trip to fetch water from the nearest water points takes more than 30 minutes. According to the MSNA 2020 findings, 38%, 41% and 46% of the assessed village reported that that far water points, Lack of water containers and Few water points, respectively, were the main primary water access challenges. Concerning sanitation, the Menchum division in the North West is the most affected with almost 27.9% of population practicing open defecation (OD). As far as access to basic sanitation is concerned, pits without slabs, reported by 63.2% of the assessed villages, are the main sanitation facilities used by affected population, making it difficult to maintain them. With regard to Hand washing facilities, 24% of the assessed villages reported that almost no one has access to HWF. The situation is not good considering the presence of COVID-19 pandemic and frequency of cholera outbreaks in the South West region. Menchum (61.2%) and Momo (43.9%) are the most affected divisions in the North-West and South-West regions. Based on these needs, the organizations plans to provide WASH services using minimum WASH standards of SPHERE and/or national standards. The project will deliver an integrated package of WASH infrastructure and services improvements to communities and institutions, with due consideration for solutions that are resilient particularly to Climate related disasters, hygiene promotion, and capacity building activities for the main stakeholders, within the framework of National WASH priorities. It will provide tangible improvements in living conditions for an estimated 380,000 people. The expected ultimate outcome of the project is reduced burden of WASH-related diseases among men, women, boys and girls and improved community and IDPs resilience to hazards and diseases outbreaks in SWNW.
