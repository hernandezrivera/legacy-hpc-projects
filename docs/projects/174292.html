Project Summary

ACTED’s proposed 12-month intervention aims to deliver life-saving CCCM and Emergency Shelter and Non-Food Item (NFI) assistance, directly benefitting 9,000 displaced and conflict-affected persons in Dikwa, Monguno, Mafa and across two informal out-of-camp sites in Borno State. The Action will build on ACTED’s previous experience in the target sites, providing urgently needed S/NFI provisions to complement ACTED’s on-going CCCM interventions in 8 camps in Dikwa, 6 camps in Monguno and GGASS Camp, Mafa. Moreover, ACTED will offer critical CCCM assistance to two informal sites and out-of-camp settings in Borno State, ensuring access to critical services for vulnerable communities, underserved by the current response. The Action will build on extensive coordination and collaborative efforts implemented by ACTED with OCHA and Sector partners in order to provide comprehensive S/NFI and CCCM assistance modalities, which address critical risks, support 2020 rainy season preparedness and complement COVID-19 readiness and response efforts for vulnerable IDP populations in both formal camp sites and informal out-of-camp settlements in Borno State.
Emergency S/NFI assistance will be delivered to 6,250 vulnerable, displacement-affected households in Dikwa, Monguno and Mafa via protection-centered and CCCM-led referral pathways, through the provision of 400 Bama-type shelters, 400 Emergency Shelter Kits and 420 NFI kits. ACTED proposes to repair and rehabilitate 450 dilapidated Dikwa-type shelters, previously constructed under a NHF-funded project in GGASS Camp, Mafa in between 2017 and 2018. ACTED’s S/NFI component will directly contribute to the S/NFI Sector’s decongestion strategy in Dikwa and aims to relieve critical and immediate S/NFI needs, targeting vulnerable IDP households that have received no prior assistance, or that are living in damaged and vulnerable shelters and require assistance ahead of the 2020 rainy season.
To address the multiple critical risks compounding IDP’s protection, health and dignity within both formal and informal camps, ACTED proposes comprehensive CCCM services to improve strong coordination amongst operational actors, strengthen rationalization of aid at camp-level, and ensure robust risk communication and community engagement as fundamental to pragmatic, principled, and protection-oriented programming. ACTED will catalyze on strong operational experience and existing robust relationships with IDP and host communities in Mafa to continue static CCCM site facilitation services in GGASS Camp, while complementing LGA-level COVID-19 emergency preparedness and response efforts through effective information management and site facilitation at camp level. In addition, a mobile CCCM response will aim to increase access to life-saving CCCM services amongst IDP populations residing in two informal camp site and camp-like settlements through the establishment of information centers, capacity building of participatory governance structures and RCCE.
ACTED anticipates that the project will benefit 8,350individuals with S/NFI assistance (calculated at 5 persons per household), as well as all 2,750 IDPs situated across two informal camp and out-of-camp settlements (calculated using IOM DTM data). However, as ensure double counting of beneficiaries receiving shelter solutions and NFI assistance is avoided, only individuals receiving shelter assistance are accounted as direct beneficiaries (6,250). In total, ACTED expects to assist 9,000 direct beneficiaries.

1 - Needs

Identified needs and activities proposed by ACTED have been informed by monthly CCCM site profiling assessments conducted by ACTED as leading CCCM agency in 16 camps across Dikwa, Mafa and Monguno throughout 2020; ACTED’s CCCM progress monitoring assessments carried out in target camps in November 2019 and April 2020; a shelter assessment conducted in GGASS Camp Mafa in March 2020 and REACH’s 2019 MSNA. Common themes highlighted by assessments findings, as well as field observations and monthly beneficiary complaints and response reports included: high over-congestion rates and prevalent gaps in Shelter/NFI service provision, increasing protection risks and overall vulnerability of IDP populations to COVID-19 and other disease outbreaks. In Dikwa, ACTED’s CCCM assessments recorded deplorable, severely over-congested shelter conditions, with high makeshift prevalence rates, including 60% in Shuwari, 70% in Kamchejin, 90% in Modu Kasa and 100% in Mohamed Kyarimi, rendering the practice of social distancing and other COVID-19 prevention measures practically unfeasible to avoid epidemiological expansion. Similarly, in Mafa and Monguno, ACTED’s CCCM site profiling estimated a mere 3.5m2 average of individual living space and assessed only 61% of overall shelters meeting SPHERE standards. Additional protection and safety risks resulting from S/NFI service gaps were highlighted in ACTED’s March 2020 shelter assessment in GGASS Camp, Mafa. Assessments recorded all 945 presently occupied Dikwa-type shelters, previously constructed with NHF funding (2017-2018), as severely damaged, aggravating health conditions amongst vulnerable populations groups. In tandem, non-food item needs continue to affect IDP households, significantly increasing risks of adopting negative coping strategies, and rendering affected families vulnerable to associated protection risks. ACTED’s November 2019 multi-sector needs assessment in Monguno recorded 75% of respondent households reporting not having previously received any NFI aid, underscoring omnipresent NFI needs. The recent outbreak of COVID-19 in Borno State has further exposed the extreme vulnerability of IDP populations, with unpredictable population movements compounding transmission risks and camp-level outbreaks threatening to further deteriorate pre-existing humanitarian drivers. Overcrowded conditions and S/NFI gaps underscore poor social determinants of health, increasing IDP vulnerability to not just COVID-19, but also malaria, Lassa fever and cholera, the latter posing elevated risks of flooding expected during the 2020 rainy season. Accordingly, the presence of CCCM agencies serves as a first-line defence against these compounding critical camp-site risks, including fire hazards or flooding, while operationalizing effective camp management mechanisms for emergency and COVID-19 response coordination. This is particularly relevant for underserved informal IDP camps, without a tangible presence of humanitarian partner , leaving ACTED as CCCM agency to fill critical gaps in service provision and install COVID-19 prevention and response measures to avert excess mortality and transmission risks to the host community. Critical needs to extend live-saving CCCM services to underserved, informal sites throughout Nigeria’s Northeast was further corroborated by REACH’s 2019 MSNA, which highlighted significant needs amongst 313,443 IDPs living in urban host communities or informal settlements. Findings informed ACTED’s CCCM Strategy for 2020, introducing a mobile response to target informal sites and settlements, 80% of which are primarily concentrated in Borno State.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Key protection principles have informed the design of the project, and will be actively present as a core component of implementation. ACTED will ensure that activities conducted prioritize the wellbeing, safety and dignity of beneficiaries at all times. Activities will avoid incurring any harm, will ensure meaningful access to S/NFI assistance, and will promote accountability, participation and empowerment of beneficiaries. All ACTED staff will uphold protection principles at all times, as signatories to ACTED’s Code of Conduct, including ACTED’s Policy on PSEA. Moreover, as a leading CCCM agency in all proposed target sites, ACTED will actively engage with GBV and protection partners to refer protection cases identified through the CRM. In order to ensure comprehensive protection mainstreaming across the project, the following measures will be taken for each of the protection principles: 1) Prioritize safety, dignity, and do no harm: ACTED will continuously monitor the security context in areas of intervention. For distribution locations ACTED’s team will ensure that they are located in safe and accessible locations and at a reasonable distance from beneficiary homes. Distribution times will be decided with regard for the safety of individuals that are required to travel, to avoid harm when traveling to or from the site. All distribution sites will have gender-segregated queues with mandatory COVID-19 social distancing measures and disinfectant materials provided on site. Under the CCCM component, ACTED will integrate gender and protection mainstreaming, ensuring inclusion of vulnerable groups in decision-making processes. Strong referral mechanisms will be enacted, enabling direct referral of any vulnerable IDPs to relevant service providers, including victims of assault or GBV. 2) Ensure meaningful and equal access: Beneficiary selection will be conducted based on need and vulnerability, and will be conducted in liaison with site CCCM Teams, protection partners. When possible, the AMEU will conduct household level assessments to inquire on target individuals’ living conditions and needs against selection criteria. During distributions, particularly vulnerable groups brought forward through protection or CCCM referrals will be prioritized, including women-headed households, the elderly, PWDs, and unaccompanied minors. Elderly individuals and persons with disabilities (PWDs) will be provided with priority access at the distribution site and supported with the provision of hired casual labor to carry home distribution kits. Moreover, the CCCM Team will strengthen participatory camp governance structures, facilitating direct inclusion of beneficiary groups into decision-making processes, facilitating greater equitable and meaningful access to assistance programs. ACTED will take additional steps to ensure that PWDs can be reached, as well as other vulnerable groups, and ensure that information on services and facilities is widely distributed throughout the sites in ways that do not rely on literacy. 3) Promote accountability, participation, and empowerment of beneficiaries: Beneficiaries will be members of camp committees, ensuring strong participation in camp governance, channeling ideas and influencing the wider humanitarian coordination and response planning, and play a vital role in the implementation of site maintenance activities, providing foundation for community-led camp management and interventions. Daily laborers, hired to support of distribution to vulnerable beneficiary members, including PWDs, will be recruited from the IDP community to provide employment opportunities, with hiring process reliant on a strict equal opportunities basis and considerate of protection and gender aspects. Accountability will also stem strongly from the CRM, ensuring timely and appropriate response to complaints. Moreover, PDM exercises will assess beneficiary satisfaction and inform further programming.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

ACTED’s established Complaints and Response Feedback Mechanism (CRM), mainstreamed throughout all CCCM-led interventions in Northeast Nigeria, facilitates safe, accessible and transparent feedback channels for affected populations. ACTED’s CRM enables beneficiaries to voice concerns, complaints and suggestions, while informing ACTED’s partner coordination efforts at camp-level and thereby increasing overall accountability of service providers and the wider humanitarian response. Installed CRM mediums, including complaints boxes, complaints desks, a telephone hotline and email, allow for contactless communication methods that mitigated transmission risks of COVID-19 at camp level, while granting anonymity for confidential reporting of protection concerns and feedback to ACTED as CCCM agency. ACTED’s independent AME Unit will maintain responsibility for collecting and responding to all reports, addressing complaints through protection-centered case-management support and systematically investigating, referring and following up on beneficiary complaints with protection partners and other service agencies in a timely and transparent manner. ACTED’s trailed and tested CRM provides an effective two-way communication channel, ensuring direct links to target populations, and inclusion of beneficiary and broader community voices in program implementation. Further, information gained through the CRM is looped back into the design of program approaches, as ACTED continuously adjusts intervention activities according to feedback received. All ACTED staff will be strictly compliant with the code of conduct, and any breach of the code of conduct uncovered through the CRM will be responded to with serious measures.
