Project Summary

A core component of Camp Management roles and responsibilities is to engage with displaced populations to:
a) ensure access to assistance, protection and services in camps and camp-like settings.
b) Inform, consult, mobilize and support community structures in a location, such as camp leadership or women’s groups, to address barriers to participation, particularly for underrepresented groups, so they can participate in camp activities and decision-making processes at every stage of the camp lifecycle.
CCE, community owned enhanced coordination and management systems are required to ensure equitable access to services for displaced persons in informal settlements. People displaced to informal settlements and camp like settings are not having their basic needs met due to inconsistent service provision, exclusion from accessing services, and the sheer absence of services due to poor targeting of settlements, while they are at risk of forced evictions. The lack of core services and site planning contributes to serious public health risks, preventable deaths, and increased risks of gender-based violence especially for women and girls. Existing coordination and management mechanisms are largely informal, with most sites being managed by Informal Site Managers (community leaders or gatekeepers), frequently imposing a barrier to constructive and consistent community communication engagement with affected populations. These mechanisms have limited accountability and little adherence to minimum standards, exposing displaced populations to recurrent protection violations, including diversion of assistance and violations of safety and integrity of individuals.
The IASC, HAP, Sphere core standards among others acknowledges the need for AAP and that it is vital for recipients of humanitarian assistance to feel that they have a voice and agency in determining the assistance that is needed. In addition, it is also recognized that with strong community communication engagement strategies, recipients of humanitarian assistance can provide relevant and timely feedback to improve the humanitarian response, overcome disputes and engagement challenges that are sure to arise in the humanitarian context in Somalia.
This proposal presents a comprehensive package of innovative Community Communications and Engagement (CCE) activities, led by the development of both physical and radio, community meetings involving host and IDP communities with all stakeholders including local authorities and aid agencies. This will ensure a coordinated engagement strategy with all parties, that provide full accountability to all affected populations and mitigates risk of conflict between the two groups and or any other kind of conflict and explore viable strategies to durable solutions as the IDPs, host communities will come up with their own solutions to their problems as CCP documents, shares with development partners and other humanitarian actors operating in Sool and Nugaal for inclusion in their plans according to each organization area of focus and intervention to avoid duplication and offer maximum Accountability to Affected Population (AAP).
This project will build upon CCP expertise in CCE and Communication with Communities (CwC), particularly the pre-existing networks of listening groups, focus groups and community partnerships in addition to, a systematic and formalized meeting system. This will be implemented in Sool and Sanaag and their surrounding collective sites, and once implemented they will provide clear avenues for all humanitarians in these locations to further engage and communicate with the communities they serve. The primary purpose of these meetings will be to make sure that information is shared between all sides rather than a place for decision-making. They are designed to supplement and consolidate IDP/ community engagement rather than be any form of the decision-making process.

Project Activities

Activities• Drafting and consultation process of localized solutions and guidance notes in CCE with the help and guidance of a CCE expert hired by CCP to work with the camp committees, shelter and Camp Management and Preparedness Programme (CMPP) working groups, CCCM partners• Support training on the rights of IDPs, and host communities on CCE and human rights, gender and diversity awareness, and the rights of men, women, girls, boys, children, persons with disability and older persons.• Setting up of community-led CCE working groups in each of the camps as part of the camp management structure, including female members, persons with disabilities, minority groups representatives of the camp sections and the CMPP members• Training in community engagement for local leaders from all civil society groups so that they are equipped to; represent their own communities or groups at the meetings, develop constructive relationships with humanitarians. • Training NGO staff to fully participate in CCE activities including in the meetings and effectively utilize the CWC methodology in their daily activities. • The recording and broadcasting of all meetings with specialized follow-up programming (radio shows and programs) via DALJIR radio stations including targeted programming representing each participating cluster and working group. • The continued collection of listening group data including homogenous listening groups working with vulnerable populations (such as women, children, persons with disability and the elderly). • Produce and facilitate the translation, into local languages, and dissemination of IEC training materials on CCE and CwC.• Through CCE/ CwC foster space for dialogue among the authorities, IDPs and civil society organizations, participate, support development of national/regional plans relating to the establishment and management of communal settlements incorporating solutions and exit strategies which are voluntarily laid out by the affected population.• Raise awareness among IDPs, HC, local and national authorities of the need to promote the self-reliance of IDPs and other affected populations as early as possible for durable solutions.• Ensure mainstreaming of cross-cutting issues including protection, environment, HIV/AIDS and age, gender and diversity through CCE strategy• Link CCE strategy to assistance provided in communal settings with return and reintegration policy that includes durable solutions• Use CCE to develop a Community-based Environmental Action Plan (CEAP) to manage the environment and surrounding area. This identifies critical environmental and related livelihood and social issues that can cause conflict between host and camp communities because of reliance on natural resources for economic activities and support.• Engage IDPs/ HC through CCE to identify the different capacities and skills of the members and agree on the areas on which training and capacity-building opportunities are needed. (CCP recognizes that even IDPs have got talents and ideas that can be supported to greatness)• Establishing of 4 information centers as feedback mechanisms , within the IDPs and community settings for inquiries and referrals e.g. aid delivery points, legal aid projects or information centers, and on existing procedures for issuing replacement identity documents, procedures for land, housing and property restitution, and support for family reunification

Mitigation Measures

CCP Safeguarding Policy will be our guiding policy, The project intends disseminate information on sexual violence concerns and available services that survivors of such abuses can benefit from and further share information on how such abuses can be reported not on the on the side of the beneficiaries but also the staffs that shall be implementing the project. By capacity building this project will improve the channels of community engagement for the most vulnerable which will directly impact humanitarian efforts in the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse issues. The project consultation didn't only look at the community leaders but focused further on the different ages and genders across the selected locations for information and opinion which better informs the projects.The project intends to have feedback reporting suggestion boxes mounted in strategic points, whereby the person giving feedback or complain has the liberty to disclose his/her identity or choose to remain anonymous, community meeting in relation to the project activities with the community leaders to solicit feedback and further also intends to hold monthly meetings with women and girls who attend activities at the WGFS to address their concerns on the activities enrolled and make changes on the activities.

Please provide further explanation

CCP has already and will continue to engage the community in gathering evidence of the needs and available resources and use this to agree on the purpose, scope, and timescale of the engagement and the actions to be taken for community to achieve long-term and sustainable outcomes, processes, relationships, decision-making, and implementation monitoring and evaluationCCP will strive to ensure that camps, settlements and IDP-hosting areas conform, to the extent possible, with the principles of protection-based physical planning as regards site-selection, layout, and distribution of aid and design of services. This includes, for example, ensuring adequate distance from conflict or other sources of violence; security lighting; community participation in family-plot layout; allocation of adequate space per family; safe access to resources, such as food, water and firewood, and services, such as police, camp-management offices, sanitation facilities, schools, markets and community centres; and establishment of child-friendly spaces. Work together with partners to ensure that gender, age and disabilities are taken into consideration. The project will monitor the safety of beneficiaries through CCE dialogue and feedback reporting mechanisms framework taking into account safety needs of women men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities across all age groups.In line with the grand bargain, the APP is core to this project. By continuing to contribute to ongoing beneficiary satisfaction surveys through the CCCM cluster CCP can monitor beneficiaries’ perceptions of how the level of information provision and representations is meeting their needs. The feedback mechanism proposed by CCP will monitor the effectiveness of our communications and engagement with affected communities. The project is highly sustainable since it takes a community-led approach focusing on CCE, DRR, CMPP, and CEAP
