Project Summary

(New Proposal)
Similar to other parts of Oromia Sawena, Dawe and Serer Woreda's of Bale Zone specifically have been suffering from serious drought. The identification of drought-affected areas, carried out by UN
has focused on Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI), which includes factors
such as precipitation and heat. Findings show an estimated 6.8 million people living in
areas meeting “dry” criterion. This project targets
specifically Bale Zones (Oromia) with ESNFI Response for Drought-affected HHs in Seweyna and Dawe Serer woredas.. The project will target a total of 15,575 individuals (3,115 HHs). Of the total:
- 9,267 individuals who are IDPs will receive the Lowland ESNFI kit budgeted at 133 USD.
- 3,972 individuals who are non-displaced but in sub-standard shelter (15%) will receive the Lowland ESNFI kit budgeted at 133 USD.
- 2,336 individuals who are disabled (15%) will receive the Lowland ESNFI-Disable kit budgeted at 168 USD.
Ethiopia is experiencing a prolonged drought following three consecutive failed rainy seasons since late 2020 affecting 6.8 million people living in Oromia, SNNP, Southwest and Somali. Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 29.7 million people need humanitarian assistance in 2022. The same document revealed that the drought affected over 8 million people living in Oromia, SNNP, Southwest and Somali Regions. Worsening the situation, the March/April 2022 rains are below normal, making it the fourth consecutive failed rainy season .
The drought and conflict induced displacement are exacerbating food insecurity and malnutrition, pushing people to migrate longer distances in search of water, pasture, and security. The disasters have severely affected both crop and livestock production. People that have been affected by drought and conflict have experienced losses in crop production/missed crop cycle, damaged infrastructure such as irrigation schemes and/or water catchment systems and resulting in lack of income and loss of assets. In certain areas in the southern lowlands, crop production loss was as high as 70% due to the 2021 drought. As per the 2022 HNO, a total of 14.4 million people, of which 64% are in extremely severe need and require assistance to meet their agricultural needs.
CRS has a robust emergency program, implementing ESNFI activities in West Wellega, Gedeo and Guji, East and West Hararghe, West Gonder, South Omo, and Tigray through its local church partners. CRS’ proposed implementing partner, Ethiopian Catholic Church Social and Development Commission Branch Office of Robe (ECC-SDCO-BR), has a long operational history in the area with experience of different development and emergency project. ECC-SDCO-BR has assisted IDP/returnees. In addition, CRS and partner are prepared to scale up response efforts to meet the acute needs of IDP’s returnees’ populations more comprehensively. CRS and its partners will map and address barriers to access that could influence the people’s gendered and inter sectional ability to participate in ESNFI interventions. Additionally, responses will ensure that ESNFI services are within safe and easy to reach locations for the affected population. To ensure quality programming and ownership, the community will actively participate in all phases of the project cycle.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

CRS and it's implementing partner are both part of the Caritas International is network and share common values and approaches as well as a long history of working together to support local communities in times of crisis across Ethiopia. CRS works with local partners to identify and assess needs and collaboratively work on project design. CRS and its local partners will be responsible for the direct implementation of the intervention. CRS Ethiopia’s Emergency Project Officer and Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) Project Officers will lead the monitoring and evaluation of the project. CRS' robust MEAL Unit will spearhead all monitoring activities using the emergency contextualized Simple Measurement of Indicators for Learning and Evidence-based Reporting (SMILER) M&E system. The project will develop brief tools for data collection, implement participatory data planning, monitoring systems, and organize meetings with community members that involve both women and the youth (male and female), elders, people with disabilities, people with special needs, and other relevant social community groups to solicit issues and comments that will be considered in the ongoing project implementation. CRS accompanies each partner for an agreed number of days per month aligned with critical programming activities to provide technical support and to monitor activities. CRS provides support and oversight during partner-led registration/validation and distribution events. CRS staff lead the following activities in close collaboration with partners: gender and protection assessment, market assessment, capacity building on GBV/PSEA, accountability and staff technical skill, setup of compliant and feedback mechanism, beneficiary targeting, all post distribution monitoring activities, engaging with the national shelter clusters, and working groups as appropriate, reporting financial and narrative report to donors are key activities. The MEAL Project Officers assess the progress and implementation of each activity, in-line to meet set indicators and Sphere standards. CRS has robust financial, management, and logistics processes in place to ensure the highest quality programming from our partners through routine partner assessments

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

CRS and its partner are committed to saving lives, protecting livelihoods, and enhancing resilience to shocks of the most vulnerable households in conflict-affected communities. Target communities are primarily valued stakeholders at every stage of the project cycle. Accountability to project participants and communities is one of the core values in CRS. CRS and its implementing partner will highly involve target project participants in conducting the feasibility study, implementation, and in monitoring and evaluation activities. Regular meetings with community members that involve men, women, boys, girls, and people with disabilities will be organized in coordination local Government at the woreda level. Depending on the preference of project participants, CRS will design and apply Community Feedback and Response system to facilitate two-way communication. Emphasis on Women and Children. Project activity designs and project plans will be discussed in focus group discussions and results will be incorporated into project implementation plans and activity design. FGDs will be conducted with women and men in separate sessions. To further reinforce accountability, promote social cohesion and mitigate conflict sensitivities, CRS and partners will also ensure that targeting of project participants to be in consultation with the local community representatives in which the most vulnerable segments of the society are fairly included in all activities. Project activity/distribution areas will be selected based on the feedback/comments from the local communities to ensure no negative effects to people accessing sites. Verification of beneficiary lists with the local communities will also be done. This will be assessed by conducting post distribution monitoring after the distributions are made. In order to avoid/minimize the possible exploitation (abuse of power) of the affected communities with the power imbalance dynamics there will also be posters developed with respect to individual and/or group entitlements along with expected staff conduct. There will also be community sensitization on staff code of conduct and program participants protection aspects to prevent happening of possible violations and improve community awareness on how to interact with CRS and partners staff during project implementation
