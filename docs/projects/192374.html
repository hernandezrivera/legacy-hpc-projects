Project Summary

The project is the continuation of a first phase (October 2020 - December 2021) through which La Chaîne de l'Espoir strove to support the access of the most vulnerable populations in Lebanon to the medical and surgical care necessary to improve their health condition, particularly following the Beirut port explosions of August 4, 2020.
In accordance with the mandate of La Chaîne de l'Espoir, this project aims to continue this support, and to strengthen access to secondary and tertiary care for vulnerable populations through financial coverage of surgical care relating to multiple specialties (trauma, neurosurgery, etc.).
The project thus contributes to the reduction of the financial barriers (but also physical and social) encountered by the beneficiaries, in a context of strong economic, political and social deterioration – this being characterized in particular by a significant reduction of the purchasing power of the populations coupled with an increase in price of medical and surgical care.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

CDE identify vulnerable people, aged 0 to 60, in need for tertiary healthcare services but not in capacity to afford them through various sources: patients calls directly CDE's hotline, referrals from hospitals, referrals from other NGOs, etc. The services cover general surgico-medical care such as trauma, neuro-surgery, etc - with an average cost per patient estimated around 1,388 USD.CDE contracted 4 hospitals with high healthcare services' quality, located in Beirut (Rosary Sisters, Hotel-Dieu de France, Beghazi Medical Group) and Mount Lebanon (Abou Jaoude). After identification of patients and approval provided by CDE, patients originated from across Lebanon benefit from the required life-threatening and life-saving tertiary healthcare services within one of the 4 hospitals. CDE cover most of the hospitalization costs, leaving a reasonable patients' share - however there is no patients' share for extremely vulnerable patients. Throughout the whole process, CDE ensures regular follow-up with the patients and their families and externally refer them when additional needs are identified (psychological support, food security, protection, etc). Globally, CDE is proactive in collaborating with the other few actors intervening in the tertiary healthcare sector (such as the UNHCR, UNRWA, MoPH, Ordre de Malte, etc) to ensure there is no duplication of assistance: CDE assists Lebanese patients (so no duplication with HCR/RWA) and covers pathologies not covered by other actors.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

CDE ensures its codes of ethics, its child protection policy, C19 policy and its PSEA policy are strictly respected at all times (existing alert and CF mechanisms in place). For instance, documents such as the Code of Ethics is shared with, explained to and signed by CDE staff, partners and suppliers. CDE continuously analyzes the context and the exclusion factors (gender, age, disability, etc) to foster social cohesion and to design conflict-sensitive projects to mitigate the barriers identified. Provided the large portfolio of surgeries covers, it allows CDE to reach out adequately to 0-60 yo women, men, girls and boys - all followed-up by CDE even after discharge. CDE team, partners and suppliers are sensitized to ensure safe identification, treatment and follow-up of patients are made through an inclusive lense, provided barriers to health services are most commonly faced by women/girls, elderly, people with disabilities and SGBV-survivors (financial, physical and social barriers).

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

AAP is one of the core values of CDE's code of ethics. As such, accountability is taken into consideration at all times: conduction of needs assessments, face-to-face multi-sectorial needs assessments, visits of patients to hospitals, direct line patient-CDE via CDE's hotline/CFM (phone/whatsapp), patients' post-surgical follow-up, satisfaction surveys, etc: all feedback (including beneficiaries' and non-beneficiaries, i.e. vulnerable people contacting CDE but not eligible) are considered throughout the cycle (pathologies to consider to cover, prevalence in specific areas, coverage of transportation, etc). CDE continuously involves its partners, suppliers and coordinates with other NGOs and relevant authorities in order to collect both quantitative and qualitative information. These various set of data are analyzed on a regular basis and allow CDE to adjust its implementation and further project design to further tailor-make assistance to the most vulnerable communities in Lebanon.
