Project Summary

SCSD proposes a 12-month action uses a flexible integrated programming approach that includes agriculture and livestock interventions, using pre-positioned in-kind, voucher and Cash for Work (CFW) assistance modalities.
This program is a planned action, contributes to the second sector objective of the FSL cluster which is "Support self-reliance of affected households by protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income generating opportunities to save and sustain lives" and aims at increasing the resilience and livelihood opportunities for most vulnerable male and female farmers and livestock breeders of both IDPs and affected host communities, living in the sub districts of Afrin, Jandairis, Azaz, Mare and Daret Azza (Aleppo Governorate), and the sub districts of Harim, Dana, Salqin, Kafr Takharim, Qourqeena, Armanaz (Idleb Governorate) in NW Syria, in order to revitalize of agricultural and livestock sectors in the target locations in the medium-long term.
Where various farming and livestock breeding were major pillars of providing employment and livelihood opportunities, this program will target 1,500 farmers (1,000 olive farmers, 500 summer vegetables farmers), and 1500 livestock breeders (1,000 sheep breeders own 1-10 sheep/or goats and 500 cow breeders who own one cow).
The target farmers and livestock breeders will be supported with agricultural and livestock inputs needed to help then to return to their income generation activities through the most appropriate modalities. Provision of livestock and agricultural support will be synchronized with technical support on the best agriculture and livestock practices that will maximize the impact of project interventions.
Moreover, this program aims at creating short-term job opportunities (one month during olive harvesting season) of most vulnerable women (600 women over 18 years, of them 300 women of the host community and 300 women from IDPs) through CFW intervention for the harvesting of olive orchards owned by the target olive farmers.
SCSD will identify conduct multi-sectoral needs assessment to verify needs, priorities and gaps, determine level of severity and identify specific vulnerable groups within communities. To ensure the most vulnerable households are selected, SCSD conducts a socio-economic vulnerability assessment scoring exercise based on aggregated vulnerability scores. The beneficiaries will be prioritized according to a clear vulnerability and eligibility criteria.
The overall target number of beneficiaries is 3,600 households / 18,000 individuals (1,500 HHs supported with agriculture interventions, 1500 HHs supported with livestock interventions and 600 HHs supported with Cash for Work assistance modality).

4 - Needs

According to to the FFS HNO 2020, the agriculture sector has had significant losses since the beginning of the crisis in 2011 affecting both crop and livestock production with a noted decline of 49 per cent and 51 per cent respectively. Widespread damage to irrigation systems and shortages of labor and quality inputs such as seeds, fertilizers and fuel due to imposed sanctions on Syria has affected the country’s production. It is estimated that during the first three years of the crisis sheep numbers fell by 45 percent, goat numbers by 30 percent and cattle numbers by 40 percent. In addition, slight reduction in sheep and cattle were reported during 2018/19, as compared to the previous year. The constraints for livestock production include prevalence of common epizootic disease, slow success rate of artificial insemination livestock feed prices and destruction of veterinary services. The overall impact is high prices of livestock and their products that generally show an upward trend compared to previous years. youth, PWDs and members of local councils, people in the targeted location was depend on income generation activities-whether agricultural/livestock activities or different income generating activities associated with agriculture and livestock value chains. Because of the conflict, several barriers have been identified which have left a negative impact on the local communities and livelihood opportunities which led to a high suffering because of the lack access to income for families. Women-headed households and PWDs remains to be one of the most vulnerable populations within the target locations, as they are restricted by both movement and access to the labor market. Income generating small businesses had been crippled totally or partially as a result of socioeconomic situation, spending of savings and the accumulation of debt that financial inability to continue the income generation businesses.

5 - Implementation of activities

The program includes the following main livestock and agricultural activities: 1. Support 1,500 Livestock breeders (1,000 sheep breeders & 500 cow breeders) who own 1 cow or maximum 10 sheep/goats with compound fodders. Each sheep/or goat will be supported with 0.75 kg of compound fodder & each cow will be supported with 10 kg compound fodder daily sufficient for 4 months. 2. Establishment of Mobile veterinary clinic & provision of veterinary services and vaccinations campaigns (Enterotoxemia, Smallpox, and Foot-and-mouth). 3. Delivery of training program on the best practices for livestock breeding. 4. Distribution of agricultural inputs through the most appropriate modalities for 500 male and female farmers sufficient to plant 4 dunums of summer vegetables (1 dunum Tomato, 1 dunum cucumber, 1 dunum eggplant & 1dunum pepper). Each targeted farmer will receive 8 gram of tomato hybrid seeds, 200 gram of cucumber hybrid seeds, 20 gram of eggplant local seeds & 20 gram of pepper local seeds. Each dunum of summer vegetables will be supported with 20 kg of NPK & 20 Kg of Urea fertilizer 46% and 50 liter of fuel for supplementary irrigation.5. Provision training program based on GAPs for summer vegetables farmers. 6. Support 1500 male and female Small and Mid-sized olive framers (owned 3 to 10 dunum) with agricultural inputs, agricultural services and harvesting support sufficient for 3 dunum. Specifically, each olive farmer will be supported with 100 kg compound NPK, 2 liters pesticides, 1 Scissor, 1 Hand Saw & 12 tins (capacity of 16 liters). 7. Delivery of training program on the best practices for olive farming.8. Implementing of olive harvesting (3 dunums ) through CFW to be conducted by 600 women. Each woman will receive 6 USD per day in order to perform the harvesting activities for 30 days, followed by exchanging of cash vouchers by Hawala agent. 9. MEAL activities (verification, base line, PDM, end-line and final evaluation of the program).

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk1: Sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment) of women & girls. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation: Establish a confidential complaint mechanism, Train staff on code of conduct, Code of conduct in all staff contracts & Address any SEA complaints. Risk2: Tension between host communities, IDPs & returnees. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation: Ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs and not based on status, Selection criteria are transparent and groups are fully informed, engage with the communities to sensitize the population, Make effort to target a % of vulnerable host community in every intervention that aims to support the IDPs, Establish feedback mechanism and conduct follow-up visits if needs be and Involve the local stakeholders throughout the process. Risk3: Risk of physical harm at distribution points (security, safety). Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation: Carry out thorough safety assessments on the areas where the distribution sites are planned to be set-up, postponed or cancelled, Well organized distribution to avoid overcrowding, Training to staffs on establishing and managing distributions and crowded management, Application of checklist at distribution sites, Ensure that food/vouchers distribution are completed in time for beneficiaries to reach their homes during daylight, Consider specific risks to women and seek community opinion on how to best avoid and mitigate risk, Communicate with national and local authorities, stakeholders and community leaders, Establish priority lines for vulnerable persons, such as disabled, elderly and pregnant/lactating women and Set-up a door-to-door distribution for the very vulnerable HHs. Risk4: For cash and vouchers there is the risk of corruption at the stage of beneficiary selection. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation: Strengthen over-sight mechanism, develop a range of measures and internal procedures to minimize the risk of corruption.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption1: SCSD has full access to the targeted communities and the beneficiaries regularly throughout the life cycle of the program. Risk: the escalation of conflict exacerbates insecurity in targeted area affecting the SCSD’s ability to operate and access it. Mitigation: SCSD will commit to ongoing monitoring to ensure the operational context is secure enough to ensure access to the targeted communities, Hiring of field staff from the local community of the targeted areas, Purchase of materials from the local market with guarantee the Do No Harm principles, Communicate, coordinate and consultation intensively with LCs, stakeholders and other NGOs operating in the areas, Re-direction of programming into other areas. Assumption2: Aid delivery continues to be accepted and possible in Syria despite of the presence of various armed groups. Risk: Implementation of the durable activities is prevented/stopped by regular clashes, violence, or an upsurge in criminality including theft or kidnapping. Mitigation: SCSD will monitor the security developments and reported criminal instances/violence along its main delivery routes and in its areas of operation. If an increase in criminality/violence is noted, alternative routes will be used and additional warehouses will be rented to mitigate against the possibility of an armed group seizing control of all of SCSD stock inside Syria, or consider alternative areas/activities under the planned intervention. Assumption3: Markets are functioning properly in the areas identified. Risk: Due to the unpredictable situation in NW Syria, there is a risk markets are not functioning properly due to lack of access and/or supplies. Mitigation: SCSD will conduct a Rapid Assessment for Markets in the initial stage of the program and situation of the market will be monitored on regular basis by the program team. In case the situation would change, SCSD would consider relocating the area of intervention where the required conditions are met.
