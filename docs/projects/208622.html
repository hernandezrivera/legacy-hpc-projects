Project Summary

"The overall results of the project will include supporting humanitarian system to improve its tools, accountability and reporting modalities for identifying and addressing the needs of crisis affected women and girls. This will include enhanced collection of sex and age disaggregated data for evidence-based identification and prioritization of needs and strengthened capacities of Humanitarian Coordinators, Humanitarian Country Teams and Humanitarian Clusters/Sectors and GBV sub-cluster and PSEA/AAP mechanisms to effectively respond to the intersecting needs of women and girls with the most needs.
To achieve the project objectives, the project will be using a two-pronged approach. At the field level, there will be the provision of sustained capacity support, in collaboration with OCHA, to UN led country-based humanitarian coordination mechanisms – including the Humanitarian Coordinators, Humanitarian Country Teams and clusters/sectors -This will be through:
• Development of specific evidence, disaggregated data and gender analysis to inform the development of OCHA led Humanitarian Response Plans to ensure they adequately identify and address the specific needs of affected women and girls.
• Establishment/Enhancement of inter-agency and inter-cluster coordination Gender in Humanitarian Working Groups to support the mainstreaming of gender across all clusters.
• Complimenting the work of the GBV sub-cluster by utilizing the promotion of empowerment for women and girls as a strategy towards mitigating their exposure to protection risks, including exploitation, abuse and gender based violence.
• Accelerating Grand Bargain localization agenda through the capacity building of local women’s organizations and networks and facilitating their right to engage with humanitarian planning, decision making and monitoring/accountability (with a focus on those organizations that work with crisis affected women and girls, including those at heightened risk of violence, exploitation and abuse).
• Capacity building of humanitarian leadership, stakeholders, including cluster leads, government officials, implementing agencies etc on the integration of gender into humanitarian action.
• Development of localized accountability frameworks in partnership with local/women’s organizations to hold humanitarian actors accountable to existing policy commitments, standards and designated roles and responsibilities relating to the integration of gender into humanitarian response."

1 - Besoins

"Crisis affected populations everywhere face difficult challenges. But in the case of women and girls - who make up approximately half of the 235.4 million people in need of humanitarian assistance globally - the disadvantages, exclusion and violence they face are exacerbated due to pre-existing gender-based discrimination and inequality, negatively affecting their ability to access the services they need, the enjoyment of their rights and their subsequent ability to survive and recover from crisis shocks. Further discrimination is often manifested through other forms of intersectionality, including age, disability, religion, sexual orientation and/or gender identity. The crisis that Niger has been going through since the coup of July 26, 2023, comes as the country was already facing a serious security, humanitarian and climatic crisis affecting its 26 million inhabitants (50.3% women), including nearly 4.3 million people in need of humanitarian assistance. Women and young girls, the first victims of these multiple crises, require special attention.Whilst the humanitarian system has made progress in recent years in terms of commitments and available tools for the integration of gender equality and the empowerment of women and girls into humanitarian action , much remains to be done to translate policy and process into concrete action. Gaps include identifying and addressing the specific humanitarian needs of diverse groups of women and girls in any given crisis context - including, but not limited to, GBV mitigation and response and ensuring their meaningful input as key contributors to the decision-making processes of humanitarian planning which directly impacts how they and their dependents access lifesaving services. Key to addressing these gaps and ensuring on-the-ground, tangible, transformative change is strengthened accountability of humanitarian actors to crisis affected women and girls.Essential to delivering effective humanitarian response is ensuring that crisis affected populations are not seen as homogenous and recognizing the different determining factors that directly impact how different strata of population groups access and influence lifesaving services. Gender is the most important and most basic determining factor in setting a person’s social and economic standing in society, but it is not the only one that has an impact. Other diversity factors include disability, social class, caste, religion, nationality, sexual orientation, gender identity – the intersectionality of each with others having a profound but differing effect on an individual’s life experience. Establishing localized accountability frameworks with a starting point of gender ensures that humanitarian coordination and response are accountable to policy commitments and standards on women and girls, are more effective, reaching those with the most needs and integrating the voices of those with the most needs in its planning cycle. "
