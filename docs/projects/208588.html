This is a 10 per cent increase from the 3 million people projected within the same period in 2022. The number of food-insecure people in the BAY states is expected to increase to 4.4 million during the 2024 lean season (June to August). This is slightly (2 per cent) higher than it was during the 2023 lean season when about 4.3 million people faced a severe food crisis. The March 2023 analysis classified nine LGAs including Bade, Fune, Geidam, Gujba, Jakusko, Nangere, Tarmua, Yusufari and Yunusari as crises (CH phase 3 ). The report also projected that about 2 million children will be malnourished with an estimated 700,000 of them suffering from life-threatening Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM).
To address the above-mentioned identified needs, COOPI proposes actionable solutions that will contribute to save and change the lives of the targeted beneficiaries so as to address the issues of food and nutrition insecurity (FNI). To improve food security in the targeted areas, COOPI will provide an unconditional general food assistance (in-kind and cash-based) targeting the most food-insecure crisis-affected women, men, boys and girls (IDPs, returnees, refugees and host populations) in Yobe state northeast Nigeria. The project activity will target 6 LGAs of Geidam, Yunusari, Yusufari, Fune, Fika, and Nangere. The project will ensure adequate access to food by the beneficiaries. Hence, improving the food security of the population and resilience to shocks in the targeted project areas.

1 - Needs

The Government of Nigeria and its partners released the results of the October 2023 Cadre Harmonisé (CH) analysis on food insecurity projecting that about 3.3 million people in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states will face high levels of food and nutrition insecurity from October to December 2023. This is a 10 per cent increase from the 3 million people projected within the same period in 2022. The number of food-insecure people in the BAY states is expected to increase to 4.4 million during the 2024 lean season (June to August). This is slightly (2 per cent) higher than it was during the 2023 lean season when about 4.3 million people faced a severe food crisis. The March 2023 analysis classified nine LGAs including Bade, Fune, Geidam, Gujba, Jakusko, Nangere, Tarmua, Yusufari and Yunusari as crises (CH phase 3). The UNOCHA situation report also projected that about 2 million children will be malnourished with an estimated 700,000 of them suffering from life-threatening Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM).Moreover, COOPI recently conducted Post-Distribution Monitoring Surveys in August and September 2023 across Fune, Nangere, Geidam, Yunusari and Yusufari LGAs and findings showed that the total average Coping Strategy Index (CSI) across the 12 strategies was 33.3 in September which is lower than the score 37.8 achieved in the PDM of August. On the other hand, the average reduced Coping Strategy Index (rCSI) was 16.1 in September which has almost doubled from the August PDM where the rCSI was 8.4. The Food Consumption Score (FCS) results showed that 36.08% fall within the poor food consumption score which is better than the consumption for the August PDM which saw 42.44% of the beneficiaries falling into the poor food consumption score. Also, 41.75% fall within the acceptable food consumption score which was also an improvement to the August result which showed 24.23% falling within the acceptable food consumption score. Therefore, although improvements have been observed as a result of the ongoing project funded by WFP, targeted households are still in dire needs of food, nutrition and livelihoods assistance.Furthermore, the October 2023 Integrated Food Security Phase Classification Acute malnutrition Analysis (IPC AMN) report estimated that nearly 4.41 million children aged 0 – 59 months are suffering and will likely continue suffering from acute malnutrition in the period of May 2023 to projected period lasting until April 2024 in northwest and northeast Nigeria. This includes 1.04 million cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and 3.37 million cases of Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM). In addition, 585,000 pregnant or lactating women are acutely malnourished and will likely continue to be malnourished and are in need of treatment.The IPC AMN report also classified the severity of acute malnutrition in 4 LGAs of Yobe State including Machina, Nguru, Yunusari, and Yusufari as serious (phase 3) while the severity in Bade, Bursari, Geidam, Jakusko, Damaturu, Fika, Fune, Gujba, Gulani, Nangere, Potiskum, Tarmuwa, Karasuwa) were classified as alert (phase 2).

2 - Activities

The overall objective of the response is to improve and prevent further deterioration of food and nutritional status of Yobe State crisis-affected communities through immediate responses coupled with early recovery support to enhance their ability to absorb future shocks and stresses. The project is targeting 412,301 beneficiaries for food distribution while 45,353 children and PLW are targeted for nutrition To address the above-mentioned identified needs, COOPI proposes actionable solutions that will contribute to save and change the lives of the targeted beneficiaries so as to address the issues of food and nutrition insecurity (FNI). To improve food security in the targeted areas, COOPI will provide an unconditional general food assistance (in-kind and cash-based) targeting the most food-insecure crisis-affected women, men, boys and girls (IDPs, returnees, refugees and host populations) in Yobe state northeast Nigeria. The project activity will target 6 LGAs of Geidam, Yunusari, Yusufari, Fune, Fika, and Nangere. The project will ensure adequate access to food by the beneficiaries. Hence, improving the food security of the population and resilience to shocks in the targeted project areas.Overall the project will contribute to food and nutrition security among vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities affected by the crisis in Geidam, Yunusari, Yusufari, Nangere, Fune and Fika in Yobe State. It will also, prevent the deterioration of malnutrition and reduce excess mortality among 6-23 months’ children and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) in the targeted LGAs. The activities are described per outcome and outputs.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

In the target areas women and girls are most vulnerable as violence and cultural orientation expose them to child marriage, sexual abuse, illiteracy, emotional/physical violence, and exploitation while boys are most at risk of recruitment by AOGs. COOPI will consider issues of gender norms and practices and make sure that gender roles and responsibilities do not discriminate against people or act as a barrier to accessing services. COOPI will map out safe and unsafe areas and identify the underlying causes of gender discrimination in the communities. It will work in partnership with government institutions and the community to comprehend the impact and provide solutions to reduce gender discrimination and improve participation. COOPI will ensure that gender is considered on a needs basis in all interventions, and men and women are equally represented in community structures as well as facilitators working with children. COOPI will strengthen protection mainstreaming by promoting safety, meaningful access, dignity, and ensuring the well-being of conflict-affected populations, including people with disabilities, across all its activities. COOPI will mainstream safeguarding in all program areas and processes such as ensuring that implementation approaches do not further expose beneficiaries to harm such as family separation, worsening beneficiaries’ situations, crisis/conflicts in the community, procurement of inferior materials etc. Potential red-flag areas the COOPI pay particular attention to include; FDPs selection in an unsecured location, activities depriving children and women from going to school, exposing beneficiaries to more vulnerable situations, setting criteria that support family separation, engaging unskilled personnel, unguided data information sharing, bridging of data protection protocols, exposing GBV survivors to further harm, causing stigma and discrimination among community members. To prevent such scenarios, protection will be mainstreamed in all activities and outcome areas to ensure that all activities meet the needs of beneficiaries, and ensure services are rendered in a safe and dignified manner that does not expose beneficiaries to further harm. A help and feedback desk and complaint boxes will also be set up at each distribution site for the people that would like to remain anonymous

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

In addition, COOPI will capitalize on the existing accountability units within the MEAL department to ensure that participation, feedback, and complaints mechanisms are integrated in all strategies, plans and program and provides a range of rapid and accessible channels of communication with affected populations. These channels will be used to inform the affected population and project participants on procedures and processes so they can make informed decisions and choices. The components of accountability to affected populations are intrinsically linked and built on each other in COOPI’s mission accountability systems and would be adequately considered throughout the implementation of the proposed project. In addition to the WFP’s toll-free line, COOPI will build on the findings from previous humanitarian accountability assessments conducted in the target locations to determine communities’ preference on the type of feedback mechanism to put in place, prior to the set-up/strengthening of the mechanisms for the project. These feedback/complaints whether negative or positive will be timely addressed in a confidential manner and will inform corrective measures in the project. This is done through dedicated Gender and Protection/CFM Assistants whose responsibility will be collecting and responding to complaints and feedback. COOPI would ensure that its staff adhere to the principles of “Do No Harm” and as such will periodically evaluate actions of the project (including staff and community members) to ensure that harm is not coming to any of the beneficiaries as a result of the project implementation. Where any harm may be identified, it will be documented for accountability and quickly addressed
