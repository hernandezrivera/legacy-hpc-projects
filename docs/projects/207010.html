Project Summary

This project will support conflict affected IDPs in target communities with direct services. As part of our gender responsive and disability inclusive approach most activities have at least a 60/40 female/male percentage split, as well as a minimum 10% children with disabilities target. Both IDPs and host communities will benefit from the services, with more weight on provision of services to IDPs. The selection will be vulnerability, not status, based, with at least 20% host community selected so as to mitigate the risk of causing tension between populations, and leave sustainable impacts in the target communities.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Child Rights Situational Analysis: The project will collect data on the six grave violations against children, and community knowledge and perception of child rights, including reintegration of children associated with armed groups. Street Child will ensure a robust data collection and analysis system is in place with manual data collection and digital archive and analysis system. This will be disaggregated by age, gender and disability. Street Child ensures the community leaders and key community informants are well informed about the project staff data collection exercise, and the trust is built between the project staff and community members. To ensure children and community support, CCPC’s and local authorities will be supported by the projects, sensitized about child rights and six grave violations, and understand the basic know-how of the project’s information and data collection exercise, and reporting mechanism. Case management: A case management system, based on CPMS and inter agency guidelines for case management and CP, will be conducted by Wiwanana, to include support to UASC and FTR, GBV support, and alternative care. Case workers will work alongside MHPSS and vocational mentors, to support children, particularly in reintegration of those rescued and released from non-state armed groups. Inclusive Community dialogue sessions and training, with a component on engaging men and boys, disability inclusion in humanitarian action, will be carried out as GBV and armed group recruitment prevention strategies, as well as to increase understanding and reduce stigma associated to both groups, support CAAFAG reintegration, and spread awareness on CP issues, including GBV.CSM will lead the MHPSS strand, following the Common Elements Treatment Approach, based on evidence-based treatments for depression, anxiety, substance use, and stress. Children and adults will benefit from individual and group counselling, peer to peer support, community MHPSS events such as sport events and local celebrations, adhered to level 2 and level 3 of the IASC MHPSS pyramid. Cases identified by the case management team or MHPSS team for clinical support will be referred to relevant actors such as the local health facilities, MSF and WHO. Multipurpose centres with a safe space will be used for these activities and recreational activities for younger children. All staff, alternative carers, and community leaders will be trained in MHPSS and PSEA, to be able to identify risks, behaviours, and symptoms, and use PFA to support children and parents. MHPSS activities will all be designed in a gender sensitive, inclusive and culturally sensitive way and targeted programmes for children with disabilities as appropriate.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

• 3,345 children (60% girls, 40% boys, and 10% children with disabilities) will receive direct case management services, with at least 1600 UASC, and 1000 GBV cases; • 60 adults (70% women, 30% men) will be trained as alternative carers to support UASC. • 10,246 girls and women (10% with disabilities), will be reached through GBV activities such as GBV identification and reporting training, kits, etc. • 10,666 men and male adolescents and CBCPC members will be reached through GBV awareness raising and sensitization, training on women’s rights and prevention methods as part of men engagement programs • 20,912 children (60% girls, 40% boys) will benefit from structured programmes including entertainment/recreational activities recreational activities in multipurpose centres in one year, • Individual and group counselling will be provided for 8,000 children and youth (60% girls and 40% boys, 10% with disabilities) in the multi-purpose centres • Peer to peer MHPSS support will help 3000 children (1800 girls, 1200 boys), 300 boys and girls with disabilities) • Community MHPSS events (sports events, local celebrations, commemoration of World Mental Health Day, etc.) will target 32000 children and adults.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Street Child will apply a robust risk management support to the consortium members. The key risks identified are as the following: • Security – security SOPs will be adopted when starting field activities due to the heightened risk in Palma, Mueda, Mocimboa da Praia and Meluco. Also coordination will be sought with INGO security structures. Security of the staff is a consideration given they will be engaging with sensitive topics and working directly with CAAFAG, so community engagement and sensitisation will be prioritized throughout. CAAFAG could be at risk if they are recognized or ostracized by the community, so all work with CAAFAG will be done under the do not harm principle, to ensuring their safety. Additionally, the program staff will be briefed about potential risks expose to the community given. Street Child will mitigate the risk by maximizing the confidentiality of the data collection and reporting and anonymous reporting process.• Population movements – population movements make continuous support such as CM and FTR a challenge, therefore all community engagement will stress the importance of communicating planned and unplanned movements. • Community acceptance – in particular due to engagement with CAAFAG, potentially with regards to messaging on GBV and early marriage, and crucial to the overall buy in and success of the project. In order to mitigate the risk that the project is not accepted, and therefore does not have the desired impact, all initial engagement will be done with key stakeholders from all relevant beneficiary groups, as well as leadership. • Creating tension between peoples – to avoid the risk of creating tension between host and IDP communities, beneficiary criteria will be vulnerability, not status, based, activities will target those from host and IDP communities, and selection criteria and rationale will be explained by field staff every step of the way.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

All staff will be trained in PFA and CP principles. Community mapping of perceived risks and activity planning will take age and gender into consideration, and all assessments will be based on SADDD (sex, age and disability disaggregated data) and include gender specific aspects , so as to encourage the collection of a wide variety of feedback on gender sensitive programme design. Equitable access to all services and activities will be sought through i) ensuring the recruitment of both male and female staff, ii) conducting specific gender and age-appropriate activities. The latter is specifically responding to different risks posed to children based on their gender. There is a higher risk on girls in early marriage and teen pregnancy, greater risk of SGBV due to presence of armed groups and frequent movement, domestic labour, and deprioritisation for education. On the other hand, boys are exposed to risks such as recruitment into armed groups and subsequent social stigma, becoming unaccompanied or separated, and child labour. Gender will also be a consideration when training alternative carers, ensuring girls could be placed with female carers. As the pre-existing risk of GBV, including exploitation, is compounded by the conflict, all staff will be trained on how to spot GBV warning signs, support survivors, referral systems, and GBV survivors are a case management priority and positive, non-violent masculinity.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project will ensure participation of children and people with disabilities throughout and directly targeting between 5-15% of total beneficiaries. Capacity building and sensitization on disability inclusion and inclusive child safeguarding will be mandatory for all staff and focal points to ensure meaningful inclusion and participation. All data collected will be disaggregated by disability to monitor participation and access to services by children with disabilities. The project will collaborate with disability specific organizations and OPDs to support with identification of children with disabilities as well as for capacity development. The project will use both targeted and mainstreamed interventions to facilitate inclusion and participation of people with disabilities. In addition, communication, attitudinal, policy and physical accessibility barriers will be identified and addressed. All information produced and disseminated through the project will be in multiple accessible formats to ensure information reaches to people with different disabilities

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Sustainability of this intervention will come firstly from the capacity building of national actors, allowing them to form new partnerships and bringing them into the fore of the humanitarian response of Cabo Delgado. Frequent training on child rights and child protection for CBCPC members and local authorities, coupled with awareness raising and sensitization around MHPSS and grave violations against children, ensure increased community’s understanding of the CP issues and provide the space for them to be the main agent of change in the community.
