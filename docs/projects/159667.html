Project Summary

The protection rights and needs of estimated 3.2 million people, of whom 64 percent are children, and 48 percent are women and girls, are affected by armed conflict and violence and destitution triggered by climatic shocks, including prolonged drought conditions and floods. People in need of protection include households with significant proportion of persons with heightened vulnerability households with vulnerable members such as people with disability, medical conditions, children, elderly or pregnant/lactating women. The vulnerability is further increased if those members resume the role of the only household head. The total figure of people in need also comprises of households forced to adopt negative coping mechanisms (such as hazardous child labor, early marriage or family separation), families facing safety concern, at risk of eviction or with unresolved land disputes, without awareness of or access to protection services, and excluded from participation.
Of the total people in need, about 1.9 million are displaced, living in collective settlements, where often neighbor urban poor and returnees who have not managed to reach sustainable. The majority of affected IDPs lack any prospects for sustainable return or local integration, and wish to integrate locally.
Another 1.2 million people in need for protection are members of host communities, including returnees, who are also facing protection concerns in areas most exposed to insecurity and large population movements, being at risk of displacement themselves, upon exhaustion of other coping strategies.
Among the people in need 48 percent are women and girls, while children constitute 64 percent of the people in need. Thus, girls and boys remain a group particularly exposed to protection risks, such as violence (including GBV), recruitment, exploitation, and neglect). An estimated 480,000 people in need live with disability stemming from physical, mental, intellectual or sensory impairments, and as a result, face serious risks of exploitation, ill-treatment, neglect and stigmatization.
A large portion of the displaced communities in need are members of marginalized groups, who lack support network in the areas of displacement or residence. About 884,000 people live in districts which are currently mostly under the control of armed groups, or are directly exposed to the effect of armed conflicts (see protection severity map, districts scored 5). Another 2,3 million people in need, including 1,6 million displaced people live in districts hosting large number of IDPs (both in total and compared to the local population), including people displaced in 2019, and where conflict-related risks for physical safety and insecurity remain very high (child recruitment, deaths and injuries, and sexual violence).
The proposed project will have targeted both drought and conﬂict displaced population in Hiran region of Somalia. The primary objectives of the project will be to improve a) protection of conﬂict affected children by preventing violence; abuse; exploitation and neglect through preventative and responsive child protection interventions and B) Strengthen prevention and response interventions against sexual and gender based violence against most vulnerable groups specially displaced women and girls.
The targeted IDP communities in Hiran, Bay and Bakool region faced numerous protection threats and risk on regularly bases specially boys and girls; and women. Violence against the children are too common especially physical abuse; neglect and exploitation as well as increased incident of sexual violence against women and girls. This project will have two prong approaches towards addressing the protection concerns and risks ; ﬁrstly by undertaking preventative approaches where appropriate community based structures will be established; trained and will subsequently undertake awareness actions within their communities and as well as empowering children and women with life-skills

Mitigation Measures

IDPs in the targeted locations are discriminated from accessing public services and are sometime denied access to security due to the displacement. Clan-based discrimination is a common incident in all the targeted camps specially IDPs household from minority groups as well as female headed households are particularly affected by the discrimination. Gatekeeping from the landowners and their proxies exploited IDP due to their vulnerability and sometimes subject them to absolute deprivation from aid service or even public services. Sexual exploitation is relation to access and document for humanitarian aid has been identiﬁed as common. Sexual violence against minority women and girl’s groups is extremely common and most often unreported due to lack of clan protection. Preventions/Mitigation Measures: We will have conducted awareness on SGBV and discrimination issues through structured engagement with relevant stakeholders including religious leaders and business people. We will make concreted efforts to bring local authorities and traditional communities leaders to raise their voices on the clan discrimination and will call for the protection of IDP minority population. IDPs will be empowered to raise their voices on matters that affect them and will be asked to prevention clan based discrimination and calls for minority protection will be raised through out the project period. The Community based Child Protection structure will play a key role in ensuring that all protection issues are identiﬁed and reported through appropriate channels.Due to existing community norms; girls and women are often subjected to exclusion due to male dominancy in the community. girls and women needs are often overlooked and are not included ion community decision making processes. Children and elder people aren't also consulted in decision making within their communities and their actual needs are also not considered. girls and women are sometimes faced the risk of sexual exploitation due their vulnerability and lack of protection. Mitigation measures: all ages and genders will be included in the project activities; awareness will be made on ending gender and age based discrimination. We will ensure to have a balance male and female representation as well as age groups in all relevant activities.Displaced population in the targeted locations face the threat of forced eviction or live in IDP sites where there is exploitation is too common due to lack of secured land tenure for the IDPs.Land owners or their proxies often taken some of the humanitarian support from the IDPs as part of land rental cost. Armed men from both state and non-state actors have multiple of times sexually assaults women and girls within the IDP sites or outside of the camps thereby create a sense of insecurity and fear for IDP populations. Sexual violence against minority women and girl’s groups is extremely common and most often unreported due to lack of clan protection.

Please provide further explanation

This is short-term humanitarian project with the primary objective of strengthening protection of conﬂict-induced IDP in three regions of Hiran of Somalia. This project has community on community based child protection system which can contribute in the long run system building for the targeted communities. 6 child welfare committees will be formed and trained; these communities based structures will be trained and mentored during the project period. it is anticipated that these community structures will be able to continue the running of community based activities such as community awareness on protection issues; engaging with relevant state and non-state actors in terms of protection of civilian in their communities as well as ensuring that all community incidents are reported and managed through the appropriate channels and principle referrals with services providers is undertaken.
