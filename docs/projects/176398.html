Project Summary

At least 1.9 million school-aged children including those with special needs (3 to 17 years) continue to face an array of shocks and have been impacted by closure of schools occasioned by COVID 19 pandemic, school destruction by floods and displacements due to conflict (MOGEI, 2020; UNICEF Country Reports 2020).
Multiple displacements of vulnerable girls and boys; including men and women, with particular significant damage to at least 23 schools/ temporary learning spaces in Pibor, Gumuruk, Lekuangole and Boma areas of Pibor County (South Sudan Flooding Situation Report, Nov 2020). This coupled with rising COVID 19 cases in the country (COVID Taskforce, Feb 2021) preventative measures announced by the Ministry of Health are being implemented, with consequent closure of schools, further interrupting learning (Ministry of Health Updates, 2020).
Pibor County continues to face unprecedented food insecurity levels, with recent IPC phase 5 rating in some Payams of the county (IPC December 2020). This has had further implications on children well being including livelihoods for survival, coupled with interrupted school feeding, increasing child protection concerns which have included recruitment/ use of children into armed groups/ forces (CAAFAG TW, 2020 Updates) in addition to inadequate access to safe water, hygiene and sanitation, particularly among girls, women and children with disabilities.
The Ministry of Education and General Instruction is gearing up plans for school opening in 2021, while observing COVID 19 prevention and in line with this; and building on actions which SPOCI through UNICEF has supported at least 9,450 girls and boys through Education in Emergencies support in Pibor, SPOCI aims at targeting 4800 beneficiaries (2000 girls, 2000 boys, 400 men and 400 women) through provision of services entailing school infrastructure establishment and rehabilitation, school feeding, health information on disease prevention, protection and access to clean water and sanitation services, teachers training and PTA support.
Proposed actions will be integrated with WASH, Protection, Health and Nutrition integrated programming while taking into consideration Accountability of Affected Populations, Gender Equity, Protection concerns including GBV and sound environmentally friendly approaches. SPOCI is a national NGO with over 5 years programmatic presence in Pibor and is a member of National NGOs Steering Committee, member of the ICWG and AAP Technical Working Group and Monitoring and Evaluation Working Group. In addition, SPOCI has fostered strong linkages with communities, state/county line ministries in addition to strong and competent team including staff redeployment to scale up interventions based on needs.

1 - Needs

At least 1.9 million school-aged children including those with special needs (3 to 17 years) continue to face an array of shocks and have been impacted by closure of schools occasioned by COVID 19 pandemic, school destruction by floods and displacements due to conflict (MOGEI, 2020; UNICEF Country Reports 2020). Multiple displacements of vulnerable girls and boys; including men and women, with particular significant damage to at least 23 schools/ temporary learning spaces in Pibor, Gumuruk, Lekuangole and Boma areas of Pibor County (South Sudan Flooding Situation Report, Nov 2020). This coupled with rising COVID 19 cases in the country (COVID Taskforce, Feb 2021) preventative measures announced by the Ministry of Health are being implemented, with consequent closure of schools, further interrupting learning (Ministry of Health Updates, 2020). The situation of girls and boys, including those with special needs has been exacerbated by cyclical waves of inter-communal violence and age set fighting among youth (OCHA Updates, 2020; Flooding Reports 2020). This is in addition to related protection concerns facing girls and boys, including abduction and use of children in armed hostilities (Protection Trends, 2020; NGO Forum Updates, 2020; REACH Situation Overview Reports, 2020). According to the latest December 2020 IPC report, Pibor County is among others that have been projected to have IPC Phase 5 populations. These contribute negatively to the wellbeing/health and subsequent malnutrition which has consequent cognitive development among children (girls and boys). Recent IMC SMART Survey Report in Pibor County indicated GAM prevalence of 20.7% and SAM prevalence 4.9% based on WHZ and the presence of bilateral oedema with children being worse affected. Poor access to safe water, hygiene, sanitation and feeding practices, particularly in schools, with heightened COVID rising cases, are likely to further aggravate vulnerability of girls, boys, men and women. School WASH facilities were submerged with at least 11 boreholes nearby schools having been destroyed by floods, compounded by collapsed latrine facilities in addition to widespread open defecation with potential risks exposure to water borne diseases; coupled with poor sanitation coverage including schools and lack of hand washing facilities, poor knowledge and practices on personal and environmental further fuel the situation (IRNA Report, 2020; SPOCI Situation Reports). The Ministry of Education and General Instruction is gearing up plans for school opening in 2021, while observing COVID 19 prevention and in line with this; and building on actions which SPOCI through UNICEF has supported at least 9,450 girls and boys through Education in Emergencies support in Pibor, SPOCI aims at targeting 4800 beneficiaries (2000 girls, 2000 boys, 400 men and 400 women) through provision of services entailing school infrastructure establishment and rehabilitation, school feeding, health information on disease prevention, protection and access to clean water and sanitation services, teachers training and PTA support. Proposed actions will be integrated with WASH, Protection, Health and Nutrition integrated programming while taking into consideration Accountability of Affected Populations, Gender Equity, Protection concerns including GBV and sound environmentally friendly approaches. SPOCI is a national NGO with over 5 years programmatic presence in Pibor and is a member of National NGOs Steering Committee, member of the ICWG and AAP Technical Working Group and Monitoring and Evaluation Working Group. In addition, SPOCI has fostered strong linkages with communities, state/county line ministries in addition to strong and competent team including staff redeployment to scale up interventions based on needs.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

SPOCI shall refrain from, and shall take all reasonable and appropriate measures to prohibit its employees or other persons engaged and controlled by it from exchanging any money, goods, services, or other things of value, for sexual favors or activities, or from engaging in any sexual activities that are exploitative or degrading to any beneficiary. All SPOCI staff have received trainingon PSEA and have signed organization’s PSEA policy including newly recruited staff. SPOCI does not tolerate any form of discrimination against ethnic groups, clans, minorities or communities. SPOCI shall ensure that the selection of beneficiaries is transparent and conforms to established selection criteria and is applied objectively and without prejudice.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Implementation committees shall include other vulnerable groups such as people with disabilities, women, girls, boys and men including illiterate ones who shall be guided and supported to ensure their views and concerns are taken into consideration. These will have an equal opportunity to provide feedback and complaints including key contacts their can use for confidential reporting and feedback.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Stop Poverty Community Initiative (SPOCI) will select and brief AAP project implementation committees who will be elected from the community and will include people with special needs. These committees will be mandated oversee implementation in addition offer means for communities to provide feedback on the project and to submit complaints, and to ensure that they receive a timely response; review how complaints are received and handled, consult with communities on how they could best submit complaints, and develop a plan for establishing mechanisms that shall be discussed and implemented in consultation with other keystakeholders.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Stop Poverty Community Initiative (SPOCI) will select and brief AAP committees who will be elected from the community and will include people with special needs. These committees will be mandated oversee implementation in addition offer means for communities to provide feedback on the project and to submit complaints, and to ensure that they receive a timely response; review how complaints are received and handled, consult with communities on how they could best submit complaints, and develop a plan for establishing mechanisms that shall be discussed and implemented in consultation with other key stakeholders.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

SPOCI will ensure communities and school management commitees are consulted, sensitized and involved in beneficiary targeting and selection whose criteria will include both displaced and host communities, people with special needs etc with selection agreed upon to avoid/ mitigate potential conflicts in the community. Implementation will be conducted in nearby locations and will be selected in close consultation with women and local authorities including ensuring appropriate safety audits are conducted to ensure safety of beneficiaries particularly women and girls to mitigate potential protection concerns. Key protection concerns including messaging on PSEA including their rights will be communicated among targeted boys, girls, men and women, in addition to providing key confidential reporting channels through identified school based and community based feedback focal points. Girls, boys messaging will include on ways their can easily avoid potential UXOs/ mines via mine risk awareness as part of life skills among children including reporting of suspect devices through teachers, parents, caretakers among other key stakeholders. Messaging on other protection concerns including gender based violence will be done as well. Beneficiary and key rehabilitation/ establishment including other activities will involve consultative sessions in addition to two way feedback to ensure beneficiary views are incorporated where applicable.
