Project Summary

This project aims to maintain the ongoing health services within the targeted locations and facilities in order not to overburden the existing health system in the area in case of closing any of these facilities. The main delivered services are primary health care including medical consultations, referral services, and medicine distribution. The project includes supporting seven primary health centers of IDA in northern Aleppo, A'zaz district as the following:
1- IDA PHC in Aleppo, Azaz, Azaz, Azaz C1564
2- IDA PHC in Aleppo, Azaz, Suran, Suran C1664
3- IDA PHC in Al Rayan camp, Aleppo, Azaz, Shamarin, Shamarin C1566
4- IDA PHC in Ekdeh camp, Aleppo, Azaz, Shamarin, Shamarin C1566
5- IDA PHC in Shmarekh, Aleppo, Azaz, Shmarekh, Shmarekh C1557
6- IDA PHC in Bab Al Noor camp, Aleppo, Azaz, Shamarin, Shamarin C1566
7- IDA PHC in Bab Al Iman camp, Aleppo, Azaz, Shamarin, Shamarin C1566

4 - Needs

According to an assessment conducted in 2020, intensified hostilities between government forces and non-state armed groups in the north-western governorate of Idlib have caused the displacement of 700,000 people since the beginning of December 2019. This adds to the 400,000 already displaced between May and August. IDPs are moving further north towards cities and IDP camps. The cold and rains exacerbate humanitarian needs with tents flooded and IDPs exposed to low temperatures while sleeping in precarious shelters or in the open air. Increased fuel prices and a fuel shortage hinder transportation for civilians, but also for aid delivery. Other access difficulties are due to the volatile security situation and muddy roads. Protection, food, shelter and NFI, and health are all areas of concern.

5 - Implementation of activities

1. To provide IDPs and host communities with high quality and free primary health services in 7 basic health structures by assuming the running costs and providing medicines and consumables.2. Introduction of a quality management framework, training and coaching (on the job) of health personnel based on current needs.3. Collection of health and epidemiological data for the Early Warning and Response Network (EWARN) and activation of emergency plans for epidemics.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The team will be trained on how they best deal with serious and life-threatening situations. They will be provided with regular updates and recommendations to assure their safety and security. Coordination with Syrian Civil Defense in areas of implementation will be followed up to make sure their prompt access and response whenever needed. Another coordination with the health facilities, camp managements, and emergency response focal points will be enhanced. In the case of extremely severe insecurity, the work will be suspended based on safety and security focal point instructions with immediate coordination with the donor. Risk management plan considers the following: 1. Attacks on patients and/or project personnel due to a deteriorated security situation. Likelihood: lowMitigation: Immediate cessation of activities and waiting for a change in the situation.2. Destruction of health structures, plundering of furnishings due to a deteriorated security situation.Likelihood: lowMitigation: Suspension of activities; analysis of the situation, rehabilitation and/or replacement of missing equipment.3. Closure of Turkish border crossings for persons and goods, resulting in delays in project implementation.Likelihood: middleMitigation: Stockpiling (medicines, consumables); Sufficient number of medical staff living in Syria.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

To mitigate any risk that could affect the project implementation, IDA will develop Risk Management Matrix to set and plan for any possible risk that could be faced. Access:1- Risk: Access to project location is getting more harder or prevented by the local council or the controlling armed group. Mitigation: IDA has a valid MOU with LC covering project implementation duration. Operational:1- Risk: overlapping of services whether during the planning or implementation phase. Mitigation: IDA is active NGO since 2012, and has active projects in the areas. Also, the locations of these facilities will be shared and reported with the health cluster through HeRAMS system. IDA will manage this issue with the cluster, WHO and local council. 2- Risk: a shortage of needed supplies ( fuel, drugs, medical consumables) Mitigation: IDA has the authorization to deliver any humanitarian assistant through border gates.3- Risk: facility targeting and shifting control-lines. Mitigation: IDA will implement a specific security plan and will make sure the buildings are fortified against targeting. Moreover, IDA will share the locations of the facilities with a de-escalation mechanism to avoid targeting from conflict parties. IDA can evacuate the facilities and shift to other project locations in case of front-lines changes. Financial: 1- Risk: corruption and abuse use of cash. Mitigating measures: IDA will assure that all accounting and financial management practices are in line with IDA financial instruction.
