Project Summary

The overall objective of the project is to strengthen the protection referral mechanism for vulnerable migrants at the four official Border Crossing Points (BCPs) in border areas between Haiti and the Dominican Republic in order to ensure that irregular and vulnerable returning migrants receive live saving, emergency protection assistance upon arrival in Haiti.
The four (4) Border Resource Centers (BRC) have been set up in Anse a Pitres, Malpasse, Belladere and Ouanaminthe in order to receive deportees and other vulnerable migrants in decent and safe conditions and are the cornerstone of the referral mechanism in the border area.
Vulnerable individuals include but is not limited to: persons at risk of statelessness, unaccompanied minors, pregnant women, victims of trafficking, returnees registered in the PNRE, etc. 7800 vulnerable migrants referred to and registered in the Border Resource Centers will be offered a holistic protection package tailored to their specific needs. The package includes: psychological first aid support and medical first aid support, livelihood activities and delivery of documentation.

Needs

The unregulated migration flow between Haiti and the Dominican Republic (DR) is considered the most prominent intraregional migrant corridor in the Caribbean4. In 2014, The Africa Caribbean and Pacific (ACP) Observatory on Migration estimated that between 500,000 and 700,000 Haitians have crossed the Dominican Republic border in the last decade, of which only 4,000 held a valid visa or work permit. In the meantime, it is important to highlight that, according to IOM, approximatively 100,000 weekly commuters cross into the DR.In 2013, the Dominican Republic’s Presidential Decree 327-13 concerning the National Plan to Regularize Foreigners in Conditions of Irregular Migration (PNRE in Spanish) granted permission to the Dominican law enforcement authority to forcibly expel individuals of Haitian descent who are unable to produce the newly imposed identification documents reflecting their legal status in the country. Consequently, since June 16th, 2015, Haiti has faced a significant protection crisis with the return of thousands of irregular migrants from the DR. Nearly 230,000 persons (32% of whom were female) were deported and/or spontaneously returned from the DR into Haiti between 2015-2017. Among those crossing the border at either official or unofficial crossing points are highly vulnerable potential refugees or stateless individuals, unable to exercise most of their rights either in the DR and in Haiti. Together with vulnerable female migrants and unaccompanied children require specific protection assistance. To further accentuate the complexity of migration management between the neighbouring countries, and as a consequence of the increased nationalist discourse in the DR, The Dominican Army and its Specialised Border Unit (CESFRONT) have intensified their militarization of the border as well as expulsions and deportations of Haitians. In January 2018, at the Ouanaminthe border crossing point, countless irregularities and potential human rights abuses took place when an estimated 14,000 individuals were deported. This represents the highest number of deportations recorded on the island since June 2015. The trend continues during the year of 2018 with an average of 10,000 Haitians deported each month and 858 vulnerable migrants were registered at the BRCs from June to September 2018.In addition, the zones surrounding the border with the Dominican Republic remain underdeveloped in Haiti as are its institutional capacities; especially so in comparison to its neighboring country. Economic conditions force a number of Haitians to seek better opportunities in the Dominican Republic through irregular and sometimes dangerous means. Endemic, exploitative practices such as the exploitation of children as domestic servants, transactional sexual exploitation of women and children, and various forms of bonded labour affecting men, women and children remain prevalent across Haiti. Poverty, lack of access to social services and lack of information on the dangers of irregular border crossing are some of the root causes which launch women and children as victims to these forms of exploitation.

Activities or outputs

• Delivery of protection assistance Vulnerable individuals include but is not limited to: persons at risk of statelessness, unaccompanied minors, pregnant women, victims of trafficking, returnees registered in the PNRE, etc. Vulnerable migrants referred to and registered in the Border Resource Centers will be offered a holistic protection package tailored to their specific needs. The package includes: psychological first aid support and medical first aid support, livelihood activities and delivery of documentation. • Strengthening of protection referral mechanisms for vulnerable migrants Being the cornerstone of the protection referral mechanism in border areas, IOM will ensure that the four (4) BRCs are functional and up to internal protection standards.The Standard Operational Procedures (SOPs) for the assistance of vulnerable migrants was validated this year by the authorities. IOM proposes to organize different coordination meetings with local protection actors at the border in order to evaluate the operationalization of those SOPs on the field and discuss the gaps and lessons learned. This will allow to adjust the SOPs to the possible new trends and realities at the border. IOM will support the six (6) refurbished reception centers for migrants to ensure they can assist the most vulnerable migrants referred by the local protection actors and the BRC. The assistance comprises: accommodation, meals, clothing and non food items (NFIs) such as tailored hygiene kits. • Awareness raising campaign on Trafficking in Persons (TiP) and safe migration Awareness raising campaign on safe migration and Trafficking in Persons will be key in preventing abuses, exploitation and trafficking, especially in times of crisis. In order to do so, IOM proposes to widely disseminate radio spots and to conduct sensitizations in highly strategic places such as schools, churches, market places, brothels and through a door to door approach. In addition, IOM will continue operating the hotline 840 set up in the aftermath of the hurricane Matthew, which purpose is to: 1/sensitize the population on human trafficking and safe migration; 2/provide individual counseling and 3/refer protection cases to appropriate actors.
