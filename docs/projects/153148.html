Project Summary

South Sudan continues to present a complex humanitarian and food and nutritional security situation, characterized by national and localized conflicts, displacements and a deteriorating economic climate. An estimated 1.5 million children under five years, and pregnant & lactating women are acutely malnourished. Similarly, 4.4 million people (43% of the population) are estimated to be facing crisis, emergency or catastrophe (IPC Phases 3, 4 and 5) acute food insecurity (IPC, 2018). The proportion of ad hoc localized nutrition surveys reporting global acute malnutrition (GAM) levels above the World Health Organization (WHO) emergency threshold of 15 percent was 57% among the 37 SMART surveys conducted between January and September 2018. About 76% of the SMART surveys conducted during the same period and locations in 2017 and 2018 reported GAM prevalence above the emergency threshold (≥15%). The persistent high malnutrition levels are attributed to high levels of food insecurity, displacements, conflict and poor infrastructure which hamper availability and access to basic health and nutrition services, low harvests, dysfunctional markets, poor infant young child feeding (IYCF) practices and high morbidity. The project will ensure that populations affected by conflict and displacement have access to emergency nutrition services in order to prevent morbidity and mortality due to acute malnutrition. WFP will continue to work with cooperating partners to support an emergency nutrition response through blanket supplementary feeding (BSFP) and treatment services for moderate acute malnutrition targeting children aged 6-59 months, pregnant & lactating women, and people living with HIV/TB across South Sudan. BSFP and targeted supplementary feeding programme (TSFP) services are provided both to IDPs and host populations. WFP also conducts direct delivery of life-saving nutrition services in hard-to-reach areas under the Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM). The project implementation will use an integrated approach through synergies with activities supported by other clusters (health, FSL, WASH).

Needs

The food security and nutrition situation in South Sudan remains of public health concern with an estimated 1.5 million children aged 6-59 months, and pregnant & lactating women acutely malnourished while 4.4 million people are facing crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity according to the September 2018 IPC analysis. The same IPC analysis found that a total of 31 counties in the former states of Warrap, Unity, Upper Nile, and Jonglei have critical levels of acute malnutrition (GAM: 15.0 – 29.9%) while 20 counties in Lakes, Jonglei, Unity and Upper Nile reported serious (GAM: 10.0-14.9%) levels of acute malnutrition. Displacement and poor access to basic health and nutrition services further compound an already extremely difficult food security and nutrition situation. WFP currently supports MAM prevention and treatment services in the 10 former states targeting residents, IDPs, refugees and host communities. This project will not only support the continuation of existing services but will allow a scale up of coverage in life saving nutrition services particularly in high burden states.

Activities

Under this response supported by WFP in collaboration with cooperating partners: (1) Children aged 6-59 months and PLW screened in communities and identified as malnourished receive appropriate treatment through TSFP as recommended by CMAM guidelines and the expanded criteria will be used as strategy in some areas when WFP/UNICEF /Nutrition Cluster jointly agree. (2) Preventive ration of specialized nutritious food to children U5 and PLW in counties with GAM rates above 15% through the Blanket Supplementary Feeding Programme (BSFP). (3) Families of discharged U5 are linked with the preventive program activities in their communities. (4) The project implementation will use an integrated approach through synergies with activities supported by other clusters (health, FSL, WASH, Protection, NFIs).WFP ensures supplies preposition at the SOs level and deep field to reduce pipeline shortfalls during the rainy season. River deliveries and airlifts are conducted to UNS, Unity and Jonglei States to the final distribution point where access is impossible by road to ensure the programs are not disrupted. WFP has food aids monitors and highly experienced monitoring and planning teams who ensure implementing partners activities and stocks are monitored to ensure proper utilization and handling. Logistic unit carries out training to cooperating partners on warehouse management. WFP also has resource management unit that ensure the timely supplies procurement and delivery to the partners.
