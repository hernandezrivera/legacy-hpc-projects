Project Summary

This project is aimed to provide basic services of WASH, Livelihoods, Education, Health and Nutrition, Protection and Shelter /NFIS in all settlement sites and localities. The project will run for 12 months in El Fashaga and EL Fawo localities in Gadarif state targeting 13851 direct beneficiaries and 7400 indirect beneficiaries in WASH and education sector while in Blue Nile State targeting 3,780 direct beneficiaries and 2,400 indirect beneficiaries in WASH and FSL sector in Ed Damazine Ar Rusayris localities. Besides this project will target 28,864 direct beneficiaries and 7,541 indirect beneficiaries who have been affected by conflict, yearly flooding with poor agricultural practices, poor access to quality nutrition services and poor health seeking behavior and dilapidated wash, hygiene and sanitation services in the Shikan, Algoz and Dilling Localities of greater Kordofan which are prone to problems of food insecurity, poverty and environmental degradation.
In West Kordofan project will mainstream protection activities to address the urgent needs of 33,604 South Sudan Refugees and 6,014 vulnerable host communities in Kailak, El Mairam, Lagawa and El Nuhud Localities through a comprehensive packages of Education, WASH, and FSL & livelihood services ensuring target groups basic needs are addressed.
Recent Ethiopian refugees’ influx is increasing and so for more than 45 thousands had fled to Sudan from Ethiopia. These refugees are accommodated in UM Rakoba camp, village 8 in Gadarif and COR/UNHCR is looking to identify a new settlement site to accommodate upcoming refugees. Under this project IRW will extend its support in WASH, Education, FSL, Shelter/NFIs, Protection and CVA in all these camps in Um Rakoba, village 8 and newly identified site by the government. Project aimed to reach around 10,000 beneficiaries including male 57%, female 43 %, children 45 % and elderly people by 4% among the Ethiopian refugees in camps.
The proposed project will complement and linked to existing IRS projects in the proposed project sites and will include rehabilitation of WASH facilities, supporting hygiene and sanitation facilities, quality education to refugees and host communities, providing quality health and nutrition services in targeted area, providing refugees and vulnerable host communities farmers with seeds and tools, protection services and ensuring sustainable environmental practices,. Cash transfer program is also part of strategy to grant unconditional cash grant to eligible Ethiopian refugees families in camps in Gadarif state. IRS will coordinate the project activities with other partners and respective government departments in the project sites including UN agencies such as COR, UNHCR, UNICEF, WFP, FAO, UNOCHA and WHO and other humanitarian actors.

1 - Needs

WASH: During the recent floods, many water points and latrines were damaged affecting the basic infrastructures. The situation is expected to further deteriorate over the coming period, increasing the number of people who need assistance. Burden has increased more due to recent influx of Ethiopian refugees. Open defecation in camps and surroundings may cause health hazards. Consistent distribution of Personal Hygiene items is an area requiring urgent attention. Acute watery diarrhea (AWD) risks still persist. Hygiene awareness is urgently needed to mitigate ongoing risks. Installation of hand washing facilities and ensuring consistent provision of antiseptic soap is highly needed to prevent the spread of COVID-19 and other diseases. Education: Most schools do not have adequate WASH facilities. Drop-out rates are high among refugees, particularly girls in upper primary classes. Lack of school feeding also drives drop-out and retention issues. Refugee children who are out-of-school face serious protection risks, including risk of exploitation and abuse, as well as SGBV and early marriage for young girls. Health and Nutrition: Findings of S3M 11 report indicated serious health and nutritional conditions that require urgent intervention, especially in food assistance and nutrition services through implementing GFA and TSFP program. awareness sessions are needed To give knowledge on identification of malnourished children and exclusive breastfeeding. Malnutrition continues to threaten child survival and development. Weak support, limited capacity of health cadre remains a serious concern. The chronic malnutrition rate is 38 percent with 11 out of 18 states recording stunting prevalence above 40 percent, considered very high by WHO standards. Stunting not only negatively impacts the physical development of a child but also a child’s cognitive development, ability to complete school, and income earning potential as an adult. Micronutrient deficiencies are rampant among children under 5, school aged-children and women of reproductive age, particularly in iron, vitamin A, and iodine. FSL: Based on the IPC food insecurity June – December issued in July 2020, an estimated 9.6 million people (21% of the population analyzed) are experiencing crisis or worse levels of food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above) and are in need of urgent actions. This is the highest urge ever recorded compared to previous years. Around 2.2 million people are facing an Emergency acute food insecurity situation (IPC Phase 4), while nearly 15.9 million people are estimated to be in Stress (IPC Phase 2). This is also a high urge compared to previous analyses. Food insecurity remains alarmingly high with increased and protracted displacement, economic decline and inflation, and high food price hikes exacerbated by the impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic. The lockdown measures significantly decreased commodity movement, market function and cross border trade, and compromised livelihoods, labor opportunities, reducing purchasing power and food access for the vulnerable population. Protection: Activities will be mainstreamed to protect refugees’ dignity. There is need to improve conditions and support refugees to access basic services with dignity and advocate for their rights to receive quality specialized protection services for children, boys and girls, women and men.Shelter/NFIs: Refugees are provided with temporary shelters and tents but still they are in need of shelter particularly if rain spells remain continue for longer period, then existing shelters cannot provide them protection for longer time. NFI are used by refugees as they left their homes with nothing. Immediate needs of NFIs including hygiene and dignity kits along with winter cloths, blankets, sleeping mats, mosquito nets and kitchen utensils are needed to enable the refugees and IDP live with dignity.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The protection will be mainstreamed in all sectors. The project will ensure that services access points are no cause of protection concerns or give way for GBV occurrence. Awareness raising workshop on early marriage and protection against SGBV will be conducted for all stakeholders at the localities level. Community based protection committees that will compose of communities and authorities will be established and will be trained. The committees will be actively engaged for close monitoring, identification and reporting of cases. Government stakeholders will also play active role in addressing the identified cases. IRW will promote for the SGBV timely responses by government even after the closure of the project. The protection will be a key feature within the intervention. In all the awareness session woman and girls will be sensitized about the protection issues and to reduce the risk of such incidents. For example, facilities and water points will be constructed in safe locations that minimize risks of various types of GBV. Fetching water in most part of remote areas in Sudan is the responsibility of women and young girls. However, this project will spare women an ample time and give them the opportunity to carry out other activities and participate in the development process of their communities, state and country. Hygiene promotion will be also be gender sensitive, with topics applicable for girls and for boysHygiene promotion will be also be gender sensitive, with topics applicable for girls and for boys. To deal with GBV and other cases, IR will establish a referral mechanism with the health facility at the locality level to treat the GBV patients, referral mechanism with the social welfare department to lead if there is any protection issue reported. IR teams with the support of the CHPs/volunteers will refer the victims to the health facilities or the social welfare.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

IRW through implementation of project intervention is going to use people- centered approach put in place and the affected persons and their needs at the heart of the organization response to ensure that refugees’ communities are continuously involved in decisions making and all implementation processes that directly impact their lives. Implementation will start with launching of the projects at the State level to bring on board the relevant government authorities for coordination and support. At the project target area level, we will start the intervention by having the launching and establishing of project steering committee. The committee will be provided with the details of the project activities, implementation plan and budget. The committee will comprises of the different category of community members (male, female, boys and girls) and also with representation of people with disability. Regular project review will be conducted together with relevant government bodies and the project steering committee. To further ensure accountability, IRW will establish a complaint response mechanism in the project target area, where all forwarded complaints will be handled as per IRW’s complaint response mechanism and policy. Raised complaints and provided response will be recorded and made accessible to any interested stakeholder to maintain transparency and accountability where complaints nature do not need confidentiality. IRW will be accountable to the community members themselves through quarterly locality-level discussions with communities and stakeholders. IRW will listen to any concerns or suggestions they have and incorporate them into the project implementation improvement plan as much as is feasible. IRW has an effective complaint mechanism through which the community can channel their complaints and concerns at all times without victimization. IRW puts an emphasis on accountability and transparency in its implementation approach. IRW has Safeguarding policies and Focal Point at Country Office level. IRW is very serious for any act by staff against the communities it serves. Any complaint against IRW staff with regards to SEA, IRW has zero tolerance policy and immediately take measures as per policy and procedure of the organization.
