Project Summary

The proposed project will contribute to Improve the food security status of assessed food insecure people through life-saving and life sustaining food assistance in Idleb and Aleppo Governorates. Main Activities listed below:
Food Security:
• Distribution of multi-round Cash for Food Vouchers (unrestricted-unconditional)
• Distribution of multi-round Food Baskets (covering 1600 Kcal per person per day)
• Distribution of Ready to Eat Food Baskets
• Distribution of Bread
• Distribution of Cooked Meals

4 - Needs

The disruption to people’s livelihoods and reduced access to economic opportunities has impacted the resilience capacity of many communities in Syria. As a result, many people are resorting to harmful coping strategies for survival according to HNO 2019. The CCCM Cluster in collaboration with Humanitarian Needs Assessment Program (HNAP) indicated that 299k individuals were displaced in December 2019 only.In January 2020, Government of Syria continued the military operation in Maret Al-Noman and started a new military campaign towards Saraqeb town located on the M5 and M4 Roads in the western southern countryside of Idleb governorate in addition to launching continuous and massive airstrikes campaign on the western countryside of Aleppo Governorate. The Airstrikes targeted many communities include a camp for IDPs leaving tens of casualties between dead and injured, this forced an additional wave of IDPs to move to other locations in Northern Idleb and Aleppo governorates. the latest update from CCCM Cluster on January 29th indicated that the total number of IDPs in the northwest of Syria since 1st December 2019 reached 517k individuals. The majority of those displaced moved to Afrin, Azaz, Jandairis and Atareb subdistricts in Aleppo Governorate and Dana, Marret Tamsrin, Idleb and Salqin subdistricts in Idleb Governorate. And the main needs for these IDPs were NFIs, Shelter, Cash and Food. REACH initiative conducted a rapid assessment with key informants interviewed in opposition held areas of western Aleppo to estimate the number of people intending to leave western Aleppo in the first week of February (Jan 29 – February 5) and to identify the sub-districts to which they intended to move and the results of this assessment showed that the majority of IDPs has the intention to move to other locations and the destinations identified by the most of the IDPs are locations in Northern Aleppo Governorate in addition to Northern Idleb Governorate.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Syria Relief staff will participate in protection working groups, and child protection and GBV sub-clusters to take lessons learned for mainstreaming of protection in all Food Security projects. In order to reduce risks of Sexual and Gender-Based Violence, considerations will be made with respect to the distribution locations in order to make sure that there is no sexual exploitation occurs through the implementation process and this issue is going to be managed and monitored through the complaint mechanism and roles of the SR MEAL staff that will always be available in the targeted locations. Moreover, SR team will be coordinating closely with partners in order to develop a special tool for monitoring the issue related to this topic. SR will focus on protection of beneficiaries especially women, and children from sexual abuse and harassment in schools, camps, and communities. In this regard monitoring and reporting tools will be developed and implemented and during the project cycle. SR will focus on enhanced participation of women in project design, implementation and monitoring of activities. All proposed activities are in full alignment (UNSCR) 1325. They have also been designed to be culturally appropriate, inclusive, and confidential. In this project, Syria Relief will ensure the safe access to services for all genders and age groups at all times. This measure will protect beneficiaries. Likewise, by installing complaint mechanisms in the targeted locations, which will be checked by Syria Relief staff during the time of the project, we will allow any protection issues related to this project to be known and seek immediate advice from the senior project staff in Turkey for a response.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: - Closure of Bab Al Hawa crossing border resulting zero or limited access for staff or goods inside Idleb - Lack of access to targeted areas for the team inside Idleb due to Security situation - Limited capacity for staff to transfer funds to Idleb due to Turkish regulations - Turnover of staff - Delays in the procurement process as a result of prices fluctuation - landowners refusal for signing the MOUs for rehabilitation of WaSH infrastructure due to frictions with local councils. - Delays in project implementation due to the Turkish restriction on transporting goods inside Idleb, Hama, and Aleppo - Delays in project implementation due to the interference of the armed groups in procurement/ imposing taxes /selection of the project locations. - Overlapping of project activities and double agreement by local councils as a result of a lack of coordination. - Syrian regime offensive onto Idleb governorate (airstrikes, a ground offensive, chemical attacks…) - Violent armed conflict between opposition armed groups in Idleb. - Turkey takes control of Idlib governorate. - loss of the neutrality of the organization. - Being associated with any particular conflicting group(s) (much in line with the aforementioned, loss of neutrality) - Fuel sources directly contributing towards that of the war economy - violation of humanitarian principles or code of conduct by staff/ organization loss of confidential data (Data security) - noncompliance with donor requirements in line with a signed agreement - corruption, mismanagement of funds and fraud Assumptions and Mitigating measures: -Constant Security monitoring and analysis of Security Risk Environment and Political Developments which impact on Access - Cross border SOP’s in place. - Registration in Syria with the local authorities. - Compliance with Turkish law, - Full compliance with the Turkish authorities’ regulations for NGOs. - Constant monitoring of Turkeys political and regulative developments which may affect NGOs.
