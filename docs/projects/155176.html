Project Summary

The Site Development activities proposed in this project complement the camp coordination and camp management activities CARE is already undertaking, which are funded by IOM in the camp(s) of operation (Camp 16, and potentially Camp 13), extending the impact of the small-scale site infrastructure works as well as ongoing shelter programming funded by other donors. The activities proposed under this project will build on and further address the priorities self-identified by communities in specific infrastructure and sites. It will be supported by complementary activities in WASH, Shelter, NFI and GBV to ensure that a holistic approach to address needs and support individuals and groups with specific needs, particularly those at risk. This includes women and girls (particularly adolescent girls), the elderly and persons with disabilities. As a core focus of CARE's mandate is supporting women and girls, the design and implementation of site development activities will be supported by CARE's Gender, GBV and Protection teams which are engaged early on to help facilitate community mapping of risks (e.g. through safety audits), engagement and participation, and the identification of priorities and feasible options for infrastructural work. This cross-disciplinary Site Management and Site Development team also coordinates other activities such as information provision, complaints handling, follow up with vulnerable individuals/families, and supporting subsidiary activities (such as temporary or permanent relocation) to ensure that a supportive, dignified and respectful process is maintained throughout. As part of the IOM-funded Site Management activities in the camp(s) of operation, CARE will strengthen/establish contextualised common and community-based Complaints, Feedback and Response mechanisms (CFRM). These will identify and integrate existing partner and community accountability mechanisms to streamline feedback collection, and strengthen the referral mechanisms to ensure timely responses to complaints.
ACTIVITIES:
1. CAMP OPERATION
- Conduct community risk assessments and safety audits with a gender focus, updating current fire safety plans through findings
- Develop and disseminate a fire safety plans, taking into account the specific needs, capacities and vulnerabilities of different groups (e.g. persons with disability, elderly, young children, pregnant and lactating mothers, and female headed households)
- Establish a risk reduction action plan for each block and sub-block establishing action plans and procuring contingency supplies, taking into account the abovementioned vulnerabilities.
- Facilitate relocation and decongestion activities undertaking requisite site enhancement and infrastructural development activities, through a participatory and protection-led approach.
2. IMPROVED LIVING CONDITIONS IN THE CAMP
- Participate in a community-led integrated (multi-sectoral) site plan and site improvement plan at the macro level (camp) as well as micro level (block)
- Contribute to the development of a community approved Camp Strategy and support community to implement it providing required tools and resources.
- Install and maintain required infrastructure, protection and safety equipment including bridges, stairs, pathways, fire points, drains, handrails and accessibility features, and stabilize high risk slopes.
- Offer training and local labour employment for community members to undertake site enhancement and planning activities, including for females wherever feasible (e.g. through community outreach, facilitation, engagement, information sharing, and monitoring of activities).
3. SUSTAINABLE APPROACHES: SLOPE STABILISATION
- Conduct reforestation and environmental rehabilitation activities, promoting social forestry and slope mitigation through planting trees, grass plants and other relevant vegetation.

Needs

On 25 August 2017, violence erupted in Rakhine State, Myanmar, triggering a massive refugee influx into Bangladesh, 728,000 refugees have since arrived, bringing the total refugee population to 921,000 (ISCG 2018). Of this, almost 864,000 (almost 94%) of refugees are living in camps characterised by extreme congestion, overcrowding (62% of households reported sharing their shelter with other households; UNHCR-REACH MSNA, July 2018) and the risks associated with inappropriate shelter and settlement conditions - hazards of landslide, mudslide and flooding, risk of fire outbreak, threats to health, wellbeing and safety, and serious protection risks, particularly related to gender and Gender-Based Violence (GBV). Due to this congestion, and linked to the proximity of the refugee settlements amongst or close to host community households and agricultural lands, there is no room to expand many of the camps further. This requires a carefully choreographed process (which may or may not involve temporary relocation) that requires measures to improve the overall settlement conditions, including increasing effective access to water, latrines and hygiene information, information and guidance on how to maintain safe shelters and healthy homes, and specific measures to improve safety, such as solar-powered lighting for which 53% of households reporting solar lamps as a priority need (UNHCR-REACH MSNA, July 2018). Cox's Bazar is amongst the poorest districts in Bangladesh and has struggled with food security and is prone to cyclones, monsoons, and mudslides, receiving triple the global average precipitation annually. Despite constraints, host community members welcomed the arriving refugees, sharing food, shelter, and supplies. The refugee's extended presence has strained community resources, leading to deteriorating inter-communal relations. As humanitarian partners strive to serve refugees, host communities are receiving little attention, despite the diminished resources and amplified risks they face, including fewer livelihood opportunities, stripped land, and rising costs. As friction between host and refugee communities grows, women and girls in host communities are experiencing a significant rise in GBV. This situation coupled, with extreme congestion, leaves them vulnerable to natural disaster, fires, and infectious disease outbreaks. It is estimated that around 336,000 host community members in need of assistance in the areas in or surrounding the camps in Ukhiya and Teknaf. In Camp 16, for example, host community households reside inside the camp perimeter, now surrounded by a sprawling refugee settlement. They constitute around 5% of the total population of the camp and face the same challenges in terms of shelter and settlement conditions, access to infrastructure, and settlement-level risks given the close proximity. CARE Bangladesh is currently the lead Site Management Agency in Camp 16 funded by IOM and is likely to expand its presence in Camp 13 through multi-sectoral activities. CARE's portfolio includes Shelter, WASH, food assistance, Protection and GBV-prevention, Shelter, Sexual and Reproductive Health, and Nutrition. Based on daily field assessments, there is a big gap in maintenance of existing infrastrutural facilities eg: access roads, solar street lights and household solar lanterns, pathways, slope stabilisation, drainage channels, solid waste management.
