Project Summary

Jonglei, Western Equatoria and Eastern Equatoria states are among others in the country where WASH needs have been worsening, aggravated the COVID 19 pandemic, unprecedented floods (in Jonglei mainly) with vulnerabilities affecting particularly IDP, displaced, returnee and host populations, including disabled, girls, women, men and boys who continue to face violations of their rights including killings; and aggravated sexual /gender based violence particularly among women and girls (Protection Trends 2021; REACH Reports 2021).
Displaced women and girls continue to be dis-proportionally affected by protection related WASH issues, with GBV (be it rape, sexual assault, physical assault, forced/child marriage, or psychological/emotional abuse-including abductions) occurring due to risks associated with insufficient and unsafe access to water, hygiene and sanitation facilities. This is in addition to high malnutrition which is further aggravated by unprecedented food insecurity levels Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates above emergency threshold of 15% in most of the target counties with 1.3 million children under the age of 5, and at least 676,000 pregnant and lactating women at risk of acute malnourishment (FSNMS, 2021); and 8.3 million people, expected to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the 2022 (HNO 2021; FSMS 2021).
This is in addition to high prevalence of WASH related diseases such as acute watery diarrhoea, respiratory diseases, eye infections often associated with poor access to WASH (UNICEF Situation Reports, 2021; South Sudan Health Cluster Sitreps 2021).
Community Aid for Relief and Development (CARD) is proposing to target 24,000 beneficiaries made up of 4000 men, 4000 women, 8000 girls and 8000 boys who include people with disabilities with a WASH integrated response, in line with the WASH Cluster 2022 HRP strategy in addition to responding to most vulnerable populations with acute needs that will provide an integrated WASH response with nutrition and health, which will contribute in reducing the burden of WASH-related diseases as aggravating factors for malnutrition with key actions aimed at breaking the transmission cycle of main communicable diseases, improving food intake of malnourished children and contributing to the sustainability of health and nutrition objectives. This will be accompanied by actions that address live-saving needs in disaster contexts in restoring immediately access to basic WASH services and build resilience building in flood affected areas/ counties.
CARD has been operating in the targeted counties for the past eight years with established offices presence in targeted counties. CARD has competent staff, logistics and community goodwill, in addition to having good relations with the host communities, local leaders, RRC officials and state/county ministries etc. These strong networks coupled with established local networks will be engaged and mobilized to rapidly implement the proposed activities.
In addition, CARD will ensure involvement of beneficiaries in planning, implementation, and monitoring. CARD takes a “do no harm” approach, by employing community-based protection measures. CARD’s strategy will also ensure upholding core principles or programming via quality interventions, ensuring AAP, inter-cluster synergies, protection of beneficiaries including GBV and an innovative integrated multi sector approach. CARD will in addition regularly review and adapt ways of working with affected populations and increase levels of transparency and participation of affected boys, girls, women and men including people with disabilities. CARD will also mainstream cross cutting measures-gender equality and women and youth empowerment, protection, environmental conservation, AAP including taking into account Gender Based Violence mitigation measures.

Most vulnerable populations residing in these region cited insecurity as a primary concern particularly in Jonglei which has witnessed unprecedented flooding extremes with at least 65,000 IDPs residing in Bor South, Twic East, Duk, Jalle, Bangachorot, Jam , Liil, Mabior biol and Yolthar among others in addition to recent inter-communal fighting at border areas of Baidit (REACH Situation Overview: Jonglei, 2021/ 2o22). The current flooding extremes has led to submerging of basic life support amenities including boreholes, latrines, farms and disrupted livelihood activities. As needs are worsening, the most vulnerable and displaced populations, including girls and boys (including those with disabilities who are disproportionately affected) continue to face violations of their rights including sexual /gender based violence. Sexual and Gender Based Violence (SGBV) has remained a major protection concern for women/girls in Jonglei and Equatorias with SGBV reported as the main protection concern for women by the highest proportion of assessed settlements in (46%), remaining largely unchanged from previous months (Protection Trends, 2021). 7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiary feedback including any complaints raised will be tackled CARD in consultation with implementation committees including making appropriate changes where necessary to ensure beneficiary views are taken into consideration. This will also include referalls through other agencies in case of cross sectoral support.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CARD will ensure vulnerable members including people with disabilities are engaged and actively participate throughout the project cycle, including being part of implementation committees and have their feedback taken into account. These will be supported at their areas of residence/ homes including sharing of key contacts and focal points for one on one support. AAP through two-wayfeedback by engaging and giving vulnerable people an opportunity to feedback on implementation and CARD ensuring their views are tacked and they are provided with relevant information.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CARD will ensure it holds community consultations and engagement in regard to PSEA including sharing key details of beneficiary rights and reporting channels, which shall be confidential, either through community chosen PSEA personell and PSEA feedback desks including direct phone contacts of Juba offices for confidential reporting. CARD staff and associated personnel havesigned a code of conduct that includes PSEA including having in place communication to target beneficiaries on their rights and reporting mechanisms in case of violations, which include PSEA.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

CARD will hold consultative sessions at the beginning of the project including throughout implementation in addition to formation of implementation committees to spearhead beneficiary messaging on the project including collection of feedback and complaints that will inform implementation.
