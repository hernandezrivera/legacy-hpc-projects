Project Summary

The project aims at ensuring the that women, girls, boys and men are able to meet their basic needs; and are safe in their communities and receive support when they experience harm in the targeted locations in the Far North region. The project will provide multi-purpose cash assistance, gender-based violence (GBV) and protection programming to 8,619 beneficiaries to help them meet their basic needs, be safer in their communities, and receive support when they experience harm.

Besoins / Needs

Far North region – Since 2014, this region of Cameroon is the second most affected by the Boko Haram crisis; as well as response measures taken by the local authorities. More than 50 per cent of the total population living in the Far North – 1.9 million people – need humanitarian assistance. With an upsurge of violence since the end of 2018, number of IDPs (270,870 people), returnees (110,574 people) and Nigerian refugees out of camps (46,854 people) increased by 7% in 2019. The crisis has exacerbated recurrent protection concerns, restricted basic services, devastated the economy and created a climate of destitution and fear. In addition, with half of the population under the age of 18 and a poverty rate of 74 per cent, young people are more vulnerable and exposed to recruitment by non-state armed groups and other grave child rights violations. Out of the six Divisions affected by this Boko Haram crisis three are severely affected including Logone and Chari, Mayo Sava and Mayo Tsanaga. A needs assessments conducted by IRC in September 2018 in Mayo Sava and Mayo Tsanaga shows that 73.53% of households surveyed resorted to negative coping strategies to meet their basic needs, presenting an additional protection risk for women, girls, and vulnerable individuals.
