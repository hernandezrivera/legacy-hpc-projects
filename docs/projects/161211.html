Project Summary

Multiply displaced communities face significant health challenges in parts of Jonglei and Eastern Equatoria, which prior to the conflict had limited or no access to health care services. The project seeks to provide access to essential lifesaving health care services to vulnerable populations focusing on the major causes of morbidity and mortality in the counties of Ayod, Duk, Twic East, Pigi and Fangak (Jonglei); and Kapoeta South, Kapoeta North and Kapoeta East (Eastern Equatoria State)
CMD aims at fostering improved access, prevent, detect and respond to epidemic prone diseases, improve resilience among vulnerable populations and deliver an integrated response cutting across health, WASH, Nutrition, food security and protection. The project will implement and expand essential primary health care emergency clinical packages targeting the most vulnerable, carry out preventive health measures through immunization targeting children, PLWs and <5s. Working alongside health cluster leads; CMD will implement outbreak investigation and response in the targeted locations while mainstreaming accountability to affected populations.
An expanded program will include HIV/AIDS/TB care and reproductive health (RH) services including SGBV. CMD will implement integrated response for severe acute malnutrition with medical complications especially in locations with high cases of malnutrition as well as implement integrated response for epidemic prone communicable diseases including diarrheal, vector, Viral Hemorrhagic diseases with WASH/Nut/FSL. Emergency health protection for people with disabilities will be provided across all project locations.
In the locations of Ayod, Fangak, Kapoeta South, North and East, CMD will carry out referrals for Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (CeMONC), minor trauma and surgery, and work with development health partners to scale up integrated response to SAM with medical complications.
The project will directly benefit 8,333 people (51% female, 48% IDPs, 45% vulnerable host communities, 7% returnees and other groups in the locations of Ayod, Duk, Twic East, Pigi and Fangak (Jonglei); and Kapoeta South, Kapoeta North and Kapoeta East (Eastern Equatoria State)

1 - Needs

The adverse effects of conflict, erratic weather patterns, lack of skilled and adequate personnel, issues with access continue to undermine efforts to attain acceptable standards in health service provision in Greater Upper Nile and Eastern Equatoria states. With a protraction of the humanitarian crisis, communities are stretched in their efforts to cope with emerging needs, resulting in increased morbidity and mortality. Food insecurity and malnutrition persist in Jonglei and Eastern Equatoria with over 50% of the population projected to be above phase 3 IPC classification in the early part of 2020. With populations slightly over 1,1930,000 persons more than half of these women and children. (NBSS, 2019), over 60% of these are in need of humanitarian support despite a period of calmness over the last 6 months. Health cluster projections place the number of persons to be reached in 2020 at 7,442 in Ayod, 51,472 in Duk, 10,052 in Twic East, 19,455 in Pigi, 36,608 in Fangak, 4,666 in Kapoeta East, 57,148 in Kapoeta North and 8,606 in Kapoeta South; with more than half of these women and children. Despite gains done through humanitarian support, non-functionality of health facilities, low levels of health supplies and shortage of staffing have all been a contributory factor to a slag in achieving desired results and projections. Some of the locations targeted lie under the Sobat and Eastern flood plains and are further prone to flooding, resulting in upsurges in communicable diseases. IPC projections for the locations targeted indicate high levels of food insecurity and malnutrition, alongside WASH cluster needs analyses that indicate low levels of sanitation and hygiene practices and lack of adequate clean and safe water. Mobility of populations due to several drivers such as conflict, food insecurity and livelihood opportunities will further necessitate adoption of mobile clinics as a methodology to counter possibility of disease outbreaks amongst multiply displaced populations, mining and pastoral communities. Significant disruption of markets, high levels of inflation have further lessened abilities of communities to access essential drugs, thereby relying on pipeline supplies provided by partners. EPI coverage remains low in the county, as a result of lack of cold chain facilities, access constraints and low or inadequate staffing. Protection issues are widely reported in the locations targeted, with limited capacity of the community structures to clinically deal with cases of SGBV. The project will directly benefit 8,333 people (51% female, 48% IDPs, 45% vulnerable host communities, 7% returnees and other groups in the locations of Ayod,Duk, Twic East, Pigi, Fangak, Kapoeta East, Kapoeta North and Kapoeta South.

2 - Activities

As per the health cluster guidelines and packages CMD will carry out the following activities in locations of Ayod, Duk, Twic East, Pigi, Fangak Kapoeta South, Kapoeta North and Kapoeta East through screening/detection, provision of essential health care package, management of common illness/treatment, referral, promotive and preventive health services, surveillance/EWARN/reporting and health promotion/risk communication. These include: • ICCM • CHP • PHCU • PHCC/UPGRADED PHCC • MOBILE TEAM • County Hospital.
