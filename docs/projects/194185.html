Project Summary

This project aims to reduce the risk and impact of Explosive Ordinance (EO) through land release operations - Non-Technical Survey (NTS), Technical Survey (TS) and Clearance - and Explosive ordinance Risk Education (EORE).
Through Land release interventions the project will minimally release a total of 600,000 square meters of contaminated land (75,000 square meters in Darfur, 175,000 square meters in Blue Nile and 350,000 square meters in South Kordofan, as safe space for humanitarian actors and local populations and can be utilize for their daily livelihood activities. The provision of the risk education activities (include direct EORE session, Training, public information disseminations and campaigns), will enable 107,800 (26950 boys, 26950 girls, 26500 men and 27400 women) to adopt safe behaviors in the presence of EO.
Moreover, the project will contribute to continues update of Information Management System for Mine Action (IMSMA) with new Data, for better planning for the MA operations in project target areas in Blue Nile, South Kordofan and Darfur states.
The project intervention is inline with Mine Action sub sector objectives 1 and 2.

1 - Needs

South Kordofan, Blue Nile and Darfur states are the among the most landmines/ERWs-contaminated states in Sudan respectively. In Sudan there are 391 active hazardous areas (HA) ( 222 confirmed Hazardous area (CHA) and 169 suspected Hazardous area (SHA), 243 active hazardous areas in SK ( 42 CHA, 21 SHA active hazardous areas is SK and 63 Blue Nile ( 151CHA and 92 SHA ) and 77active HA in five Darfur states( 33( 18 CHA, 15 SHA) in ND, 1 SHA in SD, 5 ( 1 CHA, 4 SHA) in ED, 29( 7 CHA,22 SHA) in WD, 9 SHA in CD) .The same states have also borne the most casualties of LM/ERWs-related accidents in the country where and according to IMSMA Sep. 2022 ,SK reported 752 causality (200 killed( 60 men, 10 women, 26boys, 12girls,92 Not Specified), 552 injured(238 28 87 26 173 Not Specified) ), BN reported 464 causality(149 killed (84men, 1women, 32 boys, 3 girls, 29 Not Specified , 315 injured( 187men, 15 women, 59 boys, 4girls, 50 Not Specified) and 655 reported causality in 5 Darfur states( North Darfur ( 47 killed (5 men, 3 women, 29 boys, 3 girls, 7 Not Specified ), 152 injured (29 men, 9 women, 75boys, 16 girls, 23 Not Specified ) South Darfur(19 killed( 2 men, 2 women, 8boys, 4 girls, 3 Not Specified injured), West Darfur(21 killed( 2 Men, 1women, 16 boys, 2 girls), 85 injured (24 Men, 1women, 34boys, 20 girls, 6 Not Specified) ), East Darfur(19 killed (3Men, 2 women, 9 boys, 3 girls, 2 Not Specified) , 61injured (16 men, 1 women, 32 boys, 10 girls, 2 Not Specified) and Central Darfur( ( 13 killed (1men, 7 boys, 3 girls 2 Not Specified, 97 injured (25men, 10 women, 47 boys, 11girls 4 Not Specified) . LM/ ERW are considered as significant threat to the safety and security of the local population in these areas. LM/ERW contamination causes social and development disruption, preventing the local population from their normal human activities, little emergency assistance can be provided, and economic development is hindered to take place in these areas. The presence of landmines/ERW posts a significant and one of the primary physical risks to death and injury of children both boys and girls and adult as well in humanitarian contexts. In addition, their presence exacerbates psychosocial distress among children, their care givers, families and communities and their impacts on family livelihoods can leave children at greater risk of violence, abuse and exploitation. Women and girls on the other hand are also vulnerable either as direct victims or as part of families who become even further economically, socially and psychologically marginalized by the loss of key income earners and burden of care for accident survivors. Incidences are occurred due to lack of knowledge about the risk of landmines/ERW or for economic and domestic reasons. Women are collecting water, children gathering firewood or playing, men working on the land or tending cattle. Anyone who goes about their normal day in a mined area is at risk. Based on the role women play in society and their daily routine jobs are more likely to expose to landmines/ERW danger. The action is urgently needed to support severe settlement of IDPs and returnees, especially for in the affected parts those states, because their homes were in disputed areas. Initially, the numbers of causalities by mines/ERW are very high. Most victims were children herding cattle and domestic animals. However, there were also some reports of farmers who were injured or died because of farming on un-cleared mine fields. This planned project will reduce the number of incidents in village’s residential sites close to minefields and dangerous hazards areas. These landmines/ERW represent a new danger to IDPs and returnees as most of the strike locations are close to villages and/or pasture and agriculture lands. The threat thus significantly facing post-conflict communities in many parts two states. It is therefore landmines/ERW Risk Education is the most practical way of decreasing

2 - Activities/Outputs

Explosive Ordinance Risk Education (EORE) through direct methods Formation, Training, equipment, and deploying of 7 EORE mobile Teams 2 in south Kordofan, 2 in Blue Nile and 3 in five Darfur states, consist of 28 personals (14Male and 14Female), 7 team leader and 21 trainers, targeting people at risk population in prioritized areas in those states for 10 months. GAH EORE teams disseminate EORE messages through participatory approach using different methodologies including (Interpersonal Communications, Direct EORE Sessions, street theatre, songs, and puppet show. The team is planned to target 15400 people in 440 sessions (two session per day* 22 day per month) for 10 Months, 35 person per session. In total 107,800 will be reached with direct EORE messages in the target areas (26950 boys, 26950 girls, 26500 men and 27400 women), Printing and distribution of EORE Information Education and Communication (IEC) material (20000 leaflets ,10000posters and 5000booklets), Conduct EORE 20 Public campaigns targeting 10,300 beneficiaries (2,900 boys,3,600 girls, 1500 men and 2,300 women),To select and train 500 community volunteers(50% Female, 20 trainings, 5 days each, 25 person per training) including Mines, ERW survivors to act as EORE Recourse Persons in the targeted areas) to pass the important messages among their respective communities Develop, record and broadcast EORE Radio Messages though the local radio stations in the two states targeting 14,000 at risk communities in the two states (2,200 boys, 2,700 girls, 4,500 men and 4,600 women)Conduct 2 KAPB survey per and post to the project activities to measure the behavior and attitude change among the targeted communities Report any mew DA, SHA and mine/ERW accident to IMSMA. The team is trained on using IMSMA formats. Moreover GAH will enhance access to services for ERW survivors through referral them to other relevant service providers ( PSS, physical rehabilitation and material assistance) Provision of Mine Action community liaison base on NMAC task and priorities through exchange information between Respond to humanitarian development needs through land release operations, Release land contaminated with EO for productive use; Equip, train and deploy 3 MTT team one in South Kordofan, one in Blue Nile and one in Darfur States, in addition to deploy 3 NTS teams (2 BN &2 SK) , the NTS teams will support the MTT's with the data collection and assessment. The MTT Team consist of 1 Technical Field Manager, 1 team leader, 1 medic , 1 community liaison and 8 deminers and 3 drivers , each NTS team will target 60 community to the none-technical survey for 40 community with total number of communities 160 community , the same all identified or confirmed or suspected hazard will be subjected to Technical survey by the MTT team who will conduct the following Land release activities including but not limited to ( NTS , TS , Manual Clearance , EOD , Post clearance assessment , to facilitate smoother Aid service delivery , safe and meaningful access to basic service and productive use of the land the targeted beneficiaries , the planned activities and intervention will be conducted in close coordination with NMAC and UNMAS putting community priorities in to account.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

GAH is already actively engaged in the targeted localities in SK,BN and Darfur states. Key leaders of the IDP's, returnees, refugees, and host communities will be directly involved in the collection of baseline data, which will ensure that the project is well-established and benchmark that enables to measure the impact of the implemented activities. A participatory inception workshop will be held in the target area to familiarize beneficiaries and other stakeholders with project objectives, proposed activities, and timelines. The workshop will ensure the equitable representation of all targeted communities including people at risk. GAH had established and maintained effective feedback mechanisms to receive comments, suggestions, and complaints, using a variety of communication channels that are accessible to all project stakeholders for both confidential and informal feedback. GAH has a zero-tolerance policy on abuse of power and sexual exploitation. The Code of Conduct and PSEAH will be displayed in the centers, PSEAH is also included in the project staff as part of their contractual agreement. A refresher training will be provided by GAH focal person on PSEAH to project staff and other partner staff. GAH has two hotlines through which partners as well as community members can raise concerns or provide suggestions that they cannot otherwise voice in public. For GAH, the hotlines lead to the board of directors and the general manager. GAH will regularly collaborate with partners on feedback referral and response processes, when necessary. GAH will facilitate communication with project stakeholders at key stages of the project cycle, sharing information and communicating via language and media that are culturally appropriate and accessible to all. GAH will consult stakeholders to ensure that communications and messages used in the campaigns and sessions are accepted by and comprehensible to all community members. In media reports, neither organization will use any language nor did images that may suggest that girls and women who have undergone any type of GBV are “spoiled” or otherwise stigmatize GAH will promote participation among the target communities and empower the community by making sure that beneficiaries know they have a right to equitable access to services and safe assistance, and where and how to obtain it. GAH will hold quarterly feedback community meetings to receive feedback and suggestions to improve interventions quality and gain the stockholder confidence.
