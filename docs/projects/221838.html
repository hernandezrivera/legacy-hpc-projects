Project Summary

This intervention will deliver essential hygiene and dignity kits in conflict affected districts of Macomia, Meluco, Mocimboa Da Praia and Nangade, in Cabo Delgado Province, northern Mozambique. The goal is to create safe, disease-free environments that allow host communities, returnees and IDPs children and adolescents to resume their education. Additionally, the intervention will help control water-borne disease outbreaks in these areas.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project will provide critical lifesaving disease control mechanisms that will enable both hand-washing and water treatment in communities affected by conflict and areas affected by both conflict and cholera. In schools, the project will create safe, disease-free conditions for school-aged children to return to classes. It will target children returning to schools with depleted hygiene resources due to a high influx of students, prioritizing schools with higher number of returnees and IDPs. Additionally, in conflict areas that are also identified as cholera hotspots the project implements cholera control measures to prevent the spread of the disease.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The project’s priority actions include the installation of hand-washing stations with soap and water available, and training of hygiene promoters that will do promotion awareness activities in primary schools that were damaged during the conflict and/or are experiencing a high influx of displaced students in Macomia, Meluco, Mocímboa da Praia, and Nangade districts. Additionally, the project will provide sanitation items that will help female students to manage menstrual hygiene waste.In conflict areas that are also at elevated risk for water related disease outbreaks such as cholera, the project will focus on supporting communities by raising awareness, promoting improved hygiene practice and distributing essential hygiene items like soap and chlorine, and promoting lifesaving critical hygiene practices.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Risks are assessed during the design phase, rather than during implementation, drawing on lessons learned from previous years by both the Mentor Initiative (MI) and other sector partners. Staff receive training in accountability and Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). All providers undergo background checks, and PSEA is included as a key topic in every training session, along with information on MENTOR’s complaint mechanisms.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Gender component and a transversal element of the program, with a section dedicated to it in the training of hygiene promoters, as there are considerations to be made in terms of gender (menstrual hygiene management, key hand-washing moments involving feeding babies, etc).It is also present in the design of the latrines, which are adapted for women of menstrual age, allowing enough space for changing of clothes and including waste bins for disposal of menstrual waste, with appropriate signs. Awareness sessions are also given regarding Menstrual Hygiene Management when sanitation items are donated for latrines for female students, including appropriate signage.Regarding promotion awareness sessions, gender inclusive messaging is ensured, as diseases impact genders differently, exposure to water. Etc.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

MENTOR has strong ties and collaboration with Humanity & Inclusion (HI), a partner specialized in identifying people with disabilities through the application of the Washington Group Questionnaire, and MI’s MEAL Officer was trained in the application of this questionnaire. Hand-washing stations designed by MENTOR are accessible for people with reduced mobility.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Students are trained about how to avoid environmental pollution by maintaining the school grounds, including water points, clean and free of garbage, with adequate drainage systems that avoid water stagnation.All latrines incorporate waste bins for waste disposal, and environmental health (safe waste disposal, safe water transport, storage and treatment) in a key component of the messaging disseminated by hygiene promoters. Regarding hand-washing, the project promotes the use of Tippy Taps, which minimizes water consumption.
