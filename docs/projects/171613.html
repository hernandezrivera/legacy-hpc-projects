Project Summary

2020 recorded the highest number of displaced people since 2017 due to the long-standing conflicts, recurring floods and the impact of locust and the COVID-19 pandemic. Banadir has the highest displaced people in need. An estimated 1.4 million (705,615 girls) crisis-affected children, including children living with disabilities (CLWD), urgently need education assistance. Accordingly to an own survey, children of the most vulnerable IDP households (single female headed hh (SFHH), hh with pregnant and lactating women, hh with disabled children, hh with less than 1 meal per day, etc.) are excluded from education because hh cannot afford to pay tuition fees and learning materials. Further, schools are often not equipped to teach children with special needs, and prevailing cultural attitudes favour boys in education while girls get restricted for house-bound activities.
Diakonie Katastrophenhilfe (DKH) and implementing partners (IPs) seek to facilitate crisis-affected vulnerable and marginalized boys, girls and disabled 3,300 children (1,650F, 330 disabled) from internally displaced persons’ (IDPs) households (HH) to have access to safe and quality education by provision of conditional and restricted Vouchers for Education (v4e) and learning materials, and 30 safe temporary learning spaces (TLS) in the Banadir region. The TLS are each managed by a teachers’ association (TA) of 5 teachers each, (76 female teachers), teachers drawn from the IDPs. Also, 30 Community Education Committees (CEC), each composed of 7 members comprising the TA representative, parents, and members of the camp management committee (CMC), oversee delivery of education and related protection in the IDP settlements.
Quality education is delivered in the TLS through capacity building initiatives in school management on safety and safe water provision, enhancing hygiene and sanitation promotion. vulnerable learners are enabled to pay fees and access learning materials through distribution of 2 conditional v4e per learner per term; to be redeemed in shops and the TLS, which TLS use to pay teacher incentives and cover costs for maintenance and provision of disabled and gender-friendly WASH facilities, and COVID-19 compliance materials. DKH aims to provide monthly food vouchers to the learners, conditionally to school attendance and in place of school feeding. Teachers are trained in protection and pedagogical skills, in addition to operation and administration of TLSes. Beneficiary selection criteria comprise newly displaced most vulnerable HH (see above) and children with special needs. The CECs, CMCs and parents will be sensitized on the importance of enrolling CLWD and ensuring an enabling and protective environment at the TLS. The project will enhance the protective role of education and integrate child protection activities at the school. TLSs are placed inside the IDP camps in coordination with the CMCs to enhance the safety of the learners reducing the distance to school and mitigate vulnerability against forceful eviction, hence social integration into the neighbouring community. The temporary design of the TLS ensures 360° escape routes for the children in case of a physical attack. Gender separated latrines and handwashing facilities are present in the TLS. The TLS shall be transformed into ordinary schools as the displacement protracts. There are ideas to expand this project approach to the IDP camps in Galkacyo in 2021 or 2022 in nexus with a BMZ-funded TVET project that targets displaced drop-out pastoralist youth for economic reintegration.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The project prefers the use of Cash and voucher assistance as it provides families with a source of income through CVA, in addition to offering girls protection from the increased risk of physical and sexual violence in an emergency situation. The process of redeeming the vouchers also enables the running of schools through redemption to pay the teachers.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

The project has considered market functionality through conducting assessments, including market and seasonality analysis. The cash will be provided based on the minimum expenditure basket, ensuring that while the beneficiaries purchasing power is increased they will be able to get access to livelihoods from the existing markets. The results of the seasonality analysis will inform the appropriate time of the year to implement the interventions, to ensure that the markets function as normal and beneficiaries are able to access goods and services from the markets. Besides, the amount of cash being injected will be little and may not have a negative effect on the prices of goods in the local market. During the implementation of the project, the team will liaise with other stakeholders to monitor the price behaviour and supplies for commodities to inform the effects the cash intervention is having on the local economy. Decisions will be made appropriately to ensure the cash is used for the right purpose, with no or little effect in the market functionality.

Mitigation Measures

Exclusion and marginalization: i. DKH will use a beneficiary selection criteria building on vulnerability and marginalization comprising, newly displaced HH; and beneficiaries with specials needs (Pregnant, disabled and lactating mothers); HH with ailing members; female-headed HH with no means of income and child-headed HH; to select most vulnerable HH to identify the children for the project support.ii) DKH will work with the community leaders including the CMC to profile school-going children who may be challenged to access education due to vulnerability caused by poverty and disability and will be identified and enlisted for school supportiii) DKH will also conduct sensitization on the rights of the beneficiaries and ensuring the selection criteria is usediv) The project will encourage community participation in the selection of the beneficiaries Stakeholder Interference:i) DKH will provide a secure learning environment to the IDP children while the teachers will be trained in first aid (including psychological first aid) and the first aid kits will be equipped ii) DKH will continue to encourage the employment of female teachers, as has been the trend, to build a more inclusive environment with tolerance and respect iii) DKH will offer psychosocial support to children who have been impacted by displacement iv) DKH will ensure all staff, implementing partners, and teachers working with the school children sign and adhere to a humanitarian Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and rights of beneficiaries. v) In cooperation with protection actors, DKH will enhance the capacity of CECs and teachers to monitor, report, and refer protection cases in accordance with standard operating procedures. Beneficiary selectioni) The design of the TLS offers child-friendly spaces whereby the location and access routes to the TLS poses no risks to children and teachers. The children and teachers and specifically the women and girls, don’t have to travel long distances since the TLS is located within the camp site and the class timing is safe for girls and women to travel to and from school without exposure to harm. The TLS provides clearly marked gender-segregated latrines ii) DKH and partners will reduce the risk of harm to schoolgirls and female teachers by advocating for female teacher enrolment and develop and clearly communicate a school Code of Conduct that respects the safety and dignity of girls and female teachers. iii) iDKH and partners will advocate for equal access to education for girls and sensitize the community through the CECs the value and benefit of girls’ education. iv) DKH and partners will maintain and enhance the existing complaints and feedback mechanisms to be more accessible, confidential, and well understood by beneficiaries and in particular the girls and women. v) DKH and partners will reinforce the capacity of parents and teachers to act as a protective layer for children against violations e.g. FGM, early forced marriages, hazardous labor. Protection - Land disputesi) DKH’s approach to the risk of TLS eviction has been to capacity builds the CECs who engage with relevant government bodies (MOE), local authorities, and landowners to negotiate for land use for the TLS. The CECs enter into land-use agreements to allow the TLS to be set up and continuous checks are done on possible threats of eviction. ii) Incorporation with protection actors and CECs, the project will establish reporting mechanisms to report security incidences concerning the learning spaces to advocate against attacks on TLS. iii) This project with the participation of the community will use information on the existing the power dynamics to resolve land ownership disputes and also inform monitoring activities and TLS relocation spotsiv) The TLS infrastructure designs make them mobile and easy to move in case there is an eviction.

Please provide further explanation

In future, and asas exit strategy, as much TLSes supported by DKH and its partner as possible shall be transformed into ordinary schools that continue serving the humanitarian needs of IDPs in education in the settlements along Afgooye Road in Banadir. This will be gradual as it is a process requiring the coordinated and timely engagement of different actors. The intervention aim is to have the TLSs recognised recognized and supported by the local administration in the long run. DKH and partners in Somalia continues to support the TLS while at the same time engaging, as the displacement protraction continues, the education administrators, including the Ministry of Education (MoE) towards taking up the management of the facilities. DKH and partners continue to strengthen the capacities of the local school management structures, including CECs, TAs, and teachers on various areas of management and will useful in the long long-term delivery of education in the facilities, if taken up by the government. Selection The selection of beneficiaries will be guided by a clear criteria that will ensure inclusion of children living with disabilities, gender gender-disaggregated information, and targeting and address the needs for school feeding through FSL activities to promote retention of school children and increased access for vulnerable children (IDPs and vulnerable children).
