Project Summary

The objective of the project is to strengthen protection services for the population affected and affected by disasters and civil wars, and through community protection interventions; The project aims to strengthen the delivery of child protection services and interventions for girls and boys with a focus on those most at risk of violence, exploitation and abuse, and to increase access to safe community spaces to empower women and children; To provide mental and social health services to children affected by the conflict; Improving psychosocial resilience through the social capital of IDPs and host communities; The project aims to improve the psychosocial well-being of the children most affected by the conflict in Sudan. and increasing access to child protection and reintegration services for conflict-affected children and adults in South Darfur through the implementation of a comprehensive community approach and the full range of CPIE activities.

1 - Needs

Boys and girls of all age groups continue to face multiple protection concerns and violations of children's rights on a regular basis. Civil unrest, tribal strife, ongoing displacement, depreciation of money, worsening poverty, drought, exposure to violence, floods, and persistent lack of access to services and even basic necessities greatly affect the lives and well-being of children. Gross violations of children's rights remain a source of grave concern. The current protection landscape and spillover effects of the crisis have cumulatively eroded children’s resilience; stresses family coping mechanisms and social safety nets; I left a legacy of psychological distress. The social and economic impact of the current political instability is making society highly dependent on humanitarian assistance and families resorting to harmful coping strategies affecting adolescent girls and boys in particular. The Child Protection Area of ​​Responsibility (CP AOR), together with its partners, is committed to strengthening its coordinated and rights-based approach, prioritizing resolution and promoting social cohesion in humanitarian response strategies and the 2023 Results Framework.The child protection response in 2023 plans to reach 1,750 people (900 IDPs, 400 residents, 450 returnees) in clusters 2. NoP will prioritize prevention programmes, while maintaining a community-based approach in its interventions targeting the most vulnerable girls and boys who are suffering or at risk of suffering of violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect, including separation from family and child. work, severe psychosocial distress, physical and sexual violence, and early marriage by addressing the underlying causes.The project aims to achieve the following list of objectives 1- Community awareness to enhance GP/CP protection, especially for children, girls and boys, and people with special needs, and pay attention to women's issues.2- Providing technical and legal assistance in the field of land and ownership, and paying attention to legalizing lands and registering them with the authorities in order to avoid potential conflicts over land and hawakir.3- Providing psychological, social, health and legal support services to GBV/SGBV survivors4- Providing operating costs for women’s centers to carry out some work related to psychological support activities for female survivors of sexual violence (small income-generating projects) and handicrafts (manufacture of juices, knitting, embroidery, making improved stoves, Literies class)

2 - Activities/Outputs

Activity 1Raise awareness about services and the Child Helpline by providing girls and boys with specialized child protection response services, including case management for child survivors of sexual violence, child labour, child recruitment, unaccompanied children in need of childcare, and children in need of Alternative care, identification documents and training of community committees on safety corridors for children in emergencies in targeted areas Activity 2Train community members on accountability and prevention of Child Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and establish complaints mechanisms at the community level in coordination with other sectors, including education, SNFI, FSL, and health, water, sanitation and hygiene to ensure that safety procedures are followed for children. Activity 3Training a number of community members of both sexes on mine risks, child labor and gender-based violence, including child recruitment, separation, sexual violence, child marriage and female genital mutilation. Activity 4Train social researchers and counselors to work as mobile teams to address the vulnerabilities of children identified as being at particular risk of abuse, neglect, exploitation or violence, provide psychological support to children at risk and teach them life skills including reading, writing and arithmetic. Activity 5Qualifying child-friendly centers in targeted areas, activating child protection networks and community volunteers, and enhancing the capacity of human resources to respond to child protection concerns among child protection actors and other duty bearers. Activity 6Providing income-generating projects for survivors, the disabled and those with other special needs Actvity 7Establishing community child safety groups and teen clubs and providing them with the special needs of teenage children of both sexes
