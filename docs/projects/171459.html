Project Summary

The combined effects of widespread and severe flooding, desert locust infestation, socioeconomic impacts of COVID-19 and the cumulative impacts of previous shocks, has resulted in massive challenges in the WASH sector. Approximately 4.6M Somalis are in need due to insufficient access to potable water/inadequate sanitation services in many districts, thus negatively affecting the dignity of the vulnerable and displaced communities. Lack of adequate WASH services to the population is a main cause of ADD, leading to malnutrition in the country. The proposed action will be implemented in Galkayo, Hobyo, Cabudwaaq, Beletweyne, Afgoye and Banadir Districts, targeting vulnerable populations affected by different cyclic crises and by the novel Coronavirus. The aim of the proposed intervention is to save lives, alleviate suffering, maintain human dignity. The intermediate outcome of the project is to reduce the vulnerability of crisis-affected people, especially PWDs, women, girls, boys: this will be done by increasing their access to safe water supply, adequate sanitation and hygiene promotion and at the same time contributing to health, nutrition, food security and education outcomes. To achieve this Cesvi will address the immediate needs of the crisis-affected host community and IDP households. The proposed intervention also aims at restoring access to safe and equitable basic water, sanitation and hygiene infrastructures where pre-existent infrastructures are in place and need to be rehabilitated/replaced. It is imperative for Cesvi to meet the needs of all beneficiaries: a disability inclusive approach and disability mainstreaming will be adopted. Gender mainstreaming will be ensured through all phases of the intervention. In line with the WASH cluster’s guidance, the main objectives of the action will be:
• Contribute to reducing acute needs among most vulnerable settlements and communities (including men, women, boys and girls,PWDs) in a dignified manner through life-saving WASH assistance.
• Enhance access to safe and equitable basic WASH services in targeted locations (including non-IDP men, women, boys and girls, PWDs) in a dignified manner.
• Enhance the safety, equity and accessibility of the WASH services and infrastructures for the vulnerable groups (including women, boys girls, elderly and PWDs).
In order to achieve these objectives, this action will be strongly integrated with Cesvi's programs (Health, Nutrition, FSL) in the same areas. Cesvi has been operating continuously in Somalia since 2006, working with displacement affected populations, urban and rural communities in FSL, Education, WASH, Health/Nutrition. Responses cover emergency, recovery and resilience. Cesvi is part of the Building Resilient Communities in Somalia (BRCiS) consortium, that takes a bottom-up approach to support communities in developing their capacity to resist/ absorb shocks without undermining their ability to move out of poverty. Since 2011 Cesvi responded to protracted crisis (drought), and recurrent shocks (conflicts, floods) with an integrated package of services (Health/Nutrition, FSL, WASH, DRR, NRM, Shelter), also entailing cash-based interventions. Cesvi is implementing Health and Nutrition Specific programs while promoting nutrition sensitive WASH programming. This is done through (a) prevention of malnutrition and other illnesses via strengthened and resilient behaviors and awareness and (b) better detection of malnutrition and other illnesses and early referral to relevant services at community and facility levels, (c) improved sustainable access to potable water, hygiene and sanitation, (d) treatment of malnutrition and other diseases when these services are not otherwise provided. These activities are coupled with the promotion of market integrated, shock-proof livelihood opportunities. The proposed intervention will be harmonized with the other ongoing programs, from Cesvi and other actors present in the targeted locations.

Project Activities

Activities Objective 1: • Emergency water trucking with temporary distribution systems for flood/drought affected communities in Galmudug,Hirshabelle (targeting 15,000 people x avg. 2 months). Cesvi will consider using water vouchers where applicable. • Extension/rehabilitation of 5 existing water supply systems in Hiran,Mudug,Lower Shabelle Regions. • Construction of 200 emergency HH shared latrines for flood-affected IDPs in Beletweyne. Cesvi will consider using a mixed CfW + build approach.• Distribution of HKs inclusive of HWTSS and MHM (2 months min.), for 3000 HHs. Cesvi will consider supporting the local market through vouchers (involving local vendors)/cash assistance to purchase the WASH items. • Organization of 20 cleaning campaigns (solid waste removal/disposal) in the 4 targeted regions. CfW modalities will be considered. Activities Objective 2: • Rehabilitation of 4 Shallow wells, as per cluster standards, in Afgoye District with also the construction of water tanks for water storage during periods of scarcity/contamination. • Rehabilitation of 6 BHs with full compliance to cluster guidelines (2 in Cabudwaq,2 in Afgooye,2 in Beletweyne districts), with addition of solar system, elevated water tanks, pipeline extensions, water kiosks; • Improvement of 1 berkads for harvesting/storing storm water in Beletweyne. • Repair of 1 water point (addition of a desalination system) in Beletweyne district. • Reinforcement of the existing water committees in the targeted locations to ensure proper maintenance of water services; In Beletweyne District, the DRR component in flood/drought prone areas will be reinforced. Women participation will be reinforced to guarantee decision-making and responsibility for water are equally shared by women and men. • Construction/Rehabilitation of gender sensitive latrines in 3 schools in Galkayo district, and in 5 health care facilities (Banadir/Afgoye/Beletweyne districts). • Construction of 300 HH shared latrines in the IDP camps in Galkayo,Beletweyne,Banadir. Under Obj. 1 and 2: all latrines/water points will be at a reasonable walking distance. All latrines will be built with solid walls and with locks at doors, will have light, will be separated by gender (or alternate options if relevant). HH will be consulted in the design, locations, delivery of water/sanitation services, considering ageing and disability as a vulnerability as well.Activities under Obj. 1 and 2: • Organization of Hygiene promotion campaigns in the targeted locations, also entailing a component on Co-Vid prevention with the installation of handwashing facilities (+water provision, soap distribution). Cesvi will conduct HP sessions also through the health/nutrition teams working in 6 static facilities and 12 mobile clinics in the targeted locations. Crosscutting: all categories and layers of the population will be involved in all phases of the intervention (needs assessments, consultations, design, implementation, evaluation). Consultation with women, men, PWDs, elders, marginalized groups will be organized. Boys and girls will also be encouraged to contribute, in accordance with their age and maturity. A gender sensitive, participatory approach will be adopted at all stages of the intervention to ensure that adequate and efficient support is provided by equally involving women, girls, boys and men of all ages in the project design. Disability will be mainstreamed through the entire intervention. Cesvi will consult with PWDs and will encourage their participation in all decision making processes. Agencies and experts dealing with disabilities in the areas of intervention will be consulted in order to get a better insight of the situation. WASH related information will be in formats that PWD can understand/access. The WASH facilities will be designed and constructed after consulting with different groups, among which PWDs, and according to the principles of universal designed so they can be used by all.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

In the proposed intervention, Cash/voucher will not replace all traditional WASH activities but will be used to complement in-kind activities. The impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic have increased the demand of the populations in regards to WASH goods and services and at the same time affected the supply of the commodities. Therefore, using modalities such as Cash and Vouchers Assistance (CVA) to implement Market-based programming (MBP) approaches have the potential to re-build the WASH market systems and increase the purchasing power of the affected population.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

A market analysis alongside traditional WASH assessments will be performed to inform an intervention that promotes a people centered approach as well as economic recovery. Cesvi actively participated in the JMMI conducted in August 2020 and lead by REACH. The results of the initiative informed the proposed intervention. Moreover, Cesvi will continue monitoring and assessing market functionality in the proposed areas of intervention. Cesvi CVA’s component will be based on constant update of the market assessment and response analysis. The CVA component will be adopted in the assistance to the affected population. A mixed approach will be adopted: CVA + in-kind or CVA + direct building, according to the situation on the ground. Where possible, Cesvi intends to provide HHs with CVA, through e-cash or e-vouchers modalities (according to the situation), to purchase water and access sanitation materials. Where possible, Cesvi will give priority to CFW for latrines construction/rehabilitation and for HP activities such as the cleaning campaigns. All NFIs and/or hygiene services, where distributed in in-kind, will be procured from the local markets. More specifically and as mentioned in the Activity section Cesvi is planning to implement the following activities through a mixed approach: CVA + in-kind or CVA + direct building: Emergency water trucking, construction of emergency HH shared latrines for flood-affected IDPs, distribution of hygiene kits inclusive of HWTSS, organization of cleaning campaigns (Solid waste removal and disposal). Cesvi will coordinate and report to the Somalia Cash Working Group and its subnational WGs (specifically the SNCWG for Galmudug, covering Hiraan, Galgaduud, Mudug, and the SNCWG for Mogadishu, covering Lower Shabelle, Middle Shabelle, Banaadir), and with the WASH Cluster through the Cash 3W matrix. Cesvi has an extensive expertise in cash based interventions in Somalia, having received funds, since 2013, from DFiD, UK- Aid, SHF, DEVCO, AICS and WFP dedicated to cash based activities (UCTs, vouchers, Cash for Work). Cesvi has assisted more than 100,000 vulnerable persons, both in rural and urban contexts, with Cash Transfers during the various emergencies that hit Somalia after the 2011 famine. Cesvi is part of the Building Resilient Communities in Somalia (BRCiS) consortium, a well-known actor in cash based interventions. Key staff have been trained on Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) at different levels (internal level, consortium level or by the Cash Learning Partnership (CaLP).

Mitigation Measures

1) Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, donot participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or areexcluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities: MM1: Cesvi will ensure the community engagement of all groups during all phases of the project, trough participation and inclusion of all categories and layers of the population . An inclusive community and authority consultations will be carried out in order to identify vulnerable, under-represented HHs, with specific attention to age, gender, minorities, disability, and clan representation. Moreover, marginalized groups, PWDs, women, boys and girls will be by default among the prioritized groups. MM2: a clear strategy for reaching marginalized communities will be drafted. It will be based on Cesvi’s Country Strategy and will be tailored on the proposed intervention. MM3: The complaint and feedback mechanisms will be strengthened in order to identify any condition of exclusion or any issue encountered by the most vulnerable groups. Awareness-raising sessions on Cesvi CFM will be organized in order to reach every group in the targeted areas. Information will be communicated through various means to reach the broader community. 2) Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms: MM1. Cesvi will grant the maximum level of transparency with the affected populations by providing them with accessible and timely information on selection criteria for targeted assistance, organisational procedures and processes that affect them. MM2: a strong , confidential and well-understood Complain and Feedback Mechanisms is in place and the population is well aware of it. Awareness sessions on the CFM will be organized in order to reach every group in the targeted areas. MM3: within Cesvi’s CFM the complaints are welcomed and accepted. This information will be reinforced through awareness sessions. MM4: measures are implemented timely to build trust in the efficiency of the CFM. The complaints will be managed in a timely, fair, and appropriate manner that prioritizes the safety of the complainant and those affected at all stages. MM5: The complaints-handling process for communities and people affected by the crisis is always documented and in place and this will be the case also for the proposed intervention. The process always covers programming, sexual exploitation and abuse, and other abuses of power. 3) Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection ofbeneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunityconflict over access to resources: MM1: Cesvi will conduct a systematic, objective and updatedanalysis of the context and stakeholders so as to identify possible risks of interference. A continuous monitoring in the area will be necessary to adapt activities to new risks/exclusion. MM2: Cesvi will use inclusive needs-based targeting with joint, transparent verification processes. Cesvi will design and implement appropriate activities based on an impartial assessment of needs and risks, and an understanding of the vulnerabilities and capacities of different groups. MM3: Cesvi will pursue the maximum level of transparency with the affected populations by providing them with accessible and timely information on selection criteria for targeted assistance, organisational procedures, and processes that affect them. The beneficiaries list will be validated by the different stakeholders including beneficiaries themselves. MM4: A strong CFM is in place and the population can reach Cesvi any time to express their concerns/complaints. .

Please provide further explanation

Having considered that without access to basic services and livelihood opportunities, durable solutions cannot be achieved and recognizing that displacement is not simply a humanitarian issue but development and state-building challenge, Cesvi will implement a comprehensive strategy involving all key stakeholders creating synergies with other multi/integrated approach of wash, H&N, and food security activities in all target location with. The urgent life-savings needs of the affected communities and the livelihoods component will be addressed together with the long-term vision targeting the needs and vulnerabilities of IDPs and host communities simultaneously through a building resilience approach. This strategy together with a stable environment will allow displaced people to have the opportunity to opt for local integration solutions in the areas where they took refuge. Cesvi is already working with UN-Habitat and the BRA in Mogadishu to contributing to the Durable Solution framework in Banadir.
