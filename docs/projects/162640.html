Project Summary

The aim of the project is to respond to the life-saving needs and improve the resilience of affected populations by providing timely life-saving primary health care. This project is in line with Health Sector Objective: “Increase equitable access to humanitarian life-saving and life-sustaining health services for those most vulnerable and in need” and Objective 3: “Strengthen health system capacity to support continuity of care, strengthen community resilience, and enhance risk mitigation measures.”
IOM will focus on at least seven localities and this include: Mellit, Tawilla and Um Baru localities, North Darfur, El Radoom and Um Dafug localities in South Darfur, Ghubaysh locality in West Kordofan and Abyei PCA areas. High rates of displacement or return have been reported in these localities. The most vulnerable people, such as women of reproductive age, pregnant and lactating women (PLW) and children under-ﬁve (U5) living inside or outside of internally displaced persons (IDPs) camps as well as their host communities are at high risk of morbidity and mortality due to unavailability of sustained health services necessitating the need for timely life-saving health supports.
Under Objective 1, IOM will provide life sustaining health services for the most vulnerable people affected by conﬂicts, natural disaster and diseases outbreak by delivering a minimum basic package of primary health care services (BPPHS). The BPPHS will be provided through supporting existing health services facilities in the project target areas. The BPPHS will include: outpatient consultation of communicable and non- communicable diseases; ensuring the availability of essential medicine, supporting maternal and child health services, facilitated referrals and health promotion activities to ensure aid reaches people affected by crises such as conflicts, outbreaks of disease and natural disasters; and strengthening child vaccination services (the expanded programme of immunization (EPI)). IOM will also focus on enhancing early screening of malnutrition among U5 children and PLWs as well as supporting referral and follow-up treatments and management. These activities will be implemented in close coordination with other sectors such as nutrition and WASH as well as State Ministry of Health (SMOH). In addition, IOM will establish mobile health services to affected populations in areas if there are no health facilities to run the BPPHS.
IOM will promote the continuity and sustainability of the BPPHS by strengthening capacity of service providers. Hence, under Objective 3, IOM will focus on training of health care providers including community health care workers (CHWs) to build their capacity to provide essential and timely life- saving health services on prevention, enhancing risk mitigation means and close monitoring of outbreak disease trend. It will also promote the reduction and prevention of maternal and child mobility and mortality by focusing on the speciﬁc needs of U5 children and PLWs. IOM will encourage a direct participation and interaction of community members to tackle issues of malnutrition and negative health seeking behaviour and practices. As such, mothers and community caregivers will be trained on topics of the promotion of good IYFC practices and supporting community-based nutrition screening in strong coordination with the partners in nutrition sector. In addition, a minor rehabilitation and/or refurbishment of health and nutrition centers will be implemented to ensure the continuity of service as well as strengthening community resilience.
The project is in line with Sudan's Multi-Year Humanitarian Response Plan Outcome 1: “Populations affected by natural or man-made disasters receive timely assistance during and in the aftermath of the shock.” Outcome 2: "Displaced populations, refugees, returnees and host communities meet their basic needs and/or access essential basic services while increasing their self-reliance.”

1 - Needs

The health needs continue to be signiﬁcant, especially among the most vulnerable displaced populations, due to the ongoing economic crisis that has disrupted the ability of the public health sector to provide adequate healthcare. Increasing population movements within Sudan has contributed to the increase of disease outbreaks and food insecurity with a systemic lack of nutritional knowledge has further strained health capacity. Furthermore, 350,000 people were affected by the seasonal ﬂoods in 2019 with effects expecting to continue into 2020 as disease outbreaks associated with the ﬂooding, such as cholera, dengue, chikungunya, rift valley fevers and malaria, continue. The severity of these disease outbreaks was, in part, due to the limited access to WASH services, lack of vector control strategies, low coverage of vaccines and unavailability of essential medicines. The BPPHS in most Primary Health Care (PHC) facilities is very limited with only 30 percent of PHCs able to provide full package of BPPHS across Sudan. Some available PHC facilities are only partially functional and many are in remote areas and can only provide limited services due to lack of supplies and dilapidated infrastructure. Among crisis-affected populations, women and children are especially at risk of malnutrition, and gender-based violence (GBV) and high maternal mortality rates due to limited supports on maternal and child health. To improve delivery of health assistance and strengthen the overall health systems, resources need to be allocated to establish functional PHC facilities that offer BPPHS, referral systems to secondary and tertiary care facilities and support health worker training. Since 2014, IOM has been providing life-saving health and nutrition assistance to support affected communities in Darfur states, West Kordofan and Abyei PCA area. IOM interventions have contributed to enhancing access to life saving health services. However, further efforts are required for ensuring the continuity of services to strengthen community resilience. Indeed, the BPPHS in northern Abyei and West Kordofan state remain extremely limited. According to IOM’s ﬁled assessment, there are only four clinics in northern Abyei. Availability of essential medicine is the key issue in these areas. In South Darfur, urgent health interventions are needed, particularly for communities in two localities of Um Dafug and El Radoom where there have been high number of returnees or refugees from neighbouring countries. Between 2017 and 2019, IOM supported communities in Um Dafug town but the project interventions were limited to livelihood activities. According to IOM’s assessment conducted in January 2019, there was only one local clinic in Um Dafug town, which was partially destroyed due to a previous conﬂict and suffering from a shortage of health care staff, the minimum required medical equipment, safe water facilities and absence of reliable ambulance for referral services. In North Darfur, since 2018, IOM has been providing health and nutrition services to four localities, Um Baru, Tawilla, Mellit and El Fasher, and plans to continue its interventions in these localities as many of displaced are still in need of timely health service assistance. IOM will continue supporting the target localities to increase community resilience through increasing access to the BPPHS as well as strengthening capacity of health providers and communities. The proposed services will be delivered complementary with past and on- going health and nutrition interventions in North Darfur, West Kordofan and Abyei as well as other activities in basic services and livelihoods that are being implemented to support durable solutions under the context of UNDAF.

2 - Activities / Outputs

I. Main activities under Objective 1: 1. Conduct needs assessment per locality in coordination with SMOH and the sector leading agencies to assess needs and gaps: Community meetings per locality will be held and the management committees will be established. 2. Rehabilitation and/or refurbishment of selected PHC facilities and/ or set- up mobile clinics in areas where there is no health clinic – at least one clinics/mobile clinic per locality will be supported in coordination with the SMoH. Essential medicine and medical equipment will be provided as needed. 3. Provide a complete range of the BPPHS encompassing: a. General outpatient consultation; b. Child health, includingimmunization (routine and campaigns); c. Management of communicable diseases (such as influenza, malaria, Acute Water Diarrhoea (AWD)) and non-communicable diseases (such as diabetes, high blood pressure), including support to the prevailing disease surveillance, early warning and response system; d. Sexual and reproductive health, including maternal and new-born health, management of Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs); e. Management of mental health and trauma care; f. Environmental health, including waste management and vector control; and malnutrition screening (a massmid-upper arm circumference (MUAC) screening sessions) and support to management and treatment of malnutrition in nutrition sector. The project will facilitate referrals from community to primary, primary to secondary/tertiary emphasizing adequate continuity of care. IOM will support the SMoH and target clinics/mobile clinic for operational cost to deliver the BPPHS. II. Main activities under objective 3: 5. Organize and conduct capacity building activities: 1) Train/re-train medical and health care staff at the target clinics to enhance their skills and knowledge to provide a quality of the BPPHS. 2) Capacity building workshop for community health workers (CHWs) and the training will cover various topics on various topics on community based nutrition and health services such as MUAC screening. • This training will be designed as training of trainers (ToT) aimed at building their capacity to support community members such as mothers and community caregivers. • At least 10 peer learning group/mother to mother support groups per locality will be formed at the project catchment areas to work as key messenger and mentor to promote community health and nutrition status. • The trained CHWs will educate the group members on community based nutrition screening, the promotion of good IYCF and hygiene practice as part of education to prevent malnutrition and common diseases. III. Monitoring and evaluation: 1. Conduct a regular monitoring (monthly base) and post assessment. 2. Ensure a regular attendance in the sector meeting at ﬁeld and Khartoum: • IOM will share key outputs and challenges including lessons learnt and good practices to the sector leads, SMOH and other sector partners such as local Non-Governmental Organization (NGOs/ Civil Society Organizations (CSOs) for further programming.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project will employ a right based approach to promote the protection and human rights of the vulnerable displaced peoples such as IDPs, returnees and other affected communities. The project will pay a special attention to women of reproductive age, PLW, U5 children and persons with disabilities who do not have adequate access to essential health services and vulnerable to large scale of epidemiological event and disease outbreaks. The planned interventions will focus on the development of women’s capacities, strengthening women’s participation and direct interaction with communities as well as health providers and addressing negative health seeking behaviours to promote gender equality and reproductive health rights. Protection against gender-based violence such as harassment sexual exploitation and abuse, targeted women, girls and persons with disabilities will be strengthened.

7 - Accountability to Affected People and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse
7.1 - How will the project share information and collect community feedback and use it to improve your activities? Please select as many as relevant.

Face to face with individuals (in other venues/locations) through aid workers

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The project will embrace accountability to affected populations, including host communities in all processes of project design and implementation. Vulnerabilities will be taken into consideration starting from the first phase -immediate assessment of critical/humanitarian needs of affected migrants as well as local communities to the implementation of the interventions. Efforts will be made to engage in particular women, PLWs, children and youth. The project will empower these groups by encouraging direct participation in community- based health awareness activities. As part of capacity development, the peer learning groups/mother to mother support groups will be formed and trained as mentor and key messenger addressing negative traditional practice as well as protection threats such as harassment sexual exploitation and abuse, targeted women, girls and persons with disabilities. Accountability will also be promoted through the capacity development of CHWs and health providers as well as NGOs/CSOs in the health sectors as duty bearer to inform to the sector partners in health and protection if protection threats were reported. Applying IOM's experiences enhancing the protection of migrants through the provision of direct assistance services, IOM will provide direct assistance through psycho social support, counselling and medical assistance to victim in strong coordination with the sector partners as needed.
