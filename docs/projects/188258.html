Project Summary

Community-Based Mobile Teams Response aims to help maintain the women, men, children's right to survive and development in the Syrian conflict, by protecting children, women, and men from harm, abuse, neglect, exploitation and violence through strengthened community involvement. Violet will work through three project goals in 2 years:1) to increase the community-based interventions by 6 Volunteers and Protection Committees, 2) to increase access for 10,000 displaced children with their parents (women and men) living in camps for existing relief, protection services. 3) To support age-appropriate development of 20,000 internally displaced children, women, and men living camps by providing community-based psychological first aid and psychosocial support through the active participation of children and parents. Violet is establishing 3 mobile community-based protection units to reach the vulnerable children, women, and men at Idleb and Aleppo in NW Syria. These so-called "magic" caravans will be equipped with toys and materials for protection activities. Through these activities, the children and parents (women and men) will receive psychosocial support services. The plan activities are aimed to target children and parents: 1) who have lived in the camp, 2) who have just arrived, 3) who affected by floods, and 4) who at-risk or have any child protection and/or protection concerns, Moreover 5) children, parents, and community members who want to contribute in the project activities. The main problem in the targeted areas is not necessarily a lack of services, but more detailed knowledge of and access to them, there is a strong focus on local referral mechanisms. Violet expects to be able to refer 500 children and parents to other services through the referral mechanisms. Conduct structured PSS activities; Child Resilience and Parenting Skills for 500 children and 100 parents by 6 community committees and protection teams in the existing health facilities, RCs, camps, schools, and CFS in the targeted locations. Violet believe that positive defense mechanisms can be activated by providing more access to life-saving basic services for children in need and by encouraging positive self-development through protection activities; In the long run, the built capacity of those children and their parents will result in a collective resilience that supports not only the individual but also society as a whole in the humanitarian crisis in Syria.
Violet has previous experience and solid intervention through the targeted locations, using community acceptance of its health and emergency services. Violet is still providing many ongoing projects in both Idleb and Aleppo governorates, by multi-sectoral approach, through existing hospitals and PHCs, in addition to more than 70 schools and CFS, RCs and camps. Violet will build on its experience within the emergencies after clashes or floods, especially on its Ambulance and Transit units in Idleb and Aleppo.
All activities will design based on gender sensitivity, diversity and group ages, and provide the sessions by female staff. 9 - Protection Risk Analysis

1-Risk of intra-community tension due to service delivery locations or types, if one community is served and a neighboring community not served. Likelihood: Medium Impact: High. Mitigating: Ensure a comprehensive service mapping is undertaken and communication with communities can be proactive and informed. Ensure consultation with all communities related to the location, to understand perspectives of communities are all understood and addressed. Liaise with neutral parties as much as possible and triangulate all information from all sources prior to decision making. Established community-based groups/committees to enhance community-based protection mechanisms. Ensure information sharing with community members on services available.2-Different vulnerable groups might not have the same access to services. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigating: Follow existing Guidelines already available on PSS. Ensure consultation with communities that may be at particular risk of exposure and risk of exclusion. Select staff for especially outreach volunteers with adequate knowledge of the community, also respecting balance in gender, age, social profile.3- Girls and\or PwDs are not able to access to Caravan: Likelihood: Medium to high. Impact: High. Mitigation: Monitor the risks on the access within project. Divide the schedule by gender, provide the sessions by female, and arrange all rehabilitation needs for the Caravan. Design the activities appropriate to PwDs.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Violet targets PSEA risk through a) training on PSEA for staff, BNFs, vendors; b) integrating PSEA in AAP with safe and accessible complaints mechanism; c) appointment of PSEA focal points in each location to handle reports of misconduct, d) support to survivors through case management, e) regular information of affected community on their entitlements.Potential risks:1) Misconduct of staff against child safeguarding principlesMitigation: initial and refresher training on CSG responsibilities, behavior training for staff, assign focal point for CSG, child-friendly AAP sessions including CSG information and how to raise complaints, zero tolerance on misconducts and rapid action after investigation.2) SEA misconduct from project staff to parents or childrenMitigation: provide information sessions to parents (sex-segregated if preferred) on expected behavior from staff and humanitarian workers, explanation of complaints mechanism, availability of AAP staff face-to-face for sensitive complaints.3) Request of SEA for selecting BNFs to the servicesMitigation: clear information on the provided services in terms of quality and quantity and that nothing is expected in return in any case, emphasize on complaints mechanisms, provision of multiple channels to raise complaints, referral to inter-agency hotlines as alternative. Violet will conduct FGDs/KIIs to ensure participation of women and girls during all phases of the project, especially in design of the activities and monitor


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: Safety and security situation will getting worse. Risk: Limited access to target areas. Mitigation: Indiscriminate attacks on civilians and vehicles. Intensification of the conflict resulting in shifting frontlines, mass population displacements of the context. Direct communication with focal point and rapid evacuation as per Violet’s training with highest priority on safety of team and BNFs. Access via remote methods.Assumption: Increased in the number of displacement Risk: The current staff and activities will not be able to overstretched capacity. Mitigation:Readjustment of activities for a larger audience of BNFs. Allocation of additional volunteers to the activities. Using additional funding resources from the donor and other projects too.Assumption: The displacement movement is continuous to other locations. Risk: Inability to follow children’s cases Mitigation: Updated the displacement movements on daily basis. Close coordination with other organizations using referral mechanism. Assumption: The COVID-19 situation is still increasing Risk:Access loss to Caravan. Mitigation: Improve the hygiene and wash protocols and materials. Decrease the number of BNFs in the group sessions, and keep thesocial distance in both group and individual sessions. Resume the activities through online approach, with providing internet cards and use additional resources if needed by other projects, sectors, and donors. Keeping the donor updated with any additional fund required.
