Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims to cover the needs of different groups of population in healthcare services with accent on further sustainability of services. For this purpose, the project will combine:
• immediate coverage of urgent needs of affected population by work of mobile health care teams, provision of medicatons/payments via cash card for medical purposes:
• educational activities for target members of local communities on providing care for their relatives/close ones in need (elderly, disabled or heavily wounded during military conflict) and for other residents interesting in securing their heath;
• increasing the number of professional doctors in the affected regions by involving of doctors as private entrepreneurs (which is possible thanks to Healthcare reform in Ukraine)

Needs

Situation in Eastern Ukraine influenced by the military conflict remains complicated with 4.4 mln people affected, 3.4 mln in need (OCHA), 1 519 238 IDPs (Ministry of Social Policy). Access to quality essential health services is an immediate need for 2.2 mln More than 130 health facilities require rehabilitation, 66% in 5km reported damage during the crisis. (2018 Humanitarian Needs Overview). This means problems in access to medical services for up to 450 000 people from 20-km zone and IDPs, especially from regions with the highest level of concentration. Accessibility of health care services has the lowest level of satisfaction (60%, IOM, 2018). Among the most frequently mentioned coping strategies is ‘reducing essential health expenditures’ (25%), one third of the IDPs have not seen a doctor over a year (OCHA Humanitarian Bulletin, #27). 60% of IDPs and inhabitants of contact line are elderly. Vulnerability assessment (HelpAge International, 2018) showed: • elderly spend most of income on medicines (55.8%); • 97% have at least one chronic disease, 36.7% two or more;• 79% need medicines regularly;• 40% reported as the reason for non-taking medications their high cost;• 56.9% have limited mobility and partly in need of other’s help (75.9% women), 17.2% are immobile.Over 55,000 children still live within 15 km of the contact line (UNICEF, 2018) with limited access to healthcare. Among IDPs and contact line residents there is a high proportion of women, including those caring for children and elderly family members. The special aspect is prevalence of women-headed households. Caritas assessments showed gender-related specifics: women take more care of health with less serious illnesses, but men often have bigger need for hospitalization/rehabilitation but are not asking for medical assistance; older women left without support of younger family members are susceptible to health problems; women in couples are more likely than men take caregiver’s role even in need of assistance.Provision of health care is influenced by health reform with introduction of institute of family doctors. Many doctors from close to conflict areas relocated, access to medication is limited (pharmacies are mostly closed). The solution of the problem may be support of family doctors with individual license (not commercial medical practice but not affiliated with medical institutions doctors who directly signed agreement with National Health Service and receive budget subventions for every patient with whom sign declaration). Such doctor has the right to direct the patient to secondary care units, whose services will also be paid from the budget. They need to have required equipment and facilities, which are not always available to physicians who would like to work independently. But with appropriate support of internal funding it’s possible to cover the primary needs of such future family doctors as a future investment into sustainability of medical services. Currently there are several projects supporting IDPs and people in the areas close to the contact line with medical consultations and medications (including Caritas ones) but work of medical specialists there have no legislative background. Registering them as individuals who provide services of family doctors can help to make relations patient-doctor-healthcare system more stable/sustainable. With NGOs they are currently affiliated can overcome the obstacles of the initial period of work. Such family doctors can work directly in the settlements (where available) or in the form of mobile clinics. Crucial necessity is not to limit healthcare project only to people from contact line. Caritas assessment showed that IDPs spend half less for health needs as local population because lack of resources which means that access is still limited and need activities aim on improvement of health of this group.

Activities & outcomes

The proposed intervention aims to provide immediate healthcare assistance to the most vulnerable people from the area close to contact line and IDPs with further accent on the sustainability of action based on the health reform in Ukraine. Overall objective: introduction of the possibility of primary care provision and its further sustainability to cover the medical needs of particularly vulnerable groups affected by the conflict in Eastern Ukraine.Specific objectives:1. Medical and social assistance to targeted vulnerable beneficiaries is provided by mobile health care teams (HCT).2. Healthcare knowledge and skills of targeted groups of population improved as result of intensive educational and prevention activities.3. The number of professional medical specialists in the affected regions is increased by involving of family doctors with individual license who receive funding from state/local budget.Activities/outcomes:1.1. Direct healthcare services are provided by mobile HCTs to most vulnerable inhabitants of close to the line of conflict area (5km and 20 km) and IDPs (no more than 20% of beneficiaries) in the fixed centers. They include medical consulting, diagnostics, redirection to appropriate specialists, medication prescription. Mobile teams will provide registration of people during visits to settlements to evaluate vulnerability and needs. Specific vulnerable groups that have lack of movement ability will be considered at first. Each mobile HCT is composed of one medical specialist, social worker and nurse trained for provision of such kind of work. The team will visit each patient multiple times during the life of the project.1.2. Distribution of monthly payments via cash card/vouchers for medical purposes (prescription medicines, medical treatment, surgeries, diagnostics, medical tools and hygiene). A monthly amount of 1.000 UAH per person is based on Caritas experience (monthly amount in Home Care Program) and research (monitoring of the medical aspect of the Basic Needs project for the question "Does amount 1000UAH/month correspond your needs?" - 76% of beneficiaries have replied that it is sufficient). In case of necessity (for medical treatment) the entire amount can be transferred as one-time payment. Registration, evaluation of needs, selection and distribution will be provided by professional doctors (1 per each center, 5 total). 1.3. Provision of services for IDPs in specialized centers in Dnipro, Mariupol, Kramatorsk, Zaporizhzhia and Severodonetsk (activities 1.1, 1.2).2.1. As focus of the program undertakes support to people who have bad access or ability to reach life-saving medicine, the staff will also provide medical consultations for treatment, hygiene, healthy lifestyle, reproductive health (with special emphasis on adolescents). 2.2. Educational activities for residents of contact line by HTCs on specifics of home care for their relatives/close ones in need (elderly, disabled/heavily wounded during military conflict) to assure for them dignified life in a familiar environment.3.1. Selection and support of doctors ready to work as individual family doctors (both local based and from the members of HCTs) – consulting and legal support of the process of registration, licensing obtaining of necessary regulatory documents.3.2. Purchase of equipment for medical practice of selected doctors (or additional equipment as some centers are partly equipped) as MoH requires. Caritas Ukraine has experience in implementation of health care programs for IDPs and contact line residents inhabitants from 2014, already worked in more than 40 locations in the conflict line and have basic data regarding humanitarian and military situation there, contacts with local power authorities, representatives of international humanitarian organizations and local activists and volunteers. Caritas Ukraine has developed security strategy with determination of various possible levels of risk .
