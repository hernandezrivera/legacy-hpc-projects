Project Summary

PADO has proposed WASH assistance for 6624 returnee population in return areas of SWA to cope up with urgent WASH needs in communities and schools. Proposed sites will be finalized based on identified needs through involvement of Agency Planning Officer, PHED, education department, local communities and coordination with FATA Disaster Management Authority (FDMA) and WASH cluster to ensure maximum transparency and accountability. The proposed WASH interventions will focus on life-saving and dignified WASH support through provision of water supply and sanitation facilities along with hygiene promotion awareness in communities and schools of South Waziristan.

Needs

As per UNHCR (dated 31 October, 2018) all of 79,723 families (100% of total displacement) successfully returned to their area of origin in Tribal District South Waziristan when their area was de-notified by FATA Secretariat and after being cleared from the miscreants. The prolonged displacement of more than 6 years have left devastating effects on mental and physical health of returning families specifically women and children. According to Vulnerability Assessment led by OCHA and World bank, Access to safe water and sanitation (WASH) is limited, with severe implications for health, nutrition and well-being in newly merged tribal districts (formerly known as FATA). Limited electricity hampers the operation of existing water supply schemes, the rocky terrain makes hand-pumps generally ineffective and dispersed settlement patterns make it difficult to provide WASH services in a cost-effective manner. As a result, poor sanitation, a lack of water, low water quality and poor hygiene practices are widespread. 45% of the population in areas of return rely on unsafe drinking water. 36% of returnees have no toilets and practice open defecation. Two-thirds of the population do not have water facilities close to their homes, thus increasing safety concerns and the burden of domestic work on women and girls who are primarily responsible for fetching water. This has a detrimental effect on household hygiene, child care, women’s and children’s health and girls’ school enrollment. A lack of WASH facilities in schools also affects girls’ education, as do limited WASH services in health facilities. Water-borne diseases are prevalent, with diarrheoa reported by 33% of households. According to the Detail Needs Assessment (DNA), data analysis conducted by WASH cluster, the sources of drinking water available in South Waziristan are mainly open springs 41% and 31% open springs which is not potable at the point of collection. Only 7% use unprotected handpump. Similarly, the analysis further shows that 97% women girls jointly collect water from the sources. Water source distance from house to collection points are within 500 meters 44%, 500 meters to 1KM 11% and 1KM to 3KM 1%. Over 61% of the respondents shared that they do not have access to latrines at HH level. 97% of the 39% respondents who have access to latrines, constructed their latrines themselves. One latrines is shared by 2-4 families (96%), 5-7 families (2%) and more than 7 families (2%). Latrines are mainly used by adult women, children and elderly men and women. 61% who not have access to latrines mainly go to fields/bushes (90%) for open defecation. Major reason for not constructing latrines was that it is expensive and people are unable to afford (95%). 62% of the assessed returnees, throw solid waste/garbage anywhere as there are no designated places. However, 10% throw waste in garbage pit and 26% in designated public disposal area. Furthermore, Laundry Places are not available for 48% women in the HHs. 7% reported confirmed cases of Diarrheoa in the last 2 weeks with highest instances in children under 5 years of age followed by age bracket of 6-17 years and 18- 55 Years. Malaria is reported by 5% of the respondents with highest instances in 0-5 years (17%), 6-17 Years (67%) and 18-55 (17%). Higher rates can also relate to inappropriate household and environmental hygiene practices leading to lack of vector control. Culturally water carriage is mainly done by female who are the largest water carrier group. This social responsibility will overburden women and affect their overall health status as they have to cover long distances when carry heavy water containers every day. The situation is also expected to further expose young girls to several protection related risks and negatively impact enrollment of female students in schools because traditionally and socially parents are reluctant to send girls to schools if dignified WASH facilities in schools are not functional.
