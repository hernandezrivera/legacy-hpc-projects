1 - Needs

The protracted humanitarian crisis in Sudan continues to impact the already fragile health system and reducing the capacity to provide basic health services and respond to the multiple emergencies affecting the country. The annual cycles of floods, disease outbreaks, civil unrest, border conflicts, and ongoing economic crisis have further impacted the accessibility to health services. The decline in service provision by the public sector is forcing the population to seek health services at the private sector that is dominating the scene with 69.3 per cent of current health expenditure (National health recovery and reform policy and strategic plan NHRRP 2020-2022). According to the 2021 multi-sectorial needs assessment MSNA 72.3 per cent of the population had to pay in full for their health services despite a reported coverage by the national health insurance fund of 67 per cent. During 2021 the availability of emergency medicines declined steadily to reach 43 per cent compared to 57 per cent during 2020. http://www.nmsf.gov.sd/wafra .the Project was targeted 4 state in sudan Blue Nile ,south Darfur , South Kurdufan and East Darfur targeting 300,055 affected people .

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

World Vision will ensure that the project promotes safety and dignity of beneficiaries, particularly vulnerable groups such as women and girls. Responsibility of child care, and maternity in the household is primarily done by women (mothers and grandmothers). The project will ensure that women have direct access to information services delivery at convenient hours with the targeted clinic open from morning to afternoon World Vision will also take deliberate steps to seek the view of women and girls on the suitability of services and act on the feedback to ensure their needs are met Households with people living with disabilities whenever identified, will be supported through the community and CHWs to access services and information. 7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Complaints and feedback mechanism World Vision will encourage beneficiaries to provide feedback and complaints throughout the implementation of the project. At one selected WV supported clinics site, WV will attempt to install suggestion boxes and community members sensitized on how to use them for complaints and feedback. The feedback provided will be handle confidentially as per WV Program accountability Framework. World Vision will maintain an “open door” policy whereby the community members will also be able to present complaints related to project at the World Vision offices in Damazin Daien ,Khadugli and Nyala. Community members will be encouraged to present their complaints and feedback, through writing letters addressed to Program manager or make phone calls to any of the project staff. Upon receiving complaints, World Vision will review the complaint, take appropriate action if necessary and provide feedback to the beneficiary.
