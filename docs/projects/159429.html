Project Summary

This project will be targeting the most acutely vulnerable children in specific area C locations of Tubas and Qalqilia in addition to Hebron H2 area. Those children along with their parents have little access to basic needs particularly education. The above target children have been imposed to certain violations and restrictions that hindered the primary needs to be directly delivered by the Palestinian National Authority according to Oslo Agreement terms.
In line with the 2019 Education Cluster Specific Objective II, which is focusing on most vulnerable schools to be supported in responding to emergencies through preventive measures, catch-up classes and school-based psycho social services, and also in accordance with the Strategic Response Plan Objective II:" The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL", AFKAR will be closely working with the MoEHE General Directorate of Counseling on delivering its activities by setting up a joint committee consisted of MoEHE, AFKAR and HWC people for effectively and efficiently assuring the best delivery of services in areas targeted. AFKAR will be relying on its strong renewed partnership since 2009 with the MOEHE and also with the HWC's collaboration in executing this project, AFKAR will be leading activities for those schools in providing school-based support to those vulnerable school children and staff on one hand and for most severe cases, HWC will be taking the lead to address who have been exposed to attacks and badly affected these cases at their health centers.
Schools of those areas are in dire need of psycho social support services as the pervasive and coercive environment of violence is an all too common reality there where children are attacked by either the Israeli soldiers or settlers. That type of need is decisive to the quality inclusive education where female and male children will get resilient through such activities.
Thus, this project will be improving the psycho social well being of children through two levels of intervention by the two organizations. The project will be targeting 10 schools of the three areas: 6 schools in Hebron H2 area, 2 schools in Tubas and 2 in Qalqilia province. According to MoEHE classification of schools, those schools are highly classified as the most high tense spots.
As per the renewed Partnership Principles between AFKAR and MOE, MOHE, MOF, and LACS for the upcoming three years signed in July, 2019, AFKAR has an access to work in all areas targeted as well as in any location in the West Bank including Jerusalem through a focal point in each directorate. Besides, HWC has its strong record in providing its quality intervention all areas particularly in Hebron H2, Tubas and Qalqilia. This would give a strong credit to the implementation of the activities.
AFKAR will be working with female and male children from different classes offering them with warm up and breaking ice activities through various activities including facial paintings, dancing and singing. Round table discussions will be executed for older students to be able to diagnose the situation and provide alternatives for their reality. Such activities would improve their psyche once they talk about their situations and providing alternatives. Teachers, counselors and PTA of the schools will be involved in all cycles of the project. This will make the schools a supporting and appealing environment for bettering the situation. Therefore, teachers will be given training, PTA members will also be trained to cope with cases at home in afternoon sessions at HWC centers in the three areas. The idea beyond the first level will be enhanced as AFKAR and HWC will target the most severe cases who face nightmares and negative stress and share regular reports to the MOE.

1 - Needs

Due to the protracted and military Israeli occupation, the occupied Palestinian Territories are still fragmented into 3 major areas , over two thirds of which classified as Area C are fully controlled by Israeli military occupation. This situation has created a de facto situation by which the National Authority has little intervention on those areas in addition to Jerusalem and H2 Hebron locations. A profound pervasive and coercive environment of violence has become an all too common reality particularly in the above mentioned locations. According to the UN CAAC Bulletin, 2017, 290 education related violations in the WB affected 47,924 boys and girls which means an increase of 34 incidents in 2016. Most of these attacks were taken place in the three major areas mentioned above. This spate of Israeli attacks whether by settlers or/ and Israeli soldiers, has had more students and teachers on a heightened pulse. In preparing for this project, gender and age related data is assessed to best result in program impact for boys and girls. The violations in Hebron H2 area are predominantly attributed to the closure obstacles and check points installed since 2015. Besides, the withdrawal of international presence has worsened the situation in that area. This new situation would likely increase the tension and potential to more threats to children primarily female while commuting in and out. About 37% of the boys and girls are limited in their access to education and suffer from the lack of a quality conducive learning environment. The recently constructed fence in Hebron H2 now cutting off over 1800 residents from the most basic needs including education and health further increases the psychological stress on children. In Hebron and due to security measures, students have been exposed to attacks and the image of Israeli and settlers presence impose a sense of insecurity and anxiety which will profoundly affect their psyche. Girls have been facing a high risk of drop out because of social variables fearing for their safety during the school commute. Accordingly, and for filling the gap and completing the role with the Ministry of Education to this end, AFKAR will be selecting 10 equally female and male schools, which are classified according to the MoEHE standards of highly exposed to double attacks . Six of them will be in Hebron H2 area where the other 4 will be distributed in most hot Area C regions of Tubas and Qalqilia where AFKAR has an access and past experience in carrying out activities in schools. These locations have also health centers for our partner HWC which will be their destination when executing after school activities for PTA and easy to reach targeted schools for further support for health cases.

2 - Activities

Output I Students, teachers and PTA psychological condition is improved. Activities: Activity I.I Provision of school based psychosocial support to vulnerable school teachers and counselors. Description: Training 40 female and male teachers in addition to counselors on stress relief techniques to deal with their own stress as well as techniques to help their students using chartered manuals. This training will be ensuring the sustainability of the project objectives wither in their schools or others. Activity I.2 Provision students with warming up activities. Description: AFKAR will be offering such activity for 900 children to help them get involved and break the ice. These activities will include dancing, facial paintings, singing, etc for targeted 10 schools classes (4 + 5 + 6)= 900 female and male children. An expert from our partner (HWC) in addition to the school counselor if present, will be there to figure out the cases who need further support for the next phase. Activity 1.3 Provision of round table open sessions and debate style activities at schools for graders (7+8+9+10) = 1200 students. Description This will be conducted in the form of debate and round table discussions to give opportunities for them to express themselves freely and critically and thus offer alternatives for better outcome. Issues will be related to human rights, gender role and other cross cutting issues they see as their priorities to discuss over. An expert from our partner (HWC) in addition to the school counselor if present, will be there to figure out the cases who need further support for the next phase. Activity 1.4 Carrying out 24 awareness sessions for 120 PTA members. Description AFKAR and HWC will be working together to carry out 24 sessions for 120 PTA members at HWC's centers in the three regions targeted. These sessions will be on PSS and identifying signs of stress and referral mechanism. Parents will help the team to discover certain cases who suffer from traumatic situations and thus refer them to further support. Activity 1.5 Providing further health support particularly those with severe cases to HWC health centers. Description After having diagnosed of the most needed cases for further psychological health support, all cases would be referred to HWC centers to start mitigating the effects of traumatic effects upon them through a number of follow ups based on cases. Activity 1.6 Execution 5 two day summer camps for selected students Description 5 two- day summer camps will be carried out in the three major locations targeted. All activities will be on protective and psycho social support. Activities will include interactive drama where children are exposed to 10 minute sketch and then they are encouraged to criticize the sketches and later give alternative solutions via acting the roles. Output II 10 schools targeted are equipped with psycho social tools required to PSS activities. Activity 2.1 10 tool boxes will be distributed to help execute extracurricular activities. these tools and instruments include sports tools and drama tools, speakers, stationary and art equipment. Activity 2.2 Equipping 10 schools targeted with LCD to help demonstrate manuals, documents and any other necessary pictographs and contents to help ease the stress and enable people in need of such tools for learning.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

Feedback and complaints from the beneficiaries will be collected via field visits mechanism. A generic email would be set up to be a free platform to give feedback, complaints and suggestions for improvement of performance. They will be gathered directly after site visits and be added to posts on the generic email through our monitoring and evaluation staff. The team will be in charge of visiting the ten locations in area C Tubas and Qalqilia and Heron H2 area. Satisfaction facilitations are provided to the beneficiaries to come up with both feedback to improve our intervention from one hand and to collect all their complaints to directly call for a meeting with the Joint Committee (JC) (AFKAR, HWC & MOE representatives) which is responsible for the whole process of the project cycle. After that, the JC calls for a meeting with officials in MOE to discuss all the complaints and feedback and request for positive response on issues raised.AFKAR has strong record in reporting some complaints via the Joint Committee. AFKAR has got the buy-in from the MOE via its strong record of intervention. This helped us in affecting the decisions makers in the MOE and were able to modify certain strategies and policies. The latest success story was about a teacher in Hebron (Ms. Zeinab Abu Hadid- Muhammad Ali Al-Muhtasib School). In June, she moved to Widad Nasser Addeen Female Secondary School where AFKAR is not targeting. She did a complaint to include her new school to be one of 2019/2020 project locations. The MOE and AFKAR have agreed although this is against MOE procedures in targeting more than six schools in one directorate. AFKAR was able to convince the MOE representative to accept her request even though it will be adding more financial burden on AFKAR.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

This project aims at providing humanitarian response with insight into the Do No Harm principle. AFKAR denounces all types of discrimination including gender, religious, political and socioeconomic background. DNH actually guides our intervention and keeps us aware of avoiding target groups to additional risks through looking at the broader context and mitigate potential effects on the social fabric. When designing the project, AFKAR places some emphasis on design and management methodologies in an endeavor to allow a better understanding of the negative effects of the projects on groups of children and with those with added difficulties and special needs. Thus, safeguarding of children is AFKAR's primary responsibility. Every member of AFKAR staff has been trained to take this task as a fundamental mission when carrying out any activity. AFKAR's staff were capacitated to prioritize safety, dignity and avoid doing harm through carrying out certain tasks:o An open discussion should be opened with all children, female and male separately to focus on threats facing them inside the school and while they commute to school. The locations targeted are classified hot spots and very risky and highly potential to Israeli settlers and soldiers' threats such as harassments for female children and teachers (GBV), violence for male children and teachers and humiliation. During discussion, AFKAR should be fully aware of children involvement in discussing the threats and providing solutions or alternatives to avoid or reduce such types of threats whatsoever. AFKAR is fully aware of giving the opportunity to target groups to frame the meanings of the project in all phases to foster the sense of ownership and be highly engaged in accomplishment for better efficiency and effectiveness.o AFKAR's staff should be highly aware of keeping children's voices accounted and not being treated as subjects or passive recipients.o AFKAR should work with all people concerned such as children, teachers, principals and parents to support equal and safe access to education and training and activities opportunities with no discrimination of any kind. AFKAR should highlight on exclusion of girls and children with disabilities or those who live in impoverished and remote areas. o When training teachers, and parents AFKAR dedicates some time to give an introduction of the Child rights and different types of abuse as an attempt to better understanding of child protection during their work and at home.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project has major components by which equal and impartial access to assistance services are guaranteed and all types of vulnerable groups including female and people with specific needs are targeted:I. Inclusion: AFKAR has been focusing lately on ensuring the inclusion of all types of different groups including girls, women, people with disability and people from impoverished and disadvantaged locations. They should be engaged in all phases of programming whether the programs are developmental or humanitarian. Their voices should be accounted making sure that their concerns and needs are heard. Accordingly, open discussions and self expressions are highly encouraged. After such discussions, the team have to assess all their needs and the education priorities. Even the learning materials should be identified to meet all types of targeted groups; whether they are relevant to emergency response plan with special focus on psychosocial support making sure that no one is behind.While identifying the risk assessment, disability, and gender perspective are on the core of analysis through identification of barriers facing those vulnerable people. AFKAR will be offering tools to ensure such enabling into the processes of the project. In addressing risk assessment from the very beginning would help us identify barriers. Once recognizing the risks, we would assure the protection mainstreaming perspective. This analysis leads to provide the general analytical framework for informing programming decision, including how to operationalize protection mainstreaming decisions. Knowing the risks faced by vulnerable people provides the basis for addressing them. AFKAR will include in its process an appropriate mix of desk review of existing sources of information mainly by the MOE and other international agencies and site visits discussions.II. Collection Data: AFKAR will be collecting and monitoring data on project indicators which include numbers of individuals accessing education services with disaggregation by age, gender and people with disabilities. This would help specify the barriers and then provide enabler to address such barriers. In collecting the data, AFKAR will be using the most appropriate tool for disaggregating program data by disability in humanitarian crisis which is the Washington Group Short Set of Questions. III. Children and Parents Engagement: AFKAR will be encouraging female and male children and parents in engaging in all suggested activities and bodies particularly Student Councils and different clubs e.g. Debate Clubs and Parent- Teacher associations. This would likely enhance and empower them. This exposure would make response more pertinent to all needs of target groups. Parents would also be involved in education matters and close to children needs and threats. They would be capacitated to make response more participatory via their engagement. Other democratic values will also be instilled while leading the PTA and all segments of community are guaranteed to be involved
