Project Summary

The FSL II programme is a proposed action submitted to the United States Agency for International Development (USAID)’s Food for Peace (FFP) for funding (pending donor approval) and is a continuation of the ongoing FSL I programme (ending 31 March 2019) funded by FFP.
FSL II is scheduled to start on 1 April 2019 and will be implemented for a 12-month period. This submission covers the period of 1 April to 31 December 2019.
FSL II aims to contribute to increased food security for vulnerable displaced and host community households in Idleb and Aleppo Governorates. Like the ongoing FSL I Programme, FSL II will comprise of two components: Household Food Security and Bakery Support.
The Household Food Security component targets an average of 250,000 food-insecure individuals per month with in-kind food assistance (food kits) and cash modality (food restricted vouchers). FSL II targets to benefit 29,500 HHs with repeated assistance and 12,500 HHs with one-time emergency assistance.
The Bakery Support component provides an approximate 600,000 individuals living in supported bakeries’ catchment area with bread at a reduced and stabilised price. This is achieved through subsiding up to 50% input (flour and yeast) of bread production thus supporting local bakeries to produce a stable quantity of bread that meets local needs. This support is conditioned upon the selling of this bread in the local markets at a reduced and stabilised price agreed with and monitored by NGO X’s Cash and Markets, and Monitoring Teams. Approximately 18 bakeries are targeted to benefit from this assistance in FSL II.
This project is implemented in partnership with several other INGOs whose names have been concealed for security reasons. One INGO (whose name has been provided via email to the FSL Cluster Coordination Team) will submit their planned activities under this project separately based on a prior agreement between the two agencies. The two agencies have coordinated carefully the programme targets and figures to avoid duplication.
This funding has not yet been secured. FFP approval pending.

Needs

Of the overall 13.1 million people in need in Syria, 10.5 million are food insecure, and 6.5 million, or 54% of the population, are facing acute food insecurity. This is in large part due to the depletion of assets with 9.8 million people being in need of livelihood support, while the average price of the food basket is now 800% higher to pre-crisis levels. According to the data from the mobile Vulnerability Analysis and Mapping report, one in four of the surveyed Syrian households (HH) had inadequate food consumption, reflecting a diet of low or medium diversity – mainly consisting of bread, rice, sugar and oil. The same survey found that the most affected by food insecurity include displaced HH, HH headed by women, large HH and HH living in rented accommodation. Idleb, Aleppo and Hama Governorates continues to host the second largest number of displaced persons in Syria (c. 1.2m IDPs of a population of 2.45m ). Approximately 56% of communities surveyed by REACH in May 2018 reported that humanitarian assistance was the main source of food for them. In 63% of surveyed communities in August 2018, REACH found that some HH reported resorting to negative coping strategies to meet their food needs in the previous month. Aleppo has the highest food insecure population, followed by Rural Damascus and Idleb. The Food Security Sector estimates that Idleb, hosting over two million people, has nearly 65% of its people in need. Of them, 35% are acutely food insecure and 30% are at risk of food insecurity (HNO 2018). One of the main drivers of food insecurity in Idleb remains the high concentration of IDPs (44%) and their increasing vulnerability. At household level, the Food Consumption Score (FCS) has shown worrisome trends in the last years, with the highest number of households categorized as having poor food consumption being in Rural Damascus (50.8%), Homs (24.8%), Idleb (21.6%) and Aleppo (20.0%). The Household Dietary Diversity Score (HDDS) has also shown worrying trends, with significant decrease among the general population compared to the previous years. Idleb scored medium and poor HDDS among 55% of its population, while Aleppo has 58.5% of its population scoring medium to low HDDS, with higher percentages among IDPs. These results, in turn, lead to the population altering their food consumption by using various strategies to adapt to reduced access to food. The food consumption-based reduced Coping Strategies Index (rCSI) has been found at 9 in Idleb and 11 in Aleppo with Hama scoring the highest rCSI (26) among all governorates. The most frequently cited coping strategies are: relying on less preferred or less expensive food; borrowing food or relying on help from relatives; reducing the number or meals per day; reducing portion sizes; and reducing consumption by adults in order for small children to eat.

Implementation of activities

In this proposed action, NGO X and selected partners will provide approximately 10,000 households (HHs) with 41,000 food kits, and restricted food vouchers to a further 18,500 HHs, in #10 distribution rounds in 2019. Following on from the pause in the Bakery Programme between December 2017 to April 2018, a thorough review of the bakery selection process was carried out, including an analysis of the challenges in retaining humanitarian independence in selecting bakeries, and a revision of its related Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs), as well as security situation considerations. Due to the reduced budget in 2019, NGO X aims to reduce its allocation to 1,500 MT and its bakeries to 24 Bakeries. This reduced number will also enable NGO X to provide greater oversight and monitoring of the bakery programme in line with the revised SoPs. NGO X will provide target bakeries with up to 50% of flour and yeast inputs enabling more than 300,000 people to access bread at a reduced and stabilised price. Part of the emergency response is to cover food needs for internal displaced families and NGO X is planning to reach 8,500 HHs with 31,500 food kits and 15,000 HHs with 30,000 RTE kits based on their needs and reported situation. This designed to be on off distribution. However along with the one-off assistance, NGO X will implement short term food assistance (repeated for 3 months for displaced HHs) for 2,000 HHs. The operating environment within NGO X’s Area of Operation (AoO) has grown increasingly complex from both a security and political standpoint. There are signs that the situation in areas currently under the control of opposition armed groups, primarily the Hayat Tahrir Al-Sham (HTS), in Idleb and surrounding areas may be rapidly deteriorating, with the build-up of Government of Syria (GoS) forces around Idleb a serious concern for the people of Idleb and the humanitarian community. At the time of writing, a ceasefire agreed between GoS and the military factions in the south pave the way to a new wave of displacement northwards. NGO X has developed a series of contingency plans based on several scenarios to ensure the organisation remains agile and responsive to population movement. The inclusion of Al Bab and Azaz districts in North Aleppo as new areas in this action is reflective of NGO X’s contingency planning, and NGO X is currently engaging with authorities in Turkey to secure approval to enter these areas – recent engagement with the authorities has been positive and NGO X is optimistic that it will gain access to Azaz shortly to carry out assessments. The humanitarian response has been further complicated by the establishment of the Syria Salvation Government (SSG) and their attempts to control civic administrations. In terms of food security, thus far, the most notable effects of this have been the establishment of the Ministry of Economy (MoE) and its subsidiary for wheat systems management, the Mills Foundation, who have sought control over the supply chain of flour and bakeries and are attempting to dictate which bakeries they will permit organisations to support. NGO X is committed to adhering to humanitarian principles in providing aid to affected populations and will maintain strong lines of communication with relevant key actors on the ground to ensure that all the food security, emergency and WASH programme guidelines, SoPs, in line with donor requirements, are followed. NGO X has strong mitigation measures in place to avoid any aid diversion or inference from armed actors, including HTS.

Protection Risk Analysis

NGO X conducted a rapid needs assessment in Idleb and Aleppo between October and December 2018. This assessment included aspects related to livelihoods, shelter, NFI and protection. This sector is very difficult to assess, with respondents often shying away from providing straight and honest answers. Yet, 17% of NGO X surveyed HHs in Idleb reported having witnessed or heard of cases of abuse or other protection concerns, with all reporting feeling comfortable to refer such cases to community services they were aware of. Surprisingly, only a minority of HH reported women and children (including with disability) facing any protection risks while accessing aid. However, those responding positively to these questions mentioned the lack of ID documentation, risk of forcibly removing aid, harassment and verbal abuse. While armed recruitment is an issue recognised by most beneficiaries, it was only cited in IDP camps in North Aleppo as a protection risk. Child marriage, albeit rare, continues to be a coping mechanism employed by some families. Gender-based violence, often hidden, remains another issue which, even in small incidence, must be taken seriously in aid programming. Other INGO and donors (including ECHO) have consistently reported weapon contamination as a major and real protection risk to affected communities. Following findings from NGO X’s Vulnerability Assessment carried out in mid-2018 and other routine needs assessments, monitoring and evaluation activities, NGO X have revised the organisation’s Food Distributions Standard Operating Procedures to include measures for households with restricted mobility or enhanced vulnerability / protection risk. Such measures include door-to-door distribution measures (‘home deliveries’) in addition to other measures taken by NGO X’s distribution, monitoring and safeguarding teams. Other measures are advised by NGO X’s in-country and Turkey-based security teams who thoroughly and regularly monitor the security situation in NGO X’s operational areas. Distribution teams also take caution in deciding on the place and times of distribution taking into account security teams advice and inform beneficiaries on these adjustments. Social Workers recruited by NGO X accompany distribution and monitoring team to identify vulnerable households that need assistance beyond NGO X’s mandate or capacity and refer them to community services in line with the Protection Cluster referral and case management guidance. For this purpose, a service mapping exercise has been carried out by NGO X with support from the Protection Cluster to inform referral pathways. A Special Needs Fund is being currently established with a view to provide additional material or service / referral assistance to the most vulnerable households. NGO X’s Safeguarding Reporting Framework is now in place with trained Safeguarding Focal Points being in place and ready to identify or record reports on any protection concerns against NGO X beneficiaries committed by NGO X or partner staff or other programme stakeholders. These cases are recorded and investigated with due seriousness and the investigation outcomes are reported to NGO X management who then decides on the appropriate actions.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: - Continued access to targeted areas and vulnerable populations remains permissive, supported by continued adherence to NGO X's acceptance strategy and the JOPs; - Security conditions remain sufficiently stable; - Sufficient and timely access to required inputs through procurement, reliable supply and available transportation continues to be achieved; Border crossing remains open;- Food price inflation does not outpace overall inflation and household economic gains; - Food inflation and currency exchange rates remain relatively stable and predictable;- Cash transfer channels remain open and sufficient to handle the volume of payments; - Local markets continue to function with an adequate supply and diversity of food items. Risks:1. Access restrictions to geo area due to active fighting and/or shifting of frontlines would prevent the humanitarian actors to provide timely emergency relief to the affected population.2. Closure of the border for commercial shipments or not renewal of SC would have a very heavy negative impact on the cross-border activities for both NGOs and UN agencies as it would prevent the transhipment of the NFIs across the border from Turkey into Syria.3. Tension between host community and IDPs due to lack of context/needs understanding or poor communication and/or tension between included and excluded IDPs might lead to conflict between target groups and complaints against the humanitarian organisations. Mitigation:NGO X will apply a conflict-sensitive approach by means of the following: 1. Context analysis of all programme delivery locations is updated regularly to ensure that:• NGO X is aware of all possible demographic, ethnic, religious/sectarian and governance changes in the area. With such awareness, NGO X will adapt the programme in accordance with the new changing environment in order to prevent the perception that NGO X provides aid to only one group/family/tribe or any other discriminatory level. • NGO X keeps constantly updated on any new actors or stakeholders which may have an impact on the conflict or the context in the programme area, and NGO X assesses the positive or negative connections between new actors and existing stakeholders. Staff will be informed about NGO X’s communication strategy with new stakeholders and how NGO X’s implicit message should be transmitted to eliminate the risk that NGO X legitimises any one actor or group in conflict.2. NGO X will diligently assess all collaborative actors to ensure that any possible procurement procedure which is conducted in the programme area will not empower any one group/actors/family and cause a negative impact on the conflict. 3. Field staff both in Syria and Turkey will be trained on Conflict Sensitivity and they will be able to approach their programming in conflict sensitive way. 4.
