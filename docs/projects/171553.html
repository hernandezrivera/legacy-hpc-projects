Project Summary

WASH Cluster has 47 WASH partners including 23 INGOs, 13 LNGOs, 4 UN agencies, 3 Educational institutions, 4 local authorities. All partners operate at different levels in the WASH sector and many of them are contributing to several humanitarian planning initiatives such as HRP, COVID-19 response plan and WASH contingency plans.
UNICEF aims to ensure the maximum operational integration and technical harmonization among these partners, which will be facilitated through the WASH Cluster. It also aims to enhance the WASH coordination system, mechanisms, and tools to ensure that 1) the WASH humanitarian needs in Palestine are identified, 2) all the WASH partners develop their response projects to address these needs, and 3) all the proposed WASH responses satisfy the national and international standards and match national priorities.
Through this project, UNICEF as Cluster Lead Agency is proposing to implement several interventions that aim to increase the efficiency and effectiveness of the WASH humanitarian project cycle in Palestine: 1) needs and technical assessments to identify the most vulnerable groups with limited access to basic WASH services; 2) capacity building programs for WASH Partners to improve partners capabilities to address the identified WASH needs; 3) developing WASH coordination mechanisms and tools to ensure efficient communication between the Cluster partners and humanitarian country team; and 4) leading and coordinating the development of national and sub-national humanitarian response and contingency plans.
UNICEF will allocate three specialized staff members to handle all the tasks mentioned above on national and sub-national levels; these staff members will work directly with all WASH Cluster partners to ensure quality coordination of WASH interventions in Palestine, providing them with technical support.
The WASH Cluster team will also carry out tasks related to monitoring, evaluating and reporting of Cluster partners planned, ongoing and implemented activities. It will also ensure the maximum accountability of WASH Cluster partners to the beneficiaries, local authorities, and humanitarian country team. Moreover, the Cluster will support and encourage its partners to mainstream protection, gender, and socioeconomic vulnerabilities in their plans and responses to ensure the appropriateness of the assistance provided for all the vulnerable groups.
Moreover, the WASH Cluster will track and monitor the application of the relevant age, gender and diversity mainstreaming in partners WASH plans and responses during 2021, and ensure that all the technical guidance, proposed and implemented WASH interventions satisfy the special needs of these vulnerable groups.

1 - Needs

According to the 2021 HNO, more than 1.6 million Palestinians (950,000 in Gaza, and 650,000 in the West Bank) suffer from limited access to WASH services in the West Bank and Gaza. Their access to water, sanitation, solid waste services and flood mitigation measures affects their quality of life and exposes them to several risks, most importantly health and protection risks. In Gaza Strip, the continued closure enforced by the Israelis has negatively impacted the WASH sector, the restricted access of fuel and WASH infrastructure operation and maintenance materials and tools has reduced the capacity of 25 WASH service providers. As a result, 500 WASH facilities were affected and are no longer functional. In the West Bank, the Israeli restrictions in Area C, Hebron H2 and East Jerusalem affect access to adequate WASH services for 574 communities. The Israeli planning and building policy in Area C prevents Palestinian communities from developing their WASH infrastructures; during 2020, 33 demolition incidents against WASH infrastructures and assets were recorded.Poverty, unemployment, and the national economic deterioration pose a threat to these vulnerable groups and will eventually affect the ability to properly apply WASH measures and practices. The low-income families in West Bank and Gaza are already adopting negative coping mechanisms to survive, such as reducing their water consumption, using an unreliable and low-quality water resources and dumping the wastewater in open areas and agricultural lands.Moreover, during 2020 the situation was further exacerbated due to the outbreak of COVID-19. According to WHO, the provision of safe water, sanitation and waste management is essential for preventing infectious disease outbreaks and protecting human health. Many households and institutions in Palestine have limited access to water and sanitation services and are not able to apply these basic and crucial recommended measures.Under the aforementioned circumstances, many national, international and UN agencies along with the local authorities are intervening to support these communities to improve their access to WASH basic services. According to its mandate, each agency and local authority deal with the WASH vulnerability based on their technical and operational capacities. UNICEF is taking the lead to coordinate all the WASH humanitarian responses being provided by different agencies. According to 2020 records, many of the cluster partners participate directly in developing and implementing the country's humanitarian response plans, including HRP, inter-agency response plans, and contingency plans. During 2020, 24 WASH Cluster partners participated in developing and implementing the COVID-19 response plan, and 19 partners participated in the 2020 HRP. UNICEF, through the WASH Cluster, is taking a leading role in coordinating partners' participation in these plans and providing the required support to ensure the maximum integration and harmonization among its partners. Moreover, the WASH Cluster coordinates with its partners and the local authorities to ensure its partners' operation and technical harmonization. The Cluster also supports the partners in identifying the most vulnerable groups, prioritizing the targets, unifying the related technical standards, developing standard operating procedures (SOPs) and monitoring and tracking partners’ interventions. In order to utilize the partners’ full capacities, the Cluster is supporting its partners to develop their technical and operational capabilities to increase the efficiency of their responses. The WASH cluster has also provided age, gender and disability (AGD) disaggregation of peple in need in the WASH sector for its partners (Female: 797,390, Male: 821,499, Girls: 399,421, Boys: 418,281, People with Disability: 35,546), in addition to technical guidance on targeting with relevant AGD disaggregation in the West Bank and Gaza, so that partners are able to provide the required AGD analysis for their proposed interventions. Moreover, the WASH Cluster has allocated two gender focal points to support partners in the AGD mainstreaming during the planning and implementation of their WASH projects.

2 - Activities

During 2021, the WASH Cluster will operate based on its 6+1 core functions and will continue its effective engagement in the country inter-cluster platforms and coordination mechanisms such as national and sub-national Inter Cluster coordination Groups (ICCGs) and inter-agency assessments and plans. Moreover, the Cluster will adopt different vulnerability identification, and response approaches, including emergency development nexus, linking relief, recovery, and development (LRRD), right, and needs-based approaches. The Cluster will work directly with relevant local authorities to ensure that all the identified needs and proposed responses match the local authorities' strategies and priorities. Moreover, the Cluster will highlight the humanitarian WASH vulnerabilities and its consequences to the authorities to ensure the inclusion of these vulnerabilities and needs in the governmental strategies and agendas. The cluster will also develop an integrated coordination response approach to ensure that all WASH vulnerability sources are identified. In addition, the related WASH response plans are being developed and implemented according to high standards, and all WASH Cluster partners have the adequate capacities to design and implement the appropriate response that satisfies the most vulnerable groups. Moreover, the Cluster will contribute to implementing the WASH humanitarian responses by supporting its partners in allocating the required resources. In 2021, the Cluster will develop a donor mapping for all the proposed WASH interventions and will approach these donors by highlighting the WASH needs and explaining the partners' response approach and actions. The WASH Cluster during 2021 will coordinate and facilitate the following activities: Activity 1: Developing/updating WASH Cluster national/sub-national contingency plans: WASH Cluster will coordinate with its partners and the related public authorities to identify the potential risks and hazards in the West Bank and Gaza. Cluster and its partners will determine the most affected groups by these potential hazards, propose the appropriate WASH response, measure partners' capacities to respond to these risks, and develop the related response triggers and SOPs. Activity 2: Conducting/ facilitating WASH Cluster partners’ capacity building events: The WASH Cluster will evaluate the Cluster partners' strategic, programming and technical capacities; thus, identifying their capabilities gaps and develop the related capacity building program. Several topics will be included in this program, such as information management, monitoring and evaluation, advocacy and WASH programming. Activity 3: Coordinating and providing Information Management (IM) support for the planning and implementation of WASH Cluster partners interventions: The Cluster will provide the required coordination, technical, and IM support to partners to ensure that all their WASH-related responses are designed and implemented according to the agreed national and international standards. The Cluster will provide the proper tools, including national and sub-national meetings, to ensure the integration of responses and eliminate any overlaps among partners WASH responses. Activity 4: Monitoring the development, planning, and implementation of WASH partners HRP projects: using several tools and mechanisms, the WASH Cluster will keep tracking, recording, monitoring and reporting its partners' performance and achievement during the planning, design and implementation of WASH humanitarian responses, these tools including WASH 4Ws system, fund tracking, HRP updates sheets, in addition to conducting field visits to ensure the quality of the provided response. Moreover, the WASH Cluster will establish a tracking system to follow up on the accountability to the affected populations. Activity 5: Developing the required WASH interventions’ technical and coordination standards and SOPs: WASH Cluster, with the support of the related technical working groups and the related national authorities, will develop unified technical standards and SOPs. These SOPs will ensure the technical harmonization among partners and that all the provided WASH assistance satisfies the related national and international standards. Moreover, the Cluster will ensure the maximum learning, knowledge, and experience sharing among its partners through highlighting the good practices and including them in the unified technical standards and SOPs. Activity 6: Leading, facilitating, and conducting WASH need and impact assessment: the WASH Cluster, in coordination with the Cluster area focal points, will keep monitoring the WASH sector status in the West Bank and Gaza, and will assess any urgent needs, context change, extreme conditions that affect the most vulnerable groups accessibility to adequate WASH services. Moreover, the Cluster will update its HNO by collecting the related data from primary and secondary resources, which will be later shared by the Cluster along with an evidence-based updates and information about the WASH vulnerability in oPt, this ensures that the decision was made with the collaboration and agreement of HCT, government, and international levels. Activity 7: Coordinate for the mobilization of the required resources for implementing the 2021 HRP WASH interventions: by developing a ranking system of the proposed WASH HRP interventions, the WASH Cluster will coordinate with its partners to ensure that the required WASH assistance will prioritize the most vulnerable groups.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Early engagement of selected communities will be enhanced by involving communities and their representatives at all project design, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation stages. Target beneficiaries have been consulted during the project design through focus group discussion and will be continually involved in its implementation. Channels of communication for feedback, complaints, and information sharing will be established through satisfaction surveys and recording goods of services delivered to the end user so that assistance is delivered according to the principles of “do no harm”.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

This project is designed to contribute significantly to gender equality and gender-sensitive programming, focusing on the needs of gender-specific vulnerabilities faced by high risk pregnant and lactating women and their newborns, and boys and girls under the age of five.
