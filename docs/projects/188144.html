Project Summary

This project aims to conduct systematic community wide explosive ordnance survey across Northeast Syria, in order to provide comprehensive contamination baseline data. This contamination baseline data will be made accessible as an open-source live map, in order to significantly alter current data by providing a detailed geographic picture of actual and suspected explosive contamination (EC). Additionally, information on land use, blockages and impacts at a community and canton level will also be included, along with information on the overall coverage of the survey.
The information collected will enable the North and East Syria Mine Action Center (NESMAC), emergency and development actors, donors, and local authorities to assess the threat of EC on planned activities or needs.
This survey, unlike other surveys be data auditable. Allowing stakeholders to manage the survey and avoid gaps through geo tracking. Adding to the traditional NTS question, "where is there contamination data?" and adding the question "where isn't there data of contamination?" With aim of avoiding repetitive baseline surveys, identifying communities with no known hazards as well as defining identified hazards. Enabling other stakeholder to prioritize activities that avoid potential contamination.
The anticipated outcome will be a regional EO risk analysis that forms the basis for a NES wide data lead strategic plan. The development of regional prioritization mechanism in partnership with the key stakeholders will lead to a priority driven auditable coordination mechanisms.
This project is a partnership between MAG and iMMAP, with the latter in the lead

4 - Needs

In NES, 359 communities are impacted by explosive ordnances (EO). People have to deal with this on a daily basis, and this frequently claims victims. It also obstructs crucial humanitarian and rehabilitation activities, such as the provision of water, education and agriculture. To date there has not been a comprehensive EO contamination survey in NES that provides completed and consistent standardized data. Significant survey has been carried out by various agencies in line with their individual projects. However, the activities have primarily been restricted to the main areas of operation. Data is often presented in various formats using different collection methods resulting in inaccurate and inconsistent data. Further, data is focused on agency needs, resulting in significant geographic and impact related gaps. As a result, it is difficult to properly determine the effects and impacts of explosive ordnance contamination on conducting needs analysis as well as on planned projects. Consequently, prioritization and coordination both among HMA organizations as well as other stakeholders is limited and this may possibly hinder the cost-efficiency of programs as well as the overall humanitarian response.

5 - Implementation of activities

This project will conduct a systematic community wide EO survey across NES in order to provide comprehensive contamination baseline data, which will be made accessible as an open-source live map, including information on land use, blockages and impacts. All stakeholders can use the information collected to assess the threat of EO contamination on planned humanitarian and rehabilitation activities across NES. This project is a partnership between iMMAP and MAG. MAG will manage and deliver the physical data collection. Its community liaison teams will conduct NTS. iMMAP will establish the data quality management system to review data daily so that entry and upload will be carried out within 48 hours. This will ensure real time quality management that will enable errors or re-work to be carried whilst teams are still in the local communities. Data collection will be based on standardized reporting formats that comply with NTS protocols and Information Management System for Mine Action (IMSMA) and will also include the recently approved data requirements in International Mine Action Standards (IMAS), using real-time data uploads to a cloud-based system. The forms and data entry will be as automated as possible to reduce data entry errors. iMMAP will establish an open portal that is available to anyone. The raw data will be held co-jointly between the project and the NESMAC ensuring data security and access to local authorities and all humanitarian sectors.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

In the context in NES there is always a risk of a sudden change in the humanitarian and security situation. The main risk is a continuation of EO contamination claiming lives, also because over 40% of the population has not received any risk education. This risk is amplified by a decreasing availability of funding for HMA. Related to this is the risk that the Self Administration and NESMAC will continue to struggle to deliver in their role as Mine Action authorities. This in turn creates a risk that a lack of proper prioritization limits effective clearing efforts. Additionally, without proper coordination, the risk of information gaps immediately resurfaces. Finally, there is always a risk of recontamination, especially around frontlines close to the OPS areas.
