Project Summary

Increase access to safe drinking water, hygiene and sanitation for 942 Refugees and host communities in Basundah. Create 8 new wells with handpumps. Rehabilitate 8 old wells with hand pumps. Construct 24 community latrines with washing facilities. Construct 500 latrine slabs for household use. Establish humanitarian presence. Develop capacity of host community to absorb further refugee influx.

1 - Needs

UNHCR report dated 14 August 2021 recorded 942 new arrivals in Basundah, eastern Sudan, near the border with Ethiopia. As of 30 September 2021 UNHCR reported a total of 57081 Ethiopian Refugees in eastern Sudan. This number is expected to continue increasing as long as the Tigray conflict in Ethiopia remains unresolved. Vulnerability of Refugees have been exasperated due to their flight for safety from Ethiopia. Livelihood activities of Refugees were completely cut off. In their fight for survival Refugees have limited resources to invest in safe water sources. Hygiene facilities in Basundah are basic or none-existing. Poor living conditions increase susceptibility to diseases preventable through safe water and sanitation. Due to social and economic structure women, children, disabled and the elderly persons are often worst effected during refugee crises reducing their ability to access basic services. JAM, in coordination with local government and other humanitarian actors on the ground, is preparing to support Refugees in Basundah through emergency WASH programs. JAM will continually weigh new information to determine suitability of programs and adjust to accommodate situational change. During the initial stage JAM WASH program focus will be to increase access to safe drinking water, hygiene, and sanitation facilities. Apart from survival and health benefits this WASH program is critical to restore the dignity of refugees.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Output 1. Engage affected communities.Indicator 1.1: Meet with affected communities, local government, and other humanitarian actors. Jointly assess needs, priorities and most vulnerable households.Indicator 1.2: Support affected communities to form WASH committees to effectively self-govern solutions and maintenance of WASH programsIndicator 1.3: Beneficiary WASH committees must be gender and age balanced. It must be representative of the community and not be dominated by a particular individual or group. Financial management of the committee must be stable and trustworthy. Vulnerable households, including the disabled, must be prioritized in receiving aid.Indicator 1.4: Beneficiary WASH committees must take an active role in all WASH interventions and play a leading role in decision making. Output 2. Increase access to safe water of appropriate quantity as per SPHERE standards. Refugees and affected host communities have access to safe water of appropriate quantity and quality (as per WASH cluster standards).Indicator 2.1: Construct 8 x new wells equipped with hand pumps. Decision to drill by machine or dig by hand will be based on, geology, local experience and available technology. Where population density is high hand pumps may be substituted with solar powered water distribution systems.Indicator 2.2: Rehabilitate 8 x existing wells and equip with hand pumps. Hand pumps must be easy to repair and spare parts available on local market. Indicator 2.3: Construction / rehabilitation of wells and handpumps will involve WASH committees transfer knowledge and empower self-maintenance. Indicator 2.4: WASH committees will ensure equal access and benefit of water points without discrimination against age or gender.Output 3. Increase access to sanitation facilities.Indicator 3.1: Construction of 24 x temporary communal latrines and showers to cater for new arrivals without household latrines. Facilities will be gender segregated and cater for the disabled.Indicator 3.2: Construction of 500 x concrete latrine slabs. Latrine slabs will be distributed to households after households prepare 3m pit and screen.Output 4. Increase hygiene practices and reduce spread of communicable disease. Affected refugees and host communities receive hygiene kits and information on safe water, sanitation and hygiene practice.Indicator 4.1: Number of community mobilisers trained. Community mobilisers are representative of community and gender balanced.Indicator 4.2: Number of households receiving hygiene kits, including soap.Indicator 4.3: Percentage of targeted households implementing hygiene practices.
