Project Summary

The project objective is to improve the living conditions and protection of IDPs in sites and ensure equitable access to services and assistance of all persons in need. Through the project, SYPD will strengthen the predictability and effectiveness of multi sectorial interventions at site level and/or areas of concentration of sites by establishing CCCM coordination mechanisms with a clear focus on moving toward attaining durable solutions with full participation of all stakeholders.
Through the project SYPD will also achieve the following cluster objectives;
Strengthen the predictability and effectiveness of multi sectorial interventions at site level and/or areas of concentration of sites; This will be achieved through establishing or reinforcing CCCM coordination mechanisms and participation at the settlement level and other appropriate levels.
The project will ensure that site level information is available and shared with stakeholders and an improved management of sites and settlements. This will be achieved through community engagement, participation, in the transforming the living conditions and safe access to services in selected sites.
Other objectives will include promoting the involvement and participation of affected community to ensure effective response and ownership is increased, ensuring that affected populations living in sites have the information they need to access services, ensuring that settlement sites are safe and hygiene conditions through drainage channels and sanitation facilities, and strengthening community self-management to promote durable solutions for displaced people in sites

Needs

About 2.6 million people are displaced in various settlement sites in Somalia with no access to protection and basic services (UNOCHA August 2018). The IDPs continue to face destitution as the 13 main IDP settlements are classified as Crisis (IPC Phase 3) between now and the end of 2018. While declining food prices and increased employment opportunities have improved food security situation in most urban areas, IDPs Settlements continue to face exclusion from such gains due to lack of access to resources and the limited opportunities for displaced persons. Out migration from the rural areas continues to be reported in Baidoa, Bakool, Lower Shabelle, Banadir, Gaalckacyo, Galmudug and Hiraan regions. For example, recent trends indicate continued high number of IDP influxes into Baidoa and very few exits. According to IOM tracking matrix, in September 2018 alone, a total of 26,212 movements were observed at Flow Monitoring Points in Somalia, representing a significant decrease in comparison with August when 34,219 individuals were observed. This decrease is attributed to the closure of five FMPs due to budgetary constraints, as well as the decrease of movements in Bossasso and, to a lesser extent, Buuhoodle. The great majority of flows identified (68%) were incoming against 32% outgoing, reaching levels comparable to July 2018. While insecurity remains a valid reason for movement to IDPS settlements, destitution and absence of humanitarian assistance and basic services is a major driving factor. An assessment by the IOM’s movement trends monitoring, most of the arrivals to Baidoa over the past few weeks cited either lack of food or insecurity, while the remaining cited health reasons and lack of water as the underlying cause. Continued migrations have resulted to overcrowding in settlements putting pressure on existing facilities and coordination capacity for accessing services.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The building of resilience of vulnerable individuals, households and communities in Somalia to future shocks remains of paramount importance to tackle the key risks and address the underlying causes of vulnerability for IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities in terms sustainable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene services. SYPD will work towards strengthening self-reliance of, and working towards comprehensive solutions for, forcibly displaced people, both refugees and IDPs, by ensuring a more active engagement community structures, development actors, government ministry of water, local authorities, private owners and other donors such as the Somalia Stability Fund and the World bank.Even in the context of protracted displacements, the environment in Hiraan, Lower Shabelle, Bay region, middle Shabelle still presents opportunities for durable solutions and resilience building. SYPD will work with the community leaders, relevant ministry at the local and national level, and local government authorities to find durable solutions including secure land tenure, capacity building and income generation activities and hand over mechanisms for community based projects. During the project implementation, SYPD will mainstream capacity building initiatives for community water infrastructure management systems in order to create a strong management structure and eventual hand over mechanisms.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

SYPD will ensure that beneficiaries safely access basic services without causing harm by promoting meaningful access, accountability and participation of beneficiaries in the service provision. In particular, SYPD will promote the participation of beneficiaries by establishing and strengthening project committees and building their capacity through training and resources support. The beneficiary committees will be involved in identification of vulnerable groups and monitoring the project implementation and feedback/complaint mechanisms. SYPD will safeguard against protection risks by ensuring that all services such as items distributions are carried out during the day, in locations close to IDPs residences and clearly guarded with crowd control mechanisms to avoid theft, intimidation and gender-based violence. Where men and women are all involved in the collection of items, SYPD will keep separate lines for women and men. Care will also be taken to ensure children are not exposed to hard work or exploitation by having to carry heavy items. Each children headed household will be assigned a guardian during the collection of supplies to avoid exclusion and abuse. For example during the construction of shelters SYPD will adopt designs that ensure safety and security and provide dignity for beneficiaries including family unity, respect for cultural norms and the protection for girls and women. Each shelter will be provided with proper lighting to enhance protection and lockable doors to provide protection and privacy. Shelters will be constructed to avoid flooding and site settlement planning to avoid fires and provide accessible routes to common facilities.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

SYPD will ensure that beneficiaries safely access basic services without causing harm by promoting meaningful access, accountability and participation of all gender (men, women, boys and girls) in the service provision. In particular, SYPD will promote the participation of beneficiaries by establishing and strengthening project committees and building their capacity through training on gender mainstreaming and resources support. The beneficiary committees will be involved in identification of vulnerable groups and monitoring the project implementation and feedback/complaint mechanisms. SYPD will safeguard against protection risks against women and girls by ensuring that all services such as items distributions are carried out during the day, in locations close to IDPs residences and clearly guarded with crowd control mechanisms to avoid theft, intimidation and gender-based violence. Each shelter will be provided with proper lighting to enhance protection and lockable doors to provide protection and privacy. Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

SYPD will ensure that beneficiaries are not exposed to armed conflicts and violence by nature of the assistance provide by undertaking community consultations during the project mobilization and implementation to understand local dynamics and take due care in project design. In particular, SYPD will ensure the participation of all on affected communities, inclusion of all stakeholders in camp coordination and camp management activities including host communities and IDPs.
