Project Summary

This project aims to achieve the following objectives:
• Increase access to life-saving and life-sustaining coordinated, equitable humanitarian health services for those most vulnerable and in need
• Strengthen health sector capacity to prepare for, detect and deliver a timely response to outbreaks
• Strengthen health system capacity to support continuity of care, strengthen community resilience, and respond to IDP movements.
The project will contribute to strengthening health care and address human suffering in NWS by ensuring the sustainability of health services and improving the health conditions of those in need, including hosts and IDPs across gender and age groups; children, men, women, the elderly, and people with disabilities, by supporting, fully or partially, 18 HFs (5 hospitals,6 specialized centers, and 7 PHCs) in Idleb, Alraqqa, and Aleppo, in addition to supporting 60 ambulances with fuel and operating 4 vehicles for non-emergency cases within the referral network that coordinates the referrals between more than 130 HFs.as follows:

• PHCs services: RH, dental, MHPSS, vaccine, lab, bandage, management of childhood illness, nutrition, and management of NCDs and CDs
• Hospitals services: general surgical, obstetrics, RH, internal medicine, laparoscopic, urinary surgeries, orthopedic, ophthalmic, and related surgeries.
• Specialized centers services Treatment of thalassemia, specialized radiography, dialysis, and providing patients with blood products and blood transfusions for them as well as providing the surrounding hospitals with blood units.
The HFs are located in areas with high priority according to health cluster, distributed in densely populated and accessible locations, most of the people are IDPs with a very low level of medical care and bad economic situation, while all of the HFs were included within the central referral network, and have close coordination with IPC central desk as well as HFs commit to sharing the health data through HIS, 4Ws monthly.

3 - Needs

According to HNO 2022: 12.2 M people will need health services, of which 4.4 M are displaced, 1.33 M will be children aged under 5 years (including an anticipated 503 000 newborns) and 3.38 M are women of reproductive age (15-49 years). Half a million elderly people will require inclusive health services, as will people with early onset non-communicable diseases (NCDs), which are estimated to account for 45% of all deaths in Syria. Disability impacts an estimated 1.3 M people, placing them at greater risk of exclusion from health services. According to WHO Emergency Appeal 2022, Syrian Arab Republic: the fragile health system has been repeatedly strained through multiple concurrent emergencies and challenged as a result of ongoing insecurity, the COVID-19 pandemic, a debilitating socioeconomic crisis, and chronic challenges that continue to affect the availability and quality of health services across Syria and the physical and mental wellbeing of the entire population. The emerging cholera needs and risks are an additional layer to the already complex, protracted Syrian crisis. The response to a cholera outbreak must focus on limiting mortality and reducing the spread of the disease. The number of confirmed and suspected cases continues to rise.

4 - Implementation of activities

Provide essential PHC and STHC services, including outpatient and inpatient health services, nutrition, and provision of MH consultations and interventions by MHGAP doctors. Providing health facilities with essential medicines and medical supplies. Providing referral ambulances with fuel, and operating 4 vehicles for non-emergency cases. Strengthen the capacity of health care providers: conducting training need assessment, providing training packages, and supervision visits to evaluate the capacity improvement of staff. Increase access to health services supporting 18 HFs and rehabilitating them to be suitable for beneficiaries and ensure easy access for PWDs, creating areas that secure the privacy of patients and prevent sexual exploitation. Community health services will be delivered through conducting HH visits and screening disease cases to be referred to the HF. Coordinate the humanitarian health response through participating in health sector coordination meetings held across response hubs, creating service mapping of the different humanitarian actors, and coordinating with health partners to create referral pathways. M&E activities: developing the M&E plan, setting project indicators, conducting regular visits, preparing the satisfaction reports, highlighting the needs and feedback of beneficiaries, and ensuring reporting (4Ws, HeRAMS, EWARS/EWARN, and the surveillance system of attacks on healthcare facilities).

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

1- Risk: Sexual Harassment: among employees, volunteers and in work environment context, Level: Low, Mitigation / Prevention: all UOSSM staff have been trained on PSEA and above all has signed a TOR and are aware of the internal policy of PSEA which has been monitored by both the HR & MEAL teams. 2-Risk: Sexual Exploitation and Abuse: between service delivery personnel, towards beneficiaries, Level: Low, Mitigation / Prevention: All UOSSM staff (employees and volunteers) signed on a code of conduct that includes of refers to the commitment to PSEA, that is issued by the UN Secretary-General Bulletin for the PSEA procedure. In addition, UOSSM has a PSEA focal point in addition to a dedicated hotline to receive complaints from beneficiaries in case of PSEA incidents. 3-Risk: Gender-Based Violence: targeting the vulnerable groups with communities (i.e., women, elderly, children, special needs …), level: Medium, Mitigation / Prevention: The project design is built on robust need assessment and is also targeting the most vulnerable groups in the community without discrimination or exclusion. Above, the project is a multi-sectoral one where all sectors are aware and acknowledged of detection and referral of GBV either internally to the protection department or externally to other service providers.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: UOSSM will coordinate closely with the health clusters, as well as local authorities and humanitarian actors inside Syria throughout the proposed intervention, informing the cluster if and when adjustments are needed to the intervention. Locations remain accessible for the team and the beneficiaries. Inflation and currency exchange rates remain within an acceptable range that does not affect planned deliverable quality and quantity. Local qualified staff required for project activities are available and adequate. Risk: The conflict prevents beneficiaries and team access to facilities. The close of borders prevents the shipments of supplies and medications to access facilities. New peak of COVID-19 and the spread of cholera outbreaks. mitigation: Develop a strong risk management strategy, (preparing an emergency plan /plan B/ according to the expected scenarios in conflicted areas. Capacity building plan for the staff. Enhance coordination with the health partner and active participation in the TWG to fill up the gaps in medical supplies Activating community COVID-19 treatment center in light of a new peak of COVID-19 (UOSSM can implement if the fund is provided). Activating Cholera treatment units and oral rehydration points. Implement strict recommendations on quarantine, isolation, and provision of IPC means and PPEs
