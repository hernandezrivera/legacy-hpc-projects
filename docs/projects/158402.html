Project Summary

The project aims at answering to the immediate sheltering needs of the most vulnerable inhabitants of Ibo District affected by Cyclone Kenneth providing them with a safe, covered and living space which can guarantee them a minimum level of safety, security, privacy and dignity. Specifically, the project will: 1) assist 270 extremely vulnerable HH in the early recovery of their destroyed houses; 2) provide technical guidance and materials to support the self- reconstruction of 100 houses; 3) train 270 households on Build Back Safer techniques.
Beneficiaries will be selected among the most vulnerable families (i.e. elderly living alone and with no source of income, single women pregnant or with many kids with no source of income, disable and sick people living alone and with no source of income) identified thanks to a first house-to-house survey carried out by Oikos from 29th April to 20th May 2019 and covering the whole population of Ibo District. Those families have been also assessed through a second level house vulnerability assessment done in Matemo and Ibo Islands from 21st to 29th June (Survey Results available upon request) looking more specifically at their housing situation which is could expose them to other threats and risks (robbery, abuse, etc).
Priority for the reconstruction and supply of materials will be given to inhabitants of Matemo (70% over the total beneficiaries), the island of the District most affected by the cyclone (100% of houses got uncovered), more isolated, with limited livelihood opportunities and access to safe water, and with the highest number of displaced people where life conditions are more critical. Final identification of the beneficiaries will be done through a validation process at community level involving a community committee created at bairro level involving chef do bairro, chef do quartierao, religious leader, women and youth leaders.
Reconstruction will be based on culturally acceptable construction standards; safe building practices and standards; re-use of local materials including the reutilization of fallen coconut trees for the house and roof structure. All these solutions are expected to able to boost the local economy. The use of metal sheets is finally exceeded to enhance future opportunities for rain water harvest.
Project steps:
Selection of final beneficiaries based on vulnerability list and participatory validation process
Know how sharing of improved reconstruction techniques: set up of a “learning site / house model” in every construction area to share improved and safer reconstruction practices and techniques to be used in reconstruction activities. Total (indirect) beneficiaries 1.350 HH
Support the reconstruction of partially or totally destroyed houses: Based on two level of vulnerability the project will directly support: 1) 270HH reconstructing their house involving local skilled team under the supervision of construction experts; 2) 100HH to self-reconstruct their houses providing construction materials and supervision. Based on the pre-feasibility assessment, intervention will be of two Types: (Type A) complete new house for those vulnerable whose houses was totally destroyed (90%); (Type B) new roof structure and roof cover for those vulnerable whose houses was only partially destroyed (10%). (Additional information on reconstruction intervention Type A and B available upon request). Total beneficiaries 370HH
Implementation of a pilot on the re-use of fallen coconut trees: Activity will be implemented in Matemo where a massive number of coconut trees felt down due to the cyclone. The project will introduce the use of a sawmill machinery for wood cutting and will define an appropriate protocol for coconut trees use in houses reconstruction for future replication. A total n. of 45 houses of Type A will be done
