Needs

The unstable security situation in Borno State continues to hinder the rebuilding of the already weakened health system and limit access to lifesaving PHC services, including routine immunization, outpatient consultations and sexual and reproductive health (SRH) services. There is also an increased risk of communicable disease due to movement and overcrowding of IDP and returnee populations. With active migration of refugees/IDPs from one overcrowded place to another, the risks of many communicable diseases (including cholera) are sharply increased. Damasak currently lacks the healthcare human resources to cover the existing population needs. For example, the Sphere Standard recommends one basic health unit/10,000 population; one health center/50,000 people; and one district or rural hospital/250,000 people. However, in Damasak, the MCH (health unit) provides health and nutrition service for more than 100,000 population. The Sphere standard also recommends 22 qualified health workers (medical doctors, nurses and midwifes)/10,000 population; at least one medical doctor/50,000 population; at least one qualified nurse/10,000 population; at least one midwife/10,000 population; and one Community Health Worker (CHW)/1,000 population. The existing number of health workers in Damasak is not adequate. For IDPs coming out of Boko Haram-controlled areas and returning from Cameroon and Niger to border LGAs, there remain urgent protection needs. Women and girls are at heightened risk of rape, forced or early marriage, sexual exploitation, and/or transactional sex (i.e. in exchange for food, critical household items/relief items, etc. given the extreme level of household vulnerability) because of the ongoing conflict and lack of services/resources. As is anticipated in conflict settings, this increased risk of GBV is partly attributable to sub-standard shelter and/or living conditions including overcrowding, inadequate protection and security mechanisms for vulnerable individuals as well as non-traditional means for resource acquisition (collecting scarce fire wood or collecting water in new locations). WASH conditions remain a significant concern and link directly to a variety of public health issues as IDPs return to their original LGAs in Northeast Nigeria. Conflict-affected families increasingly lack access to adequate water and sanitation infrastructure due to damage/destruction to facilities and overburdening of WASH facilities in camps and host communities. As a leading contributor to morbidity and mortality, the current poor WASH conditions in camps, host communities, and newly accessible areas remain a priority concern for humanitarian assistance. Diarrheal disease is the second most common health concern and can exacerbate conditions like severe acute malnutrition. The Round 3 of North East Food Security and Nutrition surveillance reveals that there is a significant increase in global acute malnutrition (GAM) and severe acute malnutrition (SAM) rates in most of the domains as compared to the previous two rounds carried out in October 2016 and March 2017. The aggregated analysis reveals that GAM rates are at a serious level in Borno (11.4%) and the SAM rates are also above the critical level at 2.2%. Nevertheless, in-depth analysis of the data reveals that northern Borno State’s SAM rate is 4%, almost twice as much as the state level estimates while central Borno 3.1%. Accordingly, there remains a need to address the existing nutrition problem in Dikwa, Ngala and Banki (central Borno) as well as Damasak (northern Borno).

Activities

Key activities will include:● Establishment of and support to health clinics in the form of medical commodities and staff training to facilitate primary health care (PHC) and sexual and reproductive health (SRH) services, among others; ● training, provision of outreach materials/supplies, and supportive supervision to Community Health Workers (CHWs) to enable them to resume community outreach; ● establishment of safe spaces with case management and psychosocial support (PSS) services for survivors of gender-based violence; ● establishment of child friendly spaces with case management and PSS services for children aged 3-17 and set up child protection community based mechanisms to identify and respond to child protection concerns● rehabilitation/installation of essential WASH infrastructure, including latrines, bathing facilities, and solar-powered boreholes;● distribution of hygiene kits coupled with hygiene promotion;● sanitation interventions, such as installation of waste points, drainage interventions in flood-prone areas and clean-up campaigns, to reduce the environmental health risks;● Support for optimal infant and young child feeding (IYCF) with integrated multi-sectoral interventions● Training of CHWs and health workers and support for them to provide appropriate services for the management of SAMFHI 360’s nutrition, health, protection, and WASH activities will comprise a fully integrated package to promote comprehensive support to IDPs, thus reducing risks of morbidity and mortality and contributing to improved wellbeing of displaced and conflict-affected individuals in Borno State.
