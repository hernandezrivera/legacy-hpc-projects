Project Summary

Humanitarian emergencies can crucially affect individuals’ psychological, social, and emotional wellbeing. These effects may be acute in the short-run and if left untreated, can have severe long-term consequences that undermine the mental health and psychosocial wellbeing of the affected population. MHPSS services are one way of seeking to reduce negative impacts and provide aid to affected communities.
This Strengthening Resilience, Coping with, and Mitigating Psychological Trauma of Children and Caregivers in Highly Affected Areas project aims to strengthen the resilience, as well as to reinforce positive coping mechanisms needed to process trauma, and mitigate the psychological trauma of the ongoing Israeli occupation, settlers’ violence, and COVID- 19 on the most vulnerable children and caregivers in Palestine. Children and caregivers will have access to our newly established mental health hotline in order to safely report on any sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) they are subjected to. We will continue to encourage and promote our communities to report on SEA in a safe and confidential manner. We will consult with community leaders, engage in community mobilization, and raise awareness on the protection from sexual exploitation and abuse, with particular focus on children caregivers. Targeted children and caregivers will be offered assistance commensurate with his/her needs, such as psychosocial support, medical intervention, legal assistance, and reintegration support). These will be provided through our ongoing programs dealing with gender-based violence and child protection. Reported cases of SEA will be dealt with promptly and in coordination with the appropriate authorities, while ensuring anonymity and respect of every child and caregiver for their protection. Our highly experienced team of psychologists have the necessary training on gender- and child-sensitive issues. TRC will track and report on the number and result of reported cases, and will analyze periodic reviews to guarantee reported cases are responded to quickly and effectively.
Mental health needs in Palestine have been exacerbated in the last decade due to recent escalations in occupation-related violence, and more recently, Covid-19 and the ensuing restrictions imposed as a result of the pandemic, in addition to the increase in domestic violence. As a result, this project aims to target the following areas in the West Bank where major MHPSS intervention is needed most: H2 – Hebron (Khallet Jaber, Haret El-Shaikh, Tel Ar-Rumaideh, Old City of Hebron, and Jabal Johar), Tubas & North Jordan Valley (Kardala, Bardala, 'Ein Al-Beida, Tayasir, Al-Malih, Al-A’qaba), East Jerusalem (Hizma, Jabaá, Abu Dis, Eizariyya, Wadi Joz, Ar-Ram), and Nablus (Burin, Beita, As-Sawiya, Al-Lubban Ash-Sharqiya, Urif).

1 - Needs

Palestinian children in the West Bank and East Jerusalem are constantly exposed to violence. The political and socioeconomic conditions they grow up in are extremely difficult. The politically-motivated violence and clashes with the Israeli occupation forces has severely affected Palestinian children, often resulting in their arrests, detention, and ill-treatment in which these situations frequently lead to traumatic consequences. For example, according to a report by OCHA in 2019, one in every five families in the H2 area of Hebron city has had a child who was arrested at least once. These children encounter harassment when crossing checkpoints, are attacked by Israeli settlers in areas close to Israeli settlements, and are regularly exposed to constant unrest and violence. More specifically, expansion of settlements, an increase in settler violence, housing and structure demolitions/confiscations and forced evictions/displacement are intensifying pressure on the most vulnerable Palestinian communities. Since the beginning of the Covid-19 epidemic, children and parents have been under stress as a result of being confined to their homes. The pandemic is harming the health, social and material wellbeing of children, with the most vulnerable children hit hardest. With school closures, social distancing measures, and confinement, Palestinian children and caregivers have been exposed to domestic violence, increase in anxiety and stress, and reduced access to vital family and MHPSS services, putting them at risk of further psychological distress. During this time, TRC dealt with a rise in the number of cases where many families requested MHPSS services from TRC’s psychologists. Further, the pandemic has identified East Jerusalem as being in critical need of continued MHPSS deployment. TRC is already active in East Jerusalem implementing a project supported by UNICEF in Silwan, Shu’fat Refugee Camp, Jabal Al-Mukabber, Issawiya, and the Old City. With this project, we aim to extend our services to Hebron, Nablus, Tubas, and Sheikh Jarrah (in addition to the aforementioned neighbourhoods in East Jerusalem). Additionally, prior to the outbreak, these communities have been continuously exposed to violence and ill-treatment due to the on-going Israeli-Palestinian conflict; therefore, there was a great need to deploy and maintain access to crucial MHPSS services. Following the outbreak, these families continue to suffer in silence while their psychological disorders continue to manifest, compounded by pressure caused by the conflict as well as the coronavirus. Children at home are suffering and unable to comprehend and cope with the quick changes to their daily routines. Women and men, especially caregivers, are burdened with addressing the suffering of their children, as well as taking on additional roles, such as taking care of their households to provide for their families.The pandemic has uncovered the bitter reality that Palestinian communities are not well-equipped with the knowledge and resources to identify and address their own trauma, let alone the trauma of children. Additionally, they are not aware of their inherent human rights. Families themselves require comprehensive treatment due to exposure to psychological trauma of adult family members, being former detainees, or victims of the conflict. In many cases, adult victims of trauma and ill-treatment are at risk of perpetrating similar acts of violence and ill-treatment upon the children in their households, often manifesting in domestic violence, child abuse, or neglect. Hence, TRC aims to continue to support the resilience of children and caregivers, develop their coping mechanisms, and continue to realize their rights to survival and development and by doing so attain their full potential. Since early 2018, TRC reached out to 1,187 adults through 51 organized workshops to enhance child protection measures through increased awareness of positive parenting, psychological first aid, crisis intervention, identification of symptoms of trauma and appropriate responses and referral pathways, Covid-19 health and hygiene measures, stress management, and debriefing during lockdown and quarantine. The project will intervene to reflect on people’s experience to multi-sourced violence and ill-treatment, and provide specialized multidisciplinary care for the most vulnerable children and caregivers in the target areas through regular direct professional rehabilitation and therapeutic services. TRC’s coordination mechanisms in the targeted communities goes with different coordination and cooperation with the local authorities “village councils, municipalities”, directorates of MoE, MoSD, protection networks, in addition to the local CBOs working in the targeted locations. In Jerusalem, TRC’s coordinate with the East Jerusalem Coordination Committee under UNICEF supervision. With this mechanism, TRC is able to receive referred cases for MHPSS. In addition to that, TRC had been working in these communities, and well known as specialized MHPSS organization. The beneficiaries in general are reached through TRC’s outreach program in addition to TRC’s Free Support Line where it covers the whole West Bank including Jerusalem and covered Gaza Strip.TRC is part of the MHPSS sub- cluster, in addition to GBV- sub- cluster, this reflects TRC’s mandate in Child Protection mainly and in enhancing the well- being of the most marginalized people. TRC in its work, focuses on the coordination with the main ministries such as MoE, MoSD, MoH, MoSD, MoWA. This cooperation is reflected by signed MoUs. TRC is part of the Palestinian Network on Protection against Sexual Abuse and Sexual Exploitation- PSEA Network”. TRC follows the national referral system in related to the cases of GBV, this goes with the ongoing network channels.

2.3. CHILD PROTECTION & MHPSS RESPONSES2.3.1. PFA and Crisis Intervention - psychological first aid and crisis intervention counselling for 2,5002,200 beneficiaries “1,260 child, 840 Parents- caregivers, 100 CP caregivers”, through the deployment of 2,5002,200 PFA target communities’ in H2- Hebron “Khalet Jaber, Haret Eshaikh, Tel Ermaideh, Old City of Hebron, and Jabal Johar”., Tubas& NJV “Kardala, Bardala, 'Ein el Beida, Tayasir, Hammamat Al Malih, Al A’qaba”, East Jerusalem “Hezma, Jabaá, Abu Dis, Aizaria, Wadi Joz, Ar Ram”, and Nablus “Burin, Beita, As Sawiya, Al Lubban ash Sharqiya, Urif”. This is done via coordination with schools, local CBOs, and community leaders, providing counselling, stress debriefing, coping exercises, and psycho-social support aimed at alleviating symptoms before becoming chronic. Case Management for 800 beneficiaries “480 children, 320 adults- caregivers’ parents” -human rights-based holistic individual and group therapy for children and caregivers. Each beneficary will receive family counselling before being referred to more comprehensive (6-8 sessions per beneficiary) cognitive behavioural therapy, Eye Movement Desensitization & Reprocessing therapy, & Narrative Exposure therapy. TRC in its activities works on applying its approved policies mainly Safeguarding and PSEA. The staff received continuous refreshing sessions on these two main policies in addition to others, and this covers the consultants, board of trustees, and all the contractors with TRC. Zero tolerance in any violation of these polices. 2.3.2. CHILD PROTECTION: Capacity building for CP service providers: TRC will organize 8 workshops for 100 CP service providers, social workers, and staff of the Child Protection CSOs including CSOs delivering CP services to children with disabilities and operating in the project targeted areas (25 per workshop). Through this activity, internationally certified TRC’s Psychologists in the fields of Narrative Therapy, Narrative Exposure Therapy, PFA, and Psycho-Education will provide highly specialized workshops for participants who work closely with trauma-affected children and families in order to improve efficiency and effectiveness of interventions, contribute to partner coordination, joint interventions, and referral pathways for comprehensive access to appropriate services, and to support the standardization of data management and information sharing across the network for reporting, analyses, and studies2.3.3 Psycho-social awareness sessions: TRC will conduct 44 workshops (2 per area) for 880 participants- parents’ caregivers- (20 per workshop) on mental health, debriefing and stress management, protecting children’s rights, promoting positive parenting to support children suffering from traumas, and how to address stigma and social exclusion. These workshops will respond to the most critical needs of each community and, due to prevailing fears over covid-19, raise awareness on safety and health measures to protect from exposure. During TRC’s project in different localities, TRC specialized team had provided workshops on positive parenting and discipline, crisis counselling and debriefing, awareness raising of children’s rights, and capacity building of parents and teachers to identify, support and provide protection to children. Participants expressed the need to continue these community-based events to support and empower families, children, and concerned stakeholders to work cooperatively in addressing the critical status of children in West Bank including East Jerusalem. During these workshops, TRC will further address crises and traumas due to the conflict that have been compounded due to the on-going covid-19 pandemic as children and families endured movement restrictions and home-confinement to prevent spread of the disease. 2.3.5 MHPSS: Provision of Mental Health and Psychosocial Services (MHPSS) for adults (including caregivers) affected by conflict related violence. TRC will provide individual and group therapy for the most vulnerable 100 staff of child protection service providers supporting vulnerable children project targeted areas. TRC will provide individual and/or group therapy including CBT, EMDR, narrative therapy, and narrative exposure therapy to support CP service providers in recovering from their symptoms in order to ensure that children are in an environment that is supportive of their recovery, and that the risks of further exposure to trauma is mitigated. It is important that they are able to recover from their own traumas, first-hand. On average, each person will be treated for 4-8 sessions; chronic cases may be provided with additional sessions based on the severity of their symptoms. Through this intervention, CP service providers will be better equipped to support the psychological recovery of vulnerable children, as well as tend to their own mental healthcare.The number of beneficiaries is reflecting part of TRC annual reached beneficiaries for the MHPSS in West Bank including East Jerusalem.Since the start of COVID19 pandemic, TRC applies the PSS health protocols through reaching the beneficiaries. Sanitzers and prevention procedures are applied through the individual and group sessions. For the awareness, TRC can provide the online tools for carrying these activities in addition to individual sessions in case of any possible lockdown. TRC’s Fee Support Line covers the needed consultancy and complains in regard of any GBV and PSEA. TRC’s email for complaints is published through social media, brochures, website, etc…TRC's staff receive continuous technical and emotional supervision in addition to the annual technical capacity plan. This is covered by the core program budget

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

TRC is a human rights organization specialized that provides MHPSS to the victims of violence, torture, and ill- treatment.TRC is a human rights organization specialized in MHPSS provision to victims of violence, torture, and ill treatment.Do No Harm is one of TRC’s core principles where we work to prevent and mitigate any negative impact of its actions on affected populations, including children and people with physical and mental disabilities. This principle is reflected in TRC’s internal policies that guide its humanitarian work in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem.Major procedures of TRC apply in reference to the Do No Harm principle, which are-- Signed consent by parents or eligible caregivers particularly when working with children under the age of 18.- Cases are provided with the contact details of the General Director and quality assurance committee.- Regular field visits by the clinical supervisor on duty.- An evaluation form is periodically filled by the beneficiary. TRC will also ensure high standards of child safeguarding, and deployment of holistic interventions that do no harm. The details of each beneficiary will be documented in the encrypted online case management system, in the field using specialized tablets, which are synced to a central system at TRC’s office in Ramallah. All clients will sign consent forms and parents/caretakers will be required to sign consent forms on behalf of their children. In ensuring privacy practices, TRC will not include any identifiable information, such as contact details or national ID card in public reports.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

TRC beneficiaries are the most vulnerable and affected by violence, torture, and ill treatment. This includes the victims with physical disabilities. TRC works on finding friendly working environment for its beneficiaries with physical disabilities. The outreach program enables TRC’s specialized psychologists to reach them directly in their communities. Visits and sessions reach them in their houses, local CBOs, and local authorities “village councils, municipalities” for group and some individual sessions. TRC specialized clinic- psychiatric clinic- and TRC’s main office is set to serve the beneficiaries with physical disabilities in case they need to visit the clinic and centre. Having easily accessible locations, elevators, three underground parking with ramps for wheelchairs and walkers. TRC with its MHPSS services, treatment, and rehabilitation, works in cooperation with specialized SCOs working in the West Bank including East Jerusalem. Many cases of the MHPSS beneficiaries are referred to these specialized organizations for additional services they are in need of. This goes with the acceptance of the beneficiary and his/ her caregivers in children’s cases.TRC’s disability and health inclusion strategies involve the inclusion of people with physical and mental disabilities that are designed to identify and remove obstacles, such as physical, communication, and attitudinal that hampers individuals’ ability to fully participate in society, in the same way as people without disabilities. Specifically, this involves obtaining fair treatment from others (nondiscriminatory practices); making communications and the physical environment more accessible to as many individuals as possible; and eliminating the belief that people with disabilities are less capable of doing things (tackling stigma and stereotypes). Including people with physical and mental disabilities concerns input from people with disabilities, generally through disability-focused programs. This is conducted through implementation, monitoring and evaluation of programs.
