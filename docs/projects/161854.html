1 - Needs

Global acute malnutrition (GAM) levels in children aged 6-59 months remain consistently concerning, with over one million children suffering from SAM and MAM across 18 LGAs in the BAY states. This includes 367,000 children under five (179,830 boys and 187,170 girls) suffering from SAM and 727,000 (356,230 boys and 370,770 girls) with MAM . The crisis has also exacerbated pre-existing conditions of malnutrition, driven by a number of inter-related factors, including: weak health infrastructures; poor infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices; limited access to safe water and health and sanitation services; prevalence of water-borne diseases; poor hygiene conditions; and seasonal food insecurity.

2 - Activities

Malnourished children in targeted communities diagnosed in a timely manner and provided with appropriate treatment / referral services; women of child giving age provided with education on caring for infants and young children; and, community workers and health staff’s capacity to manage child malnutrition improved.- Sensitize and mobilize targeted communities on the project- Set up community project implementation committees- Conduct baseline nutrition assessments in target communities (only where such information on malnutrition does not exist)- Create referral linkages with health facilities including signing MoU with State Primary Health Care Development Agency (SPHCDA)- Procure health commodities, supplies and equipment- Establish OTPs and SCs- Recruit and train workers manning the e-ranger mobile clinic- Carry out routine screening for malnutrition and referral to treatment centres- Conduct CMAM and IYCF training- Carry out health and nutrition training- Conduct food demonstrations using locally available foods - Establish support groups for care givers of malnourished children- Build and transition project aspects to government and local CSOs once sufficient capacity has been developed- Carry out joint monitoring with government agencies
