Project Summary

"FIVDB will support in building local capacity, and improve resilience, bringing sustainable results and increased local ownership. Because the crisis is multifaceted and protracted, the FIVDB will undertake a holistic approach to enhance economic opportunities and livelihood scopes. The project will promote women's empowerment through context-specific interventions driven by gender and inclusive development analysis and shall provide off-farm livelihood income-generating activity (IGA) support to host community members to build resilience. FIVDB will conduct a labor market assessment to determine employment opportunities for the targeted population; the technical, intrapersonal, and interpersonal skills needed; beneficiaries’ skill gaps; and private and public sector training opportunities.
Based on the labor market assessment and review of relevant cost-effectiveness systematic reviews, the project will pay for time-limited skill training, prioritizing youth, especially members of extremely poor households, to prepare recipients for formal employment opportunities. Skills training will incorporate appropriate youth-focused sectors (e.g., ICT, mobile repair, carpentry, boat repair, construction) and gender-sensitive training practices. The project will organize need-based technical training to improve knowledge and skills for participants to 1) enhance income-generating opportunities, 2) link project participants to market systems, and 3) establish access to government and private sector service providers for both on- and off-farm IGAs. Targeted participants will receive business plan development and business management training, as well as mentoring and support in group coaching sessions. In addition to business plan development training, graduation participants will receive technical training in their chosen business(es). The participants will receive cash assistance and cash for productive assets to establish ventures based on approved business plans. Participants will receive soft skills training on leadership, communication, social cohesion, disability inclusion awareness, and GBV. The project will focus on connecting participants with local traders, value chain actors, and private sectors to ensure access to sustainable livelihoods and income-generating activities. The project will coordinate with other humanitarian actors (e.g. UNDP, UNICEF, UNHCR) to identify areas of synergy and leverage points and to design specific interventions to reduce long-term disaster risks and strengthen resilience to climate, conflict, and other hazards for vulnerable communities.
The project shall identify and select participants from the most vulnerable individuals from vulnerable women and youth, at least 3% persons with disabilities, marginalized groups, households with LGBTQI+, and other disadvantaged groups through community consultations. The project participant’s data will be segregated maintaining SAAD criteria and the outcome and impact of the project will be assessed following OECD criteria. The project will ensure rigorous coordination with existing actors in the project intervention areas to avoid any duplication in project participant selection and response. FIVDB aims to include 60% of women as project participants in the proposed program and from overall beneficiaries. It will work in close coordination and collaboration with all the relevant sectors including livelihood and skill development, energy and environment, etc., NGO platform, local government and all other relevant stakeholders. Community engagement has always remained the key priority of FIVDB and for this project also community participation will be ensured from design to the implementation phase. The overall program will aim to address gender inequality by increasing women’s ability to influence decisions and equipping them with knowledge and confidence to access government and private services."

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

"The host communities in Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh, are grappling with significant challenges related to livelihoods and skill development, particularly in the context of the Rohingya refugee crisis. This situation has intensified competition for resources and job opportunities, compounded by frequent natural disasters, economic instability, and rising prices. 61% of households rely on loans or support from family and friends as their primary income source, while only 34% derive income from their own production and 31% from business activities. Alarmingly, 29% of households report a lack of nearby income opportunities highlighting critical gap in available work. Gender disparities are evident, as households with female members engaged in income-generating activities show lower participation rates compared to their male counterparts. This reflects cultural and economic barriers that hinder women's investment in skills development. There is an urgent need for targeted skills development programs for women to enhance their labor market participation and stabilize household incomes. The ILO Skills Demand and Supply Assessment (2024) highlights that a lack of necessary skills, work experience, or qualifications among workers is a major barrier to hiring. It identifies 49 hard-to-fill occupations, including masons, construction, electricians, and hospitality roles like tour guides and front office executives, indicating a pressing need for targeted training programs. Women face social constraints in accessing Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET), leading to lower enrollment rates. The report underscores the necessity for more female trainers to encourage female participation in TVET programs and calls for the development of training programs aligned with industry requirements, particularly in trades with existing skills gaps.In the tourism and hospitality sector, employers report significant gaps in problem-solving skills (60% of surveyed employers), communication skills, and leadership skills .The assessment emphasizes the importance of comprehensive capacity-building initiatives that are gender-sensitive and tailored to the local economy's specific demands. The influx of Rohingya refugees has led to increased competition for jobs, resulting in reduced employment opportunities for local youth. Many engage in informal or casual labor, which often lacks stability and fair wages. There is a scarcity of vocational training programs tailored to young people's needs, exacerbating their difficulties in securing decent employment. Girls are particularly vulnerable, often facing early marriage or pregnancy, which impedes their educational and employment prospects. Cultural norms restrict girls' mobility and access to job opportunities, while gender-based violence and safety concerns hinder young women's ability to seek employment outside their homes.Individuals with disabilities and marginalized groups encounter significant barriers to employment due to discrimination and a lack of inclusive job opportunities. Many rely on negative coping strategies, such as borrowing money or reducing essential expenditures, indicating a lack of sustainable income sources. A substantial portion of the population, including persons with disabilities, depends on humanitarian assistance, with 18% of households in the Baharchara Union reporting receiving such support. Furthermore, 51% of households with at least one disabled member show signs of psychosocial distress, which can further hinder employment prospects and integration into the workforce (JMSNA, 2023). Access to education is critical for employment opportunities; however, households with disabilities face barriers to education, impacting their employability. Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (JMSNA), 2023ILO Skills Demand and Supply Assessment, 2024"

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project shall prioritize the safety and dignity of the affected population by conducting a thorough analysis of the protection context within the community. This includes identifying vulnerabilities and risks faced by different groups, particularly those most at risk, such as women, children, and individuals with disabilities. By utilizing participatory assessments, the project will gather input from affected populations to understand their specific needs and contexts. The analysis shall guide the project intervention strategies to minimize harm and enhance the well-being of the targeted participants. The project shall tailor outreach strategies to reach the most disadvantaged individuals including female-headed households, disadvantaged women, vulnerable youth groups, persons with disabilities, gender-diverse groups, and marginalized populations. The project shall ensure that information about available services is widely disseminated and accessible in easier and more appropriate languages and mediums. Community engagement will be integral to the approach, as the project will involve local stakeholders in decision-making processes to foster trust and ensure that the interventions are relevant and effective. A safe, accessible, and functional CFRM will be established where 10% of randomly selected beneficiaries who have given consent will be surveyed to identify their overall satisfaction with the input provided. The project shall regularly monitor and evaluate activities, incorporating feedback to adapt and improve the services. Training on protection principles adhering to humanitarian standards and existing services in project intervention areas will be ensured for all staff. The project shall rigorously collaborate with relevant actors to enhance protection, avoid duplication, and address risks.

2.3 - Age & Disability

The project will prioritize the inclusion of older persons and persons with disabilities by adhering to the Humanitarian Inclusion Standards for Older People and Persons with Disabilities, as well as the IASC Guidelines on Inclusion. FIVDB will implement targeted programming that aims to engage at least 3% of Persons with disabilities, ensuring their specific needs are addressed. The project will incorporate the Washington Group Questions (WGQ) enhanced set for effective identification and referral of Persons with disabilities and will conduct regular accessibility audits to identify barriers in facilities and service points. Additionally, the project will provide training for staff and volunteers on inclusive programming, ensuring that all information regarding the project is accessible and that Persons with disabilities are actively involved in project activities. The project shall ensure necessary adjustments in the intervention modalities based on the specific needs of the identified persons with disabilities to ensure an inclusive program. Where possible the project shall provide porter services to people with specialized needs for carrying assistance and priority queuing, as needed and provide door-to-door service for women and girls with disabilities. Alongside the project will ensure accessible complaints and feedback mechanisms in all facilities. By integrating these elements, the project aims to empower Persons with disabilities and older persons, facilitating their meaningful participation in all project phases. The project shall collect and provide gender, Sex, Age, and Disability segregated data of all project participants and extremely vulnerable individuals as identified by each sector. The project shall ensure accessible information during awareness-raising sessions.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project is designed based on the findings from REVA 7, ILO market demand and supply analysis, JMSNA 2023, and other relevant assessments to accurately reflect the needs and priorities of the targeted communities. The project shall ensure regular consultations with affected populations to explain the beneficiary selection criteria, ensuring transparency and inclusivity. By involving community members in evaluations and sharing results in accessible formats, the project aims to foster a sense of ownership and trust between the project and the communities. All the assistance will be tailored based on the priorities and needs of the targeted participants followed by relevant assessments engaging affected communities. Implementing effective Complaint and Feedback Response Mechanisms (CFRMs) to gather feedback from beneficiaries regarding the adequacy and quality of the humanitarian response is a strategy of the project. Regular monitoring will be conducted to assess community perceptions, utilizing existing CFRM and relevant assessments. These insights will guide project adjustments and improvements, ensuring that the voices of marginalized groups—especially women, youth, gender diverse groups, and persons with disabilities—are addressed. This approach will not only enhance the project's responsiveness but also promote community empowerment. Furthermore, the project will ensure that all demographic groups are included in AAP planning and implementation prioritizing the specific needs of vulnerable women and youth, older persons, persons with disabilities, gender-diverse groups, and marginalized populations to ensure their representation in decision-making processes. By providing information and updates about changes in services or assistance in clear and understandable formats, the project will maintain transparency and encourage ongoing dialogue with the communities. All IEC will be available in English, Bangla, and Burmese transcription in accessible formats.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

"FIVDB is an active member of the PSEA network and a designated PSEA focal shall maintain regular coordination and work in collaboration with the network. The organization has an active PSEA policy and all staff must sign the PSEA code of conduct of FIVDB which is aligned with IASC core principles. All staff, contractors, and vendors shall receive mandatory training on the PSEA, gender, Safeguarding, GBV, and all relevant policies of FIVDB to maintain a safe and inclusive environment. This includes mandatory reporting protocols, ensuring that every allegation, regardless of its origin, is communicated to the UN Resident Coordinator. Regular SEAH risk assessments will be conducted by the project through community engagement.All Identified risks shall be addressed with the technical support of the PSEA network.The project shall regularly raise awareness among all project participants regarding PSEA, CFRM, and reporting procedures through accessible and understandable culturally appropriate IEC materials, and awareness sessions, followed by community consultation. All the facilities will be installed with visible and accessible complaint and feedback boxes followed by community consultation. The complaints and feedback will be responded to and followed up regularly ensuring community engagement. The project will conduct periodic assessments of the functionality of the complaint and feedback mechanism system engaging beneficiaries and community people. FIVDB will take necessary actions regarding SEA complaints that are in line with the SOP for the SEA complaint referral. FIVDB has a dedicated gender cell to deal with all SEA complaints and take necessary actions. Regular monitoring of PSEA practices and functionality of CFRM will be ensured based on a developed checklist including PSEA indicators. The project focal shall work to create and maintain an environment that prevents SEA. PSEA will be a standing agenda at all project coordination meetings. "

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The program is designed to equip communities with knowledge and skills for natural disasters and monsoon seasons, available DRR services by disseminating messages, and involving community members in response efforts. It also facilitates staff and outreach workers' effective participation in disaster response by providing information on damage and needs assessments, as well as social mobilization interventions, disaster risk reduction (DRR), and emergency preparedness. Moreover, the project adopts an integrated, cross-sectoral approach that recognizes the links between WASH (water, sanitation, and hygiene), site management, and DRR committee in natural resource management, all of which contribute to overall community resilience. FIVDB will ensure effective coordination among relevant government entities (DRRO, RRRC, CPP), ISCG’s operational partners, and sectors, along with the Energy & Environment Network (EEN). Alongside this, the project will coordinate with other humanitarian and development actors including camp DRR committees to ensure that climate adaptation and mitigation strategies are effectively integrated into all interventions. The project will refrain from any construction that could harm the environment and will discourage the use of plastic. All project staff will receive orientation on proper waste disposal and waste management practices. The project will conduct a disaster vulnerability assessment during facility selection and shall equip facilities with safety materials including fire extinguishers. FIDVB’s own DRR contingency plan aligned with ISCG’s guidelines for DRR will be adopted in the project. The project training modules will include Early warning system , DRR and environmental issues for project participants to improve their knowledge regarding DRR and other environmental issues

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

"Yes. The project emphasizes improving the ability of host and impacted communities to mitigate, adapt to, and recover from shocks. This includes establishing community DRR plans, which typically encompass strategies for responding to emergencies. The project includes components for monitoring and learning, which are crucial for adapting strategies based on evolving risks and the effectiveness of response measures. The EPR plans will be aligned with ISCG’s Mult Hazard Response Plan 2022, Standard Operating Procedures (e.g. cyclones and Fire SOP), National Anticipatory action protocols, and National Plans for Disaster Management (2021-2025). The project addresses the importance of coordination with humanitarian organizations to leverage resources and expertise in emergencies. During the project implementation, FIVDB will adhere to Sector-specific contingency and business continuity plans, EPR plans, and the EPR WG work plan to ensure effective coordination with Sectors, EPR WG members, and ISCG’s EPR focal point to address potential issues. FIVDB will ensure membership in the EPR WG to harmonize approaches and build synergy with overall EPR mechanisms. "

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes. The project will conduct labor market assessments to identify employment opportunities and the necessary skills for youth aged 18-24. It will prioritize youth from extremely poor households for tailored training programs that prepare them for formal job markets. The initiative will explore apprenticeships with local businesses, providing hands-on experience and mentorship to enhance employability. Youth will receive support for self-employment and entrepreneurship through training in business skills, including financial literacy and management. This will help them establish their own ventures and improve their income-generating potential. The project will incorporate gender-sensitive training practices to ensure equitable access to opportunities for both young men and women, addressing specific challenges faced by young women in the region. Continuous monitoring and mentoring will be provided to ensure that youth participants are progressing and receiving the support they need to succeed in their training and entrepreneurial efforts. Through these strategies, the project aims to equip youth with the skills and resources necessary to improve their livelihoods and contribute positively to their communities, ultimately enhancing their resilience in the face of economic and environmental challenges. The project shall apply IASC Youth Guidelines to ensure that the project is youth-responsive. Overall, the project shall create participatory approaches that actively engage youth in program design, implementation, and monitoring, ensuring their voices are heard. The project shall emphasize on age and culture-appropriate youth engagement strategies to ensure a more responsive program for targeted youth.
