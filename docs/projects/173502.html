Project Summary

This 12 months project is designed to address the child protection strategic objective 3: "Conflict-affected boys, girls and young people benefit from strengthened community-based services and prevention actions including reintegration services" targeting 260 (150 girls, 110 boys) formerly associated with the Non state Arm group in Selected IDP camps and Host community in Borno State. These group of Children and other conflict affected children will be provided with quality case management support through Identification and documentation, Placement in interim care, Follow-up of cases, Tracing and Reunification with primary caregivers and Referral and support to access basic services. The project also target to provide 3000 children (1700 girls and 1300 boys) through structured psychosocial support in terms of recreational/creative/social activities for girls and boys particularly for those under 13 years of age link with Life skills education for adolescent girls and boys and Individual and group counselling and mental health support for girls and boys affected by psychosocial and mental distress.
Another component of the project includes advocacy and sensitisation for ending any form abuses and violence against children with the influencer community base stakeholders and persons of concern to ensure children are accepted by the community and family.

1 - Needs

Girls and boys in north-east Nigeria continue to face multiple protection risks and violations of their rights. The threats and risks posed by these protection risks have been exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic which has not only negatively affected delivery of life-saving child protection services but has also weakened the capacities of families and communities to care for children. The COVID-19 pandemic highlighted the situation of children living without parental care, especially those for boys who had been sent away by their families for religious education purposes as well as the situation of children on the streets. There is a strong need to build the capacity of child protection actors, particularly NNGOs, in providing family tracing and reunification services.More than 6,000 children (more than 50 per cent girls) are estimated to have been associated with armed groups, abducted, killed or victims of violent attacks by armed groups since the beginning of the conflict.The need for strengthening community-based mechanisms for child protection has been strongly highlighted by the COVID-19 pandemic. At least 1.4 million of the children in need have very limited or lack access to core child protection services (27 per cent in Adamawa, 55 per cent in Borno and 18 per cent in Yobe). A strategic approach is required to make child protection services more available at the community level whilst building on and strengthening the community capacities for take prevention and response actions for children’s protection.
