Project Summary

The project seeks to continue providing comprehensive primary health care services through 18 health facilities, mental health and psychosocial support services through 10 stand-alone MHPSS centers and integration within health facilities and services for key populations through 4 Drop-in centers. Registration of stand-alone MHPSS centers is on- going while the medium to long term strategy is to integrate the centers within protection services, in coordination with relevant sectors.
The project will adhere to the Minimum Package of Essential Health Service for Primary Health Centers in the Rohingya/FDMN Camps with measures put in place to support partners. This will include enhanced monitoring by Camp Health Focal Points, feedback with partners through monthly partner meetings and resources to fill gaps identified in 2021 e.g., back-up doctors and equipment.
Integration of health and nutrition services in some facilities will be introduced in coordination with health and nutrition sectors. The project will continue close collaboration with WASH, Protection, GBV, and other relevant sectors.
The project will provide inpatient mental health services through 3 facilities, 2 of which will be located within the camps (Ukhiya and Teknaf). Health facilities will provide Sexual and Reproductive Health services to women, adolescent and boys, access to HIV prevention and referral for treatment and clinical management of rape. Disease surveillance, preparedness and response to disease outbreaks and disasters will be ensured through an active trained and equipped community-based health workforce, stockpiles of emergency medical supplies and support to Emergency Medical Teams.
To support COVID-19 outbreak response, the project will support two SARI ITCs, one quarantine centre and support COVID-19 vaccination of refugees as per the Government of Bangladesh guidelines. Partial support will be provided to the Sadar ICU for part of 2022 as the main support currently provided will end in 2021.
In line with Health Sector’s Accountability to Affected People (AAP) Framework and Protection principles, the project will ensure refugees and host communities have access to timely, accurate and relevant information on their roles, entitlement, rights and feedback and complaint mechanisms. This includes sharing information in local language, holding regular health facility committee meetings etc., with aim to ensure and monitor meaningful engagement and consultation of the community on assistance.
Data collection through a robust reporting system including use of the integrated Refugee Health Information System (irHIS), and coordination with the MoHFW and Health Sector reporting requirements will be maintained including DHIS2, 4Ws, EWARS etc. Data will be disaggregated by age and gender based on the age categories of the Health sector and the UNHCR Health Information System.
The project will maintain active participation in Health sector coordination and working groups (including CHWG, MHPSS, SRH, CMWG, EpiWG etc) and aims to support in strategic decisions in health programming.
Referrals of patients for secondary healthcare services will continue, facilitated through ambulances and staffing. UNHCR will cover emergency referral costs to secondary and tertiary level for 50% of the camps.
The project will increase access to secondary healthcare for refugees and host community through completion and equipping of the Sadar Hospital outpatient department (OPD) complex and establishment of the Ukhiya Specialized hospital in partnership with MoH&FW/DGHS and MoDMR/RRRC. This specialized hospital will provide out-patient Dental, Ophthalmology, Ear, Nose and Throat, Orthopaedic, Physiotherapy, NCD management, telemedicine and emergency services supported by bio-chemical, haematology and radiological diagnostic services. UNHCR will coordinate with partners through the Health Sector on this hospital.

1 - Identified Needs

The health status of the population remains stable with a Crude mortality rate of 0.32/1000/month and an Under 5 mortality rate of 0.5/1000/month in UNHCR’s area of operation. The population continues to be faced with crowded living conditions and threats to disease outbreaks and seasonal emergencies. In 2021, COVID-19 continued to affect the community, with two surges of the pandemic resulting in a higher number of cases seen this year in comparison to 2020. In the UNHCR supported COVID-19 facilities (2 SARI ITCs and one ICU/HDU/Severe ward), over 4,700 patients with probable or confirmed COVID-19, or in need of critical medical care have been managed since the beginning of the pandemic in 2020. There is need to continue operating the SARI ITCs in 2022 as infections of COVID-19 continue to be seen. Cases of cholera, dengue fever and diphtheria seen in 2021 within the camps, indicate the need to strengthen disease surveillance, prevention and outbreak management in the camps. The UNHCR supported health facilities attended to over 450,000 primary healthcare consultations between January and September 2021 while the network of over 1400 community health workers that UNHCR coordinates through the Community Health Working Group referred over 850,000 individuals from the community to various services. Through the health sector mapping of health facilities, the gaps in health facilities need to be addressed to ensure that health facilities are accessible to the persons of concern, while maintaining rationalization of facilities. Sexual and reproductive health indicators all remain below the standard for the refugee population, with less than 90% of pregnant women receiving at least four antenatal visits in a pregnancy and less than 80% of deliveries occurring at health facilities. Maternal deaths continue being a scourge throughout the camps, with the continued need to improve access to Basic and Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal care. Access to mental health services, especially clinical care needs to be strengthened. Between January and September 2021, mental health specialists provided 7,476 mental health clinical and psychosocial out-patient consultations. There is no access to inpatient mental healthcare services for the refugees and surrounding host community, while community based mental health and psychosocial support services need to be expanded. As of mid-2021, 2,791 patients had been referred for secondary care to facilities outside the camps. The limitation to access to secondary care means that patients wait for long periods while some do not get the necessary care. There is need to cost-effectively expand access to secondary health services for the refugees and surrounding host community through national health services. According to the 2020 Rapid Gender Analysis conducted by ISCG Gender Hub, CARE, Oxfam, ACAPS-NPM and UN Women, increased difficulties accessing health services have been reported during the pandemic, with increased feelings of distrust, dissatisfaction and misinformation. Women, children, transgender persons and persons with disabilities all face more barriers in accessing health services and notable reduction in access to crucial services such as sexual, reproductive and maternal health services. Containment measures have also contributed to disrupted access and use of these services especially for women and girls. An increase in mental health issues in particular among men and boys was noted. Access to vaccination services was noted to be lower amongst women during the first round of COVID-19 vaccination campaign.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1- Provide comprehensive primary health care services in line with the minimum essential service package and tailored as necessary to the specific needs of people of all age groups and persons with disabilities, LGBTIQ and people who sell or exchange sex. Integrate mental health, through MHGAP trained doctors, and nutrition in primary health services.Activity 2- Promotion of access to rehabilitative and palliative care for people of all age groups, including those with disabilities.Activity 3- Access to promotive, preventive healthcare; Provide Information Education and Communication materials; Capacity building of Community Health Workforce; Provide sufficient coverage of Community Health Workers.Activity 4- Provision of Essential medicines and medical supplies; capacity building of staff; Data collection for surveillance; Implement the integrated refugee Health information System as an upgrade to the HIS.Activity 5- Provide secondary healthcare services through equipping, staffing, providing medicines and medical supplies in Ukhiya Specialized Hospital. Maintain 10 ambulances for emergency medical referrals. Support patients with translation, obtaining services and payment of hospital referral charges. Support life-saving emergency referrals for non-UNHCR partners in UNHCR’s area of responsibilityActivity 6- Provide Sexual and Reproductive Health and HIV Services to women, adolescents and men through health facilities, and education on SRHR at community level. Provision of Clinical Management of rape for survivors and follow the referral pathway to protection, legal and psychosocial services. Provision of SRH, HIV, and psychosocial services to key populations through Drop-In Centres. SRHR services provided at 12 facilities (including 4 DICs).Activity 7- Maintain psychologists and psychosocial counsellors at health facilities and community-based points of access including community centres and client homes. Supervise and monitor volunteers of health and protection partners to assist in identification and referral of refugees with mental health needs. Implement the community para-counselor modality.Activity 8- Enhance prevention and treatment of COVID-19: Provide treatment for refugees and host community through 2 SARI ITCs. Maintain one quarantine facility for contacts and new arrivals. Community engagement and awareness raising, community-based surveillance, and patient referral.Activity 9- Support management of major public health risks including outbreak & disaster preparedness & response: Support emergency response during outbreak through enhancing community awareness, early case identification, case management and referral through public health messaging by CHWs and mobile medical teams. PFA and first aid by CHWs, camp wide referral through ambulances to functioning facilities. Maintain stocks for emergency response. Support outbreak prevention through community-based surveillance.Activity 10- Support Health Sector (HS) coordination and information management: Continue contribution and engagement in HS (coordination, strategic advisory group and working groups). In support of the HS and in close cooperation with relevant partners, chair and co-chair Community Health and MHPSS working group (depending on annual election outcome). UNHCR's coordination role of the CHWG would include ensuring the over 1400 CHWs are trained as per the CHWG core curriculum and assure sufficient coverage of CHWs. UNHCR would ensure that the community-based surveillance system is functional and data and reports are available. UNHCR will ensure through this CHWG coordination role that required information, education and communication materials are available within the community. Other activities that require the support of CHWs e.g. supplementary immunization activities will be coordinated through the CHWG. In support of the HS, strengthen field level health coordination through dedicated CHFPS (camp health focal points).
