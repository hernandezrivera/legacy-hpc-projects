Project Summary

The project aims at enhancing the food security of affected communities through provision of live-saving agricultural inputs and livestock support to 176,075 individuals, of which 54,583 are women, 51,062 are men, 36,76 are girls and 33,454 are boys. The project is targeting affected host resident communities, returnees and IDP in Blue Nile, South Darfur, East Darfur and South Kordofan states of Sudan, distributed in 18 localities: Arusarys, Edmazain, Geisan, Baw and Alkurmuk in Blue Nile, Abu Karinka, Eldayin and Bahr Al Arab in East Darfur, Beleil, Kas, Mershing, Nyala Janoub and Nyala Shamal in South Darfur and Abu Jybaha, Abu Karshola, Arshad, Diling and Kadugli in South Kordofan.
The support will include agricultural inputs (cereals seeds, hand tools and trainings). 69,250 individuals will be supported with cereals seeds, the kit/HH will include 3kg of improved millet seed, 9kg of improved cereals seeds and 22kg of unshelled groundnut seeds. Another 55,400 individuals will be supported with a kit of hand tools (donkey ploughs, rake, hoe), communities will be consulted on needed tools and HHs will select either the seed kit or the tools kit.
Additionally, livestock services support will be given to 51,425 vulnerable individuals, they will be supported with vaccination of their livestock against common diseases, in the same locations.
Other -unbudgeted- activities aim at building the resilience of same beneficiaries can be summarized as follows:
The resilience of 124,650 individuals toward climate change adverse consequence will be enhanced through providing training on Climate smart agricultural techniques and Famer Managed Natural Regeneration (FMNR), the approach of FMNR which is very successful in many countries in Africa like Kenya and Somalia will be introduced to Sudan and will be piloted in some selected areas that suites this approach.
The project will also support natural regeneration of pastureland through scattering of pasture seeds in some selected area nearby or within the traditional animal routes, in close collaboration with local Ministry of Agriculture in the 4 states, involving the communities of such area (farmers and animal breeders) in the activity, to increase community's ownership of their natural resources and participate on building peaceful co-existence between these 2 groups that frequently conflicts over the limited natural resources.

1 - Needs

Acute food insecurity in Sudan is escalating rapidly as a result of sharp rises in the cost of food and other commodities, low harvest, and ongoing conflict crises. According to the latest IPC analysis, up to 11.7 million people will be in a crisis (IPC Phase 3 or worse) levels of food insecurity. This number includes 8.6 million people in IPC Phase 3 (Crisis) and 3.1 million in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency). Particularly in the states of Darfur and Kordofan and in eastern Sudan, a spike in localized conflicts caused population displacements, which, when combined with the economy's decline, increased levels of acute food insecurity. Gender inequality has further increased and impacts the overall food insecurity situation, as women and girls have significantly lost control of and access to vital productive resources (land, livestock, finances, farm inputs). Traditional sources of livelihoods for women, such as farming and animal husbandry, have been depleted as they have been forced to sell productive assets such as livestock, goats, sheep and farm tools, which has increased their vulnerability to recurrent shocks of floods, conflicts and dry spells. Cultivation is now at very low levels as hunger makes people weak and sick, leading to increased vulnerability and reliance on relief aid. Negative coping mechanisms for women and girls include reducing their food intake and selling productive household assets. The main positive mechanism is collecting firewood for sale. However, this has led to a double burden of work on women and girls, as they have become more engaged in tedious household work (fetching water, collecting firewood for sale, taking care of children) besides maintaining the household budget. (SDN HNO 2023).WVI areas of operation includes Darfur, Kordofan and Blue Nile. These states are centers of conflicts in Sudan. In 2023, 50% of needy people are in these states (HNO). The project will respond to these needs through provision of live-saving agricultural inputs, to increase access of these communities to food and improve their food security. Majority of farmers in the 4 operation areas lacks enough knowledge on adaptation to climate change and institutions like MoA lacks enough resources to provide extension service addressing such problems, leaving farmers unequipped in face of these serious consequences. Moreover, the climate changes consequences like drought create and aggravate the tensions between farmers and animal breeders due to increase competition over the diminishing limited resources. The macroeconomic factors, climate change, conflicts and the prevailing vulnerability among farmers and animal breeders in the 4 states had impacted communities and withdraw them form any means to stand these factors or be prepared for any future crisis

2 - Activities/Outputs

Act.1 Provide lifesaving agricultural inputs and trainings for vulnerable farmers to enhance their access to food. Cereals seeds (improved seed of sorghum and millet) and cash crop groundnut will be provided to 69,250 individuals (including 21,468 women, 20,082 men, 14,443 girls and 13,157 boys) in 18 localities in the 4 targeted states, including 3 localities in East Darfur and 5 localities in each of Blue Nile, South Darfur and South Kordofan. The kit/beneficiary HH is composed of 9kg of sorghum, 3 kg of millet and 22 kg of unshelled groundnut.Agricultural hand tools in form of Donkey ploughs, hoes, rakes, etc. will be provided to additional 55,400 individuals (including 17,174 women, 16,066 men, 11,634 girls and 10,526 boys) in the same localities, making the total individuals for agricultural inputs 124,650 HH. The targeted HH will choose either the seed kit or the tool kits, depending on their currents needs for such inputs. The communities will be consulted for the suitable type of tools, while technical consideration will determine the best variety of cereals for each locality. The selection of beneficiaries will be done in FGDs with communities in their villages, HH will be selected depending on their vulnerability, including HH headed by women, HH with PWD, HH lacking any sources of income, additionally for this agricultural support, HHs need to have land for cultivation (owned, borrowed, etc.). All beneficiaries will get trainings on Technical Package of cultivation of improved seeds, 3 training will be provided, 1 at distribution site and 2 at selected farms inside villages in all localities, to correctly cultivate their cereals and ensure high harvest's potential of improved seeds and no negative impact on environment Act.2: 51,425 individual's animal breeders supported with livestock treatments.Due to pastoralist vulnerable households having very low access to veterinary services, making their herd subject to common diseases and endanger their livelihood, WV will provide urgent vaccination services for vulnerable people depends on raising animals for living, to 51,425 individuals, including 15,942 women, 14,913 men, 10,800 girls and 9,769 boys. In collaboration with Animal resources department of local Ministry of Agriculture (MoA), vaccination campaigns with be conducted with the goal of vaccinating livestock against common diseases in the 18 localities, 4 days of vaccinations will be done in each locality. Expected for each campaign to vaccinate 1000 animal per day, total animal to be vaccinated is expected to be 72,000 animals. The vaccination will depend on availability of vaccines at the ministry. Other Not budgeted activities: Act.3 Provide trainings on climate smart agricultural techniques and FMNR (farmer managed natural regeneration) to vulnerable HH, to build their resilience in face of climate change consequences.the seed and tool beneficiaries (24,930 HH) will be trained on climate smart agricultural techniques training, tailored to each state. The training on FMNR will be provided in some selected areas that suites such technique. Act.4 Pasture restoration through scattering of pasture seeds in areas used to be full of such plants but not anymore.In collaboration with MoA and at some segment of the traditional animal routes of migration, the project will scatter seeds of natural vegetation that used to be in area in the past and disappeared due to many factors, including overgrazing and climate change. the intervention aims as bring back natural pasture and hence support animal breeders' livelihoods through support animal feed, while participating at reducing tensions between farmers and animal breeders over limited land and water resources at/nearby animal routes

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

the 4 states suffered from deteriorated production per unit area for farmers over years for many reasons, including traditional cultivation methods that reduces soil fertility and climate change. The intervention is designed to increase farmers and animal breeder skills when dealing with their natural resources (land and water). The provided improved seeds are one way of adapting to climate change, as short maturing and drought-resistance varieties of seeds will be provided in areas will drought or /and frequent dry spells. The provided inputs for cultivation will be supported with technical trainings that will teach farmers the optimal use of inputs to eliminate any potential negative impact on environment. while the vaccination of animal will positively impact pastoralist and their livestock. The scattering of pasture seeds activity will help restore natural pastureland and conserve environment at animal migration areas of the 4 states while the trainings on FMNR and climate smart agric techniques will equip farmers with new advance knowledge that help conserve natural resources and bring sustainable positive environment management

6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The communities need assessments had been fed in the design of the project, where main concerns for access and safety of community members is considered, the selection criteria that includes vulnerability and other criteria has been set to ensure fair access of all community members to project benefits. The project has set women heading HH as one of vulnerability criteria for selecting beneficiaries, as known from need assessments. In order to make clear and ensure the safety of targeted beneficiaries, during implementation, the selection criteria will be explained to all community members and the special targeting of women will be explained to male members of the community, to ensure that women selected for support are well known and that they can safely access their planned benefits. team will use all possible means (observations / prior experience with the community/complains from women, etc.) to monitor the situation and listen to any concerns that might come from women and respond to that..The nomination of the beneficiaries will be through the FGDs, where all community members will nominate who have the selection criteria and nominated HH will be well known in the community, so no one can assume the personality of other members, also the inputs distribution will be done for the direct beneficiaries, and she/he need to sign/fingerprint for receiving his/her assistance.The project team will also make use of any positive local wisdom, in order to ensure no harm for most vulnerable groups, like PWD and womenthe distribution sites will be selected to do-no-harm to women and children, by ensuring safe and easy access of them to such places ( in Centre of villages, at appropriate time of the day)

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The organization has put accountability to affected people as top priority. This is done through it structures, as the organization Senior Leadership Team (SLT) is directly responsible of ensure that the set processes and measure are in place for the entire organization. The MEAL department is following directly this through well-established team, headed by MEAL manager at National Office level and supported by MEAL officers in each base of operation, each base of operation has a focal point that coordinate compliance to accountability procedures/measures. A technical advisor for child protection and safeguarding is part of senior management team that ensure the respect of/adherence to AAP principle across all programs and sectors. There are many mechanisms for community feedback in place, including Suggestion boxes, hotline for support, help desks and some community-based structures, including different associations. The most use mechanism will be Through complaint boxes, which is well established within WVI in Sudan, A complaint box is put in each WVI office in all field locations and communities are informed about such boxes and they can drop their complaint there or handed over to any team member who will drop in the box after. Every complain will be responded to, and feedback will be provided appropriately to the concerned person/community if the complaint is anonymous. the same boxes are present in WV facilities, like health facilities and water points, and each complain in such boxes will be directed to the concerned department (FSL, WASH, etc.) and a feedback is provided accordingly
