Project Summary

The humanitarian crisis triggered by the appearance of the so-called Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant (ISIL) in 2014, and the subsequent military operations by the Iraqi government, forced the displacement of over 5 million persons within the territory of Iraq. Since then, IOM has been an active member of the humanitarian community in supporting the Government of Iraq in responding to the crisis within, among others, the shelter, non-food items and camp coordination and camp management sectors.
In 2019 IOM aims at building the capacity of national actors for preparedness and response to crises while, at the same time, providing direct assistance through, among others, camp coordination and camp management programme activities facilitating the coordination of provision of services to affected populations through:
• Camp Management Operations
• Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) Cluster Coordination Support
• Area-Based Approach through Community Resource Centres (CRCs)
• Capacity Building of National Actors
Through each of these interventions, IOM seeks to ensure meaningful participation of local actors and camp residents to strengthen their ability to manage (formal and informal) displacement sites without direct support from IOM or other international actors.
IOM developed its CCCM response for 2019 based on the analysis of the current displacement and return trends, as well as the plans of Iraqi authorities regarding camp consolidation and camp closure. Furthermore, IOM has taken into consideration the intentions expressed by Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) in and out of camps in reference to their plans for return, local integration or in some cases extended displacement in camps or camp-like settings.

Needs

The latest IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) report indicates the number of people still displaced in Iraq is 1.9 million, 54% of them have been displaced for over 3 years. The CCCM Cluster estimates that throughout 2019 the remaining caseload living in camps will be 416,500 individuals, in addition to estimated 116,000 who are displaced in collective centres and informal sites. It is estimated that 51% of these populations are women and overall, 51% are children. Over 4 million people have already returned to their areas of origins, this trend, however, has recently been decreasing. The majority of the remaining IDPs displaced in informal sites or urban settings (81%) do not plan to return within the next year. The large majority of IDPs in camps (78%) also do not want to return within the next year. The IOM Return Index reports that 41% of IDPs are not returning due to their house being destroyed or damaged, while 29% point at insecurity in their areas of origin as a reason not to return (REACH Intention Survey – August 2018). Other factors that prevent IDPs from returning include access to livelihoods and services and existing tensions in their community of origin. Factors of vulnerability such as female-headed or minor-headed households, low socio-economic income, and mental health issues contribute to explaining the low intention of return, making likely that these cases will stay in protracted displacement. In Anbar governorate, 32,398 IDPs reside in 45 government-managed sub camps in Ameriyat Al Falluja-AAF (31) and Bezabize-Bzbz (14) camps. Only 21% of IDPs residing in camps in Anbar expressed intentions to return to their areas of origin. Obstacles differ significantly in Anbar, and the destruction or damage of their homes, the absence of financial means to return, lack of livelihood of opportunities or fear of discrimination in location of origin are mentioned as the main obstacles to return. Camps in Anbar are under risk of eviction by the Government, and strict restrictions of movement outside camps, put in place since late August by the armed forces, are increasing the vulnerability of the residents putting obstacles to their access to services and livelihoods. The general infrastructure of the camps need upgrade and maintenance, along with the need to consolidate some of them due to low occupancy rate in relation with the number of space available. This is necessary to increase cost-effectiveness in the use of resources. In Baghdad 1,493 IDPs reside in Al Ahel, Al-Nabi Younis and Zeyona camps also, like in Anbar, under threat of eviction and are in need of support to strengthen their camp management skills, camp governance structure and are also in need of large care and maintenance activities in order to ameliorate their conditions of living. Only 1% of IDPs residing in Baghdad expressed intention of return to their areas of origin within the next 12 months. They point as main reason for not return the destruction of their homes, absence of livelihood opportunities and of financial means. In Ninewa Governorate 71,129 IDPs reside in Haj Ali and Jad’ah camps. The former is managed directly by IOM and the latter by RNVDO. Only 11% of IDPs in Ninewa expressed intention to return to their areas of origin in the next 12 months. This is mainly due to the destruction of their homes, absence of livelihood opportunities and financial means. The large population residing in the above-mentioned camp are at risk of protracted displacement, which will require a longer-term exit strategy, and the strengthening of their resilience and governance structures. Major upgrades of infrastructure are need along with coordination of service provision and monitoring of gaps, need for referrals and case management. Other locations in Ninewa like West Mosul and Sinjar Mountain or in Tikrit, Beiji and Samaraa in Salah Al-Din, have IDPs living in informal settlements, which, by its own nature, have limited access to services and presence of humanitarian actors. These settlements are in dire need of mobile camp management assistance to address the most needed issues related to risk, access to some services like electricity and information regarding service providers. Finally, In Salah al-Din, which hosts 156,258 IDPS. Only 9% of the population residing in camps expressed intention to return to their places of origin in the next 12 months. The principal obstacles to return are lack of finance, having their homes destroyed or damaged or fear/trauma associated with returning. A large population displaced in SAD live in informal settlements with low access to services, need for mobile camp management assistance, and support for risk reduction activities and access to information concerning service providers.

Activities

1. Direct Camp Management in Haj Ali and Support to RNVDO in Jad’ah and government staff operating formal sites in Anbar and Baghdad as mentioned in previous section. IOM will continue providing direct camp management services in locations where it has been appointed as Camp Management Agency. In addition, IOM will provide material and financial support, on-the-job training and guidance to local camp management actors to ensure the provision of core CCCM services (coordination of partners, mapping needs, community governance, as well as ensuring that all assistance provided is age and gender appropriate); as well as supporting the camp consolidation process. 2. Care and maintenance of camp infrastructure in formal sites mentioned in activity 1. IOM will identify the service gaps in formal sites and provide care and maintenance to mitigate risk and hazards, and to ensure adequate living conditions. In addition, IOM will facilitate access to camp services for girls, boys, women and men and persons with specific needs, for example People with Disabilities, while also considering the different gender and age needs. IOM will mitigate risks of Gender Based Violence (GBV) through safety map exercises to identify risks and needs with relevant local actors and camp residents. When camp consolidation or closure is achieved, if necessary IOM can support camp decommissioning activities. When possible, some of the activities will involve cash for work targeting IDPs unskilled (and sometime skilled) workers, with focus on most vulnerable households, to help them engage in income generating activities. 3. Deployment of Mobile Camp Management in informal settlements: Mobile Camp Management teams will identify informal settlements in Sinjar and West Mosul in Ninewa, in Tikrit, Beiji and Samaraa in Salah Al-Din, and provide CCCM response in IDPs and returnees' informal settlements and the host communities hosting them, prioritizing recently retaken areas. The proposed activities include: the identification and training of site representatives; establishing informal community committees; mapping the gaps and needs, taking into consideration the different age, gender needs and other vulnerabilities and referring them to relevant partners. Other activities include conducting awareness raising sessions and prioritizing interventions, which contribute to mitigate GBV and other related protection incidents; providing maintenance and upgrade of facilities for risk mitigation and improving condition of living, considering the different identified vulnerabilities. 4. Training of camp management agencies and National Actors for Preparedness and Response in Crises. IOM will conduct a Learning Needs Assessment, in coordination with other CCCM partners, to identify capacity-building needs for the Ministry of Migration and Displaced and local actors in Baghdad and other locations covered by this project, and develop sessions in good practices and lessons learnt of, amongst others, Camp Coordination and Camp Management. Support can be provided on other topics according to the needs identified, including the development of community structures and feedback mechanisms, inclusion of camp residents in decision making, information management and registration, referrals to services, and utilization of protection principles such as do no harm principle across all activities involving beneficiaries. 5. Area-based Approach through 3 Community Resource Centres (CRCs), IOM will work at neighborhood level to promote durable solutions and support increased access to essential services for those who are not adequately covered by the social protection system in East and West Mosul, Ninewa and in Fallujah, Anbar. IOM will do so by engaging displacement affected communities in areas of return with a focus on improving access to basic services either directly or indirectly through agencies. Additionally, IOM will seek funding from development donors for longer-term intervention linked with recovery and reintegration.

Protection Risk Analysis

• Armed actors continue to violate the civilian nature of camps through arrest without court orders of individuals, along with restriction of movement limiting the mobility of all IDPs due to perceived affiliation with armed groups; • Pressure on humanitarian actors to share sensitive information on displaced population groups at risk due to perceived affiliation with extremist groups; • Risk of protracted displacement due to continuous obstacles to access durable solutions and reduction of provision of services in camps increasing vulnerabilities overall but specially to people with specific needs such as women, children, youth and people with disabilities; • Continuous risk of sexual or labour exploitation and abuse of women, girls and boys, enhanced by low income and family separation;• In informal settlements, beneficiaries may be at the risk being victim of criminal activities, exploitation due to low income or being identified with minorities that might be at risk; • Risk of eviction by private and government actors.

Mitigation Measures

• Advocacy and sensitization on humanitarian principles and Guiding Principles on Internal Displacement for armed actors and government officials to sensitize on the importance of maintaining the civil character of camps and the need of due process for arrests; • Ensure coordination with Protection partners to refer cases and to train armed actors and officials of government on humanitarian principles and Guiding Principles on Internal Displacement; • Advocacy and sensitization on data protection principles to reduce the constant requests for sensitive information of vulnerable groups; • Advocacy and continuous cooperation with Government officials and humanitarian partners to develop multiple and locally relevant sets of durable solutions including but not limited to return, local integration or resettlement• Community mobilization and strengthen the knowledge and use of information and feedback mechanisms for submission of complaints or request for information by the resident population.• Risk reduction and mitigation activities in informal settlements and community mobilization to increase knowledge of information and feedback mechanisms, knowledge of service providers.• Increase awareness raising on conflict management, gender based violence, early marriage, human trafficking issues, and removal of organs, among others.• Mobilize youth groups on subjects related to peacekeeping, community integration, skills acquisition and creative, productive, team building, cohesion-oriented activities such as sport tournaments, theatre sessions, etc.• Risk reduction and mitigation activities in informal settlements and community mobilization to increase knowledge of information and feedback mechanisms, knowledge of service providers; provision of risk reduction infrastructure like partition for private spaces and security. Facilitation of access infrastructure to people with mobility disability.

How is your project accountable to affected population

• The project intends to create or strengthen complaint and feedback mechanisms to allow affected population to file issues and provide them with adequate and timely feedback through referrals to service providers relevant to the cases submitted. In Haj Ali and Jad’ah camps, complaint and information desks and other tools are already in place, and information technology systems are being tested to enhance the quality and responsiveness of referrals. In Anbar and Baghdad, mechanisms will be created or strengthen to systematize the reception and management of complaint or information requests. Once information systems test phase is finished in Ninewa, IOM will extend its implementation to other locations. This will target mainly formal camps only, while informal settlements will be provided with information on the IDP call centre and service providers contacts in their areas.• Also, IOM will support the creation or strengthening of representative committees that are inclusive of more vulnerable population and ensure their participation at all levels. Participation will be enhanced through on-the-job training of elected representatives to increase transfer or responsibilities and involvement in camp coordination meetings. Specific groups will be mobilized to integrate into interest groups such as Women or Youth groups, but also Education or groups of people with special needs to participate with representatives in the camp coordination meetings and provide with inputs through focus groups discussion sessions. Camp Management Committee members in Haj Ali are expected to be renovated early 2019, mobilizing the entire population to elect leaders per block and then per sector. A block representative and a deputy will be elected per block and sector promoting participation of females as candidates to ensure specific issues are brought forward in the governance structures.• Community mobilization and awareness on sexual exploitation and abuse as well as sensitization of reporting mechanisms will be enhanced and coordinated with protection partners and community leaders, especially Women and Youth groups.• In addition, IOM’s accountability will be strengthened by promoting awareness and access to the IDPs Call Center and by third party monitoring exercises in and out of camps.
