Project Summary

The proposed intervention builds on IRC’s previous and ongoing protection programming in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa states. The intervention will target both IDPs, returnees and host community members through a well-coordinated approach that will foster access to specialized services to prevent and mitigate the harmful effects of violence on women, children and men. This will be achieved through a focus on prevention of harm by seeking to ensure that women, men, boys and girls are safe in their wider communities and receive support when they experience harm and response actions that ensure women, men, boys and girls are protected from the consequences of harm. IRC will provide or facilitate access to safe and dignified specialized services that can help women, men, boys and girls to survive, heal and recover when they experience harm. This will be done by ensuring that:
• Community members have information about laws, their rights and availability of services: Through protection monitoring activities, the IRC will identify and monitor protection trends/risks in order to improve contextual understanding for purposes of ensuring responsive services throughout the action; protection reporting will also be shared with relevant humanitarian actors to support improved humanitarian response and coordination, as well as evidence-driven advocacy. Monitoring teams will also actively share information on rights and service access with households and community focal points to improve access to services; cases in need of response services that are identified during protection monitoring will be supported through a safe referral process, if informed consent to do so is received. Protection monitoring will be conducted through, weekly safety audits with women and girls at the transit center, camps and surrounding communities, gender/age/ability segregated FGDs, site observation and household/community level key informant interviews.
• Community members take individual and collective action: Through community-based protection (CBP), guided by IRC’s new guidance for CBP that sets out a 5-step chronological process to maximise impact and promote sustainability, IRC will involve IDPs and returnees in the prioritization, planning and implementation of activities. This will be primarily achieved by engaging Protection action groups and other community based structures working with other sectors in IRC. To promote a specialized focus on protection risks facing individual segments of the IDP and returnee population, new or existing community groups will each have specific objectives while at the same time working together across groups to ensure holistic and mutually reinforcing actions to advance the protection of IDPs and returnees.
• Services and structures are safe and reduce opportunities for harm: This action will ensure that protection is mainstreamed across all sectors throughout the project period by promoting four key elements of protection mainstreaming: meaningful access to assistance and services; prioritizing safety, dignity and doing no harm; accountability and participation and empowerment of beneficiaries. IRC’s Protection Mainstreaming Monitoring and Evaluation System and global protection cluster resources will guide the projects’ protection mainstreaming interventions. Foundational capacity building activities for project staff are continuously conducted to strengthen and promote a shared understanding of protection mainstreaming, including child safe guarding principles, integrating GBV in humanitarian assistance; and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse. IRC draws from its client responsiveness approach which fosters the effective functioning of feedback and complaints. The approach details systematic and deliberate processes to regularly listen to and collect diverse perspectives of populations of concern, use their feedback to inform programing decisions and explain to clients what actions have been taken in response to their feedback.
Twice a year the IRC conducts protection mainstreaming beneficiary assessments for feedback on the integration safety and dignity, meaningful access, participation and empowerment of target populations into project design and implementation. IRC sensitizes the community on IRC’s accountability for quality services and on the ways they can provide confidential feedback. The IRC Child Protection team works together with other IRC sectors to provide trainings/information on the IRC Child Safeguarding Policy during trainings for their volunteers community structures members and awareness raising sessions for community members, protection mainstreaming is also achieved through the development of protection mainstreaming action plans which are be monitored and adjusted throughout the project. Joint monthly community dialogues are held by protection teams with Bulamas, community structures, community/opinion leaders and community members to discuss uptake of project services, key challenges, gaps including issues on protection, GBV and child protection identified. The forum is also used to provide feedback and discuss ways to mitigate risks and threats. Decisions are communicated back to the beneficiaries through community leaders, social support groups and during one on one or group sensitization sessions.
• Relevant stakeholders follow rules and laws to protect civilians: IRC will strengthen the knowledge, skills, capacity and accountability of duty bearers in both government institutions (e.g. SEMA, NEMA) and at the community level (e.g. religious and traditional leaders). This will be achieved through technical and material support; monitoring duty bearers’ actions and through pursuing an evidence based advocacy strategy targeting duty bearers at all levels, including where appropriate, at the national level and regional levels. Knowledge and skills will be enhanced through trainings to ensure duty bearers access information and know about protective rules and laws pertinent to the prevailing context in the North East. This will focus on protection principles; housing, land and property (HLP) rights and responsibilities; protection mainstreaming and legislative frameworks governing IDPs such as guiding principles on internal displacement and dignified returns, the Kampala Convention on IDPs, humanitarian law, human rights, the Nigerian constitution and state laws of interest.
• Women, girls, boys and men have their urgent protection needs met: IRC protection team will strengthen referrals for specialized services for women, children and other vulnerable individuals or groups, to ensure they receive timely, appropriate and adequate services for their healing and recovery. Internal referrals will be provided for beneficiaries, including those identified through protection monitoring, by community groups, or through self-reporting to IRC’s information centers or IRC staff. Externally, IRC will also participate in updating and disseminating referral pathways developed by the protection subsector working groups (Child protection, General Protection and Gender Based Violence) and ensure that people with specific identified protection needs are referred to relevant organization for specialized services. IRC will also support access to civil documentation (in partnership with National Population Commission (NPC), National Identity Management Commission (NIMC)) and legal services (including legal counselling and case management services) and provide targeted individual assistance to address immediate needs of victims or survivors of violence (including urgent medical support. TIA which can be in form of cash or in-kind is provided to enable most vulnerable individuals in a situation where no other alternative response, assistance or support exists to meet their most urgent protection needs with dignity and reduce their vulnerability and exposure to risks and promote meaningful access for the most vulnerable. It aims to build on rather than replace existing support mechanisms, meet immediate/most urgent needs and prioritize the most vulnerable.

Needs

The ongoing armed conflict in North-East Nigeria has triggered a complex and volatile humanitarian crisis in which human rights are regularly violated. Grave human rights violations recorded include sexual violence, kidnapping, forced marriage, arbitrary detention, family separation, disappearances, forced recruitment, commercial sexual exploitation, and brutal violence against civilians resulting in injury, trauma and death. Many of the displaced lack objective and reliable information about their rights and how to access available services. Many others lack civil identification documents, often preventing them from proving their nationality, moving freely, and accessing services, safety and justice. Thousands of children have been separated from their families or exploited by armed groups. Women and girls, already exposed to harmful gender dynamics, face high vulnerability to GBV as a result of conflict and displacement. Even as many refugees and IDPs return to their communities, new displacements continue to occur in unsafe or inaccessible areas. Some returns and new displacements have been induced by poor humanitarian conditions, while others have been coerced. There is an urgent need to identify and act upon durable solutions, and to ensure that all returns are voluntary and occur in safety and with dignity.The project will therefore seek to address the following needs:• The need to address protection risks arising within the household. There are high incidence of child-headed households, early pregnancy, forced marriage, domestic violence, child abuse and GBV. • The need to assist people who are returning to their communities.• The need to establish protection structures and foster a more protection-sensitive culture amongst government actors and communities to create a safer environment for beneficiaries. To this end, stronger engagement is needed with government actors, through both advocacy and capacity-building support.• The need for greater availability of timely and reliable information on protection issues that is accessible to humanitarian actors.The need to assist the many individuals lacking the legal documentation necessary to access services and exercise their rights to protection. Housing, land and property issues are prevalent, including loss of title documents, forced evictions, and lack of access to land.• The need to mainstream protection principles and promote safety and dignity of beneficiaries across all humanitarian services.
