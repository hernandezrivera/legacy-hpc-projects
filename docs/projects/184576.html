Project Summary

As a report from the HNO 2022, Conflict, drought, COVID-19, and flooding are some of the main shocks that drive displacement and largely affect at least 2.9 million internally displaced persons in Somalia.
Banadir (Daynile and Kahda districts), L/shabelle (Marka and Afgoi districts), Bay (Baidoa districts) and Hiran (Beletweine district) host the most huge vulnerable majority IDPs and those IDPs are living sites under in precarious conditions with the majority not having their basic needs met due to inconsistent service provision or exclusion from accessing humanitarian support.
According to the detailed site assessment finding and site verification assessments conducted by the CCCM cluster, more than 85 per cent of sites are informal settlements settled on private land with the majority of sites located in urban areas.
The lack of access to land coupled with a weak land tenure system, multiplicity of land ownership, land disputes, weak judicial systems to seek redress, and a lack of proper national land policy further exacerbates the vulnerability of IDPs. This has led to frequent incidents of forceful evictions pushing IDPs into worsened situations requiring both emergency and prolonged assistance, and records of gaps and issues raised accordingly to CCCM Cluster monthly service monitoring and complaint feedback mechanism (CFM) trend analysis.
The forced evictions, the current displacements and the in-proper coordination system caused to increase the vulnerability of the displaced persons specially women, elderly, refugee returnees, minority groups and people living with disability (PLWD) as indicated by CCCM cluster putting further stress on that demography of displaced families and limiting their ability to cope.
The IDP population of Daynile, Kahda, Baidoa, Marka, Afgoi and Beletweine continues to demonstrate characteristics that make the group more vulnerable than individuals living in urban or rural host communities and to they are also having inclusion challenges or overall disenfranchisement in accessing essential services communities and amongst the populations that have the most acute needs in IDP sites PLWDs, individuals from minority clans, women, and youth face extensive barriers in accessing humanitarian services preventing a dignified living conditions for such demographics.
Evictions are also contributing to the increasing of vulnerability As of August 2021, 92,042 individuals have been evicted from their settlements stemming from a total of 2,304 incidences of forceful eviction perpetrated by landowners.
Over 56 per cent of all evictions occurred within the Kahda and Daynile districts in Banadir which accommodate some 1,808 IDP sites or 848,760 IDPs. Furthermore, 11 per cent of households in IDP settlements (17 per cent of Banadir IDPs) believe that they are currently at risk of being evicted from their shelters.
Living conditions in IDP sites across Somalia are exemplified by settlement overcrowding with poor quality of built infrastructure and dire sanitary conditions. Over 40 per cent of IDP sites are currently not able to accommodate increases of population size to the established settlement.
Inadequate settlement design in IDP sites disproportionately affect women and girls through the inability to safely access all services with 46 per cent of vulnerable groups perceiving themselves as not safe living within their respective IDP site. Such conditions are exacerbated inaccessible IDP settlements where 75 per cent of IDPs cited that they do not feel safe leaving their shelters at night.
HINNA’s HINNA’s CCCM project seeks to supporting the suffered vulnerable displaced persons thoroughly formation of camp coordination structures at the site-level to improve living conditions and protection of IDPs and ensure equitable access to services and assistance for all displaced persons living in the targeting IDPs sites for moving forward to ensuring the durable solutions for IDP communities.

1. - Projects activities

Activity 1.1.1: In consultation of the CCCM cluster HINNA will consistently update in the existing services mapping or establish a new service mapping of partners operating in sites at the areas which is not yet established the service mapping – this exercise is to Mapping out of the existing resources and sites infrastructures in the targeted settlements to fatherly identify gaps and inform responses/share information. And thoroughly this activity enhances the strategic implementation of all stakeholders in terms of needs and gaps in the targeting IDP sites to plan the right site and the right population to targeting the humanitarian services. Activity 1.1.2: Establish and Conduct Monthly based CCCM coordination structures meeting with the presence and active participation of the government counter-parts humanitarian partners in the IDP sites to sharing information, referrals, gaps and to avoid matters that can cause duplication of services. Activity 1.1.3: Through recruiting to multifunctional mobile teams HINNA will conduct site verification exercise at all the 6 districts of the project locations and with the coordination support of the district authorities and active humanitarian partners in the targeting IDP sites HINNA will establish and Publish sites verification exercise database to collecting and providing of multi-sector information on access and availability to basic services and gaps also regular service monitoring. This data database will contribute to its dissemination to inform humanitarian response at each level and also the database will help humanitarian partners to selection process of the target camps. Activity 1.1.4: For better sites infrastructure, sites physical management and camp coordination systems HINNA will support the communities to establishing IDPs Camp Management as representatives of site governance at the camp levels to working the involvement of the beneficiaries at all the stages of the project phases, and also they will participate decision making process at the site levels, develop of site rules and support the humanitarian actors to selection process of the camps and beneficiaries Activity 1.1.5: HINNA formulate and Conduct Site planning training for the newly and existing governance structures and focal points to help them equip with site management and camp coordination skills as to facilitate a participatory management and maintenance of public spaces. Activity 1.1.6: HINNA will register and train a team with the skills of CFM and then HINNA will Establish and Open Community Feedback Mechanism (CFM structures) in the target locations for the accessing of the IDPs to reporting of concerns, complaints and any constraints existing in the IDP camps to keep the project’s accountability and participation approach 1.1.7: With the Support if the established CCCM committee HINNA will conduct community-led site maintenance activities to ensure the upkeep of sites through cash for work activities, site maintenance led by the site maintenance committees in the target locations, the conditional cash for work, beneficiaries who will support in site maintenance through digging drainage systems, pit-holes for garbage collection, bush clearing and clear pathways.
