Project Summary

DKH and its implementing partners in Somalia (IPs) seek to contribute to protecting vulnerable populations affected by the cyclical shocks, locusts, and conflicts in Somalia against hunger, malnutrition, and destitution. The project targets a total of 34,800 people (5,800 households) vulnerable internally displaced persons (IDPs), destitute host communities, as well as food insecure and vulnerable households, particularly poor and marginalized women, men, and children. This is aimed at improving access to food and safety net response and livelihoods protection through conditional transfers to address food access, in addition, to support rehabilitation of key livelihood assets, and unconditional cash/vouchers and livelihood inputs targeting riverine areas in Somalia. Beneficiaries will be identified through existing vulnerability criteria, based on the IPC 3&4 phasing, identifying the needy women, child-headed households, the elderly, persons living with disabilities, and other marginalized groups. DKH and IPs are also cognizant of the various gender challenges in the context and will deliberately target specific groups particularly identified as being vulnerable, such as IDP households headed by women. In addition, an inclusive community-driven approach will be applied to ensure that women and men are involved in the planning, selecting, implementation, and approaches of programmes, especially on decisions of the selection of cash programming schemes, taking into consideration the different obligations, challenges, time constraints, and priorities of women and men across the seasonal calendar.
The project interventions will be designed to address emergency response food-insecure households and will benefit from unconditional multi-purpose cash assistance, cash for work/assets (CFW/CFA), and cash vouchers. The cash amounts distributed with be guided and based on the minimum expenditure basket (MEB) per region of interventions, as defined by the Somalia Food Security and Nutrition Analysis Unit (FSNAU). Further, the project will also aim at supporting community resilience through targeting restoration of communities’ assets that were depleted by drought, floods, or conflicts. This will be done through supporting the identified beneficiaries with conditional cash-for assets activities, including cash for restocking, and rehabilitation of surface water points for animals and irrigation. Most vulnerable IDP households with school-going boys and girls and people living with disabilities shall be supported with food vouchers. Further, the rural populations, especially those facing devastation of the desert locusts, will be supported to produce fodder for the livestock, and particularly women will be targeted. Riverine farmers will be supported with agricultural inputs, taking into consideration the seasonal calendar activities. Additionally, DKH and IPs will also seek to strengthen social capital among communities on the use of the rehabilitated water facilities for productive utilization. Priority community livelihood assets, mostly water sources will be rehabilitated and maintained as per community priority areas, which will be determined through a participatory approach to ensure that they are productive for the communities targeted. DKH’s IP will promote the use of improved agriculture technologies through the promotion of early-maturing, high yielding, drought-tolerant & disease tolerant crop varieties, small-scale irrigation, provide farming tools and seeds, train of farmers, and conducting market assessments to inform interventions. Protection, gender, and human rights approaches will be mainstreamed in the program, ensuring that deserving cases are selected to benefit from the project, especially vulnerable women and those living with disabilities. Usually, the participation of women in CFW activities is low and the project intends to raise female participation rates.

Project Activities

Objective 1: To improve immediate access to food by the most vulnerable households among the targeted populations in South-Central Somalia through the provision of conditional and unconditional transfers. Activity 1.1: Conduct Pre-Crisis Market Analyses (PCMA) and complementing existing studies according to the EMMA Toolkit in the targeted locations to define the details of the cash transfers.Activity 1.2: Provide Unconditional Multi-Purpose Cash (UMPC) through mobile money transfer to IPC 3 & 4 people in targeted areas for a period during acute needs to 1000 households Activity 1.3: Provide conditional cash for assets to drought-affected communities that live in vulnerability criteria that allow them to do physical work (IPC 2&3), targeting 1200 households for over 3 monthsActivity 1.4: Provide food vouchers to most vulnerable IDP households with school-going children that have people living with disabilities, single mothers, low coping capacity in Banadir IDP camps, targeting 3,300 households Activity 1.5: Post Distribution Monitoring with sampled cash beneficiary households OBJECTIVE 2: Protect and restore livelihoods, related food, and income sources, through the provision of seasonally appropriate livelihood inputs and technical support in rural and peri-urban settings Activity 2.1: Provide agriculture inputs to riverine agro-pastoralists near Beletweyn/ Hiraan. Activity 2.3: Support the pastoralist communities to restore their household livelihood, especially women, with goats to restore their livestock lost during drought and conflicts. Target is 500 householdsActivity 2.4: Conduct community-based Disaster Risk Reduction and Sensitization sessions

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The use of cash enhances the decision-making and dignity of the beneficiary as they can make an independent choice, with the flexibility provided by cash, to purchase what they need. While cash enhances the beneficiaries' income level, it also contributes to strengthening local market functionality with the injection of the cash into the local markets. Further, cash transfer is quicker and more cost-efficient than providing in-kind commodities, in addition to the items purchased by households being culturally appropriate and suit their specific needs.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

The project has considered market functionality through conducting assessments, including market and seasonality analysis. The cash will be provided based on the minimum expenditure basket, ensuring that while the beneficiaries' purchasing power is increased they will be able to get access to livelihoods from the existing markets. The results of the seasonality analysis will inform the appropriate time of the year to implement the interventions, to ensure that the markets function as normal and beneficiaries are able to access goods and services from the markets. Besides, the amount of cash being injected will be little and may not have a negative effect on the prices of goods in the local market. The cash amounts distributed with be guided and based on the minimum expenditure basket (MEB) per region of interventions, as defined by the Somalia Food Security and Nutrition Analysis Unit (FSNAU). During the implementation of the project, the team will liaise with other stakeholders to monitor the price behaviour and supplies for commodities to inform the effects the cash intervention is having on the local economy. Decisions will be made appropriately to ensure the cash is used for the right purpose, with no or little effect on the market functionality.

Mitigation Measures

Mitigation 1 – Exclusion and marginalization: Women, girls and boys are not included in consultations and decision-making processes: I: DKH's partners will ask the communities to have women, boys and girls participating in meetings, II: In meetings, women, boys and girls will actively be involved in discussions, III: DKH's partners will pay attention that committees are gender and age group balanced composed, IV: where appropriate, women, boys and girls will be particularly asked about their consent with decisions, V: some activities are designed in a way that they require the selection of women, boys or girls as beneficiaries Mitigation – External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance. i) DKH follows a code of conduct that prohibits any sexual or other exploitation of beneficiaries, this will be adhered to by all partner and implementation staff and any deviations will be dealt with accordingly. ii) Based on DKHs code of conduct, all national partners of DKH must also have and apply a code of conduct with such content, iii) A reporting system ensures that PSEA cases get known and disciplinarily treated, if necessary prosecuted, iv) Gender parity of staff at DKH and amongst implementing partners ensures that is in direct contact with beneficiaries helps to prevent PSEA cases or to report such cases, V) partner organizations are liable for the correct behaviour of their staff. Mitigation 3 – External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: Delivery of humanitarian assistance affects peaceful coexistence and spark intercommunity conflict over access to resources: i) Where multiple communities use the same resource, DKH and the partner have guidelines to form multi-communities resource management committees that allocate the resource in a transparent way to the user communities, ii) In the creation of the asset that contains the resource, members of all user communities will be involved to ensure that there is buying through structured consultations and equal participation, iii) Target communities will be consulted before any assets development is done to ensure that they are in acceptance with the creation of identified asset(s) before works can start, iv) All targeted user communities of resources must build a covenant through participatory methods about the agreed use of the resource, v) Where possible, such agreements shall be recognized by local authorities through engagement with DKH and implementing partners.

Please provide further explanation

While the project focuses mainly on the emergency aspects of the response, providing immediate support to displaced communities including host communities, internally displaced persons (IDPs) affected by drought, floods, and insecurity, the design is embedded to include interventions that have long-term effects hence contributing to the humanitarian-development nexus in Somalia. The project upholds consultation and use of the existing community structures, which in most cases are the host community leadership and this will promote peaceful co-existence between the IDPs and the host community. The Project also focuses on strengthening of the community structures ensure effective us of the community assets for productive agriculture. They will be trained to ensure effective decision-making on the utilization of the community resources, enhancing peacebuilding and cohesion in the target communities. The project will also put efferts to train the local communities on improved agricultural practices and this will directly contribute to the development of the skills in agronomic practices, which will eventually lead to improved agricultural productivity. The programme is further developed through participatory approaches to avoid bias or discrimination between IDPs and hosts, in addition to ensuring ownership of the results. Through working with national implementing partners the project contributes to the strengthening of the capacities of the local capacity in resource mobilization and management. The project will be implemented in consultation and within specific guidelines developed to ensure longer-lasting effects in the areas of implementation.
