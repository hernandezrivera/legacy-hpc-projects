Project Summary

With reference to project number PRO-MIN-161554-1, under HRP 2020, this project aims at adjusting the operational capacity to constraints posed by the Covid-19 pandemic in Northeast Nigeria through a proposed intervention that offers a quick response tailored to minimize and mitigate the impact of Covid-19 and Explosive Ordnance in conflict affected communities through the implementation of contagion prevention messages and WHO international good practice guidelines, as well as guidelines suggested by DRC / DDG and other organizations such as GICHD among others. The messages will be delivered along with mine action activities especially those related to prevention. Thus, returnees, IDPs, and host communities’ safety in raising their awareness of the threat from Explosive Ordnance (EO) and Covid-19 and to promote safe behavior and good practices among the large number of civilians at risk particularly the children in the shortest possible time. Further, the action will undertake Non-Technical Survey (NTS) to identify and report not only confirmed or suspected hazardous areas to enable safe freedom of movement, but also to gather and prepare community’s needs assessments, enable early recovery activities and expansion of humanitarian operations. In addition, the project will support in building the capacity of the Police EOD personnel in Northeast Nigeria to enable them dispose explosive ordnance (EO) to prevent harm to civilians and to remove obstructions to securing livelihoods needed for recovery from conflict.
The proposed project will have three integrated teams with a transversal advisory axis focused on Covid-19 and EO mitigation. The three teams are Explosive Ordnance Risk Education, Non-Technical Survey, and Explosive Ordnance Disposal (EOD). All the team will closely work with the relevant stakeholders to (a) promote safe behavior and international good practices to prevent Covid-19 cases and reduce accidentally with EO through delivery of tailored awareness messages; (b) identify and report confirmed or suspected hazardous areas and prepare communities’ specific needs assessments with special reference to Covid-19 and medical assistance; and (c) build the capacity of the Nigerian security force to dispose dangerous items following strict protocols of prevention in times of outbreak. As such, the proposed intervention will provide multiplier effect as it ensures returnees, IDPs and host communities’ are provided with the knowledge to prevent Covid-19 contagious, to act safely in the event of encountering explosive hazards in their environment, determine their needs and the prioritization or future intervention and clearance operations, and build the capacity of the government to dispose Explosive Ordnance (EO). Hence, the action will create a secure environment for vulnerable people for development, releasing contaminated land for productive use, and by removing blockages for humanitarian and development assistance to take place. Hence, mine action is key not only for saving lives but as a precursor for peacebuilding, stabilization and sustainable development. As such, the proposed intervention is a key contributing element of the nexus between peace, health and security and development.

1 - Needs

While COVID-19 affects people without discrimination, when the virus hits a displacement-affected community, it is likely to hit harder due to existing vulnerabilities and weakening coping mechanisms such as the prevalence of chronic diseases, limited access to health care as well as running water, basic sanitation and hygiene, further compounded by the latent overcrowding which characterizes many camp and settlement dwellings and renders social distancing near impossible. As such, the impact of Covid-19 could accentuate the humanitarian crisis in BAY States given the northeast states have been experiencing a significant humanitarian crisis since 2009, which has resulted from the on-going armed conflict between Armed Opposition Groups (AOG), Nigerian Security Forces and the Multi-National Joint Task Force (MNJTF). A decade long conflict has left north-east Nigeria littered with dangerous contamination from a wide range of Explosive Ordnance such as: rocket-propelled grenades, artillery shells, mortar Bombs, air-dropped weapons, and grenades that did not explode or have been abandoned. Although the full scope and scale of contamination is unknown, it is assessed to be considerable. Reports indicate that as much as 20 percent of munitions that were fired did not explode on impact, resulting in high volumes of unexploded ordnance contaminating communities. The increasing hostilities related to the Chad Basin Campaign since last year, have contribute to the rise of the direct risk of accidents with Explosive Ordnance, especially to IDP population in BAY (Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe) states. An undetermined scale of contamination from a wide range of ammunition that have been fired, used, launched and propelled but not exploded plus others abandoned during this campaign remain in the ground as a risk for host communities, IDPs and returnees. At least 03 accidents and 02 accidents related to EO have been report in BAY States in 2019 and 2020 respectively. In addition to the direct risk to the population and the impact on humanitarian, early recovery, and livelihood activities, the presence of Explosive Ordinance is providing a readily available source of explosives to construct improvised explosive devices (IEDs), which compounds the threat to communities. There have been numerous reports on the presence of different types of Explosive Ordnance (EO), which have been verified and unverified. DRC/DDG conducted a mine action assessment in 2015 on which different types of ERW, seen by IDPs in various areas of Borno state, have been mentioned. Subsequently, in 2016 the United Nations Mine Action Service (UNMAS) conducted a scoping mission in northeast Nigeria, which further confirmed the presence of EO in populated areas. While the exact nature of the items was not confirmed, various reports of pressure-plate, victim-activated Improvised Explosive Devices (IED) which function like landmines have been collected by UNMAS, INSO, and DRC/DDG teams on the ground through their interaction with IDPs, returnees, and host community members. There are also numerous reports of unexploded ordnance (UXO) found in populated areas, on farming land and/or on roads, namely in Konduga, Mafa, Ngala, Bama, Dikwa and Gwoza (Borno). The presence of EO represents a serious protection issue, as IDPs, returnees and government workers involved in early recovery and rehabilitation work (e.g. roads, schools, agricultural support, water provision, etc.) are at risk of being injured or killed by explosive hazards. Further, the impact from explosive ordnance can be raised to exponential levels taking into account Covid-19 recent outbreak in BAY States considering the high vulnerability levels of the population and the lack of coverage and medical care that exist in BAY States.

2 - Activities

The proposed intervention will have three broad activities as described below. 1. Risk Education: Based on the delivery of risk education focused in EO and Covid-19 in all three north-eastern states, DRC/DDG has learnt and implemented following key strategies which will continue to inform the design and delivery of its future mine action programming in the region: • Continue to collaborate and share information with military, and national and state authorities, e.g. NEMA and SEMA, as keeping them informed of DDG activities helps to facilitate access and acceptance. • Adaptation of EORE materials to include Covid-19 prevention messages • Before initiating implementation of activities, it is essential that key stakeholders and INGO, for example camp managers, are identified and their authorization is obtained in order to facilitate access and acceptance. • When delivering RE in host communities, target three homes at a time in order to keep numbers manageable, i.e. 10 people, and avoid drawing larger crowds, which could expose staff and the beneficiaries to the risk of COVID-19. Design and delivery of DDG’s risk education programme is also based on data collected through Knowledge, Attitude, Practice (KAP) surveys conducted in Yobe (August 2016), Borno and Adamawa states (March 2017), on adults and children’s knowledge on mines/ERW, risk-taking behaviors and beliefs as well as the most at-risk groups. The level of effectiveness of RE activities will be measured against these baseline surveys. More importantly, target will be divided a 50:50 Of between adults (aged 18+) and children (aged 6-17) as adults make up the majority of the ERW related casualties. Further, more females will be targeted than men given knowledge gap is almost always slightly higher for males than females according to KAP surveys. Under the intervention, the following key activities will be implemented with a Covid-19 emphasis: • Risk Education Sessions (IDPs/Returnees/Host Communities) • Risk Education Sessions (INGOs/NGOs/Local Government Authorities) • Emergency Response Risk Education Team • Public Service Announcements • EORE Messages through Drama 2. Non-Technical Survey: DRC/DDG Nigeria will utilize its current structure, specifically its GIS specialist to identify areas of intervention that need to be targeted and prioritize needs from communities to mitigate the impact of Covid-19. This will be done using DDG Nigeria’s current database of EO reports, Casuality Reports which will be updated regularly. It will also incorporate an open source reporting, knowledge gained from local security providers and other INGOs and UN organizations. DDG Nigeria will keep the engagement with Federal and state level key stakeholder’s during the execution of the strategy. The surveys will then be conducted, and information mapped and recorded in the IMSMA database and shared with interested INGO. The proposed intervention will have the following key activities, namely: Desk Assessment, Planning, Deployment of Teams and CL Strategy, Communities Needs assessment related to Covid-19 and IED Threats, Marking Areas, Sharing findings with Communities/Authorities, and Reporting and Prioritization.3. Explosive Ordnance Disposal Training: DDG will enhance the Police EOD capacity through provision of training as per the existing international standards to reduce the protection risks associated with the Explosive Ordnance (EO) in North East of Nigeria. To this effect, DDG will make use of 3D printed Inert Explosive Ordnance (EO) and Advanced Ordnance Teaching Materials (AOTM). This activity also includes the mentoring phase after each EOD Training. Currently there are no permissions in place for EOD activity except by Nigerian Security Forces, this is likely to change as the dynamics of the conflict changes and the knowledge gained through Non-Technical Surveys will assist in the safe return of IDPs to their place of origin.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

DRC/DDG will use an inclusive method during the need assessment where at risk communities are involved. A participatory approach shall be employed to generate interest and ownership at the community level from the beginning of the project. Further, the needs assessment shall address the different needs, vulnerabilities and expectations of various groups and shall be sensitive to culture, gender, and age. A review of existing community social networks, key community opinion leaders (both male and female) and local development committees shall be included in the needs assessment. The information collected through participatory methods shall be validated by the different segment of the community members. In addition, regular meetings will be held with different segment of the community to get feedback throughout the life of the project. These meetings sometime require to hold separate meetings for women and girls due to cultural sensitivities.
