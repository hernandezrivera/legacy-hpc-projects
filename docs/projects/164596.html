Project Summary

The planned project will support 2 participatory irrigation projects in Ariha and Salqin Sub-Districts (i.e. existing systems) in vulnerable communities in north of Syria by rehabilitating irrigation systems through providing and maintaining water distribution pipes, providing water pumps, electricity generator sets, repairing electricity network and providing electrical panels to irrigate 200 hectares. In addition to provide and install one additional participatory irrigation project solar panels with required electricity equipment as an energy source for water pumps to irrigate 50 hectares in Jarablus.
The project will rehabilitate a bakery in a high severity community in Afrin managed by the local council, the rehabilitation work will include the bakery premises, electricity network, and about 3,000 meters of access road connecting the major mill-bakery-main route, in addition to repair, provide and install bread baking equipment to increase bakery productivity 8 MT of flour per day.
The project will recover the community capacity throughout rehabilitating basic service and infrastructure of a war affected community.
The project will assist and contribute to the protection of the most vulnerable groups of women, men, girls and boys in all age groups and disabled people in the targeted communities, moreover beneficiaries have been involved in project designing and implementation through a close coordination with local council, elected WUA management board members and community representatives, also beneficiaries will be engaged in evaluation through satisfaction survey and complaint mechanism.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2020 draft version: The number of people estimated to be food insecure is 7.9 million or 39 percent of the total population of Syria. Of this at least 569,477 (6%) are estimated to be severely food insecure. The majority of those are located in Aleppo, Idleb, Rural Damascus and Ar-Raqqa governates. the increase in the number of people in need particularly those who are moderately food insecure is largely due to households depleting their savings and assets as well as struggling to find livelihood opportunities to meet their food needs, as the crisis continues into its ninth year. The report pointed that some 69 percent of Syrians are believed to be living on less than USD 1.90 a day, more than double since 2011 . moreover, Unemployment rates are believed to be at 50 percent, with only 16 percent of women working in the labor force, QRCS conducted a multi sectoral assessment to evaluate the food security, bakeries, and bread situation in the targeted communities. 26-50% of people in 33% of surveyed communities are not able to secure food for all HHs members on a daily basis, while the majority of the purposive sample are spending 61-80% of their income for food, according to the assessment the monthly need of bread for one HHs of 5 members is between 7-10 USD living in communities suffer from high percentage of unemployment reaches to 90% in 30% of the sample, furthermore in NW and north of Syria 75 bakeries are not working in addition to 60 communities have no bakeries at all, which is considered as a logical result of communities infrastructures devastation, report shows that 51% of the communities infrastructures have been 30-70% damaged (QRCS MS Assessment, 2019)

5 - Implementation of activities

The project intervention will be throughout:- Providing and installing water pumps, control panels, elec. cables, water pipelines and electrical generators to operate and rehabilitate 2 participatory irrigation projects.- Providing and installing solar panels to operate one participatory irrigation project.- Rehabilitating one bakery and providing and installing bread producing equipment..- Rehabilitating 3 Km of roads connecting bakery with mills and main routes. Project implementation cycle will start from coordination with the communities representatives and local councils, performing market assessments.locations selection will be built according to the communities severity.Qatar Red crescent society will implement the project by Syrian expertise through an infield team with wide knowledge of the Syrian context and belong to the targeted communities, all members have high awareness and long experience in the humanitarian service providing and had been trained on GBV, PSEA, doing no harm, protection and security aspects.Trained M&E team will be assigned/hired locally on this project who will becharged with collecting information regarding beneficiaries and data analysis and will alsoclosely monitor and verify the services and renovations rendered to beneficiaries toensure correctness, timeliness and efficiency. An end impact phase will also beundertaken to obtain beneficiary feedback on quality, quantity and further needs for theservices/goods received. Monitoring will involve sporadic and regular visits to the projectsites, interviewing staff and beneficiaries regarding the activities and continuously feedingback observations to the project manager in order to promptly take mitigation orcorrective action when the need arises and to ensure activities are implemented as planned, and to raise any monitored misuse as well.
