Project Summary

The project aims at improving the access of the most vulnerable and in need IDPs, returnees and refugees to life saving and life sustaining health services in 12 governorates in Syria: Damascus, rural Damascus, Homs, Hama, Daraa, Qunaitra, Tartous, Latakia, Aleppo, Hasakeh, Derezzor and Raqqa. The project is designed for a special focus on strengthening community resilience through community based health component. The project envisages five main interventions, targeting in total 846,300 IDPs and returnees, all interventions are aligned with the health sector response strategy 2019 :
(1) Support of free of charge access of 450,000 IDPs and returnees in 5 governorates to basic package of primary health care services through supporting 13 PHC facilities and annexed 8 mobile clinics run by local NGOs in 5 governorates. The package include basic medical consultation inculding mental health psychosocial support, basic investigations, medicine provision for the most common acute and chronic diseases, and health promotion and disease prevention services for individuals and families offered by health counselling clinics The clinics will have the capacity to identify and medically respond to GBV cases by the trained qualified health professionals. PHC services will also have the link with other health and non- health services to facilitate the referral of the patient and meet other needs affecting health.
(2) Support the referral of 11,300 people in need to secondary health care services to save lives or prevent complications and disabilities in 6 governorates through 3 local NGO partners in Damascus, rural Damascus, Homs, Hama, Tartous, and Aleppo. Referral to hospitals and support of cost of care, medical supplies or devices will contribute to the equity in health for the most vulnerable IDPs and returnees.
(3) Assist in the coordinated efforts of WHO to revitalize the public health system of health care services through rehabilitation, furnishing and equipping of 18 public health facilities(15 PHC centers in rural areas and 3 selected critical wards In district hospitals) in 8 governorates (Rural Damascus, Homs, Hama, Latakia, Aleppo, Qunaitra, Daraa, Derezzor ) which are expected to ensure the access of 225,000 People in need for basic and emergency care (4) Support the access of the most vulnerable IDPs and returnees in primary health care to medicines for infectious and chronic diseases, and support some of the urgent needs of limited critical wards in public hospitals to life- saving medicines, when gap is identified
(5) support community based health through 40 health points led by 80 community health workers embedded in 40 community centers and satellite centers run by 10 local NGOs in 10 governorates targeting 160.000 PoCs in their catchment areas and working on individuals, families and communities levels to building knowledge and capacity of the community in health through establishing community representative teams who can persistently convey health message and improve health awareness level, in addition to building the capacity of the community for health initiatives through close and joint work in identifying health problems and risks, building self care skills, and facilitating community led health initiatives and projects in response to the identified health problems. Community based health will be guided by the national health plans and strategies in neonates, childhood, nutrition for special groups, maternity, elderly, mental health, communicable and non-communicable diseases, and disability.
Community participation at all stages with an AGD approach will improve the access to health, the ownership of the community in health related issues, will enhance the overall resilience of people in need, and will contribute to the equity in health. Additionally, the health points will provide 100,000 IDPs and returnees in need (out of the total mentioned above in PHC services) with basic primary health care service

Needs

The project targets IDPs and returnees in urban and rural areas in 12 governorates: Damascus, rural Damascus, Homs, Hama, Daraa, Qunaitra, Tartous, Latakia, Aleppo, Hasakeh, Derezzor, and Raqqa. Public PHC facilities in urban areas are overstretched as a result of the relocation. Majority of destroyed PHC facilities are located in rural areas. Based on UNHCR sub offices field observations, sub working groups meetings and the health sector needs assessment, in 2019 the needs in the targeted areas in this project include: free of charge basic primary health care services. Rural areas in general suffer gaps in PHC coverage due to MoH limited capacity to rehabilitate and run destroyed PHC facilities. Private clinics in rural areas offering charged services, cannot ensure equity in the access for the most vulnerable IDPs and returnees who already suffer poverty and limitation in livelihood opportunities. Access of the most vulnerable groups in rural areas such as children under five, pregnant women, older persons, patients with disabilities or chronic health conditions to basic care becomes a challenge without transportation means or close by PHC. Statistics in PHC services supported by UNHCR during 2018 showed 63% female, 12% under five, and 12% older persons. Main needs of male and female children were management of infectious diseases, immunization, and nutrition. MHPSS services were the common need among male and female, youths, adults and older persons. The most common acute diseases were respiratory infections, gastrointestinal infections, UTI, and skin lesions (caused by scabies and lice), while chronic diseases recorded were hypertension, diabetes, ischemic heart disease, asthma, peptic ulcer, hypothyroidism, malabsorption /malnutrition and degenerative arthritis. Access to medicines remains one of the main challenges in all locations, especially for patients with chronic conditions requiring in average 5000 SP monthly (based on average consumption of the patients supported in PHC)to cover the cost of medicines. Rehabilitation of PHCs is the optimal option to restore free of charge public services in the long run (est. 60 PHC facilities meet the criteria for rehabilitation in the targeted areas).Public hospitals in cities are overcrowded, with limited funding and supply and recurrent maintenance problems. District hospitals are severely affected in general, but gaps are greater in the north east where some critical services such as burn and trauma are not existing. The private sector, although covering some services, doesn’t ensure the access of the most vulnerable or the equity in this access. IDPs, returnees and the affected people in host community are mostly depending on local NGOs supported by health agencies to ensure the access to secondary care. Nearly 40% of the emergency referrals in 2018 in Damascus and rural Damascus related to the cost of medical supplies, surgical devices, or medicines which were not available in the public hospitals. Through 8 health counselling clinics established in SARC PHC clinics in the third quarter of 2018, it was noted that the 3,364 patients with chronic diseases, pregnant women, mothers of children with acute or chronic health problems needed awareness raising regarding the health condition, treatment, prevention means. It was also noted that the engagement of the families and individuals reflected positively on the patient management outcome, satisfaction and the load on PHC services. Field observations in 97 community centers confirmed that improving the level of awareness and engaging the community in health is essential, with the disease profile that have contributing factors amenable to change. The communities in rural and urban have resources, as proved by the crisis, which could be oriented to enhance national health priorities targeting the most vulnerable groups.

Implementation of activities

Activities will be implemented indirectly through 12 partners: 11 local NGOs including SARC and 1 INGO, and directly by UNHCR for activities involving procurement. UNHCR sub offices in 6 governorates are responsible for implementation monitoring. Support of primary health care services activities will be implemented by three local NGO partners (SARC, Social care society, and Tamayouz) in 5 governorates: Damascus, rural Damascus, Homs, Hama and Aleppo) will be responsible for running 13 primary health care facilities already equipped and functional. The partners will be responsible for staffing and ensuring the capacity building in the health facilities. HIS will ensure reporting on the specific indicators. Field visits and meetings with the partners by UNHCR sub offices will ensure continuous monitoring and technical support to the partners. The quality of services will be assessed by UNHCR balanced score card tool. 8 mobile clinics linked to PHCs and staffed with a minimum of 4 staff will cover the disadvantaged rural areas as per monthly schedule, based on assessment of needs, severity scale and coordination with other health actors and stakeholders. Reporting on mobile services will be part of the fixed PHC monthly reporting. An SOP on health services for IDPs and returnees will be guiding the partners in the provision of primary health services and in the referral of people in need to secondary care services. (1) 3 NGO partners in Damascus, Homs, Hama and Aleppo will be responsible for establishing the system of referral which include sources of referrals, referral path, follow up on the cases referred, evaluation of care and reporting. The system of referral is available to other local NGOs, public hospitals, and ambulance teams of MoH and SARC to ensure impartial assistance to all in need based on the set criteria of the medical cases. The referral system should ensure confidentiality, privacy and dignity of PoCs. An electronic referral system, developed initially for refugees’ referrals to secondary care, will be used for IDPs and returnees referrals, in line with referral SOP. The system is part of the HIS and ensures close monitoring and quality reporting on the services provided. (2) The rehabilitation of PHC centers will be implemented by UNHCR partner PUI in 8 Governorates. The selection of public health centers for rehabilitation, furnishing and equipping is coordinated with DoH, MoH central, and UNHCR sub offices, based on a set selection criteria. The rehabilitation works based on the approved BoQs will be included in the partnership agreement.(3) UNHCR- Supply unit jointly with the health unit will be responsible for procuring medicines to cover the needs of partners’ PHC clinics, based on disease profile in partners’ clinics and the national drug list in Syria. Partners will be responsible for receiving the medicines, proper stocking, distribution, monitoring the consumption of medicines and reporting. UNHCR supply unit will procure furniture and medical equipment for the rehabilitated PHC centers, based on MoH standard list of furniture and equipment for PHCs. (4) 10 partners (local NGOs) running 40 community centers will establish health points which will accommodate two community health workers and one small clinic. The community health workers will form and lead community teams of 8-15 members. CHWs and community teams capacity building will be conducted in cooperation with MoH, UNICEF, UNFPA and WHO to address the areas of priorities in the national health plans such as NCDs prevention related measures, neonate care at home, IYCF , CMM, and MISP. Priorities will be based on local assessments. Health promotion and health awareness plans and projects will be developed by the community teams. Staffing and running cost of the health points will be supported. CHWs shall ensure the link with other activities in the community center for more efficiency in addressing health problems and risks.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: POCs are exposed to gender based violence and sexual exploitation.The mitigating measures : The capacity building of the partner in PHC includes CMR course for health professionals. At least one health professional in each PHC clinic is equipped with the knowledge and skills to medically manage GBV cases. Required medicines are available. Female gynecologists are hired, mix of male and female physicians in other clinics such as pediatric and internal medicine is always preserved by the partner. UNHCR health unit is contributing to the update of the referral pathways of GBV survivors. UNHCR local NGOs partners are encouraged to participate in health, protection and GBV sub working group meetings at the governorate levels to ensure an updated referral pathways for GBV survivors at local levels. MHPSS multidisciplinary teams are available in 6 out of 13 PHC supported facilities, the rest will be included in MHGap training for health professionals. COC of partners include PSEA and capacity building of staff includes SEA.A female nurse or female physician assistant is always present with the male physician when patients are received and examined. Most of the receptionists first receiving the patients are female. - The scope of free of charge services is published. Information regarding services are visible inside the health facilities. A counselling clinic is established with female staff, forming another channel for communication with PoCs visiting the PHC. Focus group discussion organized with groups of PoCs in the PHC clinic, feedback guide managerial decisions. Suggestion box and complaint handling system. Health counselling clinics further improve the communication with the community through assigning the time for individual and groups discussions. Risk 2: Lack of equitable access to health services. Mitigation measures: Reporting of services need to include dis-aggregated data for age groups. The inter relation between health services and other protection services supported by UNHCR, i.e. relation and referrals between PHC facilities and community centers. The outreach volunteers in community center allow for more identification of vulnerable groups and facilitated access. The health points in community centers and community health teams improve the identification of vulnerable groups and the equity in accessing health services. The health sector coordination meetings include dedicated sessions for reproductive health and MISP by UNFPA. Partners local NGOs are participating the meetings.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1)Risk: Partner management change, would affect the work of NGO and jeopardize the project implementation. Mitigation:By adopting measures such as : having backup staff, clear policies, guidelines and SOPs in place, encourage the partners to build knowledge management system, this risk will be reduced and expected damages could be brought to minimum. 2)Risk:The exclusion of one of the partners from the list of NGOs approved for cooperation. Such sudden decisions would have severe impact on the project implementation and deprive PoCs of assistance. Mitigation:By having a pool of more than 14 partners who have diversified skills and capacities, shifting some of the activities to other partners could minimize the negative impact.
