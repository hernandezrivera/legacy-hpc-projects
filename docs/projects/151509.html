Project Summary

Mercy-USA seeks funding for twelve months for emergency programming in the Central South Zone of Somalia. The proposed WASH program will emphasize reduction of diseases and hazards among host communities and IDPs displaced by the recurrent humanitarian situation, which has contributed to poor environmental health conditions, unsafe water sources and poor sanitation and hygiene practices. Mercy-USA will carry out hygiene promotion activities as a preventative measure in protecting and improving the health of emergency affected populations with a special focus on the family unit including children <5 and PLW. Hygiene promotion activities will be integrated with Health, Nutrition and AFS activities adopting to areas specific to public health matters in order to protect beneficiaries from communicable diseases including scabies, skin, eye infections and diarrhea-related diseases like AWD, cholera and dysentery. Mercy-USA will construct 60 VIP latrines and 60 hand-washing stations in within schools and health facilities located in areas with high malnutrition rates and large sanitation gaps where children and patients do not have adequate access to latrines or hand washing stations, leading to open defecation, exposure of children to fecal oral diseases and low enrollment rates especially for girls. Water supply sub-sector will seek to rehabilitate 5 main communal boreholes that are not operating optimally. Water sources rehabilitation will be sustainable by ensuring community involvement and installation of solar power.

Needs

According to the baseline survey conducted by Mercy-USA in November 2017, WASH practices are still poor and there is still need for improvement. In Hobyo, the sources of water as reported by survey participants were: 32.2% tap water, 26.9% boreholes, 20.1% protected wells, 13.9% public pans, 3.4% unprotected wells and 3.4% water tanks, with the main method of water treatment being decanting (71.3%) while 3.7% reported not treating water before drinking. Only 52.9% of respondents use water and soap to clean their hands, and 28.9% wash their hands at the three critical times. Open defecation is a challenge, as it was practiced by 28.1% of the survey respondents. In Kahda, an urban town, 90.4% of the respondents reported using tap water, 6.6% public pans and 3% boreholes for drinking water. The rates of access to a latrine are significantly higher at 97.9%, though there is still need to improve hygiene practices as 58% wash hands with soap, 23.2% wash hands at the 3 critical times and 56.7% do not treat the water before drinking. It was observed that 56.8% of the respondents practice clean and covered water storage .In Gedo region, the main sources of water as reported by survey participants are 30.6% unprotected wells, 30.1% dams, 12.4% protected wells, 11.9% boreholes, 8.4% public pans, 6.1% tap water and 0.5% water trucking. 30.2% of respondents do not treat the water before drinking. Those who wash hands with water and soap are 52.9%. Only 49.9% of survey respondents reported washing their hands at the three critical times. A little over one-fourth (27.3%) of the households visited and observed were found to have clean and covered water storage .In Hiiraan region, a different trend was noticed: 72.6% of the respondents reported to use the river as their main source of water, followed by 8.7% for tap water. Additionally, only 14% of respondents reported washing their hands at the three critical times, while 25.4% reported using soap and water.

Activities

WATER SUPPLY SUB SECTOR Rehabilitate and protect 4 shallow wells (1-Gedo, 1-Mudug and 2-Hiraan regions). Rehabilitate and protect 5 borehole (2-Gedo, 2-Mudug and 1-Hiraan regions). For all sites with rehabilitated water sources, a Water Management Committees (WMCs) will be established comprising of a minimum of 10 persons (6 males, 4 females); SANITATION SUB SECTOR Construction of 60 VIP latrines and hand washing stations for health facilities and schools in Gedo and Hiraan Regions. Provision for appropriate disposal of menstrual material will be provided. Separate VIP latrines will be constructed for male and female, sited in safe locations and illuminated at night. HYGIENE SUB SECTOR At community level, the program will train 10 Hygiene promoters (HP) 50% men and 50% women who will supervise the work of 100 community mobilizers. All hygiene promotion staff (110) will undergo a 5 day comprehensive training on PHAST. Distribution of 2,000 standard hygiene kits to guardians of children who have been admitted to nutrition SFP/OTP program in 13 health facilities. All health facility staff and teachers will facilitated to come up with an AWD/Cholera preparedness plan based on their existing resources. Household Water Treatment and Storage (HHWTS) networking group will be formed at each health facility. Support for one KAP/coverage survey to measure program indicators.COORDINATIONRegular update of 4W matrix; Participating in regional and district level coordination meetings. Initiate district coordination meetings in locations where there are none. ; Participating in cluster technical working groups when required; ENVIRONMENTAL HEALTH Construction of one standard incinerator with a waste separation zone and waste bin for each of the 4 health facilities in need of such items.The operator of the incinerator will receive appropriate training, demonstration and a complete set of protective clothing and equipment.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Mercy-USA will seek to ensure that there is a sense of community ownership of the program both at the health facility, community and school levels. Mercy-USA will ensure that the communities commit to the longer term operational and maintenance costs of all WASH infrastructure upon the exit of Mercy-USA’s program. The costs of management, operation and maintenance of all infrastructure are in line with the capacity of the communities to pay, as verified through initial community discussions during the needs assessment process. Mercy-USA will educate the management committees on all major and minor future needs. Guidelines will also be given on decommissioning of latrines that cannot be rehabilitated. Mercy-USA has also factored sustainability in the design of WASH facilities specific to the needs of users (school children and women recovering from child birth). All latrines are conveniently desludgible and quality materials will be used in the construction, including correct curing processes and concrete classes. For water supply infrastructure, Mercy-USA will employ the use of renewable energy as opposed to the use of diesel powered generator. This will significantly reduce the operational cost of the water sources allowing access of water to vulnerable groups who are often closed out due to lack of purchasing power. Mitigation measures will also be in place to protect solar panel and components from theft and vandalism. A key aspect of the exit strategy is capacity building; through Mercy-USA’s overall strategy of making communities self-sufficient, the organization works intentionally to transfer skills and knowledge to local Somali staff. Mercy-USA’s aim in working with local authorities and community units in the target communities is to build their capacity to take on increasing responsibility for program planning, implementation, monitoring and gain some ownership of the program. This will enhance quality of program output and ensure accountability to affected population. Community units and local authorities will be facilitated in the development of area specific disaster risk response plans complete with mitigation measures and optimal use of existing resources. Mercy-USA also collaborates with relevant government Ministries for joint planning and supervision of activities. Mercy-USA has a good reputation and has developed mutual trust with Somalia Government agencies and local authorities in the areas where it is currently working and the newly proposed areas. Mercy-USA’s continued engagement at all the target sites and with local project staff strengthens its work. Through training and OJT mentorship and capacity-building, project staff will be better equipped to run related interventions. Should a post-emergency environment arise, this engagement will allow the community to respond and manage their own needs. Mercy-USA has an overall goal of eventually transferring responsibilities to the established government authorities.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Major risk 1.Withdrawal of support from government or key community representatives. Mitigation measures for this risk will be as follows - Host and IDP communities will be engaged at every phase of the project cycle; aid will be provided transparently and sensitively according to level of need. Major risk 2.Failure of rains; higher malnutrition rates; low food stocks; spike in new IDPs. Mitigation measures for this risk will be as follows - Along with the international community, Mercy-USA will track humanitarian conditions and identify additional funds if necessary to support new arrivals.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Major risk 1. Delays in the implementation of programs due to long negotiation processes. Possible service delivery interruptions. Mitigation measures for this risk will be as follows - Organize inception meetings with stakeholders to continue to receive buy in and support. Sign MOU with clear roles and responsibilities of the key stakeholders. Use UN cluster system and other coordination forums to improve communication channels. Organize handover of assets to the community to build ownership. Major risk 2. Design of WASH infrastructure that does not address the needs of different gender and ages. Mercy-USA will advocate for the full involvement of different gender and ages, particularly in decision-making roles, especially in all Sanitation, Water and Hygiene promotion activities.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Major risk 1. Major militant threats, attacks, or exercises; violence and loss of life. Mitigation measures for this risk will be as follows - Mercy-USA will implement program activities through community acceptance and will work with the local communities to ensure the safety of operations. Major risk 2. Robberies, destruction of property, attacks on staff or beneficiaries. Mitigation measures for this risk will be as follows - Regular and ongoing staff security and safety training; active and rigorous evacuation plan detailing roles of local and international staff; close engagement with international humanitarian actors.
