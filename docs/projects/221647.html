Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims to address the urgent WaSH (Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene) needs of conflict-affected populations in Cabo Delgado, Mozambique, with a focus on the most vulnerable communities in the districts of Mueda, Nangade, Mocimboa Da Praia, Palma, and Macomia. We are targeting vulnerable IDPs, returnees and non-displaced populations. Due to the ongoing instability in this region, safe access to essential services remains severely limited, increasing the risk of waterborne diseases and poor health outcomes.
The project aims to deliver life-saving assistance through comprehensive WaSH services to mitigate immediate health risks associated with inadequate water and sanitation infrastructure. Key activities include water trucking to ensure rapid and reliable access to safe drinking water, rehabilitating water points, drilling boreholes, and implementing rainwater harvesting systems to establish sustainable water sources. The project will also support communities to increase access to safe sanitation, by constructing emergency communal latrines and supporting construction of family latrines. Additionally, it will rehabilitate damaged water networks and install WaSH facilities in markets and public spaces (including health facilities and schools), thereby enhancing public health and supporting economic resilience.
To enhance hygiene and prevent disease transmission, the project will distribute hygiene kits, including dignity items, to vulnerable households and carry out community-based hygiene promotion activities, focusing on handwashing, safe water storage, and improved sanitation practices. Additionally, waste management initiatives will be introduced to create cleaner and healthier environments within the target communities.
In an emergency context, this project will take immediate life-saving actions to reduce health risks, restore dignity, and improve living conditions.
This initiative aligns fully with the three core objectives of the WaSH Cluster:
• CO1: Provide inclusive, gender-sensitive, life-saving WaSH services, items, and facilities for vulnerable populations affected by conflict, including displaced individuals, returnees, and those in hard-to-reach areas.
• CO2: Deliver inclusive, gender-sensitive, life-sustaining WaSH services, items, and facilities to conflict-affected populations, including host communities.
By adhering to these objectives, the project not only addresses immediate humanitarian needs but also supports broader protection and resilience-building efforts in Cabo Delgado.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

This project aims to deliver critical, life-saving WaSH support to conflict-affected communities in Cabo Delgado, Mozambique. Focusing on vulnerable communities, among IDPs, returnees and non-displaced populations, in the districts of Mueda, Nangade, Mocimboa da Praia, Palma, and Macomia, the intervention addresses urgent needs for safe water, sanitation, and hygiene services to mitigate disease risks and enhance overall well-being. Key activities include:• Water trucking to provide immediate access to safe drinking water.• Rehabilitation/repairing of water points and borehole drilling to ensure immediate, yet more sustainable water sources.• Rainwater harvesting systems.• Construction of emergency communal latrines and support communities to access safe sanitation (by supporting them to construct family latrines).• Distribution of hygiene kits, including menstrual hygiene management items, and community-based hygiene promotion to encourage safe practices.• Rehabilitation of WaSH infrastructure in markets and public spaces (including health facilities and schools) to support public health and economic recovery. This humanitarian comprehensive approach is designed to provide immediate lifesaving support to improve health outcomes, while strengthening community resilience and support long-term recovery among the most vulnerable populations in northern Mozambique.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The proposed project takes a careful approach to assess and mitigate potential risks, ensuring that vulnerable communities in Cabo Delgado are not harmed. Acknowledging the complex social and political dynamics among IDPs, returnees, and host communities, SI will prioritize transparent communication and community engagement to reduce tensions related to resource allocation and promote local ownership. In light of the region’s security challenges, activities will be conducted in hard-to-reach areas with strong safety protocols, including regular security briefings, emergency response measures, and close coordination with local agencies to protect both beneficiaries and staff. Environmental impact assessments will be conducted to ensure that water extraction and waste management activities are sustainable, preventing harm to local ecosystems and water sources. To minimize health risks, the project will adhere to WHO and Sphere standards when constructing and maintaining WaSH facilities, ensuring water quality and proper waste disposal to reduce contamination risks. WaSH facilities in busy markets and public spaces, such as health facilities and schools, will include designated handwashing stations, separate latrines, and regular waste collection to support hygiene and prevent disease transmission in high-traffic areas. A robust monitoring and evaluation system will be implemented to ensure the project remains adaptive, incorporating community feedback and making adjustments as needed. By taking these precautions, the project will deliver essential lifesaving WaSH services effectively, while prioritizing the safety and well-being of all stakeholders.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The proposed project actively promotes meaningful access to assistance by ensuring the full involvement of all community members, especially vulnerable groups such as women and the elderly, in the planning and decision-making processes. From the outset, the project will conduct inclusive community consultations with representatives from diverse demographics groups in the targeted areas, ensuring a clear understanding of the distinct needs and challenges faced by women, disabled people and elderly individuals. By establishing women’s and elders’ advisory groups and conducting focus group discussions, SI will ensure that their voices are central to shaping key activities such as site selection for water points, the safety and inclusivity of latrines, distribution modalities, and preferred FCRM mechanisms, among others. To further empower women, the project will train female community members as hygiene promoters and water point monitors, creating leadership roles that encourage community ownership. These roles provide women with a platform for meaningful engagement while ensuring hygiene practices reach every household. Additionally, the project will design accessible WaSH infrastructure, incorporating ramps and handrails at water points and latrines to accommodate elderly individuals and those with limited mobility. This inclusive design approach, combined with active participation and representation, guarantees that all individuals—particularly women and the elderly—have safe, empowered access to essential services, ultimately fostering a resilient and self-reliant community.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project will identify people with disabilities through a community-based approach, engaging directly with community leaders, families, and conducting regular surveys (vulnerability surveys, KAP, baseline assessments, etc.). During initial assessments, project staff will collaborate closely with these groups to identify individuals with disabilities and understand their specific needs and barriers. Community workshops and door-to-door visits will help ensure that those who are isolated or less visible are also reached, providing a comprehensive understanding of the challenges they face in accessing WaSH services. To ensure that people with disabilities have full access to WaSH facilities, the project will implement inclusive designs tailored to diverse needs. Latrines will be adapted with features such as handrails, grab handles, and accessible chairs to assist those with limited mobility. Water points will be equipped with ramps and non-slip surfaces, ensuring safe access for individuals using wheelchairs or mobility aids. Accessible handwashing stations will be installed at suitable heights, with extended levers for easier use by those with reduced hand strength. Furthermore, hygiene promotion activities will be designed to accommodate individuals with visual, hearing, and cognitive disabilities. Visual aids, clear and easy-to-read signage, and audio support materials will be provided to make hygiene information accessible to all community members. By actively involving people with disabilities in the planning and design stages, the project will develop tailored solutions that address their specific needs, creating a WaSH environment that promotes dignity, safety, and independence for every individual.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The project is committed to minimizing its environmental impact, particularly in water access and waste management activities. Recognizing the potential effects of borehole drilling, water trucking, and latrine construction on local ecosystems, SI has integrated environmentally - conscious practices to ensure sustainability. For example, water extraction for boreholes will be carefully managed to prevent the depletion of local water sources, with regular monitoring to maintain a balance with natural water levels. Waste management will follow responsible disposal practices to prevent soil and water contamination. This includes the safe design of latrines with consideration of water levels and proximity to local water sources, in line with Sphere standards, to protect groundwater. Additionally, SI will implement measures to minimize waste during the packing and unpacking of hygiene and NFI kits, reducing packaging materials and eliminating single-use plastics, while ensuring responsible disposal of any waste generated. Latrines and waste disposal areas will be selected to minimize contamination risks, and construction processes will prioritize minimal disruption to the natural landscape. SI also seeks to prioritize the purchase of materials with a low carbon footprint and will encourage the use of locally - sourced materials, such as bamboo and macongolo, for infrastructure such as family latrines. Finally, SI will enhance its distribution activities by aligning them with existing waste management initiatives already planned in the field, including awareness campaigns on proper waste management and the environmental hazards of plastic. By integrating these environmentally-friendly practices, the project ensures that essential services are delivered in a way that respects and preserves the surrounding environment.
