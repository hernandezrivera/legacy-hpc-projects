Project Summary

This project is targeting conflict and flood-affected host communities, returnees, and IDPs living in Nyirol, Pigi/Canal, Tonj East and Yei counties who are at high risk of gender-based violence, death, injury, and life-threatening diseases and reproductive health-related illnesses aggravated due to violence. The primary purpose of the project is to support vulnerable people to recover from crisis, seek solutions to displacement, and build resilience to acute shocks and chronic stresses brought about by the conflict. The target population is 14,434 individuals implemented in a period of 12 months. Narrative Hub as one of the women-led organizations has 3 staff trained on GBV programming and guiding principles, will implement this project directly in the three target locations in collaboration with the target population, the local administration, line ministries, and partners on the ground to achieve maximum project results. Narrative Hub will build upon existing efforts and target vulnerable populations while coordinating closely with the GBV Sub-Cluster and other partners implementing this project and ensure that the standard IASC guidelines for prevention and response services are adhered to. The project will focus on rebuilding broken community protection mechanisms that were compromised by the protracted conflict and caused grave human rights violations of men, women, boys, and girls. Narrative Hub proposes to contribute to the HNO 2022/23 and sub-cluster cluster objectives 1: Reduce the suffering of GBV survivors including women, men, girls, boys and ability-challenged persons through the provision of multi-sectoral specialized GBV response services with activities such as: strengthening GBV case management and referral pathways; strengthening Psychosocial Support services and collaboration with MHPSS working group; strengthening the provision of static legal services and mobile court clinic; providing capacity building trainings on various topics of GBV to GBV actors and frontline service providers
Objective 2: Vulnerable women, men, girls, and boys including ability-challenged persons have improved access to the provision of GBV prevention and risk mitigation interventions with activities such as: strengthening Psychosocial Support services and collaboration with MHPSS working group; strengthening GBV awareness interventions and risk mitigation measures; strengthening the implementation of primary GBV preventions interventions; strengthening coverage of dignity kit distribution and programming as part of GBV risk mitigation measures; conducting training on livelihood support and income-generating activities including alternative fuel sources for girls and women such as energy-efficient stoves; conducting Fuel Efficient Stoves ( FES) programming to reduce risk of gender-based violence and generate income
Objective 3: Prevent and mitigate GBV risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience with activities such as: strengthening early warning information, GBV preparedness and contingency planning and provision of livelihood support; and enhancing GBV preparedness, contingency planning and resilience building to prevent and respond to GBV. It is expected that by the end of the project period, lives of vulnerable women and girls will be saved, they will have increased access to protection services and equipped with positive life skills and positive coping mechanisms. All activities will be implemented in a way that prevents the spread and transmission of COVID 19 and Ebola Virus.

1 - Needs

The Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022, Attacks on civilians by armed cattle keepers, and cattle raids continued to displace people. Cattle raids were characterized by multiple forms of violence against civilians, including killing, injury, rape, abduction, looting and destruction of housing and property. Incidents have occurred in remote areas where victims of violence or survivors of sexual assault had little to no access to health services. Access to Justice has been an impediment for a long time. UNDP’s Access to Justice, Rule of Law and Human Rights Strengthening Programme, in collaboration with UNMISS, deployed a mobile court mechanism has begun hearing such cases of women rights violations. The people of South Sudan continues to be impacted by violence throughout 2021, according to the report. Between January and December 2021, UNMISS Human Rights Division (HRD) documented more than 982 violent incidents affecting at least 3,414 civilians, subjected to one of the four major types of individual harm (killing, injury, abduction and conflict-related sexual violence). Men made up 75 per cent (2,567 men) of the victims, women 14 per cent (481 women) and children 11 per cent (366 children). According to REACH Humanitarian Situation Monitoring in Warrap (January-March 2021), in Tonj East, KIs in 50% of assessed settlements where the presence of IDPs was reported, reported that more than half of IDPs were living in the open without shelter (0% in December. Conflict dynamics appeared more pronounced in Greater Tonj than in the rest of the state in the first quarter of 2021. The current Ebola Virus threat, COVID-19, sub-national violence, and flooding continue to strain access to the very limited basic services and disrupted local markets in Tonj East. The humanitarian country team in South Sudan has since recommended immediate humanitarian response scale in Tonj East. REACH Humanitarian Situation Monitoring, Jonglei State South Sudan April - August 2021 reports that during FGDs with participants displaced from Nyirol County in April, participants expressed that those that returned to their settlements in Nyirol County were unable to resettle as flooding continued to impede their access to shelter. IRNA conducted in Yei in February 2022 indicate that the armed conflict between the SPLA and SPLA-IO entered Yei County in 2017 and forced most of the inhabitants to flee to DRC as refugees, while several others went into hideouts within Yei Center and other parts. The conflict left behind a trail of destruction of homes, properties, and livelihood. Flood-driven displacement, was likely strongly connected with livelihood-driven displacement as livelihood collapse was presumably a direct consequence of flooding. In August 2021, reporting of people leaving their settlement as a consequence of flooding was particularly high in Canal/Pigi (100%), and Nyirol (97%) Counties. An IRNA was conducted across Pigi/Canal County between 3rd – 5th December 2021 to determine the effect of flooding on the population. From the assessment, it was estimated that a population of 35,462 has been affected by the flooding in the locations where this assessment was conducted. This population is in urgent need of humanitarian assistance; mobile health care services, food rations, NFIs, education and protection services. In most of the assessed locations, women and children are the most affected. A number of protection concerns were noted in the settlement areas. Most of the families came without any household items. Many are sharing spaces without any privacy to women and girls. In both Diel and Canal, there were reported cases of children, GBV and sexual exploitation. Conflict emerged as another driver of displacement; it was reported across several counties in Jonglei Sate, including Pigi/Canal (50% of assessed settlements where people had reportedly left), Nyirol (40%), and Duk (20%) Counties.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

While Narrative Hub sets up a feedback and complains mechanism at the project location where the beneficiaries are, at Juba level, a committee composed of senior management level Juba staff and led by the M&E will be in-charge of receiving those feedbacks directly from the field locations and conducting the feedback analysis. Each feedback will be collated and grouped accordingly into areas that will ensure they are better addressed. Those that are related to programming will be addressed from the perspective of the beneficiaries advise and lenses because the beneficiaries are at the centre of the project. Whether it is changing the meeting or distribution location or time, that will be changed, and beneficiaries will be informed of the changes through downward feedback from Juba office. The M&E staff will travel to the field locations to communicate directly with beneficiaries and also notify the field staff of the changes that has been made.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Narrative Hub has presence in the targeted locations and hence there is already community engagement and trust with the beneficiaries as well as the local authorities, who already had given some of the needs to help design this project. Narrative Hub will spend some time with beneficiaries at the inception of the project to create awareness on the project and its goal including how to report complains, give feedback and how they will be addressed. Narrative Hub will then set up a gender and child sensitive AAP Committee composed of the same beneficiaries. Beneficiaries and other agencies on ground will be involved in monitoring to continuously review the implementation. Gaps will be identified and discussed jointly and addressed in order to meet the main goal of the project. Key community members including individuals living with disabilities, women and girls will be involved during joint monitoring of the project and from the different constituency groups. The AAP Committee will engage beneficiaries, listen to their stories and give upward feedback. For beneficiaries who can write, a complains box will be put around distribution locations, incase there is a distribution, or put up at the office gates for upward sharing of complains and feedback. Where necessary, focused group discussions will be held at project sites with beneficiaries and community leaders to provide feedback. Sampling for FGDs will be representative for all affected groups of people. Where distribution is involved, a PDM will be conducted to harvest beneficiary feedback.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Narrative Hub will ensure that training is conducted to all the staff members both at Juba office and field staff on PSEA before the beginning of the project implementation. During signing of contracts, all staff will sign a PSEA code of conduct. The training will then be extended to beneficiaries during project inception. This training will to beneficiaries will be done by Juba based staff members and not by field teams to avoid biases, victimization or any conflict of interest. The beneficiaries will be trained on communication channels available to them to report PSEA and contact persons.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

A complains and feedback committee will be selected by beneficiaries to engage every beneficiary (PWDs, individuals who are not literate, women and girls), listen to their stories and give upward feedback. Where necessary, focused group discussions of each constituency (PWDs, individuals who are not literate, women and girls), will be held at project site with them to provide feedback. Where distribution is involved, a PDM (which will involve individual interviews, FGDs and observations) will be conducted to harvest beneficiary feedback.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

This project is conceptualized based on protection risk analysis and will mean-stream protection at every level. NH is mainstreaming protection in all its programming by ensuring that the most vulnerable groups such as women and girls and others such as PWDs, elderly, receive protection when receiving services at any stage of the project cycle through by regularly conducting safety audits before the services are provided, and also every information provided by the said beneficiaries during the intervention is given utmost confidentiality. NH is a very active national NGO on child protection in Jonglei State and will employ a protection advisor for this project to work closely with established committees such as the AAP Committee to guide the process with a serious protection lenses. Information provided by survivors, victims of sexual exploitation and abuse or beneficiaries will be safeguarded and jealously protected so that it cannot be used to expose them to harm and then, they will be referred to any level that they can seek further help. NH with other agencies will develop a referral pathway in the target locations, and will ensure that the meeting sites or locations are safe and closer to beneficiaries. Barriers to access particularly the meeting locations will be discussed with all the beneficiaries in separate groups (constituencies) before it will be agreed upon, where everyone is comfortable to access. Monitoring tools NH uses have a section of monitoring the safety of beneficiaries, and other barriers to access as protection is mean-streamed in the tool and also have a section that asks questions as regards the use of the items support and whether the beneficiaries feels comfortable using them such as dignity kits. The project activities will address inequalities and protection threats by analyzing power dynamics in the community. Local leaders will not be in charge of determining who will benefit but beneficiaries with support from our staff. All new NH staff will sign PSEA before engagement into the project. Our actions will address both basic needs that improve protection of those at risk of violations. NH is also a very active member of Protection Cluster and GBV.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

The project is geared towards mitigating climate change and building resilience. Mitigating impacts of climate change by using energy saving cooking stoves and supporting livelihoods among the beneficiaries reduces global warming that causes flood and also livelihoods opportunities helps beneficiaries construct more permanent structures on higher grounds. The project also has a component of capacity building including on early warning.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

This directly targeting GBV and therefore 100% in the locations identified to address prevention, risk mitigation, and response to GBV, contributing to 100% GBV minimum package.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

This intervention directly targets GBV with some components of livelihood support to building resilience and increase self-reliance. The beneficiaries are able to increase their income in a secure environment, and therefore able to reduce the number of people in IPC 4 by 20% and IPC 5 to zero.
