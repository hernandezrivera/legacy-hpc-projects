Project Summary

The FSL project aims to improve the food security for the most vulnerable women, girls, boys and men in conflict-affected areas and put at greater risks in the face of COVID-19, and support women and men engage in sustainable livelihoods, in the agriculture and in other sectors alike. Through enhanced targeting of particularly underserved communities and enhanced community-based approach, the project covers three levels of actions, from immediate relief, through self-sufficiency, and longer-term resilience. It will invest special attention and efforts to support both transition in GCA and responding to enduring humanitarian needs in NGCA, and integrate the age, gender and diversity factors influencing food security and livelihoods, including factors of feminised poverty and care dependency.
At a first level the project aims to ‘ensure immediate access to food’ for the most vulnerable women, girls, boys and men living along the ‘line of contact’ in both Luhansk and Donetsk NGCA. The assistance will take the form of food vouchers, and be organised through quarterly rounds to take account of seasonal variations. In addition, PIN will provide short-term, immediate assistance in the form of food kits for those most at risk in the face of COVID-19. Through this modality, those individuals with greater health risks and no direct support system will have access to food items while minimising their exposure to COVID-19 infection risks.
At a second level, in GCA the project will then aim to improve longer-term, sustainable food security for women and men and their households with access to small landplots near the LoC. Such assistance will take the form of seasonal activities, with distribution of vouchers for seeds, of drip irrigation kits, and delivery of trainings. It will enable smallholder farmers in underserved communities to increase their self-sufficiency throughout the year. As such the project will directly contribute to the cluster objective to ‘Improve food security through the provision of emergency and time-critical agriculture assistance’.
At a third level, in GCA the project will eventually aim to support access to employment and micro-, small businesses for women and men in communities near the LoC. Trainings will be held, not only on employment, vocational and business-related topics, but also to foster individual empowerment. Special attention will be paid to support women- and youth-led entreprises for equal access to opportunities and as vectors of development for their entire communities. On short-term and seasonable basis, cash-for-work opportunities will be created around community needs, whether to rehabilitate priority infrastructure or realise other community-based initiatives, account taken of age, gender and diversity. This will serve both poverty reduction, community social cohesion and living conditions in the communities. As such the project will directly contribute to the cluster objective to ‘Enhance resilience of vulnerable people affected by conflict and/or COVID-19 through restoring of non-agriculture livelihood and promote resilience to shocks (including wildfire, unemployment, higher prices, and other hazard) at community level’.
Throughout these actions, PIN will maintain a robust Community Feedback and Accountability Mechanism though its community-based approach and engagement with women, girls, boys, men, elderly and people with disabilities to raise their voices; enhanced consultations and testing of participation and social accountability mechanisms; feedback and complaints hotline, response, referrals and analysis for further adjustment of the assistance.

1 - Needs

Food insecurity remains a key need for 1 million people in eastern Ukraine. The military conflict remains one of the main drivers of food insecurity in 2021 along with the economic shock caused by COVID-19 epidemic, which has further exacerbated the already dire situation of frontline communities. Due to insufficient data and access difficulties in NGCA, the exact number of food insecure people is unknown, but it is estimated at some 285,000 people. At the same time, recent GCA PIN estimates in affected areas confirm the high proportion of people aged 60+, people with disabilities, single parents, large families, unemployed people and families receiving benefits that cannot cover their basic needs, so 28 % HHs having poor and borderline food consumption. Lack of livelihood opportunities continues to drive food insecurity, a critical issue in view of COVID-19 related food prices’ increases. Evidence shows that food self-sufficiency is low even for those having access to land, as they are often unable to afford inputs needed to grow produce. Still, private land lots often remain the only source of sustenance for the residents of frontline communities and provide some income generating opportunities for private farmers in case of produce surplus. Young people tend to leave these areas for lack of opportunities, worsening the local poverty. According to PIN estimates, more than half of the population here are 60+ people who do not have sufficient income due to small pensions of about $ 70 a month. Mine damage, hard work and old age have resulted in 41% of local people with disabilities and chronic diseases, and more than 20% are single parents, mostly single mothers. Gender, age and diversity disparities deeply play out. Destitution often comes with the exacerbation of feminized poverty in the context. Elderly women are disproportionately represented among the target beneficiary group, and often particularly exposed to various forms of destitution. If gender disparities in income tend to decrease at pension age, they remain substantial, with on average lower pensions for women as a result of life-long gender inequalities in the labour market. Critically, women’s income at pension age disproportionately depends, much more than for men, on such pension and other forms of social assistance: such cash often becomes impossible to access in the context of COVID-19 restrictions. Often left isolated, elderly women thus become disproportionately at risk of further impoverishment and dependency on support from others. Additionally, it often happens that elderly women in particular are in effect responsible for caring for grandchildren: in that sense, vulnerability linked to age (as well as gender and diversity) affects then not only one beneficiary but the household. At different age groups women bear the brunt of care responsibilities: being the predominant workforce on the frontline in health and social sectors, but also informally through unpaid care work in their families and communities. They have become under particular pressure to continue providing care despite restrictions in resources and movements. School closures for COVID-19 prevention also mean a disproportionate impact on women and adding to their time poverty. While facing increased responsibilities, women benefit from lesser resources, due to unequal pays and more precarious working arrangements. They are overly represented among poorer workers and bear the core of economic pressures linked to COVID-19 restrictions. Disability itself is a pivotal factor, often pushing vulnerable women and men into further poverty and food insecurity, especially when without official recognition and benefit and care support. For all these reasons, needs assessed based on cumulative vulnerabilities to target the most vulnerable for humanitarian assistance will take account of hidden gender, age and diversity biases, in response to structural inequalities.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

CO1: Ensure immediate access to food for the most vulnerable people affected by conflict and or СOVID-19. Activity 1: 5000 food vouchers will be issued along the LoC in NGCA in Luhansk and Donetsk region for the most vulnerable women, girls, boys and men. Assistance will be provided in four rounds of three months each, reaching 1,250 beneficiaries per round (implementation period of the 1st round JAN-MARCH; 2nd round - APR-JUNE. 3rd - JULY-SEPT., 4-OCT.-DEC.) Activity 2: In view of COVID-19 risks, PIN will provide 1000 food kits to the most vulnerable women, girls, boys and men in NGCA who are (because of pre-existing health conditions) at higher risk in case of exposure to COVID-19 and left without relatives/care support/ transport. Such beneficiaries will be able to receive food without leaving their homes. Assistance will be provided in two rounds of three months each (1st round - APRIL-JUNE; 2nd round - JULY-SEPT.). CO2: Improve food security through the provision of emergency and time-critical agriculture assistance Activity 1: 500 vouchers will be issued to the most vulnerable women and men with access to smallest landplots near LoC GCA (0-5 km) for the purchase of seeds, tubers and seedlings. The purchase of seeds will increase the efficiency of home farming, ensure food security, create self-employment and improve the seed stock. The voucher scheme will allow beneficiaries to select seeds based on their individual needs, income generating aspects and food security for autumn and winter. (JAN. - APRIL) Activity 2: Provision of drip irrigation kits will increase productivity for 200 rural most vulnerable households (taking into account of gender, age, diversity factors) with access to smallest landplots but insufficient water near LoC GCA (0-5 km). The use of drip irrigation will reduce the consumption of irrigation water, simplify working conditions for vulnerable populations and reduce the risk of plant diseases. (NOV. – JAN.). Activity 3: To improve knowledge and skills in agriculture, trainings will be conducted for 1400 members (taking into account of gender, age, diversity factors) of households located near LoC GCA (up to 20 km). PIN's activities include raising awareness among women and men farmers about efficient farming and the correct use of drip irrigation. (NOV.-MARCH). CO3: Enhance resilience of vulnerable people affected by conflict and/or COVID-19 through restoring of non-agriculture livelihood and promote resilience to shocks (including wildfire, unemployment, higher prices, and other hazard) at community level Activity 1: To reduce unemployment and increase the efficiency of small and micro businesses, trainings will be held for 400 women and men beneficiaries / individuals or enterprises near LoC GCA in Donetsk and Luhansk regions (0-20 and 20+). Special attention will be given to women- and youth-led entreprises, for equality as well as efficiency for poverty reduction, as micro- and small businesses tend to be more accessible for women. Trainings include not only business-related vocational and business trainings, (taking into account of gender, age, diversity factors affecting opportunities and obstacles in access to employment and the market), sales skills, hiring and personnel management, etc (together with local stakeholders and institutions), but also empowerment topics to ensure more equal access to opportunities. (JAN.-DEC.).Activity 2: The Cash-for-Work Program will be implemented for 300 beneficiaries to rehabilitate priority community-based infrastructure chosen by diverse community members, or other community-based initiatives (account taken of gender, age and diversity factors to ensure equal access to opportunities) and provide temporary work during two months for women and men living along LoC GCA. These activities help to reduce to some extent poverty, promote community involvement and ensure access to better living conditions.(JUNE-AUGUST)
