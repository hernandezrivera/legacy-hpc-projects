Project Summary

IOM aims to increase economic opportunities for displaced and conflict affected populations (Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and host communities). IOM will target governorates with high levels of continuing displacement, emphasizing Diyala, Kirkuk, and Salah al-Din. IOM will provide emergency livelihood services to the most severely affected IDPs living in camps and informal settlements to set the foundations for eventual, voluntary return and to strengthen individual and community resilience.

Needs

The Emergency Livelihoods Cluster estimate that 2.4 million people will require emergency livelihoods assistance in 2019. This figure includes 0.7 million IDPs living in camps and informal settlements, 1.5 million returnees, 0.14 million highly vulnerable residents in host communities. While the return of the Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant (ISIL) conflict affected IDPs to their places of origin in Iraq continues to increase, the pace appears to be slowing, leaving populations who remain displaced either in or at risk of protracted internal displacement. This increases individual and community vulnerability. IOM also estimates that more than half (54%) of IDPs have been displaced for three or more years and the remaining 46% for up to three years. REACH (August 2018) has found that nearly two-thirds of IDPs overall plan to remain in their places of displacement over the next 12 months. IOM Displacement Tracking Matric (DTM) found that 40% of IDPs surveyed report lack of economic or job opportunities in their areas of origin as a major reason for their continued displacement (IOM Integrated Location Assessment (ILA) 3, 2018).Since 2014, in governorates under ISIL occupation, poverty rates doubled to 41.2%. Even though the impact was limited in the rest of Iraq, poverty increased in all governorates, including in the KRI resulting from the confluence of financial crisis with protracted displacement. In Kurdistan, forced displacement is correlated with the poor performance of most socioeconomic indicators. According to the KRI Ministry of Statistics / IOM DTM Demographic Survey (September, 2018) the displaced population in KRI has, on average, a less balanced structure in terms of sex and age, higher dependency ratios and lower literacy and employment rates. In Iraq, women together with youth are the population groups most in need of access to economic opportunities. In particular, among displaced populations, female headed households and young men between the ages of 14-17 have been found to be the most vulnerable. Female unemployment is twice that of their male counterparts (56 percent and 29 percent respectively). Iraq has one of the lowest labor force participation rate in the region (19% compared to 74% of men) and it is estimated that only 1 in 10 adult women with up to intermediate education participate in the labor force. Opposition from the direct social network, including family and community, has been identified as the main barriers women face to engage with economic opportunities. It is critical to assist vulnerable IDPs and host communities by supporting small and micro businesses through assets replacement grants.

Activities

IOM approach contributes to boosting access to livelihoods opportunities, with a specific focus on female and youth headed households: • Identify needs and gaps in communities through assessments, including community consultation. Firstly, community needs assessments will identify the economic scenario within an area which will determine the modality of the assets replacement grants. Secondly, IOM will conduct a market assessment (including rapid labour market assessment and where necessary supply chain analysis) to identify local sources of income and key industries and economic sector. • Individuals are profiled using the IOM livelihoods Household Profiling Form. Individuals are prioritized according to selection criteria which assess vulnerability (using a tailored multidimensional poverty index) and relevant livelihood skills and experience. IOM will prioritize the most vulnerable from the displaced and host communities, and a strong focus will be on female and youth headed households, especially those resorting to negative coping mechanisms.• Selected Individuals are enrolled in a one- week long business development and financial literacy training • Provide assets replacement grants to prioritized individuals to start their business, prioritizing female headed households. Each selected small business will receive and average of 500 USD, with the main objective to re-establish, rejuvenate or kick-start a business.• IOM will utilize a wide range of Monitoring & Evaluation tools developed to effectively measure indicators of the project objective, outcome and output levels. The data, collected through focus group discussions, face-to-face interviews and follow up calls to beneficiaries, will also be used for evaluation purposes, and the M&E team’s field visits will be utilized to produce a final evaluation.

Protection Risk Analysis

• In Iraq, many displaced women generally suffer from restrictions of movement, as a result from an attempt by the community to ‘protect’ them. In this context, livelihood opportunities for women are very limited, and are mostly available in a setting that is deemed appropriate (i.e. in or around women’s homes or in state owned enterprises). • Pre-existing marginalized members of the community who face challenges accessing livelihoods, such as youth and women are excluded from programming. Exclusionary criteria based on livelihoods skills and experience does not allow for the participation of marginalized groups. • Security situation affecting the ability of staff to access areas.

Mitigation Measures

• IOM livelihood interventions aim to be gender-appropriate. IOM emergency livelihoods programming which specifically seeks to provide income generating opportunities for females (for e.g. through targeting businesses with female headed households), will also engage with males, including through awareness raising and advocacy. • IOM undertakes community assessments which specifically identifies and engages with vulnerable community members, to ensure their participation in programming. Field staff are properly trained to facilitate meaningful participation. Recruiting a diverse staff base representing marginalized groups (i.e. women, youth, religious minorities). For e.g. IOM community assessments include separate consultations with women and youth to identify their specific needs and programming responses. Programming is thus balanced and tailored to the context and needs. Exclusionary criteria based on abilities is utilized for business grants. • IOM has over 1300 staff operating directly in all governorates within Iraq (including in the proposed project locations of Diyala, Kirkuk, and Salah al-Din), providing significant access to at-risk populations, including in areas of high insecurity. IOM has established relationships in GoI and KRG with key government focal points and maintains strong relationships to ensure necessary bureaucratic processes for support. IOM can rely on existing access and coordination arrangements in all governorates to support access to beneficiaries.• Staff are trained on following do not harm principles in interventions and signed a code of conduct, receive PSEA training and have a reporting system. IOM is already capacitating front line emergency staff through internal and external trainings and practical support to effectively identify and respond to protection risks within emergency operations to ensure the delivery of humanitarian assistance that incorporates protection principles and reduces risks to promote safe and dignified access for all affected populations.

How is your project accountable to affected population

IOM’s livelihoods methodology is based on community engagement and ownership. It is, therefore, essential that both engagement and ownership be promoted across the timeframe of project planning, implementation and monitoring and evaluation. IOM community assessment processes are a cornerstone of IOM’s work and ensures Accountability of Affected Populations (AAP). This approach ensures a two-way dialogue between IOM and the affected population, particularly in terms of programme design, monitoring and evaluation, and throughout programme implementation.IOM assesses, plans and designs programming directly with affected individuals and other relevant stakeholders. For e.g. community consultations directly engage with men, women and youth, while market assessments consult with Chambers of Commerce and influential business owners. IOM processes offer the community the opportunity to be a central actor in the monitoring and evaluation of projects. Continual field presence and consistent face-to-face interactions between IOM staff and displaced populations and community members allow IOM to collect and address feedback and adapt to the evolving situation. A monitoring & evaluation plan is utilized for all IOM programming, including surveys to measure impact and satisfaction. IOM has also established and maintains a Community Communications Center (3C contact center). Through the 3C IOM will conduct continuous monitoring during project implementation, as well as follow-up of the various livelihood services completed. This will enable an analysis of programme impact of programme activities over time through direct communication with IOM livelihoods participants. Through the 3C IOM livelihoods M&E staff will conduct regular verification field visits and/or group discussions to triangulate the findings, and better understand trends arising in terms of specific programme activities.IOM teams seek to share information and coordinate with otheractors working in project location to coordinate response and complement and complete the community’s objectives. In addition, programmes that invest in local economies create opportunities to engage young men and women, therefore, enhancing stability by mitigating tensions in a post conflict scenario. IOM collects age and gender disaggregated data for all activities, striving for gender balance. It includes demographical information to ensure equal participation of different communities and including of vulnerable populations. IOM strive to hire gender balanced teams when possible.
