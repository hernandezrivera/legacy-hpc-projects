Project Summary

The purpose of this project is to provide Life saving GBV assistance to Survivors of GBV in camps within Monguno and Yola. This project hopes to address issues of Gender based violence is both approaches of prevention and response.
In prevention, HARAF will focus on building community structures by engaging groups of men, women, boys and girls carefully selected based on a criteria of zeal and passion towards ending GBV. These groups will have at least 20 persons per group to be engaged in focused group discussions and after each meeting develop an action plan to be executed by the group before the next meeting. Also we will engage in other preventive activities like capacity building of community and religious leaders, security actors and volunteers.
HARAF will train the volunteers to be engaged in the program on Gender Based Violence and PSEA. Topics will include concepts of GBV, causes of GBV, Types of GBV, contributing factors of GBV, consequences of GBV,PSEA Inclusive. Since we will be working with the community structures including community leaders, security actors, as a means of prevention, They being frontline personnel's will be trained on GBV which will serve as a means of enlightenment so that they can support the campaign against GBV.
As a means of prevent GBV we will engage community volunteers who will be selected from the community as a means of overcoming language barriers. These Volunteers will be engaged in sensitization of both men, women, boys and girls about GBV within the community
In GBV response, HARAF will Focus on providing life saving assistance to GBV survivors though provision of quality Case management services and psychosocial support involving individual and group counseling, engaging the women and girls in recreation activities, skill acquisition activities as well as informal education for adolescent boys and girls.
This project will target women and girls providing them with this services as well as engaging men and boys in accountable practices.
Dignity kits will also be provided to women and girls who meet the selection criteria to further boost their dignity through the support via the dignity kits
services including GBV case management will be provided within the women and girls' safe space to ensure privacy and safety to GBV survivors and allow for a condition favorable for her to express herself freely and access support accordingly.

1 - Needs

HARAF already has presence in Monguno with a working relationship with existing partners present in Monguno this presents us with a strategic advantage, this project is multi-sectoral hence provides best value for money. This project will benefit from cost shared as other projects are already in play in this location, significantly reducing operational and logistics cost. With an already working supply chain in place, considering our present NFIs work in Monguno, HARAF has an advantage with procurement and logistics, suppliers are already in the process of providing this items, hence additional supply will prove faster than starting the entire procurement process from start . Health and Protection activities in Monguno targeting IDPs in camps/camp like-settings will benefit largely as HARAF already has knowledge of the working environment The project will significantly contribute to the capacity development of service providers of the multi-sectoral GBV response as well as coordination of GBV work at the community level. Needs assessment results indicated that both police and health workers had none to very little knowledge on gender and GBV, and many participants did not think dealing with GBV survivors as part of their responsibility. Hope And Rural Aid Foundation is already on the ground working in this sector in Monguno, Mubi, Yola and Maiduguri, providing case management, PFA, training on GBV, community awareness and referral of GBV cases. To date, Hope And Rural Aid Foundation has actively attended to approximately 29 GBV cases in Mubi and Yola. . An analysis of this data shows that 73% of those who reported GBV cases to Hope And Rural Aid Foundation case management services had never reported the case before. In addition, the data indicates that most of the case were reported after Hope And Rural Aid Foundation GBV community awareness cases with 76% either being referred for services by the CVs and / or self-referral by the client themselves. This is an indicator of there being limited services to address GBV-specific needs. New arrivals, more especially those from newly liberated areas continue to seek services even as the host community also seek services due to lack of the GBV services in the host communities. So far, approximately 10% of the cases we have received in Mubi and Yola are from new arrivals. This points out to the need for continued GBV specific services. 8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Concern will be given to ensure that protection concerns will be considered during planning by the indicators that will guide the monitoring of the project. We intend to mainstream protection by incorporating protection principles and promoting meaningful access, safety and dignity in humanitarian aid. The following elements must be taken into account in all humanitarian activities:1 – Prioritize safety &dignity, and avoid causing harm: Prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of our intervention which can increase people's vulnerability to both physical and psycho-social risks.2 – Meaningful Access: Arrange for people’s access to assistance and services – in proportion to need and without any barriers (e.g. discrimination). Pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services.3 – Accountability: Set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints.4 – Participation and empowerment: Support the development of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights, including – not exclusively – the rights to shelter, food, water and sanitation, health, and education.
