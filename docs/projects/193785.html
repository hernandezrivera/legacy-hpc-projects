Project Summary

This project is in full partnership with the Ministry of Health (MOH). The project aims at providing integrated sexual and reproductive health and rights (SRHR) services to all those in need including the most vulnerable groups; specifically, women and adolescents girls in area C in Hebron and Ramallah districts, through equipping the safe motherhood centers (with equipment, medications, and skilled staff) as part of the preparedness plan (MISP), to be able to deal with emergency obstetrics, SRH +GBV services in crisis and emergencies.
This will be achieved through the following main activities:
- Build the capacity of MOH emergency staff at emergency centers and also at nearby secondary health care services to be able to deal with emergency early pregnancies, obstetric conditions, and imminent birth, and provide the basic life support techniques in a highly qualified manner.
- Provide an integrated package of SRHR and GBV services within three targeted centers in Area C including services for women, young girls, and adolescents including people with disabilities.
- Enhance Community outreach on essential SRH service packages e.g. danger signs in pregnancy, family planning, etc. Information on when and how to reach services will also be provided.
- Support three centers (2 in Hebron, One in Ramallah district) with needed equipment and supplies to be able to provide emergency obstetric care per quality standards.

1 - Needs

The health sector in Palestine suffers from a general scarcity of resources in all aspects driven by the protracted and complicated political crisis along with recurrent outbreaks of emergencies and most lately the COVID-19 outbreak which further exacerbated the strain on the Palestinian health sector. This situation affects in particular the already vulnerable populations in Palestine, mainly residents of Area C, H2, and East Jerusalem, which suffer from the limited sustainability and provision of services. A significant challenge lies within the sustainability and provision of SRHR services during emergencies as these services are often neglected during times of crisis even though they are essential and must continue to be provided. The provision of SRH services, including maternal health care and gender-based violence (GBV) related services, are central to the health, rights and well-being of women and girls. The need for SRH service provision is of particular relevance to the women of reproductive age in Area C and H2, especially those pregnant and lactating. They face continuous difficulties and various hardships in accessing vital SRH services and a continuum of care due to the unavailability of equipped facilities. In area C of the West Bank and according to the HNO 2023 matrix, the total population projected for the year 2023 is around 377,544, among these, an estimated number of 55,000 women and girls will be at risk with different kinds of vulnerabilities including high-risk pregnancies, unmet need for family planning, adolescent girls in need of psychosocial support and mental health interventions, and also unmet needs for GBV services. Though Palestine has seen significant progress over the years in reducing maternal mortality and an improved maternal mortality ratio from 38 per 100,000 live births in 2009 to a record-low 5.9 in 2017, numbers have not only stagnated but are now deteriorating. According to the latest MOH 2021 annual report, the maternal mortality ratio has increased to 47.7 per 100,000 live births, where 78% of cases are reported as preventable. Additionally, based on the analytical report on maternal deaths (2020), four women arrived dead at the hospital; deaths occurred at home and 20 deaths took place inside the hospital. A pregnant woman in need of urgent support might spend at least 40 minutes reaching the closest hospital which constitutes a serious threat to her life as well as the baby. The challenges of accessing emergency medical treatment remain a source of worry and distress to many women and vulnerable populations in remote locations including men, youth, boys, and girls. These worrying trends and reports call for urgent intervention. The three-delay model in accessing safe childbirth is crucial in understanding the need for intervention as pregnant women residing in remote areas cannot access medical treatment urgently, emergency obstetric care at hospitals is not always of high quality, and staff at emergency sections lack training on stabilization of urgent cases and life-saving techniques and support. Therefore, UNFPA, through its intensive partnership with the Palestinian Ministry of Health, proposes this project to enhance the capacity of the emergency program within the Ministry to enable it to provide sexual and reproductive health services to women in need in remote areas, including emergency services for pregnancy, childbirth and newborn care and GBV needs. The project specifically targets three emergency facilities in Area C selected based on a Report conducted in 2022 assessing all MOH safe motherhood and delivery centers which identified three facilities as the most urgent to improve and equip to increase the timely access to SRH services in parts of Area C. The targeted facilities are Beit Rima (Ramallah), Althahriya and Beit Ola (Hebron). The facility in Beit Rima covers seven main villages with an estimated population of over 36,000. The facility is approximately 30 minutes far from the city of Ramallah and should therefore be equipped to deal with emergency cases before transferring to the nearby hospitals. Moreover, there are no services at these centers to identify high-risk pregnancies and provide the proper services. For the two centers in Hebron, 50 minutes at least is needed to reach the closest secondary care facility in addition to the dire need for introducing EMOC services including the availability of equipment and supplies. As described above, the MOH emergency centers are not fully equipped to provide SRH services. At the targeted centers and through this project’s interventions, private friendly space will be rehabilitated to be used by women and girls’ beneficiaries attending the three emergency centers. However, it’s worth mentioning that only one center possesses an ambulance that can transfer the patients to a nearby hospital if needed, and this ambulance is not in service on a 24-hour basis. At all targeted facilities and nearby hospitals, Hebron (Alia) and Ramallah, emergency staff will receive capacity building sessions to be equipped with the skills needed to deal with high-risk pregnancies, urgent deliveries and obstetric emergencies such as bleeding and other complications to save the life of women and their babies. Additionally, the three centers will be supported with the necessary devices and equipment to provide safe birth and maternity services and provide services at the highest level of quality.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Capacity building for MOH emergency staff at emergency and safe motherhood centers on Minimum Initial Service Package for SRH (MISP), EmOc care and basic and advanced life support. Secondary healthcare emergency staff will also be targeted in these training and capacity-building workshops. Activity 2: Support the three centers in Area C with supplies including reproductive health kits, disposables, devices and equipment (e.g: delivery beds, CTG, portable ultrasounds, etc.) to enable the centers to provide a full package of SRHR services.Activity 3: Rehabilitation of space within three centers for safe and private consultations for all women, including pregnant and lactating, adolescent girls who might need information on menstrual hygiene or any other topic, also GBV services and psychosocial support through these spaces can be provided based on individual case needs.Activity 4: Enhancement of the outreach community awareness program in the targeted locations, in close coordination with other organizations and active social networks in the area, to reach women and young people with key messages mainly on symptoms of high-risk pregnancy and family planning messages etc. Community engagement will also be enhanced not only through conducting activities with the community but there will also be an intermittent discussion with beneficiaries and community members through focus group discussions and meetings with the local councils at the targeted villages. UNFPA had had a great experience targeting safe motherhood centers at Aljiftlik clinic, where women and men attend a full participatory day for all community members to identify the SRH needs of women, men, also adolescents to be integrated within those centers. On the other side UNFPA will work toward identifying the potential community capacity to plan and participate in providing services, i.e any doctors or midwives in the targeted communities will be reached and mobilized to be part of the local community task force to better plan for services.

3 - Indicators

The proposed project components and activities will be integrated into the annual work plan between UNFPA and the implementing partner, MOH, for which a detailed monitoring and evaluation plan will be established to specify the actions necessary to track progress on project results and ensure quality assurance of the interventions through a predefined set of quantitative and qualitative indicators. The partner is responsible for the daily monitoring of the interventions and providing regular narrative and financial reports for UNFPA to have full oversight over progress on budget and activities. UNFPA staff will conduct visits to the field and obtain feedback from the targeted population and regularly monitor the implementation progress through planned reviews and discussions with the partner. 10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

UNFPA will act upon the gained understanding with the priority to consider and uphold the rights and to not cause any further negative impact and harm. Safety and security will be guaranteed by upholding dignity and protection in the provision of services focusing on privacy, acceptance and a person’s choices. Partners and other actors of the project will be trained and mentored to deliver non-judgmental, disability-sensitive, and high-quality integrated PHC/SRH services ensuring confidentiality, and privacy, and respecting person’s choices about the care of those seeking help, including persons with disabilities. Safeguards are to be put in place in ensuring confidentiality to not compromised when mobility is restricted. At the same time, the specialized personnel needs to be supported in delivering services and upholding their right and dignity by being treated equitably and fairly.In relation to health facilities, safety and protection will be reached by identifying and securing surrounding areas minimizing threats and vulnerabilities, especially following and implementing guidelines on COVID-19 IPC. Reasonable infrastructure adaptations will be made when needed e.g. ramps, railings, as well as information and communication means like braille sign language interpretation to partners’ health facilities in order to make services more inclusive for all.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project design takes into consideration needs based on gender, age andvulnerabilities, as well as disabilities since the need for SRH and accessibility toservices, are different. Primarily, the project is ensuring that selected communitiesreceive SRH services to improve their quality of life. This project is designed to provideequal and impartial accessibility without barriers and discrimination through allplanned activities. Impartiality is upheld through striving for a large outreach andaccessibility by coordination with other operating actors in combination withcontinuously conducting needs assessments. It is important to highlight andpromote women, girls, and young people’s rights to health care and how/where toobtain it through comprehensive awareness and education sessions e.g. utilizing existingreferral systems.The project is based on the needs of women, girls, and young people, buttake into consideration possible differences in urgent needs, faced risks and facedtraumatic experiences, which are gender, age and disability-specific, vulnerabilitiesand connected to the location of residency. It will primarily target identified vulnerablewomen, girls, young people for crucial SRH including considering the arrangement ofpeoples’ access and availability to services including adapting services andinformation (developing a specific alternative communication strategy in order toensure reaching everyone, especially those at risk of exclusion).That is achieved by offering different alternatives in order to be able to widen the reach and met needs,through existing health clinics (fixed and mobile) and home visits when applicable andenhance services and interventions through other channels such as phone and virtualplatforms. This will allow barriers to be tackled that may restrict freedom of movementand/or people from seeking help, for instance, making sure all persons with disabilitieswho are in need for SRH services are considered, and those who cannot accessservices due to for instance restrictions related to no access to internet orthe technological device, sociocultural norms and restrictions by tackling stigma andmisconceptions at all levels.
