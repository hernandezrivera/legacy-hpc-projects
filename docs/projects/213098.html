Project Summary

The program’s overall objective is to strengthen social cohesion amongst conflict affected communities in North Syria and contribute to long-term peace and stability of Syria. Strengthening cohesive communities is vital to supporting the stability that is finally emerging in North Syria, and reinforcing post-conflict economic recovery. The combination of improving inter-communal relationships and reducing resource-based tensions will serve to prevent the escalation of some economic-based disputes into violence – for example between host and IDP communities over livelihood opportunities or social service provision – and reduce the ability of political actors to manipulate ethno-religious divisions and mobilise violence. To this end, and in close collaboration with local and neighborhood councils, Mercy Corps will implement community service projects to support local economic development and address resource-based tensions. Mercy Corps social cohesion approach is grounded on a sound context, conflict and conflict sensitivity analysis that supports the identification of viable entry points for strengthening social cohesion within and between communities on different sides of the conflict divide. Mercy Corps supports community members with the knowledge, skills and confidence they need to analyze opportunities and impediments to strengthening social cohesion; and to formulate and implement options for strengthening social cohesion at the community level. Through participatory methodology including community mobilization, dialogue, interest based negotiation and the use of sports and creative arts; Mercy Corps will encourage positive interactions between and among community members from across the conflict divide. Mercy Corps will also support conflicting communities to address drivers of conflict through the joint implementation of projects that address resource based conflict drivers. Mercy Corps community mobilization methodology multi-pronged approach to social cohesion Conflict sensitivity is at the heart of all social cohesion activities. The project will develop a conflict sensitivity strategy, guided by various conflict sensitivity analysis tools including the do no harm matrix and stakeholder analysis matrix, building off the findings of context and conflict analysis in the areas of implementation. Mercy Corps social cohesion project will ensure the meaningful participation of all sections of the community with an emphasis on women and youth (male and female).

0 - Project Summary (extended)

PSEA strategy: Participant safeguarding is central to Mercy Corps’ values, and we uphold a zero- tolerance approach towards any form of sexual exploitation and abuse. In order to prevent safeguarding violations of any kind, all staff, casual labor and external contractors receive orientation and training, and are required to sign the Code of Conduct. The safeguarding training is comprehensive and is delivered to all Mercy Corps and partner staff. Where MC and partner staff have higher levels of contact with participants, they will receive tailored capacity strengthening on Protection Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and will participate in quarterly events, which address PSEA concerns in target communities, and facilitate the roll-out of PSEA tools and preventative actions. Partner staff will have the opportunity to risk assess their own areas in terms of safeguarding and create proactive action plans. Community participants will be informed on what safeguarding is and how they can report any concerns through sensitisation work across programs. Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures: (1) Increased inflation. MC will conduct market assessments in order to monitor for price increases. MC will coordinate with UN clusters/peer agencies and follow modalities agreed upon by clusters. (2) Key local stakeholders do not have the interest and/or capacity to fully engage in the project. MC will conduct a stakeholder mapping at the beginning to identify local stakeholders, who have the capacity and willingness to support interventions designed to promote resilience.
