Project Summary

The main humanitarian needs in Sudan are a result of instability and recurrent conflicts. This project aims to address these needs through a multi-sector approach that aligns with the strategic objectives of the Food Security and Livelihoods, Education, and Gender-Based Violence sectors. It is part of the Humanitarian Response Plan 2024, specifically designed to respond to the current conflict in Sudan.
The project will primarily target the most vulnerable internally displaced persons and host communities in Kassala and Red Sea states. Specifically, the project will focus on areas such as Rei Kassala, Reifi Aromaand, Reifi Shamal Ad Delta, Reifi Khashm Elgirba, Reifi Wad Elhilaiw in Kassala state, and Port Sudan, Sawakin, Haya, and Sinkat in Red Sea state.
The intervention aims to improve access to lifesaving assistance for food-insecure individuals and communities, while also addressing issues related to food security, gender-based violence, child protection, and education services.
The current crisis has had a significant impact on education, leading to school closures and a lack of safe learning environments. Additionally, there is a lack of food, which increases vulnerability and the risk of sexual harassment incidents.

For Gender Base Violence
The project aims to raise awareness about gender-based violence (GBV) in the community through campaigns and various initiatives. It includes conducting 300 awareness-raising sessions for community leaders and establishing Women's Centers with operational support. Additionally, the project supports women and girls by providing startup capital for income-generating activities and vocational skills training. It also includes the provision of dignity kits and support for Community-Based Protection Networks' operational costs.
The project further supports the operational costs of four Women's Centers and provides life skills and recreational activities for 1200 women and girls. Additionally, it aims to distribute 20000 dignity kits to the most vulnerable girls and women.
Child Protection:
For protection project will target the 2000 most vulnerable IDPs and host community children, the proposed activities aim to deliver basic Child Protection services including Case Management and referral services, conduct Child Protection Awareness raising and information Dissemination, and Provision of structured mental health and psychosocial support services to girls and boys (individual and group). 3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

To ensure that gender and protection are mainstreamed in the program, JASMAR will abide by Do No Harm principles. JASMAR will also strive for gender balance in recruiting staff at all levels and will train officers responsible for delivering services and inputs to participants on gender issues and gender-sensitive approaches. JASMARs’ in-house gender focal point will provide a solid foundation for the program. Recognizing the critical role of women in agriculture and household nutrition, a minimum of 40 percent of participants will be women, specifically under FSL interventions. Limited access to education services forces school-age children to walk long distances to reach schools. Lack of GBV services often exposes women & girls to additional protection risks; GBV interventions will help to reduce these risks through provision of the essential infrastructure, which will offer community-based GBV services at the community level. In addition, JASMAR will ensure that women and girls are represented in the community-level committees – a minimum of 50 percent of committee members.
