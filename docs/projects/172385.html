Project Summary

The overall objective of this project is to strengthen capacity of Ministry of Health, National Center for Disease Control and health sector partners for rapid detection of public health risks and events with a focus on COVID19/TB/HIV, prompt risk assessment, notification and response through enhanced sentinel surveillance, event based surveillance and community surveillance structures. Furthermore the project will support the collection, processing, analysis and dissemination of critical health information including disease surveillance data and access to the availability of life-saving services.
The project is in line with objective 3 of the health chapter of Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP). It is also in line with strategic objective 1 of the same document.
In this project, WHO will address the capacity gaps to ensure compliance of all health facilities and sentinel sites with the minimum standards of disease surveillance reporting with a focus on COVID19, ILI/SARI and TB/HIV surveillance. Vulnerable groups including migrants, returnees, displaced families and poor host communities will remain the direct beneficiaries of the project as it will detect, respond to and contain outbreaks at an early stage.
Considering the potential for rapid and exponential growth of COVID-19 outbreaks, new cases and clusters should be identified and reported as rapidly as possible, and data should be included in any relevant epidemiological analyses within 24 hours of diagnosis. National authorities should include COVID-19 as a mandatory notifiable disease with requirements for immediate reporting.
Surveillance systems should be geographically comprehensive, and surveillance for vulnerable or high-risk populations should be enhanced. This will require a combination of surveillance systems including contact tracing for all levels of the health-care system, at the community level, in closed residential settings and in other vulnerable groups.

1 - Needs

Libya needs significantly strengthened surveillance capacities for priority diseases like COVID19 , TB, HIV and others to rapidly identify and care for cases of these health events, trace and quarantine their contacts, where needed and monitor disease trends over time. Comprehensive national surveillance for COVID-19 will require the adaptation and reinforcement of existing national systems, where appropriate, and the scale-up of additional surveillance capacities like ILI/SARI surveillance, Event Based Surveillance and Community based Surveillance strategies. Digital technologies for rapid reporting, contact tracing and data management and analysis may support these capacities. Robust comprehensive surveillance, once in place, should be maintained even in areas where transmission has been suppressed or controlled, even if there are few or no cases. It is critical that new cases and clusters of COVID-19 be detected rapidly beforeoutbreaks and/or widespread transmission occurs. Ongoing surveillance for COVID-19 is also important to understand longer-term epidemiological trends, such as incidence and mortality among different age groups, which population groups are at higher risk for severe disease and death and potential epidemiological changes over time. It is important to maintain routine syndromic surveillance for other infectious diseases, especially those caused by respiratory pathogens, such as influenza and respiratory syncytial virus, through surveillance for influenza-like-illness (ILI), severe acute respiratory infection (SARI), atypical pneumonia and unexplained fever, with sampling and laboratory testing of all or a subset of cases. This is critical for understanding trends in other diseases with similar presentations to guide appropriate public health preparedness and clinical management.The current needs of COVID-19 surveillance are to support the existing COVID19 surveillance system in Libya to• enable rapid detection, isolation, testing, and management of cases• monitor trends in COVID-19 deaths• identify, follow-up and quarantine of contacts• detect and contain clusters and outbreaks, especially among vulnerable populations• guide the implementation and adjustment of targeted control measures, while enabling safe resumption of economic and social activities• evaluate the impact of the pandemic on health-care systems and society• monitor longer term epidemiologic trends and evolution of SARS-CoV-2 virus• contribute to the understanding of the co-circulation of SARS-CoV-2 virus, influenza and other respiratory viruses, and other pathogens.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

4.1:WHO’s network of field officers work at the municipality level, where all emergency activities are implemented. The field officers report to WHO’s office in Tripoli on a regular basis. Local community networks, CBOs and community influencers ensure the accountability of WHO and partners’ activities at the municipality level.Foster mutual accountability on the domestic budget made available for C-19, as well as for external pledges.WHO is also a member of the United Nations Evaluation Group, which brings together the evaluation units of UN departments, specialized agencies, and affiliated organizations to share best practices and advocate for the importance of evaluation for learning, decision-making and accountability.WHO’s fundamental principles of integrity, accountability, independence, impartiality, respect and professional commitment are documented in its code of ethics and professional conduct4.2: Previous implementation of COVID related activities had the experience of communities against COVID-facilities and doctors, splitting distributed supplies between East and West, tribe approach when distributing supplies. This would be mitigated by ensuring distribution of supplies based on equity and equality starting from distribution, using the most closest sea port available in the region for supplies in that region instead of receiving it far away from the area and advocating for addressing these risks at the highest levels by leadership of WHO in Libya. . 4.4 : Project activities not only expose the WHO staff to COVID19 but as the COVID assistance increases , more people get involved in the field in COVID related activities which are indispensable for the COVID response itself. The project at the same time addresses the issue by providing PPE supplies and IPC training and testing assistance to the healthcare workers, WHO staff in office and the field and the communities.4.6: It is made sure that no personal data regarding case management of patients is being collected during any of the project activities. WHO ensures a strict internal and external controls for data protection and data privacy. 4.23: The project will achieve its overall objective by coordinating the financing and response actions needed to reduce avoidable mortality and morbidity and reduce the economic, social, and security impacts of the pandemic both for migrants and the Libyan population. The main challenges faced by the Libyan population post C-19 that need to be addressed include the failure to rapidly mobilize financing for appropriate detection and response efforts in Libyan migrants in particular and the Libyan population in general will result in increased mortality and morbidity, significant economic consequences and reduced resilience. By focusing on the containment, diagnosis and treatment of patients, this Project seeks to help control the pandemic and limit socioeconomic losses. Some of the project activities, including those focused on training and capacity building, will help strengthen the preparedness of the country to combat other future epidemics, and they are expected to be sustained even after this Project closes. There is a growing awareness in Libya of the importance of preparedness against epidemics and pandemics in general, and this is an agenda that is expected to receive growing support and financing in the future.

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

CFM in Libya: With the support of the the Government of Luxembourg, the ETS is bridging this gap by establishing Tawasul (“communication” in Arabic) a Common Feedback Mechanism (CFM) in the form of a single, toll-free, country-wide hotline number that affected populations can call to obtain information on humanitarian assistance programs; submit their feedback; and get referrals to the humanitarian organizations that are best-suited to handle their needs. In turn, the CFM helps humanitarians work more efficiently towards meeting those needs. Through partnerships with 6 UN agencies and 5 Non-Governmental Organizations (NGOs), the CFM particularly aims to assist women, children, Internally Displaced People (IDPs), migrants, refugees, asylum seekers and digitally illiterate individuals.
