Project Summary

In projection period of August to December 2020, and in the total absence of humanitarian food assistance, an estimated 409,850 people (67% of the population) will face Crisis acute food insecurity or worse, an increase of 8% from the 201,866 people (63% of the population) from the same period in 2019. The recent survey conducted by ACF in Akobo, Bor South and FSNMS (in Ayod),reported that critical nutrition situations with global acute malnutrition (GAM) prevalence of 19.4% in Akobo West, 25.5 % in Ayod and 20.0% in Bor South and above, the World Health Organization emergency threshold. Humanitarian situation in Jonglei (Ayod , Akobo West and Bor South Counties ) remains critical due to repeated devastation, displacement and at the same time returnees.
Previous nutrition responses have been stretched by an influx of IDP’s and host community. Most families are without employment; most population heavily reliant on humanitarian assistance. (WFP 2018-2020).
CMD will be implementing minimum package of Integrating Out Patient Therapeutic Program (OTP) and Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP) within existing health facilities by providing routine treatment using simple medical protocol to SAM without medical complications. In addition, CMD will integrate Health, WASH and FSL into Nutrition targeting the 26 OTP/TSFP sites and 3 SC’s within Northern and Southern Ayod this includes Pagil, Menime, Normanyang, Kandak, Mogok, Jiech, Gorwai, Padek, Kuachdeng, Haat, Kharmun, Mhaar, Ayod town, where CMD has existing health facilities while SC will be situated in Mogok, Gorwai and Padek to treat 780 SAM children with medical complications. In Bor South and Akobo West CMD will integrate OTP/TSFP services and leverage on its ongoing responses on thematic areas of WASH, Health and Education. CMD currently runs WASH interventions in Ayod ,in Bor South and Akobo, provision of nutrition services through integration of WASH and Health within the existing health facilities. CMD proposes to continue delivering nutrition services in Ayod County, Akobo West and Bor South in collaboration with MoH/CHD, partners and local authorities through supporting initiatives for the decentralization of OTP/TSFP and SC sites, increasing awareness through community mobilization on: IYCF/MIYCN Practices for prevention of Malnutrition, socio-mobilization for vitamin A supplementation, deworming and advocacy for the proper use of LLITN. Provision of Hygiene Kits and livelihood kits to caregivers admitted with SAM/MAM Children into the Program.
With UNICEF and WFP collaboration, they will enable CMD provide targeted emergency nutrition supplies and services to a SAM caseload of up to 5787 (Including 1020 children with complicated severe acute cases) in need (< 5 years both boys and girls) and MAM caseload of up to 13,261(<5 years) and PLW’s caseload of 12,902.
CMD will train Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs) and Mother-to-Mother support groups from host community to acquire skills and knowledge on Integrated Management of SAM and Maternal Infant and young child feeding practices so as to provide quality nutrition services. Health staff and Nutrition staffs will be capacitated for integration of SAM and MAM management into health programmes especially on malaria screening and referral of severe cases to OTP and Stabilization centers.

1 - Needs

The findings of the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) analysis for June to December 2020 indicate a worsening of food insecurity in Ayod, Bor South and Akobo due to prolonged heavy floods which destroyed all the crops with a projection that 611,716 people within 3 states (63% of the total population) will face Crisis (IPC Phase 4 and 5 ) acute food insecurity or worse in the projection period of February to July 2020. Increased with 37% compared to 2019 projection. According to the most recent SMART surveys in each of these counties, the nutrition situation in Ayod, Akobo West (Barmach)and Bor South (Makuolcuei) remains critical, one of the worse in South Sudan. The three mentioned Counties are likely to fall under extreme Critical level (IPC Phase 5). Nutrition cluster target caseload indicates has GAM rate of 25.5%SAM rate of 3.8%,MAM rate 19.6% and prevalence of malnutrition in PLW is 38.2%.Ayod has GAM rate of 20.2%;SAM rate of 3.6%,MAM rate 12.6% and Prevalence of malnutrition PLW is 34.2%.Akobo West GAM rate of 20.0%,SAM rate of 3.8%,MAM rate 16.2% PLW prevalence 32.4%.If the current drivers of food insecurity worsen through the end November 2020 and in the absence of humanitarian assistance, there is a heightened risk of emergency as large populations are already experiencing severe food insecurity led by current floods. Settlements within the locations are using extreme coping strategies such as only children eating, and spending days without food more than doubling between August (36%) and October (45%). The level of acute malnutrition is expected to deteriorate from October onwards with late lean seasons as most households are expected to deplete food stocks from own production. CMD will help in providing Therapeutic foods for the Acute Malnourished children (SAM/MAM) Malnourished children under 5 years (<5-year children) and pregnant and lactating mothers (PLW’s) and treatment of Severe Acute Malnourished children with medical complications in Akobo West (Barmach payams),Ayod(Mogok, Pagil, Wau,Padek and Kuachdeng Payams), Bor South( Makuolcuei Payam). The approved fund will help Severe Acute Malnourished children caseload of 5,787, 13,261 Moderate Acute Malnourished children under 5 years (<5-year children) caseload and 12,902 pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) and caregivers to the existing OTP’s and SC and rehabilitation of OTP sites and SC within health facilities in the entire Paymas, through incorporation of TSFP services. A lack of intervention will erode previous gains and worsen the nutrition situation and increase the effects of Acute malnutrition within the states. The high SAM and GAM rates have been attributed to severe food insecurity, suboptimal infant and young child feeding practices (IYCF) widespread insecurity, displacement, Floods and poor access to services, high morbidity and extremely poor diets. Access to food and water is very limited women and adolescent walk for long distance source for food and water. IPC projections place Ayod , Akobo and Bor South falls under IPC phase 4 and 5 with possibility of sliding into phase 4 in the event of absence of humanitarian support. Communities cultivate once a year, with seasonal erratic patterns hampering harvest. Lack of clean and safe water adequate for domestic and livelihoods use is directly linked to high malnutrition. CMD will integrate FSL into nutrition where vegetable seeds and tools will be distributed to women admitted with malnourished children, at the OTP sites, demonstration of kitchen garden training will be done at the nutrition sites, where mothers will be encouraged to practice home gardening to reduce malnutrition in in the community. CMD will provide beneficiary lists identified to ensure that those nutrition caseloads are included in food and livelihood support.

2 - Project activities

The project will focus on reduction of morbidity and mortality, and suffering from protection threats and incidents of most vulnerable (5,787 SAM,13,261 MAM and 12,902 PLW) Ayod (4000 SAM,12,006 MAM and 11,035 PLW’s) (SAM 1,126,MAM 1,715,PLW 1,114 in Akobo), and Bor South (SAM 661,MAM 907,PLW 754) by 36% in August and 45% in November respectively. The Project will target 6 bomas in Ayod) Normanyang, Buot, Mhaar, Kuachdeng, Paguong and Gaar,1 payams in Akobo West (Barmach payams)and Bor South (Makuolcuei -Baldit Payam).The project will support 3 existing SC’s ,6 OTP’s and SFP in Ayod,1 OTP/TSFP in Akobo West and 51OTP/TSFP in Bor South in order to provide services to the affected population within and outside the villages of Ayod, Akobo West and Bor South. The project is expected to run for 1 year in order to reduce the number of SAM cases in Ayod, Akobo West and Bor South. Integration of nutrition into FSL, GBV, WASH and HEALTH will be encouraged through capacity building of the MTMSGS, CNV’s, Health and Nutrition staffs. Health and WASH education will be encouraged and shared to the caregivers/mothers during admissions at the OTP sites. The program will ensure all caregivers in the Stabilization centers and OTP have received seeds and tools admission of SAM cases and will be encouraged to practice kitchen gardening. The trained CNV’s will provide services through moving to the villages, screening, identify, passing messages on MIYCN to mothers, family MUAC Screening Approach, follow up of the recoveries and refer SAM cases to the OTP sites. Mother to Mother Support Groups trained will be able to provide messages on the important of IYCF to the caregivers/mothers at the facility level and community level. CMD Nutrition staffs and health staffs trained will provide quality services to the SAM cases within the OTP’s and Stabilization centers. During admission nutrition is integrated into health and so screening of malaria will be at the triage, MUAC, Distribution of ILLT to SAM/MAM admitted children, referral of SAM cases who are severely malnourished is done within Health and Nutrition compound where CMD provides all these services initiate the same in the new established area.CMD M&E officer will be visiting the field monthly and with field supervisors will monitor the Nutrition activities across the 3 counties(Ayod, Bor South and Akobo West)and its payams and share the reports to CMD Juba office, UNICEF/WFP focal point person, Nutrition Cluster and UNOCHA. CMD will leverage on its ongoing responses on thematic areas of WASH, Health and Education. CMD currently runs WASH, Health interventions in Ayod, mainly through the provision of WASH services at nutrition OTPs and SCs run by CMD. CMD will link/integrate the program with Health partners on ground.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

PSEA information will be cascaded to beneficiaries through clear communication channels, starting with staff and stakeholders who will be trained on PSEA protocols. Posters, Banners will be availed specifically on PSEA but also other IEC materials will have information on PSEA. Workshops, trainings, seminars will have sessions of PSEA, while CMD takes on specific campaigns on PSEA.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Feedback from the most vulnerable will be obtained through direct interviews in languages they understand most, and focus group discussions. Visual and picture messaging will be utilized too, to pass on and obtain information from the most vulnerable.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

In accordance with humanitarian protocols pertaining to feedback or complaint mechanisms, CMD will implement specific activities in regard to this. Feedback mechanisms will be enhanced through establishment of clear communication pathways, that will be clearly communicated to communities in local dialects that are most understood by beneficiaries. Mechanisms of obtaining information from beneficiaries will be enhanced, through interviews with key informants, beneficiary households, as well as community members. Suggestion boxes will be installed at key field locations, that will be regularly attended to by designated personnel. CMD will set up hotlines, that key stakeholders can reach incase of any feedback/complaint. Feedback/complaint reports will be documented and shared with stakeholders, aimed at informing future project decisions.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback on key project activities will be used to make adjustments on project deliverables, as well as enable proper future planning. Beneficiary feedback will be documented and shared with key stakeholders.
