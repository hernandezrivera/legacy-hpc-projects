Project Summary

According to Somalia’s Humanitarian Needs Overview, an estimated 2.9 million people are displaced in Somalia (2021 HNO). The displaced population’s coping framework have been affected over successive years of drought, floods and conflict, leaving them vulnerable to shocks and inhibiting a sustainable return to places of origin. The October Flash updates shared within the cluster in October, only 2 of the 37 IDP sites in Guri'el are being supported with CCCM services which some 22 percent of IDP sites in Guri'el have been served eviction notices within the last three months and 67 percent of other sites in the districts of Jowhar and Wanlawayn are at high risk of flooding and recurrent tribal conflicts. In Gaashaan and Damaanyo sites, food and non-food items (NFI) support are not being delivered.
The Project will monitor the displacement conditions in all the targeted districts, especially where there are no CCCM coordination structures in place or no IDP assessment of any kind has been conducted. The needs of women, girls, men, boys of all ages, the elderly, people living with disabilities, and minorities will be identified and addressed throughout the planning, implementation, and monitoring processes. The surrounding town of Jowhar such as Mahadaay and other areas will also be covered within these improvement services. A recurrent tribal infights have caused for huge displacements to the indigenous and minority tribes in Mahadaay in the past years.
SOMLIFE will take into account variations in gender, tribal origin, PLWD, and age that predispose the target population to sexual exploitation and abuse, extremes of the weather of floods and diseases, and differently influence their coping strategies. The project's core activities which are aligned to the CCCM cluster strategy will unfolds as follows:
(1) Strengthening local capacity to respond to the primary CCCM needs in these 3 districts by working with community structures and coordinating with other actors to complement and cover gaps in CCCM services.
(2) Improving the capacity of Camp Committees, Leaders and host communities by providing training in selected priority areas. (3) Enhancing collaboration with stakeholders at the local level to improve coordination, prudent use of resources, and increasing synergy between various actors for the maximum benefit of the target communities. (4) Provision of community-based CCCM Services and referral of people with various health conditions to the health facilities. (5) Identifying the gender excluded from access to services and opportunities in the design policy and/or project-level responses that attempt to address the barriers in the project cycle implementation, carry out monitoring and evaluation (M&E) to check whether planned resources and actions have reached women, men, boys, girls, the poor and all the excluded and adjusting/redesigning on a continuous basis based on M&E findings.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

The mitigation measures are as follows: 1- SOMLIFE will engage the authority for clearance and provision of security during the project's operational exercises (distribution). 2- Do not construct distribution in an open ground, use community centers or fenced compound to restrict access to only listed beneficiaries. 3- Where distribution is conducted at the agency's office, provide transport allowances to beneficiaries back to their camps or homes. 4- Use cash/food vouchers where beneficiaries can visit a supplier anytime to receive their share instead of open distribution. 5- SOMLIFE will coordinate with the service providers and cluster members to ensure that the services are delivered in a humane manner without discrimination of the marginalized, including people with disabilities. 6- SOMLIFE and IPs will put in place a community feedback mechanism (CFM) where people will be mobilized and sensitized to be aware of the services available and their rights to access the services without discrimination, in addition to being made aware of the selection criteria to ensure that the people selected to benefit from the various services are deserving and meet the criteria and contrary to which will be report through the CFM.7- SOMLIFE and community members will also conduct sensitization and promotion of participation of vulnerable groups (women, girls, youth and PLWDs) and ensure that there is inclusion in the addressing the needs of the various segments of the people targeted.8- Assess the ownership of land if government owned or private before establishing any facility on the land. 9- Engage local authorities on the allocation of public land with lease of over 5 years to ensure long term sustainability of projects 10- On private land ensure agreement is signed with the land owners for a lease of over 5 years to establish community structures like community centers in an IDP camps. 11- Engage directly with the owners of the land rather than gate keepers to understand land tenure processes. 12- Validate with the regional authority’s tenure documents produced by private land owners to ensure claimed land owners are known before proceeding in any engagement.13- SOMLIFE follows a code of conduct that prohibits any sexual or other exploitation of beneficiaries, this will be adhered to by all partner and implementation staff and any deviations will be dealt with accordingly.14- The project will empower the community to identify areas in and around the camp that could be potentially unsafe or risk of violence, especially GBV around the bathrooms, latrines, routes to school, health canter or water points.
