Project Summary

Mine Action has been identified as one of the key areas of concern within the Protection Cluster as civilian casualty rates are expected to increase in line with the rate of IDP returns. This has already clearly been reflected in casualty data collected and analysed by the Danish Refugee Council-Danish Demining Group (DRC-DDG) in 2018. As reflected in the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2019, three major interventions are crucially required: (1) humanitarian demining, to allow the contaminated land and infrastructure being put back into productive use, sustaining jobs and livelihoods whilst directly supporting existing DRC-DDG humanitarian initiatives in the Livelihoods and Legal sectors; (2) Mine Risk Education (MRE), preventing injuries and saving lives for individuals still living in conflict-affected communities; (3) capacity building of state Mine Action (MA) institutions through the provision of training and equipment as well as national-level advocacy on MA legislation, standards, and frameworks. With this three-fold approach, the project will significantly contribute to several protection cluster objectives and to the overall HRP 2020 Objective 2.

1 - Needs

As to the (1) humanitarian demining, DRC-DDG has recorded a total number of 1,597 mine/ERW casualties in 818 accidents from June 2014 to January 2019 – this translate into 28,5 casualties per month. According to the most recent Landmine Monitor report, Ukraine ranks third in the world for casualties from such devices. Since 2016, the DRC-DDG has been conducting non-technical surveys (NTS) and collectively with other international organisations mapped approximately 15km2 of the hazardous area. Most of the contamination is likely within the buffer zone which remains to be comprehensibly surveyed and quantified. The most vulnerable populations there are those remaining in affected areas for economic reasons, due to poor health or age or because they are financially dependent on remaining family members. This makes contamination with mines/ERW/IEDs a serious protection concern. According to OHCHR, a total of 279 conflict-related civilian casualties were recorded in 2018 and of these 42,7% were a result of mine/ERW accidents. Furthermore, a June 2018 REACH survey covering 2,565 households in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts (GCA) uncovered local perceptions of the mine/ERW issue similar to those documented by the DRC-DDG NTS of 2016 and KAP survey of 2017. The survey found that households were most likely to perceive the location of mines to be in small urban areas (52%). The second most commonly reported location was near their household (42%), followed by agricultural land (18%), and along the road (15%). Finally, there are two gender and age considerations the DRC-DDG will make: internal and external. Internally, the DRC-DDG strongly encourages female applicants for demining positions. Currently, the DRC-DDG already employs two female deminers, showcasing that traditional perceptions of demining are passé. Secondly, the current data shows that mine and ERW-related casualties happen far more frequently among adult women and men (93%) which is why the efforts of this project will primarily be focused on them. This includes (2) MRE activities. A detailed analysis based on the data captured in the DRC-DDG casualties database shows that in the age group 0-17 years 42% of accidents occurred as a result of tampering with ERW whilst in the age group of 18-60 years casualties were mainly inflicted during the following activities: traveling (30%), household work (15%) and crossing EECPs (9%). From DRC-DDG ECHO pilot project of delivering MRE sessions at the EECPs, it is evident that over a half of beneficiaries (54%) in these locations are women; this will be taken into account for the modality and content of the MRE sessions under this project. Contamination from mines and other ERW not only stands to erode the resilience of the most vulnerable people left behind in conflict-affected areas, it also poses as serious and widespread obstruction to recovery and reconstruction efforts. Unfortunately, Ukraine has not received the corresponding level of donor attention, albeit requiring (3) demining capacity building and equipment to tackle a challenge of such proportion. The very essence of the problem in Ukraine is not the lack of human capacity, nor the lack of will to get the job done; it is the lack of a framework and methodology around which to do so. As just one example, Ukraine still does not have a centralised accident reporting mechanism and database for logging casualties of landmines and UXO, therefore DRC-DDG has taken the initiative to collect this information from a variety of credible sources into a database (currently holding over 1,500 casualties). Where applicable, this database also contains a desegregated breakdown by age and gender – continuously informing the targeted programming of MA activities. Such targeted investments are key to avoid a more protracted and costly interventions later.

2 - Activities & outcomes

Activity 1: Mine Risk Education, Direct Sessions. Output: Increased safety and security of school-aged children, conflict-affected communities, and IDPs. Outcome: Higher awareness about the risks associated with mines/ERW. Attitudes are effectively changed to encourage less risky behavior and civilian casualties decrease. Activity 2: Mine Risk Education, Training of Trainers. Output: Personnel from schools and communities within the target area are trained to provide MRE, creating a more sustainable capacity. Outcome: Higher awareness about the risks associated with mines/ERW. Attitudes are effectively changed to encourage less risky behaviour and civilian casualties decrease. Activity 3: Land Released. Output: Land and infrastructure is cleared of landmines and ERW. Outcome: Fewer civilian casualties, income generation opportunities are increased, resilience of conflict-affected communities is restored. IDPs are able to safely return. Activity 4: Non-Technical Survey. Output: Eastern Ukraine continues to be systematically surveyed in order to accurately quantify the scale and scope of the threat. Hazardous areas are mapped, analysed and prioritised according to humanitarian impact. Outcome: Existing capacity can be deployed more effectively according to the humanitarian needs. Ukraine is cleared of landmines and ERW in a more efficient and well-coordinated manner. Activity 5: Training of national institutions. Output: State institutions are trained in key skills such as information management, manual demining, EOD, task mapping, MRE. Outcome:The government of Ukraine is better positioned to tackle the issue of landmines and ERW in a unified, standardised and transparent manner. Activity 6: Advocacy interventions at the national level. Output: Government of Ukraine is encouraged to bring about much-needed legislative reform in the demining sector. Outcome: A national mine action centre is created leading to improved and more effective coordination of mine action.
