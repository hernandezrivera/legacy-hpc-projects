Project Summary

IOM aims to facilitate the immediate, adequate and equitable delivery of services to the displaced population in camp and camp-like settings through camp coordination and camp management (CCCM) services, information management mechanisms, and sector coordination of partners and stakeholders.
CCCM activities will continue to provide site facilitation support and site-level coordination in formal and informal camps in the following Borno locations: MMC, Bama (including Banki), Dikwa, Gwoza (including Pulka,) Ngala, Monguno, Mafa, Jere, Kala Balge (Rann), and Damboa; and in the following Adamawa locations: Yola North, Yola South, Girie and Song.
As Sector Co-Lead, IOM, with UNHCR, is accountable for the overall CCCM, Shelter and non-food items (NFI) responses in Northeast Nigeria. The sector will continue to ensure access to multi-sectoral assistance in camps and camp-like settings, advocating and identifying durable solutions for displaced families and affected communities and building national capacity through CCCM trainings for national authorities and local partners

1 - Needs

After more than 13 years of conflict, violence and insecurity, North-East Nigeria continues to remain a large-scale humanitarian and protection crisis, affecting the lives, livelihoods and wellbeing of millions of people in the region as well as cross-border. Population movements still present major humanitarian challenges to the already stretched resources. While camp management structures have been established under the lead of the National Emergency Management Agency (NEMA) and State Emergency Management Agencies (SEMA) in the main sites hosting internally displaced populations (IDPs), camp management staff presence and multi-sectorial response gaps remain in 2022. The latest Displacement Tracking Matrix Round 42 (July 2022) assessments have accounted for 2,455,190 displaced persons and 1,983,130 returnees. In the last three months, an average of 700 new arrivals to IDP sites have been recorded per month. In August 2020, the Borno State government (BSG) announced they would close all camps and resettle over 1.8 million IDPs from camps in Maiduguri Metropolitan City (MMC) and Jere to various communities within the state territory. The government-led efforts have created several risks and hardships for those requested to leave the camps. Before closure, Maiduguri was hosting around 300,000 IDPs in formal and informal camps managed by IOM and NGO partners. The BSG identified to close 10 formal camps in Maiduguri that were using government facilities. Eight of these camps, hosting 139,041 IDPs, were closed from May 2021 up to August 2022 . Between 27 July and 02 August 2022, , Dalori II IDP camp was closed , and 1,741 HHs were relocated to Soye in Bama LGA and to Dalori village in Konduga LGA. Dalori I camp was closed between 4 - 7 August, affecting 7,478 IDPs. Gubio camp closure also commenced in December 2022 with a phased approach beginning with the relocation of IDPs from Marte, so far relocating 4,368 individuals. The exercise is expected to continue and will be extended to the camp populace from other local government areas (LGAs). Following the closure of the camps, numerous IDPs have integrated into the host communities in Maiduguri, and in surrounding LGAs. Others have returned to IDP sites in LGAs like Bama and Gwoza, where IDPs experience high levels of overcrowding and congestion. Muna El-badawy camp hosting 51,199 IDPs is the only camp in MMC remained to be closed. IOM will continue its CCCM interventions in IDP camps currently still hosting a number of IDPs and continuous new arrivals, especially following the camp closures in Maiduguri and Jere. The spontaneous nature of informal sites coupled with existing gaps in humanitarian assistance makes individuals in displacement sites extremely vulnerable to many risks, including, but not limited to, flooding, wind, disease outbreak, etc. The living conditions of IDPs and their situation on the ground remains substandard with most of them depending on humanitarian assistance for years. Most of the IDP sites are in locations with poor drainage conditions causing floods during the rainy season. These flooded sites pose a risk of disease outbreaks and continued damage to infrastructure which is already strained. As such, sites with these conditions require continuous site care and maintenance interventions to ensure basic standards and living conditions.

2 - Activities

IOM will continue to provide and enhance its site facilitation capacities in formal and informal camps and camp-like settings to ensure that IDPs have access to basic services and improved living conditions. This will be achieved through the continuous deployment of mobile camp management teams. IOM CCCM operations will be guided by the CCCM Camp Management Standards and agreed humanitarian standards in Northeast Nigeria, ensuring community participation in all aspects of camp life. IOM CCCM will continue the provision of the following services: • Coordination and information management. • Communication with Communities.• Site improvement and care and maintenance of camp/site infrastructures • Community participation • Protection mainstreaming and disability inclusion IOM will continue to support IDPs living outside the camps by establishing community centers, mobile teams, and community structures. Following the camp closure and in line with the CCCM sector strategy, IOM will incorporate an area-based approach/programming where CCCM activities are implemented in an area and target all the persons and displacement sites in that area. The focus is on multi-sectoral needs and localization as well as support existing coordination and governance structures. IOM will also support the decongestion strategy of the Sector in Pulka and Dikwa through site preparation, development and community mobilization to provide adequately spaced shelters and mitigate further risks related to safety and health. IOM CCCM will continue the management of reception centers for new arrivals in Bama, Banki, Dikwa, Ngala, and Gwoza. CCCM Capacity Development programs will continue for government counterparts, IDP communities, national authorities, and key stakeholders and partners. IOM, through its role as a co-chair of the CCCM/Shelter/Non-food items (NFI) Sector, will improve coordination of sector partners and continued support to coordination mechanisms set up for deep eld locations and hard-to-reach areas.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

IOM mitigates protection risks and minimizes gender-based violence (GBV) and sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) risks in camps and camp-like settings through enhanced community participation and consultations with communities, such as identification of risks and hazards with representatives of the communities to plan site improvement works and install safety measures; inclusion of women and girls in decision-making fora in camps; training of entry points areas on prevention of and sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA); training of camp management staff and community volunteers on GBV and PSEA; and working with Protection, GBV, Child Protection and Health actors in sites ensuring referral pathways are functional and active. IOM’s commitment to preventing and mitigating risks of GBV in emergencies, including in camps and camp-like settings, is captured in its Institutional Framework for Addressing GBV in Crises (GBViC Framework). IOM’s CCCM response includes the outlined risk mitigation measures in this Framework and works with various Protection and GBV actors in planning and implementation. As part of regular CCCM activities in camps, the camp management teams will raise awareness on GBV and PSEA, reporting mechanisms and referral pathways.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

IOM will ensure accountability to the affected population throughout all stages of the project life cycle. During the design of the project and throughout the planning stages, IOM will promote active participation and feedback from the targeted population in assessing their needs and specific vulnerabilities, including those of women, youth, elderly, and persons with disabilities, among others. Mapping of community structures and having focus group discussions with IDP representatives, Persons with special Needs such as persons with disabilities, child headed households, pregnant and lactating women as well as other vulnerable groups will ensure the community is fully involved in the decision-making process. IOM will also seek regular feedback from the targeted population during the implementation of the project, including through the camp management committees. Affected populations may also provide feedback or submit complaints through the suggestion boxes or other elements of the community-based complaint and feedback mechanism. This mechanism is managed by the IOM Camp Coordination and Camp Management Unit. IOM has already established complaints and feedback mechanisms (CFM) in many sites across Borno State which have been functioning well as two-way communication between the community and the agency. These are also shared with other agencies accountable for their respective sectors.IOM has already taken several steps to ensure accountability to affected population: findings from beneficiary surveys, some of which have already been incorporated into the design of this project. IOM also actively uses practical recommendations from beneficiary feedback surveys as advocacy tools at the sector level to influence change.
