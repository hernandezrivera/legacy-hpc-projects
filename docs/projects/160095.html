Project Summary

The purpose of the project is to save lives and alleviate suffering through safe access to services and resources with dignity by targeting delivering quality lifesaving management of acute malnutrition to children of 0-59 months of age with SAM and MAM respectively including PLW with MAM with improved emergency referral services for communities in Jonglei State. The goal of the project is to contribute to the reduction in malnutrition related mortality and morbidity, and improve access to high quality lifesaving emergency nutrition interventions for the most vulnerable populations notably U5 children and pregnant and lactating women.
In spite of ongoing intervention, GAM rate is still at 27.4 %, MAM at 21.5% and SAM at 5.9 %( HDC 2019) which are above the WHO threshold for emergency intervention.
These statistics show the significant need to step up efforts on provision of essential emergency nutrition services for the next 12 month, therefore, treatment of SAM and MAM for children below five years as well as PLW continues to be one of key need for saving lives of most at risk in Jonglei State.
This project intents to reach 1,800(M=864, F=936); SAM cases and 10,425(M=5,004, F=5,421) MAM cases for children of 6-59 month of age respectively as well as 3,012 MAM cases who are vulnerable people among IDPs, returnees and host communities who have no access to Nutrition interventions.
The proposed project objectives will be:
1) To deliver timely equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving management and prevention of acute malnutrition for the most vulnerable and at risk, especially girls and boys under five years of age, adolescents and pregnant and lactating women in Jonglei by the end of 2020.
2) To increased equitable access and utilization of quality Maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition services to prevent under nutrition among the most vulnerable and at risk in Jonglei by the end of 2020;
3) To improve timely submission of quality nutrition information system to enhance planning for nutrition response in Jonglei by the end of 2020.
4) To increased equitable access to integrated Nutrition, Health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection interventions targeting vulnerable groups in Jonglei by the end of 2020.

1 - Needs

The nutrition situation in Duk remains critical due to a combination of dry spells, poor harvest, and past conflicts. The current deterioration in food security and nutrition is primarily due to physical insecurity, the effects of the economic crisis and depleted stocks from the last harvest has made most households food insecure (FSNMS, July 2019). The County is one of the hardest to reach areas of the country, because of poor road conditions and insecurity inhabited by an extremely vulnerable and traumatized community majority being women, girls, boys, and elderly people who are physically and psychologically exposed to multitude of stressors. The Nutrition Information System (NIS) indicates a significant increase in admissions in the period January to June 2019 compared to the same period in 2018 with average monthly increase of 380 new admissions of children under five with severe acute malnutrition. The increased numbers of admissions are attributed to increased coverage of nutrition services OTP/TSFP sites, increased food insecurity, displacements and movements, poor WASH services and disease burden of population with 39,000 IDP by flooding.JDF has also observed key social and cultural determinants of malnutrition to children under the age of five years such as food sharing, introducing water to babies younger than 6 months old, and polygamy families with limited resources to look after family. In addition, hard to reach areas have inadequate supplies to implement CMAM, IYCF and VAS campaign with in adequate consistent technical staffs for provision of CMAM, IYCF and VAS campaign associated with few of them trained on CMAM.In spite of ongoing intervention, recent SMART survey still revealed that GAM rate is still high at 27.4 %, MAM at 21.5% and SAM at 5.9 %( HDC 2019) which are above the WHO threshold for emergency intervention. There is also high burden of preventable diseases caused mainly by malaria (80%), diarrhea (15%) and pneumonia (5%) in U 5 years which have a direct negative impact on the nutrition status of young children and calls for high sensitization campaigns for timely health seeking behavior, good hygiene practices and public health initiatives (RRHP Report 2019). The presence of multiple risk factors over a prolonged period contributed to increased incidence of Malnutrition, malaria, RTI and AWD, widespread SGBV and a high number of trauma wounds including gunshots.These statistics show the significant need to step up efforts on provision of essential emergency nutrition services for the next 12 month. This has placed Duk County to be one of the cluster priority counties for nutrition intervention for 2020.As per HRP 2019, the target groups was 22,830(M= 11,643 & F=11,187) children 6-59 month of age in which SA1 has contributed to the achievement of 989(M= 413 & F=572) out of 1,583 total SAM while MAM at 2,214(M=924 &F=1,290) out of 18,235 total MAM in various OTP sites, however, the situation still requires intervention.This project targets delivering quality lifesaving management of acute malnutrition to 1,800(M=864, F=936) SAM cases and 10,425(M=5,004, F=5,421) MAM cases for children of 6-59 month of age respectively as well as 3,012 MAM cases who are vulnerable people among IDPs, returnees and host communities. JDF will continue with nutrition interventions at the community level with a special focus on the nutrition needs of both boys and girls and encourage caregivers and community members to prioritize both boys and girls while seeking nutrition services. JDF will continue with management of SAM and MAM, Preventive nutrition interventions through MIYCN/IYCF-E, community mobilization and monitoring, surveillance and analysis focusing on static nutrition sites as well as mobile services ensuring continuity of care. JDF will focus on integrating activities with other sectors like Health, WASH, Education, FSL and Protection

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

The risk analysis emerging from a community- and rights-based approach that is underpinned by a comprehensive system of accountability will inform and shape the overall strategy, enable the design of appropriate preventive, responsive and environment-building interventions by protection actors. The project will ensure an equitable and meaningful access by girls, boys, women and men of all ages and diverse backgrounds to protection and assistance programmes. The project will employ participatory assessments at key stages, including as part of the Joint Needs Assessment, incorporating systematic engagement to identify the diverse perspectives, risks and capacities of groups within crisis-affected communities, including men, women, girls and boys, and groups such as older persons, persons with disabilities, persons belonging to minority groups, and persons of diverse sexual orientation or gender identity complemented by Do no Harm and conflict analysis. The project will ensure that information dissemination includes, as minimum, accessible and timely information on cluster strategies, targeting criteria, geographical focus, services provided and entitlements, programmatic changes, and community feedback and complaints mechanisms; The project will translate messages into local languages and deliver them through context appropriate, protection sensitive methods and channels such as, face-to-face meetings, notice boards, radio, mobile phones, and public fora, community outreach workers, religious leaders, training and awareness-raising sessions, social groups and community centres; Ensure that visible and accessible feedback and complaints mechanisms are in place, tailored to the operationalcontext and the preferences of communities, which can appropriately handle complaints like violations of the law (such as sexual exploitation and abuse),institutional policies / codes of conduct; and quality and appropriateness of humanitarian programmes.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Beneficiary accountability is one of JDF Quality standards and reflected as a cross cutting theme throughout the project cycle. JDF adheres to Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP) principles, one of which is beneficiary accountability. Community consultation and participation are crucial aspects of JDF programme design processes and are critical in terms of ensuring success and promoting future sustainability. This approach fosters community ownership and enables JDF to focus on the sustainability and appropriateness of interventions. JDF is accountable to the women, men, boys and girls whose lives it aims to improve, and places this responsibility at the core of its humanitarian policy. JDF’s commitment to social accountability, which includes a complaints and feedback mechanism by engaging the community from the initiation of the project in order for the community to own it. Stakeholders meetings will be held in the location identified to enable community members to communicate issues and concerns associated with the health care services they are being provided, to provide feedback and monitor implementation of the response. The mechanism will have multiple access points (community consultations, suggestion box, Health facilities, telephone and email) and information about its availability will be posted at all health facilities. The community will be routinely consulted and provided with information regarding the project implementation. This will include engaging existing County Local leaders, state representatives, community elders, women, youth groups, religious leaders and representatives of beneficiaries. Other reasonable modalities for feedback that is useful to the communities/beneficiaries will also be considered and complaints will be taken seriously by the project’s management to ensure concerns are addressed and will help guide the fine-tuning of the project to enhance positive beneficiary experience.
