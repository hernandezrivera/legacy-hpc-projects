Project Summary

Economic livelihoods across Syria have been severely impacted by the crisis. This impact is particularly apparent in the agriculture and livestock sector. Increases in input and production costs, and disruptions in value chains, coupled with reduced availability of power and water resources are all factors leading to decreases in production levels, poor availability of locally produced foods, rising sector-related unemployment, and placing added strain on already weak social structures and communities in general.
Taking into consideration the above trends, the project aims to improve livelihoods and food security of 1,300 unique beneficiaries including vulnerable female and male producers within Hama, Tartous and Rural Damascus. Building upon the strong linkages between the rehabilitation of local economy and household livelihoods, and food security, R2R project aims to enable vulnerable households to protect, sustain and re-establish livelihoods
within a longer-term resilience building goal. As such, this action is developed to:
1. Bridge the gaps within local value chains, namely knowledge, information and human
resources by establishing Farmer Field Schools (FFSs) in the targeted villages in collaboration with public agriculture extension workers.
2. Improve access to agricultural and livestock productive assets and inputs, particularly for females and youth. Under this target the following activities will be implemented:
2.1. Provide basic agricultural including vouchers for olive milling, livestock and food production inputs for rural households
in the target villages.
2.2. Established and equip community owned agricultural and livestock productive assets groups in the target villages of rural Hama, Damascus and Tatous.
Target villages will be selected carefully to satisfy a specific set of criteria that AKF has established for communities where the resilience model could be employed with minimal risk:
• A mix of vulnerability segments and hosting IDPs and returnees with relatively a large number of vulnerable women headed households.
• A history of social activity and implementing community-based projects
• Agriculture and livestock are the predominant livelihoods activities
• Potential for diversified agricultural and livestock activities
• Accessible and safe (e.g. no mine risk)

4 - Needs

As per HNO 2020, 11.06 (5.54F; 5.52M) M people are in need of some form of humanitarian assistance including 4.65 M people in acute need. 69% of Syrians are believed to be living on less than US$ 1.90 a day, more than double since 2011. The number of food insecure people increased by 22%, from 6.5 M in 2019 to 7.9 M people in 2020 representing 39% of the total population of Syria. The main drivers of food insecurity in Syria are related to the deteriorating economic environment, loss of livelihoods and limited physical and financial access to food. Populations continue to require food assistance and often resort to harmful coping mechanisms, most frequently (47% of households) by skipping meals, reducing portion sizes, lowering the quality of consumed food and buying food on credit. Syria’s GDP in 2019 is estimated at US$ 20 billion, 2/3 lower than in 2011. State subsidies have reduced from an estimated 10 % to an estimated 5 % of Syria’s GDP.The HNO 2020 indicates that around 70% of the assessed households reported that food accounted for more than 65% of their expenditure, over 37% of food insecure households reported having outstanding debt and 57% adopted negative coping strategies. Based on AKF Syria’s internal study, in Salamieh district alone, 80% of HHs minimized expenditure on non-food items; 51% skipped payments for rent or debts; and 48% took out more loans to afford food and medical care. In addition, 28% of HHs took-on jobs that are considered dangerous e.g. harvesting in areas known to have mines to support their households. As per HNO 2020, 2.53M people in the target governorates of Hama, Rural Damascus, and Tartous are in need, representing 22.87% of the total people in need in Syria. While in relation to Food Security and Agriculture, Rural Damascus have severely food insecure population, Hama has population being at risk of food insecurity, while Tartous is one of three governorates with the highest price for a standard reference food basket.

5 - Implementation of activities

To expand farmers’ agricultural and livestock technical knowledge and skills, 20 Farmer Field Schools (FFSs) in 10 villages will be established and invested in as platforms for learning among local communities. Through a partnership with the Ministry of Agriculture and Agrarian Reforms (MAAR), agriculture and flock management best practices will be introduced to farmers who largely rely on traditional methods of farming. FFSs will reach a total of 500 farmers (30% females) to improve their technical knowledge and skills in agriculture. The project will improve olive production by providing 800 small-scale farmers with inputs and small equipment such as plastic jerrycans and olive milling vouchers to press around two tons of olive harvest. To improve livestock health, the project will support 400 rural livestock farmers (20 % female) with veterinary kits and 200 tons of balanced feed which meets the energy and protein requirements for sheep and dairy cows. The project will support 100 females to establish agro-processing units through technical and business trainings as well as providing basic equipment to produce molasses, dry vegetables, Za’ater, orange juice and milk products.The project will also establish and equip 10 community-based organizations (CBOs) that are representative of the ethnic, social and gender composition of the 10 targeted village. Under AKF’s supervision, CBOs will establish and equip 20 community productive assets groups to facilitate community access to critical production equipment, thereby increasing food production and enhancing farming households’ resilience against economic shocks. An estimated 400 households would benefit from community assets such as olive harvesters, seeds, shredders for olive pruning, refrigerators, and electrical shearer machines.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

A: Increased risk of inter-community tension where beneficiaries may be exposed to discrimination from other farmers who are not targeted under this project. Likelihood: Low; Impact: Low. With the help of CBOs, AKF-Syr will communicate the project objectives and targeting approach and criteria, and aim to strengthen knowledge sharing of new agricultural methods and communal benefits within their community. B: Women who own land assets or businesses may hand day-to-day control over to the male relatives, as a result of social pressure or because they do not feel confident enough to manage assets themselves. Women may also face some difficulties in using the new agricultural equipment and seek the help from other male farmers which may expose them to exploitation. Likelihood: Low, Impact: Medium. Regular and confidential monitoring and compliant reporting system will help to identify and investigate any cases of exploitations and take actions against it. Strengthening women decision making through increase their access to technical knowledge and expanding their participation in CBOs. C: Gender related cultural and traditional norms are more appreciative of men knowledge and work than women. As such, female farmers participation and work could be underestimated and may be exposed to verbal abuse by other male farmers while working together. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Medium. AKF will maintain regular monitoring of women contribution along with active complaint reporting system, and facilitate gender equality awareness raising or orientation sessions to targeted farmers to facilitate positive interaction and appreciation of female participants.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: The deterioration of the security situation limiting the movement of staff and beneficiaries. Mitigation: AKF-Syr regularly monitors the regional and local security situation using a variety of primary and secondary sources. In the event that mobility of staff and beneficiaries in program areas is deemed a high risk, AKF will delay the implementation of activities until the situation is able to be reassessed and determined safe for travel and implementation. Likelihood: Low, Impact: High Risk 2: Limited availability of quality goods, services, equipment and difficulties experienced in successfully procuring required items for delivery to Salamieh and other program areas. Mitigation: AKDN has extensive experience in rural livelihoods programming in extremely difficult environments. AKF will seek to source from local suppliers and when required expand procurement tendering offers to within the Governorates. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High Risk 3: Inflation and exchange rate fluctuations may affect the level of funds available at the field level. Mitigation: AKF regularly monitor exchange rate gains and losses to consider the impact of fluctuations on the project and revise as needed. Likelihood: High, Impact: low.
