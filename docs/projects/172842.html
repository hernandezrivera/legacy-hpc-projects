Project Summary

The project aims at improving food security & strengthen community resilience to climate shocks &conflict through enabling sustainable increases in agricultural and livestock productivity & adopting alternative livelihoods which will decrease competition over natural resource. The ultimate beneficiaries of the project are mainly the farmers and pastoralist, IDPs, returnees & poor residents in Blue Nile, North Darfur and Kassala states (around 80150 people). Agricultural tools, drought resistant seeds & seedlings, vegetables and horticulture will be provided to increase household food production. Promotion of climate smart & resilient crops & livestock production systems, protection of livestock based livelihoods in pastoral & agro pastoral community through provision of emergency livestock assistance, livestock watering, restocking with goat & poultry; awareness raising on the important of healthy diet in addition to women training on food processing for malnutrition, these inputs will build the capacity of the targeted communities to achieve their own food security & improve their livelihoods by increasing their production & income to better able them to withstand future shocks, droughts & other natural disasters. Solar powered women farms will enable produce quality diversified food items for household consumption and income & will reduce work load compared to currently practiced irrigation methods. Establish women saving & loan groups & support it with clean fuel (LPG cylinders & stoves) to establish revolving funds thus reducing wood collection work load & negative health impacts of indoor pollution. Process of women farms will be supported by nurseries, business management & agro processing trainings, linkage to markets & solar lights for safe gathering and mobile charging. The project will support assets protection and increase the ability of the target communities to decrease post-harvest losses & increase their resilience to shocks through provision of cash for work & cash for assets. The project will facilitate regeneration of ecosystem through a range of activities that aims to increase access to water, pasture and forestry which in the past directly contributed to conflict between farmers and pastoralists. Training will be provided on the management of these resources and environmental conservation techniques. The project will support the formation & reinforcement of farmers’ groups & community based associations strengthen their institutional capacity and consolidate their links to government service providers & other actors; to facilitate delivery of other project activities. The Project will apply participatory approaches for prioritizing & planning community needs to ensure that community planning & service delivery is tailored to local needs. More over community awareness on gender, protection & environment will be conducted. As in the targeted states men & women have different and unequal access to agriculture and livestock production inputs and technology (women represent about 49% of the total population yet are responsible for more than 65% of agriculture and livestock livelihood activities); therefore, the project will ensure that everyone in the affected community women, girls, boys and men have a voice in the development & implementation of the project & have access to opportunities that the project gives. Restocking with livelihoods kits, vegetable growing & marketing will enable women and youth to better engage in income generating activities and production process. The project will be run in environment friendly pattern, saving natural resources & increase target population awareness about environment issues through discussion & orientation sessions. In addition, COVID 19, will be mainstreamed in project activities and its implementation to protect beneficiaries and stake holders form infection by the disease and affect their food security negatively .

1 - Needs

Food security: In 2020, about 7.1 million people (49% men, 51% women), representing 16% of the total analysed population, are expected to face high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above), (Blue Nile 240,428 people, Kassala 482,311 people and North Darfur 560,925 people) through December 2020 and are in need of urgent action to save lives, protect livelihoods and reduce food consumption gaps. Around 2.4 million people have been displaced due to flooding, and political instability/social unrest resulting from tribal clashes in Eastern Sudan.Despite the signing of a comprehensive peace agreement between the Sudanese government and the Sudan Revolutionary Front Rebel Alliance (SRF), political instability and social unrest (resulting from tribal clashes in Eastern Sudan which led to the disturbance of livelihoods in Red Sea, Kassala and Gedarif states) continued during October 2020. Over 875,000 people were directly affected by flooding, including around 155 deaths; over 175,000 houses either destroyed or damaged; and around 1.4 million hectares of cropped land submerged by waters.The latest IPC results October 2020 estimated that 1,013,908 individuals (mainly in the North Darfur state, Kassala, and Blue Nile) are experiencing Crisis or worse levels offood insecurity (IPC Phase 3 and above) and need timely lifesaving food and livelihoodsassistance in Sudan. Out of these, around 269756 individuals are facing Emergencylevels of food insecurity (IPC Phase 4). However, estimates by WFP show increase from the 58 per cent who may not be able to afford the price of the local food basket and is facing continued food access challenge. Moreover, higher production costs are anticipated to continue increasing the already high cereal prices, which are not expected to decline significantly. Security: The security situation in the country has improved and IDPs have experienced more freedom of movement. Despite this however, IDPs—particularly women and girls—still face high protection risks and continue to be harassed, and occasionally killed or raped. On the other hand, between July and September 2020, IOM’s DTM registered 12,316 people—95 per cent internally displaced persons (IDPs) and 5 per cent IDP returnees in Sudan, and this is due to improved security situation in some states. Institutions: The limited organizational, networking and advocacy capacity of CBOs & national NGOs is a major constraint to their playing a more decisive role in coordinating & effectively delivering humanitarian support to the affected populations. Women lack adequate representation & have low voice in decisions for their communities. Strengthening the capacity of these institutions in various areas will enable them to plan, prepare & effectively respond to future food insecurity and livelihoods hazards. It worth noting that, Practical Action has extensive experience through different projects in developing the organizational, institutional & technical capacities of civil society organisation and government institutions. Environment: Climate change and related natural disasters (foods, droughts, and dry spells) have a strong bearing on the socio-economic situation of communities and households and can contribute to the number of people requiring humanitarian support. Environmental degradation ( e.g. land and forests) and unsustainable use of natural resources are both contributory factors and consequences of the humanitarian situation in Sudan. The degradation of natural resources has forced the majority of the rural population to overgraze range lands, expand cropping in marginal lands to compensate for low yields & cut trees for fire wood & charcoal making for sale as coping strategies. People have also become involved in conflict & competition over dwindling natural resource base. These activities will continue to further degrade the environment unless efforts are exerted to support, conserve and restore the natural resources.
