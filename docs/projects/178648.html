Project Summary

War Child Holland (WCH) and its consortium of six Syrian implementing partners will provide an education response to 10,000 highly vulnerable children in communities in Northwest (Idlib and Aleppo governorates) and Northeast Syria (Deir ez-Zor and Raqqa governorates) across a 12-month action. Recognising the interconnection between protection and education, the action will increase access to non-formal education and strengthen the protection of vulnerable girls and boys who are out of school or at risk of dropping out, to build their resilience and provide a pathway back into a safe and protective environment in school and within their communities. The action will include non-formal education interventions including Accelerated Learning Programme (ALP) for out of school children; guided self-learning modality for informal settlements with no access to learning spaces to keep children connected to learning; remedial and homework support for children at risk of dropping out; effective teacher professional development through a coaching system; and material support in terms of stationery and light rehabilitation. The action will adopt an integrated approach through developing the capacity of teachers on child protection and enhancing the accessibility and safety of the school environment. This prevention and response approach will address immediate and urgent identified risks to access to education while contributing to long-term change.

4 - Needs

The project will address needs that will support the elimination of barriers to access to education. Those barriers include: poverty, displacement, security, distance to safe learning spaces/schools, child labor, unavailability of number of learning spaces or trained personnel, and disabilities hindering access to school or at school if buildings are not equipped to facilitate access. 94% of all school-aged children are living in areas with severe, extreme or catastrophic education conditions mainly concentrated in Aleppo, Idleb (HNO 2021, p.68). The ratio of functioning classrooms to the school aged population varies greatly with six governorates having a ratio of 45:1. Idleb at 63:1 and Aleppo at 59:1 have the highest ratios (WoS Education Sector HNO 2021 Summary, p.16). Learning gaps of children due to long years of disrupted learning. A Reach survey of Raqqa, Deir ez-Zor and Menbij found that no education facility was available in 49% of assessed settlements. Diagnostic/placement tests from WCH’s programing in Raqqa and Deir ez-Zor in 2020 found that 35% of children aged 10-14 who had enrolled in the program were displaying competencies below the level of grade 1, a further 23% were operating at grade 1 level (WCH Northeast Syria Needs Assessment 2021). In Northwest Syria (NWS), People in Need reported that an estimated 46% of girls and 41% of boys are out-of-school, most often for more than a year (WCH NWS Needs Assessment 2021). Teachers’ Capacity: Teachers are critical in ensuring availability and quality of education as frontline workers. They need to be supported through capacity building and adequate remuneration. In some parts of the country teachers have been working without pay for over a year (HNO 2021).

5 - Implementation of activities

WCH in partnership with local actors will provide activities to ensure a sustainable impact in education. The project will enhance access to education by rehabilitating classes and establishing temporary learning spaces, where no schools are available, that will provide appropriate learning programs as per children’s assessed needs to encourage sustainable reintegration and/or retention in formal schools. ALP will ensure children receive the cycles necessary to support a sustainable reintegration. Remedial education programs will ensure retention as children recover gaps in their learning. Guided self-learning programs will reconnect children to learning to ensure that no child is left behind. This program is necessary for out of school (OOS) youth (mainly boys) who joined the working force to support their households and girls who are not sent to school and run the risk of early marriage. A teacher coaching program based on INEE’s Teachers in Crisis Contexts and WCH’s Core program to ensure teachers’ and children’s wellbeing, provides teachers with trainings, coaching support and a space for shared learning for a sustainable professional development. A focus on positive discipline is made to reduce and limit a culturally accepted in-class violence. WCH will pilot as of September 2021 social emotional learning as an integrated approach that complements WCH’s evidence-based Deals psychosocial support (PSS) programs. For M&E processes in projects, partner teams are supported technically by WCH with tools and analysis. WCH also crosschecks quantitative vs qualitative data and guides adaptations based on results; external third party evaluations are also used to confirm analysis and collect participant feedback.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk: Unsafe learning spaces and insufficient WASH facilities Mitigation measures: Each project location will be identified and set up using WCH CFS guidelines, which will also be used to train partners on minimum standards for assessing the risk and establishing safe spaces. To ensure they are fit-for-purpose, spaces will be lightly rehabilitated where required and then equipped with minimum standard safety equipment, winterisation materials, stationery and recreational kits. In all cases WCH will ensure the sites and their surrounding entry and egress points for families and children alike are cleared of ERW (through referrals to relevant actors). To increase equitable access to the facilities, an inclusion checklist will be applied, so that the needs of disadvantaged groups are considered into program design (IDPs, CwDs, adolescent boys, girls). The checklist will assess accessibility and meaningful participation in the needs assessment; outreach activities and inclusive development of activities; engagement of marginalised groups; and representation in monitoring and evaluation activities. Protection risk: Schools are targeted by parties to the conflict after rehabilitationMitigation measure: Assessment of security and safety prior to rehabilitation within the limits of foreseeable analysis. Protection risk: Restriction of beneficiaries' access to services or access to project sites due to COVID-19, including possible limitations on supply chains and procurement processesMitigation measure: WCH and its partners will maintain contact with relevant actors to be aware of any upcoming changes so that project activities can be adapted to minimize the effect of these changes.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Protection risk: Sexual abuse or attempt of sexual abuse of a childLikelihood: Low. Impact: HighMitigation measures:1) Train partners to review/set anew a child safeguarding (CS) policy for their organisations2) WCH program team monitors partners and general media postings on activities and alerts child protection (CP)/safety focal point at WCH whenever there are concerns (directly discussed with partners and action plan agreed upon)3) Staff of partners on the ground have one CS focal point who reports to WCH any breach4) Child safety concerns identified through internal monitoring and evaluation are discussed and followed up with partners For all field staff (including facilitators and teachers) and before activities begin, WCH will provide trainings on CS/PSEA. Trainings will be provided on WCH Safeguarding policy, to ensure awareness, response and compliance across all partners. WCH has developed child-friendly PSEA materials that can be displayed in classrooms and CFS, as well as child-friendly identification and reporting mechanisms, to ensure the safety of children. These are monitored through the project lifecycle by the CP team at WCH, and regular training and updates are conducted with partner field staff and management teams. To avoid inefficient referrals that do not result in service provision, referral pathways for PSEA and other follow up services will be mapped for all project locations and regularly updated throughout the lifecycle of the project.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Protection risk: Security incidents directly affecting WCH program and/or partners (staff, facilities, beneficiaries)Likelihood: Medium. Impact: HighMitigation measures: Partner analyses local conflict dynamics and can anticipate developments impacting humanitarian response. Follow up by WCH on security situation (trends) and measures; security fund included in budget to support cost of material damage to facilities or cost of measure to increase safety; Development of a Duty of Care Policy for partners in coordination with other INGOs; Train community-based partners in security management and analysis of security threats; Ensure contingency measures are in place and agreed upon. Protection risk: Financial mismanagement or fraud by partnersLikelihood: Medium. Impact: HighMitigation measures: Closely follow implementation of financial monitoring procedures agreed with partners; Ensure monthly reporting of all financial expenditure with multiple forms of verification (videos and photos of activities and material with dates, talking to people on the ground). Verify monthly financial report (by programs and financial staff) before next monthly funding transfer. Protection risk: Intervention strategy is not supported by the community/resistance by children, caregivers and/or community membersLikelihood: High. Impact: LowMitigation measures: Train partners to involve children in the design and development of the program, and to listen to their concerns (participatory methodologies). Involve partners in the development of the program – through regular discussion and structured meetings. Ensure involvement and support from parents and community members throughout the program. Continuous dialogue with local leaders.
