Project Summary

The project aims to provide broad support to WASH activities and lifesaving response aimed at increasing access to inclusive, accessible, conflict sensitive and gender sensitive WASH services for at least 400,000 people affected by the crisis. The project will contribute to the following objectives of the WASH cluster: Sector Objective 1: Support to water, sanitation/sewage, and solid waste management systems to ensure regular services for affected people in Syria, Sector Objective 2: Deliver humanitarian WASH supplies and services, and improve hygienic behaviours and practices of most vulnerable people, Sector Objective 3: WASH coordination structures facilitated and enhanced and Sector Objective 4: Improve WASH facilities and services in institutions to minimize sub-standard WASH conditions of the most vulnerable people in Syria.
Under 1st objective: Islamic Relief plan a number of activities including repair and rehabilitation of water systems, support to water quality assurance, operation and maintenance of water supply systems, repair, rehabilitation and operational support to sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems. Under the 2nd objective, IR plan to distribute essential WASH NFIs, hygiene promotion, and awareness raising on Covid-19 and Cholera disease. Under the 3rd objective, IR plan to strengthen prevention and mitigation of WASH-related protection risks, while under the 4th objective four, plan to support WASH facilities and services in schools and child-friendly spaces in close coordination with Education and Protection Clusters.
The action will target the groups identified in Islamic Relief needs assessment as most in need, including IDPs (displaced, in camps, and in communities), host communities, and particularly women and girls and persons with disabilities). Islamic Relief will work with gender-balanced local community volunteers which will include representatives from each community group, community leaders, and local councils as entry points for community consultation, mobilization, and as key implementation actors. The proposed response aims to meet critical, unmet needs as identified in the previous sections and the gender-based analysis around the lack of safe and accessible WASH services, compromising health and dignity. The proposed actions are developed on the basis of primary need assessment conducted by the IR team. The actions aim at reduced suffering, increasing and maintaining human dignity, and saved lives of conflict affected IDP and Host communities, women, men, girls, and boys in NW Syria (Aleppo and Idlib Governorates), Syria.

3 - Needs

The crisis in Syria continues to strain the socioeconomic foundations of the Syrian people. The outbreak of cholera in Syria is a direct result of the deteriorating water situation. WASH infrastructure requires significant support and substantial investments, According to the assessment of the WASH facilities conducted by Islamic Relief in sub-districts of Azaz, Aghtrin, Mare’, Suran and Al-Bab, the main water source accessed by the communities include water trucking, followed by groundwater sources (wells) provided by pipe network. With 39% of respondents indicating that their water source is not permanent, communities have to rely on alternate water sources such as water trucking, closed well, and bottled water, which are costly and unsafe. The current conditions of the main sewage/drainage network are not adequate with 70% of the respondents indicating that the communities are getting rid of sewage and wastewater using the Sewage Network. In addition, 83% of the respondents mentioned that communities are heavily reliant on free public garbage collection for waste management. This problem is further compounded by the lack of garbage bins with 91% of respondents highlighting that people are lacking community garbage bins. IRs assessment also concluded that the targeted communities face challenges related to hygiene practices which have contributed to negative coping mechanisms applied to result in waterborne diseases and other diseases associated with poor hygiene practice

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection and gender mainstreaming, IRW has an extensive understanding of the diverse vulnerabilities regarding gender, age, and other vulnerable groups, whose needs are taken into account at all stages of program design, implementation, and monitoring. Specific and disaggregated data and information regarding gender, age, and vulnerable groups will be collected during the needs assessment to support ongoing adaptation of the intervention. When conducting FGDs, IR will ensure that all gender, age, and vulnerable groups are consulted and represented. Within implementation, the following measures will be taken: the facilities developed and/or rehabilitated throughout the project, will be set in locations that allow reducing the threats related to their access. Community consultations will be held to ensure appropriate choices for these locations and. Water and Sanitation facilities will be equipped with Lighting devices (solar-based solutions) in IDP camps, the design of emergency latrines should consider the diversity of the population (age, gender, disability) and NFI packages to be delivered. IR is strongly committed to accountability to crisis-affected communities and has a strong suit of policies, code of conduct, and operational processes at the global and country-level to ensure and safeguard beneficiaries, staff, and wider stakeholders. IR staff abides by a staff Code of Conduct informed by, amongst others, our ‘PSEA, ‘Dignity at Work Policy’.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Conflict settings place women and girls at acute risk of poor health outcomes and sexual and gender-based violence. IR’s CFM uses multiple channels to collect beneficiary complaints and feedback including a complaint box, hotlines, during-distribution and post-distribution monitoring, community committees, casual enumerators and program staff. In addition, during action activities, random interviews, spot checks and dialogue will allow beneficiaries to report concerns. The received complaints and feedbacks will be classified and handled as per IR policy for CFM and high-risk complaints, such as those relating to PSEA, child protection, or corruption, will be prioritized and promptly managed. Confidentiality and data protection are ensured as per Islamic Relief’s data protection policy. IR’s commitment towards Safeguarding against misconduct will be upheld, and options for safe access to IR’s feedback mechanism, especially to the most marginalized groups such as women, children, persons with disability, and those who cannot read and write, will be sought through WhatsApp, Telegram, Skype and SMS. IR will promote establishment of adequate management plan; promote involvement /engagement with communities in distribution planning and feedback from different groups on package for improvement; training of staff and enforcement of code of PSEA code of conduct among partner staff.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The action plans will be adapted in consultation with the donor to ensure emergency lifesaving WASH activities are implemented for the new arrivals. 2. Challenges in money transfer from Turkey to Syria. Very Low Likelihood- High Effect. All financial transfers, as of September 2020, are carried out through the Posta ve Telgraf Teşkilatı, PTT bank branches (Turkey’s national postal and bank services) located in Aleppo (Jenderes - Afrin - Azaz), which clearly reduces the risks of transferring money. 3. Covid-19 transmission among staff and/or beneficiaries- Medium Likelihood-High Effect. IR is working in coordination with the WASH cluster, Health cluster, and local water and health authorities. During the distribution of action items/supplies, all precautionary measures including the provision of handwashing soaps, IEC material, and other essential hygiene items as well as maintaining and ensuring social distancing by staff and beneficiaries, will be followed. 4. IR ensures a proper registration system for staff/contractors’ laborers and adds zero-tolerance disclosure on child labor in the contracts.
