Project Summary

Faced with weak attention to the post-disaster reconstruction stage by government institutions, around 2700 families impacted by the 2010 earthquake from Port-au-Prince and Titanye, on their own and in an organized way, formed the Philadelphie community, located in the Cabaret commune, West Department.
Much of this habitat, since it was generated post-disaster, shows slumming, deterioration and serious lack of basic services due to the lack of technical and economic resources, which has caused previous vulnerabilities to reproduce. Housing conditions in Philadelphie are directly linked to the conditions of extreme poverty. Key problems in Philadelphie can be summarized as follows:
-Poor Housing Quality: High level of population poverty, associated with low awareness on safe construction are key contributing factors for the poor quality of the housing in the area, making them vulnerable to disasters. Of the 251 households surveyed for the program base-line 44,6% of the houses are in bad conditions, with zinc roofs and walls, and soil floors.
-Unplanned urban growth: 64% of the families do not have access to sanitary facilities and 99% do not have access to electricity. 81% of the households surveyed affirms that the main access roads of the community are in bad state. There is no urban development Plan which can support government and other actors interventions that could immediately improve the lives of the households.
-Unclear Land Tenure: Land insecurity poses an ongoing challenge to formal housing development. In lower-income areas, such as Philadelphie, land tenure is governed by a range of informal arrangements between renters and owners and the lack of land security hamper investment.

-Low levels of Economic Income and Family Economy Management: Counting 251 families in the baseline for the program, 50.2% of the households surveyed recorded an income of less than $ 16 per week, of which 24.3% recorded an income of less than $ 5 per week.

TECHO, in partnership with Habitat for Humanity Haiti, aims to support and assist the community of Philadelphie in its journey toward social and economic development and to become a resilient community. This will be achieved through a holistic approach focusing on participatory capacity-building and promoting, simultaneously, social action and awareness. The project implementation methodology is underpinned around the following pillars:
-Community Development: Strong participatory involvement of the community is the backbone of the project strategy, prioritizing the involvement of the families, local leaders and local authorities at the different stages of the project, to create ownership and lay the foundations for a long-term sustainability.
-Housing Related Disaster Risk Reduction: Focus on developing construction skills in the targeted community to ensure local masons have the skills to build back safer, as well as ensure disaster risk reduction concern.
-Chore Houses and Dry Toilets Participatory Construction: Support the participatory construction of 60 chore houses and dry toilets for the most vulnerable families, identified by TECHO and HFHH using a comprehensive selection criteria.
-Participatory Urban Planning: Work with local population, the Municipality, UCLBP and others government agencies to develop a comprehensive urban plan which integrates environmental dimension, streets and public spaces identification as well the planning of future public spaces for infrastructure.
-Land security: Work with local authorities and community members to provide minimum land security to Philadelphie inhabitants. This will include the mapping of existing parcels and the introduction of data in the legal system
-Increase of income and improvement of professional skills: To ensure the improvement of family economy, the project will work on the development of technical and professional skills of at least 50 members of the community, through Professional Training Courses.

7.3 - In which LGA are you planning to undertake Nexus activities?

Community of Zone 90 Village Philadelphie, Cabaret commune, West Department, Haiti. Zone 90 Philadelphie is a peri-urban community in Haiti located in the commune of Cabaret, West Department. It is located 12 kilometers from the largest nearby city, Cabaret, and 18 kilometers from Port-au-Prince. The community can be reached by the national road n°1. From the main road there is a dirt track that goes to Philadelphie, which is difficult to access during the rainy season. Most of the inhabitants access on foot or by motorcycle taxi. There is no access to public transport.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

For TECHO, the territory is a socially constructed space, thus this program is born as a sustainable participatory proposal within the post-disaster reconstruction cycle. In this sense, it is not just about providing infrastructure to the community, but it is intended to promote community participation in all phases of development of the projects. For this, the following methodology is applied: 1.Community participatory diagnosis: On the first visit to the community in August 2018, during a meeting with the community leaders committee, the slum profile of the community was developed. This diagnosis also served to confirm the commitment of the leaders in reaching the goal of community development. In July 2019, TECHO and community leaders conducted a baseline to gather information on the socio-economic conditions of the community. To do this, TECHO relied on the deep knowledge of the leaders over each area of ​​the community. At the beginning of the program, the TECHO team will train community leaders in the methodology of Community Participatory Diagnosis, which will be the basis for the elaboration of the Community Action Plan. 2.Project implementation: -A Working Group will be created, made up of community leaders in charge of coordinating community work and leading the programming and execution of the activities, with the support of TECHO. -The beneficiary families will attend pre-construction workshops where they participate in the decision making and design of their homes. -During the construction of the different community infrastructures, as well as in the process of choosing the professional training courses, an active community participation will be promoted. 3.Evaluation and monitoring: TECHO will be in continuous contact with community leaders to monitor the project during the next 3 years, to provide training and awareness campaigns, and to collect feedback from the different activities and solutions implemented.
