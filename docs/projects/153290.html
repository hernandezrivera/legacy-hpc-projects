Project Summary

Due to the impacts of conflict, over 6 million will be at Crisis/Emergency Levels of food insecurity by March 2019. In 2018, two thirds of PLW and half of children under 5 were malnourished and these trends are likely to continue in 2019. Continuing high levels of morbidity, poor health, nutrition and hygiene practices, and gender inequalities along with food insecurity have resulted in high levels of malnutrition. Rubkona, Guit and Leer in Unity and are likely to have Critical levels of acute malnutrition (GAM 15-29.9%) while Aweil West and Aweil North in NBeG are anticipated to be at Serious levels of acute malnutrition (GAM 10-14.9%) by March 2019 (IPC, Sep 2018). Juba PoC the nutrition of the population remains poor with GAM and SAM rates in December 2017 12.8% and 2.1% respectively and likely to be similar in 2018.
Concern will continue to support curative and preventative nutrition services through the CMAM approach in Juba PoC, Bentiu PoC and Guit in Unity and Aweil West and Aweil North in NBeG based on Concern’s long experience in these locations. Concern will also support nutrition interventions in Leer through its partner Nile Hope. Children under 5 with SAM will be treated through OTP, while children under 5, PLW and elderly (in PoCs only) with MAM will be treated in TSFP. Children under 5 with medical complications will be referred to SCs as inpatients. CNVs and lead mothers will screen for malnutrition and make referrals to OTP and TSFP to increase community outreach. Prevention through BSFP and improving MICYN and hygiene practices. BSFP will be implemented by Concern in Juba PoC and Aweil West and other Nutrition partners in Unity and Aweil North. Improving MIYCN practices will be through individual MIYCN counselling at nutrition sites; and community volunteers such as CNVs, MtMSGs and lead mothers and fathers based on MoH guidelines. Considering the roles of men in the community as the main decision makers, lead fathers and Male Change Agents will train other men on MIYCN. Concern will promote access to nutritious food through vegetable gardens and sack gardening and in NBeG, the project will be integrated with Concern’s FSL programme.
The targets will be girls and boys under 5, PLW, caregivers and elderly (in PoCs only). For curative services, admissions will be based on anthropometric measurements for girls, boys, PLW and elderly. As women are the primary caregivers and particularly vulnerable to malnutrition in emergencies and displacements, they will be the main target for MIYCN activities. Women will be encouraged to join MtMSGs. Given men’s roles as decision makers, they will also be targeted for MIYCN to allow them to analyse how their roles can impact on their spouses’ and children’s nutrition. Considering waterborne diseases is a main cause of malnutrition, the project will be integrated with WASH in Unity and NBeG (WASH is implemented by other partners in Juba PoC) and will be targeted in the same areas. In NBeG, the programme will be integrated with the Health to improve health practices and treat illnesses that cause malnutrition. SNFI interventions in Unity and Juba PoC will allow for clean and dignified living conditions and access to NFIs such as Jerri-cans and kitchen utensils to reduce risks of waterborne diseases.

Needs

Since the start of conflict in December 2013, around 1.96 million people in South Sudan continue to remain internally displaced with over 203,000 IDPs in PoCs and around 2.47 million refugees in neighbouring countries (OCHA, September 2018). Due to the impacts of conflict and erratic climate, currently 6.1 million people in South Sudan are thought to be at Crisis and Emergency Integrated Phase Classification of food insecurity) (IPC, September 2018). Although the peace process has been revitalised in August 2018, sporadic conflict and insecurity persist across the country including Concern’s areas of operation in Unity. While levels of malnutrition are likely to improve as food becomes available with the harvest before the end of the year, by March 2019, nutrition is likely to deteriorate due to poor food production and households depleting their food stocks; high levels of morbidity; poor access to health and nutrition services; poor water and sanitation infrastructure; and poor health, hygiene and nutrition practices. Concern’s operational areas of Juba PoC, Bentiu PoC and Guit in Unity and Aweil West and Aweil North in NBeG have unacceptably high rates of malnutrition. In Juba PoC, there continues to be a poor nutritional status of the population as most IDPs are completely dependent on food aid, high levels of disease and poor hygiene and nutrition practices. Concern’s SMART survey in December 2017 showed a GAM rate of 12.8% and SAM rate of 2.1%. Child Minimum Dietary Diversity was only 15.4% and 28.4% of mothers were not practicing exclusive breastfeeding to children under 6 months. From September 2017 to August 2018, 567 children under 5 SAM, 1,567 children under 5 and 616 PLW with MAM were admitted in Concern’s CMAM programme. This trend illustrate the need to continue with nutrition interventions in Juba PoC. In Bentiu PoC, GAM and SAM rates have been at 16.4% and 3.1% respectively with humanitarian assistance preventing further decline (Concern SMART survey, December 2018). 29.6% of mothers in Bentiu PoC still do not practice exclusive breastfeeding for children under 6 months while there is poor dietary diversity with only 11.9% of children under 5 consuming 4 or more food groups (Concern SMART survey, December 2018). In Guit, GAM and SAM rates are slightly below emergency thresholds at 13.9% and 2.9% respectively in postharvest period. Poor MIYCN practices also persist with only 54.8% of children under 6 months were exclusively breastfed and 6.9% of children under 5 received 4 or more food groups (Concern SMART survey, December 2018). High malnutrition rates are likely to persist in Rubkona and Guit at critical levels of GAM rates from 15-20% given the impacts of conflict, poor health, nutrition and hygiene practices and food insecurity. NBeG remains one of the locations with the highest risk of malnutrition driven by high rates of morbidity of 157/1,000 in children under-5; poor hygiene and nutrition practices and erratic rainfall patterns in 2018 disrupting crop production. In NBeG, in December 2017, GAM and SAM were at critical levels in Aweil West and Aweil North. In Aweil West, GAM was at 14.6% and SAM at 2.7%, while in Aweil North, GAM was at 18.5% and SAM at 4.8% (Concern SMART survey, December 2018). Acute malnutrition in both Aweil West and Aweil North is currently classified as Serious (GAM 10-14.9%) (IPC, September 2018).

Activities

SO1: Delivery of life-saving nutrition services to prevent, identify and treat severe and moderate acute malnutrition among children under 5, PLW and elderlyActivity 1.1: Training of Concern, CHD and SMoH nutrition staff and CNVs on MoH CMAM guidelines: Concern will provide training/refresher training on the MoH’s CMAM guidelines for management of acute malnutrition to its nutrition staff.Activity 1.2: Management Acute Malnutrition: Concern will support curative services at 2 nutrition sites in Juba PoC; in Unity 2 sites in Bentiu PoC and 7 in Guit; and in NBeG 25 sites in Aweil West and 24 in Aweil North. Guit, Aweil West and Aweil North will have mobile sites to reach remote communities during rainy season. Children under 5 with SAM will be treated through OTP with RUTF as well as amoxicillin and deworming tablets. Children with SAM and medical complications will be referred as inpatients to SCs either operated by partners in PoCs or directly by Concern in Guit, Aweil West and Aweil North. Children under 5, PLW and elderly (in PoCs only) with MAM will be treated through TSFP. Children under 5 will be treated with RUSF while PLW and elderly with CSB++. Children discharged from OTP will be referred to TSFP to ensure complete recovery.Activity 1.3: Community outreach: Coverage will be increased through CNVs and lead mothers who will screen for malnutrition and make referrals, follow-up of defaulter and non-responders through house-to-house visits. Mass MUAC screenings will also be conducted by Concern.Activity 1.4: BSFP: Concern will support BSFP to children under 5 in Juba PoC throughout the year and children under 5 and PLW in Aweil West during the hunger period to prevent acute malnutrition. Other nutrition partners in Bentiu PoC, Guit and Aweil North will implement BSFP.SO2: Improved MIYCN and hygiene practices to reduce the prevalence of malnutrition in under 5 children, and PLWsActivity 2.1: Training/refresher training of on MIYCN: Concern will provide training/refresher training to all Concern staff as well as SMoH, CHD, CNVs and lead mothers on the MoH’s MIYCN guidelines. Activity 2.2: Support MtMSGs to provide MIYCN and hygiene education: MtMSGs comprise of groups of 10-15 mothers and 1 lead mother. Lead mothers’ role will be to pass behaviour change messages on MIYCN and hygiene practices to mothers/women in their community through individual and group counselling sessions. Activity 2.4: Support men to provide MIYCN: Despite men’s roles as the primary decision makers according to social and cultural norms, they are often not targeted for MIYCN. Concern will support lead fathers and Male Change Agents to discuss with men on how their roles impact on nutrition of their spouses’ and children and to provide MIYCN education.Activity 2.3: MIYCN education: Concern will support MIYCN education through community education sessions using volunteers such as CNVs, MtMSGs and Male Change Agents; campaigns such as World Breastfeeding Week; and hygiene activities under Concern’s WASH programme. Activity 2.4: Integrated with Concern’s FSL programme, Concern will support kitchen gardening and sack gardening to improve access to nutritious foods.Activity 2.5: Baby tents: Baby tents will provide a safe space for mothers to breastfeed, receive breastfeeding counselling; discussions on breastfeeding, weaning and complementary feeding; and activities to improve mother-child relationships. Activity 2.6: ECD: Child friendly spaces provide safe spaces for mothers to discuss topics such as child nutrition, maternal and child health and hygiene practices. SO3: Improved nutrition situation monitoring, analysis and utilisation of early warning information for timely coordinated response decision making Activity 3.1: SMART, SQUEAC and KAP surveys: Concern will conduct SMART surveys in all programme locations to obtain anthropometric data, CMAM coverage and information on nutrition, health and hygiene behaviours to inform programming

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

For admission to OTP and TSFP, caregivers will be informed of admission criteria based on anthropometric measurements including using visual information due to high levels of illiteracy. Concern staff and CNVs will provide information on the rations of RUTF for OTP and RUSF and CSB++ for TSFP and their use to treat acute malnutrition. CNVs will also conduct follow-up visits to ensure caregivers are using therapeutic and supplementary food correctly. Information will be provided on details of the MIYCN activities including the selection process for lead mothers, lead fathers and membership of MtMSGs. Beneficiaries will also be informed of the Complaints Response Mechanism process and the monitoring process and the importance of monitoring as an opportunity for them to provide feedback. Given high levels of illiteracy, information will be visual, such as using IEC cards with pictures to illustrate admission criteria to OTP and TSFP. Emphasis will be, where skills and knowledge are available, recruitment of local staff, such as from within the PoCs who know the local languages and social and cultural norm and thus are better able to communicate with beneficiaries.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Given the different roles of women and men in child rearing and decision making, activities, particularly MIYCN activities will be specific for female and male members of the communities. Women and men will be targeted separately for groups such as MtMSGs and Male Change Agents so they can be freer to discuss the issues and identify solutions regarding nutrition of their spouses and children. MtMSGs and baby tents provide a safe space for women to discuss openly MIYCN topics. Men’s groups allow men to analyse their role in the household and how impacts on their spouses’ and children’s nutrition.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

Concern considers protection including protection against SEA throughout its programmes. Research on GBV in South Sudan conducted in 2017 found that 65% of women and girls had experience physical or sexual violence in their lifetime. Protection of beneficiaries will be essential during the programme. Women and girls in particular are at risk of GBV especially when travelling to nutrition sites. Concern has a Programme Participant Protection Policy, known as the P4, and Code of Conduct along with a Child Safeguarding Policy and Anti-Trafficking in Persons Policy which all staff and those involved in programmes must sign. Concern staff members receive yearly training on the code of conduct and it associated policies. Included in these policies in mandatory reporting of beneficiary sexual exploitation and abuse. Concern’s nutrition staff will work closely with protection partners for a referral pathway in the event of identification of protection cases such as through the CRM. Beneficiaries will be sensitised on measures that they can take to reduce protection risks such as travelling in mixed groups to nutrition sites and only during daylight hours

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

As well as collaborating with Nutrition partners, SMoH, CHDs and community leaders, Concern consults with community members to inform the design of nutrition programmes such as members of MtMSGs and caregivers attending OTP and TSFP. Concern has conducted SQUEAC surveys with caregivers which allows for consultations with caregivers on how they access CMAM services and identify what barriers prevent them to access these services, which is used to estimate coverage of Concern support CMAM. Concern conducted two evaluations of recently completed programmes in the proposed locations that involved with obtaining feedback from beneficiaries of nutrition interventions that will allow continuing what works well and make improvements in this proposed programme.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

South Sudan ranks 187/189 countries in the 2017 HDI and the country loses 36.3% when the value is discounted for inequality (HDI reduced from 0.388 to 0.247). This reflects the strong patriarchal society and culture; and traditional gender roles subject women to play secondary roles in the community. This place them at a disadvantage with household decision-making and this disempowerment exposes women and girls to risks including early/forced marriage and sexual and domestic violence. While the laws in South Sudan provide for the protection of women and girls against discrimination, abuse and GBV, they are often misinterpreted or ignored. In 2017, the Protection Cluster reported 3,585 GBV cases (95% female, 19% children); an increase of 35% from 2016 (Protection Cluster, 2017). This is in particular in locations affected by conflict. In Juba PoC, 34% of women had been exposed to intimate partner violence with 70% of these not seeking formal help, and 33% of women being survivors of sexual violence (No Safe Place, 2017). During displacements, families often become separated especially female members who flee to avoid sexual exploitation and males to avoid recruitment into armed groups. As a result, there are large numbers of female-headed households where women have to take additional roles such as collecting food or income generation in addition to childcare and household roles. In PoCs, overcrowding, often up to 3 families living in the same shelter, can lead to tension and violence with lack of privacy for women and children increasing the risk of GBV. This can impact on adopting MIYCN practices such as breastfeeding. In remote locations in Guit, Aweil West and Aweil North, there are considerable protection risks for caregivers if they are located far from nutrition sites. PSNs face mobility challenges in accessing nutrition sites and often they send other family members with their malnourished children leading to difficulties in follow-up and risk of defaulting

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Women as per social and cultural norms in South Sudan have limited decision-making roles, where decisions are made by men. This can include on whether a woman may bring her child to the nutrition site. In conflict affected locations such as Guit where men become more mobile to avoid recruitment into armed groups and in Aweil West and Aweil North where there is high levels of male migration to seek employment opportunities. This can increase workload burdens on women and reduce time for attending nutrition services and MIYCN education sessions. Women with disability are excluded from decision-making processes, 48% of women with disabilities reported never having access to decision-making process through community participation and engagement mechanisms . Poor infrastructure is another main barriers for access especially during the rainy season, some villages are not accessible to deliver basic supplies and for mother and care takers to bring their children to the health facilities. To address such structural challenges concern will preposition itself and deliver essential supplies to the nutrition facilities during the dry season and use other local transport means in collaboration with the communities. Concern will work with the communities to rehabilitate and maintain community rural access road through food or cash for asset approach in Aweil West in NBeG.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Concern will engage with local authorities to inform them about Concern, its operations in line with humanitarian principles to ensure acceptance. Women of childbearing age will be encouraged to join MtMSGs and supported with individual and group counselling sessions at nutrition centres. Trainings will be scheduled so they do not interfere with childcare responsibilities. As men have dominant roles in decision-making, they will be targeted for MIYCN interventions, both community leaders but also partners of women involved in the project. Men will be supported to share MIYCN knowledge with other men such as through Male Change Agents and lead fathers to understand how their role can impact on their spouses’ and children’s nutrition. With reduced access in rainy season, Concern will preposition stock and ensure high levels of training to staff at sites.All Concern’s staff working in the nutrition sites as well as those involved in the programme (such as partners, suppliers and consultants) will be inducted and required to sign the Code of Conduct, Programme Participant Protection Policy, Child Safeguarding Policy and Anti Trafficking in Persons Policies. Included is mandatory reporting of abuse and exploitation. In PoCs, nutrition sites are in central locations minimising distances for caregivers to travel. Outside of PoCs, nutrition sites are in safe locations such as beside health facilities. For remote locations, mobile nutrition sites will reduce travel times for caregivers. Concern will conduct security assessments prior to setting up mobile sites to ensure locations are safe and secure. OTP and TSFP will only operate in daylight hours and caregivers will be sensitised on measures to reduce risks such as moving in mixed groups and in daylight hours. During OTP and TSFP days, cases will be triaged where PSNs such as elderly and disabled receive attention first. Protection cases identified during OTP or TSFP days or the CRM will be referred to Protection partners
