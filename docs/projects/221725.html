Project Summary

In line with the South Sudan 2025 HRP and the FSL Cluster priorities and cluster objectives, in 2025, the Food Security and Livelihood Cluster aims (1) to provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations facing IPC phases 4, and 5; (2) to enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing, and livestock support; and (3) to reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability both to absorb and adapt to shocks and contextual changes. Alliance For Action Aid (AFAA’s) project is aimed at serving 2750HHs (16,500 individuals) from disaster-affected households in IPC 4/5 in Unity State including cash/voucher assistance to meet their immediate food and nutrition needs, Agricultural inputs (seeds, tools, & fishing gears) to improve their resilience and training to help them to recover their livelihoods. The project aims to improve the food security and livelihoods of IDPs, returnees, host and Refugees communities through provision of timely and appropriate main season agricultural inputs/kits (crop and vegetable seeds, and fishing kits), (linked to cluster objective 2), capacity building & training to withstand the impact of shocks, asset building and market support and training in improved agronomic and fishing practices, (linked to cluster objective 3). AFAA will also provide emergency cash assistance and livelihoods support to save lives and avert total collapse of livelihoods in the target Counties. The provision of agricultural and fishing inputs/kits, training in agricultural production and fishing is expected to improve the food production and in turn the food security of the target households (HHs) thus supporting them to recover from crisis and placing them on an accelerated path to early recovery and resilience to acute climate shocks.
AFAA seeks to support 962 HHs (5775 individuals) in the targeted Counties with agricultural and livelihood kits. The kits will be procured and distributed directly by AFAA and some will be secured from FAO pipeline. Training on improved agronomic and fishing practices, marketing and group dynamics will be done through model farmers and fisher folk who will undergo a Training of Trainers (ToT) and subsequently train the farmers and fisher folk in their communities. The knowledge gained from these trainings will improve the agricultural and fishing practices of the target HHs thus improving food production, addressing their immediate food security needs and building their resilience to future shocks. The provision of agricultural inputs and fishing kits and subsequent enhancement of agricultural production and fishing is expected to contribute to improvement of the Household Diversity Score (HDDS) and Food Consumption Score (FCS) of the target HHs
AFAA will employ a community-based beneficiary selection approach which will entail establishing Project Management Committees (PMC’s) to lead the selection of eligible beneficiaries based on the beneficiary selection criteria. Vulnerable HHs with severe food insecurity including female, child, disabled/chronically ill headed HHs; persons living with or at risk of HIV/AIDS and survivors of Gender-Based Violence (GBV) will be targeted in this project.
AFAA will hold regular meetings with the PMC’s and community members and conduct Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) to get their feedback on the project implementation. AFAA will ensure that their feedback is incorporated in the implementation of this project and design of future projects. Further, to promote accountability to the affected populations, AFAA will use the community-based complaints and feedback mechanism to provide a platform for beneficiaries to voice any issues or concerns.

1. Needs

In 2025, the greater Upper Nile areas of South Sudan will face a dire food security situation, requiring scaled-up and sustained humanitarian assistance to save lives and prevent suffering from hunger, malnutrition, and disease. South Sudan's populations in need of humanitarian assistance, particularly those in food security crisis or emergency are predicted to be among the worst figures since the country’s independence 14 years ago. Unity State is among the most-affected by the current food crisis as Per the most recent IPC data (Between Sept-Nov, 2024): Unity state with a total of 702,000 people (60.6%) are facing crisis or worse acute food insecurity of which, 177,000 people are in IPC 4 (Emergency), and 525,000 people are facing IPC3. During the lean season, the food security situation will seasonally deteriorate in Unity State and Ruweng Administrative Area with an estimated 830,000 people (71.6 percent of the state’s population) likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 255,000 people will likely be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, and 575,000 people will likely be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. During this period, 5 counties (Guit, Leer, Mayendit, Panyijiar, and Rubkona) will be classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, and 4 counties (Abiemnhom, Koch, Mayom and Pariang/Ruweng) will be classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. Severe food security outcomes will likely be driven by depleted household stocks of harvested crops, diminished coping capacities, increased dependency on markets among other factors. For HHs whose 2024 harvests are destroyed by flooding, meaningful food security improvement is not feasible until at least the 2025 harvest of cereal crops, approximately a year away. These populations are in urgent need of sustained humanitarian assistance to access basic meals and avoid malnutrition and diseases, as well as support to rebuild their livelihoods for the future. The current food crisis is driven by multiple factors including protracted sub-national conflict and insecurity, high disease burden, national economic crisis, climate-change driven shocks which include both drought and flooding and poor childcare feeding practices. At the same time, local currency value has depreciated, and the price of staple foods have continued to soar in line with regional and global commodity price trends driven by the global food crisis and conflict in Ukraine, as well as localized market supply disruption due to flooding. Following four previous years of unprecedented flooding, 2024 is 10 percent higher than in 2023, turning out to be a fifth successive year of severe flooding, forcing many families to abandon their homes and flee to safer areas. Unity state is among the most severely affected both in severity and extent of flooding. The majority of the population in these States are agro-pastoralists relying on subsistence farming. However, four years of successive catastrophic flooding (2020-2024) have destroyed croplands, pastures, and productive assets like livestock, contributing to low agricultural production, and forcing some to abandon their homes and villages or isolated them from others. Severe flooding has cut off the over-land access to these communities for the better parts of 2020 -2024. Families affected by this emergency are unable to secure even basic meals and are completely dependent on humanitarian food assistance, due to lack of livelihoods and diminished purchasing power. Vulnerable populations, especially those that have been displaced or are isolated by floodwaters and destroyed roads, face not only hunger but also dramatically elevated risks of malnutrition and communicable diseases. Children, vulnerable adults such as pregnant or lactating women, and persons with disabilities are disproportionately affected. On that note, AFAA will respond to this emergency, and resilience Program in Unity.

2. Project activities

The project will prioritize targeting of HHs who are most vulnerable or at highest risk of malnutrition, including displaced families.1.1.1: AFAA will provide unconditional cash transfers to 1238HHs (7,425 Individuals) in IPC 4+ during lean season to help families purchase food or other essential needs during most severe months of food insecurity. 1.1.2: Community-Based Participation and Planning (CBPP): The CBPP exercise will focus on the full participation of selected community planning teams, who in participatory ways, will identify community needs, suggest some recommendations and draw the final community’s plan. 1.1.3. Formation and training of Committees. Each Committee will comprise of 10 members (5 women, 5 men) who will be trained in beneficiary selection criteria, roles, protection mainstreaming & monitoring of the project implementation. 1.1.4. Beneficiary selection: AFAA adopts participatory, vulnerability and rights-based approaches in identification, verification and registration of target beneficiaries. AFAA will furthermore strengthen community communication to ensure beneficiary and non-beneficiary communities are well informed about the project, beneficiary criteria, and how to provide feedback or complains to AFAA. The committee members will identify and register the beneficiaries according to AFAA’s vulnerability criteria for targeting food-insecure women/child/chronically ill-headed HHs, People with disabilities, widows, elderly, pregnant/lactating women, orphaned, Survivors of GBV. 1.1.5. Distribution of complementary cropping and livelihoods inputs: AFAA will Protect and stabilize livelihoods for 963 vulnerable HHs (female/male 51/49%) (5,775 individuals) through provision of Livelihoods inputs (seeds and tools plus fishing gears and distribute Crop kits (Maize, Sorghum and Cowpea), vegetables (Amaranth, Collard, Okra, Tomato, Onion, Watermelon, Pumpkin and Maloda) and Fishing kits (hooks, monofilament and spools of twine). This will also improve food availability and income from sale of surplus vegetables, 1.1.6 Formation and training of farmers and fisher folk groups: AFAA will facilitate the formation of farmer and fisher folk groups. ToT training on improved agronomic practices, fish preservation, marketing and group dynamics will be provided. The trained farmers and fisher folk will then sensitize and build the capacity of the other farmers and fisher folk in their communities. Training on crop disease control including training of farmers and surveillance on Fall army worm (FAW) and Desert Locusts control through Community based surveillance and Early Warning. Training farmers on grain/seed harvesting, storage and support through traditional granaries and hermetic bags. Training on post-harvest management and market linkages. Fisher folk training in good fishing techniques, preservation, processing and marketing and on the use of fishing kits received. 1.1.7. Support provision of veterinary services through community based initiatives to 3,300 (550) livestock. 1.1.8. Provision of livelihoods skills training: Female-headed HHs and fisher folk supported through training on IGAs. IGA’s will be linked to the other two activities of fishing and vegetable production on market linkages. Some markets in Unity, Upper Nile Lakes and Central Equatoria States are functional and AFAA will target this market-based activity in these areas 1.1.9. Facilitate formation of VSLA groups and supporting with start-up kits and supporting small scale farmers on group formation, value addition to their produce and linking them to markets and agro-input dealers and other value chain actors 1.1.10. Monitoring and reporting: AFAA will also conduct post distribution monitoring after every distribution to ascertain the appropriateness of the response.
