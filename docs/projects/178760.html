Project Summary

Based on WASH assessments in 2021, 12.2 million people in Syria are estimated require WASH assistance, 8 million of whom are considered in acute need, in particular, displaced people living in camps/shelters or informal settlements, host communities and returnees. Before the crisis, only 65 per cent of the population had access to 42 built-up sewage treatment plants. This number has signiﬁcantly decreased because many of essential facilities are no longer operational due to damage and/or unavailability of indispensable essential operational supplies. This makes communities vulnerable to signiﬁcant health risks. Hence, UN-Habitat Syria proposes herein a comprehensive intervention with multiple components working together to reinforce the preparedness of Syrian communities to respond properly to a potential outbreak of COVID-19 disease, by improving health and environmental conditions, through decontamination measures, safe management of solid waste, and supporting community-based initiatives to address disease outbreaks in targeted areas.
Objectives:
UN-HABITAT proposes an area-based-approach project which target vulnerable communities in speciﬁc areas and/or neighborhoods through rehabilitation of essential infrastructure and basic services. Building on the strong foundation of assessments which have been undertaken through UN-HABITAT’s Municipality Technical Office Support (MTOS) program to inform detailed thematic recovery plans, further targeted analysis will guide the priority locations for the proposed activities at the area/neighborhood level. The overall activities of the project will ensure:
• Repair and rehabilitation of water systems
• Support to water quality assurance, operation and maintenance of water supply systems
Repair, rehabilitation and operational support to sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems The project aims to reach target 250,000 of baseline 500,000 acute PIN (direct and indirect) in (Daraa, Deir- Ezzor, Homs, Rural Damascus, Hama and Aleppo ).
Some locations like Homs and Hama were prioritized in this project to ensure works complementarity with other projects implemented by the UN-Habitat in order to extend the impact of provided support in these areas to the most possible, especially with regard to COVID-19 interventions.
- Aleppo city 85,000 beneﬁciaries.

- Homs city 11,000, and Qaryatain 2,000 beneﬁciaries.

4 - Needs

UNH will focus on infrastructure and services in both rural and urban communities, continuation of light but durable rehabilitation of water and sanitation infrastructure, and solid waste management. As public WASH systems are the most economically viable and equitable way to provide services at scale, light rehabilitation of WASH systems must be combined with support to operation and maintenance, including electricity systems, and capacity building of technical staff. Given challenges with electricity network functionality UNH will also prioritize use of renewable energy wherever feasible. Due to imposed restrictive measures, the Syrian population relies on humanitarian community for provision of chemical treatments for drinking water. Transmission of COVID-19 is anticipated to continue in 2021, therefore, mainstreaming COVID-19 response throughout all regular WASH activities is crucial to mitigate risks. Besides the criteria in HRP, the selection of targeted locations will be based on the following criteria: • Being in line with the Shelter Sector and HRP 2021 strategic priorities and objectives, as well as severity of needs • Being priority locations identiﬁed through detailed ﬁeld assessments and consultants with local experts and municipalities (MTOS) • Complementarity with ongoing projects already through UN-HABITAT resources and donor commitments • High numbers of people in need, vulnerable and expected returnees in target locations • High complementarity with area-based interventions in Shelter, Protection, and ERL The total acute PIN in priority locations is:122,000 in Harasta, Duma, Qudsia, Arbin, Jaramana103,000 in DEZ and Abu Kamal170,000 in Aleppo3,000 in Hama26,000 in Homs and Qaryatain

5 - Implementation of activities

Implementation will adopt the following sequence: • Update the Municipality’s action plan identifying the priorities and areas of intervention through an area-based approach aimed to target speciﬁc geographical areas characterized by complex issues, through an integrated, multi-sector, and participatory approach, where communities’ representatives, international organizations, ministries and public departments, are jointly identifying priorities in a comprehensive manner that takes into account the interdependencies between the identiﬁed issues• Through the project implementation, and in coordination with Municipalities and local authorities. the local community will have a signiﬁcant supervisory role in terms of projects’ selection and implementation•Detailed assessment for the activities in priority neighborhoods• Tendering process, awarding, and contracting• Implementation• Completion of work and handover to the relevant stakeholders In addition to regular reporting to the FTS, all activities and indicators will be reported to the sector through regular 4Ws mechanisms. UN HABITAT has developed monitoring mechanism including site visits reports, progress reports, inspection tools and ﬁnancial tracking tools. All these tools and templates will be utilized during the implementation to enhance the monitoring process. The Municipalities will have key role in monitoring the implementation through the technical committees that shall be formed to supervise the work and issue the oﬃcial handover reports, also the implementing partners are required to provide regular reports showing the progress on the ground.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

UN-Habitat staff and non-staff are bound by UN-Habitats Code of Conduct, which directly refers to the Protection of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and to Gender Based Violence (GBV). Several awareness sessions took place in 2019 and 2020 to raise awareness of PSEA to staff, in addition in sessions for contractors and implementing partners.UNH team has to complete online PSEA and gender-based violence e-courses as part of their inductionAs part of on-going efforts to improve accountability, UNH is committed to implement a beneficiary complaints and response mechanisms in all of its project to allow beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on UNH work, as well as the contractors’ work, including potential PSEA or GBV issues during post-delivery monitoring, whether people were asked to exchange anything for aid. Specifically during hygiene kits distributions where issues related to exchange of assistance for sexual favors might accompanying by the threat of removal from beneficiary listsAll PSEA and/or GBV issues will be addressed first by the Project manager, who will share the concern with the management, in order to establish a solution or way to proceed to enhance the protection of the PSEA/GBV survivor. When needed, the Head of Program will scale the issue to Regional and Headquarters offices consulting with the gender specialists’ team, will look for potential solutions and/or an appropriate way to proceed
