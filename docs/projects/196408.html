The roles and activities of community health workers (CHWs) are tailored to meet the unique needs of the communities they serve. A CHW's role depends on factors such their education, training, lived experience, and experience working with specific populations. CHWs may perform the following roles:
• Create connections between vulnerable populations and healthcare providers
• Help patients navigate healthcare and social service systems
• Manage care and care transitions for vulnerable populations
• Educate healthcare providers and stakeholders about community health needs
• Provide culturally appropriate health education on topics related to chronic disease prevention, physical activity, and nutrition
• Advocate for underserved individuals or communities to receive services and resources to address health needs
• Collect data and relay information to stakeholders to inform programs and policies
To ensure system strengthening the following programmatic areas will be in the focus of the implementation of this project:
- Ensuring Access to Essential Primary Health Care Services
- Integrated Health Service Delivery
- Referral services
- Capacity building
Strengthening the capacity to efficiently implement the COVID-19 and Cholera Preparedness and Response Plan 2022 in NWS
Risk communication is key to improving familiarity with and adherence to preventive measures, in particularly during health emergencies. Failure to communicate the right message effectively can result in loss of trust, damage to the economy and loss of lives. For risk communication to be effective, risk messages have to be shared with the public in an openly and timely manner, so as to reduce the knowledge gap and to convince the public to adjust their behavior during a crisis. In addition to disseminating recommendations that are easy for the public to understand and comply with, trust in the source of the message is important for an effective risk communication.
This project aims to Increase the community awareness of the COVID 19 and Cholera symptoms, means of transmission, and prevention measures including hygiene promotion (HP) messaging in northwest Syria.
This project will continue the same scope of services for the first project by increasing two aspects:
- Using the virtual information and engagement platforms in the local communities involving trusted community influencers and using different social media platforms to ensure widespread among communities with providing opportunities for answering questions and providing up-to-date recommendation about COVID-19 and Cholera response including text, voice, and video.

3 - Needs

Syria remains a complex humanitarian and protracted emergency characterized by over ten years of ongoing hostilities and the resulting long-term consequences, more than 15.3 million of population in Syria, approximately a 3.2 M increase from last year, are estimated to be in dire need of life-saving health assistance in 2023.According to HNO 2022: 14.6 million people in Syria require humanitarian assistance. an increase of 1.2 million from 2021.Long-standing needs of an estimated 6.9 million IDPs remain staggering, particularly for over two million people in 1,760 informal settlements and planned camps, often hosted in inadequate shelters and with limited access to basic services.In NWS, 4,6 M population (2,8 IDPs out of them 1,8 M are living in camps) out of the total population there are 4,1 M in need and 3,1 M are food insecure people. HNAP recorded 19,545 new internal displacements in north-west Syria in September – the highest record so far in 2022 - largely driven by the deteriorating economyThe first cholera case in north-west Syria was confirmed on 19 September.The outbreak is spreading fast and as of the 4th of October, 13,059 suspected and 900 confirmed cholera cases including 60 associated deaths (CFR 0.46%), have been reported from 13 of 14 governorates in Syria.These additional crises continue to overstretch the already weak health system and have direct consequences on vulnerable communities and patients, increasing morbidities and mortalities.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

AFAQ coordinated with health cluster and humanitarian actors to determine the gaps and selected the in-need communities for intervening. AFAQ will assess the potential risks of women, girls, boys and men and people with disabilities to ensurethat health services are conducted with no harm. AFAQ is integrating protection mechanisms to understand the needs of community for targeting them through: Delivering services impartially to ensure reaching to all in-need BNFs without discrimination, with prioritizing the safety and dignity of BNFs and avoid harm. Identification of suitable BFSs, times and dates, considering needs of female and children. And secure BFSs like safe locations and movements. Hiring the majority of staff from females. Collecting and monitoring of data disaggregated by sex, age, and disability. Monitoring and overseeing potential barriers to accessing the health services. Conducting exit interviews to adapt the counseling and preventative health services to respond the need.AFAQ will develop a service map that identifies all Protection service providers within the operational areas.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

AFAQ has different channels for information dissemination including, but not limited to, WhatsApp groups, IEC materials, fixed posters and banners - PSEA messaging and awareness-raising will be done with beneficiaries through one or more of the following (track and report):SMS/WhatsApp messaging• All project staff trained on GBV prevention and response in emergencies and PSEA, in order to ensure full adherence with the Do No Harm principle with strict dealing with Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) and GBV issues. AFAQ is working to improve the speed, accuracy and comprehensiveness in reporting serious allegations of sexual exploitation and abuse; the pace and quality of investigations; and follow-up and accountability. Urgent action upon receipt of a report of sexual exploitation and abuse creates the best conditions for investigations. If an allegation of misconduct by AFAQ personnel have been reported and the allegation are substantiated, the perpetrators will be held accountable and face sanctions that correspond to the seriousness of the offense. This can range from a reprimand to dismissal. If the acts may amount to criminal conduct, AFAQ can refer the matter for criminal prosecution by the staff member’s state of nationality.
