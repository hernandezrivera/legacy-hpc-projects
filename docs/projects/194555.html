Project Summary

The project is designed to improve quality, increase access, and strengthen the capacity of primary and secondary health services, integrating MHPSS/GBV and WASH, by reinforcing the national health system at State, County, Boma, and community level. The project will target 30,000 people in need.
In close collaboration with State Ministry of Health (SMoH) and the County Health Department (CHD), MdM will support both static and medical mobile units (MMU) across Bor South, Twic East, and Duk. MdM partners with Sudan Medical Care as its local implementing partner, who have extensive experience in the aforementioned areas. The project includes provision of technical guidance, support supervision and on-the-job training for health facility staff and community-facilitators/community health workers, emergency referrals for critical cases, repairs of health and WASH infrastructure, provision of essential medicines, consumables and medical equipment, referrals, and linkages to the next level of care and with relevant actors for non-medical services including protection.
MHPSS and GBV will be integrated through counselling at hospital, PHCC, and MMU level, and through awareness raising and a referral system at community level, and will target 3659 adults and 1200 children. WASH will be integrated by rehabilitating essential infrastructure. The project takes a holistic approach in ensuring support is given at all levels, responding to COVID-19 needs, and by integrating the MHPSS, GBV, and WASH components. The main objective is to meet the basic health, MHPSS, and GBV needs of the population in Bor South, Twic East, and Duk counties, focusing on persons with specific needs such as women of reproductive age (project target: ANC4+ 50% and SBA-30%), pregnant and lactating women (project target: 288), at-risk pregnancies (project target: 43 women with high risk pregnancies) and children under 5 (project target: 1200), mental health and psychosocial support cases (project target: 81 consultations) among displaced people, returnees and host communities.

1 - Needs

MdM, with support of SMC, conducted a Needs Assessment (NA) in November 2021 covering Bor South, Twic East, and Duk, and aligns with the findings presented in the 2022 HNO. A similar NA in collaboration with TearFund and SMC that will also include Nutrition and WASH needs is scheduled for November 2022 and will cover Uror county in addition to the previous three counties. An exploratory mission will be also done in Warrap State in collaboration with ACF to understand the nutrition, GBV, health & MHPSS needs/gaps that could determine new locations and areas of intervention for 2023. In November, through a team of consultants, an environmental diagnosis assessment in Bor South was conducted to evaluate the impact of MdM's humanitarian interventions on the environment and community, and determine how environmental factors and climatic change, including persistent floods, have impacted health delivery, access and service utilization. The report of the latter assessment is expected in early 2023 and will help inform the design of future humanitarian action to address the evolving needs in all intervention areas. Due to severe insecurity and climatic-shocks, people’s physical and mental well-being deteriorated significantly. Duk in 2022 was reported as one of the counties with the highest number of people who were in a severe state of need (pin: 114,038) and was considered a p particular area of concern with the lowest health service availability (HNO 2022). Only 8% of health facilities in South Sudan offer the full package of BPHNS, and 58% are relatively functional. Structural gaps exist in access to qualified health workers, essential medicines and supplies, and adequate WASH infrastructure. The project is addressing these identified gaps in the activities and indicators. The number of people across the 3 counties in need of health assistance identified by the health cluster in 2022 is projected to drop, however the flooding and insecurity challenges remain unabated and, coupled with decreased funding resources for health services, these factors continue to pose a significant challenge of access to humanitarian assistance and available health services. The project will target 30,000 people. MdM will prioritize the following areas, in accordance with the HNO 2022 and Needs assessment results: Neonatal and child health services: The extremely low immunization coverage (49% Penta 3) will be addressed. Screening and application of referral pathways for malnourished children will be applied (target: 1200 (504 male,696 female). Sexual and reproductive health: the project will address the high maternal mortality rates; low four-ANC visit coverage, skilled birth attendance and institutionalized delivery; and poor BEmONC accessibility which contribute to pregnancy and related morbidity and mortality (target: ANC4+ 50% and SBA-30%). Communicable and non-communicable diseases: Driven by displacement and loss of livelihood, especially vulnerable groups such as malnourished children and pregnant and lactating women are more prone to common and infectious diseases, for which there is a need to strengthened referral pathways for nutrition (target: 650 women) Emergency preparedness and response driven by flooding and a lack of WASH infrastructure; the number of water-borne diseases is expected to be high. Covid-19 also remains a threat. Strict surveillance and timely response are therefore key in the response (target: 80%)MHPSS: approximately 22% of the total SS population is expected to suffer mental disorders (WHO). MHPSS services are not integrated into the national health system and remain inadequate. There is a need for qualified MHPSS workers, improved integration into health services, and a strengthened referral pathway (target: 81 MHPSS consultations)GBV/CMR: An increased need for CMR services in health facilities was identified. Improved referral pathways are thus needed (No. of GBV survivors attended to within 72 hours).

2 - Project activities

Guided by the National Health Policy for South Sudan (2016-2026, IASC MHPSS Guidelines in Emergency Settings, WHO Pyramid Framework and BPNS for South Sudan, MdM will ensure implementation of the National Policy to address priority health needs and enhance provision of basic essential health services while ensuring protection mainstreaming and gender integration in Bor South, Duk and Twic East counties. The project focuses on strengthening the capacity of the CHD/MOH and local implementing partners to deliver maternal, child and neonatal health, SRH, prevention and treatment of communicable and non-communicable diseases, immunization, health promotion and community sensitization/awareness creation on common health subjects, MHPSS by enhancing quality standards on diagnosis, treatment, timely referral and community-based prevention practices and behaviours. MDM will provide technical guidance, support supervision and on-the-job training for health facility staff and community-facilitator, emergency referrals for critical cases, repairs of health and WASH infrastructure, provision of essential medicines, consumables and medical equipment, referrals and linkages to next level of care and with relevant actors for non-medical services including protection will be provided for at least 11 target health facilities including seven PHCCs, 4 PHCUs and 4 MMUs and 2 secondary health facilities I.e., Bor State Hospital and Panyagor County Hospitals in the 3 counties. At PHCU level, the intervention will focus on quality primary level assessment, diagnosis and care for common mild acute childhood illnesses using IMNCI and adult conditions, including trauma, treatment for non-communicable and communicable conditions, SRH, health education, routine immunization, and referral; at PHCC level, outpatient care and short term inpatient diagnosis and treatment for common communicable and on communicable diseases, childhood and maternal services including EPI,IMNCI, BEMoNC and referral services integrated with MHPSS/SGBV care will be provided. Sexual Reproductive Health care will be enhanced through support for CEMoNC and CMR. By supporting the WHO/MOH EWARS and in conjunction with BHI, the project will improve real time detection, reporting and response to communicable and notifiable diseases through strengthening of community-based surveillance and early-warning system. COVID-19 surveillance, risk communication and community engagement will be integrated with enhanced IPC protocols and screening, for early detection, monitoring and referral through the RRT for case investigation, management and contact tracing. MDM will provide support to the Isolation Unit at BSH to strengthen diagnosis and case management of COVID-19 cases. Community facilitators at PHCUs will disseminate comprehensive and simplified package of SGBV/MHPSS and WASH, health prevention and promotion messages at household and community level with referrals for health, counselling and legal services. The facilitators will also target vulnerable groups plus men and boys to be advocates and allies in the prevention of SGBV. Referral mechanisms from the community and within the health facilities will be strengthened with particular focus on reporting and referral of SGBV cases and acute severe life-threatening illnesses. MHPSS counselling will be provided in six different health facilities in Bor South and two facilities in Duk and Twic East counties. The MHPSS services are freely available for any person (voluntary walk-in or referred) who presents emotional distress, including but not limited to those linked to medical disorders, trauma, malnutrition, and SGBV. In addition to the two counsellors and a clinical officer at Bor State, MdM has trained 8 counsellors from Twic East and Duk and enhanced their capacity to deliver mhGAP-based counselling and related MHPSS services. MDM will also operate a MHPSS hotline.

3 - Project indicators

The impact of the project corresponds with the Health and Nutrition Cluster’s planned priorities and achievements with several results related to the Protection Cluster strategic objectives. 1) Reducing excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic-prone diseases and health insecurities by supporting 11 health facilities plus 4 mobile medical units with essential drugs and medical supplies, training, and technical supervision and 7 health facilities with an MHPSS/GBV component as seen in the activities 2) Disease surveillance and response, including diagnosis and treatment will be monitored through all data submission in the DHIS tool and measured by percentage of alerts raised through national EWARs investigated within 48 hours (project target: 80%) 3) Delivery of an integrated package of high-quality essential health and MHPSS/GBV/SRH services including EPI to reduce the high maternal mortality and U5 mortality rates and mitigate the transmission of vaccine preventable, and other infectious diseases 4) Improving access to and utilization of integrated life-saving quality health care services to crisis-affected and vulnerable populations through static, mobile and community-based health service delivery systems. To ensure and measure the quality and life-saving aspect of the health care services, the project will undertake regular supervision and monitoring visits to all supported health facilities, documented by supervision checklists and the health facilities’ supervision records, and is measured by the number of health facilities supported with IEHK supplies 5) The number of persons receiving MHPSS/GBV services will be measured by records kept by the counsellors of all the diagnoses and the number of consultations conducted. 6) To measure access, utilization and the lifesaving aspects in child and adult health services, the coverage of PENTA3 in under-one-year old’s (project target:75%; 1252) and measles in under 5 years old (project target 36%; 432) will be recorded through the EPI records. 7) MdM also aims to increase access to services for survivors of GBV, clients with disabilities, mental health disorders and the elderly. Health care staff (10) will receive refresher training sessions on the CMR as well as PFA. The MHPSS/GBV community facilitators and counsellors will be trained on the referral pathways and inter-sectoral SOPs to strengthen the referrals from community and PHCU level to PHCC and hospital level where CMR is available. The success of the capacity building on CMR will integrate MHPSS among health care personnel will be measured by recording the number of GBV survivors receiving CMR services (project target: 10). While following the strategic objectives of the Health Cluster, the project is also committed to achieving better critical protection related and specialized services through the integrated approach of the project to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas. This will be achieved through training and deploying a team of counselors and community facilitators in basic skills in offering remote, one-on-one and group based GBV and MHPSS services and referrals/linkage to other partners/services like protection. To measure the reach of the specialized services, the number of MHPSS consultations will be recorded and reported. Additionally, MdM will make prevention and mitigation of GBV/protection risks a priority through enhanced preparedness and mitigation measures. The reach of community-based sensitization and awareness creation will be measured by recording the number of persons reached through awareness sessions conducted by the MHPSS/GBV community facilitators on topics such as stress identification and management, suicide prevention, copying mechanisms, etc. for STI, GBV and COVID-19 affected people (project target: 3659).

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

MdM continues to conduct periodic need assessments, as well as regular meetings and participation in key clusters with different stakeholders, community leaders and local and international organizations. In addition, MdM works in collaboration with local partners such as SMC (Sudan Medical Care), with an extensive knowledge on community sensitization, giving us the overview and tools to adapt the interventions to the specific needs of the targeted population, particularly those from the most vulnerable members. Therefore, the messages will be adapted to the socio-cultural context, as well as to the ages and genders of the right holders. For example, counsellors and Community Facilitators will be trained on recording oral feedback for those who are most vulnerable and cannot access the health facilities or are unable to write or have an opportunity to give confidential feedback. MdM established a women’s and girls’ centre in Duk county where women and girls gather to learn skills and share experiences to empower themselves and improve their health. The same platform will be used to gather their feedback and perception regarding the action by MdM. In addition, the team will conduct radio talk shows on specific Health, MHPSS/GBV topics translated in local dialects besides running radio jingles in the same dialects. Through the radio talk shows, the project team will engage with beneficiaries across the board and be able to respond to any concerns but also receive feedback to help adapt to the needs of all including highly vulnerable persons. All participation and consultation tools will be age and disability inclusive and will have a gender and anthropological approach.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

MDM recognizes the right of beneficiaries and community members to give feedback and seek responses from the activities being implemented by incorporating a complaints and feedback mechanism system. This tool fosters transparency by establishing two-way interactions between the community and the project team and also enhances the ability of the community to spot-check pros and cons from their perspective. When setting up the Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM), MDM included the community in the system design by making sure that the community choose 1) their preferred channel of sending complaints and responses, 2) Identified the appropriate location suitable for them to installed suggestion boxes and 3) selected the preferred language to use in the system. The CFRM provides feedback and seeks response in relation to MDM activities in a manner that is safe and dignified, confidential, appropriate and within the local context and majorly non-threatening and accessible. MdM commits to the inclusion of the targeted communities into the project decision making and embeds community engagement, public communication, and management of project information into its projects. MdM incorporates CFRM in its interventions through communication, transparency, learning, and adaptation. During the first months of the project, the M&E Officer will collect data from the affected population on their preferred way to receive information and give feedback or complaints and the channels will be adjusted as necessary. Throughout the project, beneficiaries and other stakeholders will receive information about the project activities. This will be channelled through different ways including verbal communications and other methods based on beneficiary preferences. A Complaints Feedback and Response Mechanism that ensures confidentiality and is accessible to all beneficiaries, community members and stakeholders will be implemented through: - Satisfaction Surveys; Satisfaction surveys will be conducted throughout to measure the quality of services, giving patients the opportunity to share experiences, criticism, and highlight strengths & weakness of staff & resources. - Comment boxes; Comment boxes will be installed in supported health facilities for patients’ recommendations and sharing of their experiences. Only MdM staff will have access to these submissions and will collect and report responses weekly. When relevant and appropriate, the feedback and/or complaints will be discussed with partners. - The establishment of a phone number and email address that will be communicated to beneficiaries - Organizing and facilitating IEC material validation workshops with relevant and key members. - Conducting satisfaction surveys after awareness raising sessions and training sessions. - Appointment of CFRM focal persons in each health facility and each community who receive training on how to receive and report complaints in a confidential manner. By opening multiple communication channels (phone, email, comment boxes, community meetings, satisfaction surveys), MdM increases the accessibility and diversity in responses, thus allowing for an improved monitoring of project performance and effectiveness. Finally, MdM commits to answering non-sensitive incoming feedback and complaints within a month, and sensitive feedback and complaints in a shorter time frame depending on the level of incidence. In the case of PSEA, this is a maximum period of two weeks.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

A context-appropriate Community Based Complaint Mechanism (CBCM) will be developed to facilitate feedback and case presentation from the communities we work with. In order to facilitate the reporting process, the following 3 different reporting mechanisms are established for each person to select the most appropriate one for their situation:1. Procedure set out in MdM’s Code of Conduct and Ethical Behaviour.EmailOr2. Community complaint mechanism established in each context.Or3. Someone from management/coordination who you trust. All reports of SEAH, and subsequent follow-up, will be recorded in a secure and confidential database. The complaint will be officially collected within 24 hours, and a specific team will call a meeting to analyse the case, assess the immediate risks and identify the next risks within 72 hours of receiving the case. This team is composed of:- Person responsible for PSEAH at the Headquarters and Focal Point at Headquarters or in the field.- A person in a responsible position at the corresponding headquarters or field (Country Coordination/Project Coordination/Headquarters Coordination, member of the Steering Committee, who will have the maximum responsibility for decision-making.- If necessary, in the process of investigation an expert in managing cases of harassment, sexual exploitation, and abuse, outside the association. Once the case has been resolved, the HHRR Department, as appropriate, will be available to take appropriate action (e.g., disciplinary sanctions, written censure, etc., depending on the outcome of the investigation).Anyone involved in a case must be available and have time flexibility if necessary to participate in the process. Temporary suspension of work may also be considered.All documentation of the investigation conducted (incident report, investigation report / final report, evidence, witness interviews, external and internal referrals, security plan, signed consent form) must be kept completely confidential and recorded in a secure location with limited access to the investigation team.Within the first two weeks of receiving the case, the victim/survivor should be informed of the progress of the case management. They will have been informed beforehand of its receipt.The whole investigation process will take no more than 4 or 5 weeks. The person from MdM Spain responsible for PSEAH will collaborate in the coordination and contact with the project teams. Complaint mechanisms should incorporate the possibility of referring cases from SEAH involving staff from another entity (e.g. a UN agency, or other NGO), to follow up the case where this is legally possible.MdM opposes reprisals. Any person employed, volunteering, from the association or with any contractual link to MdM who makes a good faith report of possible sexual exploitation or abuse, or harassment, assists, testifies or participates in any investigation or proceeding, or who reasonably opposes such conduct in the workplace, shall not be adversely affected in the terms and conditions of their employment, and shall not be discriminated against or dismissed on this basis.Focal Points will be assigned to each mission/project and will be responsible for informing and training all staff in PSEAH, raising awareness, promoting good practices and developing and implementing mechanisms that are culturally appropriate and based on the reality of each context, developing awareness-raising tools that are close to and adapted to age, language or diversity, for staff who are hired, voluntaries, or any person linked to MdM and communities. MdM will ensure that communities we work with are fully informed of the expected behaviour of the organisation’s staff and know how to report any suspicions. Their functions include providing support to prevent and respond to possible cases of PSEAH, raising awareness through briefings, promoting good practices, supporting victims/survivors, reporting complaints

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback from beneficiaries and staff is essential to ensure accountable, safe, inclusive, and meaningful service provision. Feedback from beneficiaries will be recorded, analysed, and presented in a systematic way. Emphasis will be placed on closing the feedback loop, meaning that the person who raised a complaint will always receive feedback on their case and that as far as possible, beneficiary data will be used to inform programmatic decision-making. Every three months the M&E focal point will produce an accountability report and a workshop will be held to discuss the main findings and decide on action points. Other means that will be employed to receive and respond to feedback and complaints received include community level meetings and focus group discussions targeting key informants and opinion leaders in the community Moreover, MdM’s CFRM ensures follow-up of the feedback/complaints from the entry points through: - Complaints or Feedback receipt and categorization: Complaints or feedback are sent to a coordination committee that categorizes the information into 1. Feedback, 2. MdM complaint, 3. External complaint, 4. Complaint out of the scope of the mechanism - Complaints and Feedback handling category: 1. Feedback/suggestions are shared in a confidential manner with team leaders and supervisors as input for programs, 2. MdM coordination committee decides how to assess and address the complaint about quality of services or staff conduct; General Coordinator is involved in serious complaints, 3. Complaints about other services are shared with the relevant organizations as appropriate; General Coordinator is involved in serious complaints, 4. Complaints out of the scope of the mechanism do not require follow-up - Response and organizational learning: Complainants should receive a response, preferably within 1 month; Complaints and actions taken are recorded in an internal MdM recording keeping tool; Monthly reporting of key complaints and feedback is sent to coordination. All collected feedback will be reviewed by the MdM team and, if appropriate, partner organizations. Appropriate changes, communications to health staff, policies or processes will then be made, with information shared to the community through one of the already established communication channels.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Multiple efforts will be undertaken to address the protection risks associated with the project. The affected population will participate in program design through an assessment and participatory discussions that include questions on their basic needs and preferred ways to address them, access to channels of information, as well as protection risks associated. A beneficiary feedback mechanism emphasizing on mutual communication will be in place. The priority will be to offer a variety of channels to raise feedback or complaints, so that affected populations with different needs will be able to access and use the mechanism. Beneficiaries will be consulted on their preferred mechanisms to give feedback, ensuring that people with different vulnerabilities and characteristics are part of this assessment. Furthermore, to ensure staff neutrality, the mechanism will be managed by the M&E team, who will receive on-the-job training, including training on protection, to ensure that feedback and complaints, especially any PSAE complaint will be dealt with in an appropriate, confidential and time efficient manner. At the same time, clear SOPs and referral pathways will be developed, including managing critical or sensitive cases that will be registered on a separate database with limited access and will be answered within 24 hours. Other practical measures will be incorporated in the activity design to minimize risk of exposure to violence due to distances to medical services. Mobile clinics will be prioritised, to offer quality maternal and child healthcare, SRH, and SHBV/CMR to women in a safe way, ensuring they do not need to take dangerous routes to reach these services. The health workers running the mobile clinics will receive training on SGBV and mental health topics to better address protection needs that are identified in the rural communities. Regarding data protection, MdM counts on a reference guide on the management of data related to health and life stories of persons in the field, “For Ethics in the Field”, (https://www.medecinsdumonde.org/en/actualites/publications/2012/10/08/ethics-field), which details several tools and guidelines based on Humanitarian and Do No Harm Principles. Program users must understand what we are going to do (consultation, examination, treatment, interview, film, photographs) and must give their full and free consent. Specific guidance is provided in case of child mistreatment, acts of torture or cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatments and victims of any kind of violence. MdM has the duty to inform, to protect and to attest following a request from a victim of violence.

MdM will support provision, printing and dissemination of IEC materials on SGBV/MHPSS, with culture and religious appropriate language, to be used for sensitization and awareness by the community facilitators or health workers . MdM will deploy a toll-free telephone hotline manned by the counselor(s) at Bor State Hospital to provide remote SGBV/PSS assistance to callers who are unable to physically reach the providers.
