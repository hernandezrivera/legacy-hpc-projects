4 - Needs

Education in Northeast Syria has been substantially disrupted as a result of the unique education system imposed by SDF. The psycho-social safety and physical protection of children have been seriously compromised throughout the crisis with over 80 percent of students reporting that they feel anxious, worried or stressed. 50% of children, especially those in areas under ISIS control, are facing constant challenges in accessing education safely and coping with the distressful impact of conflict and hostilities. Children are very negatively affected by the regional conflicts, the imminent displacements and from being out of school for many years. Families of children with disabilities in conflict or crisis often lack the means or financial ability to provide their children with the necessary equipment. The Education Committees and HAO reported that the total number of schools in Al Hassakeh is 500 serving 175,000 students. Out of 500 schools, there are 81 in Derik city. The total number of students is 11,703 (Primary schools: 7,955, secondary schools: 2,519, high school: 1,070 and Kindergarten: 159). The schools are hosting displaced children from NES areas and the possibility of that number of displaced children increasing due to the ongoing conflict in Idleb is very imminent. In Raqqa, the total number of the operating schools is 214 providing education services to 99,250 students. 4,000 of them are IDPs as the education committee in Raqqa reported. The majority of schools do not have supermarkets or restaurants for students to get food. In Der Ezor, the total number of primary schools is 599, providing education services to 216,806 children.

5 - Implementation of activities

SELECTION CRITERIA FOR SCHOOLS:Schools with the majority of their students from families under the poverty line, having at least %50 IDP students, having at least %60 of students who are not supported with food distribution at home, and schools that are not within camps. PROJECT ACTIVITIES:- Coordinate with the FSS Cluster, Education Cluster, HAO, Education Committee and partners in the region to make sure that no other entity is planning to address the same gaps we are intending to address. - SWEDO will arrange field assessment visits to the intended schools to make sure the gaps we intend to address still exist and afterward identify the most vulnerable schools for the project. - Coordination of the procurement of locally-sourced food items: clear and transparent information on procurement requirements and procedures will be shared with food suppliers. - Procurement of locally-sourced food items: evaluation of offers, suppliers’ capacity and preparation of a contingency plan to ensure constant, timely supplies and adequate cash flow to suppliers. - Establishing a warehouse management system inclusive of warehouse rent, in/out records, period cleaning, and arrangement of food items storage space, cold management, food hygiene, and food quality regular inspections, waste management records, and procedures. Establishing a transport management system inclusive of contracts for transport to and from the warehouse to schools, loading/offloading of food items, transporting food quality and waste management. - Preparing a calendar of meal preparation and distribution to schools. - Training for teachers, operators in schools and children on protection, safe environment, and GBV. - Training of female school staff on food hygiene, safe food handling, in coordination with schools’ administration. Establishing a procedure at the school level for food waste, plastic items, and food packaging waste.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

VIOLENCE From the extensive experience of SWEDO in implementing protection activities, the following key risks related to violence can affect the program: violence against children, violence between children, exploitation of students by teachers and attacks on children. The mitigation measures aim at ensuring that the school is a safe space for children. Therefore, all teachers and the staff working with children will be trained and required to sign a Code of Conduct for minimum behavioral standards. The involvement of parents and relatives is key to allow access to school facilities to happen in a safe way, eventually organizing group walks or escorts. CHILD LABOR Children can be exploited to loading/unload heavy material, prepare meals, clean the school premises or be assigned to heavy or risky works such as the disposal of food wastes or using hazardous tools. The mitigation measures include awareness campaigns to make sure that teachers, family members, and the overall community are aware of WFP standards on child labor and a mechanism for complaints is in place. PHYSICAL BARRIERS, EXCLUSIONS, AND ABUSEIdentify vulnerable groups of children with the assistance of school teachers and members of the community is fundamental to activate complimentary support. During the implementation of the project, our staff may receive information from school staff or community members concerning children that can’t attend school due to barriers, social or economic exclusion. Even targets of such cases are outside the scope of the program. SWEDO believes in the importance of putting any extra effort to ensure the safety of education spaces and improve student’s attendance. Therefore, any case raised by school staff or community members to SWEDO project staff will be treated by coordinating with WFP and relevant counterparts such as the Committee of Education.For abuse cases, a PSEA focal point will be designated along with training to raise awareness.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Consistent with SWEDO’s standard approach to humanitarian programming, the school feeding is based on the “Do No Harm” principle, with program activities designed to be culturally appropriate and tailored to the context while preventing competition over resources. We will make every effort to mitigate the potential for conflict stemming from program resources by: maintaining a conflict-sensitive approach to implementation and ensuring that our partners do the same; and promoting open community engagement and transparency in materials distribution and assistance. SWEDO is a signatory to the Joint Operating Principles (JOPs), prepared through the NGO Forum and adopted by the UN, as a pledge to avoid influence by and involvement of all armed militant groups. The JOPs govern interaction between humanitarian actors and all armed parties to the Syrian conflict, including USG-sanctioned groups. Among other principles, the JOPs state that humanitarian organizations will not accede to requests by armed parties to the conflict: • To provide beneficiary information.• To be involved in or have influence over, the selection of staff, or personal details of staff;• To provide armed or other escorts to humanitarian vehicles or personnel;• To influence the content of needs assessments; or• To deliver assistance to combatant forces or individual combatants or to give control of humanitarian stores or commodities to combatant forces. We will continue meeting with local leaders (i.e. local councils, relief committees, and other stakeholders) in the target areas to ensure their awareness and understanding of the JOPs and humanitarian principles of impartiality, neutrality, and independence as a condition of providing assistance. Staff will clearly communicate to all stakeholders that no interference by any armed group – sanctioned or otherwise – in program implementation or commodity control will be tolerated.
