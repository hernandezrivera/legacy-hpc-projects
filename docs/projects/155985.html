Project Summary

SSince the beginning of the Syrian crisis in March 2011, Armadilla and Zahret Al Madan (ZAM) have been working in favour of the low income urban communities of Southern Suburbs of Damascus, which felt dramatically the negative effects of the conflict, providing assistance to the most vulnerable families of the area. The project will be developed into the ZAM Centre, which is located in Midan Kawakibi, and provide support to the families through an integrated programme which include children and youngsters with disability care (physical and cognitive rehabilitation therapy), women empowerment (PSS activities and small vocational training), and distribution of essential goods (such as food parcels).
Around 5,300 people refer to the ZAM Centre for relief and protection assistance, 91% of which are IDPs, coming from the areas of Hajar Al Aswad, Yarmouk camp, Sbeneh, Set Zeinab, Babila, Yalda, forced to flee in search of a safer place to stay. To these family, it should be added the ones sent by Ministry of Social Affairs– with whom Zahret Al Madan coordinate its action - to the Centre for assistance and support.
The project purpose is to provide support to 650 vulnerable families (about 5,300 persons), in order to tackle their food insecurity status through the distribution of food parcels.
All families attending the ZAM centre should be considered extremely vulnerable; the initial conditions of poverty, low income, and scarce access to basic services of these families have been further aggravated by the negative impact of the conflict, as a consequence of displacement from their home places (see below, for the needs analysis).
The expected impact also consider that being food the main concern for the families, through receiving food assistance, they can use their own scarce resources for other vital necessities (healthcare, education for children etc.).

Needs

In October 2018, an assessment was conducted among 700 families, who are beneficiaries in various sector of the humanitarian assistance by Armadilla and Zahret Al Madan (e.g. families which children with disabilities are assisted by the Rehabilitation Unit of ZAM) on their economic status and capacity to cope with problems affecting their household basic needs.The survey has been conducted by Armadilla and ZAM staff, developing a specific questionnaire in which the beneficiaries highlighted their satisfaction for previous assistance, update their family and economic status (number of family members, annual income etc.) and express their concerns and needs. This allowed Armadilla and ZAM in creating a general baseline for the target population of the programme and, consequently, of the possible activities to be developed.The survey revealed that 23% of beneficiaries have no income, 11% have income less than 10,000 SYP/month, 61% have income between 10.000 and 20.000 SYP/month, and 5% have income more than 20,000 SYP/month.This situation is aggravated by the fact that that food and non-food commodities showed remarkable increases. The situation has increased their dependence on external assistance (humanitarian aid) to cover basic needs like food and food items, and healthcare/access to basic social services. Moreover, two critical points should be stressed:1. Among the families assessed, which are the basis of the present proposal, 12% of the entire target group, 656 persons, are children/youngsters with disability (cognitive and/or physical). This situation deeply hindering the capacity of those families in cope with the challenges presented in the context, and2. Many household breeders (79%) are lone women, which have lost their husbands during the crisis.Armadilla and ZAM monitoring among a sample of beneficiaries (130), shows also that 75% of the families have a Food Consumption Score under 42, and therefore should be considered as in a borderline situation and at risk of food insecurity.Moreover, as stressed in the summary above, Zahret Al Madan coordinates its efforts with the Ministry of Social Affairs, which address to the ZAM Centre vulnerable people coming from different areas and seeking for support. There is, therefore, an increase of families assisted in various forms by Zahret Al Madan Centre, which updates the list of people in need on periodical basis and which, according to their vulnerability status and specific needs, may be included in the project.Lastly, due to the scarcity of resources, not all the needs of the target beneficiaries could be attended in the last one year and half. Armadilla and ZAM provided the more vulnerable families with support to physical and cognitive rehabilitation for children/youngsters with disabilities, as well as with Psychosocial sessions addressing parents and relatives of such minors. The lack of provision of food assistance had a negative impact on the lives of families, mitigated by the capacity of Zahret Al Madan to address the most vulnerable in seeking support among other Institutions and/or associations.

Implementation of activities

Activities will be developed according to both general standards and good practices, and the experience gained by the Organisation in the field of similar activities and in the Area in the last years.Activities will be implemented in strict collaboration with the local partner, which will also coordinate with any local relevant stakeholders and actors (MoSA, Community leaders, key beneficiaries). Armadilla will coordinate with international stakeholders, providing data regarding the results, the challenges, and any other relevant pieces of information regarding the activity and its impact. In detail, the following activities will be performed:1. Update beneficiary list, and chose of the beneficiaries according to the resulting vulnerability status. The list will be drafted in collaboration with the local partner, which will include also people in need addressed from the MoSA to the Centre.2. According to the available financial resources, a procedure for the purchasing of goods on the local market will be realized.3. Monthly distribution of food parcels will be held in the ZAM Centre in Midan Kawakibi. People registered in ZAM as subject of the activity will be advised to go to the Centre in the day/hour previously established. . Partner’s volunteers and workers will take care, under the supervision of Armadilla staff, to communicate with beneficiaries date and modalities of the distributions, in order to avoid any possible logistics or security problems. Quality control of the goods will be realized by both Armadilla and ZAM staff, supported by a mechanism for potential beneficiaries’ complaints.4. Training on FCS, HDDS and WDDS will be delivered by one Armadilla expert to ZAM volunteers, in order to increase the capacity of the local partner in performing accurate surveys on the individual food insecurity conditions of beneficiaries.5. At least two formal evaluation moment with key beneficiaries will be held in the ZAM Centre, in order to receive complaints and suggestions to improve the quality of the activity in the future. The evaluations will be preceded by surveys on the impact of the activity among the families.6. Internal evaluations (mid-term and final) performed by Armadilla monitoring expert, supported by ZAM and Armadilla local staff through single interviews to beneficiaries, which will serve to stress any critical point faced, the adopted solutions to solve problems in the future and so on. The project does not foresee at this stage a shift from the food assistance to livelihood.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Access to the Area of the activitiesThe Zahret Al Madan (ZAM) Centre, located in Midan Kawakibi , where the activities will be implemented is situated in an area so far considered safe as it never witnessed heavy clashes. However, the Organisation will be in continuous communication with the Syria Logistic Cluster and will organize activities and movements only after consultation with it. The activities will be held during the opening of the Centre, between 10 and 15, in order to avoid staff and beneficiaries’ movement during the peak hours and to enable them to come back to their homes before getting dark.Availability of goodsGoods will be purchased on the local market, looking for the best vendor in terms of economy and capacity of guarantee the provision of service. A back-up list of potential suppliers guarantees the possibility of finding the goods in case of problems. Lack of permission to enter in Syria for expatriate staff Local staff of the Organisation will be able to perform the proposed activities, thanks also to the long collaboration with the local partner which has implemented in the course of the last 5 years more than 7 projects similar to the present proposal.
