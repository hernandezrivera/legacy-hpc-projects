Project Summary

SI has been present in Raja county since 2017 with WASH and Food Security interventions allowing a good understanding of the area. In addition, the recent multi-sectoral assessment conducted there, fine-tuned the analysis of the context, especially concerning the most recent humanitarian needs. This solid experience allowed SI to design the intervention described hereafter. This project will develop two distinct implementation strategies targeting two types of locations, in order to fit with the context and to meet with the specific needs of the most vulnerable.
Firstly, SI will reach the villages and remote locations of Raja County where the recent returnees settled. There, SI will mainly target the returnees who came back 3 months ago, as well as the most vulnerable IDPs and Host Communities Households. SI will distribute Emergency Shelter kits to these identified households and support the families to build their shelter. In order to complement this support, SI will also distribute NFI kits. The creation of distribution committees and complaint committees will encourage community participation and involvement in the project. In addition, SI will ensure regular visits and feedback desks in the villages to maintain the link with these communities. This aspect is particularly important considering the fact that only very few actors are intervening in the area.
Secondly, SI will intervene within the most stable urban areas like, but not limited to Raja town, and will target the HHs that have been set up few months ago. The project will support them with distribution of NFI replacement kits and with shelter reinforcement, including a protection mainstreaming scope. This activity will support these targeted families in their resilience and positive coping mechanisms.
All the project will pay a specific attention to protection, gender and do not harm aspects.
The project is targeting the prioritized locations with unmet needs. Due to its remoteness and unstable security context, and despite the high level of needs, Raja has received limited humanitarian assistance. For years, the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) reports have classified Raja mostly in IPC phase 3 or 4. The IPC report published in June 2019 classified Raja in IPC phase 4 or emergency. In this respect the needs are critical and diverse. Thus, SI will ensure that the proposed project will reach the very remote parts of the county. Being based in Raja, SI will ensure quickness in needs analysis and responses for the new arrivals and affected vulnerable populations, especially thanks to its acceptance and knowledge of the area.
The project will be linked with the ongoing and future humanitarian interventions in Raja, including the ones implemented by SI. S-NFI assistance will be well integrated in SI’s WaSH in Nutrition project, an ongoing intervention in consortium to respond to health and nutrition needs. Moreover, in 2019, SI has organized assessments in remote areas with the participation of protection actors to have an integrated and conflict sensitive analysis. In this respect, SI will ensure that protection partners are much involved in the planned response with a risk analysis being done before and during each intervention.
This project will contribute to the HRP SO2 (Protect vulnerable women, men, girls and boys though provision of specialized and integrated services): Raja has experienced cycles of conflicts having completely or partially destroyed shelters. Majority of its population has been displaced and is currently returning. According to the Oxfam South Sudan gender analysis report, 51% of the respondents are linking GBV cases to shelter. In this regard, SI project in Raja will ensure reaching out mostly women and at risk vulnerable families with shelter.
Depending of futheer needs assessments and coordination with the partners, SI could plan an intervention in KAju-Keji, considering its experience in the area and the needs.

1 - Needs

Since the recent Peace Agreement signed in August 2018, Raja county has been relatively calm after several years of armed conflict. This positive evolution of the context is giving some confidence to the population to return. However, the situation remains critical for the population of the former Western Bahr El Gazal State (WBG) where 162,000 IDPs are still present. April 2019 IOM DMT report stated that there has been a recent influx of about 3500 returnees (individuals) coming back from Darfur in Sudan and settling in Dulu, north of Raja town. These return movements should increase throughout 2019-2020, linked with the improvement of the perceived security situation in Raja county. These returned populations often show a dire need of humanitarian assistance. If movements continue, Raja county could witness a spike in humanitarian needs in most parts of the county. In addition to that, host communities are already facing acute needs on various sectors. Therefore, these movements of returns will worsen their already alarming situation, by creating an extra pressure on the already limited resources. Beside the impact of livelihoods and the multiple forced displacement causing the loss of valuable assets, the clashes that happened in April 2017, have caused the host communities to bear the burden of the vulnerable people left behind, like elderlies and people with special needs.As stated above, with the recently signed peace deal, some relative peace has prevailed in Raja county since mid‐2018. In this respect, returnees started to come back, but their inability to live in a proper shelter will cause serious risks such as health issues and protection issues. Women will continue to be disproportionately affected by the lack of shelter and NFI. Indeed, according to the ‘Oxfam’s South Sudan gender analysis (March‐July 2016)’, 51% of the respondents are linking GBV cases to inadequate shelters.In order to capture the level of needs and propose relevant responses, SI conducted a multi-sectoral assessment in Timsaha (Eree County, Lol State) and Raja early 2019. Based on this assessment, 67% of households reported that their shelters size is inadequate, the main reason being that they are too small for their family (75%) with an average of 6.7 people per household. Moreover 77% of the cooking items observed were in poor conditions, 91% of households reported not having blankets, 80% not having sleeping mat or mattress and 61% not having mosquito nets. In the minority of families having sleeping items, most are in poor condition and only a few inacceptable conditions. Thus, the needs of these households linked to living conditions and NFI are critical.In addition, 2020 HNO S-NFI analysis and the assessment revealed that the crisis faced by the households in Raja county is multi-sectoral and affects all their basic needs, whatever their status: host community, IDP, returnee, etc. Those needs being attributed to limited or lack of access to safe water and sanitation facilities, high levels of malnutrition, GBV cases, high morbidity and mortality as a result of WaSH-related diseases due to flooding. In addition, based on SI's multi-sectoral assessment conducted in September 2019 in the newly accessible communities in Raja county, it appeared that 40 to 50% of the households are female-led, and thus particularly vulnerable. It also shows that 91% of the interviewed households have a shelter smaller than the standards (3.5m2 per person), only 3% feel safe during harsh weather and an average of more than 90% of the households declare their needs in terms of NFI as high. These findings justify a quick intervention, especially in remote areas newly accessible, as they have never been targeted by humanitarian interventions.Depending of futheer needs assessments and coordination with the partners and SFP, SI could plan an intervention in other areas, such as Kaju-Keji, considering its experience in the area and the needs.

2 - Activities

The project will ensure that the most vulnerable conflict affected returnees, IDPs and local population with acute needs are provided with adequate, timely lifesaving shelter and life sustaining NFI. SI will use a two folded approach, in line with the needs highlighted in the 2020 HNO and HRP objectives. The implementation be strongly coordinated with the SFP. a) Distribution of emergency shelter and NFI: this approach will ensure immediate S-NFI needs are addressed quickly to relief the affected women and children from further sufferings. SI will target the locations outside central Raja where returnees are received. After the completion of a needs assessment and risk analysis, the beneficiaries will be selected based on the Clusters guidelines (score cards) and community engagement. SI will target the returnees or recently IDPs arrived in the villages less than 3 months ago, to distribute shelter kits, including plastic sheets, wooden poles, nylon rope, bundles of rubber rope, sandbags, etc. Carpenters/artisans from the community will be trained (1 carpenter for 50 shelters in average) as they will support the households to build their shelter. The use of daily workers will depend on the capacities of the targeted families and their specific needs. A strong community participation will also be requested to achieve the emergency shelters and to help the HHs who are not able to participate, such as the elderlies and people with disabilities. In parallel, in the same targeted villages, SI will distribute 3000 NFI kits to the most vulnerable HHs, without specific status target. The NFI kits will be distributed according to the needs assessment, registration and verification, to the most vulnerable returnees, IDPs and host communities and in line with the Cluster guidelines and score card. The NFI kits will contain, in line with the Shelter/NFI Cluster Strategy 2019-2020: tarpaulins, blankets, mosquito nets, ropes, kitchen sets, sleeping mats, etc. WaSH NFI kits will be added including buckets with tap, jerrycans, filter clothes, etc. When possible the NFI will be requested from the Core Pipeline. b) In addition to this activity and in line with S-NFI Cluster HRP objective, SI will support the repair of shelters for the most vulnerable returnees in urban and sub urban areas. As indicated, the population has been displaced many times and the majority of existing shelters need repairs and rehabilitation to ensure the safety of the users, particularly linked with GBV risks. This approach will target the use of available local materials for repairing shelters, allowing beneficiaries to significantly improve their living conditions and increase wellbeing in the short and medium term. This activity will target families who came back between 3 and 6 months ago and who are already set up. It will support them to improve their living conditions and their resilience. After selecting the beneficiaries, SI will conduct a technical assessment of the current dwellings to define the scope of the works and needs. Then, SI will distribute a reinforcement kit adjusted to each specific case. The content of the kit will follow the guidelines of the Cluster and the Shelter technical working group, and will be validated with the SFP. Local artisans from Raja will be identified (1 carpenter for 50 shelters in average) and a workshop/training will be arranged for them. These skilled workers will work with daily workers to support the beneficiaries with special needs (elderlies, disabled). The works will be supervised by SI's staffs. Considering the risks in terms of waterborne diseases and malnutrition in this county, the SI team will take the opportunity of the distribution to provide awareness messages regarding hygiene and prevention of health. A Post Distribution Monitoring will be carried on between 2 and 4 weeks after each distribution.In case of intervention in remote areas, SI will request the services of the local humanitarian hubs.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The displacement-affected communities will be the main beneficiaries of the project. Indeed, this project will provide support to the return of households and IDPs of the area. In addition, SI will focus on the host communities who have been impacted by the crisis and saw their livelihood reducing.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The proposed intervention will be in line with the projects implemented for several years by SI in the area. More particularly, this project will involve existing groups, already trained, to ensure their sustainability and the project’s durability. At the field level SI will coordinate with the State Focal Point and other actors in presence such as IOM to avoid any duplication and overlapping of the distribution. The Mine Actions Organizations can be consulted to ensure the security of SI teams and HHs.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

This project will support the resettling of the refugees and IDPs, while helping the host communities to absorb this extra pressure on the limited resources.In addition, through this project, SI will contribute to the HRP SO2 : protect vulnerable women, men, girls and boys though provision of specialized and integrated services. Furthermore, SI will ensure that communities are consulted throughout the project period through systematic consultations and set up of complaints and response mechanisms to ensure quality and integration of beneficiary views in the project allowing achievement of goals in line with S-NFI Cluster HRP objective 4.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

SI’s assessment showed the context in the area has been more and more stable for a few months, allowing the beginning of the return movements and improving its access while reducing the protection risks. Furthermore, SI’s solid experience of this context will be a strong asset to monitor the situation.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Even if this project is focused on life-saving needs with emergency shelters and basic essential items, SI will pay special attention to the quality of the distributed items to ensure mid-term impacts of its program. Furthermore, a dynamic of empowerment and resilience will be given with the training of local masons and carpenters and the direct advises to the households to repair their shelters. SI will also valorise the local materials the families can most easily find without the humanitarian assistance and distribution. The collaboration with a local protection actor will allow the protraction of this specific assistance and support after ending this project. Furthermore, this project will allow SI to advocate and encourage other sector actors to implement activities (WaSH, FSL, livelihood, health, education, etc.) in the same locations in order to build the resilience of the targeted population, to support them in setting up positive coping mechanisms and to encourage their reinstallation as soon the context allows it. SI hopes a resumption of Raja market in the forthcoming months. Thus, SI will assess regularly the market and will encourage other livelihood activities. If the market analysis confirms it, SI will use cash and voucher in order to reinforce the markets and the livelihood opportunities in the area. For sustainability and exit strategy, SI will also train local carpenters and artisans for the construction and repair of shelters and dwellings, in order to ensure that they are capable to support future returnees. A one–day workshop/training will be arranged for them to ensure they understand the type of works involved, prioritization and payment mode. The artisans with limited skills will be paired to ensure transfer of knowledge among peers.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

SI will strive to gain acceptance from local authorities and stakeholders; follow strict logistics and financial procedures; streamline security risk assessments at all stages of program design; gather up-to-date security alerts from various formal and informal sources and adjust our actions. Community awareness of the principles and arrangement of the program, with Community Feedback and Complaint Mechanism (CFCM) are in place. SI staff are equipped with the capacities to negotiate access with the authorities. Access negotiation is based on a strong commitment on the respect of IHL and humanitarian principles. Specific trainings of the key people involved in access negotiation are conducted.In addition to the risks detailed above, SI will mitigate the specific risks such as risks linked to the important variations of the population size in the areas of intervention. Mitigation measures will include advocating with other partners to complement SI interventions when necessary. Another risk to take into account is the supplies transportation in some areas during the rainy season, which might cause intervention delays. The chronogram of activities will be design to implement certain activities according to the seasonal calendar. SI also has equipment able to operate despite the extreme weather conditions.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

AAP/CCE will be taken into consideration at each step of the proposed action to ensure that the points of view of the affected population are considered. They will be involved all along the project, from the assessments, to the monitoring of activities, including beneficiaries’ selection and composition of kits and design of activities. SI will involve the different groups of population during the assessments to get a precise analysis of the needs and adapt the response to each specific vulnerability. Likewise, each groups will be involved during the verification, registration and distribution, for each activity implemented. SI staffs will be well trained accodingly. Specifically, before the intervention, SI will organize focus group discussions per location with girls, boys, women, men, elderlies and people with disabilities for community consultations to ensure equitable access to the services provided. It will ensure the appropriate uses of the distributed items and also improve the mobilization and participation of the population. This approach guarantees safety, dignity and protection of affected people. At the start of each activities, SI will set up a specific complaint/feedback mechanism adapted to the local context to collect the complaints and feedbacks from the beneficiaries and take appropriate action all along the intervention. SI will pay specific attention that the ulnerable women and children's feedbacks are collected. The complaint desks will be the link between affected (vulnerable)households and SI, and will be able to spread messages, explain the selection criteria, report needs from the community, etc. SI will organize regular feedback desk within each location, to listen and register the complaints, collect information and answer.If SI has already large experience of accountability to the communities and complaints responses mechanisms, the AAP for this project will be also aligned with the standardized community-led complaints and feedback.
