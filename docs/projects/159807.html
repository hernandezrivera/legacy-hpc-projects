Project Summary

SOS Children's Villages International (SOS-CVI) is implementing emergency MCH interventions in Banadir and Bay since 1991 providing health, nutrition, and protection services to Internally Displaced People (IDP), returnees and host communities and recently expanded to Bakool. SOS Somalia aims to support the affected population to reduce the burden of diseases, improve equitable access to primary health care and improve nutrition. This will be achieved providing nutrition supplies to field and mobile maternal and child health services; and delivering fixed and mobile Outpatient Therapeutic Programs and referral of severely malnourished children with medical complication to the Stabilization Centers operated by partners. The project’s goal is to contribute towards reducing morbidity and mortality through an integrated emergency nutrition, health responses for disaster-affected vulnerable populations. To achieve this, an estimated 408,000 individuals (163,800 IDPs and 244,200 Host community members), affected by displacement and conflict in Bay, Bakool and Banadir will have improved health and nutritional knowledge and status, through improved access to preventive. Preventive components focus on Infant and Young child feeding (IYCF), Health and Nutrition education, immunization, community mobilization and sensitization. The proposed Health and Nutrition intervention is aligned to both Health and Nutrition cluster HRP priorities to focus on basic life-saving activities and community resilience-building activities in all high GAM/SAM and IDP areas. SOS CV Somalia provides referral to SOS hospital and other secondary health services providers. Services offered to PLWs will focus on the treatment of common diseases during pregnancy and childbirth such as anemia, hypertension, bleeding in pregnancy/postpartum, STIs, abortions, monitoring labour progress, safe and clean deliveries and follow up. Health education will focus on danger signs in pregnancy, childbirth and postpartum.
The project will ensure prevention and control of epidemic-prone and communicable diseases through appropriate detection and diagnosis; laboratory verification; confirmation; proper case management; adequate supplies; health education for patients; and regular vaccination programs and emergency vaccination activities for children and expectant mothers. SOS CV will monitor outbreak potential for diseases under surveillance within Somalia using the standard case definitions. Through extensive presence in communities, the project will sensitize around signs and symptoms of epidemic-prone diseases, mode of transmission, and preventative measures, and encourage early healthcare-seeking behavior. CHWs will be trained or receive refresher training to identify and refer cases of critical communicable diseases; and will be trained at a minimum to administer ORS for AWD. SOS Somalia proposes to address urgent needs and gaps in the health and nutrition sectors by offering free, comprehensive primary health care services in the target areas. SOS will also strengthen the capacity of implementation staff through joint supportive supervision with the Ministry of Health at the health and nutrition facilities. The project will employ an integrated approach to Health, Nutrition through fixed facilities and at community level to address the urgent health needs of vulnerable individuals through scaling up or setting up uninterrupted delivery of free, high quality health and nutrition services. The WASH supports provision of safe and clean drinking water in Nutrition and health sites as well as sanitation facilities. All nutrition sites will have hand washing facilities with soap and water to promote hygiene practices.

Mitigation Measures

2a.1- Collect and use data disaggregated by sex, age and diversity to effectively inform programming. 2- Identify and prioritize the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups 3- Identify individuals who lack documentation to prevent exclusion from services.4- Make facilities and infrastructure disability friendly.5- Provide information about services in both verbal and written form2b. 1- Consult women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, chronically ill, older persons, pregnant and lactating women and marginalized persons to collect accurate information about their specific needs and preferences for health centre location, design and services.2- Engage the community and committee representative to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response3- Create health committees to help maintain health structures and encourage representatives to be involved in the design of facilities and services.4- Reinforce the capacity of the community to provide sustainable health care (eg. engage and support health community workers/volunteers in vaccination campaigns; hire and train local midwives) 5- Coordinate with civil society specializing in working with persons with impaired mobility or disabilities to help identify such individuals and use them as a resource to improve service delivery, train staff and for the referral of cases.3b.1- Identify central locations and avoid areas near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire or violence. 2- Install lights outside and on the access route to the facility. 3- Consider separate waiting areas for males and females. 4- Have a proportionate number of female health staff. If female doctors/nurses are not available, consider a female doctor rotation between locations. In this case, women must be adequately informed of which days a female doctor will be available. 5- Employ health staff with skills and experiences working with children. 3d.1- Consult both displaced and host communities about health needs and assess whether health interventions could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the two communities.2- Be transparent with the affected populations by providing them with accessible and timely information on selection criteria for targeted assistance, organisational procedures and processes that affect them.3- Set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct4- Coordinate with relevant local authorities and engage the community committees and elder representatives to play an active role in preventing community conflict and create community cohesion and participation.5- Share the relevant information to the community at the right time through the right channels.4b.1- Distributions should take place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall.2- Identify central locations - beneficiaries should not have to traverse long distances or pass through conflict zones to access distribution points.3- Avoid placing distribution points near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire or violence. Weapon bearers should not be present inside distribution points.4-Consider the frequency of distributions – the greater the time between distributions the larger the quantities distributed, which puts beneficiaries at risk of attacks and pillage.5-Ensure the ration size is appropriate for the beneficiaries to be able to carry home with no additional physical or financial burden and without being dependent on others to carry it for them
