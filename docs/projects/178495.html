Project Summary

In this project, SCS will implement an intervention in non-formal education aiming to enhance safe and equitable access and improve quality of education. This will be achieved through 5 community learning centres: 2 in Hama city (Al Fayhaa and Riyadi) and 3 in its rural in Hamra, Harbanifse, Suran in which SCS will secure child-friendly, safe and inclusive spaces, including classrooms, vocational training rooms, gender and physical disability sensitive WASH infrastructure and recreational spaces. All of these districts were selected based on their level of accessibility and the severity of needs as per the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) with all the localities being rated 3 on the severity scale. The project is not funded at this stage. The centres will increase access to education for 6200 children (50% female) of different ages (5,700: 6-17 years and 500: 18-24 years) with estimated cost 90 USD per student (6% of targeted beneficiaries have physicals disabilities). Moreover, 100 teachers (50% f) will benefit from teacher training and 3,000 parents and caregivers will benefit from Back-to-Learning campaign (BTL).
In 2020, SCS implemented 3 projects in cooperation with UNHCR, UNICEF and WFP in many sectors with total budget 3,275,795 USD. These projects were able to emergency respond to the displaced, returnees and residents in most of Hama city and rural and were able overreached their target numbers to 453,158 beneficiaries. Social Care Society is one of the leader NGOs in Hama that has practiced humanitarian work in providing Protection, WASH, Health, Education, Food and Livelihoods services since 2013.
According to SCS humanitarian experience in these locations for 3 years: a generation of children and youth are disconnected and unable to access their right to learn. The most significant contributing factors that prevent access to safe and protected learning environments are poor resources. Also, a lack of decent work and safe livelihoods for family members often results in children being removed from learning and sent to work. Importantly the same challenges are being experienced by teachers, facilitators and trainers who also struggle to cope with their own trauma. In this vein, this project will contribute in three of the Immediate Response Priorities as in Syria 2021 Needs and Response Summary: "1) To ensure learning in safe environments, particularly given COVID-19 and other factors, new learning spaces and WASH facilities need to be established ... Basic winterization measures should be in place (heaters with fuel, ventilation, cemented floors, etc.). 2) To ensure that out of school children can catch-up, non-formal education services must be expanded and have clear pathways to formal education ... 3) To enable families to prioritize education, the direct and indirect costs of children attending school needs to be addressed through cash and voucher-based education assistance and teaching and learning supplies, including teacher incentives provided to schools."

4 - Needs

According to Syria 2021 Needs and Response Summary, Feb 2021 " Ten years into the Syria crisis, humanitarian needs are deepening. Ongoing insecurity and the compounded impact of displacement, combined with worsening socio-economic conditions characterized by sharp currency depreciation, with a disproportionate impact on women and children." "As well as the immediate and longer-term effects of the COVID-19 pandemic." "Many students are either behind in their learning, finding it difficult to return to school or at risk of dropping out. Younger children, adolescents and children with disabilities face further challenges accessing learning services due to limited availability of Formal and non-formal early childhood education centers, secondary education services and vocational training opportunities, or education facilities that can provide inclusive education for the estimated 15% of school aged children with disabilities." SCS has already reached the targeted locations since 2018 in many services such as Core Relief Items, Protection, Food and Livelihood except education through 7 community and distribution centers. This is why SCS plans to intervenes in Education sector. Through this education project, SCS will have the ability to provide more integrated services by referral and networking from its other centers in the targeted location.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Existing traditional practices and community structures conflict with child protection principles (e.g., corporal punishment, child rights messaging is perceived as a threat, etc.). Likelihood = High, Impact = High Mitigation measures: Thorough contextual analysis to be undertaken and ensure safe access as part of the project design. For corporal punishment specifically: 1) Teachers are trained on positive discipline techniques as part of their basic training and sign a code of conduct in the school; 2) Teachers and classrooms are regularly monitored; 3) a child safe-guarding focal point is identified in the centers. Risk 2: There is a risk of discrimination leading to certain minority groups or communities to be excluded from the education opportunities. Likelihood = Low, Impact = Medium Mitigation measures: 1) Focus Group Discussions are held with different community groups and leaders; 2) conduct community outreach activities such as back-to-learn campaigns; 3) children and youth are engaged in recreational and social cohesion activities within the community. Risk 3: Political affiliations among local community leaders may inhibit inclusive programming, adherence to humanitarian principles by different stakeholders, influence beneficiary selection, and lead to flaring community tensions. Likelihood = Medium, Impact = Medium Mitigation measures: 1) SCS will lead sensitization workshops with local community leaders and authorities to raise awareness of project aims, activities, and humanitarian principles; 2) Community feedback mechanisms are further established with the resources to action complaints and feedback.
