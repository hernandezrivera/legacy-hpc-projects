Project Summary

The proposed intervention will address key activities with purpose to prevent and respond to Child protection in emergencies through reinforcing community based child protection mechanisms and systems, providing adequate and quality case management including improving information management system. Parents and care givers of children both boys and girls will be reinforced in positive parenting while foster families who provide temporary care to separated children will be provided with inputs such as cash for protection and response to basic survival needs.

Needs

By July 2018, a total of 249,813 individuals were displaced in Diffa region– 118,868 refugees, 104,288 internally displaced persons, 25,731 returnees and 940 asylum seekers, due to insecurity, disaster and economic migration, all in need of humanitarian assistance. The precariousness of the situation – resulting from the combined impact of poverty, conflict, and displacement, including the overpopulation in camps/sites and host communities, contributed to the tripling of children protection needs between 2017 and 2018. A recent Plan International study, "Adolescent Girls in Crisis: Voices from the Lake Chad Basin", found that almost of a quarter of the girls aged 10-19 surveyed reported having been physically assaulted, and 8% sexually harassed, in the last month, including in their homes and in particular when not living with their parents. The presence of security forces was also a factor increasing adolescent girls' feeling of insecurity and mobility. Adolescent boys in Diffa also reported limiting their movements due to fear of violence, kidnapping and forced recruitment to armed groups. As a result of the ongoing sense of insecurity families increasingly resort to negative coping mechanisms, as illustrated by the increasing rate of child marriage in Diffa reaching up to 89%, above the already staggering 75% country average. Finally, girls demonstrated agency, a desire to help others in their community and great entrepreneurial skills, all of which can help build their own capacity and that of their communities. Vocational training was found to significantly improve well-being and optimism among girls.
