Project Summary

To save lives and protect the safety and dignity of 50,000 vulnerable persons affected by flooding or conflict in Pibor, Akobo, and Ayod Counties, CRS will provide in-kind and/or cash-based assistance to ensure access to life-saving NFIs and emergency shelter.
CRS will target assistance towards assessed acute humanitarian needs in communities affected by shocks especially conflict or flooding, where women, children, persons with disabilities and others with unique vulnerabilities face extremely elevate risks of malnutrition, disease, hunger, and rights violations including sexual violence. CRS will prioritize displaced persons but will also consider inclusion of host community an newly arrived returnees based on demonstrated needs in the local context. CRS will adapt SNFI cluster vulnerability criteria to select beneficiaries within targeted communities; where applicable CRS will differentiate the assistance package based on the assessed need of each household.
CRS will prioritize the use of cash-based, voucher-based, or hybrid modalities to deliver NFIs and Shelter materials, especially local shelter materials if rapid assessment demonstrates that markets are functional and accessible, and cash is feasible/preferred option. Where feasible, cash will be more efficient and cost effective, and will support local markets while promoting early recovery and contributing to building resilience among beneficiary households and communities. CRS will work to increase community capacity to implement Disaster Risk Reduction approaches, and will apply a conflict sensitivity framework to ensure the project does no harm and reinforces efforts to build peace and resilience.
While assistance will be tailored to context and need, CRS anticipates primary intervention will focus on delivering through either cash or in-kind modality: 1) Standard or light Emergency Shelter Kits 2) Standard lifesaving NFI kits for newly displaced, 3) HH NFI replacement Kits for protracted displacements 4) shelter upgrade with local materials for prot. displ.
CRS will leverage the SNFI Core Pipeline for in-kind materials, but in case of challenges with this pipeline, CRS is well prepared to procure and deliver to all target locations as needed.
CRS will implement a comprehensive community engagement strategy, centered around both active and passive feedback and community response mechanisms (FCRMs) that are customized to the needs and preferences of diverse groups within affected communities to ensure strong accountability to affected populations (AAP). CRS’ community engagement approaches will be integrated with a flexible and robust monitoring and evaluation system, which includes strong post-distribution monitoring (PDMs) following distributions. The project will apply a conflict sensitive, gender-responsive “Do No Harm” approach, considering the unique vulnerabilities and capacities of men, women, boys, girls, and PWD. CRS will ensure representation of youth, women and other vulnerable and marginalized groups in community leadership structures supported by the projects.
CRS has worked in greater Jonglei for over 10 years, implementing shelter and NFI assistance, food assistance, livelihoods, nutrition, health, social cohesion, DRR, and conflict mitigation. CRS recently implemented BHA, EU, and WFP programming in these counties and anticipates implementing the WFP FLAs for Ayod and Pibor. CRS has established field bases in these counties, strong acceptance, and a stellar reputation with local communities and stakeholders; CRS will leverage our local experience, reach, and expertise in this challenging context. CRS maintains robust systems to ensure accountability and program quality. CRS' established supply chain and logistics capacity, including warehouses at field locations, will ensure CRS can timely purchase and deliver required supplies and equipment. Staff, office space, and vehicles will be shared across programs to maximize cost effectiveness.

1. Needs

The humanitarian situation in Ayod, Akobo, and the Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA) remains severe, with food security crisis and emergency conditions driven by large numbers of IDPs and returnees, persistent inter-communal conflict, macro-economic challenges depleting purchasing power, four consecutive years of flooding, and limited agricultural production. Families affected by this emergency are in many cases completely dependent on humanitarian food assistance. Vulnerable populations, especially those that have been displaced or are isolated by floodwaters and destroyed roads, face not only hunger but also dramatically elevated risks of malnutrition and communicable diseases; children, vulnerable adults such as pregnant or lactating women, and persons with disabilities are disproportionately affected. Ayod, Akobo, and Pibor counties are historically under-developed, isolated, and seriously affected by insecurity and intercommunal conflict. Jonglei State and Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA) are among the most food insecure areas of South Sudan: approximately 80% of the population currently faces food security crisis or emergency (ICP3+), indicative of the extreme vulnerability and high level of humanitarian need in this area. IPC Projections for lean season 2024 (April-June 2024) indicate that Ayod, Akobo, and Pibor counties will experience emergency food security (IPC4) conditions. (IPC predictions released 6 Nov 2023). Given the high likelihood of conflict, flooding, or drought to impact food security in 2024, the severe degradation of livelihoods due to recurrent shocks over multiple years, and the poor macro-economic conditions (including a steady trend of inflation of food prices and devaluation of the South Sudanese Pound), there is every reason to anticipate food security conditions will represent a severe emergency again in 2024. The target counties face humanitarian needs driven by recurrent and overlapping sub-national violent conflict and climate-related shocks, including extreme flooding. Areas of Akobo, Ayod and Pibor have been severely affected for four consecutive years, during which large areas of their territory were inundated, destroying community and household assets, and cutting off some areas from all over-land and air access and destroyed including homes, water points, farmland, food stores, seeds and tools, livestock, schools, and health facilities. Rainfall in the Great Lakes Basin has been average through the first part of 2023, but a developing El Nino condition is likely to drive above average rainfall in the last quarter of 2023, and has the potential to drive rapid expansion of flood impact in South Sudan. South Sudan has experienced three civil wars and decades of multi-level, violent conflict between the government and its opposition as well as sub-national anti-government insurgencies, inter-communal conflict over resources, and rivalries among ethnic groups. Routine revenge killings and cattle raiding displaces communities, forcing them to concentrate in smaller locations with limited access to clean water and power, hygienic sanitation facilities, etc. Per IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix Round 14 – 2023, Akobo East and West were hosting 17,338 IDPs, and 14,492 returnees; Pibor was hosting 43,847 IDPs and 31,355 returnees; and Ayod County was hosting 16,116 IDPs, and 31,106 returnees, primarily integrated with host communities, CRS field teams collaborating with RRC officials report that in addition to these numbers, at least 7,000 additional returnees have arrived due to the Sudan Crisis in Ayod, and at least 10,000 in Akobo due to Sudan crisis or conflict in neighboring Gambella region of Ethiopia, many of whom are staying in Akobo town or nearby. Widespread need for assistance will remain for the foreseeable future, and it is essential to sustain or scale-up humanitarian assistance to save lives, alleviate suffering, and ensure protection and dignity.

2. Project activities

To save lives and protect the safety and dignity of 50,000 vulnerable persons affected by flooding or conflict in Pibor, Akobo, and Ayod Counties, CRS will provide in-kind and/or CVA to ensure access to life-saving NFIs and emergency shelter. Priority Activities will include: 1) Needs Assessment, prioritizing cluster-endorsed tools and coordinated assessment with RRC and other local actors. Applying participatory methodology as feasible. 2) Market assessment, cash feasibility assessment, and rapid response analysis as needed – preferably in coordination with other actors. 3) Community Engagement and consultation process, 4) Establishment of Feedback and Community Response Mechanisms with community input 5) Community based targeting and registration process 6) Dist. of Standard Emergency Shelter Kits 7) Distribution of Standard lifesaving NFI kits for newly displaced persons; 8) Distribution of HH NFI replacement Kits and shelter upgrade kits promoting local material for protracted displacements. 9)Sensitization on proper use of NFIs, shelter materials, to include protection, hygiene and other key messages 10) Training for community members/leaders on DRR11) Post-Distribution Monitoring Targeting is explained in project summary section. CRS will prioritize the use of cash-based, voucher-based, or hybrid modalities to deliver NFIs and Shelter materials where rapid assessment demonstrates that markets are functional and accessible, and that cash is a feasible and preferred local option. Where feasible, cash will be more efficient and cost effective, and will support local markets while promoting early recovery and contributing to building resilience among beneficiary households and communities. In addition, CRS will work to increase community knowledge and capacity to implement Disaster Risk Reduction approaches, and will apply a conflict sensitivity framework to ensure the project does no harm and reinforces efforts to build peace and resilience. CRS will leverage the SNFI Core Pipeline for in-kind materials, but in case of challenges with this pipeline, CRS is well prepared to procure and deliver to all target locations as needed.The project will adhere to a “Do No Harm” approach while considering the unique vulnerabilities and capacities of men, women, boys, and girls.: CRS humanitarian programming centralizes protection and conflict sensitivity, while integrating robust PSEA measures. CRS will ensure this project promotes the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and community members through the following measures: 1) CRS consults men, women, boys and girls from diverse groups and incorporates risk assessment which applies gender and conflict sensitivity lenses into project design and implementation, including considering risks around GBV and intra-household conflict/violence that may be elevated by project activities. 2) Regular monitoring activities actively gather information about the experience of project participants and other community members during programming, providing the project team with data which is triangulated with field monitoring visits and feedback received through CFRMs, to understand any unintended consequences of the intervention and adapt programming accordingly. 3) CRS interventions commonly target women per vulnerability criteria, and in this case provide services, interventions, and assets that are culturally appropriate for women and girls to utilize, own or govern. 4) All CRS staff as well as local authorities and community leaders are trained on reporting and referral systems in place for GBV, alongside the code of conduct, the free assistance they should expect, the targeting/inclusion method. 5) Service points are located to ensure vulnerable beneficiaries can access them with safety and dignity, and without traveling long distances. 6) Beneficiaries and other community members are sensitized, particularly during distributions, on the available CFRMs and how to report any concerns.
