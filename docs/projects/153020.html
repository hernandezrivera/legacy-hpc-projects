Project Summary

Insecurity and an uptick in clashes between warring factions in Leer, Mayendit and Koch Counties of Unity and Pibor County of Jonglei continue to impede humanitarian efforts, compounded by accessibility constraints, bureaucratic impediments, and cyclical waves of violent cattle raiding incidents. Recent skirmishes in this region started in Leer in April 2018, predominantly perceived as revenge attacks for monetary gains via looting of civilian properties and assets, including recent cattle related fighting and inter-communal violence in Pibor (Humanitarian Bulletins, 2018; DRC May 2018 Leer Rapid Assessment Snapshot).
These have subsequently led to a significant number of grave human rights violations including extrajudicial execution, abduction, arbitrary arrest, detention, gender based violence and abuse including gang rapes, forced recruitments of children, separation of children for families/ caregivers, exposure to mines and UXOs (Protection Trends, 2018).
VSF Suisse is currently on ground in the targeted locations and recently signed a one year UNICEF Child Protection grant that covers Pibor, Panyijar, Mayendit and Leer. Proposed action will sustain and scale up child protection responses in 2019 to address identified child protection needs.
Proposed action will target 36000 people, made up of 30,000 children (15000 girls and 15000 boys)/ youth (male and female) and 6,000 adults (3000 men and 3000 women. These includes elderly, people with special needs from IDP/ Host/ returnees populations in Pibor, Panyijar, Leer, Mayendit and Koch Counties.
Proposed activities are in line with the Child Protection 2019 Humanitarian Response Plan protection/child protection whereby child protection will continue to employ the full engagement of existing community-based child protection networks. Targeted locations have been prioritized with a significant populations facing various protection concerns including grave violations of human rights. VSF Suisse aims to sustain momentum of child protection activities through sustainable community based approaches, inter-cluster synergies – Education, Nutrition, Health, WASH etc, quality programming including mainstreaming Accountability to Affected Populations, regular protection assessments and analysis, Do No Harm approaches. In addition, VSF Suisse will coordinate with counterpart agencies operating in the proposed locations like UNIDOR, Nile Hope, DRC among others including linkages with state and national level coordination platforms to avoid duplication and form synergies with other clusters.

Needs

Insecurity and an uptick in clashes between warring factions in Leer, Mayendit and Koch Counties of Unity and Pibor County of Jonglei continue to impede humanitarian efforts, compounded by accessibility constraints, bureaucratic impediments, and cyclical waves of violent cattle raiding incidents. Recent skirmishes in this region started in Leer in April 2018, predominantly perceived as revenge attacks for monetary gains via looting of civilian properties and assets, including recent cattle related fighting and inter-communal violence in Pibor (Humanitarian Bulletins, 2018; DRC May 2018 Leer Rapid Assessment Snapshot). These have subsequently led to a significant number of grave human rights violations including extrajudicial execution, abduction, arbitrary arrest, detention, gender based violence and abuse including gang rapes, forced recruitments, separation of children for families/ caregivers, exposure to mines and UXOs. Consequently, at least 41,000 people were displaced from locations northern Mayendit while clashes in Leer saw at least 8,000 people displaced from Gandor, Mirimiri, Rubuaoy, Payak, Piling, Thonyor, Ruopnor, Kaidai, and Touchriak into Leer Town, with internally displaced populations seeking shelter at Leer Temporary Operating Base. This is in addition an estimate of 60% of the affected girls, boys, men and women - including people with special needs who were displaced to Mayendit, Mier, and Koch (Protection Trends, 2018; Humanitarian Bulletins, 2018; DRC May 2018 Leer Rapid Assessment Snapshot). In addition, recent cattle related fighting in Boma led to looting and destruction of property including child abductions. The conflict has taken a heavy toll on women, girls, boys and men who have been forcefully recruited into armed groups and forces, including abduction of women and girls serving as wives/cooks in the armed forces/groups and a key concern among boys/ youth and men. These have been compounded by unprecedented levels of food insecurity with at least 5.2 million people projected facing severe food insecurity in the first quarter of 2019; with Pibor, Leer and Mayendit projected to be in IPC Phase 5 (IPC September, 2018; FEWSNET-SS Food Security Outlook, 2018). Affected girls, boys, men and women have witnessed various atrocities and at risk of post-traumatic stress disorder, depression and anxiety disorders as well as psychosomatic problems, which often lead to suicides if unattended. This calls for immediate efforts to respond to the situation, including strengthened psychosocial support and community sensitization including longer-term strategy to address root causes. 2017 recorded a 34% increase in the number of children affected by the incidents of grave violations of their rights, including attacks and occupation of schools by armed actors. This is in addition to 8 % increase in number of children recruited into armed groups as at end of 2017, compared to 2016. Unprecedented levels of food insecurity in the three counties have negatively led to women and girls at higher risk of sexual and gender based violence including survival sex to access food and protection (Protection Trends, 2018). 2018 Protection Trends indicate continued fighting has subsequently led to breaking existing social bonds and community structures, separation of minors (girls and boys) from parents, killings, increase in gender based violence and abuse, mental health and increased psychological distress among men, women, girls and boys and further distorted community child protection/ social structures.

Activities

1. Provision of Individual / group counselling/psycho-social support (PSS) & referrals among conflict affected 10000 boys and 10000 girls 2. Monthly protection monitoring, assessment, information sharing / reporting, awareness raising & mainstreaming in the targeted counties 3. Conduct PSS/CBPSS/Comprehensive Case Management among targeted 15000 boys and 15000 girls with protection concerns4. Conduct awareness of available services and referrals among counterpart agencies targeting 15000 boys, 15000 girls, 3000 men and 3000 women5. Assess, identify and establish women and girls friendly space & child friendly spaces within the vicinity of schools/ temporary learning spaces (TLS), cattle camps, temporary IDP Protection bases and displacement villages and negotiate access for boys and girls for their recreational activities in all the targeted counties.6. Conduct mapping and establishment of functional / existing referral mechanisms among various multi-sectoral platforms7. Train community based child protection networks, local administration and volunteers to prevent and respond to recruitment of children is enhanced through training on case management, referral pathways and linkages with child protection service delivery systems 8. Identify, verify and register in collaboration with UNICEF at least 200 CAFAAGs anticipated to be released/ released in mop up exercise in Pibor and Unity and provide them with initial socio-economic material/ livelihood support and reintegration support. 9. Support 800 CAFAAGs / MVCs and their caregivers with appropriate livelihood kits (crops, fishing kits and vegetable kits) to enhance their own food production and incomes from sale of agricultural products and fishing resources10. Identify, register and enroll 400 CAFAAGs/ MVCs in appropriate vocational skills training programs to acquire relevant skills for improved access to income generating activities and employment opportunities

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

VSF Suisse will adopt a consultative project implementation approach; by information sharing and including equitable representation of male and female, youth, boys and girls, women and men (including disabled and elderly); local community leaders, youth and women representatives among other relevant authorities in decision making as well and also ensuring their full participationin project planning, selection, verification, implementation, monitoring/evaluation. VSF Suisse field teams will also conduct weekly/monthly project management meetings to review and consider anyissues emerging that may impact on the project implementation, in addition to mainstreaming of cross cutting issues such as gender, protection, HIV/AIDS and accountability etc.VSF Suisse will in addition encourage paricipation and representation - addressing needs diversity and understanding of the local context, transparent targeting criteria, collection of sex and gender dis-aggregated data, establishment of AAP spot checks by staff/ senior management and information provision on PSEA.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Context specific messages in relation to targeted areas and gender categories will be done. Men, women, girls and boys will be involved and messages disseminated separately whilst ensuring cultural considerations are taken into account. Elderly, young, youth and people with special needs will as well be prioritized and each category have its specific tailored interventions and messaging.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project take into consideration protection from sexual exploitation and abuse which is enshrined in the organization’s code of conduct that addresses prevention of: sexual exploitation and abuse by staff; sexual activities by staff with children (under 18 years); staff exchange of money, goods and/ or services for sexual favours; sexual relationships by staff with beneficiaries; exploitationof fellow workers within the organization or counterpart agencies; and maintenance of an environment that strictly prevents sexual exploitation and abuse. Any violation of the above is regarded as gross misconduct which appropriate actions taken against the perpetrators.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

VSF Suisse will ensure effective and rapid implementation of the project directly in close consultations, collaboration and involvement of beneficiary communities and counterparts and in partnership with key relevant stakeholder and partners such as Child Protection Subcluster, UNICEF, line ministries and will hold consultative meetings with community leaders, local authorities including the state/county ministries, UNICEF State leads, Cluster leads as well as community members to introduce the project, discuss and agree on roles and responsibilities; includingbeneficiary selection criteria, implementation as well as coordination modalities. This will be in addition to ensuring coordinated response with counterpart agencies implementing Nutrition, Health and WASH programming to ensure a holistic approach and achieve better impacts of the project. As a principle, VSF Suisse will consult and ensure active participation of men,women, boys and girls including youths, elderly and disabled will be encouraged to ensure that interests of various beneficiary groups are taken into account during planning and project implementation and monitoring.

Communities will be engaged and involved in implementation, across all stages of the project. Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

VSF Suisse will conduct routine gender and protection risk assessments and risk monitoring to understand and adapt to the evolving protection concerns during implementation. This is in addition to the project prioritizing beneficiary safety and dignity of girls, boys, men and women in consideration with the principles of ‘Do no harm’; nondiscrimination, equitable, gender segregated and meaningful access to proposed services/facilities and cater for specific needs of girls, women, youth and men including the elderly and disabled. Communities will also be engaged among other relevant stakeholders, including awareness and training on basic human rights while implementing various activities. For instance, the primary beneficiaries for this project are identified as host and IDP communities - men, women, boys and girls including elderly and people with special needs. This is designed to deliver services in a non-discriminatory manner for all those who are most vulnerable, and to ensure any tensions between host communities and IDPs over access to facilities and services is avoided.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

VSF Suisse does regular context analysis and protection assessment including evaluations on possible impediments and beneficiary access constraints. Ways of mitigating these including advocacy with key stakeholders is done to ensure unimpeded access to targeted beneficiaries. In addition, VSF Suisse has been operating in the proposed areas for over 20 years with established local networks. This is in addition to the organization having locally recruited staff, having good rapport and working relations with local state ministries, UNICEF etc., that would be rapidly engaged, mobilized and facilitate access the proposed areas. Close collaboration and coordination with other counterpart agencies such as VSF Germany, ICRC, UNIDO, Nile Hope, UNICEF will be involved to fast and efficientaccess to most vulnerable beneficiaries. VSF Suisse will also liaise very closely with UNMISS regular patrols to access hard to reach areas and will be in a position to secure space for persons to deploy or for supplies to be delivered, especially in the dry season.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

VSF Suisse through consultations, context analysis, regular protection assessments, coordination and participation of key stakeholders in implementation will ensure the project addresses inequalities and protection threats. VSF Suisse will in addition ensure and advocate for equittable targetingand participation of all targeted groups whilst ensuring key threats such as violence is mitigating through peaceful messaging and ensuring information on support packages is shared to mitigate exploitation. Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Activities will be implemented during safe periods and early hours of the day. Centralized implementation sites including distribution centres will be established to avoid the risk of beneficiaries walking for long distances to access services, further exposing them to harm. Inequalities such as people with special needs including elderly will as well be prioritized with services provided at their residences including prioritization and involvement. Marginalized groups will as well be prioritized. Child/women/widowed/elderly/disabled headed and those with pregnant and lactating mothers will be prioritized and identified as primary beneficiaries toensure their dignity and safety is guaranteed.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project will ensure measurement of access to beneficiaries during the initial preliminary assessments, planning and consultative sessions with beneficiary representatives among other key stakeholders. This will be based on time and distance taken to access the services/ support packages whilst taking into account the various vulnerabilities including access constraints, with meaningful mitigation measures put in place to ensure unhindered and easily access to the services/ support.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

The project will carry out regular risk and protection assessments to take into account potential hazards and protection concerns which are likely to be faced by beneficiaries. Considerations on gender, specifically women/ girls at risk of sexual and gender based violence, elderly and children who may be at risk of violence and abduction (children) including recruitment of children into armedforces/ groups. This will be done in consultations with key focal points including local leadership and line ministries and formulate mitigation measures. Focused group discussions, including observations, interviews and consultations will be used to assess potential threats and mitigation measures put in place, which will be regularly shared with key stakeholders.

Yes

Interviews and consultations with beneficiaries including local leadership and line ministries and counterpart agencies will be done including analysis of results and corrective measures and recommendations done. The project will aim at prioritizing beneficiary safety and dignity in consideration with the principles of ‘Do no harm’; non-discrimination, equitable, gender segregated and meaningful access to proposed services/facilities and cater for specific needs of girls, women, youth and men including the elderly and disabled.
