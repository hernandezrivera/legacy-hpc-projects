Project Summary

PUI’s FOCOS strategy programming continues to be undertaken in a multi-year strategic way, with 2020 representing the sixth year of implementation of PUI’s integrated-area based approach (IABA) in Syria. Through this approach, PUI make sures to create synergies between the different interventions implemented in areas in need in order to respond to the needs of the Population of Syria as a whole and to help them to go back to their pre-crisis standards of living, to enhance self-sufficiency and resilience in the long run and to promote social cohesion.
In line with PUI’s I4SC approach (initial standards, self-sufficiency and social cohesion) and the Early Recovery and Livelihoods sector’s first objective “Enhance access to market-based livelihood opportunities and production”, through its livelihood response, PUI aims to support vulnerable conflict affected populations toward achieving self sufficiency. The project will be focused on developing and enhancing income generating capacities through support to small business projects (SBP), vocational training, distribution of Professional Tool Kits (PTK) and Agricultural Tool Kits (ATK). PUI activities will cover various neighborhoods in six governorates across Syria (Rural Damascus, Deir-Ez-Zor, Hama, Aleppo, Dar’a, Lattakia).

4 - Needs

The decade-long conflict hardly hit the Syrian socio – economic fabric, leaving about 12.6 million as per the draft Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for 2021, in need of recovery and livelihood support due to degraded livelihoods and income earning opportunities, intense hostilities and the damages of key infrastructures, including productive infrastructures, which overall deepened the poverty trap and prevented the Syrian population from accessing basic services. Combined with the unprecedented economic crisis and the devaluation of Syrian pound, the imposition of new and further-reaching sanction regulations, shortage of fuel and electricity and the Covid-19 pandemic, the effects of the crisis pushed a significant proportion of the Syrian population to insecurity. However, the negative effects of the crisis were different for age and gender groups; adolescents and youth were the most affected, as exposure to/or witnessing violence negatively affected their psychological health, interrupted their educational process & prevented them from developing their mental health, which altogether might prevent them from building their capacities and consequently reduce their chances to access the labor market and prevent them from being active citizens in the future. Moreover, social norms and customs have the largest influence on females’ lives in the Syrian society. Although social norms vary according to religion, ethnicity, and between rural and urban areas, the general nature of these norms often put females in second place and give most of the privileges to men. With the arise of the Syrian crisis, women were forced to play multiple roles which placed further pressure on them and forced them to cover for the men’s absence.

5 - Implementation of activities

PUI will contribute to Sector Objective1 (SO1) through the implementation of the following activities: 1.3. Provide vocational and skills training opportunities by conducting vocational training courses targeting IDPs, host communities, and returnee populations to enable the establishment of new livelihood activities for the targeted populations by building new technical skills. 1.4. Provide support to entrepreneurship by supporting Small Business Projects (SBP) and distributing Professional Tool Kits (PTK) targeting IDPs, host communities, and returnee populations to enable the restoration of livelihood activities for the targeted populations, and contribute to rebuilding markets and communities. Advertisement: PUI will advertise for this program as broadly as possible at community level in coordination with local stakeholders.Pre-Selection Phase: Interested individuals will register by providing an application detailing their work experience and profile. Applications will be filtered based on PUI eligibility criteria. Training: A training will be provided to accepted applicants – a business management training (BMT) for individuals applying for a small business grant and a business skills training (BST) for individuals applying to receive a PTK. Final Selection Phase: will be based on the results of the feasibility study. Procurement phase as per beneficiaries needs.Follow-up phase: For the following 3-6 months, PUI staff will follow-up with beneficiaries monitor the development of their business and the evolution of their income generation capacities. 1.5. Provide support to rural enterprises and entrepreneurs through the distribution of Agricultural Tool Kits (ATK): same as PTK objective and process.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:1) No curfew or lockdown due to COVID-19 pandemic2) Stable security situation 3) Consistent humanitarian access 4) No major fluctuation of Syrian Pound5) No mass movements of populations 6) Availability of material in the local market7) Acceptance of community Risks:1) COVID-19 new waves and lockdown or curfew from the government > Mitigation measures: 1- Good coordination with the concerned stakeholders to anticipate such measures. 2- Staff, partners and contractors’ employees to use masks and apply all preventive measures to reduce the likelihood of spreading the infection.2) Variations in exchange rate impact local procurement > Mitigation measures: PUI times procurements in line with overall exchange rate trends, and will delay or re-schedule purchases as needed; exchange rate terms are also elaborated in contracts with suppliers.3) Beneficiaries awarded for the SBPs are unable to obtain necessary authorisations to establish businesses> Mitigation measures: This process will be launched prior to the procurement of productive assets. PUI will connect beneficiaries to local community centres in case they need for additional support with this process.4) Productive assets procured through the project are sold on local market by beneficiaries > Mitigation measures: Productive assets' beneficiaries will be sensitized on the process of the activity prior to the distribution of assets. Further, PUI staff will conduct frequent monitoring to ensure the proper usage of items. Further, the beneficiaries will be required to contribute to the activity by paying for administrative fees required for setting up of the business location, reducing the risk of misuse of the assets procured.
