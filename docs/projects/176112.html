Project Summary

This project aims to provide protection, assistance and solutions to 13,750(8300 female and 5450 male) Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and host communities in Pibor, Nyirol, Ayod and Tonj North Counties in South Sudan. Specifically, the project will provide protection services and strengthen community resilience to Persons of Concern (POCs), through the provision of protection services, CVA, shelter assistance and distribution of CRIs among the affected communities. HRSS is currently providing protection assistance to persons with specific needs and monitoring the movements of Spontaneous refugee Returnees in Leer, Mayendit and Panyijiar Counties and currently has presence in Ayod, Pibor, Nyirol implementing the CCCM and women economic empowerment activities and will expand its interventions to Tonj North. HRSS will also mainstream GBV and offer camp coordination and camp management activities by engaging with persons with specific needs (PSNs), Community sensitization and capacity building on conflict resolution mechanisms/ techniques, referring PSN cases and high- risk clients to other services through the referral pathways. Direct service provision will be conducted through mobile and static teams throughout Tonj North, Pibor, Nyirol and Ayod Counties. HRSS will continue to prioritize and respond to highly sensitive protection issues including forced recruitment into armed groups and detention, and coerced sexual exploitation. In addition to the efforts to increase documentation. This will be aimed at supporting the establishment of effective partnerships and frameworks for actions for responding to challenges of protection and finding solutions for persons internally displaced due to conflict and abuses of human rights and bridging the gap between relief and development. This project will support and continue to coordinate with local authorities and lead efforts to provide protection and solutions for persons internally displaced due to conflict and abuse of human rights as part of the collaborative response to IDPs in South Sudan.

1 - Needs

An estimated 4.77 million women, girls, men and boys will face protection risks and violations in 2021. In addition, 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers in South Sudan are in need of protection services according HNO 2021.Insecurity and violence have disproportionately affected vulnerable people including children, older persons, survivors of gender-based violence and conflict-related sexual violence, and people living with disabilities. Coupled with natural disasters and the very limited capacity of the government to respond, this has contributed to a combination of protracted and cyclical IDP situations with ever-shifting patterns despite recent positive developments on the implementation of the Revitalize Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in the Republic of South Sudan (R-ARCSS), violence and insecurity remain a persistent reality. Sub-national organized violence has increased during the first six months of 2020, Most notably in Lakes, Warrap and greater Jonglei state. According to IOM -DTM round 9, Published: 1 December 2020, 1.6 million people were displace including in ( Ayod-22469, Nyirol-28 671, Tonj North 87394 and Pibor 19949 ). Competition over already scarce resources has caused tension between displaced persons and host communities. Maintaining harmonious co-existence between the two groups is a necessity. Maintaining the civilian and humanitarian character of asylum requires constant engagement acknowledging the presence of combatants in/around sites/camps which also leads to increased tensions and exacerbates existing vulnerabilities of refugee populations, exposing them, particularly women and children, to increased levels of violence and insecurity. Children (0 to 17 years old) constitute 61 per cent of the refugee population. They continue to face protection risks including neglect, abuse, exploitation, child labour, forced recruitment, SGBV, and lack of access to services. Protection concerns are especially high for girls risks for early marriage, early pregnancies, and denial of education particularly for girls and children living with disabilities. Decades of conflict, poverty and poor access to services made people with disabilities (PWD) particularly fragile. PWD are often stigmatized, marginalized, excluded and left unable to access the support needed to live in dignity as equal members of society. Protection was the sixth commonly cited form of priority assistance needed for men, women and children, as reported by three to eight per cent of assessed settlements across South Sudan in October 2020. Insecurity was also cited as a key challenge in both male and female FGDs held in October 2020.

2 - Project activities

This proposed project is in line with the strategic objective of the protection cluster of ensuring provision of critical protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas and enable durable solutions for IDPs and other population. HRSS will carry out Preliminary meetings in each target county and will be held with Community Leaders and members, Local authority, and representatives from NGOs implementing projects across the proposed Counties. HRSS will continue to enhance protection assessments and monitoring to inform protection and overall humanitarian response, mobile, static protection assessment and monitoring, targeting 24 assessments in the areas of potential return/relocation/integration and preparation of the respective reports and will collect and analyze information on communities’ intentions and needs to inform solutions interventions, 36 monthly mobile and static protection monitoring and provide IPA to monitor spontaneous IDP returnees and cross border movements, Provision of return/relocation/integration assistance, as appropriate and based on the needs of identified beneficiaries Carry out of the data collection and return monitoring at the border crossing points and in return areas to inform protection and assistance response. 240 survivors of gender-based violence will have access to psychosocial support in the form of group support, group counseling, and recreational activities. HRSS will identify cases, provide psycho-socio support sessions, and refer GBV cases in a number of locations in which there are no GBV services Protection awareness raising and prevention messaging, HRSS will on a weekly and monthly basis continue to respond to persistent indicators of sexual assault and violence carried out with the knowledge of stakeholders against women and girls. Under Peace building and peaceful co-existence, HRSS will on organize 194 neighborhood assemblies including one male and one female neighborhood assembly each consisting of 14 participants monthly. Conduct 36 conflict Resolution workshops to enable the engagement between community leaders, women and youth, and religious groups to develop the skills to prevent, manage, and resolve conflictsHRSS through experienced and practicing GBV lawyers will facilitate monthly legal aid clinic at target locations to provide information on avenues GBV survivors can safely pursue to get justice. With support of trained case workers, HRSS will continue to provide case management services to 20 beneficiaries. HRSS will carry out cash based interventions and cash voucher assistance to 10% of the total target beneficiaries.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

HRSS has designated trained PSEA community focal points in each location to disseminated information about PSEA. The project also incorporates PSEA in all its activities including trainings and awareness raising in to the protection activities. HRSS will use Community-Based Complaints Mechanism (CBCM) approach which is a system blending both formal and informal community structures, where individuals will be able and encouraged to safely report incidents of SEA. Local communities will be involved in developing and approving the CBCM so that the structure is both culturally and gender-sensitive. The mechanism should have multiple entry points, allowing both beneficiaries and staff the opportunity to report at the organizational level – internally through the network’s and field agency focal points at the community level. The primary Concern of the mechanism is to aid known and potential SEA survivors, and also fulfill a prevention function through awareness-raising efforts. A PSEA CBCM will not be a separate, parallel system to other complaints and feedback structures but rather link to and build on existing structures to create one system for handling feedback and complaints. HRSS will also ensure that a person who receives assistance as part of either emergency relief or development aid through assistance programs. Persons under this title include members of affected populations including refugees, internally displaced persons and other vulnerable individuals, as well as host community members

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

Access to justice for GBV survivors is a vital part of HRSS’s mandate to promote and protect children’s and women’s rights. However, access to justice for GBV survivors in Tonj North, Nyirol, Ayod and Pibor Counties is challenging, the problem is compounded by breakdown in the rule of law and disruption of police, legal aid and court services. In addition, when law enforcement, legal and judicial personnel do not follow survivor-centered principles, legal processes can be disempowering, humiliating, traumatic and harmful to the recovery and psychosocial well-being of GBV survivors. HRSS will Provide technical support for establishment or improvement of inter-agency referral system to link survivors with additional MHPSS providers as well as health, security (i.e. police) and legal support and include provisions for safe and ethical data collection, management and sharing as well as strategies that address barriers to accessing support. HRSS will support local authorities to review legislation, policies and administrative procedures that may cause barriers to survivors to access forensic examinations, police response and legal support and work with government and other national partners to revise them to ensure they follow international standards for survivor-centered care and support Where safe access is not an issue, HRSS will promote access to information for communities, survivors and families regarding police support, legal rights and remedies within different justice systems. HRSS will also facilitate 12 monthly confidential and safe access for survivors to police, forensic medical services, legal aid and access to judicial systems (including transport and accompaniment to court). HRSS will also undertake 12 advocacy public campaigns on access to survivor-centered justice (including confidentiality, safety, self-determination, non-discrimination and best interests of the child).
