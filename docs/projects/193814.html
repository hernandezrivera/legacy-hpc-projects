Project Summary

The proposed project has been designed in line with HRP strategic objectives 2 “The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under International Humanitarian Law” and it will contribute to the Shelter/NFIs Cluster Objective 2 “Vulnerable households exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns are supported to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacity”. Specifically, the project aims to contribute to the protection of the acutely vulnerable people and conflict-affected households in the Gaza Strip through creating safe, adequate, and healthy living environment for 133 HHs in southern governorate who are living in substandard houses and lack basic shelter infrastructure and services (amounting to approximately 777 individuals, including 200 women, 208 men, 163 girls and 170 boys; among them are 22 elderly and 14 PwDs). The FHHs, PwDs and elderly headed HHs will be prioritized, in addition to cases of GBV and protection concerns. Throughout the project, a range of urgent rehabilitation works will be conducted upon the actual needs and priorities of targeted HHs, and this will be complemented by the provision of essential NFIs. The works will include rehabilitating the damaged and/or substandard shelters to meet basic shelter needs and minimum standards, including works related to physical protection and overcrowding, maintenance of toilet and kitchen facilities, improving living areas and providing adequate and essential shelter NFIs and hygiene materials and adaptation for PWDs (if any). The project will use the self-help approach, utilizing family and community assets, which included volunteer labor, cash and discounted building supplies. The project will provide unconditional restricted cash assistance at an average of $3000/HH (Based on the findings of technical assessment of the needs at each Housing Unit, including NFIs). The targeted households will be oriented and mentored to prepare their individual rehabilitation plans, identifying the most priority + − Leaflet | © OpenStreetMap rehabilitation works such as roofs, windows, doors, fixing toilets and kitchens. Also, the households will purchase the needed shelter NFI packages including bedding items, and/or kitchen sets, as well as basic Hygiene items. The project will enhance beneficiaries’ resilience by enhancing their adaptive capacities to cope with the risks of deterioration of living conditions and will empower them through the transfer of skills and expertise on the use of cash. IRW’s solid experience in the sector will enable the project team to benefit from the lessons learnt, improving the project design and implementation modalities. The project will be implemented in partnership with local CBO of relevant and appropriate it will ensure strong cooperation and coordination with local authorities, shelter/NFIs cluster and other key stakeholders. Also, the project will ensure close consultation and regular communication with beneficiaries, ensuring their awareness of the complaint’s mechanisms. The project aims to support the achievement of HRP 2023 strategic objectives. Notably, the project includes a series of activities designed to address the urgent humanitarian and rehabilitation needs of the target households and communities, thus, strengthening their resilience and coping capacities and enhancing their protection. The project will promote and utilize the community-based approach, with greater involvement of beneficiaries at every stage, while putting specific focus on women, children and vulnerable groups, and a strong Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) component.

1 - Needs

Under humanitarian need, [Priority Need 2]: 610K, Vulnerable households living in substandard shelter exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns are supported to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacity (winterization, summarization, shelters upgrade and rehabilitation, damaged shelters repair). The housing sector, especially in Gaza, suffers from great challenges resulting from the protracted crisis, recurrent escalations and the frequent weather extremes, and leading to an increase in poverty and the inability of people to build new housing units or lack of affordability to rehabilitate their shelters. With a population density of 5,800 persons per km2, the Gaza strip is one of the world's most density populated areas, suffering from a chronic housing shortage, high costs/unavailability of construction material due to restrictions imposed by the Israeli blockade, and recurrent escalations of violence. Based on (HNO 2023) PIN with shelter and NFIs by Geography in Gaza strip 591,313 (89%) which (189K North Gaza, 176K Gaza, 74K Deir Al Balah, 71K Khan Yunis and 60k Rafah). Shelter in Gaza Strip is primarily affected by challenges like occupation, forced displacement, natural population growth, on-going conflict, restrictions and limits on movements of people and goods in and out of Gaza. This led to overcrowded and inadequate housing for many people in Gaza and impacted the ability of vulnerable households to afford safe and adequate housing. Most affected people are families living in poverty and exposed to protection threats due to living in substandard shelter conditions. House is where people spend most precious time in their lives, in which they gather with family, chill, develop skills, study, work, chat, and play. House condition always has directly or indirectly influence on the life of the household's members. So, overcrowding and poor housing conditions have a number of negative effects that impact on health and wellbeing, including increased physical and mental health issues, raised risk of domestic violence, educational attainment and the need for additional humanitarian assistance. Poor housing condition such as overcrowding, damp, bad WASH facilities, and cold is all associated with physical illnesses including eczema, hypothermia, and respiratory and heart disease in both adults and children. The outdoor factors that people in Gaza suffer from, in addition to the physical features of indoor factors (the house condition) can also impact on mental health as households try to cope with the stress of living in such conditions. Based on that, IRW endeavor to promote an adequate standard of living and create a safer environment to cope with surrounding threats for vulnerable households.

2 - Activities

IRW proposes this project with the aim to contribute to the protection of the most vulnerable people living in substandard shelters in Gaza Strip. It will meet an immediate need of the extremely vulnerable households living in substandard shelters (including women, children, elderly, PWDs), improving their access to safe, adequate, and healthy living environment through shelter solutions. Specifically, Islamic Relief will respond to the identified needs and priorities of households with the worst score of socioeconomic vulnerabilities who are in critical need for shelter support. This action will be fully in line with 2023 HRP strategic objectives 2and will contribute to the Shelter & NFI cluster objective 2, helping to cover unmet needs for the related response under the OCHA HNO 2023. The assistance will fully comply with the Shelter Cluster minimum agreed standards and will incorporate the principles of protection for vulnerable groups particularly women, children, elderly and people with disabilities. IRW will carry out the project activities, considering the active engagement of the targeted HHs in the design, planning, implementation and evaluation of activities. IRW plans to achieve the project’s results by delivering the following activities: (1) Conduct Beneficiaries Selection; IRW and local partner teams will finalize the selection of 133 housing units in southern governorate based on MSNA in close coordination with MoPWH database and shelter cluster. according to the results of the technical assessment visit which will be conducted by the project team using two rankings for shelter and households’ vulnerabilities, and putting specific focus on FHHs, PwDs and elderly headed HHs. The data from the assessment will be entered and analyzed in a scoring system, presenting the most 133 eligible households. The project team will specify the amounts to be allocated for each house according to the actual needs at an average of $3000/HH. The final list of beneficiaries will be shared/agreed in close cooperation with shelter cluster and line ministry (MoPWH) as well as peer organizations. (2) Conduct orientation and communication with beneficiaries: To discuss project details, selection criteria and scope, mechanism of self-help approach in rehabilitation, safety and security measures, two-way communication mechanisms with target group with safe access and smooth procedures, concise information/advice about COVID-19 and the most importance of pre-caution measures and induction about the feedback and complaint mechanisms. (3) Provide financial and technical support to 133 HHs to rehabilitate/adapt their substandard houses through self-help approach (restricted cash): An agreement will be signed with each beneficiary individually, clarifying their roles and obligations. The project team will agree with HHs on priorities of works and NFIs and provide technical assistance in terms of material, workmanship and alternatives. The agreement has all project details including Project scope, donors, bio data, details, responsibilities of both parties, details of financial details and instalments, and the BOQ of the intended repairs and rehabilitation works. Cash will be disbursed in multiple payments (Advance payment of 40%, 2nd payment of 40% after 70% progress and 20% after finalizing all agreed works) and they will be provided to HHs after reviewing and verifying all agreements, payment reconciliation and approving the technical reports by the engineer will be conducted to proceed with the next payments. Engineers will conduct regular field visit every week to follow up, monitor and provide technical assistance to ensure adherence to the minimum requirements. Also, they will be ready to follow up and give advice to all HHs on daily basis via other communication tools. The health promoters will conduct regular sessions with the family’s members for hygienic and public health awareness to influence behavior change. IRW will conduct MEAL activities for shelter rehabilitation works and the provision of NFIs by beneficiaries and finally conduct administrative and financial closure.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The intervention has been developed with comprehensive understanding of the protection principle, especially “Do No Harm”. The project design had considered protection risks analysis and the activities are screened not to increase risks for all especially women, girls and children. IRW will ensure that women, PwDs and elderlies are represented in to ensure that their rights, needs, and views are well taken into consideration. During the verification and final selection of the target groups, all project information will be shared with the target communities to inform them about the provided assistance, and the project will prioritize the HHs occupying the vulnerable individuals at risk of protection issues (including GBV and child protection issues). GBV and PSEA issues, will be the specific focus of capacity building for staff and partners to ensure that they are able to detect cases of abuse. The project includes effective use of referral pathways for specialist support, in areas related to genderbased violence (GBV), child protection and psychosocial support. The project staff and partners are required to sign the Code of Conduct which directly refers to the Protection of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and to Gender Based Violence (GBV). IRW will first do a conflict analysis, understanding the context where we will be intervening and identifying the relations between the different groups that will be targeted. IRW will act upon understanding these interactions in order to avoid negative impacts. IRW staff will perform interviews with beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries to ensure that tensions are not rising in any way as an outcome of the project. Also, local cultural practices and gender sensitivity will be respected at all times by the project team and partners during works implementation. Accountability to the affected population has been well mainstreamed in the project design, implementation, and monitoring. A set of mechanisms will be established to ensure accountable implementation of the project activities. Beneficiaries and stakeholders will have a voice in the project through complain mechanisms held in place. A satisfaction survey will be completed at the end of the intervention to assess the final valuation of the beneficiaries toward the project works. Regarding specific harm related to COVID-19, emphasis will be put on applying protective measures and behaviors to ensure the safety and protection of all people involved in the project (HHs, project team and laborers). Eventually, the project will increase proper access for safe and healthy shelter, and thus contribute to increase access to human right and ensure beneficiaries safety and dignity through improving their life conditions.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

IRPAL seeks to prevent discrimination and to ensure equal and impartial access though transparency and accountability to affected populations. The project directly improves access for people with specific vulnerabilities in terms of socioeconomic and protection to basic shelter needs. A participatory approach was used to identify the needs and requirement by including participants at all levels and in all phases, starting with the design phase (appropriateness analysis) followed by appropriateness and adaptation planning to ensure adapted assistance accessible to all people of all genders, ages and disabilities. Based on previous experience and primary assessment results to ensure equitable access of the marginalized groups. The beneficiaries' selection process is need-based and will consider transparent and accountable processes including the feedback and complaint mechanism. women headed households, PWDs and elderlies are disproportionately affected by poor housing conditions, and will be a prioritized during the selection of beneficiaries. The technical needs for selected shelters will be evaluated individually and customized to meet the needs of the groups in terms of access, safety, protection and privacy rather than a standardized implementation for all shelters. In cases where HHs are unable to effectively conduct the shelter rehabilitation, IRW technical teams will directly implement the rehabilitation work for disadvantaged households. Further, the implementation team will ensure all planned rehabilitation work by the beneficiaries through Self-help approach, and the targeted HHs will receive a customized training on the use and management of cash. Safe access of groups at-risk of COVID-19 complications will be ensured through considering the precaution safety measures and protective tools in all sites. The NFIs and hygienic materials will be distributed to HHs meetings this criteria based on the rapid technical assessment, and the awareness sessions will promote equal knowledge and participation between females and male of good hygiene practices in the households. The protection approach will be followed throughout the whole implementation phase to ensure a solid engagement and ownership at community level.
