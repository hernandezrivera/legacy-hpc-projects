Project Summary

The consortium of Community Aid for Humanitarian Organization (CAHO), Equatoria Relief and Development Association (ERADA), Mission Trust Aid (MTA) and Mukaya Women Grassroots Empowerment Organization (MWGEO) in response to emergency food assistance and livelihoods Support to vulnerable IDPs, Returnees, and host communities of Yei, Mukaya, Lainya and Kupera of former Central Equatoria State, South Sudan, targeting 5,000 direct beneficiaries, and 800 indirect beneficiaries, (elderly, Women, widows, orphan, and the most vulnerable members of the communities). By supply of emergency food, training of selected members of the communities on food security and livelihood, supply of seeds, especially to the local farmers, farmers association.
The four organizations came together due to their familiarity to the communities, expertise strength in the field of food security and livelihood implementation.
South Sudan transitional government establishment in April 2016 signified a ray of hope to the new nation ravaged by protracted conflict. However, conflict outbreak in July 2016 with intensification of fighting in Juba and its environs, among them are Central Equatoria State (OCHA, July 2016).
This has worsened the fragile state of an estimated 4.8 million vulnerable women, girls, boys and men currently facing acute food insecurity and malnutrition (South Sudan April-IPC Update 2016).
Deteriorating security, rapidly depreciating currency, hyperinflation (667%), dysfunctional markets continue to erode livelihoods and most vulnerable access to appropriate crucial services more so among recently crisis affected counties such as Mukaya, Kupera, Lainya and Yei in central Equatoria state.
The targeted beneficiaries from these counties are 5,000 direct beneficiaries and approximately indirectly 800 beneficiaries which is the estimated population size of IDPs, returnees, and host communities from the selected counties of central Equatoria state.
Armed conflict continues to inhibit agriculture, including through repeated disruption to harvests and trade routes and high security risks around agricultural and other land. Large sections of rural Yei County remain generally inaccessible to humanitarian organizations. Assessed settlements and other humanitarian information sources reported severe disruption to livelihoods and the continued ability of rural settlements to cope is unclear.
Access to food was consistently perceived as inadequate and appears unlikely to improve in the next six months without substantial shifts in the security environment. While access to food will most likely decline through the 2018 lean season, mortality related to hunger, acute malnutrition, and disease may increase, as such currently low levels of access to health and nutrition services continue.
The selected areas have face so many challenges like displacement, lost of able supporting family members, lost of seeds, stoppage in cultivation due to fear of one losing life as far now due to current peace it will give the communities high appetite of putting in action their talent in crop production in support for livelihood.
Similar to the findings of the May-July FSL profile of Yei County, armed conflict continued to be the leading cause of disruption to livelihoods through most of the harvest season, August-February, depending on crop type and rainfall.
Armed conflict was reportedly worse in September- October than in November-December, with proxy indicators for armed conflict declining together.

Activities

According to the plans of the consortium, the project activities that will address the identified area in the following:-1. The consortium will work closely with the local authority in assessing the most immediate seeds needed by the communities through interactions with women group, the elderly youths and church leaders.2. The consortium will distribute seeds in Lainya, Kupera, Mukaya, and Yei respectively.3. The consortium will train the community volunteers on seeds distributions. 4. The consortium will collaborate together with local authorities in monitoring and evaluating cultivation to harvest of the crops and progress of the activities.5. The consortium will be carrying out training to farmers on the best methods of cultivation and formation s of farmers association that is to boost the morale of the farmers. the use of modern farming methods using fertilizers in their farms ,storage of their produces in good storage place which can stay safe until the next season and this helps a lot in reducing hunger. 6. Additionally the farmers will also be train on irrigation methods of farming this can also help them a lot during dry season as most of the areas selected have some stream, Rivers and swampy/wet lands that can reserve water during dry season hence enabling them to produce vegetable foods like tomatoes, cabbage okra during dry season which can also be sold in the local markets in Yei hence bringing income for the farmers this improve their standard of living.7. The consortium will coordinate with other partners in provision of other services that will be important to them like WASH, Nutrition, education, and NFI.8. The consortium will distribute farm tools like Rake, Pangas, Slashers, Hoes, Watering cans.9. Dissemination of fall army warm information:-awareness/sensitization will be given to farmers about army warms how to prevent them from destroying their crops such as maize, and also they will be taught about the right time for planting maize seeds so that it cannot be affected by the army warm. 10. Demonstration of standard nurseries for learning:-demonstration farms are to be set up to enable the farmers learn how to plan their farmlands and follow up of the process and management of weeding stages like first, second weeding this can result to good yields. Additionally the demonstration also acts as an example for the farmer who does not have full knowledge of agriculture. In this aspect the consortium is going to set up these demonstration farms in all the selected counties for the farmers to gain more skills which can result to high output hence reducing the effect of hunger on the entire population of the state and south Sudan at large. 11. Promote the rearing of small livestock and poultry especially among women and youth these improve their economic status of individual.12. The consortium will compile quarterly report to the donors and clusters about the progress of the project activities.
