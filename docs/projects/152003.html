Project Summary

Street Child will enhance the protection of 100,000 (55% girls) conflict-affected children and their families by strengthening the delivery of and access to community-based child protection services that include mental health and psycho-social support services, case management for children with acute protection concerns as unaccompanied and separated children and children associated with armed group from the strengthening of a preventive environment with community based mechanisms and support for local government dedicated agencies.

Needs

2.6 million children and adolescents (1.4 million girls and 1.2 million boys) in the north-east, still remains seriously at risk mainly due to the complex and protracted conflict. The conflict in North-East Nigeria has exposed threats to the mental health and well-being of children. More than 2 million girls, boys and caregivers require psychosocial support services due to the severe distress, hardship and displacement. At least 6,000 girls and boys in North East Nigeria are estimated to have been recruited, abducted or held by non-state armed groups; approximately 50% of these children are survivors of sexual and gender-based violence. Nearly 3,000 boys and girls are expected to be released in 2019 and will require socio-economic reintegration assistance, in order to not worsen their situation.

Activities

Psychosocial support in the schools, as part of our integrated education projects – this is done through after-school activities, such as recreational activities, peer gender and age clubs and in the communities facilitated by trained teachers and PSS facilitators. Total target of children for these activities is 30,000 over for 2019. 5.000 children will be targeted for effective case management, which provides children with protection concerns with an appropriate care plan and regular follow-up from dedicated case workers. FTR services for unaccompanied and separated children are provided in collaboration with partners in the sector. Street Child will guarantee provision of quality interim care for those who cannot be immediately be reunified, in order to reduce their vulnerability to other violations of their rights. Referral pathways to more specialized services are critical – as well as service mapping. proper coordination will ensure timely referral and follow up Street Child has also a team of Clinical Psychologists on staff to provide mental health care through psychotherapy in the community. Children Associated with Armed Forces and Armed Groups (CAAFAG) will be receiving counseling services from trained PSS Facilitators, who received mentoring support and one-on-one guidance from a Clinical Psychologist of Street Child staff. family and community reintegration will be achieved by economic support, advocacy and peace building activities Through our Community-Based Child Protection Committees, which are organized by existing structures and are active in each of their respective communities, they will be providing referrals, upholding child rights, and supporting the development of children. PSS Facilitators have been trained to deliver recreational and counseling services, providing a regular schedule of activity. Caregivers of most at risk children will be enrolled in our income generating activities scheme – benefiting from business and skills training, weekly mentoring and a savings scheme to support them in caring for the children in their custody. This ensure family strengthen and re-integration into their communities and help them uphold children protection.
