Project Summary

In responding to the growing humanitarian crisis in Mayendit,Leer,Koch , Pariang, Cuiebet, Yirol West and Panyinjiar, UNIDOR is proposing to strengthen the provision of critical, timely and lifesaving assistance and services to 31 360(3 000men, 3 600women, 10 760boys,14 000girls) extremely vulnerable children and their families through static and mobile responses. The project seeks to compliment, strengthen, expand and improve the already existing child protection interventions in Koch, Mayendit, Panyinjiar and Leer. The required funding will help boost both financial and human resource support as well as helping in the expansion of activities to other hard to reach areas, which include Cuebert, Pariang, Kajo Keji , Jur river and Yirol West where UNIDOR already has presence with other sectors. UNIDOR Nutrition is already in Cuiebert and Yirol West, while WASH is in Pariang. Expanding child protection interventions to these areas will result in improvement in the provision of integrated multi sectoral interventions.
The project also seeks to integrate child protection monitoring and mainstreaming into the protection intervention to improve the overall context analysis to respond to the identified needs and enhancing the understanding and analysis among humanitarian actors on the child protection context and needs
The team will focus on the following objectives:
i) Ensuring that the most affected boys’ and girls’ including unaccompanied and separated children access lifesaving individual comprehensive case management using vulnerability criteria and toolkit to reduce risks of protection.
ii) Identifying and registering unaccompanied and separated children and ensuring that they are reunified with their parents/caregivers
iii) Providing psychosocial support services to conflict distressed children through establishment of emergency Child Friendly Spaces (CFS), community based and school based PSS.
iv) Mapping, establishing and training community based structures on child protection and provision of awareness sessions to prevent and respond to child protection risks through establishment of child help desks in schools, school clubs, churches; and working with adolescents, caregivers and members of community based child protection networks.
Activities, such as sports, recreational activities and child friendly spaces will continue to be used as entry points in new payams and bomas. Adolescent, boys and girls will be targeted through Adolescent Kits for Innovation and Expression in Leer, Mayendit and Panyinjiar since it’s a new intervention that needs to be rolled out first. Adolescent boys and girls as well as young boys and girls will be involved in the design and delivery of child protection services to ensure that their voices are taken into account and activities tailored to suit their needs. The team will also actively engage the community, to identify excluded children who may be in need of assistance. Activities will also be linked to other services such as Education, Nutrition, WASH and Health.
UNIDOR monitoring and evaluation staff will use the selected indicators and targets to effectively track the progress of project implementation. They will continue to monitor on-going project implementation process: institutional monitoring (financial, physical and organizational issues that affect the project), context, objectives and outcomes. UNIDOR, together with donors will revise the monitoring tools to adapt for measuring the project objectives and results with an active participation of communities.
The CBCPN will support children’s well-being through local structures, while acting both on prevention and response to child protection concerns, by helping communities reduce risks and better protect children from violence, abuse, neglect and exploitation. In addition, the CBCPN will identify and refer case to appropriate service providers.

1 - Needs

According to the (HRP 2019), the recently revitalized peace process promises to offer new opportunities in 2019 for South Sudan’s women, men and children. However, South Sudan remains in a serious humanitarian crisis due to the cumulative effects of years of conflict and violence against civilians, which have destroyed people’s livelihoods and forced 4.2 million people to flee their homes. The number of people who require humanitarian or protection assistance in 2019 remains high at seven million, just like 2018. Some 2.2 million children are out of school and nearly 600,000 pregnant and lactating women will be acutely malnourished in 2019.Children in South Sudan have been affected by 3 500 verified Monitoring and Reporting Mechanism incidents. Over 190 000 children are estimated to have been recruited to armed forces and armed groups, whereas more than one million children have been affected by psychosocial distress. 17,125 children (9 076girls, 8049boys), are still in need of family tracing and reunification (South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview 2019).Panyinjiar has been a home for the most displaced population from Southern Unity who moved through the river with canoes and this led to exacerbate the already humanitarian situation while Mayendit County has in the previous years been experiencing sporadic community conflicts related to cattle raiding that led to displacement of people thus resulting to intensified communal violence and revenge killings. Leer County witnessed a high influx of returnees through the Adok port and according to the April SMART survey conducted by Medair, the nutrition status has detiorated and this has a negative impact on children who will definitely need Nutrion, PSS interventions and home visits for them to cope with the stressful environment. According to the June 2019 rapid assessment conducted by UNIDOR, Yirol West County has been hit with communal clashes leading to displacement of huge population. Boys and girls continue to be exposed to threats of recruitment, psychosocial distress, family separation, abuse, neglect, physical violence and sexual exploitation and sexual violence. Displaced people are also likely to continue returning home in all locations as they begin to generate greater confidence in the peace agreement. However for now, the scale and flow of such movements remain difficult to project. Children still continue to be exposed to protection risks such as abuse, neglect, recruitment, both boys and girls, abductions, sexual slavery of women and girls, exploitation and violence. Many children have also become unaccompanied, separated and orphaned and as a result would need family tracing and reunification services. Based on the above needs, there is to provide lifesaving interventions through the provision of psychosocial support, family tracing and reunification, case management in order to support their resilience to cope with the existing environment. Gender roles have changed as women take up the role of bread winners in the family by moving in search of food, water, firewood and petty jobs which expose them to exploitation, sexual violence and abuse. Girls have been forced to marry and at times they have also multiple forms of violence and have limited support systems and access to information. As a result of the early marriages, girls have faced a lot of medical complications which sometimes has resulted in their deaths during delivery. UNIDOR will continue building capacity and provide training and guidance to the already existing Community Based Networks to prevent and respond to child abuse, neglect, exploitation and violence against children. According to a Situation Report dated 11/10/2019 and a UNOCHA snapshot dated 21/10/2019, floods caused by torrential rains have also worsened the living conditions of South Sudanese people in many parts of the country in the past few months. This requires immediate interventions.

2 - Activities

The following activities will be conducted:i) Providing comprehensive case management and referrals including CBI to the most affected boys’ and girls’ including unaccompanied and separated children as well as other vulnerable children with protection concerns using vulnerability criteria and toolkit as provided by the Case Management Handbook to reduce risks of protection. ii) Identifying and registering unaccompanied and separated children and ensuring that they are reunified with their parents/caregivers iii) Providing psychosocial support services to conflict distressed children through establishment of emergency Child Friendly Spaces (CFS), community based and school based PSS. iv) Conducting trainings for staff and community/frontline workers on case management v) Integrating child protection monitoring and mainstreaming into the protection intervention to improve the overall context analysis to respond to the identified needs and enhancing the understanding and analysis among humanitarian actors on the child protection context and needs vi) Mapping, establishing and training community based structures on child protection and provision of awareness sessions to prevent and respond to child protection risks through establishment of child help desks in schools, school clubs, churches; and working with adolescents, caregivers and members of community based child protection networks.The CBCPN will support children’s well-being through local structures, while acting both on prevention and response to child protection concerns, by helping communities to reduce risks and better protect children from violence, abuse, neglect and exploitation. In addition, the CBCPN will identify and refer cases to appropriate service providers. Staff who will implement activities will sign a Code of Conduct and the Child Safeguarding Policy.UNIDOR has a PSEA policy that is zero tolerant and every staff will be trained and sign the document when signing his contract.

Throughout the programme cycle, the relationships between disability and other risk factors (such as girls with disabilities, children with disabilities who live in institutions, etc.) will be analysed. Efforts will be made to disaggregate individual and qualitative data by disability.UNIDOR will ensure that more females are recruited so that girls are free to share information with a staff of the same sex. ii) On the risk of women and girls are not included in consultations and decision-making processes, activities will included women and girls as beneficiaries and in conducting FGDs, women will be seperated from men as well as girls from boys so that they fully participate.iii) In a effort to mitigate the risk of lack of access to/awareness of referral services for survivors of protection incidents (GBV, child protection, for example), the project will ensure that awareness raising sessions on the available services is done to communities as well as other partners.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

UNIDOR will ensure that they get feedback from the most vulnerable community members during and after the project. A feedback and complaints mechanism will be put and a focal point person nominated. A Committee will be set up that represents communities.This will comprise men,women,boys and girls. Awareness raising sessions will be conducted for the community to be aware of this service.Suggestion boxes as well as a telephone number will be provided for community members to report. A register will also be provided to register complaints and the feedback.This will have a timeline so that at the end they will be able to see if complaints and feedback were handled in time.CP help desks will also be used to disseminate information to children on the complaints and feedback procedure and also informing them that it is their right to raise any concerns and the information will be treated with confidentiality.The adolescent kit for innovation and expression will also create a platform where the needs of youth and adolescents views and suggestions are heard.The project will ensure that children with disabilities as well as excluded children participate in all activities.
