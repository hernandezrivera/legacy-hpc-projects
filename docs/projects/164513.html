Project Summary

After nine years of crisis, in Syria 13.6 million of people are still in need of protection services (5.7 million children, 11.6 for MRE) and support due to continue exposure to new hostilities (NW and NES), forced displacement to seek safety, presence of explosive hazardous weapons, facing obstacles in accessing services, livelihood opportunities and digniﬁed living conditions. The erosion of coping mechanism for individuals and families, the lack of availability of support services to the most vulnerable population caused a major strain on community support structures. Although protection needs vary in the country, protection assistance has been thinned, especially the services for children and women and PwSN causing an increased risk of CP and GBV and generating high levels of psychological distress. In line with the sector priorities deﬁned in the HNO draft for 2020, the intervention is designed to support the existing protection community structures and contribute to the psychological wellbeing of the targeted individuals and communities, establishing community prevention mechanism for GBV, CP and MRE though awareness, capacity building for humanitarian actors and local partners, training and community response initiatives. The project targets a total number of 11979 individuals without discrimination on status (IDPs, returnees and host communities) and in need of protection, in sub – districts of Madiq Castle, Suran, Hamra, Karnaz, Oqeirbat, Muhradah (Hama Governorate), in the sub – district of Ma’rrat Al Numan (Idleb Governorate) in sub – districts of Jabel Samaan, (Aleppo Governorate), in sub – districts of Kafr Batna, Duma, Harasta, At Tall, , Kisweh, Jaramana, Nashabieh, Qatana, Qudsaya (Rural Damascus) and Dar’à sub district and Deir Ez Zor. All those subdistricts have a severity scale between 4 and 5. The project will be implemented by INTERSOS in partnership with SARC; INTERSOS will provide its own expertise of working in the most crisis affected regions of the world and guarantee a proper follow up to implementation and monitor the process and achievements to ensure the respect on international protection standards. While INTERSOS is planning to make awareness sessions into hard to reach areas of Aleppo, Idleb and Deir Ez Zor governorates, in Rural Damascus, Dar’à and Hama, the modality will be a mobile approach in combination with the opportunity of temporarily static interventions using trained protection mobile teams where there are no protection services available and complement other existing services by strengthening them, when are available. The protection teams will provide PSS to children, man and women in ad hoc CFS/WGFS (identiﬁed safe space in the community) through scheduled sessions and will educate on CP and GBV prevention. In areas where there are no existing CFS or WGFS, INTERSOS will promote community- based intervention for child protection and GBV though the creation of temporarily safe spaces (either mosque, school or any other community structure) involving the community in design &implementation and will train community volunteers as facilitators to have a more sustainable response. For MRE INTERSOS will provide awareness sessions to target communities, involving community leaders, existing protection committees, as well involving CFS, WGFS and schools. An intensive needs assessment exercise will be conducted at the start of the project to confirm the actual locations of service provision. During the implementation findings and lessons learned will be shared with the sector to enhance coordination and response by humanitarian actors. The selected areas of intervention are part of INTERSOS Country strategy and integration with other possible interventions ongoing (NFI winterization distribution) and will be promoted also to grant cost/efficiency as well as a more comprehensive response.

4 - Needs

INTERSOS intervention is based on RNA exercises done in November 2019 and January 2020 in some of the targeted sub – districts, the RNA has targeted sub-districts with a severity scale among 4 and 5. Main ﬁndings: KIs expressed for children the lack of specialized protection services, especially for disabled or with speciﬁc needs, causing challenges in adequate and digniﬁed access to services. Psychological distress and trauma were reported and the need of awareness and preparedness response towards child behavior, positive parenting and schooling. Children are exposed to the risk of unexploded remnants in most of the areas visited which are massively broken or destroyed, with huge amount of debris and mines determining casualties amongst which are children. So many KIs correlated positively the need for cleaning and removal, as well to reinforce the MRE for children and adults. Child labor in order to provide IGA and the increasing rate and prevalence of girl’s early marriage was reported by KIs as results of deteriorated social support networks and socioeconomic conditions. Early marriage is related to the conservative perception of girls’ roles and families are engaging in such practices to lessen the ﬁnancial burden and “protect” girls’ reputation. Child sexual abuse was reported only in some of the visited localities, but the observed trend is indicating the underreporting of incidents due to the sensitivity of the topic perceived as a social taboo. Women were observed moving around with no representation in the local institutions and lower participation and engagement in activities, due to cultural norms/traditional gender roles but also due to returns ﬂux inside the targeted areas. Communities are dealing with negative coping mechanism with the protection needs of children, women and elderly, and not responding with a structured protection services, due to the disruption of community and social cohesion and the absence of prevention and response mechanisms.

5 - Implementation of activities

The intervention will establish, strengthen end enable functioning Community Protection Committees (as main entry point in order to identify protection risks and establishing protection safety mechanism). The protection committees will be supported with intensive training on multiple themes (CP, GBV) in order to be able to provide awareness session towards local leaders, teachers, parents and promoting community led initiatives protection oriented. GBV interventions will be focus on prevention more than response, promoting activities with women’s group in the community, training them on GBV prevention, early marriage, women empowerment and leadership, providing adult with NFE and basic literacy and numeracy. Those women’s groups will identify GBV protection risks and providing awareness training on GBV prevention to other women and girls. For CP, will be establishing CFS to provide structured PSS though mobile units and recreational activities. CFS will cater to children with age appropriate activities with staff trained on child friendly teaching methodologies. The awareness recreational activities will focus on providing PSS and improving resilience in children while encouraging them to join the formal school system if they are out of school. For adolescents and youth, life skill activities will be integrated with recreational activities for effective PSS. The staff will be trained on the basics of PFA and PSS to be able to identify and support children with appropriate support and referrals. The intervention will give to educational and protection established institutions (PTA, teachers, community leaders, Community protection committees) trainings on CPMS, PSS and MRE and, based on coordination established with MoE, schools, applying awareness materials and tools used by SARC/ICRC. Regarding the capacity building component (crosscutting) INTERSOS will provide training to humanitarian actors and local partners on CP and GBV mainstreaming and response.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Awareness sessions in CP, GBV, MRE and PSS will be adapted to the social and cultural context in consultation with the community and elders and women, the purpose of each training/awareness session will be clearly explained to beneﬁciaries. The activities will be adapted accordingly to the community needs on time and spaces available (CFS morning/WGFS in the afternoons).The project staff will provide information about available services to all participants without discrimination, feedback mechanism will be established to gauge reactions to sessions and inform session design inclusive of CFFM. INTERSOS staff will be selected within the same areas of intervention, ensuring the right approach to a particular social and cultural environment. To assure that different vulnerable groups will have equitable access to provided services, selection criteria will be established upon needs and vulnerability, prioritizing women, PwSN and differently abled persons and in consultation with communities’ leaders, stakeholders and authorities. INTERSOS will actively participate in national and sub - national sector coordination mechanisms to support the harmonization and deﬁnition of the different protection services, criteria and referral pathways. INTERSOS will train staff on Do No Harm principles, global protection standards, protection from SEA (PSEA), and child safeguarding and code of conduct that respect and uphold the rights of beneﬁciaries and PSEA. INTERSOS will train the project team’s ability to identify and mitigate protection risks, establish a complaint mechanism and address any SEA complaints. Potential GBV risks and main vulnerable groups are identiﬁed though quality and gender – sensitive assessment, informing and adjusting activities and services proposed for GBV. Equitable participation will be guarantee improving accuracy monitoring and assessment data for a more effective response.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: There is no change in the political and security situation at national level. Acceptance and participation of authorities, local leaders and communities during interventions. Continued cooperation and coordination between INTERSOS and relevant local authorities and partners. Targeted groups are actively participating into the design, implementation and monitoring of the intervention. Risks: Inaccessibility to certain target areas due to security reasons, impossibility to operate simultaneously in all the targeted areas, mass displacement of population, level of tension is too high in order to carry out the activities, access not granted because INTERSOS is not well accepted since is new humanitarian actor. Mitigation measures: INTERSOS will follow-up on the security context and prompt communicate with relevant stakeholders to reschedule the intervention or planned them in other safer spaces; the mobile approach for the HTR areas and the awareness programs as entry point to the communities will allow INTERSOS to operate simultaneously, acquiring good level of acceptance and better assess the level of needs. INTERSOS field staff and SARC local branches will support INTERSOS in acquiring good knowledge of operational context and providing relevant knowledge for further context analysis. INTERSOS will follow up and monitoring on the population movement, high level of information sharing with coordination mechanisms within partners and sector. Community tensions can be mitigated by ensuring participation of the population in program design, clearly communicated beneﬁciary selection criteria and established complaint mechanisms. INTERSOS will advocate within the sector members to work on harmonization of assistance packages, quality of services though the promotion of technical national standards/ guidelines and promoting active reporting on 4W to avoid duplication or overlapping.
