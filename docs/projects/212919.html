Project Summary

This project aims to enhance the food security of the affected men, women, boys and girls, with particular attention to PWDs, in Northwest Syria (NWS), as a part of and aligned with the objectives outlined in the Syrian Arab Republic Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) of 2024.
The humanitarian project, slated for implementation in 2024, aims to address the escalating food insecurity crisis in Syria, which, as outlined in FSA Sector HNO Chapter 2024, continues to affect an estimated 15.5 million people, representing 65% of the population. This dire situation is underscored by a comprehensive needs assessment encompassing over 42,000 households, revealing an alarming increase in food insecurity compared to previous years. Key findings highlight significant food needs, particularly in regions such as Idleb (73% estimated in need of food assistance) and Aleppo (58%). Throughout 2023, limited funding and resources led to a reduction in food assistance levels, exacerbating the plight of vulnerable households. The repercussions were evident in household consumption trends, with approximately 67% of the population experiencing insufficient consumption, as reported by the WFP Remote Monitoring System. Furthermore, soaring food prices placed immense strain on households' purchasing power, and trade and financial constraints have hindered agricultural productivity, perpetuating food insecurity. Agricultural production, particularly of staple crops like wheat, remains well below national needs due to various factors, including economic downturns, weather anomalies, and disruptions to supply chains. Natural disasters such as earthquakes and wildfires have exacerbated these challenges, further compromising food access and livelihoods, particularly in affected regions.
This humanitarian project will contribute to addressing those needs by prioritising the distribution of life-saving food assistance to vulnerable households, particularly those newly displaced in Jandairis, Afrin and Maarat Tamsrin subdistricts, which are of extreme severity of needs (score 4) and high PiN according to FSA PiN Severity - HNO 2024.
SDI will aim to provide life-saving emergency food assistance to the newly displaced individuals, mostly women and children, with special attention to PWDs, including food baskets and Ready-to-Eat Rations (RTER)
The proposed activities include:
1- Provision of Food Baskets:
SDI aims to distribute food baskets to 2,000 households. The baskets will be meticulously designed in accordance with cluster recommendations. Each food basket, weighing 60 kilograms and providing about 1,600 kilocalories per person per day, is tailored to meet the nutritional needs of a family for one month.
2- Distribution of Ready-to-Eat Rations (RTER):
Additionally, SDI plans to distribute 3,000 Ready-to-Eat Rations (RTER) to households newly displaced in open areas or residing in vulnerable camps. Each RTER package, weighing 23.32 kilograms and offering 2,128 kilocalories, is intended to sustain a family of five members for five days.
SDI will directly execute these activities through its proficient field team stationed in Syria. The procurement and distribution of food baskets will be conducted with utmost efficiency to ensure a swift response to the urgent needs of the newly displaced population.
Regarding beneficiary selection, SDI will prioritize the most vulnerable households, irrespective of gender, based on eligibility criteria tailored for both food baskets and RTER distributions. These criteria encompass factors such as income level, household composition, and specific vulnerabilities such as pregnancy or lactation. SDI will continuously assess security conditions before and during project implementation to safeguard the well-being of field personnel, beneficiaries, suppliers, and all stakeholders involved. Mitigation strategies will include ongoing security evaluations and proactive risk avoidance measures.

Furthermore, to ensure operational continuity and mitigate external risks, SDI plans to source food baskets locally within Syria, leveraging market assessments to secure optimal pricing and avoid potential disruptions resulting from border closures with Turkey. The initiative will adhere to principles of Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), inclusion, and Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP), ensuring equitable access to aid irrespective of gender or demographic status.
