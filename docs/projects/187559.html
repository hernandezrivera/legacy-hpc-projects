Project Summary

The combined effects of currency depreciation, soaring prices, reduced fiscal revenue and increasing domestic debt, widespread job loss as well as unilateral coercive measures have plunged additional segments of the population into humanitarian need, most notably in areas historically less affected by hostilities and displacement. People’s ability to meet basic needs has further decreased compared to 2020, with a disproportionate impact on female-headed households, older persons without family support, persons with disabilities and children.
Agriculture has been historically on of the main source of income for Syrians. Prior to the conflict, the agricultural sector accounted for 18% of Syria’s GDP, 23% of its exports, and occupied 17% of its workforce. Of the approximately 46% of Syrians living in rural areas, 80% earned income from agricultural work. The scale and size of the sector has been dramatically reduced because of the crisis, leaving many people without income and basic food.
In Hama the area with high rates of returnees, the needs for recovery and livelihoods are high, and although people can reclaim their property (often severely damaged), income generating opportunities are minimal, pushing people to poverty. Helfaya village is located in the northern part of Hama governorate, around 25 km north-west Hama city, and it is administratively part of Muhardah sub-district. Muhardah sub-district is categorized as a severity scale 3, by the HNO 2022. According to the head of municipality, Helfaya population was around 35,000 individuals (7,000 HHs) before crisis while in June 2021 it was about 8,000 individuals (1,600 families), majority of whom are returnees. The level of destruction in Helfaya is very high and according to the head of municipality around 70 % the buildings are partially damaged and 30 % totally damaged.
The project design is in line with the the Food and Agriculture Sector requirements especially when it comes to SO2 and SO3 and will follow the sector guidance related to the composition of the agricultural kits and content of the trainings.
The objectives and activities will include:
O1: To increase access of the farmers to the agricultural inputs
1.1.Provision of agro inputs including: seeds and seedlings, fertilizers and pesticides, light farm equipment, animal feed and fodder, fuel, ancillary goods and supplies, etc. (kits as per Food and Agriculture sector recommendations (So2)
1.2.Rehabilitation of the sorting/cooling facilities (SO3)
O 2: To improve water supply to farmed lands
2.1.Rehabilitation of the irrigation channels and water wells (CfW) (SO3)
2.2.Renewing water pumps and provide solar energy outlets (SO3)
2.3.Distribution of drip irrigation systems to reduce water consumption (SO2)
O3: To increase technical capacity of the farmers
3.1.Mapping of training facilities in the areas, including DoA and government agriculture extension services and facilitate linkages to farmers (SO2)
3.2.Training of the farmers on post-harvest handling, marketing and agro-entrepreneurship, new low-cost technologies, preservation etc. (SO2)
O4:To Increase women access to income generating through self-employment in food processing
4.1.Grants to women food processing micro enterprises (SO2)
4.2.Coaching and follow up (SO2)
4.3.Awareness raising on GBV and women empowerment (SO2)
To get acceptance by the community, based on the needs assessment in the beginning of program all farmers residing in the areas of intervention will receive a support package

4 - Needs

Syria remains a complex humanitarian emergency characterized by over 10 years of ongoing hostilities and their long-term consequences including widespread destruction, explosive ordnance contamination and the largest number of IDPs in the world. Throughout 2021, the macro-economic context continued to deteriorate sharply and is accelerating into 2022. The combined effects of currency depreciation, soaring prices, reduced fiscal revenue and increasing domestic debt, widespread job loss as well as unilateral coercive measures have plunged additional segments of the population into humanitarian need, most notably in areas historically less affected by hostilities and displacement. People’s ability to meet basic needs has further decreased compared to 2020, with a disproportionate impact on female-headed households, older persons without family support, persons with disabilities and children. Assessment data from August 2021 indicate that the income gap has continued to widen, with average household expenditure now exceeding income by fifty per cent, compared to twenty per cent in August 2020, only 10 per cent of households have an income above the cost of Syria’s Minimum Expenditure Basket. In Hama the area with high rates of returnees, the needs for recovery and livelihoods are high, and although people can reclaim their property (often severely damaged), income generating opportunities are minimal, pushing people to poverty.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

RISK 1: COVID19 FCA will take into account COVID19 restrictions and risks, ensuring that both staff and beneficiaries are not being exposed to additional threats as a result of the project. FCA is aware of and will follow the relevant authority’s Covid-19 rules. Learning facilities will have Covid-19 safety protocols in place that include identification, prevention measures, awareness raising and a referral system between the learning facility and the relevant health center. RISK 2: Beneficiary and other information is not sufficiently protected. FCA has clear Duty of Care policy that clarifies the organization’s responsibilities and obligations to its staff, volunteers and associated personnel. FCA adheres to data protection rules. RISK 4: Staff or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they are serving, particularly children. The risk may be heightened for children with disabilities, those unaccompanied, orphans and younger children. Project staff will be aware and adhere to FCA's policies and procedures to prevent from sexual abuse.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

R1. Aid workers or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they should be serving, particularly children. The risk may be heightened for children with disabilities, those unaccompanied, orphans and younger children. M1.Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policies should be in place, practised and monitored. Polices will cover standards of behaviour for the organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and include accountability measures. Polices should address risks at the organizational level and the program level. Trainings and refreshers on PSEA should be done systematically. M2. FCA staff and targeted communities should have access to safe and effective reporting systems including knowing when they will get a response to their report and having the right to report anonymously. M3. Targeted communities should be aware of the organization’s policies and procedures. M4. All FCA staff sign PESA as a part of the job contract.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R1: Risks related to implementation and approvals. M1: MoU with the MoA is in process right now and before it is signed, the project will implemented in close cooperation with SARC. FCA is is in close contact with SARC at the moment thus risk of project disapproval by SARC is minimal. R2: Risk of insecurity, conflict and violence. M2: FCA will regularly security SOPs and provide refresher training for all staff. In the event that the conflict breaks out, security contingency plans already in place will be followed. Evacuation to safer areas will be evoked when deemed necessary. In case of inaccessibility as a result of insecurity, activities will be moved to safer areas while trying follow beneficiaries to their areas of refuge. R3: High inflation rate. M3: In case the price of goods or services will increase, FCA will inform the donor on the difficulties to follow the agreed plan and proposes revisions. FCA will closely monitor the exchange rate of SYP and will make sure that impact of the exchange rate on the project activities is minimal. Joint action through DINGO. R4: Transfer of funds to Syria. M4: Request donors to send funds directly to Syria. Consultations through DINGO. R5. Increased returnees/IDP caseload. M5: close monitoring of the population movement and possibility of revision of distribution of agri inputs in line with do-no-harm policy. R6. Misuse of selling of agri inputs or business grants. M6. provision of inputs in small quantities, close monitoring.
