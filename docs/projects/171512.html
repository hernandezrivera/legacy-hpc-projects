Project Summary

A combination of factors such as widespread and severe flooding, Desert Locust infestation, socioeconomic impacts of COVID-19 and the cumulative impacts of previous shocks have led to a deterioration of the nutrition situation Somalia. This was revealed by results from 37 separate nutrition surveys conducted by FSNAU and partners in July and August 2020 . Although the surveys showed that there has been some improvement in the overall nutrition situation among rural and urban populations compare to previous seasons, the nutrition situation among IDPS has shown no improvement. At the national level, the median prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) has remained Serious (10–14.9%) over the past three seasons (11.8% in 2020 Gu, 13.1% in 2019 Deyr and 13.8% in 2019 Gu). FSNAU has predicted that the food security situation expected to worsen among urban dwellers and IDPs, with Crisis (IPC Phase 3) outcomes expected as significant proportions of households are unable to meet their minimum food needs without external assistance. The survey also revealed that the socioeconomic impact of COVID-19 was a factor, associated with a decline in remittances, increased food prices, and a decline in employment and other income-earning opportunities in urban areas.
Certain population groups such as Galkacyo, Mogadishu, Jowhar and Baidoa IDPs
are of concern due to chronically high rates of acute malnutrition with GAM rates of 20.2%, 16.0%, 25.9% and 14.5% respectively. Some of the factors that are attributed to the high prevalence of acute malnutrition include high morbidity, low immunization and vitamin-A supplementation, and poor care practices and food insecurity. IMC aims to contribute to reduction of malnutrition related morbidity and mortality through provision of quality nutrition services in 4 regions of South Central Somalia which have chronically high rates of acute malnutrition. IMC will focus on three main strategies i.e. lifesaving therapeutic interventions, preventive nutrition activities and capacity building. To enhance detection and regular identification of acutely malnourished children and PLWs, IMC Female Health Workers (FHWs) and CHWs will disseminate culturally. Appropriate promotion/prevention messaging and conduct community based screening and referral of malnourished children and PLWs using MUAC and oedema measurements. Family/mother lead MUAC strategy will be used as a simple and cost effective way of increasing coverage of case detection. It also ensures caregivers/mothers have knowledge to identify malnutrition early.
Children who are severely malnourished or at risk of being malnourished will be referred to OTP and TSFP treatment centres, and in the hard to reach areas, by mobile health and nutrition units. Children with severe acute malnutrition with medical complications will be referred to nearby stabilization centres in accordance with national guidelines while observing COVID 19 prevention measures.
In addition to Individual and group IYCF counselling, IMC will promote optimal IYCF
Practices and positive deviance through Mother to Mother Support Groups. Within these groups, nutritious recipes will be formulated and tested through cooking demonstrations. All these will observe COVID 19 preventive measures. Other high impact nutrition interventions will include multiple micronutrient supplementation for women of reproductive age; Vitamin A Supplementation; deworming for boys and girls aged 6-59 months; and Zinc for treatment of diarrhoea.

Project Activities

1. Conduct individual and group IYCF-E counselling among PLWs and caregivers of children aged 2 years and below. 2. Establish community based Mother-to-Mother support groups for PLW 3. Conduct Market survey and food attributes exercise for listing food items locally available and classify based on their nutrient contribution in diet and develop six recipes out of it and trained mother to mother support groups 4. Conduct complementary cooking demonstrations 4. conduct community dialogue. 5. Scale up community based malnutrition detection and identification of acute malnutrition through FHWs and Mother MUAC 6. Provide lifesaving therapeutic treatment for Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) 7. Provide Deworming, Multiple micronutrient and Vitamin A supplementation as part of Outpatient management of acute malnutrition. 8. Intensify breastfeeding counselling and re-lactation in stabilization centres for acutely malnourished children under 6 months of Age. 9. Conduct community mobilization activities for screening and referral of malnourished children. 10. Conduct Family MUAC screening. 11. Health, Nutrition and hygiene education. 12. Capacity strengthening of health workers (such as midwives), CHWs and FHWs to implement CMAM through classroom and on-job mentoring and refresher trainings. 13. Conduct blanket supplementary feeding to prevent deterioration into acute malnutrition or relapse in children discharged from OTP.
