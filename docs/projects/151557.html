Project Summary

The project has been designed to analyze emergency dynamics, and forecast future events in order to support and implement agile mechanisms to respond to the evolving education needs in Somalia. Through this project WV will ensure that 10,800 (4580 female) emergencies and crisis affected children from primary schools are supported for various emergency education interventions. The overarching goal is to provide the boys and girls with access to quality education in emergencies interventions. Firstly, leveraging on education supplies from GiK, WV has prepositioned education school supplies including furniture at strategic points/districts for ease of dispatch in the event of an emergency occurring. Further, in line with the WV ongoing global campaign on ending violence against children, the project will continue to undertake advocacy campaigns to increase girls’ enrolment and participation since they are adversely affected by school drop outs compared to their male counterparts. Support with purchase for uniforms, and learning materials, particularly targeting IDP settlements, where school access is marginally lower will be enhanced.
WV will also ensure teachers providing education in emergencies services have complied with the Code of Conduct and ensure minimal disruption of teaching and learning by providing emergency incentives. The teachers in collaboration with the respective ministries of education will ensure learning outcomes assessments are conducted periodically to ensure quality services are being offered to the children. This will be achieved through continuous in-service training of teachers during emergencies. Teachers will also be equipped with basic skills for identification of children who are being abused or violated and facilitated to offer psycho social first aid and referral of cases that need expert attention to health facilities. In addition, to ensure that the school environment is safe and protective, the project will ensure child to child club activities are encourage to increase children’s ability to protect themselves e.g. child-led DRR activities. Accountability mechanisms will be rolled out by the project to ensure each school has a robust feedback and complaints mechanism. For sustainability of gains achieved, the project will ensure that Community Education Committees have been trained on governance and supported to develop school improvement plans which will partly be funded by the project and resources raised by the PTA

Needs

Drought and conflict has led the Somalia enrollment rates to one of the lowest in the world since the education system collapsed in early 1990s. According to the 2018 HRP, only an estimated 30% of children have access to learning opportunities, while more than three million are out-of school. An outcome monitoring exercise conducted by WV in September 2018 revealed a net enrollment of about 40%. The southern and central parts of Somalia are the most affected mostly the rural areas and Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) settlements, where only 17% of children are enrolled in primary schools.Most children, especially girls, are not only denied of their educational development, but also other important benefits such as psycho social support, protection from threats, including sexual abuse and exploitation, physical attack, early marriage, child labour and recruitment into armed groups. In addition, limited enrollment continuity and teachers’ motivation remain key challenges. Occupation and attacks on schools continue to be a concern with increase in physical attacks to schools leading to loss of lives and destruction of school infrastructure. The EiE needs of children of primary school going age include a safe and protective learning environment all year round. This includes classrooms that are equipped with teaching and learning materials such as reference books, charts and realia. To address this need, the project envisions a print rich classroom environment to improve on the children's literacy and numeracy skills. Further, the WV outcome monitoring exercise discovered that cultural practices such as child marriage pose a risk of dropping out of school. This project includes a component of life skills (child to child accountability) to equip adolescents with skills to protect themselves from violence especially those in IDP camps and hard to reach rural areas.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Boys and girls, children with disabilities, and marginalized communities are impacted differently by the crisis and displacement, having distinct humanitarian needs and facing specific education concerns. Therefore, integration of age, gender and diversity principles in service delivery and assistance will be promoted. Protection risks include widespread GBV, child recruitment, physical attacks, forced early marriages, forced evictions, limited access to humanitarian assistance due to insecurity and/or discriminatory practices. The project will strengthen inclusion by instituting an accountability mechanism for effective protection of affected populations i.e robust complaints and feedback mechanism for instance a toll free telephone line

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Gender segregated data will used throughout the project. The project will that education facilities are accessible and appropriate for all in the communities. This will take into specific education and protection needs are taken into account. This will include but not limited to making gender-segregated latrines and hand-washing facilities in the education facilities. The latrines will be equipped with locks. Hygiene kits will also be distributed to girls to address their menstruation need. While gender disparity in Somalia often exclude women from accessing information and making decision, the project will ensure education is accessed both by a girl child and boy child so that they can both have equality opportunity to quality education

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Using ‘Do no harm principles’, WV will seek to avoid physical or psycho-social risks that would be detrimental to project beneficiaries, and will serve all people irrespective of age, sex or clan. Given vulnerabilities associated with issues related to clan dynamics, WV has done a thorough analysis of the context and has conducted the Do-No-Harm Assessments for target districts. Competing over access to basic commodities and services are often one of the major causes of community conflict. To avoid provoking fraction among communities, the project will closely engage community throughout the project cycle, ensuring they are involved in site and community education committee selection, fully informed of project details, and making sure accountability system is in place to allow them voice their concerns and complaint. WV, throughout the project cycle, will ensure the safety of beneficiaries and staff during program. This will ensure timely provision of services to prevent late travels by the staff and beneficiaries which might expose them to security risks. Throughout the project duration, world vision will monitor the security situation and also collaborate with the local administration and the community to ensure that the beneficiaries and the staff are well aware. WV has the security team that closely monitors the security situation within the target areas, hence able to advice and prevent staff and also beneficiaries from being affected.
