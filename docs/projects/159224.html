Project Summary

Due to the dry and harsh Jilaal (January March) season and a significant delay in the start of the 2019 Gu season rainfall, Moderate to Severe drought conditions prevailed in most parts of Somalia between March and mid-May. Accordingly, the 2019 FSNAU Post Gu nutrition assessment findings showed that lower Juba and Gedo regions are under emergency IPC4 and are considered hotspots in need of urgent nutrition and health support interventions.
Immunization remains very low with measles’ coverage among 1 year olds being only 30% overall and 15% in inaccessible parts of South Central Zone. Only 3% of births are registered and neonatal mortality rate (per 1,000 live births) is 53. Most of the deaths are caused by common preventable diseases, like pneumonia as they cannot access most basic health services in some parts. Women and children in the IDPs are most vulnerable at risk of getting diseases due to environmental and seasonal situation Diminished funding situation in lower Juba and Gedo region also led to deteriorating state of health service delivery is of concern with risk of increased morbidity & mortality among most vulnerable girls &boys under fives, pregnant and lactating women.
To reduce the burden of morbidity and mortality, Somali Aid will provide integrated lifesaving primary health care services; improve access to health care systems by providing health interventions to the boys, girls, men and women within the target population to the nearest proximity. Health care services through consultation and management of childhood illnesses with an aim of reducing maternal and neonatal mortality will be given focus. ANC and BEmONC with referrals of the CEmONC services will be provided to the pregnant mothers.
Somali Aid aims to reach about 85,094 persons including 18,526 <5s, 5,597 <1 and 6,322 PLWs with primary healthcare services by end of 2020.
