Project Summary

A combination of convict, insecurity, mass displacement, recurrent droughts, serious flooding and extreme poverty, coupled with poor basic health service coverage has seriously impacted to food security and livelihoods and greatly increased the population’s vulnerability to diseases and malnutrition. The worst affected are marginalized IDPs and vulnerable rural communities (men, women, boys and girls) as well as persons living with disabilities and the elderly in Renk, Upper Nile state Region, South Sudan. The situation is further exacerbated by limited capacity of trained health workers, limited and scattered population, long distances to health facilities, outdated socio-cultural practices which are not conducive with good health as well as limited interventions undertaken by a number of humanitarian agencies in the area owing to insecurity and corresponding access challenges. According to humanitarian report, the maternal mortality in South Sudan in is estimated 735/100,000 live births whereas Malnutrition has increased with GAM prevalence above 15% (Post Guide technical report of September 2024). This plight calls for need to strengthening surveillance and control systems, improving access to lifesaving health services such immunization, maternal health, undertaking public health measures, and increasing awareness and communication and education among communities are substantial elements in reducing mortality and morbidity of these diseases in order to reduce excess morbidity and mortality and due disease outbreak driven by climatic shocks and convict and improve physical and mental well-being of the convict of the convict affected displaced and non - displaced communities ( women, men boys and girls including those with disabilities) and ensure protection of by providing timely response are referral service to the victims of the GBV.
The Project design will enable for consultation with all community cross-section including the persons with disability to ensure their needs are covered in addition to integration with other relevant cluster partners particularly nutrition, WASH, education, protection and education .Further the project will ensure increased integration of mental health/psychosocial support services (MHPSS) HCW and community training as well training of HCW on trauma , MCM case management as well as increasing awareness through social mobilization ,education and advocacy on disease control and health and nutrition.
Maiwut county is one of the counties in the Upper Nile state located in the Eastern part of the State bordering Ethiopia from the Eastern part to the South. It shares borders with neighboring counties of Nasir in the West and Longechuk in the north. The county is predominantly inhabited by the Nuer community (Chie-Waw, Chie-Chaany and Thiang) and the Koma tribe. The county is home to at least 120,000 individuals and records high levels of humanitarian needs as it was ravaged during the 2013 and 2016 clashes that started in Juba and escalated to the whole country where Maiwut county was terribly affected in 2017 leading to the displacement to the refugee camps of Ethiopia in the Gambella Region. This year, the county has experienced relative peace with the displaced IDPs accessing their ancestral lands of origin and returnees coming back to the country in small numbers mainly to visit, see and witness the relative peace with an intention of full return. However, these narratives changed in April 2023 when the Sudan crisis broke out which affected the refugee camps in Sudan hosting refugees from South Sudan whose origin are from Upper Nile. Maiwut County received high number of returnees coming in using commercial, trucks from the borders of Melut and Renk not until access was cut off by the r filling up swamps and blocking road access. Despite the created access constrain by rain, returnees from Sudan still keep on arriving on foot into Maiwut, County and getting integrated to the hosts community

1. Needs

• Lack of adequate Primary and secondary health facilities across Maiwut county hence many patients have to be referred to Ethiopia where there are better health care services. • Inadequate healthcare facilities: Maiwut Hospital lacks government support to improve its services or stability hence it faces difficulties in providing adequate healthcare and relies on referrals to neighboring countries like Ethiopia. • High cases of malnutrition, hunger and diseases which comprise of malaria, Pneumonia, diarrhea, skin diseases, typhoid and eye infections. • Lack of enough drugs at the health facilities. .• Lack of Infrastructures: The poor conditions of the roads lead to late reporting of children to schools since children walk long distances in muddy roads. • The poor conditions of some school buildings with leaking roofs does not provide a conducive environment for school going children. • Few schools: there are few schools to accommodate the big Population of IDP, returnees and host community. • Lack of nursery schools in all Payams of Maiwut County, hence, children aged between 3 and 5 do not have access to basic education. • Lack of safe drinking Water- there are no nearby boreholes in schools. Children move long distance to get drinking water from nearby swamps/rivers. • Lack of enough motivations/salary for teachers. • Lack of food within the community affecting the school going children as well. • Limited funding for education across Maiwut county. • Lack of training programs for teachers. • Few Adult Education centers to boost up Education for the elderly. • Children with disabilities are not given enough education attention and inclusivity.

2. Project activities

1. HCW Training: trauma, MCM, case management, at the project locations in Maiwut (all payams) 2. Provision of medical supplies including essential drugs for the targeted health facilities in the project locations in Maiwut.3. Support to early detection, investigation and response to Maiwut and all other payams.4. Provision of essentials health services including pre-natal and neo natal for new mothers and their children at targeted health facilities at the project sites.5. Provision of emergency and emergency care services to GBV case (women and girl ) at project facilities in Maiwut6. Conducting immunization on common disease outbreak including EPI at the project locations.7. Conducting health awareness, social mobilization, education and advocacy activities in disease control, health and nutrition in Maiwut, Upper Nile state through mobilization and sensitization meetings ,forums /meeting.
