Project Summary

Based on HNO 2024, total number of PIN s 16.7 M(72% of population), PWDs is 17%, children 45%.
Twelve years into the crisis in Syria, over two million IDPs, mostly women and children, remain on sites designed to serve as a last resort in the short term. Most last resort sites, such as informal settlements/camps, planned camps and collective centres, are characterized by lack of camp management systems, poor shelter conditions, overcrowding and varying degrees of access to basic services, leaving IDPs in such sites vulnerable and in need of humanitarian aid. Following the February 2023 earthquakes, which displaced 97,500 households at its peak, a significant
number of people remained in last resort sites in north-west Syria, while a majority eventually returned to their homes.
Self-settled sites are the most vulnerable to extreme weather conditions, due to lack of site planning, infrastructure, and camp management systems. In camps in north-east Syria, there were a lot of numbers of fires reported resulting in injuries, deaths and damage to infrastructure. According to the ISIMM, in the past year, many IDP sites in north-west Syria flooded, Informal settlements/camps are the most vulnerable to extreme weather conditions, due to lack of proper site planning, infrastructure, and camp management systems.
In 2023, the main shocks affecting Syria included a deteriorated security environment, socio economic deterioration, the devastating earthquakes in February 2023, and increased climatic risks.
The February 2023 earthquake caused significant infrastructure damage (including agriculture related) and caused large displacement from urban areas in the affected governorates towards rural areas that pressured both the household resources and savings of both earthquake-impacted households and host families and communities. The March Remote Monitoring report showed that around 63% of households in earthquake-affected locations were experiencing inadequate food consumption, greatly affecting their food security.
Mercy-USA will target 4500 HH with USD$150 one-off emergency cash per HH (MPC) to cover the survival needs of the affected people. This intervention is aligned with MPC guidance notes – 2024 to ensure the value covers the agreed SMEB value of 150USD.
Mercy-USA will respond to any sudden/onset crises born from natural hazards (flooding, fire, snowstorm) or man-made (displacement) across NW Syria with coordination through Cash Working Group to avoid any overlap.
Mercy-USA will conduct vulnerability assessment aligned with CWG MPC vulnerability criteria and will conduct verification for the BNFs in the affected sites before the cash distribution and will ensure the response to be implemented within 7 days from the emergency to ensure effective response is in place.
Targeting: Mercy-USA will target beneficiaries who were impacted by any sudden/onset crises born from natural hazards (flooding, fire, snowstorm) or man-made (displacement) across NW Syria. The targeting will be based on the HNO severity scale and align with the FSL targeting, taking into consideration the nutrition-sensitive criteria PLW, child under 2 years and elderly.
Mercy-USA M&E team will verify the BNFs, monitor the distribution, and conduct PDM after 2 weeks of each distribution. Mercy USA will have contract agreements with multiple vetted Financial Service Providers (FSPs) to cover all targeted locations for timely response to any emergency, so the FSP has the capacity for cash distribution with huge amount.
Locations will cover broad area in NW Syria at the district level: Ariha, Harim, Idleb, Jisr Ash-Shugur, Afrin, Jebel Saman where Mercy-USA has full access.
The project will be implemented directly by Mercy-USA without IPs.
PTT will be possible option for the MPC response in Afrin by transferring to the FSP who will be distributed to beneficiaries under supervision of Mercy-USA team.
