Project Summary

Support conflict affected individuals with lifesaving food and livelihood assistance across the governorates of Aleppo, Ar-Raqqa, Deir-ez-Zor, Idleb, Al-Hasakeh. Response will be prioritized based on the severity of the needs in the target location. Mainly, Mercy Corps will provide:
Multi-purpose cash Assistance (MPCA): whenever cash is feasible, Mercy Corps will prioritize provision of one-off MPCA to newly IDPs and severely food insecure population. MPCA will be based on the survival minimum expenditure basket (SMEB) rates of each location, which includes food assistance. In-kind distribution will be considered only when cash and voucher deemed to be infeasible.
Multi-round food assistance: Eight rounds of food basket assistance will be provided to IDPs and food insecure population covering 70% of the individual monthly dietary needs.. Electronic transfer will be utilized whenever possible. In-kind food will only be used when CVA deemed to be infeasible.
Inputs for food production: Mercy Corps will support vulnerable farmers to purchase quality summer and winter crops inputs from local markets. Each farmers will receive USD500 or equivalent amount in the form of voucher. In-kind distribution of inputs will only be considered when CVA proved inappropriate.
Livestock: Mercy Corps will provide subsidies through vouchers for fodder and animal health consultation services to vulnerable livestock herders in the targeted locations.
Kitchen Garden: Mercy Corps will provide vulnerable households with in-kind kitchen garden kits for improved vegetable and summer crop production.
Target households will be selected based on vulnerability criteria and food security indicators. Additional eligibility criteria will be considered to target farmers and livestock herders.

3 - Needs

Syria 2023 PIN figures estimated food insecure + those at risk of food insecurity increased from 14.6 to 15.3 million, with 12.1 million facing acute food insecurity, 1.8 million at risk of sliding into food insecurity, 2.1 million living in camps deemed to be 100% food insecure. This is alarming given wheat yield was 30% below the long-term average in Syria. General analysis on food consumption scores (FCS), reduced coping strategy (rCSI) and diet diversity, show food security has consistently worsened over the past two years. People consuming an inadequate diet increased from 21% in 2019 to as much as 50% in 2022. rCSI levels is an indicator monitoring resilience to face-off future shocks, where a higher score relates to lower levels of capacity of coping. Consumed diets (diet diversity) highlights a clear reduction of consumption of proteins, fruits, dairy and pulses while sugars, fats and vegetables remains largely unchanged - a significant worsening of the diversity of the diet, now largely composed of cheaper and non-nutrient rich foods. The WFP national average standard reference basket a household in Syria can afford with their monthly minimum wage shows erosion of purchasing power over the past couple of years, which steadily increased as the COVID-19 impact coupled with the intensified depreciation of SYP.

4 - Implementation of activities

(1) Multi-purpose cash Assistance (MPCA): Where money transfer agents are available and functional markets accessible, one-off MPCA provision to acutely vulnerable households. MPCA transfer based on local SMEB rates (calculated using ongoing price monitoring data), aligned with Food Security Cluster recommendations, and harmonized with other cash actors. This was supported by Mercy Corps’ January 2022 MSNA conducted in the target locations in NES, which found that the majority of markets are functional, supplying a variety of vegetables, fruits, and meat. In NWS, the market monitoring exercise snapshot conducted by the NWS Cash working group in Nov 2021 indicated that the SMEB items and the SMEB vegetable components are available in the markets however their prices are increasing which is confirming the needs for cash to support the purchasing power of the affected population.(2) Multi-round food assistance: Food response activities designed to adhere to the FSL sector recommendations for regular food assistance. (2) Inputs for food production (farming and livestock): Farmers & herders selection based on vulnerability & eligibility criteria. (4) Kitchen Gardens: Delivered via an in-kind modality, with distribution of physical kits procured by Mercy Corps through reputable suppliers to ensure quality of packages for farmers.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

(1) Large population movements in the target area. Regular monitoring of conflict dynamics n order to identify when conflict may result in population movement and program impact. (2) Local authorities restrict activities due to lack of support for protection interventions. Where programming is sensitive to authorities but a priority for local communities, Mercy Corps will support partners in intervention methods & engagement with local actors to increase opportunities to operate. (3) Deterioration of relations between IDPs & host community. Agreed vulnerability criteria will result in assistance directed towards vulnerable community members and households regardless of returnee, IDP, or host community status. Participants are identified in partnership with local communities including IDPs & host communities. Communities & local stakeholders are sensitized on selection criteria. (4) Exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers/partners. Mercy Corps has codes of conduct and safeguarding practices for partners and staff. Monitoring and evaluation systems include accountability frameworks, and beneficiary complaints mechanisms. Partners and staff are engaged in PSS and case management exercise in areas where there are limited/no services for onward referral. Mercy Corps will ensure that both program teams and partners are engaged in the appropriate cluster service mapping exercises. Partners receive resources to conduct service mapping (and feedback to the cluster).


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Participant safeguarding is central to Mercy Corps’ values, and we uphold a zero- tolerance tolerance approach towards any form of sexual exploitation and abuse. In order to prevent safeguarding violations of any kind, all staff, casual labor and external contractors receive orientation and training, and are required to sign the Code of Conduct. The safeguarding training is comprehensive and is delivered to all Mercy Corps and partner staff. Where Mercy Corps and partner staff have higher levels of contact with participants, they will receive tailored capacity strengthening on Protection Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and will participate in quarterly events, which address PSEA concerns in target communities, and facilitate the roll-out of PSEA tools and preventative actions. This process is led by our WoS Safeguarding Manager and is supported by a network of focal points within Mercy Corps. Partner staff will have the opportunity to risk assess their own areas in terms of safeguarding and create proactive action plans. Community participants will be informed on what safeguarding is and how they can report any concerns through sensitisation work across programmes. In addition, external contractors and staff such as suppliers or Third Party Monitors (TPMs) are regularly trained by department focal points who have completed tailored Training of Trainer (ToT) safeguarding training.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

(1) Deterioration of the security situation precludes access to target areas. The activities outlined will only be implemented if access conditions inside Syria remain permissive. Regular monitoring of conflict dynamics in the areas of interventions in order to identify when a loss of access is becoming increasingly likely and transition programming to national NGO partners to the extent possible. (2) Negative impact of livelihoods interventions on community and household dynamics MC is aware that cash and asset transfers, and activities that aim at promoting equitable access of vulnerable groups to resources can provoke a backlash at the community level and/or changes in household dynamics that increase protection risks. To mitigate this risk: Activity design is based on gender and disability sensitive livelihoods assessments as well as do-no-harm analysis; Field teams will engage with local stakeholders in discussions on necessary actions for creating an enabling environment. (3) Increased inflation. Mercy Corps will conduct market assessments in order to monitor for price increases. MC will coordinate with UN clusters/peer agencies and follow modalities agreed upon by clusters. (4) Key local stakeholders do not have the interest and/or capacity to fully engage in the project. Mercy Corps will conduct a stakeholder mapping at the beginning to identify local stakeholders, who have the capacity and willingness to support interventions designed to promote resilience.
