Project Summary

The project interventions are specifically designed to address the Strategic and Cluster Objectives as outlined in the STHF 2020 Allocation Paper, making use of the resources & expertise, of IHR and their geographic access to the targeted sites in Idleb. The selected population has been targeted in accordance with Severity Indexing provided by the WASH Cluster, which is aimed at ensuring those who are most in need of life-saving assistance are prioritized under this Intervention.
IHR will provide 28,164 IDP's living in 16 settlements and camps in Idleb governorate with basic WASH services, adequate drinking water via water trucking (35 liter per capita per day) while constantly assuring the quality of the water through chlorination and tested, solid waste removal, rubbish bins, storage water tank, desludging latrine septic tanks, address the gap in latrines numbers in the targeted camps by construction 33 new latrines block and making them gender-sensitive and to address the needs of elderly and disabled beneficiaries, additional consumable hygiene kits will be distributed to displaced families in targeted camps, as well as providing the necessary tools for having more clean environment, distribution for motivating personal hygiene items and promoting hygiene awareness sessions in order to increase the awareness level of the population in the targeted camps to prevent the spread of COVID19 and all will be implemented and provided according to the SPHERE and Syrian standards.
The intervention will include provision of the operational cost (fuel, oil, chlorine, and salaries) in Al Omah water station that will support IDP’s in targeted camps to obtain clean and adequate water for drinking and personal hygiene. The supporting of this water station will also result in reducing pressure on the private water vendors and help dependent people to receive water through water-trucking at affordable cost with adequate quantity.
Finally, rehabilitation of the sewage systems in Al Nahda camp that is gradually deteriorating due to damage and lack of maintenance, these networks suffer from partial destruction, which is resulted in sewage water leaks through cracks under the ground, which affects the drinkable water.
IHR will hire female staff in distribution and monitoring teams to facilitate communication and consultation between IHR staff and women and girls. IHR will hire women and men and will provide them with cleaning tools and materials to clean latrines in the targeted camps to sustain those latrines and keep them clean for use by the whole camps.
With the support of OCHA, IHR will ensure the continuation of basic services whilst also increasing access (increase quantity) and quality (meeting Sphere standards) of essential WASH services. Increased focus will be placed on ensuring displaced people have access to appropriate sanitation facilities, the Protection concerns of women and girls are considered, and environmental health and hygiene promotion are enhanced.

The conflict in Syria has exacerbated large-scale humanitarian needs around WASH for the population. Women and girls are affected by a lack of access to WASH services and facilities due to both biological and social factors. The statistic results indicated that the population in camps was were 20,148 individuals 3,358 families. Respondents confirmed that during the past 30 the main source of water days was from the water trucks.55% of respondents were unable to distinguish between water of use and drinking in terms of conservation. Some practices were undertaken to ensure that drinking water was separated from water for hygiene use. Regarding the beneficiaries' access to the water (during the past 30 days) 22% of respondents stated that they had no access to drinking water for a period of four days, 38% for more than four days, while the rest had permanent access. 40% stated that the water amount was sufficient, while 60% said that it was not. They adopted several measures and strategies to adapt to the state of water shortage, such as reducing water use, buying water by debt, drinking water intended for cleaning, reducing expenses for other living purposes in order to buy water, and reducing some personal hygiene practices such as bathing and washing. The average spending of a moderate family (6 members) to secure water per month is about 18500 SP, at an average consumption of 18 liters per day per person.
