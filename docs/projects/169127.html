1 - Needs

The Government of Lebanon (GoL) estimate that approximately 300,000 people have been made homeless as a result of the explosion, with thousands more suffering structural damage to their housings. Multiple medical facilities have also sustained damage and hospitals, which were already under pressure due to COVID-19, have been completely overwhelmed, with casualties reportedly being treated in parking lots or even being turned away. The explosion comes at a time when Lebanon was already in an extremely fragile state. The economic crisis has resulted in the devaluation of the Lebanese Pound (LBP), losing 80% of its value since October 2019. The unofficial exchange rate hovers around 8,000 LBP to the USD, although the official exchange rate remains 1,500 LBP/USD. More than 50% of Lebanese are under the poverty line, with unemployment reaching 30% in May 2020. COVID-19 has exacerbated the situation, with a WFP survey in June 2020 finding that two-thirds of households had experienced a drop in income due to COVID-19, while 40% had incurred debt to buy food or pay rent. Food prices have continued to rise, with annual food inflation reaching 190%. Food insecurity is likely to increase as Beirut port was critical to ensuring the importation of food - Lebanon imports 65-80% of its food, 80% of which was processed through Beirut port. In addition, 15,000 tonnes of wheat (approximately three months' supply) had been stored at the port, which is now contaminated or destroyed. While Tripoli port is an option for receiving imported food stuffs, it does not have the same capacity as Beirut port, nor does it have any strategic storage facilities to compensate for the grain silos destroyed in Beirut. This economic insecurity means that many will not be able to afford to repair their homes themselves, and with imports being impeded, a restricted supply of building materials may result in price increases, further hampering these families’ attempts to carry out their own repairs. In addition to the above needs, there is a risk that the explosion will lead to a spike in COVID-19 cases, placing further pressure on the health system. Due to the widespread damage and destruction of buildings, Shelter has been defined as a key need of the response, with OCHA confirming Mar Mikhael and Karantina as priority neighbourhoods. Early estimates indicate that more than 6,000 buildings have been damaged by the blast, with assessments still taking place to ascertain the true level of damage. In Karantina, according to the ACTED RNA, 80% of dwellings are reportedly destroyed or partially destroyed, with 20% of the population living in a partially damaged building. While 1,000 individuals (mostly Lebanese) were reported to be sheltering in designated evacuation centres, it is estimated that 70% of the population have left the area. In Mar Mikhael, the LRC RNA found that the majority of the damage was to doors and windows, with the majority of assessed households reporting broken glass and damaged window frames and doors. While the Lebanese host community has been deeply affected by recent events, it should be noted that Syrian refugees are especially vulnerable at this time. Many have lost their livelihoods or experienced severe financial insecurity and those lacking legal documentation may be reluctant to approach authorities for medical aid or assistance in locating missing family members.

2 - Activities

- Provision of Rubble Removal and Clearance of houses, through local responders (NGOs, CBOs, local initiatives), AS A FIRST RESPONSE. THIS ACTIVITY WILL BENEFIT RESIDENTS OF THE SEVERAL NEIGHBOURHOODS SUPPORTED BY OFFRE JOIE, A TOTAL OF 5,000 HHS.- Multi-sectorial needs assessment of household and neighbourhood level damage and coordination with the HRC and the Shelter Sector. THE ASSESSMENT IS BUILT ON THE LRC MSNA, WITH COMPONENTS REFLECTING ON HLP ISSUES, CHILD PROTECTION, EDUCATION, LIVELIHOODS, AS WELL AS DETAILED SHELTER WASH BUILDING DAMAGE ASSESSMENTS.- Mapping OF NEEDS FROM A HOLISTIC ANALYTICAL PERSPECTIVE using GIS capacity. THE GEO-ANALYSIS WILL PROVIDE FOR A MULTI-SECTORIAL RESPONSE, BOTH AT HOUSEHOLD AND NEIGHBOURHOOD LEVELS, TO ADDRESS THE SHELTER SECTOR'S SPECIFIC OBJECTIVES.- Rapid Market Assessment TO EVALUATE MARKET CAPACITY TO PROVIDE RESPONSE SCHEMES WITH ESSENTIAL CONSTRUCTION MATERIALS AND ELEMENTS, AND INFORM MARKET-BASED PROGRAMMING, IN THE PROVISION OF MINOR REPAIRS AND MINOR REHABILITATION TO THE AFFECTED HOUSEHOLDS.- Provision of MINOR REPAIRS INCLUDING INSTALLATION OF GLASS PANELS AND FIXING OR REPLACING OF DAMAGED WINDOWS AND DOORS. THE MODALITY WILL BE PROVIDED THROUGH A QUICK-FIX TEAM THAT FABRICATES TAILORED REPAIRS TO THE AFFECTED PROPERTIES IN THE ASSESSED NEIGHBOURHOODS, AND SUPPORTS IN THEIR INSTALLATION AT PROPERTY LEVEL WITHIN DAYS. THIS ACTIVITY WILL TARGET 126 HHS (257 F, 247 M).- PROVISION OF REHABILITATION to shelters including WASH facilities (water tanks AS WELL AS WATER AND SEWAGE CONNECTIONS). THE MODALITY WILL BE APPLIED THROUGH CONTRACTORS, IN LINE WITH THE SECTOR'S RECOMMENDATIONS, AND WILL ADOPT TECHNICAL GUIDELINES FROM THE MINOR REHABILITATION TTC. IMPLEMENTATION WILL TAKE PLACE AT NEIGHBOURHOOD LEVEL COVERING ALL ASSESSED NEEDS IDENTIFIED THROUGH THE THOROUGH MULTI-SECTORIAL ASSESSMENTS CARRIED BY NRC'S OUTREACH TEAMS. THE ACTIVITY WILL BENEFIT 126 HHS (258 F, 247 M).- Emergency rent coverage for alternative accommodation OVER THREE CONSECUTIVE MONTHS: supporting families who are not able to remain in their homes due to structural and severe damages, AS WELL AS ASBESTOS DISPOSAL, AND EVICTIONS TO RELOCATE TO SAFE PROPERTIES. THIS ACTIVITY WILL BENEFIT 250 HHS (510 F, 490 M).- DETAILED TECHNICAL DAMAGE ASSESSMENTS AND EMERGENCY STRUCTURAL STABILISATION. NRC WILL ENSURE BILATERAL COLLABORATION WITH THE UN HABITAT ON THE IDENTIFICATION AND MAPPING OF STRUCTURALLY UNSAFE BUILDINGS, IN COORDINATION WITH OEA AND THE MUNICIPALITIES, FOLLOWED BY A JOINT APPROACH ON ADDRESSING STRUCTURAL STABILISATION AS WELL AS THE TEMPORARY ACCOMMODATION OF HOUSEHOLDS AT RISK. THIS INITIATIVE IS INTENDED TO INFORM THE FORMULATION OF GOOD PRACTICE TECHNICAL GUIDELINES ON STRUCTURALLY UNSOUND BUILDINGS.
