Project Summary

The Big Smile Foundation through Girls Today for Tomorrow III aims at Improving enrolment, retention, and access to continuous, protective education for 5000 school-aged conflict-affected Adolescents and Children (ages 3-17, 4000 Girls and 500 Boys, 1500 Women and Men IDPs, Returnees, and Host community) in Ngala, Dikwa, and Jere Borno State through integrated education and child protection response.
TBSF through this project will address key gaps and educational needs within targeted communities, by working closely with existing Community-based structures (school-based management committee, child protection committee, community groups), key community stakeholders, government, and humanitarian actors, to identify, enroll and support continuous education for girls through referral, followups, advocacy, awareness, and capacity building. identified children will benefit from scholastic materials support, structured life skill programs, and multi-sectoral services through case management. TBSF will provide an ecological approach by working with identified vulnerable children and their families to strengthen children and caregivers bound to mitigate risks and humiliating behaviors faced by children at home, through improving caregivers' knowledge of parental care with parenting skills training and livelihood support to improve the family situation. Implementing schools with benefits from teaching supplies and aids as well as capacity building to improve the safety and quality of education. The project will engage and build the capacity of 100 volunteer teachers on pedagogy, TARL, and classroom management to meet the educational need of children. specific capacity building of community structures, school heads, and community leaders on child protection, school related-Gender based violence, and referral pathway will be conducted to improve safety within the school and community to enhance their well-being and learning resilience, existing TLS and or classrooms / WASH facilities will be renovated and equipped to ensure learning environments are child-friendly, gender-responsive, equitable, disability-friendly and the project also target provision of menstrual hygiene kits for girls.

1 - Needs

After more than a decade of conflict, the humanitarian crisis in the Borno, Adamawa, and Yola states remains one of the most severe in the world today. A total of 8.7 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance across the three crisis-affected states, this includes 2.2 million IDPs, 1.5 million IDPs, 3.9 million host community members, and 700,000 individuals in areas not accessible to humanitarian actors. Of the total persons in need, 80% are women and children. The global percentage of persons with disabilities is 15% and is expected to be higher in locations with conflict. The closure of official camps in Maiduguri municipality by the Borno State Government has resulted in secondary and tertiary displacement and upwards of 150,000 IDPs in Maiduguri will be affected (Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022 Northeast Nigeria) (Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022). In addition, the crisis in Northeast Nigeria exacerbated by COVID-19 has further worsened an already fragile education system and has strengthened barriers and educational gaps faced by girls in the northeast, where more than 4.2 million students are out of school. The National Policy on Education (FME, 2004) provides a compulsory and free education to every child irrespective of gender difference. In pursuant to the provision, various commissions including the Universal Basic Education Commission (UBEC) and a range of federally generated funding mechanisms were established to accelerate the fulfillment of the right to free universal basic Education. Yet, a decade later, over 10 million children, especially girls in Nigeria remain out of school (UNICEF and UNESCO, 2012). Despite the numerous efforts put in to ensure every child receives an education, many especially girls remain out of school. Research has shown that there are several key challenges at individual, household, and community levels that militate against girls’ completion of the education in northern Nigeria. These include the under-valuing of girls’ education by parents, caregivers, and communities; the burden of care and domestic responsibilities which either makes them not go to school or not have time for their homework. In some cases, girls are expected to take care of their younger siblings along to school and look after them during school hours, greatly disrupting their ability to concentrate or learn in school. There are also sociocultural barriers such as early marriage where girls are married off at very young ages because of cultural practices, and therefore are unlikely to return to school. Economic barriers make families take decisions against girls’ education in times of lean resources. Meeting up with the direct and indirect costs of education becomes a huge challenge for families. The increased gender-based barriers and vulnerabilities faced by women and girls due to the ongoing conflict have created limited access to protective education, literacy, and vocational skills training services for young women and girls. The resentment and targeting of formal education by Boko Haram, including the kidnapping of schoolgirls, has resulted in fear among populations living in conflict-affected areas. Access to education has also been historically low with more than one-third of children in the Northeast being out of school. Of those who attend school, 72 percent are unable to read upon completion of sixth grade. In Borno state, which is among the lowest rates in the country, only 35 percent of adolescent girls are literate (Unicef). The findings from JENA 2021 further indicated that poor infrastructure which includes classrooms, school furniture, and WASH facilities was recorded as a critical need and gap. 33% of schools in Borno reported that they were not able to enroll some children into the 20-21 calendar year whilst the most common barrier to Children attending school or enrollment as stated by the report is the inability to afford education material, pay school fees with 46%boy & 47%Girls

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

TBSF has a strong protection mainstreaming policy that guides all our activity and engagement. TBSF will ensure adherence to the policy and the use of the organization activity checklist to ensure in particular protection, gender and disability are mainstream in all activities. TBSF will ensure that all its staff and other stakeholders involved in the delivery of this project undergo adequate training on the four key pillars of protection mainstreaming to ensure that these elements are adequately integrated thought out the project implementation. This will include. 1. Prioritizing safety & dignity, and avoiding causing harm: TBSF will ensure to prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of the planned activities which can increase the affected population's vulnerability to both physical and psycho-social risks 2. Meaningful Access: Planned activities will be implemented in a way that is accessible to the target beneficiaries and in proportion to need and without any barriers (e.g., discrimination). We will ensure to pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services. 2. Accountability: We will ensure to set up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints through a robust complaint and feedback mechanism. – Participation and empowerment: We will support communities through the development of self-protection, and capacities and assist targeted beneficiaries in claiming their rights, including basic human rights, rights to shelter, food, water and sanitation, health, and education, and specialized GBV services including access to justice. All TBSF staff are responsible and will ensure the highest level of protection and gender is well mainstreamed in the project implementation
