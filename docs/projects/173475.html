Project Summary

The project aims to provide a structured and qualitative education to the children from Forcibly Displaced Myanmar community currently taking refuge in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazilla of Cox’s Bazar. To achieve the goal this project targets to provide Non-formal basic education to a total of 7,500 children for the time period. To harmonize the project with gender inequality the targeted population ratio has been divided equally by genders. Where 50% of the total targeted population will be girls and the same will go for boys. To accommodate the number of learners with adequate education facilities this project will use 75 well-built learning centers (LC) in an orderly manner at seven distinctive Rohingya camps in Rajapalong Unioun. In order to provide structured and Bangaldesh Govt. authorized education to the FDMN children, this project has been designed to follow all the guidelines from Education Sector Cox’s Bazar taking from LC constructions to teaching and learning. By keeping the ongoing education system in concern this project thought of bringing something new to the table for its learners which will also be very supportive to distant education system during pandemics like Covid-19. Teachers from both host and FDMN communities will deliver all the lessons through multimedia system in animated and interactive ways. Alongside non-formal education this project will also provide the learners with different types of life-oriented knowledge which will help them keeping their community a better place. The core target of the project is to make learners able to read, play and also keep themselves engaged in different cultural activities by maintaining hygienic latrines and hand wash facilities.

1 - Identified Needs

August 2017 has seen one of the biggest humanitarian crisis of the time. Almost half a Million of people fled their birthplace Myanmar to Bangladesh due to religious and ethnic clash. Among the population the most vulnerable population group was elderly and young citizens. After providing the mass population with emergency fundamental needs there was need of engaging children in education. Although the primary unrest has been resolved with the help of Bangladesh govt. and other global humanitarian organizations. Leading humanitarian organizations like Unicef and UNHCR came forward with education support in all the camps. But there are still a huge number of children out of education. As education has always been a fundamental right of the children, the right deprived Rohingya Children are also among them. This project is targeting 7,500 learners to get education through 75 LCs in different camps. Due to the unavailability of internet in Camps there were no way for the community or learners to access web or facilities like multimedia system. Along with the rest of the world these learners also needs to get introduced to digital teaching and learning process. So keeping that need in mind this project plans to bring out live versions of all the printed lessons through different types of interactivities. The system has already been tested in targeted areas and the anticipation was more than satisfactory. This system will also open new windows to distant education as the materials will be easily accessible from any kind of multimedia supported device from projector to multimedia bar phones. During the Covid-19 pandemic we came to know the importance and possibilities of providing remote supports. Live and interactive lessons alongside workbooks and copies can be a way of distant education if the learners can catch up with the system or idea. As the targeted population is children and they are forever vulnerable to sexual exploitations and abuse. Also the FDMN community is looking for their children’s safety inside Learning Centers. Mukti Cox’s Bazar has already been in a partnership with UNFPA to provide Gender Based Violence support to victim females/girls. Now to cope up of the changing scenarios Mukti Cox’s Bazar has introduced it’s own PSEA wing to ensure reporting link, justice and support for victims and their families.
