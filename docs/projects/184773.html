Project Summary

Medical and psycho-social services available to survivors in the target locations are insufficient, inappropriate and below the expectation of survivors. This coupled with low levels of community awareness on issues of GBV; stigma and other cultural barriers which encourage impunity for breaches in laws and convections. In most of the target locations there has been a steady increase in GBV cases due to escalating conflict especially in Lower Shebelle, Jubaland and Galmudug being one of many active military zones and presence of military frontlines. Increased forced evictions within the camps in Banaadir have also exposed more women and children to the risk of GBV in the camps thus increasing their vulnerability.
In this intervention NoFYL intends to scale up its GBV program to contribute towards enhancing protection, increasing safety, and promoting the dignity of conflict-affected women, men, girls, and boys, in target regions.
Medical and psychosocial services available to survivors in the target locations are insufficient, inappropriate, and below the expectation of survivors. These coupled with low levels of community awareness on issues of GBV stigma and other cultural barriers that encourage impunity for breaches of in-laws and convections.
Through this project NoFYL seeks to strengthen the safe spaces for women to improve protection and to widen support networks, through the deployment of specialized mobile teams composed of Social Worker, Case Manager, Community Health Worker, and Nurses for increased identification and service delivery. Medical support and psychosocial support with components of material support (dignity kits and solar lantern), which will be the backbone of this program.

1. - Projects activities

Activity 1.1.1: - Provision of Comprehensive Case management services to GBV survivors (sexual violence, recruitment, abduction, and maiming), including specialized clinical care for sexual assault survivors (CCSAS), will be provided by trained nurses and case management team within 72 hours. Central to the health service providers will be a strong referral mechanism that will help survivors of GBV in accessing a comprehensive package of integrated services for survivors. Activity 1.1.2: - Provisional of psychosocial support, basic emotional support, and mental health services. Psychosocial support to GBV survivors will be provided by trained counselors and caseworkers who will offer to support if the survivor experiences any post-traumatic incidents. Activity 1.1.3: - Provision of material support through the distribution of dignity, hygiene kits, and solar lanterns as an emergency response targeting women at risk of GBV. This project will provide dignity hygiene kits and solar lanterns to 2000 women and girls of reproductive age within the project locations. Activity 1.1.4: - Provide capacity building to 400 members from GBV AoR members and other NGO staff, service providers (e.g; social workers), and duty-bearers (e.g from the line ministry) particularly, those supporting emergency response teams. Training will be on CMR (4 days targeting 160 participants) SCA, (2 days each targeting 160 participants), and PFA (2 days each targeting 60 participants). The PMS training will target 20 NoFYL staff and, will be led by the protection cluster trainers. Activity 1.1.5: - Adopt the harmonized GBV AoR IEC messaging, print, and distribute through teams of GBV Outreach Workers in the target locations with a target population of 11,700HH combined. Through door-to-door awareness-raising and weekly community outreach, the community caseworkers each will be able to distribute over 12,000 IEC materials on GBV prevention and response for GBV, referral pathways, and available services in the local language. Activity 1.1.6: - Establishment of Women and Girls safe space in each of the target locations according to UNFPA/IRC global standards. 5,120 direct beneficiaries will regularly access the safe spaces through service provisions, 5,883HH will be reached indirectly through access to awareness services through the women and girls safe spaces constructed in the vicinity of their camps. The established safe space will provide a space where women and girls can go to at any time to feel safer and empowered and have access to information, psychosocial support, safety planning and risk reduction, skills-building, dignity kits and solar lantern distributions, literacy and numeracy training, skills training on tie & dye.Activity 1.1.7: - Supports economic empowerment and skills development of vulnerable women - 400 women will benefit in tie and dye skills training for GBV Survivors in target locations. The skills training program puts emphasis on decreasing vulnerability of GBV woman survivors and vulnerable women through participation in livelihood generating activities. The objective is to empower, educate and raise their awareness in the protection measures against GBV and to reintegrate them into the society in a sustainable and dignified ways. Interventions include technical and skill trainings combined with a startup kit at the end of training to enable them to establish small business enterprises, beneficiaries will also undergo literacy and numeracy training.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash or voucher has been selected as the preferred transfer modality for a specific component of this project to use for Livelihoods activities for women and girls at risk of GBV and will aim to help crisis-affected persons improve their economic situation and prevent risky coping strategies that could lead to GBV. Cash or vouchers will be used in case management to survivors of GBV which includes medical and psychosocial care and referrals to CVA when appropriate

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

1- Preventing risks of exclusion based on disability, gender and age. The proposed intervention has been designed in a way to enable beneficiaries to fully access and enjoy their rights, safety and dignity, while at the same time avoiding harm. All project activities will be carried out in accordance with AGDM principles: equal participation of women, girls, boys and men will be integral part of the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the overall project. NoFYL will ensure that key cross-cutting issues are mainstreamed into the proposed intervention including age, gender, diversity, environment and human rights. Gender will remain a key focus, guaranteeing consultation with female beneficiaries when undertaking protection assessments through NoFYL female staff accessing them and identifying their needs. 2- Risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure in targeted project areas: Mitigation measure being the following; - Assess the ownership of land if government owned or private before establishing any facility on the land. - Engage local authorities on the allocation of public land with lease of over 5 years to ensure long term sustainability of projects - On private land ensure agreement is signed with the land owners for a lease of over 5 years to establish community structures like community centres in an IDP camps. - Engage directly with the owners of the land rather than gate keepers to understand land tenure processes. - Validate with the regional authorities tenure documents produced by private land owners to ensure claimed land owners are known before proceeding in any engagement.
