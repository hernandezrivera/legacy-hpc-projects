Project Summary

The Purpose of this WASH intervention is to provide life-saving WASH service to conflict affected & displaced people in Kiltu Kara, Manasibu, Bobo Gembel, Guliso & Gimbi woredas of West Wollega Zone in Oromia Region. The project will be implemented in partnership with a local partner ANE. ANE will implement in two woredas; in Bobo Gambel & Gimbi, whereas WV will implement in the other three woredas.
The project is intended to directly benefit 51,700 individuals including 16,027 girls, 14,993 boys, 10,857 women, and 9,823 men. Out of theses beneficiaries, 6,250 are estimated to be elderly and people with disability.
The project is designed mainly to benefit displaced people due to conflict within Wellega zones, and cross regional from Kamashi of Benshangul Gumuz region who are currently living in the host community in Kiltu Kara, Manasibu, Bobo Gembel, Guliso & Gimbi woredas of West Wollega Zone. The response will also benefit the hosting community through increasing water access thereby reducing burden on the existing infrastructure. The immediate project intervention activities include provision of safe water supply through rehabilitation of water schemes, construction of semi-permanent latrine at health facility & schools and distribution of basic WASH NFIs including dignity kits. This project is designed for a period of 8 months implementation.
To maximize its outcome, the proposed lifesaving project includes WASH services of the communities and institution, mainly focusing on the health care facilities where different health and nutrition service are ongoing. In addition, with the objective to mitigate COVID-19 infection at health institutions and protect health professionals from the infection in emergency setup, the project includes provision of COVID-19 protection WASH supplies for five health care facilities. WV will ensure the project implementations is within the humanitarian standards, protection and accountability principles in emergency settings. Vulnerable, especially women, girls, elderly and people with disability will be highly considered for participation and consultation during all the project cycle for maximum benefit from the project.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

This project will incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity in all phases of the project, by practicing Humanitarian Program Accountability Frame (PAF) with its four major pillars; information sharing, active participation, consultation, compliant handling and feedback mechanism. Accordingly, the project is designed by engaging community members through their representatives such as community leaders and religious leaders, concerned government bodies and other partners. During startup of the program, information about the program, beneficiary selection criteria, and information on their entitlement, the beneficiary’s right to raise any complaints and complaining mechanisms will be provided for beneficiaries and will continue to be communicated to promote transparency. Location, design, safety, appropriateness and convenience of water and sanitation facilities will be decided in consultation with the users particularly women, adolescent girls, elderly and persons with disabilities. Open public meetings and public information boards will be used to share information. Towards employing the DO NO HARM principle prior to implementing project activities a risk assessment will be conducted to ensure beneficiaries and participants do not incur further harm and an action will be taken to mitigate any resulting from an activity being implemented by this project. The project implementation and monitoring process will involve the community including children, women, older people and people with disabilities, government and other stakeholders so that individuals have an adequate and equal opportunity to voice their concerns and to express their preferences. A community based complaint and feedback mechanism will be established and implemented throughout the project. WVE and ANE (Action for the Needy in Ethiopia) will consult and engage relevant stakeholders during the design of the community based complaint and feedback mechanism in order to ensure support, commitment, sustainability, community trust and ownership. The reporting mechanism will involve assigning focal person to record and follow up on any complaints received. Locked complaint boxes will be prominently displayed and regularly checked to collect feedback and to take appropriate measures. Moreover, Community help desk (CHD) will also be established. Complaint from beneficiaries will come through the community help desk and corrective actions will be taken where feedback indicates that something is not working well. Complaints and relevant feedbacks (as appropriate) will be acknowledged in a timely manner and feedbacks and personal data will be handled confidentiality. This will give opportunity to involve beneficiaries in project management by incorporating their inputs. WVE and ANE will also assess, learn about and manage the performance of this project. In addition, in project monitoring stage feedback will be gathered from beneficiaries. All staff of WVE and ANE will familiarize themselves with prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) policies through orientation and trainings, as they are required to uphold them and put them into action.Protection Mainstreaming and GBVThe project mainstreams protecting children, women, men and other vulnerable groups like the elderly and people with disabilities. Hence, this project is designed in a way that activities target the most vulnerable, enhance safety and dignity, and promote and protect the human rights of the beneficiaries without contributing to or perpetuating discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect and exploitation. Protection is mainstreamed throughout the proposed WASH activities. WVE and ANE will demonstrate and assess the overall situation of the targeted population, identify possible threats and vulnerabilities, which may trigger additional risks by planned intervention, and assess the community’s capacities and mechanisms to cope with those risks.
