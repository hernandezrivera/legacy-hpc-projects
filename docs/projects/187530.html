Project Summary

The program objective is to reduce harmful practices against women and girls because of continuing instability through providing lifesaving, specialized, quality interventions for GBV prevention, mitigation, and response, such as case management, psychosocial support, referral pathways, empowering women and girls, capacity building, and the integration of reproductive health (family planning only). Incorporating RH into GBV programming is the expertise of the MF.
This project increases measures to mitigate the risk of GBV in the humanitarian response in areas of increased need due to large-scale. • Integrating reproductive health and family planning into GBV services, through individual counseling, awareness-raising sessions, family planning materials, and referrals to other health services, for women and adolescent girls especially at Widows camps
• the needs and protection of PWDs and adolescent girls, as well as the elderly, empowering them and benefiting from their experiences, and providing activities for girls using curricula designed for them.
The project proposes to provide outreach, awareness-raising, and GBV services (the GBV tools) through two Safe Spaces for Women and Girls (WGSS), Jasmine 2 (located in Atmeh camp and owned by Maram Foundation), and Jasmine 4, so MF will coordinate with the GBV sub-cluster as needed and the services available within the area if a new center will be established
(it is likely will be established in Aqrabat). Through these Centers, Maram Foundation will provide GBV services in Idleb Governorate, Harem District, Al Dana sub-district.
Additionally, MF will establish a mobile team service to reach larger catchment areas and beneficiaries who are unable to visit the center. The project targets IDPs, host communities, widows’ camps (13 camps), widows’ families who were reallocated to other locations, and a camp with foreign widows (35 families) in these targeted locations. The women and girls’ safe spaces (WGSS) also have child-care spaces as deﬁned by UNFPA guidelines, ensuring a fully participatory approach of the female beneﬁciaries.
Through the centers and mobile teams, Maram will provide services (GBV tool kits) and ensure coverage of specific locations as follows:
• Provide specialized GBV services to survivors, including case management and individual psychosocial support.
• Women and girls’ empowerment by vocational training. in addition to awareness-raising services that are offered to all groups using the GBV "awareness-raising toolkit."
• Provide GBV programming for adolescents and youth girls for life skills using the guides dedicated to this category" Safety My Health," "Psychodrama for girls," and " I deal with teenagers."
• WGSS Center offers recreational activities, to build community support and trusted relationships.
• Provision of reproductive health services (family planning) linked to family planning by midwives.
• Boys, men, people with special needs, and persons unable to reach through mobile teams will be targeted to provide a GBV awareness-raising toolkit, and PSS activities
With regards to capacity-building, will be carried out in two phases:
• Training the project team (Psychological First Aid [PFA], PSS, SOPs, Case management, PSEA, awareness-raising toolkit)
• Training humanitarian workers in other sectors on (GBV basic, PFA, Referral pathways, and PSEA)

4 - Needs

According to the HNO and the voices from Syria 2022, about 582213 BNFs need GBV services at Idlib-Aldana According to the need assessment and 26 FGDs in 13 widows camps(for 60 women 30 and 50 and 60 ages, children ages between 10 and 13) conducted by MF, the most important CP issues that need attention are child marriage 23%, child labor 18%, neglect 15%, domestic violence 13%, financial exploitation 12%, family separation 8% and Children Associated with Armed Forces and Armed Groups (CAAFAG) 8% and Unaccompanied children 3%. These issues are a result of poverty, lack of awareness about CP risks and general information about CP Male children are allowed to stay with their mothers until the age of 14, after that, they must leave the camp, making them vulnerable to all possible CP risks. Women in some camps are not allowed to work, and they can leave camp for only 2 hours every day. many psychological and social problems are suffered by widows, also due to their isolation from society and the feeling that they are not equal. More pressure on adolescent girls, as they are almost completely suffering from a lack of freedom, movement, and decision making widow's camps are a closed and very sensitive community, it will be very risky to engage the community out of the camps, so MF will establish women committees inside the camps and provide raising awareness in surrounding areas of widows camps to enhance the understanding of gender, GBV and widows in the society in future

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: the populations will be hesitant or unwilling to engage on this topic. Possible increased reporting of SEA with increased awareness about beneficiary rights and reporting mechanisms. Risk: the potential for the very difficult working environment in integrating the SOPs and best practices of PSEA due to the sensitive nature of the topic in the culture, and/or other risks include facing increased reporting of SEA without adequate capacity to respond. Maram foundation team will be conducted PSEA Awareness-raising and information-sharing efforts and be scaled up where possible, by respective PSEA focal points supported by the PSEA Networks program and by the Maram foundation with the presence inside Syria to communicate information on PSEA. At the least, The following are standards for the affected communities will be informed on: I. The definition of sexual exploitation and abuse. II. The standards of conduct for humanitarian workers. III. Humanitarian workers have a right to humanitarian assistance without being subjected to SEA IV. Where to report SEA incidents (all possible options/channels). V. What services are available, including complaint confidentiality assessments, and how to access them. VI. What to expect after making a complaint, including potential referrals, timeframes, and the roles, responsibilities, and any limitations of actors involved. VII. What steps the Maram and the concerned agency will take to ensure safety and confidentiality.
