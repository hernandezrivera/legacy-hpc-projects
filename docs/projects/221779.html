Project Summary

Between September and November 2024, food insecurity is projected to reach alarming levels, with a staggering 6.3 million individuals—representing 47 per cent of the examined population—expected to grapple with a Crisis or worse. This is not merely a future concern but an urgent issue requiring immediate attention. Within this number, 1.71 million people are anticipated to face critical levels of acute food insecurity, classified as IPC Phase 4 (Emergency). Even more concerning is the estimated 41,000 individuals who are confronting catastrophic levels of acute food insecurity, classified as IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe).
Among those enduring IPC Phase 5, approximately 10,000 individuals are located in Malakal County, within Upper Nile State, while around 31,000 South Sudanese returnees, who fled ongoing conflict in Sudan, find themselves in similarly dire straits. This alarming data illustrates a significant increase of about 500,000 individuals facing acute food insecurity, signalling a dire need for humanitarian intervention.
The barriers to accessing healthcare in the country are equally troubling. A staggering fifty-eight per cent of Bomas lack community health teams, which are crucial for delivering essential healthcare services. Furthermore, only 59 per cent of the population can reach health facilities within an hour, and 80 per cent report encountering barriers when attempting to access necessary health services. The immune coverage remains critically low, with only 49 per cent of children receiving the Penta 3 vaccine, leaving them vulnerable to vaccine-preventable diseases.
Compounding the health crisis are the losses of agricultural livelihoods due to catastrophic flooding and sub-national conflicts. These environmental and social disruptions have left many households unable to meet their basic health needs, thrusting them into desperate conditions that demand humanitarian assistance. Malaria, which remains the leading cause of morbidity and mortality, accounts for a staggering 52.6 per cent of health issues faced by the population. The determinants of health outcomes and critical needs stretch across various sectors, including food, nutrition, shelter, protection, water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH). It is also essential to incorporate mental health and psychosocial support services in Panyikang County in Upper Nile State and Canal/Pigi in Jonglei State, target 9,854. Above 40927 and 4,000 Female
The devastation of WASH facilities, coupled with insufficient hygiene and sanitation services, as well as a severe lack of access to clean drinking water, has further aggravated the health conditions within communities. Consequently, the risk of waterborne diseases such as diarrhoea, cholera, and Hepatitis E has increased significantly. The proliferation of floodwaters will likely lead to a surge in vector-borne diseases like malaria. Overcrowding in various parts of the country, driven by the presence of internally displaced persons and damage to shelters caused by conflict and natural disasters, is also a significant contributor to the spread of infectious diseases, including Hepatitis E, alongside elevated levels of malnutrition.

This alarming shift is attributed to escalating morbidity rates and the limited availability of health services, which make access to care increasingly challenging. Effort to engage communities and identify barriers to healthcare access are of paramount importance, particularly in remote and underserved regions where resources are scarce. A national prevalence study carried out in 2022 painted a grim picture regarding violence against women in South Sudan. Many women suffer from multiple forms of violence—ranging from physical and sexual abuse to psychological harm and economic exploitation—as well as forced marriages that violate their dignity and health. Moreover, ongoing conflict has led to disturbingly high rates of depression, signaling a mental health crisis that demands immediate intervention. To effectively address these issues, the establishment of technical support and coordination mechanisms at all levels is essential. This will help ensure that humanitarian health services are not only available but also tailored to meet the intricate needs of the affected populations. Women and children are particularly vulnerable, facing elevated risks of sexual violence and exploitation, often at the hands of smugglers. This environment severely restricts their ability to navigate urban areas safely in search of essential services.At present, access to sexual and reproductive health services, as well as gender-based violence (GBV) health services, is fraught with challenges related to accessibility, availability, and quality. The provision of birth spacing options in family planning services is almost non-existent within public healthcare facilities, with most services available only in the private sector, thereby limiting access for low-income families. Female refugees encounter substantial obstacles in accessing public GBV services, further exacerbating their vulnerability in an already precarious situation. Mental health services remain virtually non-existent outside major urban areas, with around 80% of districts lacking any form of mental health consultation services. 2. Project activities

Epidemic Disease Surveillance, Prevention, and Control: This involves a comprehensive approach to monitor and manage health threats by detecting outbreaks early, investigating their causes, and implementing rapid response strategies to control their spread.- Vaccination Initiatives:We prioritize immunization against vaccine-preventable diseases. This includes procuring essential commodities for outbreak responses and ensuring the availability of emergency antigens needed for effective immunization. Additionally, we maintain a robust cold chain system to preserve vaccine efficacy.- Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) in Health Facilities:To enhance healthcare safety, we conduct regular assessments of health facilities using detailed scorecards. We ensure that facilities are equipped with necessary supplies and adhere to established protocols while continuously monitoring Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) standards in these environments.- Community Health Services:We connect community health service initiatives with facility-based care through programs like Boma Health Initiatives and Integrated Community Case Management (ICCM). This approach ensures that essential health services are accessible at the community level.- Life-Saving Healthcare Services: Our commitment extends to providing essential healthcare services that save lives, particularly for managing non-communicable diseases. This is achieved through both static health facilities such as Primary Healthcare Centers (PHCC) and Primary Healthcare Units (PHCU), as well as through mobile healthcare units. We ensure the availability of essential medical kits and provide training for health workers to enhance service delivery.- Technical and Operational Support to Health Facilities: We offer practical assistance to health facilities by conducting supportive supervision visits. This ensures high-quality health service provision and fosters accountability to the communities we serve. Regular community consultative meetings facilitate feedback and confirmation of compliance with health service standards across all supported facilities.- Health Reporting and Monitoring: We maintain effective reporting mechanisms through Health Management Information Systems (HMIS) and District Health Information Software (DHIS2). Our initiatives include compiling Health Cluster 5W monthly reports and Early Warning Alert and Response System (EWARS) or Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR) weekly reports to stay informed on health trends.- Promotion of the Humanitarian-Peace-Development Nexus: We actively work to unite humanitarian, peace, and development actors. By fostering collaboration and synergy among these sectors, we aim to maximize resource utilization and improve overall outcomes.- Monitoring Key Performance Indicators:We track several critical health indicators to assess our impact: - The percentage of healthcare facilities that meet all World Health Organization (WHO) IPC minimum requirement targets. - The coverage rate of PENTA3 vaccinations among children under one year, detailed by dministrative units. - The density of community health workers per 1,000 individuals in the population. - The frequency of healthcare consultations received per person annually, disaggregated by sex, age, and disability. - The rate of skilled birth attendance during deliveries. - The case fatality rate for malaria, categorized by age and gender. - The percentage of reported health alerts that are verified within the first 24 hours. - The success rate of reactive measles vaccinations for boys and girls aged 6 months to 15 years. - The number of health facilities that have received Interagency Emergency Health Kits (IEHK). - The number of Sexual and Gender-Based Violence (SGBV) survivors who receive Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) services, disaggregated by age, sex, and disability status
