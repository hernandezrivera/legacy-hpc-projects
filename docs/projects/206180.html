Project Summary

South Sudan is facing complex multifaceted protection crisis with 5.53 million people in need of protection services in 2024 being in severe need. The humanitarian situation is driven by severe climatic shocks, economic meltdown, and frequent conflicts resulting in increased displacement.
With populations remaining displaced, people are unable to access food, safe drinking water, livelihood activities, protection information and assistance. In particular, returnees affected by Sudan Crisis are too far from full recovery and rebuilding of their lives. This situation has resulted to increased reliance on humanitarian assistance.
Similarly, recurrent subnational violence and localized violence among community-based militias or civil defense groups in the proposed areas have displaced civilians, exposing communities, particularly children, women, persons with specific needs, people with disabilities, elderly and other vulnerable community groups to multiple protection risks and vulnerabilities, including grave violations such as killing, injury, family separation, GBV/CRSV, sexual exploitation and abuse, targeting of civilians, revenge attacks and killings, cattle rustlings, burning of house and property, restriction of freedom of movement, forced recruitment, abductions, constraints on safe and equitable access to services, housing, land and property, and other protection risks associated with negative coping mechanisms. This exacerbated the scale and urgency of these needs. Persons with specific needs (PSNs) are particularly affected in such emergencies where they face physical and psychosocial threats, including, risks of drowning, behind left behind during flights, sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) and other extreme shocks. The PSNs tend to lack food, shelter/NFIs, medicines, and assistive devices.
CINA will therefore, reach 16,000 persons of concern (5,600 men, 7,200 women, 2,560 girls, 2,240 boys) including 2,400 persons with disabilities with timely lifesaving protection services including, Protection assessments and monitoring; Psychosocial support services and access to documentation; Protection awareness and prevention messaging; Peacebuilding and peaceful coexistence; Strengthening protection monitoring systems within the community; and Prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience.
CINA will employ a number of approaches including static programming with field-based roving response in the proposed locations and use of cash assistance for comprehensive case management in line with protection principles. To ensure centrality of protection, protection analysis will be done in collaboration with Protection Cluster, to inform the Do No Harm and protection, conflict sensitive and child-centred approaches to other sectors’ programming and response and inclusion of protection aspects during data collection. Community engagements will be ensured through regular consultations on feedback, complain boxes, hotlines and streamlining of complain and feedback mechanisms, incorporated in the project design and implementation and coordinated with Communication and Community Engagement Working Group. AAP and protection mainstreaming will be upheld, including SEA prevention, survivor assistance, accountability and investigations, with technical support sought from PSEA Task Force.
CINA has a strong static presence and access to the proposed locations, currently implementing child protection, GBV, EORE and education programs and operates field offices, with qualified personnel in Malakal, Renk, Nasir, Awerial and Yirol. CINA owns two new motor speedboats, currently stationed in Malakal and providing access to locations, including their surrounding islands. This project will therefore require 400,000 USD to provide timely and unhindered protection services in the proposed crises affected locations.

1. Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan is unprecedented. Over 420,000 people are estimated to be returning from Sudan due to war. There is political instability, with growing uncertainty about political process to achieve peace, which is likely to cause more localized and subnational violence, resulting in more civilian displacement. This is exacerbated by lack of local government capacity to ensure safety, security and access to basic services, exposing vulnerable populations to increased protection risks, mainly affecting women, girls, adolescents, elderly, persons with disabilities, and other vulnerable groups, who continue to suffer violence, loss of lives, psychosocial distress, GBV, SEA and other protection threats related to negative coping mechanisms. Lakes, Upper Nile and Jonglei top the list of states in South Sudan where women and girls mentioned that their main protection concern is conflict and displacement which does not allow them access to their preferred market and violate their rights. Revenge attack sand killings, road ambushes and cattle related violence continue to prevail although there is relative but fragile stability witnessed in recent months due to peace efforts by the new state government established in 2022. IRNA reports in October 2023 show immense protection needs in the islands. About 58,069 new arrivals from Sudan have been recorded since August 2023. Undoubtedly, in 2023 and moving into 2024, subnational violence continue to rise especially in Northern Jonglei and Upper Nile. A deadly attack to Tonga in February 2023 claimed many lives, with populations currently displaced to islands and Malakal PoC. On the 7th March 2023, Kodok was attacked resulting in multiple deaths, casualties and loss of livelihoods. These violent atrocities are likely to overturn the recent peace gains between the communities in Jonglei and Upper Nile. Absence of political intervention, peace dialogues and support to the affected people’s recovery weakened the protective environment and undermined people’s coping capacities, with over 60% of IDPs intending not to go back to their areas of origin until government provide adequate security. When such conflicts and crises ensue, the most vulnerable people including children, women, adolescents, youth, the persons with disabilities, elderly, pregnant and lactating mothers, etc. suffer greater risks and vulnerabilities. The persons with specific needs usually lack access to protection information, livelihood opportunities, food, medical care, legal aid and counselling, shelter and NFIs and psychosocial support. Tensions over land issues and other community resources compound existing forms of intercommunal violence, especially against women whose HLP rights are often violated. GBV against women and girls and PWDs become rampant. The child survivors of violence(including unaccompanied and separated children and other vulnerable children) usually lack appropriate responsive services to survive and preventative and resilience services to cope. Needs of PWDs are often neglected while they suffer most.Urgent action is therefore required to deliver the desperately needed protection assistance in line with protection cluster objectives for HRP 2024 in the proposed priority locations.

2. Project activities

1. Protection and monitoring system will be conducted to inform protection and overall humanitarian response. This will be done through static programming in the state and field-based roving response in hard-to-reach areas. Protection and monitoring systems will be strengthened within the community. Findings and recommendations will be shared with protection cluster for dissemination.2. Psychosocial support and access to documentation will be provided to individuals, groups, specialized PSS and support to caregivers of persons with specific needs.3.Protection awareness and prevention messaging. This will be conducted with aim to prevent and mitigate protection risks through an integrated approach of harmonized key messaging informed by the findings from the protection monitoring analysis. Protection concerns or specific needs of people will be reached with protection awareness raising messaging.4. Peacebuilding and peaceful coexistence. This will be achieved through conducting peacebuilding awareness raising activities and training of community based protection networks (CBPN) and Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) committees on conflict resolution, mitigation, mediation and peace building. Particularly, youth will be involved as guardians for peace building in the community. Youth volunteers will be trained to provide community awareness and reporting protection incidents, including those affecting persons with disabilities and work as caretakers and champions for vulnerable groups of households with disability to voice and safeguard their needs. 5. Prevention and mitigation of protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience. Based on existing CINA's cash programming experience (as a member of Cash Working Group), the use of standard vulnerability criteria and protection risk analysis, the persons with specific needs (PSNs) will receive protective cash-based assistance in connection with safe, dignified, and voluntary return. This will be provided as part of individual protection assistance (IPA), case management and safe referrals for PSNs.
