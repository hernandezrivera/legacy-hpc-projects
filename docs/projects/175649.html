Project Summary

The project will target vulnerable populations in Awiel South- NBeG (Malek alel, Panthou and Warthou) and Terekeka (Terekeka town, Tombek and Muni- CES where most affected populations; Internally Displaced Persons, returnees and host communities are experiencing sexual and gender based violence mostly rape, sexual assaults, physical assaults, sexual abuse, child marriages and various IPV forms placed upon them due to flood situations, armed conflict, displacement and limited humanitarian response. The one year GBV interventions will focus on increasing women and girls access to lifesaving services, provide robust psychosocial support (PSS), GBV case management and enhancing protection services with focus on GBV risk reduction activities. MaCDA will conduct safety audits in order to understand the GBV trends and ensure risk mitigation measures are put in place through advocacy by relevant clusters and stakeholders. Establish, train and supervise community based protection networks CBPNs to increase women and girls safe access to friendly spaces information about GBV risks, services, risk mitigation, IGA and SRH information. Provide a platform for conducting community dialogues on protection and GBV related topics, development of community based risk mitigation plans, GBV prevention messaging (early and forced marriages, girl child education). Establishment of four women and girls friendly spaces for vulnerable women and adolescent girls to access information on both prevention and response services with focus on healing and restoring hope, acceptance, normalcy and enhancing resilience in women and their children. Strengthen the provision of quality case management services in a timely, effective and efficient manner through establishment of efficient and defined GBV referrals pathways for survivors. Participate in Coordination meetings, GBVWG and services mapping.
Build the capacity of community protection focal points and frontline service providers social workers, community volunteers, police and health workers, chiefs and women group on basic GBV guiding principles and referral pathway, GBV in emergency training, GBV case management using IASC guidelines.
Due to COVID19 pandemic and impact on women and girls, vulnerable groups including men, MaCDA will put in place risk mitigation measures as related to COVID19 while responding. The trainings, group and outreach activities will be conducted in line with WHO/Health cluster guidelines and following government directives, in an effort to preventing the spread of COVID19.
Direct beneficiaries are those who will receive trainings from MaCDA directly (e.g. frontline service providers, community GBV focal points, weekly group activities). Capacity building of service providers 132, weekly group activities 4,500 people, distribute Dignity kits to 1,000 people, fuel efficient clean cooking stoves 1,200 people, PSNs supported through PSS and case management services 300 People, 630 people received livelihoods support/ emergency material support through risk reduction activities and 1500 people supported through PSS and case management services at the WGFs. Overall total number of direct beneficiaries is 9,712 individuals
The project is designed to be implemented in Awiel South which is 50% and Terekeka 50%.
Indirect beneficiaries. These are the community members composed of Men, Women, Boys and Girls who received key awareness on GBV prevention messages through mass media-Radio, small medium awareness events and advocacy including door to door visits outside the targeted locations ( payams, Bomas and villages ) of Awiel South and Terekeka.

1 - Needs

Protection risks in Terekeka and Aweil South counties escalated in the last half of 2020 due to conflict, flooding and food shortage. In Terekeka, flooding has affected and displaced 1,354 Households (9,485 persons) in Tombek and Muni Payams (IRNA Report, September 2020). In the same county, conflict erupted in November and December 2020 leading to the displacement of 9,740 Households (46,306 individuals), destruction of properties, disruption of livelihoods and abuse of human rights (IRNA Report, December 2020). According to 2021 Integrated Phase Classification Report for South Sudan, Terekeka County has an estimated 50419 persons who are classified as emergency (IPC4) between January and July 2021. The South Sudan Gender Base Violence has an estimated of 21,579 individuals who are in dire need of GBV services in the county. In Aweil, flooding displaced more than 7483 Households (44, 898 persons) in the last quarter of 2021 (International Federation of Red Crescent Report, 2020). The IPC Report for 2021 has indicate that 34, 621 persons are in crisis (IPC4) and another 13, 848 in catastrophe (IPC5). Additionally, 22, 764 individuals are in great need of GBV services in Aweil South. As a result of flooding, displacement, Covid-19 and worsening food insecurity, the humanitarian situation is very risky for women, girls and children living in displaced and non-displaced settings in Terekeka and Aweil South. Incidences of rape have been reported and cases of physical violence are on the rise. Displaced women and girls who have ran away from their homes without enough clothes and menstrual hygiene materials are forced to put on the same clothes and adopt risky behaviours to manage their menses such as using old clothes and rags. Such practices not only risks their health but also compromises women and girl’s dignity and privacy. In displacement sites, women and girls are living in crowded spaces which deprives them privacy and exposes them to GBV. A service mapping conducted by humanitarian agencies during assessments in Terekeka and Aweil South show that there are no enough GBV response services and functional community based protection mechanisms available for the IDPs and non IDPs in the two counties. As a result of worsening economic crisis due to Covid-19 and deteriorating food insecurity, the vulnerability of women and girls will continue to worsen. Women and girls IDPs who are concentrated in one place and with no income and food are likely to be sexually exploited as they make effort to look for food to fend for their families. In the current crisis, women and girls continue to travel long distances in search of water, food and firewood. Water points, forests, military and police checkpoints and routes to distribution points are hotspots where rape, harassment and physical and sexual assault of women and girls occur. Even as women and girls continue to face significant protection and GBV risks, lack of effective community based protection networks, limited psychosocial services and weak referrals pathways have limited women’s ability to access GBV information and services. In light of these gaps in Aweil South and Terekeka, MaCDA seeks to provide GBV services in the counties in form of building the capacity of GBV case Workers and Health Providers, advocacy, CMR, Psychosocial Support Services, distribution of dignity kits, establishing and strengthening Community Protection Structures , developing and strengthening referral pathways and establishment of Women and Girls Friendly Space. Proposed services are essential to protect women and girls and also to save lives.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

To ensure feedback from people with disability, MaCDA has tailored its feedback mechanism to allow for personalized approach when collecting feedback from disabled persons. This include home visits, specially organized meetings with disabled persons and by contacting either the caregivers who may be family members or friends to gather information and views about the kind of assistance provided to people under their care. To ensure feedback from women and girls, MaCDA organizes small and separate focus group discussions with women and girls in order to obtain feedback on type, timeliness and quality of assistance provided. Focus Group Discussion are often facilitated by females in order to give confidence to women and girls to come forward with their feedback. In order to allow feedback from illiterate members of the community, MaCDA has pictorials of its Feedback Mechanism showing step by step approaches in submitting Feedback. MaCDA also trains locally hired staff on the Feedback Mechanism who can be able to communicate using local dialect to their communities while receiving feedback.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

In this project, MaCDA through its Safeguarding Officer will conduct regular sensitization and awareness-raising on the feedback mechanism. During sensitization and awareness raising on Feedback mechanism, MaCDA’s Safeguarding Officer will provide Information about MaCDA ( who we are, what activities we are doing in that community, and the behaviour they can expect from our project team); Contact details for the MacDA head office, why feedback is important for MaCDA; Who can provide feedback and report concerns; What type of feedback and concerns can be reported using the Feedback Mechanism, the principle of non-retaliation, privacy and impartiality. The Safeguarding Officer will also provide specific details on how various Feedback Mechanisms such as feedback box will be checked, frequency of community meetings or women, child and disabled friendly feedback discussions will take place; What happens when someone has given feedback (Privacy of information received, how the information will be stored, who Is authorized to receive the information, an estimation of how long feedback will be provided and avenues MaCDA will use to provide feedback).

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

MacDA humanitarian assistance adheres to principles of safety, access, dignity, empowerment, and inclusion. In order to meet the expectations of donors and affected population, MaCDA has a robust, proactive, women and child friendly feedback mechanism embedded in all of its projects. MaCDA feedback mechanism is meant to serve four purposes including: improving planning, implementation and evaluation of activities; is a means for the affected populations (beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries) to hold MaCDA accountable; to make adjustments and adaptation to activities in order to meet the specific needs of the beneficiaries and to ensure accountability between MaCDA and its donors. MaCDA feedback mechanism is used throughout project implementation. MaCDA has a Safeguarding Officer hired for each project whose role is to ensure functionality of the Feedback Mechanism. All complaints or feedback are received through several complaints channels such as a free toll number, feedback and complaint desks, and suggestion boxes and are maintained and responded to by the Safeguarding Officer. To ensure increased participation of affected population in addressing complaints arising during implementation, MaCDA will train a minimum of 15 individuals (8 Females) from the host community and affected persons on procedures and avenues they can use to submit and receive complaints. Including local community in the feedback mechanism is meant to increase the level of reporting any complaints that may arise during implementation process. All complaints or feedback received are processed appropriately, timely and fairly while ensuring utmost confidentiality, privacy and safety of complainants and those affected. A log is used to feed and track all complaints and is securely stored in soft and hard copies. Soft copies are password protected while all hard copies are kept in a secure folder and a lockable cabinet. Feedback Register is reviewed on weekly basis to ensure any open complaints or feedback are being addressed, processed and closed appropriately and the log is being kept updated. Key learnings arising from received complaints or feedback are extracted monthly and shared with Project Team for wider organisational learning. Beneficiaries and communities, especially the most marginised and vulnerable are periodically consulted to ensure they are satisfied the complaint and feedback mechanism is accessible, effective, confidential and safe.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

MaCDA staff work on principles of humanity, human rights, respect and do no harm. These principles are articulated in MaCDA’s PSEA Policy and the Code of Conduct. All forms of exploitation, abuse or harassment (including sexual, physical, and verbal) by MaCDA staff or representatives are prohibited. MaCDA will provide information about its PSEA policy during inception training with community leaders and local authorities, during the training of Accountability to affected population and project enumerators, during meetings with community members, leaders and beneficiaries and by use of brochures distributed in the community and banners which will be prominently displayed during meetings, trainings and at all project sites. MaCDA is a protection as well as GBV partner and is a member of referral pathway in the project locations. As such, MaCDA will also provide information and contact details of existing referral pathway at respective project locations to beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries to enable them seek assistance of need on need to need basis.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

MaCDA usually incorporates protection risk analysis, mitigation measures into its project design, monitoring and implementation. By involving all beneficiaries groups in the planning, implementation and monitoring of humanitarian services delivery. Making consultation with different groups separately especially in a situation where certain group of people are being intimidated of their opinion and voices. This is but not limited to women, children, people with disability, youth, male counterparts, and minority groups. Identify protection partners on ground and if available specific GBV focal points, child protection and monitoring inclusion of groups identified as vulnerable groups. MaCDA will conduct a protection and conflict mapping exercises to understand the community contexts in which humanitarian services is delivered, identify access problems, threats and act of violence , presence of armed military and man- made issues such as military installation, check points that could hinder access into the area. Inclusion of the both host communities and IDPs returnees into the humanitarian aid assistance based on vulnerability criteria selection MaCDA will appoint both male and female staff to help in identify vulnerable persons for humanitarian aid support will establish PSEA focal points in the project sites for the purpose of receiving complains, concerns and feedback by taking practical steps in addressing them MaCDA will consult with the community on appropriateness of time for conducting activities which should be done during day time and not at night, MaCDA will establish the women and girls friendly spaces in an accessible locations to benefit the women and girls.
