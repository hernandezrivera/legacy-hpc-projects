Project Summary

The project has the goal of sustaining access to education in emergencies by boys and girls affected by drought, flooding and conflicts in Galgadud (Cadado, Abudwak, Dhusamareeb), Mudug (Galkacyo, Hobyo) regions. The project target to provide direct educational support to 5 833 students in 30 schools within the two regions, and the emergency support will not only improve access but also ensure protective learning environment for both girls and boys. Hunger is one of the reasons for school drop outs, poor attendance and generally low performance by the student, as such the project seeks to address the negative effects of hunger through school feeding during school days in the 30 targeted schools. School feeding will retain children in schools since they will get enough to eat, ability to concentrate and it also increase their enthusiasm to resume and continue their daily class lessons. In addition, the project seeks to support emergency water supply in targeted schools through installation of water systems in schools. This will improve access to safe water in schools for both children and school staff. Draught has caused water shortages and forced children to drop out of school and also to drink unsafe water. Access to water and sanitation facilities will create a safe, conducive and protective learning environment for children. Similarly, the project will undertake minor rehabilitation of school blocks to improve learning environment. The project will also address child protection needs in schools through conducting awareness raising, psychosocial activities in child friendly spaces and enabling community structures able to [prevent and respond to child abuses. Furthermore, the project seeks to ensure vulnerable children/youth are engaged in life-saving learning that promotes personal well-being and social cohesion, this will build the resilience of the child, improve learning and development of the child. The wellbeing of the child will be enhanced through supporting children with scholastic materials necessary during learning, motivating teachers through training and teacher incentives. This will improve their capability to handle the social-physical needs of the child. The project also aims at strengthening the capacity of the education system to deliver effective and coordinated education in emergencies, preparedness and response within the education system through teacher training, establishment of Community Education Committees (CECs) and strengthening regional education cluster systems.

Needs

Somalia still remains among countries with the highest global out of school children rates as a result of a weakened education infrastructures and systems due to decades of neglect, conflicts and recurring national disasters such as drought that culminated in widespread population displacement and the paralysis of social services including Education. Access has been historically low with more than 3 million children out of schools in Somalia and a national Gross Enrolment Ratio (GER) for primary education at 30% for primary level and 26% for secondary level. The 2018 Somalia Joint Needs Assessment report indicates the national level dropout rate of host community around 30% and IDPs around 17%. Galmudug MoE recent assessments indicate that the total enrollment in Galgadud and Mudug regions dropped by 13.3% and 19.8 % respectively as a result of the recent and prevailing drought conditions that have led to major displacements in the region. Hunger and water shortages in schools has forced children out of school. Conflict has resulted in the destruction of school blocks while clan tensions and cultural practices continue to violet the rights of children including early marriages, forced recruitment of boys into armed groups. The girl child remains less favored to enroll in school compared to a boy child and girl child has more roles at home than a boy which tend to affect their school attendance. Access to sanitary wear for girls is a challenge which causes absence in school by girls. Boys and girls are equally affected by unfriendly school environment leading to loss of interest in education and subsequent dropout. Parents cannot afford learning materials required by children, management of schools remains weak and coordination of education response activities is weak at regional level. The capacity of teachers to address the emotional needs of children is low.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The proposed education in emergency activities will address the humanitarian needs of vulnerable hosts’ community and displaced people in a way that goes beyond service to one that builds resilience and the ability to withstand future shocks, preserves safety and dignity in order to improve the lives and self-reliance of displaced populations while simultaneously addressing the impact on host communities. The project will provide a solid ground for durable solutions, it restores the capacity of people and it is feeds into community based plans. Mercy Corp’s long standing presence in the target regions endows the project with in-depth knowledge of the vulnerabilities and strengths of the target HHs. The project will train teachers and CECs in DRR, school management, Hygiene promotion, AWD/Cholera prevention, maintenance and management of learning spaces and Water and Sanitation facilities. These will reduce vulnerability to future shocks and risks to WASH related diseases. Mercy Corps recognizes the need for close collaboration and synergy with ongoing resilience programs and will strive to leverage through existing programming platforms and link the education in emergency response to sustainable interventions. Moreover, the project design has flexibility on emergency preparedness and response to ensure timely response to new shocks.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The project will directly target areas receiving or hosting displaced households in order to mitigate the risk of exclusion based on displacement. The project seeks to address the needs of the households displaced and the selection criteria will target those households displaced. To respond to mobile vulnerable populations, the project is designed in a flexible manner to meet the needs of ever displaced people. During the targeting process local authority might influence on the selection of locations due to clan nepotism or particular interest and this way risk of exclusion of selection of the most disaster/displacement affected locations may be experienced. To mitigate this risk, Mercy Corps will adopt vulnerability-based targeting with community input approach. In addition, at the onset of the project a comprehensive community mobilization and sensitization activities will be undertaken focusing on the three pillars of; Information sharing, participation and complaint & response mechanism. This process will enable all community members including displaced communities to be fully aware of the project which will be implemented in their community. Children are more vulnerable during displacement and the project will provide specific child protection needs for the displaced children.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Mercy Corps places vulnerability due to gender and age at the center of all its emergency assistance. To mitigate the risk of exclusion, Mercy Corps conducted a gender/age analysis and noted potential risk of exclusion for example girls are sometimes excluded from accessing education due to traditional beliefs that regards adolescent girls as an age group for marriage than schooling. Adolescents girls are also at risk of exclusion during menstruation due to discomfort and stigma. The adolescent boys unlike the girls, their exclusion risk lies in physical strength, the most active adolescent boys are send to look after animals and the least active are sent to school. Similarly, the youth are recruited into armed groups than going to school. The project will take into consideration different needs for both boys and girls including special needs for adolescent’s boys and girls by designing appropriate activities suitable to address the identified risks. Mercy Corps will provide the girls with sanitary kits to meet the specific monthly needs. Also during implementation, the staff will be trained to mainstream gender and age. Culturally men/boys are not active in WASH especially hygiene promotion and the project will target men/boys, encouraging them to participate as ambassador for WASH in schools.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The target populations remain concerned with insecurity which caused their displacement and it continue to affect them in different ways include the risk of exclusion from accessing humanitarian assistance due to limited access and perceived risk by aid agencies. To mitigate this, Mercy Corps has updated security and safety plans in place to prevent and respond to threats. Mercy Corps works closely with UN agencies, local partner organizations, and stakeholders to share security information, coordination, and joint security emergency response including evacuation and relocation. Furthermore, Mercy Corps has strong community networks at the local level and in their respective work areas. Mercy Corps also has a well-established field office and a strong community network across the regions for many years which can be instrumental in ensuring the safety and security of staff and project assets as well as the overall coordination hub for activities. Additionally, Mercy Corps will instruct field staff to take the requisite field security trainings. This project will put deliberate efforts to increase most vulnerable groups’ participation and influence in contexts of peace, conflicts and other humanitarian crisis. To mitigate impact of armed conflict and violence particularly on the most vulnerable groups (women, children, minority clans etc.) the project will seek to collaborate with communities to foster peaceful co-existence and advocate for behavior change during community gatherings. The project will map out safe and protected schools/ learning sites for project entitlements to mitigate harm and ensure protection and dignity of school children is observed. Mercy Corps will also designate female matron at school level to support girls’ participation and report girls specific needs.
