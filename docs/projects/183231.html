Project Summary

As the political and economic crisis in Lebanon grows more severe, and the COVID-19 pandemic prevails, the impact on education continues to worsen. Many families are unable to cover their basic needs, leading to negative coping mechanisms which often results in children having less access to quality education. Because of the pandemic, both private and public formal education as well as informal education was shut down at the end of February 2020, disproportionately affecting the already vulnerable children in public schools. Whilst some schools engaged in distance learning as recommended by MEHE, this was not accessible for the most vulnerable children due to lack of learning devices, lack of reliable network, poor learning spaces, and the inability of some parents and caretakers to support their children's learning, further widening the gap on access to education. When in school, many children also struggle due to overcrowding in classrooms, the lack of educational materials, the inability to concentrate due to hunger, worry, fear, and many other factors.
This project aims to address some of the prevailing issues both in accessing education as well as obtaining quality education. This project will work to bring out of education children back into schools, as well as provide parents and caregivers learning support, and incentivize children to stay in school. The project will initiate outreach and awareness raising for out of education children, as well as provide referrals. Second, the project will provide homework help and after school support for children who are in school and at risk of dropping out. The project will also engage parents with parenting sessions and promote and apply SGBV risk reduction in adapted learning programs through raising awareness amongst children and caregivers on violence, bullying, cyber bullying, online safety. Finally, the project will provide cash for education, as well as support with low-cost solutions (both low-tech and high-tech) for inclusive remote learning. Provision of digital devices for extremely vulnerable cases will also be included. This project aims to support 3,186 beneficiaries in Aley (Mt Lebanon) and Batroun (North).

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

This project falls in line with several of the education sector's objectives. Under objective 1, "direct community support measures to most vulnerable Lebanese and migrant children" the project will engage in outreach, awareness raising & referrals on Out of Education Children (OOEC) to basic services and to bring them back to education. This will be conducted through Tabitha's community centers in Aley and Batroun through trusted community outreach workers. The project will also provide cash for education, and promote and apply SGBV risk reduction in adapted learning programs. Under objective 2, the project will procure educational items for children. Tabitha currently runs homework support for children in its community centers as well as provides nutritious snacks. In addition, Tabitha conducts parent training courses at its community centers, centered around themes related to both protection and education. The activities proposed in this project will link to ongoing work.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Tabitha has immense experience working on inclusion & gender mainstreaming, particularly under its protection program. Tabitha utilizes social work methodology to ensure that beneficiaries receive individual vulnerability/needs assessments done by outreach workers trusted within the community. Tabitha is mainstreaming protection in all its activities which allows maximization of the protective impact of the project through ensuring that the activities target the most vulnerable and enhance safety, dignity, and promote and protect the human rights of beneficiaries without contributing to or perpetuating discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect and exploitation. Tabitha engages in social cohesion activities that are designed to bring together individuals of different backgrounds. Project activities are designed to include any critically vulnerable individual within the population. Tabitha staff have been sensitized to gender & diversity and PSEA training plus trainings on integrity.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Accountability towards the served population and stakeholders is key to Tabitha’s humanitarian approach. Tabitha has already well established feedback and complaint mechanisms, this mechanism will be established at each location so children, parents, teachers, and anyone else who wants to is able to share their opinion, feedback and/or complaints. Tabitha has successfully participated in a series of coaching/mentoring sessions on PSEA & safeguarding integration done by ABAAD in partnership with OCHA, LHF and the Lebanese National PSEA Network.
