Project Summary

The proposed response aims to tackle the immediate shelter needs emerged as a result of the explosion, providing access to safe and liveable residential units to the affected communities of the Beirut Lebanese, migrant and refugee population, while also contributing to the multi-sectoral longer term rehabilitation of areas affected, in close coordination with national and local actors.
Tapping into the overall coordination mechanisms of the shelter sector and Lebanese institutions, as well as initiatives on the ground, this programme proposes area-based and punctual assistance to estimated 21,190 individuals (10,172 men and 11,018 women; of which 5,297 are elderly; 10,595 between 18-65; and 5,298 under 18) need via:
a) Emergency weatherproofing assistance to an estimated 300 target HH beneficiaries
b) Provision of conditional cash for rent to 1000 individuals
c) Minor repair of 1200 residential / non-residential units
d) Rehabilitation of 500 residential shelters.
e) Rehabilitation of 100 common areas within residential units
f) Rehabilitation of structural damages within 20 residential buildings
g) Implementation of 5 community support projects in affected areas
h) Capacity building of 30 national institutions and organizations on shelter response
i) Production of 10 assessment studies that are made accessible and widely disseminated
This program aims to complement other ACTED implemented/planned intervention across other sectors (such as Food Security, WASH, Protection and Health) and will target zones 71-74 and 104-110 in Bourj Hammoud, zones 66-69 in Basta el-Tahta and zones 6-8 and 10-12 in Karantina where ACTED’s historical presence and long term programming in the target areas (since 2013) made it one of the first and key responders to provide immediate relief to the Beirut Port Explosion. Within the first days of the response and in coordination with the Shelter sector, ACTED supported the implementation of the LRC MSNA assessment, jointly with the distribution of emergency weatherproofing kits. To date, ACTED has distributed 1064 core relief items (including 217 sealing off kits, dignity and baby kits) and removing over 1232 tons of debris from the streets and HHs of Beirut.
Through its shelter programs ACTED will enhance its ongoing coordination with Lebanese institutions and initiatives who have played an integral role in the immediate shelter response. In the aftermath of the disaster, ACTED provided GIS support to the Beirut Municipality through the provision of an expert to improve their efficiency of building safety checks; and a solid waste management expert was deployed to support the Beirut Municipality and their contractor to improve management of the waste dumps. Moreover, ACTED has engaged with Beirut Urban Labs, the Order of Engineers and other prominent urban planners and initiatives in Lebanon to ensure the response is people centred. ACTED has supported many local initiatives in the response (such as Arcenciel, Bassma, Migrant Community Center), through provision of resources to expand the shelter response and/or recycling efforts.
As a shelter sector actor part of the immediate response, and member of the Cash for Rent and Rehabilitation Task Forces, ACTED will ensure each form of shelter programming will align with sector harmonized packages and adhere to sector guidance to prevent generating conflict due to lack of harmonization of assistance. ACTED will continue to coordinate closely with the shelter sector to implement programs at a zone level and coordinate with all actors within the same zone. As co-lead of the Urban WASH Working Group, ACTED will continue to coordination to ensure a harmonised WASH and shelter response at HH level. Additionally, ACTED will continue to support local institutions and initiatives to contribute towards discussions within the sector to ensure harmonisation of the response beyond the sector actors.

1 - Needs

Following the Beirut explosion, widespread structural damage was reported at the harbour and in surrounding neighbourhoods, with collapsed buildings, others at risk of collapsing, damaged infrastructure/windows, and lack of access to basic services. According to the shelter sector, it is estimated that the explosions directly impacted 291,180 individuals living in 72,265 apartments located across 9,700 buildings within three km of the epicentre. During August 7-11, as part of the Shelter Sector response, ACTED participated in the household-level joint assessment (https://www.acted.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/beirut-explosion-acted-shelter-factsheet.pdf) coordinated by the Lebanon Red Cross (LRC), in Karantina, Gemmayze, Ashrafieh, Mar Mkhayel, Marfa, Madaouar, Rmeil and Saifi neighbourhoods. According to ACTED’s Rapid Needs Assessment, across respondents, shelter is the most needed basic assistance, mentioned both as first and second priorities (41% and 21% of HHs respectively). In Bourj Hammoud, 70-90% of the houses/dwellings are reportedly destroyed or partly destroyed. 50-90% of the neighbourhood population currently lives in a partially damaged house/dwelling, refusing to leave. This figure rises to 80% in Karantina, which is located nearer to the blast site, with a large share of those not inhabitable, and 20% of the neighbourhood population currently living in a partially damaged building. Displaced people were reported in Karantina in designated evacuation centres (1,000 individuals, mostly Lebanese), with an estimated 70% of the population has left the area. According to preliminary assessments by ACTED, 35% of Basta el Tahta, an already extremely underdeveloped area of Beirut, is damaged. Affordability and availability of construction materials are the main concerns with meeting shelter accommodation needs today across assessed neighbourhoods, as well as the lack of basic domestic items. Population groups living in the same area were affected in similar ways, with vulnerabilities among Lebanese and refugees from Syria.The Karantina district is one of the areas most affected by the explosion. Karantina has a population of approximately 4,638 people, most of whom are low-income (ACTED Rapid Needs Assessment 07/08/2020: https://reliefweb.int/sites/reliefweb.int/files/resources/Lebanon_Beirut%20Blast_Rapid%20Needs%20Assessment%20Report_August%202020_final.pdf), with many Syrian and Palestinian residents as well as migrant workers. Many affected HHs have no other housing options and cannot afford to rent temporary accommodation. According to OCHA's review of secondary data, affected populations in Karantina district could be displaced for up to one year. ACTED's rapid needs assessment also revealed that access to electricity and water has been severely affected in Karantina, with many power grid cables and generators severely damaged and water storage tanks and drainage pipes destroyed. Bourj Hammoud. where a high concentration of Syrian refugees settled, hosts approx. 150,000 inhabitants (WFP satellite maps, August 2020), out of which around 45,00 (according to WFP satellite data) were potentially affected by the explosion.Basta el Tahta, located in the Bachoura region, bordered by Salim Salam street, Al Maemoun street, Bechara el Khoury, was visibility underdeveloped even before the Beirut blast. This area is home to Khandaq el Ghamiq is inhabited by some of the most vulnerable communities. Preliminary ACTED assessments show that at least 35% of the area was affected by the blast. Historically, Basta’s inhabitants were mostly displaced after the civil war; nowadays, the area is home to a large community of migrant workers, Syrian refugees, and vulnerable Lebanese. Although this area was less affected by the blast than areas closer to the Beirut port; the physical and socio-economic ramifications of the blast will continue to compound the layers of complex vulnerabilities its already poor population faces daily.

2 - Activities

Output 1: Access to safe and liveable residential units is increased through temporary shelter assistance• A1.1: Emergency weatherproofing of damaged residential buildings. ACTED will continue provision of emergency weatherproofing assistance to an estimated 300 target HHs in zones allocated to ACTED by shelter sector coordination.• A1.3: Provision of conditional cash for rent to displaced individuals. In line with the sector and the Cash for Rent and Rehabilitation Task Force guidelines, ACTED will disburse conditional rent cash grants (estimated as 150USD per HH for a consecutive 3 months however might change with the harmonisation of amounts of CBT between sectors) to 1,000 displaced individuals. ACTED will identify beneficiaries of cash grants through ACTED’s ongoing and complementary programming which engages vulnerable HHs, notably in shelter, protection etc, limiting the assessment fatigue. ACTED will apply a protection lens to the selection of beneficiaries in order to further prioritize socio-economic needs.Output 2: Access to adequate residential/ non-residential units for affected populations is increasedThe shelter works implemented will be aligned with sector guidelines. The modality of the shelter response will be tailored to the beneficiary. If the household has the capacity to implement the works, ACTED will provide technical support and cash for works. If skilled workers are required, ACTED will identify, monitor and pay these workers through a cash for work modality. Contractors will be hired for major rehabilitation works, and for minor works requiring specialised tools and equipment. As a joint shelter/WASH sector response, ACTED will provide full rehabilitation to all HHs targeted. • A 2.1. Minor repair of residential / non-residential units: 1200. Minor damage to property, any level of building damage such as broken glass, broken doors, locks, collapse of false ceiling, estimated at 43% of buildings by the LRC assessment.• A2.2. Rehabilitation of residential shelters: Target: 500. Works in shelters still considered safe will include major internal works, including rehabilitation of internal non-structural walls, doorways, and electrical works etc.• A2.3. Rehabilitation of common areas within residential units: 100. Activities will include rehabilitation of stairs, structural and electrical works, etc. • A2.4. Rehabilitation of structural damages within 20 residential buildings. After the building has been assessed to be safe, ACTED will conduct structural works with a contractor. ACTED will refer households to temporary shelter programs, including LRC, for the duration of the works.ACTED is supporting Caritas to conduct a market assessment on the availability of construction materials and the shelter response will be designed in line with these findings. Output 3: Affected community assets and spaces reconstructed for enhanced safety and resilience• A3.1: Implementation of community support projects in affected areas: 5. ACTED has implemented projects to improve solid waste management (rubble clearing, technical support to Beirut Municipality, recycling), and in coordination with Beirut Urban Labs and other actors will continue support to the local projects in public spaces to rebuild communities affected.• A3.2: Capacity building of national institutions and organizations on shelter response: 30. ACTED has coordinated with Beirut Municipality (technical GIS and SWM support), ARM and BASSMA, Jobs for Lebanon and Beirut Relief Coalition (capacity building in humanitarian response), Arc en Ciel and LiveLoveRecycle etc. ACTED will continue to support local initiatives in the coming months to improve local capacity in disaster response.• A3.3: Production of assessment studies that are made accessible and widely disseminated: Target 10. ACTED has released two rapid assessments to date of the condition of shelters after the blast, and will share further assessments widely.
