Project Summary

Through the proposed interventions, SIF will directly contribute to the Shelter Sector’s objectives 2 of Syria by supporting sustainable rehabilitation of housing and related public infrastructures in Rural Damascus, Dara’a, Hama, Homs, Idleb, Deir- Ez-zor and Aleppo Governorates.
Proposed locations were identified according to their level of accessibility, SIF experience and severity of needs: 86% are in scale 2 and 14% are in scale 3. The comprehensive, multisector and multiyear approach was the determining element in the selection of the locations for 2023, with the aim of promoting the sustainability of the interventions, community self-reliance and resilience.
In line with the results of consultations with the local community and relevant authorities, and taking into account sector priorities, the proposed SIF intervention will contribute to building the resilience of 18,265 People in Need (PiN) in 2023 affected by improving housing conditions and related public infrastructure for vulnerable populations through:
1) Rehabilitating 585 damaged houses targeting 3,217 PiN in neighborhoods of Homs and Hawash, in Daraa (Daraa Al Balad, Izra and Sheikh Miskine), Aleppo, Rural Damascus (Markez Darayya, Haran Al'Awameed, Blalyeh and Moadmeh),
2) Providing and installing 1,800 Shelter Packages in targeting 9,900 PiNs, in Homs and Hawash, Hama (Kafr Zeita, Kafr Naboutha, Suran Karnas), Idleb (Khan Shaykun, Ma'arrat An Nu'man, Zerbah), Aleppo and Maskana,
3) Rehabilitating of 23 building common areas targeting 1,265 PiN in Rural Damascus (Markez Darayya, Haran Al'Awameed, Blalyeh and Moadmeh),
4) Supplying and installing 100 HH solar systems targeting 550 PiN in Rural Damascus (Kafr Batna, Jisrein),
5) Rehabilitation of sidewalks/stairs in urban area targeting 6,000 PiN in Aleppo.
Safeguarding and protection principles, including Do No Harm approach, will guide SIF team throughout all phases of the project cycle. SIF is fully committed to the SPHERE standards and humanitarian principles based on which SIF’s internal policies and procedures have been created to guide project implementation.
Interventions will be customized to the specification of the needs of the beneficiaries after the social and technical assessments conducted by SIF MEAL and technical teams and will include for damaged houses: installation of doors, windows and locks, fixing of WASH facilities as pipes, sink and light electrical repairs; and for standardized shelter package: doors, windows and basic WASH kits. Shelter packages intervention is targeted to reduce costs, challenges linked with missing HLP documents and ease process of approval from authorities. Rehabilitation of common building areas includes demolition and removal of damaged structure, reparation of staircases, close all openings protection repair/replace the main gate, lighting, rehabilitate connection to the utility networks. Installation of HH solar system will include provision of solar panels, regulator, battery, led and cables.
Specific needs will be addressed through including door locks and lighting, especially in WASH facilities, to better protect women and girls, particularly single women and female-headed households. SIF will also ensure that infrastructures are accessible to persons with disabilities and older persons and safe for children. SIF will take into account local capacities and encourage the contractors in recruiting workers from local communities. Accountability to affected population will be realized through strengthening complaints and feedback mechanisms and improved participatory assessments.
SIF will implement this project in close coordination with the MoLAE, Shelter sector, NGOs, SARC, local committees in order to bridge gaps in available services and ensure better access of People in Needs to services. SIF will closely follow Shelter Sector technical guidelines.
In 2022, SIF rehabilitated and provided shelter kits to a total of 2,415 houses benefited 13,783 PiN.

3 - Needs

In 2023, it is estimated that 5.7 million Syrian people are in need of Shelter assistanceacross the country (HNO 2023). During the last quarter of 2022 SIF identified and selected locations for 2023 based on sector severity scale, SIF experience and capacity and commitment to an integrated and comprehensive response.SIF contacted local authorities in the identified locations to discuss multisector needs and identify area priorities to select activities based on sector logframe, which were prioritized based on urgency level and SIF capacity. Data collection was conducted through Key Informants Interviews (municipal and community) and general observation by SIF team. The assessment reported that in many of the targeted localities private houses were severely damaged and lack access to electricity. Unavailability of housing had further limited returnees’ movement and early recovery in targeted communities. The populations living in these rural areas with houses and public infrastructure severely impacted by the conﬂict face severe protection risks especially vulnerable groups such as women, children, elderly and persons with disabilities. Women and girls face risks of SEA as appropriate security standards cannot be ensured inside the buildings and public spaces while people with disabilities and elderly are not granted suitable access to housing.

4 - Implementation of activities

SIF will submit assessment and implementation approvals to MoLAE. Selection of locations and PiN will be conducted in collaboration with donors, SARC and municipalities. Selection of beneficiaries will be based on sector criteria. Results of the technical assessments conducted by SIF in selected households will be endorsed by an appointed Committee composed of representatives from Municipality, governorate and SIF. The Committee will be also responsible of monitoring the implementation of activities and issuing handovers documents.SIF will rely on a contractor to carry out the rehabilitation work selected based on SIF SoP which include a transparent and objective tender process.To ensure sustainability, a guarantee period will be provided to each household in case some repairs are needed after implementation. Multiyear response will be complementary to WASH, ERL, education, and protection interventions in targeted areas, fostering an integrated approach to address needs and encourage community self-reliance and resilience.Activities will be monitored by SIF technical and MEAL teams through site and door-to-door visits. Participatory approach will be implemented through structured dialogues with groups of different ages, gender and backgrounds to ensure that protection concerns are detected and addressed. Reports to SIF coordination team, HQ and donors will reflect progress and highlight areas of concern in order to address them and ensure completion to agreed standards.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Discrimination or perception of discrimination may occur if assistance is provided to a specific group/ individuals while others are excluded. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: (1) To ensure uniform and transparent beneficiary selections procedures which are agreed among the sector members and government authorities and are clearly communicated to the community. (2) Ensure that comprehensive service mapping has taken place that risk mitigation and communication with communities are proactive and informed. (3) Ensure standardize assistance package and guideline that are followed by the sector. Risk 2: Risk of gender based violence (GBV) and sexual abuse while accessing the infrastructures. Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation: (1) conduct safety audit to ensure safe access of beneficiary to Shelter infrastructures sites. (2) ensure close coordination and consultation with local communities on the accessibility of infrastructures in order to receive their inputs and understand their perspectives in respect to safety of beneficiaries. (3) Consolation with women, girls, boys and men should be held. (4) sensitizing others sectors in gender sensitive measures in Shelter Assistance (5) ensuring locks on each door to provide privacy to girls and women and reduce risk of sexual abuse.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Sexual exploitation of beneficiaries in return of promise for assistance. Mitigation measures include the following: (1) SIF ensures that specific PSEA indicators are included in the monitoring framework.(2) Vetting process is conducted during the recruitment of a candidate at application and reference stages. (3) The code of conduct, which includes the strict prohibition of SEA, is signed by everyone working for and with SIF, including staff, SIF volunteers and contractors. After the selection of the contractors, as the same time of the signature of the purchase order, the contractor signs the SIF Code of conduct which is then attached as an annex to the contract/agreement.(4) SIF employees are trained on PSEA and a PSEA focal point is assigned in all governorates targeted by SIF. Awareness sessions of the community members on PSEA are conducted every year. Sessions includes the definition of the what is SEA and channels of reporting/whistleblowing. (5) SIF community-based complaint mechanisms is accessible to affected populations in all response locations. (6) A process, steps, and timeframe for the investigation of allegations of SEA has been developed by SIF.
