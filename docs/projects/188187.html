Project Summary

The project design and development has been based on needs identified by WASH, supported with rapid needs assessments conducted by IMAAR in Oct 2021 and firsthand consultations with local authorities/stakeholders and aims to rehabilitate and maintenance of basic infrastructures, including related drainage systems, and sewage systems, water provision networks to ensure safe and dignified living conditions for 58,650 most vulnerable host and IDP people (17,009 W, 14,662 M, 12,317 G, and 14,662 B), currently living in two sub-districts and 8 HCs under the sub-districts of Tal Abiad and Slook (Raqqa), which affected by conflicts to alleviate the suffering and improve the livelihoods infrastructure through one sector response.
The project aims to achieve the following outcomes:
Provision of WASH supplies and services in the eight targeted communities and in the sub-districts of Tal Abiad and Slook, through construction of sewerage sub and main networks to ensure adequate service, and water networks are in place in addition to sewage and water management services as needed.
WASH activities will be guided by the Basic Guiding Requirements guidance shared by WASH cluster and ensuring that HLP documentations are well carried out in parallel through field coordination with the relevant LCs.
A total of 8,203 m of sewage networks will be implemented in the targeted locations in addition to 4450 m of water networks will be implemented in the two targeted sub-districts, the construction works will include excavation and backfills, provision of gravel, provision and installation of poly pipes of different sizes and road leveling where the networks will be implemented.
This project will be implemented by IMAAR, In the context of this highly strategic intervention which has to be delivered within a period of eight months, IMAAR bring in the value addition of their local expertise in infrastructure projects throughout NW Syria, which is there since the conception of this project.

4 - Needs

The survey and interviews were conducted in many areas, which are Tal Abiad sub-district and Suluk sub-district, in addition to the following communities (Al-Amin, Al-Sukaria, Hammam Al-Turkman, Hawija Abdi, Khirbet Al-Raz, Muhaisin community).The number of population within the surveyed communities, according to what was reported by data collectors, reached 58,650 individuals as a total of the IDPs and HCs. The IDPs form 13% of the total population in the communities, which is equivalent to 6,650 IDP persons and 52,000 HC persons.When surveyed people were asked, whether people could access to clean drinking water, the answer was that people couldn’t access to clean drinking water by 100% percent. This is because the water in the wells is calcareous water and not suitable for drinking. Moreover, the hours of pumping water are few due to the lack of electricity, the lack of fuel, and the presence of wells that need maintenance.According to the surveyed people, the main source of drinking water provision is trucking water in water tanks.As for the sewage networks, it was found that the technical condition of the sewage network was bad in all areas of Tal Abiad and needed rehabilitation and maintenance, some areas needed new sewage networks provision. Recommendations: Working to provide safe and sterile drinking water to people. Working on the maintenance and installation of water and sewage networks to targeted people. Working to increase the hours of pumping water.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk1: - Adverse weather conditions will affect the implementation of rehabilitation works, and at certain times all site works may be stopped (in case of snowing & raining). Probability: M/Impact: MMS1: A fast track approach shall be followed to maximize the implementation of the rehabilitation works period, so there will be a flexible schedule. The rehabilitation works will be divided into 3 parts to be performed simultaneously by IMAAR.Security/Cooperation with Local authorities: the intervention might not be welcomed by all the authorities ‎controlling the area of implementation.‎Risk mitigation: Cooperation with local authorities should be in place since the inception of the project, and ‎coordination with the OCHA Access group should be ensured. Constantly monitor the security situation on the ‎ground.‎Covid-19 Mainstreaming: This project follows the WHO guidance on prevention and response to COVID-19, in a ‎rigorous effort to prevent the infection spread, protecting staff and beneficiaries from this hazard. Social distancing, ‎restrictions in gatherings, and hygienic measures are in place within the project.‎Possible delays and prioritization of certain sections/works.‎Risk mitigation: Conduct high coordination with all stakeholders to facilitate the rehabilitation and construction works. The ‎procurement process will be initiated to fast-track the contracting process. The sections with high prioritization will be the sections that need the most rehabi. works.
