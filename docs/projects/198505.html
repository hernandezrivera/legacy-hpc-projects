Project Summary

The major aim of the Shelter/NFI/CCCM Cluster is to improve living conditions of displaced persons living in camps, camp-like setting and host communities.
The urgent need for this project is as a result of increasing displacement of populations from other locations/LGAs into Girie, Fufore, Yola North and South LGAs couple with the destruction of shelter by wind-storm and fire outbreaks in the past few months.
HARAF proposed 8-month intervention aims to deliver life-saving Non-Food Item (NFI) assistance, directly benefitting Internally displaced persons in Girie, Yola South, Yola North, Fufore LGA's of Adamawa State.

1 - Needs

The United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs in Nigeria has disclosed that the humanitarian crisis in the Northeast remains undefeated, as more women and children were displaced in 2022.According to a new UN Global Humanitarian Overview (GHO) in 2023, millions of vulnerable people in Nigeria and beyond will continue to face a daily struggle to survive. About 8.4 million people in the north-east states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe will need humanitarian aid in 2023. About 3million people will need humanitarian assistance in NFI’s in Adamawa and Borno state . “North-east Nigeria remains one of the world’s largest humanitarian crises, with at least 8.3 million people in need of assistance in 2023. In Adamawa, about 300,000 IDPs are in need of NFI's.A specific needs assessment has been conducted to discover the need gaps present in Girie, Fufore, Yola North & South LGA of Adamawa State. as well as other intended locations., humanitarian needs across all sectors are increasing as a result of the latest waves of displacement and the ongoing crisis, specifically in the CCCM, Shelter/ NFIs, WASH, Protection, Food Security, Health and Nutrition sectors. The fresh population movements and the high influx of IDPs are leading to camp overcrowding and congestion in some of the hosting sites, where capacity has been stretched from 5,000 to 28,000 people in one month. As a result, significant numbers of new arrivals are without NFI's. The humanitarian situation remains critical in northeast Nigeria, with an estimated 7 million people in need of humanitarian assistance, and more than 1.8 million Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs). Adamawa State alone hosts more than 1 million IDPs – approximately 67% of the total number of IDPs in the northeast. This displacement, coupled with, until recently, lack of access by humanitarian responders to areas controlled by Boko Haram, has resulted in critically low levels of essential, lifesaving services. Humanitarian assistance is increasingly and urgently needed as more areas are coming under Government of Nigeria (GON) control, and thus becoming accessible to relief actors. In Adamawa alone, there are an estimated 1. Million individuals in need of food assistance, and 85,000 children are severely acutely malnourished. Life expectancy and key healthThe conflict stemming from the insurgency of non-state armed groups (NSAGs) in north-east Nigeria continues as intensely as ever. As the conflict continues into its 12th year, the attacks and insecurity have displaced millions of people, devastated agricultural production and other livelihoods, cut off essential services, and caused a crisis of protection. Violence against women, girls and children, including sexual violence, exposure to trafficking, and other forms of gender-based violence, is all too common yet underreported. Women are often forced into survival sex in exchange for food, movement and items to meet their basic needs, while some vulnerable households have resorted to early marriage and child labour. Thousands of children swell in the ranks of armed actors while predominantly women and children are compelled by non-State armed groups to carry person-borne improvised explosive devices.Insecurity due to ongoing hostilities and military operations have led to waves of mass displacement and continue to impact humanitarian operations. Vast swaths of Borno State are considered high or very high risk for international humanitarian actors, often constraining access to desperately vulnerable communities. Helicopter transport is often the only option for staff movements. An estimated number of up to 1 million people remain inaccessible to humanitarian actors.Hundreds of thousands of IDPs are concentrated in ‘garrison towns’ where the Nigerian Armed Forces defend perimeters that are risky to venture beyond. This creates congested, unhealthy living conditions, and constrains displaced farmers’ livelihood options.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Concern was given to ensure that protection concerns were considered during planning as shown by the indicator that will guide the monitoring of the project. We intend to mainstream protection by incorporating protection principles and promoting meaningful access, safety and dignity in humanitarian aid. The following elements must be taken into account in all humanitarian activities: 1 – Prioritize safety &dignity, and avoid causing harm: Prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of your intervention which can increase people's vulnerability to both physical and psycho-social risks. 2 – Meaningful Access: Arrange for people’s access to assistance and services – in proportion to need and without any barriers (e.g. discrimination). Pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services. 3 – Accountability: Set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints. 4 – Participation and empowerment: Support the development of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights, including – not exclusively – the rights to shelter, food, water and sanitation, health, and education

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

HARAF will also seek regular feedback from the targeted population during the implementation of the project, including through the camp Management committees. Affected populations may also provide feedback or submit complaints through the suggestion boxes or other elements of the community-based complaint and feedback mechanism. This mechanism is managed by the HARAF Camp Coordination and Camp Management Unit. For any distributed items, HARAF will ensure the community receives proper briefing on the selection methods and the date, time and place of distributions to take place ahead of time. During distributions, demonstrations and sensitization is done on the usage of the items and sampled post distribution visits. After the project has been completed, HARAF will conduct post-intervention surveys to ensure further accountability to affected populations and promote learning.
