Project Summary

HOPE is planning to work in increasing safe access to education and CP services for children and adolescents in Central Darfur State, which will contribute to the HRP education and CP strategic objectives for 2020. In this project HOPE will conduct, in Education: (1) Construct of 4 classrooms, provide 6 Semi-permanent, and rehabilitation of 10 classrooms, (2) Provide gender-sensitive WASH facilities, including hygiene promotion, latrines (in line with INEE and SPHERE minimum standards), (3) Provision of education in emergency materials including school textbooks, exercise books, etc.. for (3000) children (1470 boys and 1530 girls), (4) Provide with appropriate seating for (400) children, (5) Conduct one training workshop on EiE and child protection, (6) Conduct one training workshop on core subjects and teaching methods, (7) Conduct one training workshop on psychosocial support services, (8) Establish of 2 child clubs focusing on lifesaving psychosocial support, fine arts, drama & games, peace & protection, hygiene promotion, and leadership activities. In Child Protection HOPE will conduct: (9) Provide timely and coordinate emergency psychosocial support to 2600 out of school children (1274 boys and 1326 girls), with specific needs and exposed to violence, exploitation and neglect, (10) Identify /register/assess/document/ and reunify the 500 out of school children, who are have already been registered, to the school and provide them with education and NFIs materials, (11) legal assistance in accordance to national procedures through support access to birth certificates and other civil documents, (12) Provision of support for alternative care, case management, foster care of UASC with protection needs and provision of education and NFIs materials for UASC reunified to 150 children, (13) Identify /register /assess/document/ and reunify the 130 UASC with their care givers and 20 with their families, (14) Conduct training of 60 CBCPN members representing on psychosocial support, CRC/CP, GBV, civil registry, prevention of recruitment, and Sudan Child Act 2010, (15) Conduct training for 60 CBCPN members on positive discipline, code of conduct and child safeguarding policy and Revise/strengthen/update referral system pathways, (16) Community–based initiatives to foster social cohesion and community participation and inclusion, (17) Conduct 3 community campaigns awareness on issues of CRC/CP, GBV, and importance of education. HOPE Sudan will consider accountability to people affected through fully community participation, strengthen PTA as well as do follow up for all benefit targeted separately to assess the project, identify challenges and provide give recommendations to improve the project outcomes.

1 - Needs

After the longtime of conflict and prolonged humanitarian crisis, the emergency situation in Darfur raised intensively and the conflict between the government and armed groups and then between tribes as well as negatively affected children especially in Jebel Marra areas. The Projection of the Total Population in 2019 is 170,785 in Golo locality with 6,280 IDP and 52,675 returnees, as well as 204,260 in Rokoro with 22,289 IDP and 72,723 returnees (HNO 2020). The last displaced happened in April 2018 when 11,425 individuals fled to Rokoro from surrounding areas due to inter-tribal conflict (OCHA April 2018). The school children are 7571 (3711 girls, 3860 boys) and dropout are 8.5 for girls and 7.3% for boys as well as the 57.05% from school-age children out of schools (SMoE 2016), In Golo and Rokoro locality, the access and quality of education is very low, based on the following: - The number of pupils/children per classroom is more than 90 pupils, in addition to that, it is not at an adequate level even lack learning environment. - Lack of WASH facilities including latrines, water points, and hygiene promotion, the 66.6% of schools in need, even the 60% schools have access to WASH facilities, there are partial facilities of it. - There are more than 40% of school-age children in IDP camps are out of schools. - Lack of school children seating. - Lack of textbooks, exercise books and teaching aids, the ratio of the textbook. Also, a huge gap for child protection services for the below reasons: - Ongoing conflicts have been and newly displaced resulted in severe negative impacts on people in CD; casualties, trauma among children, females and other groups. - Child, women and young female have been at risk and live within the very destructive environment; do not enjoy their rights, possible incidents of GBV, poor health services, and many other issues affecting their lives. - Lack of protection services especially services to address Sexual & Domestic Violence and Family tracing & reunification, and referral system. - Lack of awareness of parents of Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC). - Poor capacities local mechanisms of Community-based Child Protection Networks for effective protection issues and concerns resolution and absence of follow up for action taken. Women and girls continue to face the risk of GBV including sexual violence when they leave the villages. Fear of reprisals from attackers often prevents rape survivors from seeking medical help. The conflict in Darfur has also increased the number of women who are the sole providers for their families. Sudan also faces issues with more chronic levels of malnutrition with one in every three children under the age of five years affected. As a result, these children will have lower lifetime earnings, and lower productivity as adults, influencing the economic growth and potential of the entire country.Due to the above situation, HOPE looks for improving access to Education and CP services. HOPE, through MoU that is signed with SMoE in Central Darfur, has access to schools with high needs in target localities as well as HOPE will fully coordinate with UNICEF and other Education and CP partners in both localities.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project is designed to minimize impacts on environment/ natural resources and enhance/ maximum benefits through: - Awareness raising on environment/natural resources and climate change as cross-cutting issues, - Considering the participatory approach among project activities for good sharing resources and a train of PTA.- For all constructions and supplies, use materials that are environmentally friendly - Planting trees in an around the school, work with parents and children to develop innovative ways of managing plastic waste.- Conduct integrate disaster risk reduction (DRR) into the extra-curricular activities of the school and supporting schools to develop DRR plans.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

To ensure protection mainstreaming, HOPE will use the below actions: - Conduct regular consultations with particularly high-risk groups of the affected population such as women, girls, and children with disabilities. - Support community engagement and interaction with schools though PTA meetings.- Establishment of complaints and feedback mechanisms.- Foster school-based Disaster Risk Reduction plans/strategies; to support activities that contribute to child protection and gender-based violence (GBV) prevention including psychosocial support, and referral pathways; - Teachers, learners, and parents, will be oriented on topics such as protection, human rights, sexual and gender-based violence and non-discrimination approach to access humanitarian aid. - Ensure that all school construction is in line with accessibility standards for persons with a disability, cash-based assistance will be provided to children with disabilities to access tools and materials to enhance their learning.- Pay attention to the choice of locations and targeting of beneficiaries to avoid perceptions that assistance is delivered in a discriminatory or non-equitable manner,- Conducting community safety audit and mapping of exiting services and ensuring that the results of these two exercises inform programming and advocacy. - Maintain transparent, inclusive and adequate communication with communities on available services; and by consulting communities and duty bearers in the planning, selection of services, feedback after service provision. - Guided on ensuring prioritization of services to groups considered particularly vulnerable, such as young children, children with disability adolescent boys and girls, children without adult care, the elderly and children and adults with specific needs ensuring that programming is tailored to respond to the needs of adolescent boys and girls.
