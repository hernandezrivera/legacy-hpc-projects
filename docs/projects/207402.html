Project Summary

The project seeks to enhance access to essential healthcare addressing the main causes of mortality and morbidity amongst the Rohingya refugees and host communities in line with the Health Sector Strategic Plan 2023-2024. IOM will provide services at 38 health facilities (14 PHCs, 18 health posts, 3 hospitals and 2 infectious disease treatment centers) standardized and aligned to the Minimum Package of Essential Health Services including emergency care, outpatient care, routine immunization, communicable disease control, non-communicable disease interventions, palliative care, laboratory services and sexual and reproductive health services. The health facilities will be deployed with adequate number of healthcare workers and supplied with essential medicines and equipment placing quality improvement measures.
The project will continue to support an emergency referral system comprising of a medical referral unit, 14 ambulances and provision of referral cost support for acute life-saving conditions. The project will support 3 secondary hospitals (2 Health complexes and Friendship maternity center) as well as upgrade the services at PHCs.
IOM will operate two integrated infectious disease treatment centers for management of outbreak potential diseases. Disease surveillance and case management services, including EWARS, sentinel sample collection/testing, and early diagnosis, isolation, and treatment, will remain integrated in IOM health facilities. IOM will continue to coordinate and build capacity of 33 mobile medical teams (10 run by IOM) of health sector partners leading the MMT TWG, to respond to emergencies.
Comprehensive Sexual Reproductive Health (SRH) services including antenatal care (ANC), postnatal care (PNC), facility-based deliveries including caesarian sections (C/S), menstrual regulation and post abortion care(MR&PAC) and syndromic management of sexually transmitted infections (STIs) including HIV/AIDS, family planning (FP) counselling and services with a focus on long acting and reversible contraceptives (LARC) and adolescent sexual reproductive health services (ASRH) will integrated into supported health facilities. Quality basic (BEmONC) and comprehensive (CEmONC) emergency obstetric and newborn care services with emergency obstetric referral pathways will be strengthened to mitigate maternal and perinatal morbidities and mortalities. Gender based violence (GBV) prevention and response services will be strengthened including clinical management of rape (CMR), Intimate Partner Violence (IPV) with safe and confidential referrals along established referral pathways.
IOM will continue to work with the existing network of 400 community health workers (CHWs) for health promotion, improve preventive and health seeking behavior, community-based surveillance. CHW will be the frontline workers for household visits, community engagement sessions and engagement with community opinion leaders. They will also actively engage men and boys in promoting SRH rights for women and girls and conduct community referrals including pregnant mothers for facility-based deliveries, immunization and address high rate of home deliveries in the camp.
Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) will be integrated into health services including assessment, counselling, support to persons with moderate to severe mental health conditions and referrals to specialized services provided by psychiatrists. At the community level, MHPSS services will be through evidence-based interventions including support groups, socio-relational activities, creative and art activities, ritual and celebration activities, sport and play activities and self-awareness sessions as per the IOM community based MHPSS approach. IOM will support MHPSS WG coordination and capacity building of partners. Through the Rohingya Cultural Memory Centre IOM will continue to document and preserve the Rohingya cultural heritage while promoting psychosocial well-being.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

An estimated 965,467 refugees are residing in 33 extremely congested camps in Cox’s Bazar and on Basan char Island (UNHCR, 2023). According to a camp-based need assessment carried out by IOM (IOM, 2023), 91% of the refugee households had illness or health issues in last three months and around half of the households reported they paid for medical care. Inaccessibility to particular medicine and treatment, overcrowding, prolonged waiting time at health facilities and long distance to reach the health facilities were commonly reported as access barrier in multiple assessments (J-MSNA, 2023; IOM, 2023). In the year 2022, a monthly average of 137,261 outpatient consultations were conducted in 39 IOM-supported health facilities, which marks a 44% increase in comparison to 2021. This is thought to be not only due to the increase in frequent disease upsurges/outbreaks, but also as a result of increased utilization of the health facilities by community. Notably, in 2023, around of half of the patients were children and 21% were elderly; and more than 62% of the patients were female. Therefore, there remains the need for strengthening the health service with particular emphasis on quality improvement, community engagement and gender and protection mainstreaming. Women, girls, adolescents, persons with disability and diverse gender expressions are vulnerable to GBV risks due to protracted humanitarian emergency context and face exacerbated barriers to access to SRH services. Health Sector 4W dashboard, 2023 reported 80% ANC 4 coverage, 82% facility-based deliveries, 97% live births and 1,044 C/S, estimated 89,000 first time FP users, 39,000 PNC and 3,000 MR&PAC. Maternal mortality rate/100,000 live births were 327, with 25.23 Infant mortality rate/1,000 live births. In 2022, MPMSR system reported 83 maternal deaths, with 71% occurring at or during referral between health facilities. 2023 J-MSNA report revealed 41% home deliveries among Rohingya refugees and 38% host community. These highlights need for quality of BEmONC services with effective referrals to CEmONC facilities with strong community engagement and referral for facility-based deliveries. Crowded living conditions, lack of proper WASH facilities and practices, and heavy monsoon in the refugee camps increase the susceptibility to infectious diseases. Routine immunization coverage still remains low with full immunization coverage at the age of 1 year is only 65%. Since 2017, several outbreaks of infectious diseases, such as dengue, COVID-19, measles, cholera, and scabies were reported. Between October 2021 and September 2023, IOM health facilities managed 21,740 dengue cases. Rohingya refugees and adjacent host communities are also vulnerable to the impacts of natural hazards and disasters, including cyclones, heavy monsoons, and fire incidents. All these threats and public health emergencies warrant the need for a resilient healthcare system and strengthened emergency preparedness and response. During the regular community and facility based MHPSS activities beneficiaries reported that stressors such as difficult living conditions in the camp and insufficient access to basic needs including the reduction of food rations to refugee households, and lack of livelihood opportunities are contributing to mental health difficulties. All these stressors have a strong negative impact on the daily functioning and psychological well-being of not only the Rohingya refugees, but also the host communities. In addition, according to an ACAPS report published on 12 May 2023, around 60 per cent of the violence and security incidence reported in the Rohingya camps in Bangladesh since 2017 happened between 2022 and April 2023 . This is marked by increasing trends of crimes such as murder, kidnapping, and illicit drug trade.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project adopted health sector August 2022 health facility rationalization as a primary consideration to a prioritized availability of essential healthcare services for Rohingya refugees in the camps and host community. All services will be aligned with the Minimum Package of Essential Health Services for Primary Healthcare Facilities. An integrated model of health services including nutrition (systematic detection of acute malnutrition among children 6 to 59 months and PLWs and referral, IYCF, growth monitoring, deworming and Vitamin A supplementation) will be adopted. Primary health care facilities will provide promotive, preventive and curative services as well as access to secondary and tertiary referrals. Community level health promotion and prevention services will be implemented through community health workers linked to primary health care facilities. Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) will be integrated into essential health services and with community-based interventions. The project will strengthen overall IOM and health sector preparedness and response capacities for health emergencies/disasters and communicable disease with outbreak potential. Since COVID-19 situation has significantly improved, IOM will scale down the capacity of SARI ITCs and maintain a section of the facilities as integrated infectious disease treatment centers to support case management of frequently occurring infectious disease outbreaks, such as dengue, AWD and diphtheria. IOM is a strategic member and supporter of the Health Sector Coordination mechanism in Cox’s Bazar. It dedicates five Camp Health Focal Persons supporting camp-level coordination. IOM is a member of the Health Sector Strategic Advisory Group, Co-Chair of the MHPSS working group, lead for the MMT TWG and member of SRH, Epi and CHW Working group. All these assignments attest to IOM’s strong role in coordination of the response to improve health outcomes of the affected population.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project will incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity in health response with the following measures: a) ensure gender, age and disability inclusive healthcare service package; b) adaptation of health facilities infrastructure such as gender, age and disability friendly latrine, ramps and railings, waiting space and privacy; c) avoid causing harm implementing standard guidelines; d) facilitate training on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and code of Conduct for staff, consultants, implementing partners, vendors. e) maintain inclusive and safe complaint, feedback and response mechanism; f) Provision of CMR and psychosocial support for GBV survivors; g) linkage with protection sector partners for protection concerns; h) deploy gender-balanced staffing; i) community dialogue meetings will be conducted with various segments of the community; j) collect, analyze and report gender, age and disability disaggregated data

3.3 - Age & Disability

Data collected will be disaggregated by age, analyzed and interpreted routinely to understand the coverage, outcome and impact of the health response on different age groups. IOM will ensure children can access health facilities safely with or without a caregiver, and that health facilities have space for children to wait in a child-friendly environment. Linkages with Camp Child protection focal point will be made to support engagement with children when child is without caregiver. The project will ensure that the health facilities are accessible to elderly and persons with disabilities and ensure accessibility through transports, wide entrances, adopted infrastructure, improve signage. Physical barriers that would inhibit the ability of elderly and persons using an assistive device for mobility will be removed in all health facilities.Emphasis will be placed on adolescent group with integration of Adolescent Sexual and Reproductive Health (ASRH) services across supported facilities.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

IOM will work through a network of community health workers, volunteers and focal points, majority of whom are from the beneficiaries, who will act as primary communication channels between healthcare facilities and the beneficiaries, including men, women, girls, boys, gender-diverse groups, older people and persons with disabilities. A Monthly community health facility group (CHFG) meeting will be facilitated at the catchment of each health facility to provide for beneficiaries’ involvement in health facilities and responsiveness of the health facilities to local needs and improve beneficiaries’ awareness of available services. The feedback and complaints mechanism, through IOM communicating with communities (CwC) unit, actively seeks the views of beneficiaries from the community and share their feedback with health program team. This will allow the health program to receive, process, respond to and learn from beneficiaries complains about the services. IOM will conduct facility-based patient satisfaction survey to improve understanding of patient satisfaction and improve service delivery.

3.5 - Is your agency/organization and are your IPs members of the PSEA Network?

Yes

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

IOM is zero tolerant for the sexual exploitation and abuse, thus will strictly adhere to the PSEA core principles. IOM will train all staff, volunteers, vendors and third-parties on PSEA. Necessary preventive and administrative measures will be in place, including sensitization meetings with the staff, stakeholders and beneficiaries and dissemination of IEC materials. The complain and feedback mechanism will address the SEA concerns. The contacts and hotline numbers for reporting will be made available.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, this project will continue to support environmental friendly health system through electrification of health care facilities with solar energy to ensure access to quality essential services for women and children, promote the uptake of health care services, cut health care costs, all the while protecting our climate. Solar power to provide enough electricity to power entire health facilities – from medical laboratory and refrigerators for vaccines, to incubators, lighting and autoclaving. IOM will also undertake retrofitting and infrastructural adaptation activities for the health facilities as required, to prevent and/or better withstand climate-related hazards. This will help health care facilities to remain operational at optimal scale during and after shocks, and/or disease outbreaks to better protect the health of the affected communities. Finally, the emergency preparedness and response measures will be strengthened contributing to a resilient health system for mitigating the impacts climate hazards, e.g. heavy monsoon, disease outbreaks and cyclones.
