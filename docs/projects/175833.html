Project Summary

Juba City and its outskirts has become a place of refuge from natural and human made disasters. The change of environment Distress has increased distress among households. Children and caregivers need psychosocial support, even as with increased need for family tracing and reunification using creative structured age and sex appropriate activities. MTT will administer Psychological First Aid and community based child protection networks (CBCPNs) to vulnerable children. Caregivers community members will be taught parenting skills based on existing tools. As conflict-affected and displaced children risk become involved in hazardous child labour, begging in the streets, the project will increase protection, especially to children outside parental care who are more at risk of exploitation. Local communities will be made to identify, report, and refer children with urgent child protection concerns through CBCPNs and existing referral pathways.
The project will promote community-based child protection committees to engage in child protection activities using gender and age in planning and implementation. Targeted will be boys and girls with special needs – especially the disabled. The project will provide life skills and resilience programming to adolescent girls and boys through peer support activities with children. Women and men will be encouraged to participate in every component of the project with the dynamics of the gender roles in mind. Similarly, they will be duly represented in all decision-making organs to provide avenues for maximum input from all gender groups. In collaboration with education and health clusters, capacity will be built for health workers and teachers with emphasis on girl-child education and provision of dignity kits. Feedback mechanisms are embedded for project flexibility. Nearby primary schools will be identified as partners with whom to mainstream Child Protection and psycho-social support in schools through capacity building events while observing the: "do no harm".

1 - Needs

The COVID-19 Pandemic and flooding in 2020 led to increases of IDPs with the accompanying disruption of routine activities, change of environment, children dropping out of school and missing out on friends, distress; restriction of movement and difficulties in accessing critical child protection services. Juba has continued to receive populations fleeing human made and natural disasters such as floods caused by seasonal rains that affected Jonglei plateau, wider River Nile area and Lol Basin in Jonglei, Unity, Upper Nile States respectively (CP Sub Cluster, Protection Considerations for Flood Response 2019). With increased cases of Gender Based Violence, there is a need for psychosocial support using creative structured age and sex appropriate activities. Children and adolescents continue to be exposed to multiple protection risks including recruitment by armed groups, family separation, psychosocial distress, sexual violence and exploitation. The COVID -19 outbreak and measures to contain the pandemic have exacerbated this situation. In the last project in which MTT partnered with UNICEF within Mangaten IDP camp in Juba in 2019, the target was 977 people (486 girls, 388 boys, 62 women and 41 men), COVID-19 and flooding has increased the target five times. This project will sensitize communities on child rights, protection, care against unexploded ordinances of war, prevention of separation and psychosocial support through public awareness sessions and community participation. It will deliver critical children protection in emergency services including Family Tracing Reunification (FTR) Packages. It will provide a platform for a multi-sector response to the affected populations targeting boys and girls within various age groups (0-5, 6-12, 13-17) having unique needs. For sustainability, training will be provided to community leaders and volunteers on basic case management cycle, children interviewing skills, safeguarding, tracing and CBPSS and Psychological First Aid skills. Their skills will be enhanced, re-organized and strengthened on community based support and referral networks. Case workers and CBCPN member(s) will conduct daily awareness on child protection and provide general psychosocial services to men and women, boys and girls within IDPs in Juba and its outskirts. Need will be satisfied to identify and document children with protection concerns, support girls and boys in vulnerable situations through case management and referrals to specialized services. MTT will continue to train CP staff on Child Protection case management and Family tracing and reunification of UASC. MTT conducted a comprehensive caseload management in Juba through a Partnership Agreement Cooperation with UNDP targeting IDPs outside the UN protection of civilian (PoC) compounds ending September 2018. Anecdotal evidence from project, which was under the umbrella of the South Sudan Humanitarian Fund (SSHF), indicated that child, early and forced marriage had increased during the conflict as families use it as a coping mechanism to alleviate economic pressures, clear debts, buy protection or settle disputes. The project is a function of the overall Child Protection Area of Responsibility (CPAOR to address the needs of the vulnerable people in South Sudan, estimated to have increased by 6.1 % in 2021. The components entail the expansion of Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS), community based child protection (CBCP) approaches; family tracing, reunification and alternative care for Unaccompanied and Separated Children); strengthening case management including cash based assistance and the extension of services to children in contact with the law; support to child survivors of sexual violence; and technical and institutional capacity strengthening of local actors, frontline child protection workers and community volunteers.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Trainings will be oriented on the risks associated with sexual exploitation of communities. MTT will facilitate beneficiaries (particularly girls) to report cases of gender-based violence and / or sexual harassment from relatives or strangers, which will be documented and referred back to the line cluster partners.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

MTT will engage members of community, able to speak the local language to conduct regular evaluation interviews, targeting disadvantaged members of the community, such as people living with disabilities, illiterate individuals, women and girls. Employment will be done with consent of camp management committee and other opinion leaders.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project engaged the communities through child protection awareness creation, identification, selection and operationalization of the child protection network, efforts were put in place for gender mainstreaming, where the project has deliberately ensured the participation of women, girls and youth. Youth have been encouraged to form youth groups as a way of ensuring their participation. The project has also engaged different stakeholders such the church leaders and the school administration, this enabled the project to acquire child friendly spaces within the church and school vicinities thus leading to the participation of children in school in the PSS activities.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

MTT will work through youth and women groups, PTAs, religious leaders and Payam Administrators and government officials (6) as feedback mechanisms to review project performance. Inter cluster working groups will also be useful feedback channels.
