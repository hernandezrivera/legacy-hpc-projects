Project Summary

This project is aim to end Open defecation, train and equip community representative on good hygiene practices, on hand washing techniques, door to door sensitization of the community,This programme will target boys, girls, women and men across all genders.
This programme goal is to end open defecation and improve the health and social economic well-being of communities by reducing the diarrhea-related aliments through providing sustainable proper sanitation and good hygiene practices including capacity building.

1 - Needs

Water and sanitation-related diseases pose a significant threat to the well-being of communities in North East Nigeria. Even in the absence of emergencies, diarrhoea claims the lives of over 30,000 children globally each week. In conflict situations, simple diarrhoeal diseases can surpass the direct impact of the conflict itself. This program places a paramount emphasis on raising awareness and cultivating sound hygiene practices to prevent diseases and instill positive attitudes towards cleanliness. Looking ahead to 2024, an alarming 5 million people in the BAY States are identified as requiring urgent humanitarian Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) assistance. The severity of the situation has worsened compared to the previous year, with 34 Local Government Areas (LGAs) now classified as being in critical situations (phase 4), up from 27. Among those in need, women and girls make up 52%, often facing protection issues as they travel long distances to fetch water. Children, constituting 60% of the population in need, are particularly vulnerable to WASH-related diseases and malnutrition. Persons with disabilities necessitate adaptive WASH infrastructures—water supply and sanitation—to preserve their dignity and hygiene. Access to safe and an adequate quantity of water remains a challenge in almost all BAY States, impacting populations in IDP/informal settlements, returnees, and host communities. Approximately 17% of assessed households report having less than the SPHERE standard of 15 liters per person per day, and 5% of households have to endure a journey of more than 1 hour round trip to access water. Specifically, in Borno, there are 9 LGAs with critical/severe water needs, while Adamawa has 12 and Yobe has 4, totaling 25 LGAs with critical/severe water needs in the three BAY states. Sanitation in IDP camps remains a persistent challenge, primarily due to difficulties in fecal sludge management, resulting in reduced latrine usability and accessibility. A staggering 71% of assessed camps have a latrine to person ratio above 1:50, failing to meet SPHERE standards. For sanitation needs, Borno has 18 LGAs with critical/severe needs, Adamawa has 17, and Yobe has 17, bringing the total to 52 LGAs with critical/severe sanitation needs across the three BAY states. Addressing these critical needs is crucial to ensuring the health, dignity, and well-being of the affected communities.

2 - Activities

1. Increased access to basic drinking water in households and institutions: We proposed to use STSI water filters, These water filters are amazing and have been Tested and successful in over 46 countries. These water filters out over 100,000 gallons of water and removes 99.8%of bacteria mainly, -Salmonella, E-coli, Typhoid, Hepatitis A/E, Cholera. STSI would use two different filter: The bucket filter -attached to a tank and bucket. These filters does not require electricity. This bucket filter filters out 100,000 gallons of water and with good maintenance and handling it can last up 3 years. ►►Video link to the innovate filters: https://youtu.be/ebP1lIf0IAw We will also conduct bucket chlorination using Calcium Hypochlorite (HTH chlorine) solution and Treatment with Aquatabs. 2. Operation and Maintenance of water facilities: We will work with water facility operators, technicians and attendants and local partners from the affected population and this aid in strengthening maintenance and building local ownership of facilities. 3. Adoption of key hygiene behaviors correctly and consistently and improved management of fecal waste: The use of community volunteers to perform house to house community hygiene promotion is one of the community engagement strategy to be use. Community mobilizers/volunteers will be strategically deployed to conduct community hygiene education from house to house, providing key messaging on handwashing with soap at critical times, keeping water sources clean, covering water, maintenance of water filters, The mobilizers are responsible for reporting any cases of diarrhea and other water borne ailment they came across and directing them to the nearest treatment centers. 4. Increased access to basic sanitation services in households and institutions:The absence of a clean environment affects human health. Sanitation aims to protect human health by providing a clean environment that prevents the transmission of disease, especially through the fecal-oral route. In Borno, pit/emergency latrines are the sanitation facilities in IDP camps and host communities. When pit latrines become full, it is critical that they are emptied in time; otherwise, the practice of open defecation proliferates, contaminating the environment with ready sources of disease. In the most densely populated camps, which were the main focus for the cholera outbreak, safe management of full pits and eradicating open defecation was critical to maintaining access to safe sanitation and a necessary measure to stop cholera transmission. Community-Led Total Sanitation Approach (CLTS) is a recent innovative and highly effective participatory approach for mobilizing rural communities to address their sanitation problems. This innovative method focuses on spontaneous hygiene and sanitation behavior change led by the entire local community themselves in order to break the focal-oral chain of transmission and stop open defecation totally. Improved management of fecal waste: We provide support by regular desludging and disposal of latrine waste and also partner with Borno State Environmental Protection agency (BOSEPA) is responsible for environmental sanitation and management of sludge disposal sites. Community volunteer’s workers recruited from the affected population will be responsible for cleaning and disinfecting latrines and garbage removal. 5. Capacity Building for Local partners and private sector to deliver WASH services: Capacity building training will be based on Train The Trainer (TOT) approach, where the trainees will be able to have the capacity to train others stakeholders and pass the knowledge down to achieve an hygienic communities.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

A feedback mechanism will be established and operationalised through stakeholder engagement meetings, anchored by project staff and community leaders, to provide and receive necessary feedback about the impact of the project, including aspects of the project that are going well, challenges, delivery methodology, context-appropriate solutions to problems, and opportunities to drive community capacity and ownership of the activities. Through the engagement of an external project evaluation expert, the aim is to ensure that objective feedback is received from support communities and beneficiaries about the quality, target and impact of the intervention on addressing the goals of the project. The feedback received at intervals during the implementation of the project will, along with the stakeholders’ meetings, influence any changes to the implementation of the project to ensure that it meets the needs of target beneficiaries.
