Project Summary

Adverse factors, like earthquake of Feb. 2023, conflict, economic decline, power shortages, and climate change, have severely affected access to Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) services in Syria, particularly in the northern regions. The conflict and earthquake have damaged WASH systems, restricting water and sanitation access in affected communities. Economic decline has impeded the technical and financial capacities of service providers and households, limiting coping mechanisms for WASH services.
In North Syria, only 54% of the population has access to an improved water source. 53% of households reported insufficient water supply. Female-headed households in NWS, particularly in camps, experience more evident water insufficiency (19.2%) compared to male-headed households (11.5%).
In terms of sanitation, challenges persist in North Syria, with 39% of households facing difficulties in accessing functional toilets or proper wastewater disposal. Around 9% of households in the north share toilets, particularly in IDP sites, and nearly half of these toilets lack gender segregation and proper amenities. Women and girls are disproportionately affected, facing increased gender-based violence risks due to a lack of privacy in toilets and bathing sites.
The project will target during 2024 and 2025 the IDPs and Host Communities in the targeted locations to improve safe, adequate, and sustainable access to WASH services and items with the improvement of public health to avoid/mitigate the risk of outbreaks caused by contamination of drinking water and lack in the SWM which might cause an outbreak in leishmaniasis either cholera.
In this project there are two objectives:
1- Increasing the accessibility to lifesaving services for the IDPs in camps, health care facilities and schools in terms of water trucking, solid waste management, septic tank desludging, latrine construction and maintenance, and hygiene promotion campaigns. The project includes planned intervention during any potential outbreak as well as during disaster. By including these two cases (outbreak and disaster) the preparedness for such non-usual conditions will increase the efficiency in response and prompt addressing of emergency needs of affected population.
2- Increasing the accessibility to resilient infrastructure and life sustaining services through rehabilitation of water systems, sanitation systems, and restoration for the SWM systems. The rehabilitation for water system will target increasing the efficiency of water station and network by conducting maintenance, replacement, and extension for the existing infrastructure and water station as well along with the establishment of water ow meters in order to re-activate the tariff system. Merging the solar system in the water station as mean of operating power source is crucial component of the sustainable rehabilitation works.
Rehabilitation for the sanitation system will target maintenance for the sewage network to repair the leakage points to avoid mixture between drinking water and wastewater along with conducting needed extension for the network in order to ensure that all compounds are connected to the sewerage network. Additionally, the treatment of waste water is to be implemented for
The Solid Waste Management restoration will cover distribution for the containers in the targeted areas and transfer the solid waste to the related landfill which will also be rehabilitated to eliminate any leakage of the leache into the ground, which might causes contamination for ground water.
The project will target 368,359 beneficiaries in 13 sub-districts with overall WASH severity scale between 4 and 5 and will contribute to improve the living conditions of the affected population in NWS and to create more dignified environment.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

As BINAA has been supporting 86 camps with WASH activities during 2023, through this proposed project BINAA desires to continue provision of critical WASH services during 2024 and 2025 to 43171 IDPs (9929 boys, 11225 girls, 9929 men, 12088 women) in 45 camps and informal settlements in Idleb and Aleppo through the following key interventions; This will include:1-Provision of safe water 35 l/p/d on daily basis needed. 2-Wastewater desludging of WASH facilities 3-Solid waste collection and deportation. 4-Procurement and distribution of consumable hygiene items through in-kind and CVA modalities. 6-Conduct RCCE interventions and COVID19 / Cholera hygiene awareness messaging in IDP camps through FGD. 7-Water quality monitoring, to ensure safe water. 8-connecting 5 camps to water network by which will be operated by using renewable power sources. 9- Maintenance and cleaning of WASH facilities in 45 IDP sites, 15 schools and 10 HCFs located in Idleb and Aleppo governorates. During 2024 and 2025, BINAA plans to target under sustainable interventions more than 372,857 individuals (85,755 boys, 96,950 girls, 85,755 men, 104,397 women) in Ar-Ra'ee, Jandairis, Maaret Tamsrin, Harim, Daret-Azza and Idleb sub-districts, seeking to achieve sustainable solutions for the affected communities, IDP sites and underserved locations, through the following: 10-Water system rehabilitation and restoration in Harim and Idleb sub-districts. 11- Implementing wastewater treatment plant in Ar-Ra'ee sub-district. 12- Sewage networks rehabilitation/ implementation in camps and communities in Ar-Ra'ee, Jandairis and Maaret Tamsrin sub-districts. 13- Solid waste management: Landfills rehabilitation works in Idleb sub-district.Throughout these interventions, the protection risks are analyzed to be mitigated as follows:• The risks Inequality in distribution; Physical and psychological harm arising from violence and coercion; Timing and process of distribution put women and girls at higher risk of GBV.Likelihood: H. - Impact: HMM: promote Establishment of adequate management plan; Promote engagement with communities in distribution; training of staff.• Quality and/or quantity not adequate as per humanitarian standards.Likelihood: H. - Impact: HMM: Ensure provision of safe water as per country Water Quality standards; Collect water from safe sources certified by water authorities or local councils.
