Project Summary

Despite the R-ARCSS and opportunities for recovery, the cumulative impacts of conflict, destroyed livelihoods and persistant insecurity meant that 7.1 million people were in need of humanitarian assistance in South Sudan. IDPs still remain in PoCs due to uncertain security conditions preventing return to home locations with Bentiu PoC having the highest population of 115,960 people (IOM DTM Oct 2019). Intercommunal tensions continue to result in displacement particularly in Rubkona, Guit and Koch. Continued conflict between armed groups in Yei has resulted in displacements. An estimated 5,712,000 people did not have access to water and sanitation services in 2019 and only 29% have population could access an improved water source (HNO 2019) and these trends are likely to continue in 2020. In Unity, high morbidity is due to illness from waterborne diseases as much of the water and sanitation infrastructure has been destroyed by conflict. This results in high malnutrition rates. Bentiu PoC remains the most populated IDP site in the country and there is a need to continue to support WASH services on site so IDPs have access to water, environmental health services, and sanitation services.. With displacement, many households do not have access to latrines leading to open defecation. NBeG, remains one of the locations of morbidity of 157/1,000 in children under-5 in South Sudan compared to a national level of 99.2/1,000 (WHO 2017). As well has poor health services this is also due to poor access to sanitation and hygiene practices. In DRC, there is currently an EVD outbreak and for locations in South Sudan bordering DRC there is critical risk of EVD spreading. The government health services are unable to deliver adequate risk prevention activities and poor water and sanitation infrastructure and practices on IPC and hygiene mean increase risks of EVD.
The aim of this programme is to reduce the risks of morbidity and malnutrition to conflict affected and vulnerbal populations through rehabilitation, operation and maintenance of water upply and sanitation facilities and improving hygiene behaviours. Where Concern implements Nutrition programmes, nutrition beneficiaries will be spefically targeted to reduce risks of waterborne illness causing malnutrition. Concern will provide
WASH services in Sector 5, Bentiu PoC and in BBR locations in Rubkona urban areas and Guit. In Leer, Concern aims to establish a partnership with Coalition for Humanity to support WASH interventions. In Bentiu PoC sex-segregated latrines and bathing shelters will be constructed and BBR locations an adapted CLTS for emergencies will be implemented. Environmental health activities will foucs on rubbish collections in Bentiu PoC and Rubkona urban areas. Hygiene promotion will involve house-to-house visits and mass campaigns implemented by HHPs and volunteers from Concern’s Nutrition programmes such as CNVs, lead mothers and Male Change Agents. In NBeG, this WASH response will be linked with Concern’s Health and Nutrition programme. Safe water access, sex-segregated latrines and medical waste disposal facilities will be ensured at health facilities. An adapted CLTS approach will be implemented in communities. Hygiene promotion will be conducted by CNVs, lead mothers and Male Change Agents from Concern’s Nutrition programme. In Yei, Concern will focus on WASH in health facilities to reduce risks of through providing improve water and sanitation infrastructure and in communities supporting improved hygiene practices with an emphasis on IPC of EVD and other infectious diseases. WASH activities will focus on reducing security, safety and protection risks when accessing water points and using latrines. Concern will continue to be the Unity State WASH Cluster co-coordinator to coordinate the overall WASH response and coordinating with state clusters in NBeG and Central Equatoria and the national WASH Cluster.

1 - Needs

In South Sudan, there continue to be high morbidity rates and malnutrition. The high disease burdens are caused by poor health and hygiene practices with lack of access to improved water sources and high levels of open defecation. Lack of access to water and sanitation services contribute to high levels of morbidity. In Unity, due to continuing localised insecurity, the population the population of Bentiu PoC is 115,960 (IOM DTM Sep 2019). Sector 5 has the highest population of IDPs at 28% placing considerable demands on water services. Alhthough Concern installed a new borehole, the population access 14 litres per person per day, below Sphere Standards. While 100% use water points as their main water source, 78% of people with disabilities face water access challenges due to distance and difficulties in carrying water (IOM KAP survey 2019). Due to congestion, latrine coverage ration is below Sphere Standards at 1:23. Knowledge of handwashing remains low with only 40% of people being able to identify three critical times or more for handwashing (IOM KAP survey 2019). In Bentiu and Rubkona towns, while 86% of households access water from improved sources, only 52.1% treat water for drinking (Concern KAP survey March 2019). In Guit, only 17% access water from an improved source and only 68.1% of households treat water for drinking (Concern KAP survey Mar 2019). Lack of water points in Guit has resulted in increased queuing times for women and girls who are mainly responsible to collect water, reducing time for childcare and impacting on nutrition of children. 37% of households state that water points are too far and 50.2% of households state long queuing times as the main barriers to accessing improved water sources (Concern KAP survey Mar 2019). Latrine access remains low with 34% of the population having latrines present in Bentiu and Rubkona towns and only 2.1% of the population in Guit have latrines present in their communities, creating considerable risks of morbidity due to waterborne illnesses in the population and subsequent malnutrition. Knowledge of handwashing is high, with 73.1% of households in Rubkona urban areas and 82.1% of households in Guit knowing at least three of the main critical moments for hand washing (including after fecal contact and before food). However, with most people practicing open defecation, the lack of privacy reduces dignity and results in significant protection risks particularly for women, girls, and boys. In Aweil West and Aweil North, there continue to be high rates in children under 5 morbidity of 41% and 42% respectively (Concern SMART surveys Jan 2019) which is results in high malnutrition rates. This is mainly caused by poor access to improved sanitation infrastructure and poor hygiene practices. Access to improved water sources is high with 77.9% of households in Aweil West and 89.6% of households accessing improved water sources in Aweil North. However, more than 90% of households in both Aweil West and Aweil North practice open defecation and 51.2% of the population in Aweil West and 54.2% in Aweil North know the 4 critical times for handwashing (Concern SMART surveys Jan 2019).Yei is a major transit hub between DRC, Uganda and South Sudan and due to an EVD outbreak in DRC, there is a high risk of EVD spreading to South Sudan. There has been lack of community awareness on EVD and community-based prevention and control strategies and surveillance is limited, particularly in locations that are high-risk bordering DRC. The poor WASH infrastructure and health system in Yei contributres also to higher risks of EVD.

2 - Activities

CO1: Beneficiaries of Concern’s nutrition responses in Bentiu PoC Sectors 3 and 4, Guit, Aweil West and Aweil North will receive a package of WASH support and where Concern does not implement nutrition responses in Bentiu PoC Sector 5, Rubkona urban areas and Yei, Concern will target partners’ beneficiaries in these locations. Concern will priotise water point rehabilitation and latrine construction in locations for CMAM services. Hygiene promotion activities involve CNVs and Mother-to-Mother Support Groups (MtMSG) who will train others through mass campaigns and house-to-house visits and on how hygiene impacts on MIYCN. Caregivers of those in OTP, TSFP and Stablisation Centres will receive support on improving hygiene during MIYCN counselling. Baby WASH will target SAM and MAM beneficiaries and groups including integration of VHCs and MtMSGs.CO2: In Bentiu PoC Sector 5, Concern will focus on waterborne diseases prevention through provision of clean water, latrine construction, environmental health services and hygiene promotion. Emphasis will be on cholera prevention due an outbreak in Sudan and frequent movement between the PoC and Sudan and congestion. Concern will collaborate with camp leadership for solid waste management with rubbish collections and community clean-up campaigns. With mayors’ offices in Rubkona urban areas, Concern will support with business owners’ groups for rubbish collections from markets. In BBR locations, an adapted CLTS approach for emergencies will be used. Hygiene promotion will focus on behaviours for vector control in households. In Aweil West and Aweil North, Concern will imporve sanitation at health facilities with rehabilitation of latrines and mobile clinics and construction of sharp pits to improve management of medical waste. In Yei, the response will focus on WASH in health facilities for IPC of EVD and other infectious diseases. Concern will rehabilitate water points, latrines and improve hygiene practices that focus on IPC. Concern will focus on community engagement activities on EVD, prevention and hygiene and sanitation promotionCO3: In Bentiu PoC, Concern will continue with operation and maintenance of 2 boreholes and water infrastructures in Sector 5 to the IDP population. In Bentiu and Rubkona towns, Concern will support the SMoWPI with rehabilitation, operation and maintenance of SWATs and water networks targeting IDPs, returnees and host communities. Concern will coordinate with CCCM and SNFI Clusters and town councils to consider IDP and returnee movements. In Guit, Concern will focus on rehabilitation of water points and maintenance. In Aweil West and Aweil North, focus will be on rehabilitation of water points in health facilities. In Bentiu PoC, Concern will construct and rehabilitate disabled accessible and child-friendly latrines. In Rubkona urban areas, Guit, Aweil West and Aweil North, an adapted CLTS approach to emergencies will involve communities identifying safe sites with privacy and dignity and they will be provided with materials for latrine construction. HHPs in Bentiu PoC and Rubkona urban areas, VHCs in Guit and CNVs, lead mothers and Male Change Agents in Aweil West and Aweill North will provide support improving hygiene practices. The main health risks will be reassessed for communities to reprioritise hygiene practices. As there has been an emphasis on handwashing, and while key, there is a need to increase focus on other practices such as food hygiene, vector control and indoor air pollultion. In Leer, Concern aims to partner with Coalition for Humanity for its WASH response in Leer.CO4: Increasing water points will decrease distance and time collecting water and thus protection risks. Water points will only operate during daylight hours and queuing times will be minimised. Latrine and bathing shelters will be gender segregated and lockable and will be designed with women and men to ensure privacy, dignity and safety and within 5km of households.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Concern will adhere to humanitarian principles of neutrality, impartiality and humanity when providing WASH services. Assistance will be solely based on need. Communities will be sensitised on the programme and entitlements to WASH services at the start of the programme. Land access is a key rights issue for the programme. In BBR locations and Yei, Concern will need to collaborate with CCCM local authorities, town councils and community leaders in land allocation for IDPs and returnees targeted by the programme for provision of WASH services to reduce risk of land disputes with host communities.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Host communities are often required to support IDPs who flee from their locations, particularly Rubkona and Yei urban areas Most of these host communities are already extremely vulnerable with high poverty levels and few resources to assist IDPs and returnees including water points and latrines. In BBR locations, Concern will support WASH services to benefit the whole population and will coordinate with CCCM and Protection partners, town councils, the SMoWPI and community leaders when planning WASH services to ensure all populations receive services. While NBeG has had no significant displacement or returnee arrival recently, if there is displacement or arrival of returnees, Concern will consult with local authorities and community leaders and WASH partners such as planning to ensure provision of WASH services to both host communities, IDPs and returnees.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

In Bentiu PoC, Concern coordinates with the CHC for planning of WASH interventions including operation and maintenance of boreholes; gender segregated latrine rehabilitation and location of new latrines; waste collection services; and hygiene promotion activities, particularly mass campaigns. In BBR locations, Concern has a MoU with the SMoWPI for supporting rehabilitation of SWATs and water networks and training staff in operation and maintenance of water supply services. With Bentiu and Rubkona town councils, Concern supports waste collection in collaboration with business organisations in markets. In Guit, Concern is supporting Water Management Committees to manage water points. While Concern constructs latrines in Bentiu PoC, in Rubkona urban areas, Guit, Aweil North and Aweil West, communities lead latrine construction through a CLTS approach adapted for emergencies. Communities identify safe sites for latrines that ensure privacy, dignity and are accessible for PSNs. Concern provides materials and supervision communities with construction. Hygiene promotion in Bentiu PoC and Rubkona urban areas is done by HHPs who are volunteers from communities and VHCs in Guit. In locations where Concern implements Nutrition programmes, volunteers from the community such as CNVs, lead mothers and Male Change Agents support hygiene promotion. Concern has MoUs with SMoHs and CHDs to support WASH infrastructure in health facilities.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The programme prioritises life-saving WASH assistance to reduce morbidity due to waterborne illnesses. However, Concern’s WASH interventions are aligned with national objectives. Concern is the Unity State WASH Cluster focal point and is involved in coordinating WASH partners to ensure their responses adhere to the national WASH Cluster priorities. Nutrition interventions are in line with the MoH guidelines on MIYCN that include improving hygiene practices. For activities on WASH in health facilities, Concern aims to align with the government’s EVD preparedness plan.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Despite R-ARCSS and improvements in security, there are still considerable protection and safety risks. Bentiu PoC, while protected by UNMISS and UNPOL still has risks, in particular sexual and gender based violence. Concern has a referral systems for proection cases to Health and Protection partners. Concern informs camp management of any protection concerns for measures to be taken to reduce risks. In Rubkona and Yei urban there is relative stability allowing for rehabilitation of water networks to ensure water supply for the population and support IDPs and returnees benefiting from Concern’s SNFI programme with recovery. However, with IDP and returnee arrivals, there is potential for tensions over land access and HLP will be considered in this programme when planning WASH services. Concern currently coordinates with CCCM partners and local authorities and in Yei, will continue to coordinate with the South Sudan law society which is involved in HLP matters when planning WASH interventions. In Aweil West and Aweil North, security is relatively calm allowing for linking with Concern’s resilience programmes that focus on addressing the causes of morbidiy and malnutrition. However there are protection and safety risks, particularly for women and girls when collecting water. Concern will incorporate protection in the programme including water points will only operate during daylight hours and locating latrines close to households, ensuring they are gender segregated and lockable and designed to ensure privacy.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

While prioritising life-saving WASH services, the response also incorporates sustainability. Focus on recruiting local staff and casual employees to operate and maintain water supply services, clean and maintain latrines, for environmental health services and hygiene promotion provides them with skills that they can use beyond the programme. The SMoWPI and WMCs will have improved capacity to operate and maintain water supply services to eventually continue theses services without on-going support from Concern. With communities, Concern has supported them to establish a user fee for water supply to reduce dependency on humanitarian agencies and fund water provision. Through the CLTS, communities will be improve their ability to construct safe and dignified latrines after the programme has been completed. Hygiene promotion through the training of trainer approach will support communities to be able to prioritise the main hygiene risks affecting their community members and hygiene practices to be prioritised. SMoH and CHD will have skills to ensure provision of water and sanitation services and hygiene promotion with a focus on IPC beyond the programme.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Concern will design latrines with communities to ensure those with mobility issues such as disabled and elderly can access latrines. WASH activities will ensure services are gender-sensitive and consider female and male roles such as in water collection. Concern will have equal number female and male HPPs and Water Management Committee and VHC members to ensure both women and men are involved in decision-making on priorities for hygiene promotion for women, men, girls and boys. To minimise risks of disputes over water resources, rehabilitation of water points will be done with community leaders, CCCM and Protection partners and local authorities to locate water points in locations that will not result in conflict. Concern will consult with both women and men in latrine design so it meets their needs for safety and privacy. As women are traditionally responsible for water collection and have the most protection risks, water points will be placed that is safe distance from households and will only operate during daylight hours and water flows are at a rate that minimise queueing times. Latrines construction will be done in consultation with communities to ensure safety and privacy and will be located with 5km of households. Latrines and bathing facilities will be sex-segregated and be lockable to reduce GBV risks. The CRM will be used to identify and refer protection cases at WASH facilities and refer to Health and Protection partners and inform future WASH responses.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Beneficiaries will be consulted on monitoring in the project, participating in management of water points, sanitation facilities, and hygiene promotion. This will be important with regards to female and male WASH needs such as if they feel safe accessing water points and if privacy, safety and dignity is respected at latrines with monitoring information from HHPs directly from communities. KAP surveys include female and male FGDs to provide a safe space to share opinions on the WASH programme and if the programme had impacts on reducing illness and malnutrition for women, men, girls and boys, if PSNs could access WASH services and if there were protection, security and safety risks and if issues such as access to water resources had a positive or negative impact on social dynamics. Concern will conduct regular conflict analyses and respects do no harm principles, incorporating in project designs. In Bentiu PoC, the CRM is managed by the camp manager, IOM. In BBR locations, Aweil West, Aweil North and Yei, Concern has a CRM where beneficiaries can provide feedback or complaints through approaching Concern staff, in writing and in NBeG through a dedicated telephone line. Complaints are reviewed by a CRM committee independent of the programme and accepted or rejected. Accepted complaints are referred to Programme Managers for action while sensitive complaints such as protection or fraud cases are referred confidentially to the Country Director. Concern has a Code of Conduct and Program Participant Protection Policy, Anti-trafficking in Persons Policy and Child Safe-guarding Policy which all those involved in programmes must sign and included is mandatory reporting of breaches of these policies. Concern aims to address PSEA issues and Concern staff and committies will receive training or awareness sessions on PSEA and Child safeguarding. Concern’s programme will adhere to CHS and teams review quarterly that their programmes are in line with the CHS.
