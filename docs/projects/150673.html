Project Summary

The project overall objective is to ensure protection for most vulnerable Gazan households who are either displaced/evicted or at immediate risk of displacement/eviction by providing adequate temporary shelter support (Shelter and NFIs Cluster Objective 1). Providing essential shelter support meets basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation (Strategic Objective 2) and strengthens their capacity to cope with the protracted crisis (Strategic Objective 3).
286 families (1.602 individuals) with the worst score of socioeconomic vulnerabilities were identified as being in critical need for temporary shelter support. Factors reflecting socioeconomic vulnerability include socio-demographic and socio-economic data, such as characteristics of the head of household (especially female/widow/elderly/PWD-headed), income and dependency ratio, poverty, debt and the use of other negative coping mechanisms, the status of household assets.
To quickly restore temporary access to adequate shelter for the identified 286 most vulnerable households already evicted or displaced/in process of eviction/at risk of eviction or displacement, NRC will provide temporary shelter support. This includes cash for rent for a period of up to 6 months, essential household NFIs including water storage, re-integration kits, winterization and summarization NFIs (e.g. shelter kit, bedding kit).

Needs

Lack of cash, social transfers, employment opportunities severely impacted the ability of vulnerable households to afford safe & adequate housing. Most affected among those in acute poverty are those living in rented accommodations unable to pay rent & either evicted or at risk of eviction becoming temporarily homeless or living in inadequate/unsafe shelter conditions. Based on available data from MoPWH, 160 renter-families are currently at risk of eviction pending final legal action. 20 out of them have their head of household already imprisoned for the inability to pay accumulated rental debt. Data from Technical Office of Supreme Judicial Council shows that highest number of imprisonment orders in 2018 were issued in Gaza and North Gaza Governorates. Also, evicted families without alternative shelter or hosting solutions are sporadically visible in public places setting up temporary shelters or moving to inadequate & unsafe places. In Sep/Oct 2018, NRC conducted 2 FGDs with 20 male and female renters in Gaza & North Gaza, KIIs with 7 landlords, 19 household vulnerability assessments of male and female renters to identify the severity of evictions/risk of eviction including gender, age, and protection concerns. Main findings are:- 10% of renters change residence every few months, 50% changed their rental units several times in recent years due to their inability to pay rent.- 95% face difficulties finding new rental unit as landlords request regular income. - 95% of renters mentioned that landlords took measures to guarantee their deserved rent: cut of utilities (10%), complaint to the police (15%), forced to sign payable bonds (20%), taking assets (10%).- 40% accumulated rental debts up to 9 months. - 25% were evicted due to inability to pay rent/receiving TSCA irregularly. - Evicted families sometimes separate core family to live with different host families or move to live in storage places or garages inadequate for living- 86% of landlords confirmed inability to collect rent, for 72% it is the only source of income.- Lack of legal awareness among tenants & landlords on HLP rights/responsibilities/eviction. 42% of landlords rent their houses based on verbal agreement. - 60% of renter-households show moderate to high socioeconomic vulnerability.- 5% are hardship cases registered with MoSD but have not received assistance.- 80% are unemployed (53%) or reporting unpredictable employment.- 79% lack basic furniture, household equipment (95%), kitchen items (47%), bedding sets (63%). - Coping strategies: stop paying utility bills (9%), change place of residence (11%), sell assets (13%), stop paying rent (15%), accumulate debts (24%). 4% live with other nuclear families to share rental costs. Children drop out of school to support life expenses. Conducted FGDs show that eviction/risk of eviction affects males & females of all ages but women/girls/boys face specific concerns. Female breadwinners (especially divorced) feel a particular sense of insecurity and fear from the landlord. Children face discriminatory behavior from landlords. Evictions/risk of eviction leads to family separations of parents & children which adds psychosocial burden on wives taking care of children separately. Negative gender/age related coping mechanisms: early marriage of girls & child labor to reduce financial burden; sharing rental unit with more than one family affects privacy especially at night for girls & boys of different ages; school dropout; irregular school attendance especially girls; frequent change of residencies affects psychosocial stability of boys/girls at home & in school resulting in isolation, aggressiveness, shouting, trauma.

Activities

To quickly restore temporary access to adequate shelter for most vulnerable households already evicted or displaced/in process of eviction/at risk of eviction or displacement, NRC will provide temporary shelter support. This includes cash for rent for a period of up to 6 months for 286 families, essential household NFIs including water storage, re-integration kits, winterization and summarization NFIs (e.g. shelter kit, bedding kit) for 226 families (out of the 286 families that will receive cash for rent). The latter will consider specific needs of males and females of all ages in the household. When cash for rent is paid, NRC will ensure that the landlord provides minimal physical and climate protection in exchange for receiving rent in advance. NRC identified the most vulnerable renters based on a socioeconomic household vulnerability assessment. The factors reflecting socioeconomic vulnerability include socio-demographic and socio-economic data, such as characteristics of the head of household (especially female/widow/elderly/PWD-headed), income and dependency ratio, poverty, debt and the use of other negative coping mechanisms, the status of household assets. The scoring system is designed to prioritize female/child/elderly/disabled headed households, heads of households with high dependency rates, large family size and overcrowded housing situations, and those with protection concerns. NRC engages male and female beneficiaries of all ages in designing the most suitable response to each renter-households’ specific gender/age/protection concerns. If NRC cannot address it directly, it ensures proper referrals. When choosing the modality of transfer, male and female family members will be included in choosing the modality best fit to their needs. Complementary NFI support will – case by case – consider gender/age specific needs and concerns especially as females are culturally responsible for household-related responsibilities. To minimize identified protection threats due to evictions and eviction-risks especially for women, boys and girls, the following activities are tailored to meet the identified gender and protection related needs: 1) Shelter field staff is trained to identify and refer cases with protection threats such as GBV, child marriage, violence against children. Boys and girls displaying behavioral problems due to having frequently moved residencies and schools or out of fear being evicted any time will be referred to actors providing psychosocial support outside school or referred to be included in NRC’s Better Learning Program that provides psychoeducation support in a large number of schools in Gaza. 2) In case of cash for rent, it will be ensured that rental contracts are signed with both husband and wife and that males and females are provided with HLP information relevant to their rights and responsibilities as renters but also to understand rights and responsibilities of the landlord. It may also include negotiating an agreement with landlords to redeem bonds securing previous debt resulting from unpaid rent. This is ensured through NRC’s Information, Counselling, and Legal Assistance Programme. 3) NFI-contributions will consider specific needs of males and females of all ages in the household.

Indicators

NRC’s M&E frameworks ensure that relevant M&E tools are used to meet planned targets with quality. Project staff, in collaboration with M&E unit, ensures that a tracking and reporting system is in place to document project progress toward desired outcomes. An indicator matrix records data from the field on a monthly basis to measure progress and provide corrective measures where needed. NRC supports quality control measures and accountability towards beneficiaries. In addition, the M&E unit conducts regular field visits to monitor project progress and feedback key findings to project teams. NRC conducts a post-project lessons learnt exercise to continuously improve efficiency and effectiveness in implementation. On a technical level, NRC shelter field and office staff tightly monitors the progress in implementation. Community outreach and engagement: during the design phase of the project, male and female households of vulnerable and affected communities especially youth and elderly were engaged during the identification of needs reflected in NRC’s conducted needs assessments (October 2018). During implementation, identified gender/age/protection concerns are addressed, if not directly by NRC than through ensuring proper referrals. At the start of the project, essential project information including selection criteria and access to NRC’s feedback and complaint mechanism is communicated to community representative structures. Project information is communicated to both male and female family members in direct communication and through information leaflets which also informs them about NRC’s feedback and complaint mechanism. NRC communicates a feedback and complaint mechanism to target-households to continuously assess the quality and accessibility of the provided response. Due to the identified risk that cash for rent might raise expectations for affected households and incentivize vulnerable households to purposeful displacement in order to access cash, community representative structures will be involved to manage expectations about the limited and temporary fund and to channel potential complaints. NRC conducts post-distribution monitoring and post-project surveys with beneficiary households to measure the efficiency and effectiveness of the assistance and the satisfaction of male and female beneficiaries of all ages. NRC’s local partner IDCO will be involved in the post-distribution monitoring and beneficiary satisfaction survey at the end of the project. At the start of the project, each household benefiting from NRC’s temporary shelter support will be provided with an information leaflet about the main project information and how to access NRC’s feedback and complaint mechanisms. Male and female beneficiary-households are engaged when defining the assistance modality that best meets their needs, are fully engaged in the implementation process, and are visited again after the end of the project as part of a post-project review to collect feedback on the efficiency and effectiveness of the project. Outcome indicatorsThe below 3 outcome indicators relate to the delivered services and will be measured through M+E monitoring visits and post-distribution surveys. The total number of benefiting household numbers will be disaggregated based on gender and age. % of assisted households living in adequate temporary shelter (target: 90%)% of households who remain in their housing unit 3 months after the provided support (target: 95%) % beneficiaries who report/are observed utilizing the shelter contribution as intended within 1 month of distribution (versus trade or selling, etc.) (target: 100%) Output indicatorsThe below output indicators related to individuals will provide sex/age-disaggregated data. # of individuals received Temporary Shelter supporttarget: 1.602 (785 female, 817 male)# of HH provided with climate protection assistance (emergency shelter or household NFIs in-kind or in cash) target: 226
