Project Summary

The project, will be implemented by RDRS Bangladesh with funding from Diaconia Evangelical Church of Czech Brethren (DECCB) and Australian Lutheran World Service (ALWS), aims to strengthen food security and resilience among Rohingya refugees and host communities in Cox’s Bazar through community-centered, climate-smart agricultural activities and sustainable livelihood programs. Building on previous humanitarian initiatives, this project addresses gaps in food assistance and self-reliance by providing practical solutions to enhance household nutrition, foster economic independence, and reduce dependency on external aid.
The two main objectives of this project are:
1. To support food security and strengthen the resilience of Rohingya refugees/FDMNs through climate-smart, context-specific food production activities and early interventions.
2. To strengthen the household food security, nutrition, and resilience of host communities through climate-smart livelihoods, focusing on food production, food management, and market linkages.
RDRS will coordinate and collaborate with key Food Security Sector (FSS) actors to ensure the project aligns with priorities and sectoral strategies. Regular engagement with FSS actors will facilitate joint assessments, knowledge sharing, capacity-building initiatives, and monitoring efforts, fostering a unified approach to addressing the challenges.
The project will be implemented in Rohingya Camps (11, 12, and 18): Targeting a combined population of 17136 individuals considering new arrivals and existing, the project emphasizes self-sufficiency through food production and economic empowerment. This project includes initiatives such as homestead gardening, reforestation, and cash-for-work initiatives that will enable refugees to cultivate their food and contribute to community resilience. The project will foster market linkages for input and output supplies, ensuring the sustainable flow of resources for home-based food production. Special efforts are directed toward building community cohesion by creating empowerment opportunities for youth and women, thereby enhancing social capital. Reforestation efforts, coupled with the planting and maintenance of green spaces, aim to reinforce environmental stability while offering paid work for refugees, thus promoting both ecological and economic resilience. Disaster preparedness is also a priority, with cash-for-work initiatives targeting flood control and other preventive measures to safeguard the camps against natural hazards.
The project will be implemented in the host Communities (Rajapalong and Jaliapalong unions of Ukhiya Upazila): The project extends its impact to the host communities, focusing on enhancing food security and resilience through diversified, climate-smart agricultural practices. By supporting income-generating activities, poultry and livestock rearing, and home gardening, it aims to empower 1500 vulnerable individuals economically. Training and market linkages will be established for effective input and output management, enabling small-scale producers to better access local markets. Youth and women empowerment programs will provide training, resources, and entrepreneurial support, fostering economic stability and community integration. Age and disability inclusion will be prioritized to ensure the active participation of all community members. Accountability to affected populations will be maintained by communities in decision-making, feedback mechanisms, and transparent communication to build trust and ownership.
Through climate-smart agriculture, self-reliant food production, and livelihood initiatives, this project aims to reduce dependency on external aid, foster social cohesion, and promote sustainable development. Community engagement and disaster preparedness will strengthen local ownership and resilience, enabling vulnerable households to meet their food needs independently.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The food security situation in Camps 11, 12, and 18 remains critical, with 93,995 individuals in 18,789 households heavily reliant on the World Food Programme (WFP) for essential food assistance. This dependency highlights the need for sustainable, expanded interventions to reduce reliance on external aid. In Camp 11, organizations such as BRAC, Uttaran, World Vision, and Bangladesh Red Crescent Society (BDRCS) have reached 4,000 of the 6,500 households, leaving 2,500 unsupported. Camp 12, with 30,167 people across 5,875 households, receives assistance for 2,750 households from BRAC, Mukti Cox’s Bazar, Shushilan, and Dushtha Shasthya Kendra (DSK), leaving 3,125 dependents on WFP. Similarly, Camp 18 faces challenges, with BRAC, Community Development Centre (CODEC), and World Vision supporting 1,200 of the 6,414 households, leaving 5,214 without adequate support. Overall, 10,839 households (58% of the total) still depend entirely on WFP’s support, indicating a pressing need for resilient, self-sustaining interventions. RDRS Bangladesh, with its extensive experience and resource mobilization capabilities, is well-positioned to lead these efforts.Community consultations reveal a preference for sustainable food sources like homestead gardening, poultry, pigeon rearing, and aquaculture, which can provide fresh produce, protein, and income, thereby reducing negative coping mechanisms. Employment opportunities, particularly cash-for-work programs, are also crucial, enabling families to access extra food items and improve overall well-being. RDRS Bangladesh aims to implement a multifaceted strategy that addresses these needs, targeting inclusivity by involving 3% of persons with disabilities (PWDs). This approach aligns with RDRS’s goals of fostering resilience and self-reliance, reducing reliance on external aid, and building a foundation for self-sufficiency.Recent assessments, such as the 2023 Refugee-Influx Emergency Vulnerability Assessment (REVA) and the 2024 Inter-Sector Needs Assessment (ISNA), underscore the urgency of these efforts, showing that a significant percentage of Rohingya households still cannot afford adequate food despite regular aid. With its established presence and expertise, RDRS Bangladesh is uniquely qualified to address food security gaps, supporting a resilient, empowered community capable of meeting future challenges independently. This comprehensive strategy not only addresses immediate needs but also builds long-term resilience and sustainability in the community.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

RDRS Bangladesh’s resilience innovation project in Cox’s Bazar, guided by the WFP Protection and Accountability Policy, focuses on safety, dignity, and inclusivity. It conducts regular safety and risk assessments to identify protection risks and establishes safe spaces for vulnerable groups. The project ensures activities are accessible to women, children, the elderly, and persons with disabilities, promoting equal access to resources. Culturally appropriate outreach methods are used to engage communities. To prevent sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), it enforces a strict code of conduct with zero tolerance for SEA, provides mandatory safeguarding training, and establishes confidential reporting systems with clear response measures. Awareness campaigns raise awareness on gender-based violence (GBV), child protection, and SEA prevention, offering support services for survivors. The project implements child safeguarding measures to protect children from exploitation. Community feedback mechanisms (CFMs) gather input from beneficiaries, ensuring transparency and responsiveness. Inclusive communication tools reach all community members, including those with low literacy or disabilities. The project acts on feedback received through CFMs, resolving issues in a timely manner and informing the community of actions taken. These strategies ensure project activities are safe, dignified, inclusive, and respectful of all beneficiaries' rights, fostering a protective environment with accountability and transparency.

2.3 - Age & Disability

The project will ensure inclusive and equitable support by targeting approximately 3% of persons with disabilities (PWD) within both the Rohingya refugee and host communities. All staff and volunteers will receive training on sector-specific inclusive programming, emphasizing best practices for age and disability sensitivity. RDRS Bangladesh will conduct assessments to ensure that all services are meaningfully accessible to individuals of all abilities. The project will include a total of 20% youth from Camps 11, 12, and 18.Throughout the project, accessible social behavior change communication (SBCC) materials will be utilized to enhance reach and engagement. For PWD and the elderly, the project will provide door-to-door services to accommodate specific needs. Additionally, the project will prioritize the participation of women, including women with disabilities, in forming community groups and leadership development initiatives, fostering inclusivity in community decision-making.Capacity-building initiatives will offer life skills training to all participants, including PWD, following sector standards for inclusion. The project will also focus on strengthening knowledge and access to existing support services, facilitating broader integration and self-sufficiency. By embedding inclusivity within all activities, this project aims to create a supportive environment for all community members, enhancing resilience and empowerment for everyone involved.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to affected populations (AAP) is central to the project's approach, ensuring that the Rohingya refugees and host communities in Cox’s Bazar are actively involved in decision-making processes and have access to transparent, timely information. RDRS Bangladesh will prioritize a participatory approach by engaging community members from Camps 11, 12, and 18, as well as the Rajapalong and Jaliapalong host communities, in the planning, implementation, and monitoring of project activities. To uphold transparency and trust, the project will establish accessible feedback and complaint mechanisms, allowing participants to voice concerns and suggestions openly. Regular feedback sessions will ensure that activities are adapted to meet the evolving needs and priorities of community members, particularly regarding climate-smart food production, livelihood initiatives, and disaster preparedness efforts. Additionally, the project will employ inclusive communication channels and behavior-change communication materials to keep communities informed and involved, emphasizing the empowerment of youth and women. By integrating AAP principles throughout its activities, the project will strengthen community ownership, foster resilience, and build a foundation for sustainable, locally-driven change in food security, resilience, and peaceful coexistence.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

RDRS Bangladesh integrates safeguarding principles into its organizational policies, practices, and programming to mainstream protection from sexual exploitation and abuse. As part of this commitment, RDRS actively engages with the Inter-Agency Standing Committee’s (IASC) for PSEA network to coordinate with the United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees’ (UNHCR) Resident Coordinator (RC) to deliver regular reports through the Inter-Sectoral Coordination Group (ISCG), including ISGC’s Senior Coordinator and heads of sub-offices in Cox’s Bazar. This comprehensive approach prioritizes the protection of vulnerable populations throughout humanitarian and development initiatives. By embedding PSEA principles into training, recruitment, and community engagement, RDRS fosters a culture of accountability and respect, enhancing safety and dignity for those served. This strategy includes regular monitoring, accessible reporting mechanisms, and the active participation of affected communities, ensuring their voices are heard and their rights are upheld. By adopting this holistic framework, RDRS not only safeguards individuals but also strengthens program integrity and trust, reinforcing its commitment to responsible and ethical humanitarian action.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The project aims to address various humanitarian needs while incorporating climate change adaptation and mitigation strategies. Key activities include tree plantation, drain line cleaning, learning sessions on Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and Disaster Risk Management (DRM), anticipatory response, and promoting climate-smart practices. Tree planting helps communities withstand extreme weather, reduces soil erosion, and absorbs CO2, contributing to global climate mitigation. Regular drain cleaning prevents waterlogging and reduces flood risk, which is essential for areas like Cox’s Bazar that are prone to heavy rainfall. Climate-smart agricultural practices equip farmers with tools to adapt to climate changes, ensuring food security while reducing greenhouse gas emissions. Additionally, organizing awareness sessions on DRR and climate change impacts educates communities about climate risks and adaptation strategies, enhancing their resilience. Involving youth as first responders and coordinating with local stakeholders streamlines disaster response efforts, creating a strong, local response network. Contingency funding (5% of the project budget) allows for rapid emergency response, providing vital resources during crises. Through these integrated efforts, the project addresses environmental issues by incorporating climate action, while also tackling broader environmental challenges within these communities.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

The project is designed to provide comprehensive emergency preparedness and response initiative to protect communities in Cox’s Bazar. Close coordination with local authorities and humanitarian agencies will ensure streamlined and effective response efforts. The project is aligned with the SOD of RDRS Bangladesh for emergency response programs in Cox’s Bazar. All Activities will be guided by SOD, adhering to the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) and SPHERE guidelines.An essential feature of the initiative is the allocation of 5% of the project budget as contingency funding, enabling rapid crisis response. This contingency fund will be utilized following a thorough needs assessment during disasters or emergencies. Procurement activities will adhere to RDRS’s established manual, maintaining accountability and transparency. Additionally, the initiative draws on insights from humanitarian trend reports and fact sheets published by the United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs to address humanitarian crises effectively. The project also emphasizes capacity building through specialized training for staff and volunteers to create a resilient response team. Youth engagement as first responders is a priority to enhance community resilience, supported by awareness sessions on disaster risk reduction to foster a culture of preparedness.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Youth engagement is a core component of the project, with a total of 1,000 youth participants in Camps 11, 12, and 18. Recognizing the vital role of young people in building resilient and self-sufficient communities, the project offers targeted opportunities for skill development, economic empowerment, and environmental stewardship. Youth will be actively involved in climate-smart food production, home gardening, reforestation, and cash-for-work initiatives, allowing them to directly contribute to household food security and community resilience.By engaging youth in meaningful activities, the project aims to foster social cohesion and create pathways toward economic independence, reducing reliance on external aid. Youth will also receive training to strengthen their capacity to drive positive changes within their communities. In 2024, RDRS Bangladesh became a member of youth working groups, contributing to the empowerment and development of young individuals. These initiatives are designed to help young people develop critical life skills, build self-confidence, and gain the knowledge necessary to navigate complex socio-economic challenges. By investing in their personal and professional growth, the project aims to ensure that youth are equipped not only to contribute to their communities' resilience but also to thrive in the wider economy.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

The project enhances self-reliance and resilience among Rohingya refugees and host communities through climate-smart agricultural practices and sustainable livelihoods. Activities include home gardening, reforestation, and income-generating initiatives that reduce aid dependency. Cash-for-work programs empower refugees economically and improve disaster preparedness. The project supports host communities with training in poultry and livestock rearing, home gardening, and business development, enhancing economic independence and market access. These efforts align with Cox’s Bazar’s goals of economic diversification, poverty reduction, and inclusive growth, fostering a resilient, self-sustaining community.

The project activities align with the development initiatives and priorities of the area, particularly in promoting long-term development and addressing urgent needs. Hygiene corners improve the health of girls, while youth entrepreneurship training fosters local economic growth, all of which contribute to the district's broader development objectives..
