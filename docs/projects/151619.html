Project Summary

Malnutrition is one of the most persistent humanitarian challenges in Somalia despite concerted efforts to address it. Acute malnutrition in children between 6-59 months is a direct outcome indicator of recent changes in nutritional status which could be attributed to changes in food security, WASH and health situations among others. The significant deterioration of malnutrition situation seen among the IDPs and Bay agro-pastorals can be attributed to high morbidity (disease incidence e.g. AWD, measles …etc), low humanitarian support, poor child feeding and caring practices. Similarly, partners on the ground generally felt that food insecurity, limited health service availability, like poor EPI coverage, and increased morbidity, poor health seeking behaviour, and difficulty of accessing clean water supply are driving factors for the current situation. Nutrition assessments in Somalia showed higher prevalence of GAM in boys compared to girls across all under five children. Overall level of acute malnutrition is Serious (median GAM of 14 per cent), this is an improvement from Critical in 2017 Gu (median GAM of 17.4 per cent). Bay agro-pastoral had the median GAM of 13.1 and the morbidity rate of above 15.1%. They also had the lowest measles vaccination and vitamin A supplementation status (14.8% and 13.9 %), respectively This project will focus on the Bay agro-pastorals where PASOS will increase the health services coverage among the refugee returnees, host communities and other vulnerable populations through lifesaving, preventive and promotive health care services such as blanket supplementary feeding programme.

Needs

Malnutrition (undernutrition) remains the major problem persisted over time in Somalia with high levels of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) well above emergency threshold of 15%. During 2016 and 2017, the humanitarian situation in Somalia continued to deteriorate because of the poor rains in Gu and Deyr seasons of 2016 and 2017, causing widespread food insecurity, deepening health, nutritional and water related issues and exacerbated by large-scale movement of people. In 2018, the overall food security situation in Somalia has improved when compared to a year ago, the latest food security and nutrition assessment by FAO’s FSNAU, has revealed. This is due to the above-average performance of the April to June Gu rains. The results indicate that most pastoralist areas have seen an improvement in pasture, water availability and increasing herd sizes. However, despite the positive outlook and gains made, humanitarian assistance needs to be sustained to support the people in need. The trends in GAM prevalence(WHZ) across remained at serious levels when compared to last six months back. Furthermore, this is evidenced by the high admissions in nutrition sites/centers over the months as well as compared to similar months of previous years. As a result, nearly 954,007 children are expected to be acutely malnourished, including 173,594 who are expected to be severely malnourished during the next one year period (Sep 2018-Sep 2019). The surveys indicate Critical levels of GAM (15-29.9%) in 12 out of 33 populations surveyed (7 IDP and 5 Rural) while Critical levels of SAM prevalence (≥4-5.6) were only seen among IDPs in Mogadishu and Guban pastoral. Among Bay agro-pastorals the GAM levels are serious at 13.1 with the morbidity rate of above 15%. In, the assessments found out that Bay agro-pastoral has the lowest measles vaccination and vitamin A supplementation status (14.8% and 13.9 %), respectively) indicating that majority of the population do not receive the services.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The Bay agro-pastorals targeted population risk of exclusion is medium since PASOS will ensure that data is collected among the beneficiaries with the help of already community structures and effective approaches such as outreach services so that all the vulnerable populations will be reached. Lack of documentation among the returnees and other vulnerable host communities in the rural areas does not mean that they are excluded but rather their vulnerability and nutrition assessment will be used. PASOS will also ensure that all the affected population understand the process the importance of receiving the required health services and micronutrients in order to improve their nutrition status. Displaced individuals will also be reached so that to receive the nutrition services in their areas of habitation.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The risks of exclusion based on gender and age is medium. The project’s gender focus creates new challenges for women and/or HH and community conflict by promoting women’s and age equality. PASOS will mitigate this risk by ensuring that activities targeting women include men in appropriate ways; male leaders engaged to support women’s involvement in decision making; gender impact of project (positive & negative) closely monitored; implementing agencies trained to refer GBV cases.Issues related to age exclusion will also be mitigated. There is a risk of excluding child headed household in the project. CHWs and local authorities will be involved in the project and will be closely monitored to ensure that they receive all the nutrition services and referred for further assistance.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The main risks include: a) Inaccessibility of project locations due to security concerns. The risk is medium and PASOS will ensure that all the project locations are in both secure and potentially insecure areas to ensure continuous implementation of activities; and will also regularly review security status with local authorities and institutions.
