Project Summary

IMC proposes to support life-saving WASH interventions to vulnerable populations in Damascus, Rural Damascus, Dara'a, and Hama governorates. Locations have been selected based on current and planned areas of intervention in the Health and Protection sectors, given IMC’s aims at further strengthening integration among its areas of expertise.
Considering the health and WASH links, in terms of prevention and risks mitigation of communicable diseases (including COVID 19) IMC proposes to complement health education sessions at IMC health facilities with hygiene promotion sessions. Furthermore, and since most of the plan is to support COVID 19 prevention, the selected locations have high population density, including Damascus, which is among the areas most at risk. Among the proposed areas of intervention (Damascus, Rural Damascus and Dara’a), IMC has been implementing health activities through its static and mobile facilities. Although the majority of the health consultations at IMC facilities are related to NCDs, water-borne and infectious diseases are also common, mostly due low functionality of WASH facilities, unaffordability of hygiene items and low awareness on hygiene practices. Furthermore, the selected areas host a high number of displaced population, some of whom still reside in collective shelters or overcrowded areas, with insufficient and inadequate WASH facilities.
IMC will also coordinate with MOLA to implement hygiene promotion campaigns and awareness activities in communities and schools; support activities related to ensure adequate IPC and hygienic standards in health facilities and schools (including capacity building, provision of supplies, light rehabilitation of WASH facilities); waste management support through provision of communal garbage bins; provision of essential NFIs (soap, head-lice shampoo, sanitary napkins, PPE for frontline workers).
In schools, IMC estimates to target 30,000 students, 20,000 in Rural Damascus and 10,000 in Hama. The schools will be identified in coordination with MOE through the support of MOLA, based on severity ranking and gaps in response. In open communities and health facilities, IMC estimates to target 54,800 beneficiaries in Suran (Hama), 2,000 beneficiaries in Hamra (Hama), 10,000 in Al-Sanamyen (Dara’a), 4,000 in Barzeh and Jaramana (Damascus and Rural Damascus), 4,500 at IMC medical mobile teams and 300 at IMC Community center in Jaramana.
IMC will focus on the most commonly spread WASH borne diseases identified through needs assessments, and will include COVID 19 related messages. Context-adapted IEC materials and community participatory methods will be used to ensure effective knowledge transfer and behavior change. In order to maintain a community healthy and safe hygiene environment, the intervention will adopt a resilience-oriented approach through capacity building activities, and will directly engage target populations in efforts to promote best hygiene knowledge, attitudes and practices. The support on waste management services at communal level will strengthen the local system, which is crucial to ensure increased availability to this basic service and thus reducing morbidity and mortality rates linked to water-borne and infectious diseases.
Recognizing the specific needs and concerns of women, men, adolescents and people with disabilities, the program will be open to all population segments, ensuring their meaningful participation in the project cycle. This will ensure that the program will contribute in empowering and increase the resilience of the target communities.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2021, 59% or 12.2 million people require WASH assistance. Specifically: water quality affects 3.3 million; water quantity, 1.4 million. Solid waste management is insufficient for 4.1 million, and hygiene supplies for 6.4 million. WASH needs in facilities, like schools and healthcare facilities, remain very high. According to the early 2020 WASH Household Assessment, 38% students complained about WASH related issues, specifically toilets cleanliness, lack of functioning toilets, barriers to effective handwashing, and protection oriented aspects of dysfunctional facilities. With the COVID-19 pandemic in addition to infectious and water-borne diseases concerns, appropriate WASH conditions in schools and healthcare facilities are critical to reduce transmission.The deterioration of economic conditions remain a main challenge for vulnerable communities and households in accessing safe and equitable WASH services such as water, hygiene supplies and tools. Some specific groups were severely affected such as women in reproductive age, who have challenges in accessing hygiene items including sanitary pads. Community transmission of COVID-19 is anticipated to persist into 2021, especially given low awareness of population, which adds to the sub-optimal livingconditions, the damaged infrastructures and the low availability of public resources.Therefore the priorities remain the mainstreaming of COVID-19 response throughoutWASH interventions, with a focus on ensuring appropriate water, sanitation andhandwashing facilities and services are available and accessible at targeted sites;providing essential supplies to enable the most vulnerable population to practice and adopt safe hygiene practices.

5 - Implementation of activities

The proposed activities will focus on life-saving WASH interventions at facilities while supporting resilience of existing WASH systems and community capabilities.. All proposed activities are in line with 2021 HRP document and 2021 WASH Sector strategy. IMC will directly implement the activities, in collaboration with MOLA, MOH and relevant authorities at local level, while also coordinating with other actors working in the same areas of intervention. . Activities will include:1) Conduct hygiene awareness and education sessions for adults and schools’ students, through specifically designed activities based on age and sex and in consideration of people with physical disabilities.2) Implement WASH interventions in the targeted facilities (including education, health and protection facilities) through minor rehabilitation, provision of supplies and capacity building for service providers.3) Distribution of essential hygiene and PPE supplies to most vulnerable population and service providers.4) Support local authorities in implementing effective and safe waste management disposal services, by providing supplies and support with capacity building activities.IMC recognizes that women, men, boys and girls are affected differently by the crisis. As such, a gender and age sensitive approach will be adopted during assessments and project implementation, ensuring the involvement of all targeted groups. Target communities will be actively engaged throughout the project cycle. IMC has an established beneficiary feedback and complaint mechanism, whereby beneficiaries can communicate and express their feedback, suggestions or complaints about IMC services and assistance.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

At the assessment stage, and through disaggregated FGDs and interviews, women, girls, men and boys and people with disabilities) will be given the voice to provide their concerns, feedback, needs and capabilities. This phase will enhance designing project’s activities in alignment with gender and age specific needs related to unequal access to WASH services. Risk1- Lack of separation of WASH facilities in schools; lack of doors/locks increase exposure to GBV risks. •Likelihood: medium, Impact: high •Mitigation: Light rehabilitation of WASH facilities in schools to maintain safe access and consultations with girls and schools’ teachers/principals. Risk 2- Areas provided within schools to IMC staff for implementation of activities expose students to risks of injuries or harm or do not allow all students to access. • Likelihood: low, Impact: high •Mitigation: Select areas for activities implementation in schools taking into consideration safe and equitable access for all students and by consulting with schools’ management prior to the start of the activities. Risk 3- Distribution of essential hygiene supplies creates tensions among beneficiaries due to lack of clarity of selection criteria. • Likelihood: low, Impact: high •Mitigation: Selection criteria to be set in consultation with health/Protection team based on vulnerability, and to be communicated clearly with all beneficiaries visiting the facilities Risk 4: PSEA and safeguarding related risks. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. MITIGATION 4: IMC policies and procedures in place, including effective, safe and confidential reporting mechanisms. All staff provided with training and refresher training on PSEA and safeguarding issues.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1- Transportation of goods from Damascus to project sites is delayed and/or suspended • Likelihood: medium Impact: high • Mitigation 1: Constant and regular follow-up with concerned authorities and stakeholders to ensure timely dispatch of supplies based on distribution plans. Mitigation 2: When feasible, procure goods from markets in targeted locations. • Residual risk level: medium.Risk 2: Delayed/lack of approvals for implementation of activities and for monitoring visits or security concerns affecting access to intervention areas. • Mitigation 2: Regular follow up and communication with relevant stakeholders to obtain approval to access non IMC facilities. Info-sharing and coordination with UNDSS and Security focal points.RISK 3: Sanctions delaying bank transfers and thus affecting timely implementation. MITIGATION 3: Maintain bank accounts with at least two banks; advocate on waiving of international sanctions; info sharing with other INGOs to identify solutions. RISK 4: COVID 19 (lockdowns and infection among staff) causing activities to be suspended. MITIGATION 4: request waiver to allow movement of staff working on providing humanitarian services; adherence to preventive and isolation measures as needed.
