Project Summary

This project supports children in Idleb and Aleppo from the host community and IDPs, which includes the most vulnerable groups (children under 12 years, IDP children, children with difficulties (incl. disabilities and disorders; later only referred to as children with difficulties) to provide equitable access for quality learning opportunities for girls and boys in both primary and secondary school level in both formal and non-formal settings. The implementation approach will be focusing on the adoption of the education program in Violet to ensure the continuity of education and provide access to learning opportunities throughout three modalities (physical, blended, distance) due to the COVID-19 pandemic. The project design complies with INEE minimum standards and safe-programming principles for children to ensure that children have access to inclusive and equitable quality education that provides lifelong learning opportunities.

4 - Needs

Based on HNO 2022, There are over 7 million children (3-17 years) in need of education support, The total people in need that Whole of Syria aims to support is 6.6 million people, The number of people in need is illustrated as follows, Children PiN (3-5 severity): 6,396,557, Education Personnel PiN (3-5 severity): 213,219, Total PiN (3-5 severity) Children+Education Personnel: 6,609,775. The national rate of self-reported attendance five to 17-year olds for pre-tertiary education (formal and non-formal learning through in-class, blended or remote modalities) for the 2020-2021 academic year was 82%. The findings of a school infrastructure survey carried out in November 2020 indicates that if the total number of school aged children five to-17 years’ old is divided by the total number functional classrooms, it is estimated that there is one functioning classroom per 54 children. these classrooms need appropriate heating during winter to keep student attendance and provide a good learning environment to resume children's education and learning.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Risk of discrimination or perceptions of discrimination if the education service is provided to a specific group, e.g. IDPLikelihood: Medium; Impact: High Before implementation, Violet conducts assessments and selects most vulnerable communities based on severity; selection criteria (eligibility/vulnerability) transparently communicated through the AAP team. AAP team has access to service mapping to communicate informed with communities about alternative services. 2) Children with special needs might be excluded from education and PSS. Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High Ensure facilities and services are suitable for special needs, e.g. mobility or learning materials, conduct community events that help understanding the importance of participating in education/PSS, consultation with local stakeholders to identify excluded beneficiaries, training teachers on conflict sensitive teaching. 3) Child labor, early marriage, recruitment into armed groups. Likelihood: High; Impact: High Conduct awareness to parents (PTA) on importance of education, complementary with other assistance (cash, food baskets, NFI) to offer support to the families as positive coping mechanism. 4) Overcrowded physical infrastructure as a risk to children’s safety including war remedies Likelihood: Medium; Impact: Medium Conduct necessary rehabilitation (incl. gender sensitive WASH facilities and mobility) for a safe learning environment and mine action awareness and safety training.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Violet targets PSEA risk through a) training on PSEA for staff, BNFs, vendors; b) integrating PSEA in AAP with safe and accessible complaints mechanism; c) appointment of PSEA focal points in each location to handle reports of misconduct, d) support to survivors through case management, e) regular information of affected community on their entitlements. Potential risks:1) Misconduct of education staff against child safeguarding principlesMitigation: initial and refresher training on CSG responsibilities, behavior training for teachers, assign focal point for CSG, child-friendly AAP sessions including CSG information and how to raise complaints, zero tolerance on misconducts and rapid action after investigation.2) SEA misconduct from education staff to parents or childrenMitigation: provide information sessions to parents (sex-segregated if preferred) on expected behavior from teachers and humanitarian workers, explanation of complaints mechanism, availability of AAP staff face-to-face for sensitive complaints. 3) Request of SEA for selecting BNFs to the servicesMitigation: clear information on the provided services in terms of quality and quantity and that nothing is expected in return in any case, emphasise on complaints mechanisms, provision of multiple channels to raise complaints, referral to inter-agency hotlines as alternative.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Risk; Inaccessibility to target areas due to insecurity, large-scale military offensives, or change in territorial control with safety and security of staff/beneficiaries being compromised. Mitigation; Violet would triangulate information with available sources, use rigorous monitoring mechanism, suspend; relocate activities to areas where access is upheld; shift program resources to other areas; switch to distance learning, distribution the SLP materials. 2. Risk; Insufficient stipends continue to result in teachers and education personnel absenteeism due to additional employment and/or leaving their jobs to seek higher-paying work. Mitigation; A standardized and harmonized stipend scale that ensures adequate remuneration should reduce turnover, absenteeism, support teachers by other sectors and resources. 3. Risk: COVID 19 cases are still increasing in the targeted location, all project stakeholders and activities will be affected, and the staff will face many obstacles to conduct face-to-face activities. Mitigation: Protection Violet staff will take into consideration all Donor, Partner, Clusters, and Local Authority's recommendations in link with WHO and Health sector guidelines. The number of beneficiaries per session will be decreased, also using online modality to conduct the activities after discussion and coordination with donor and partner. Violet covers internet charge to families. 4. Risk; students dropping out of schools. M. communication through PTA and PSS
