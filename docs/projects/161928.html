Project Summary

1. Providing basic medical services
a) Providing basic medical services
• Providing the OPD service and prescribing necessary medicine with the 24/7 emergency care.
• Providing consultation and treatment for Non-Communicable Disease (NCD) patients and follow them up on every Tuesday.
b) Training by medical specialists
• Conducting training by medical specialists such as medical doctors, nurses, midwives either from DCHT HQ or/and PWJ every 3 months.
c) Data collection/analysis/monitoring
• Collecting the data regarding the diagnosis and prescription of the patients and report it to the health sector as needed.
• A specialist medical doctor monitors the prescription practice by using the data. The result of monitoring is shared through the monthly report or specialist report.
2. Providing maternal and childcare services
Providing maternal and childcare services
• Gynecologists or midwives provide ANC/PNC services.
• Providing 24/7 labor and delivery support service
• Providing consultation regarding nutrition, and food which supplements the necessary nutrition for pregnant women and malnourished children
• Providing necessary vaccination for pregnant women and children
b) Providing family planning services
• Explaining family planning methods to all patients who come to the Hakimpara clinic, and providing the methods if the patients hope to practice it.
• Following up the patients who are practicing the family planning regularly to remind them to come to the clinic again in the appropriate timing.
c) Training by medical specialists
• Conducting training by medical specialists such as medical doctors, nurses, midwives either from DCHT HQ or/and PWJ every 2 months.
d) Monitoring
• DCHT HQ doctors or PWJ medical specialists conduct monitoring every 2 months.
3. Awareness program about health and hygiene
a) Awareness program about maternal and child health
• Conducting in 3 blocks in camp 14 and 2 areas in the host community
• Supporting the operation of awareness programs whose main actors are midwives/CHW/TBA.
b) Selecting 3 target blocks of awareness program about sanitation and hygiene
• Detecting 3 blocks with the highest diarrhea case from the clinic database.
c) Selecting the topic of awareness program about sanitation and hygiene
• Conducting research in the targeted blocks and detecting the root cause of salience of diarrhea case. According to the research result, selecting the topic for the awareness program.
d) Conducting the awareness program about sanitation and hygiene once a month in each target block
• Conducting the awareness program about sanitation and hygiene targeting mainly children, collaborating with the learning center in Camp 14. After 3 months, selecting the hygiene promoters among Rohingya, and thereafter, the promoters lead the awareness program and cleaning event.
e) Distributing personal hygiene materials
• Distributing personal hygiene materials such as soaps, toothbrushes, and toothpaste, according to the topic of the awareness program.
f) Conducting the cleaning event once a month in each target block
• Conducting a cleaning event in 3 blocks in camp 14 once a month. PWJ leads the event in the first 3 months, and thereafter, the hygiene promoters take over the work. The cleaning event is held every month in each block.
g) Monitoring
• Conducting an interview with the residents in the target areas at the end of the project term. Assessing the change in the residents’ knowledge and practice from the survey result of the previous project.

Needs

Rakhine state in Myanmar has been a traditional homeland for the majority of Myanmar-Rohingyas. However, Violence in Rakhine State which began on 25 August 2017 forcibly drove almost all Rohingyas to seek refuge in neighboring countries such as Bangladesh, India, and Malaysia. Thus, they received the name of “Forcibly Displaced Myanmar Nationals (FDMN)” among UN organizations and INGO’s around the world. Bangladesh, naturally being the closest Islamic nation with a similar culture, attracted most of the refugees into the Ukhiya and Teknaf region-the southernmost point of Bangladesh. It has been noted and named by many as the fastest-growing humanitarian crisis in the world. The total number of Rohingyas in Bangladesh, according to the ISCG Situation Report for July 2019, is 911,566.The speed and scale of the influx have resulted in a critical humanitarian emergency where the local health facilities do not have the capacity to deal with the increasing demands. Further, the seasonal monsoon brings kiosks to the temporary-shelter-dwelling refugees in the form of outbreaks and water-borne diseases. Health is one of the major concerns for both Rohingya as well as the host community. Limited availability of Health care/Nutrition seems to affect women and children including pregnant and lactating mothers in both Rohingya and host communities. Especially sexual reproductive health services and general knowledge that needs to be addressed. PWJ with the help of our local partners CIS (Community Initiative Society) and DCHT (Dhaka Community Hospital Trust) will continue to play a major role to assist the existing situation as we have done from the beginning of this crisis. This partnership will provide much-needed health services - especially Mother and Childcare, nutrition and family planning and awareness, etc. under the current conditions.
