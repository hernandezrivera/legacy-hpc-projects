Project Summary

Protracted conflicts, lack of basic services and the deteriorating economic situation in Sudan, Darfur in particular, have all had profound consequences on conflict-affected persons’ immediate physical and mental well-being, living standards and long-term resilience at a time that rule of law, access to justice and government social protection remain weak. this project aims at strengthening the communities’ resilience and capacity to address various protection risks in ALGeneina, Kulbus and Kereneik localities, West Darfur State. In doing so, it will provide the target populations with direct specialized services, including psychosocial support with referral services and legal counselling as well as main protection activities. The project also includes community-awareness raising activities which will cover human rights, GBV prevention and response focusing on women, men, girls and boys in addition to persons with disabilities.

1 - Needs

The economic crisis, including high inflation, resulted in elevated levels of food insecurity and localized violence at the same time hundreds of thousands of displaced people in Darfur and other parts of the country remain in protracted displacement, requiring humanitarian assistance, Since the beginning of the year, over 365,000 people have been displaced, many of whom were already displaced as a result of conflict and factional fighting, thousands of IDPs, returnees, and resident communities were displaced families are struggling to meet their basic needs. Between January and October 2022, about 30,000 people were affected by conflict, and 1,000 people died as a result of localized conflicts. The majority of these incidents took place in West, South, North, and Central Darfur, in west Darfur West state in AlGeneina, Kulbus and Sibra localities where many people displaced, killed, injured, occupied their houses, villages burnt, villages occupied, livestock and personal property looted. West Darfur is among the most affected States by violence connected to the root causes of conflict, including power-sharing, land ownership, access to resources and criminality. the resurgence ofIntercommunal conflicts results in the killing of civilians, the destruction of property and displacements. Access to markets is interrupted, and income-earning opportunities, market activities and trade flows have been reduced. , the conflict in Jebel Moon is also affecting Sirba and Kulbus localities. Community leaders from Sirba, the government’s Humanitarian Aid Commission (HAC) Commissioner of Sirba and representatives of Sirba youth report that the conflict in Jebel Moon and Kereneik localities is also affecting their localities, as armed nomads pass through the area to join the fighting or return from it. Daily security incidents are being reported including killing, looting and displacement of people fleeing insecurity.General protection needs are most severe in the targeted localities, where inter-communal conflict and factional fighting have been reported throughout 2022 and people remained deprive of access to government services in the areas. Such chaotic situation is exacerbated by the proliferation of firearms, competition over land and water resources, unregulated migratory routes, and weak systems for land administration. As a result of reported incidents: hundreds of civilians were killed or wounded; HLP damaged, looted, destroyed or occupied; thousands of families displaced; and public facilities destroyed occupied. Furthermore, UNAMID withdrawal at the end of 2020 created a gap in the civilian protection framework in Darfur. This left humanitarian protection mechanisms and services, and durable solutions programming by protection and peacebuilding actors, as the mainstay across Sudan.Vulnerable groups - such as women, children, elderly, persons with disabilities and chronic illnesses - face a range of challenges unique to their individual circumstances, compounded by their displacement where familial/community support networks not functioning or are overstretched. These and other groups of people suffer disproportionately from various protection risks, including violence, sexual and other forms of exploitation and abuse, and human-rights violations. For women and girls, risks of GBV remain significant, particularly when they are on the move, working in fields, collecting water or firewood. Boys and men remain especially at risk of physical violence, injury and death and arbitrary arrests, as they are often suspected of supporting armed opposition groups. Child marriage and child labor are on the rise. Access to birth registration continues to be a challenge in most locations, placing both IDPs and vulnerable communities at significant risk of not accessing basic services. Persons with disabilities continue to be marginalized and neglected, with few government or humanitarian services targeted to meet their unique needs.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Activities will start with primary consideration will be given to our accountability to affected populations, to identify, understand and support their own protection measures. Different segments of affected people need to be meaningfully engaged in all decisions and actions that have a direct impact on their well-bein, and this will be done through discussion groups which will help to identifying the most effected population and guide to provide protection services and seek solutions for legal response, project will provide legal or counselling services to 100 person during the project period, after identification of the most effected people project will provide psychological assistance including counselling and referral to 450 effected persons in three localities (150 each) to the most affected people of conflicts and crisis in the 3 localities, The assistance will be provided by social workers and (psychologists) in each locality, the services may include Case Management, including measures that will allow to have access to other services such justice and human rights services.to support and established community based structures project will establish 03 community structure in three localities 20 person in each localities including leaders, women and youth to increase their awareness of their rights and will enable the participants to strengthen community monitoring and reporting mechanisms. also 06 camps will be conducted in the 3 localities to rise awareness of this communities.The capacities building of services providers, authorities staff and justices sectors will be characterized by train 90 formal services providers on psychosocial support with a focus on protection services and referral to provide specialized services and counselling assistance for effected people including disabilities, women and girls in the targeted communities at the three localities (30 persons each). The training will be for 5 days in each locality. All this activities will be implemented in targeted area through FPDO office in West Darfur state with coordination with governmental institutes (HAC) in the state and UN agencies in the area (OCHA Sudan) in collaboration with INGOs and services providers in the targeted state. 10800person will be reached through protection monitoring activities on general protection.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Rights and community-based approaches will be at the core of the project activities.
