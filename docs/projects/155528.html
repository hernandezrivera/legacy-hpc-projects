Project Summary

The project aims to provide people affected by displacement in the South West and North West Regions with improved living condition and physical safety to promote dignity and protection of vulnerable displacement affected population. The project will target IDPs and vulnerable host communities in the two regions. Special attention will be paid to women as they appear to be more affected by the conflict and more vulnerable as well. During a recent survey conducted by NRC in the Fako division, 30% of the surveyed displaced community members attested to be living in plank/wooden houses called ‘karabout’ which are poorly constructed with sometimes 8-15 persons leaving in a room exposing women and girls to protection risks. These overcrowded households with poorly constructed shelters are prone to cold (during low temperatures), mosquitoes and water leaks which most times leads to children falling sick with malaria. There is need to provide them with improved shelter material for physical protection but also against diseases.

Needs

The humanitarian needs in the North West and South West Regions of Cameroon are concentrated in divisions such as Bamenda, Boyo, Ngoketunjia, Fako, Meme, Ndian, and Manyu. To date, very little information is available about bushy areas and villages settled by IDPs. The instability in the area has affected humanitarian interventions in relation to providing homes, due to lack of access to IDP sites in the bushes. The locations where the IDPs have fled for safety include areas where they are living with relatives and friends in homes whose space is insufficient to provide the needed separation and privacy between the different ages and sex. In some situations, a home would host almost 23 persons as depicted by NRC’s baseline assessment conducted in July 2018 in Buea, South West Region. The situation is worse in the bushes where the IDPs sleep on the bare ground with no privacy, and are exposed to harsh weather conditions.
