Project Summary

This proposed project will provide dignified access to life-saving interventions and will foster resilience and sustainability by encouraging active participation and ownership by local communities and government, as well as building local capacities. The project targets vulnerable communities in Rubkona and Guit counties in Unity State, Aweil North, and Aweil West in NBeG State, addressing WASH needs due to flooding and conflicts by providing safe water, sanitation, and hygiene services. Concern will integrate WASH with Health, Nutrition and Food Security, and Livelihoods in the target areas with Concern’s ongoing programmes and ensure complementarity with other humanitarian organizations and UN Agencies.
In Aweil North and Guit Counties, the WASH services will be only linked to health/nutrition facilities and catchment populations where Concern will conduct supervision and training of existing hand pump mechanics (provided with tools under Irish Aid and ECHO programmes) to conduct repair of any dysfunctional boreholes. Similarly, the health and nutrition beneficiaries, including mother and father groups, will be supported with targeted messages and sensitization on the adoption of behavior/practices to construct their latrines using local materials. The restoration of the water supply will also be linked to health and nutrition outcomes, including access to water for vegetable production at the household level. Concern’s WASH manager and the Engineer will conduct an assessment using WASHFIT tools and Environmental Screening Guidance to inform environmentally sensitive rehabilitation and improvement needs of WASH infrastructure within targeted health facilities.
In Rubkona County, Concern will construct four new solar water yards (SWY), prioritizing bomas/villages with larger populations with no safe water source or lack access to safe drinking water, schools, and areas of cultivable land. Concern proposes the construction of 18 new disaster risk-resilient hand pumps on new boreholes constructed on elevated platforms to protect against floods. It will rehabilitate six boreholes and solarize them. Concern will conduct routine water quality tests and share the reports with the relevant stakeholders and WMCs.
Concern will install six temporary surface water treatment systems (SWATs) to provide access to safe water in emergency settings for three to six months. The Water Management Committees (WMCs) will be formed and trained to ensure the SWATs' routine operation and maintenance (O&M). At the same time, the government will assign incentivized staff for the SWATs' routine operations, including operators, water quality technicians, security guards, and cleaners for each SWAT.
To improve sanitation in communities and institutions, Concern will construct disability inclusive nine blocks/ 36 stances of semi-permanent latrines at the health facilities/nutrition sites and schools. Institutions with highest sanitation needs will be prioritized during latrine allotment. Safety Audits will be conducted to further deliberate on access risks for women and girls and incorporate risk mitigation measures during the program implementation. Concern will also construct 24 emergency pit latrine blocks each of 4 stances. Concern will coordinate with IOM for the desludging of the pits.
Concern will integrate the health, hygiene and nutrition through Boma Health Workers, community Nutrition Volunteers and the Hygiene Promoters. These workers will also be involved in the disease outbreak response i.e. Cholera response.
Concern assessed and measured the distance between Rubkona Town and Rubkona SWAT and developed the bills of quantities for the complete scope of work of the Solar Water Yard at Rubkona SWAT. Therefore, Concern also proposes this as part of this project, which will contribute to the sustainability of the water supply infrastructure.

1. Needs

Concern conducted assessments at the nutrition and health facilities in Aweil North, Guit, and Rubkona Counties. Assessment findings revealed that the WASH vulnerabilities across all proposed target locations are compounded by flooding and conflicts. Concern and its partners conducted SMART Surveys in Aweil North (March 2024), and Guit (May 2024). SMART surveys reveal GAM in Aweil North was 26%, and SAM was 3.6%; in Guit, GAM was 24.1%, and SAM was 6.3%. All GAM rates exceed the emergency threshold of 15%. The Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS) Round 29 reported the highest burden of acute malnutrition, having the highest GAM rate of 28.1% in Rubkona County. All GAM rates exceed the WHO’s emergency threshold of 15%. Access to nutrition and WASH services is affected by conflict, perennial flooding, and displacement, leading to morbidity and mortality. Concern coordinated with the County Health Departments (CHDs), the WASH Cluster SNC Coordinator, HealthNet TPO, Cordaid, Solidarity International (SI), IOM, and communities in Aweil North, Guit, and Rubkona and visited 23 health and nutrition facilities, including 10 in Aweil North, 10 in Rubkona and 4 in Guit. Concern assessment revealed that the patients, staff, and visitors to nutrition facilities in Bentiu State Hospital face severe problems due to a shortage of sanitation facilities, and there is a dire need for the construction/rehabilitation of permanent sanitation facilities. There is an existing well that hospital management operates through a generator, but due to fuel shortages, they cannot operate for sufficient time. Concern has considerable interventions in Rubkona and especially for water supply; however, in Rotriak and surrounding populations, the WASH services are limited to meet the communities' lifesaving needs and address the outbreak of the disease, i.e., Cholera. The areas hosting displaced people due to floods and Sudan crisis returnees-induced settlements in Rubkona, Aweil West, Aweil North, and Guit face increased risks of water-borne diseases due to inadequate access to water and sanitation. It is, therefore, critical to improve WASH services, strengthen the system, and enhance government and community capacities to reduce the prevalence and impact of WASH-related diseases and malnutrition in locations with high GAM rates, WASH-related vulnerabilities, and protection risks. Given the recurrent flooding and displacements, there is a need for resilient WASH facilities to improve sustainability and promote durable solutions. Furthermore, it is critical to integrate WASH across health and nutrition centers to strengthen infection prevention and promote cross-sectoral integration. Given the already high GAM prevalence and poor access to WASH services, the proposed project is critical to improving community access to safe water and sanitation to avoid any further escalation of the malnutrition crisis and to reduce morbidity and mortality.

2. Project activities

CO1: Concern is mandated nutrition partner in Guit and Aweil North Counties. The WASH activities will be implemented in areas with high GAM prevalence and will be integrated and linked to Concern’s nutrition and health facilities in Aweil North and Guit Counties. Concern will also repair and improve water and sanitation in 5 nutrition sites in Guit and 8 in Aweil North and support water and sanitation services at the mobile clinics in Aweil West and Raja Counties. The severely malnourished (SAM) children form nutrition sites will receive WASH kits from core pipeline. Through established network of the Mother/father groups and CNVs, the care givers of the SAM children will be sensitized on the hygiene including awareness on epidemics e.g. cholera, malaria and measles etc. CO2: In Rubkona County, Concern will construct four new solar water yards (SWY), prioritizing bomas/villages with larger populations with no safe water source or lack access to safe drinking water, schools, and areas of cultivable land. Concern proposes the construction of 18 new disaster risk-resilient hand pumps on new boreholes constructed on elevated platforms to protect against floods. It will rehabilitate six boreholes and solarize them. Concern will conduct routine water quality tests and share the reports with the relevant stakeholders and WMCs. Concern will install six temporary surface water treatment systems (SWATs) to provide access to safe water in emergency settings for three to six months. The Water Management Committees (WMCs) will be formed and trained to ensure the SWATs' routine operation and maintenance (O&M). At the same time, the government will assign incentivized staff for the SWATs' routine operations, including operators, water quality technicians, security guards, and cleaners for each SWAT. To improve sanitation in communities and institutions, Concern will construct disability inclusive nine blocks/ 36 stances of semi-permanent latrines at the health facilities/nutrition sites and schools. Concern will also construct 24 emergency pit latrine blocks, each of 4 stances. Concern will coordinate with IOM for the desludging of the pits. CO3: As part of GBV risk mitigation efforts, Concern will conduct the training of its staff, contractors, consultants, and volunteers on protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Concern will ensure that all its employees and personnel comply with the provisions “Special Measures for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Sexual Abuse, Concern will conduct safety audits using sector-specific tools to identify and update protection and safeguarding risks and barriers to accessing services, ensuring that beneficiaries can access assistance safely and with dignity. These safety audits will be conducted at the water points, kits distribution sites, and the health and nutrition facilities. Concern's gender and protection staff will use the standard tool for safety audits, conducting FGDs, and community meetings. CO4: As consulted by the CHDs and the assessment by Concern team, the WASH infrastructure at the health and nutrition facilities requires rehabilitation because any other partners or CHD does not manage these facilities. Concern’s WASH manager and the Engineer will conduct an assessment using WASHFIT tools and Environmental Screening Guidance to inform environmentally sensitive rehabilitation and improvement needs of WASH infrastructure within targeted health facilities. The rehabilitation will focus on water supply, sanitation, medical waste management, and waste collection. Concern will engage with the community to ensure that the WASH facilities to be built are not located in flood-prone corridors, and the design will incorporate DRR measures. The wastewater of the solar water yards and hand pumps have no surface drainage available, which creates environmental issues in most places; this water can be used for kitchen gardening, etc.
