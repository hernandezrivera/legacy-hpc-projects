Project Summary

In 2020, the CCCM cluster will target 800,000 people out of 1.28 million persons. UNHCR CCCM project for 2020 is focused on the provision of timely, well-coordinated and integrated humanitarian interventions aimed at improving the well-being of displaced populations, their resilience and participation in decision-making processes. UNHCR aims to strengthen coordination, advocacy and capacity building of key actors to ensure quality services are delivered in an equal and needs-based manner, ensuring participation of the IDP community through regular feedback mechanisms and dissemination of information, in line with the Cluster objective of ensuring equal access and needs based assistance and also HRP strategic objective SO2 and SO1. The project covers continuous and prioritized support to new arrivals, those with specific needs and the extremely vulnerable, ensuring response and prevention takes into account peculiarities relevant to each group, integrating protection mainstreaming in all CCCM activities. A key component of the project In line with CCCM cluster objective 3 of strengthening inclusive community participation and ensuring local ownership and self-governance, and also HRP strategic objective SO3 is support to community-based structures to enhance participation in planning, decisions, response and monitoring, empowerment of communities, particularly vulnerable groups, according to Cluster objective 4 of improving engagement with vulnerable populations and HRP strategic objective SO3. The project seeks to promote capacity building and development of community management structures, including contribution into appropriate site planning that responds to the needs of concerned population. UNHCR and its partners will ensure that communication channels with concerned population are improved, diversified and centered around views of the community and serve efficiently to provide timely and accurate information to respond to communities’ needs, mitigating identified risks and attending identified priorities. UNHCR will continue to make use of its population profiling tool to ensure that relevant findings about site management challenges, location; service delivery, information needs and its triangulation with geographical data inform CCCM planning, support coordination with other partners and improve advocacy for site and services improvement. UNHCR will focus on improving feedback mechanisms in locations where it covers through systematic consultations with communities, apart from static mechanisms set for receiving complaints. A stronger involvement in PSEA prevention and response will be a priority, not only through capacity building and information awareness among partners and beneficiaries but also based on increased efforts to support formal system for receiving and pursuing reports or complaints. According to CCCM cluster objective 2 of enhancing outreach response to displaced populations, also HRP strategic objectives SO1/SO2/SO3, UNHCR and its partners will ensure stronger community networks, leadership structures and community watch-groups are trained and support each other in a coordinated manner for improving overall security and information dissemination around sites, supporting establishment/development of early-warning systems and prevention of incidents. In order to minimize potential tensions, UNHCR and its partners will ensure information campaigns, complaint mechanisms and advocacy also include members of the host communities. UNHCR will seek that the latter benefits from peaceful coexistence activities, capacity building initiatives on community empowerment and self-management, opportunities leading to labor or cash-for-work; as well as other relevant trainings. UNHCR and its partners will support the capacity building of local authorities considering a potential hand-over of CCCM activities by the end of the project, should local conditions allow it.

1 - Needs

By the end of 2020, an estimated 1.5 million persons will be internally displaced in South Sudan. The crisis has seriously impacted the most vulnerable civilians including IDPs and host communities, particularly women, children, youths, older persons and persons with disabilities. New displacement continues to be reported though in a much lesser extent, mainly in Southern Central Equatoria, Southern Upper Nile and some areas in WBG, Warrap, Jonglei and Lakes. Most IDPs continue to be settled in Unity, followed by Jonglei, Upper Nile and Central Equatoria. As per the HNO, 68% of IDPs are women and children; the most vulnerable among the displaced. The vulnerability of IDPs and host communities affected by the conflict is multifaceted, including widespread violations of rights, severe damage to social fabrics and linkages between communities, extensive destruction of property and infrastructure and the near total collapse, in some areas, of the service structure necessary for basic survival. Despite women making up the majority of the adult population, their participation and influence in formal decision making does not reflect this. According to CCCM cluster, about 10% of the IDP population live in Protection of Civilian Sites (POC) while other 8% live in collective sites and other camp-like settings such as spontaneous IDP settlements. The vast majority of the IDP population though is hosted by local communities. With the relative stability in security and positive prospects about formation of a Unity Government in the near future, South Sudan has been witnessing an increase in returns from neighbouring countries. The impact of the conflict in the economy has led to scarcity of supplies further affected by targeted attacks and rising criminality; in many instances encouraging IDPs to remain in camp-like settings. Population access to basic services and livelihoods needs to be promoted. This is essential to humanitarians and development actors considering incremental implementation to programming that can transition to recovery. Around 180,000 IDPs are hosted under UNMISS POC sites. Uncertainty about State boundaries, cantonment and successful formation of Unity Government makes discontinuation of POC unlikely. While opportunity for durable solutions might remain limited for POC and collective sites’ population, new security developments may allow movements towards safe areas of return or relocation. Potential for local integration in certain collective sites, such as Bentiu, Wau and Juba urban perimeters could be explored and quick-impact projects and strategies to transfer services to outside the site's perimeter. Through well-established protection monitoring networks, UNHCR will seek to continue its successful initiative of profiling IDP sites in regular rounds to ensure comprehensive needs assessments for better understanding of gaps and challenges based on geographical data and population profile; thereby assisting camp management to improve service delivery and address information needs. UNHCR will focus on boosting community-based structures and peaceful co-existence initiatives; enhancing community participation. As such, further engagement in integrated needs assessment, profiling of IDP and host communities remains a priority, targeting assistance to the most vulnerable. IDPs need enhanced access to the mechanism that ensures identification, referral and response to their needs to ensure reintegration in line with CCCM cluster objective of strengthening inclusive community participation. This project focus in Leer TPA, collective sites in Juba, Rubkona &Bentiu and other camps-like setting in Unity State beside the overall coordination of CCCM at the national level.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Throughout the project, UNHCR applies a rights-based approach. It will seek to ensure that the rights of IDPs and affected populations are not only known but respected sustainably. UNHCR has worked hand in hand with the government to ensure a legal framework on the rights of IDPs is developed and approved so that IDPs benefit from a more sustainable protective environment across the country. UNHCR will continue to promote the participation of IDPs in the National Dialogue, in the national consultations for the IDP Act; as well as on discussions of any policies or frameworks that may affect their current status. As CCCM actor, UNHCR and its partners will play a relevant role for the necessary community mobilization for such initiatives. UNHCR will continue to promote discussions and awareness campaigns for IDPs and host communities to know more about IDPs' rights and how to enforce them. Linked with its protection project, UNHCR has a legal assistance component on several locations where it is also a CCCM actor, aimed at increasing rights education as well as individual legal counselling attached to the possibility for free legal aid in the form of mediation or legal representation. Simultaneously, UNHCR will continue to work closely with central and local authorities to ensure awareness on IDP's rights, particularly vis a vis the Guiding Principles, the Kampala Convention as well as the provisions in the other relevant regional and international instruments.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Perspectives of the Displacement-affected population are collected through regular participatory assessments conducted by UNHCR and its partners using the Age-Gender-Diversity Mainstreaming (AGDM) Methodology in all main IDP sites locations where it delivers protection assistance and/or implements protection interventions. In addition, UNHCR also collects information on opinions, aspirations and perceptions of IDPs, IDP returnees, Refugee Returnees as well as host communities through household-level surveys deployed on regular basis in the main IDP sites where it has static presence as well as through mobile assessments in hard-to-reach areas. The combination of both qualitative and quantitative data on the perspectives of the beneficiaries informs UNHCR and its partners in the annual planning as well as during the regular monitoring of the progress obtained through the implementation of the activities

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

While some of the activities of the project relate to specific humanitarian/life-saving interventions; many of the outputs are aimed to strengthen resilience and empower communities for self-management. UNHCR will continue to strive to support government structures where feasible, and most in particular, the capacity of the Government to develop and implement the necessary mechanisms to respond to camp-like situations and get the know-how on CCCM. Further, a significant component of the Project refers to the continuous support to the affected communities themselves to develop awareness and understanding of CCCM standards, mechanisms and structures for an effective community participation and self-management. In 2020, further support to decentralized structures will also be sought, particularly with a view to increase investment in areas with potential for local integration and/or for decongestion strategies, strengthening communities to absorb and take over several roles of the CCCM actor while also securing peaceful coexistence with host communities. With the increase stability across the country, should conditions allow and depending on IDPs' views, UNHCR will seek to increase capacity building to local government structures with a view to ensure (i) enhanced participation of IDPs in the development of policies and local plans; (ii) increased awareness on protection mainstreaming and on IDPs' rights; (iii) improved understanding of CCCM standards, including community participation and empowerment.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, the project is aligned with national objectives. As protection lead and CCCM co-lead, UNHCR's project abides by the HCT strategy as well as by the relevant government policies. It is noteworthy that UNHCR has contributed significantly to the development of national policies on IDP response and Solutions to Internal Displacement and it works in close collaboration with the relevant governmental bodies to ensure international standards are pursued and the rights of IDPs taken into account during the establishment of national plans, strategies, policies and/or drafting of relevant legal instruments.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Although the current context in South Sudan is not yet conducive for durable solutions; pockets of opportunities for transitional solutions exist across the country. With the progressive implementation of the Revitalized Peace Agreement and the overall cessation of hostilities, there have been areas where stability and progressive growth have been noticed. IDPs have been increasingly expressing interest to return or relocate; particularly to recover their properties and avail themselves of livelihoods opportunities. Based on UNHCR Profiling data, many still require further information about their intended destinations to act upon their intentions; a challenge that UNHCR will treat as a priority. In 2020, the Operation will prioritize feasibility assessments in intended destinations; as well as post-return monitoring missions in order to ensure IDPs receive as much information as possible about their areas of return or relocation and therefore, can make informed decisions. As CCCM actor, UNHCR and its partners will have a significant role to play in disseminating such information through the most efficient channels. As CCCM, UNHCR and its partners will also continue monitoring spontaneous departures - whether or not permanent or temporary returns/relocations in order to ensure eventual risks are mitigated or prevented and that information is also disseminated to those wishing to return/relocate to the same areas. UNHCR will also seek further engagement with development actors, including the government structures, to ensure potential for local integration for those wanting to stay; with efforts to link such advocacy with strategies for decongestion of sites, enhancing the absorption capacity of adjacent host communities and ensuring peaceful coexistence is secured with integration projects through quick-impact interventions that benefit both IDPs and host communities.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, the project includes a sustainability/handover component where feasible. Depending on the security context and the views of the IDP community, UNHCR and its partners will also support the capacity building of local authorities considering a potential hand-over of CCCM activities by the end of the project, should local conditions allow it. While for some of the interventions, handover is yet not possible due to the limited presence or capacity of other actors to take over certain activities, UNHCR strives to ensure that community empowerment is at the core of the interventions and that participation of IDPs and affected communities is secured throughout, from the planning to the evaluation phases of the project.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

UNHCR will ensure through its AGDM methodology that the views and interests of minority groups are heard and respected; as well as the ones of women and girls, youth, older persons and other groups with specific needs. Through both qualitative and quantitative methodologies, UNHCR will ensure the project and its partners are informed about the specific needs of such groups and any particular risks they might face, especially taking into account the "do no harm" approach, seeking not to expose the latter to further risks. UNHCR will also continue to strive preventing gender-based violence throughout the implementation of its project, be it through a very strong approach on PSEA as well as through mainstreaming gender-sensitive interventions in the delivery of assistance and CCCM services, as well as through the conduct and participation in safety audits and assessments. Profiling exercises will continue to be implemented to map out particular risks and locations affecting the well-being and safety of women and girls; which findings will inform UNHCR CCCM planning and advocacy with other services providers. Further efforts to seek the inclusion, budget permitting, of members of the host communities in training and capacity building initiatives; as well as cash-for-work and casual labor opportunities will also support peaceful coexistence in addition to quick-impact projects to be implemented for that same purpose.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

UNHCR conducts at least twice a year participatory assessments using the Age-Gender-Diversity Mainstreaming Methodology in all locations where it has CCCM projects. The Participatory assessments are a condition sine qua non for the planning of the annual programming and feedback sessions to the communities are also planned so that persons of concern, including but not only Persons with Specific Needs (PSNs), remain informed about the findings of the assessments and their own recommendations can be also validated. Apart from that, continuous FGDs and KIIs are organized throughout the year through the reception desks as well as mobile missions to collect qualitative data on the performance of partners and progress of the implemented activities. In addition, based on the roll-out of the Protection Monitoring Tool (PMT), UNHCR also collects the perceptions of IDPs through quantitative data collection initiatives which informs UNHCR advocacy, implementation as well as any need for review of its project whenever necessary. Particular attention is drawn to the most vulnerable groups, whose opinions' are particularly assessed by specific interviews made through household visits planned for the PMT; in case their voices are otherwise missed in group consultations.
