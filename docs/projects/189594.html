Project Summary

In 2022, an estimated two million children and women will be at risk of acute malnutrition and will need urgent treatment. The main causes of malnutrition are directly related to inadequate dietary intake as well as high prevalence of diseases, and indirectly to multiple factors including food insecurity, sub-optimal childcare and feeding practices, inadequate sanitation conditions and hygiene practices, poor to access health and nutrition services. In 2022, some 1.46 million people, of whom 67 per cent are children, are targeted with emergency nutritional support(HNO 2022). The communities of Panyijiar and Leer Counties of Unity state and, Malakal, Longchuk and Maiwut County of Upper Nile state have experienced unprecedented humanitarian crisis which has been heightened by the recent heavy floods that has seen more than 50% of the households displaced, loss of harvests, destruction of farmlands and livestock that has plugged these counties into severe food insecurity that has led to high prevalence of malnutrition. The emergency nutrition response is targeting children under five years and pregnant and lactating mothers from host communities and Internally displaced persons (IDPS) in flood and conflict affected counties of Panyijiar and Leer counties (Unity state), Malakal, Longechok and Maiwut county (Upper Nile state) with chronic emergency nutrition needs. The planned nutrition activities will as well support health, WASH and GBV prevention interventions by creating synergies with the health, GBV and WASH clusters through Health, GBV and WASH implementing partners so as to reduce nutrition related morbidity and mortality by reducing the prevalence of global acute malnutrition in children under five years and pregnant and lactating mothers (PLWs) in Panyijiar, Mayendit, Leer and Maiwut counties. Therefore, the proposed project activities and targets include early detection of malnutrition through nutrition screening at the nutrition site and community level and treatment of 9099(2678 SAM, 6421 MAM) girls and boys under five years of age and 5033 pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition though establishment of eight (16) nutrition sites, 4 sites per county, micronutrient supplementation to 24338 under five children with vitamin A, provide behavioral change messages to 15144 mothers and care takers of malnourished children with maternal infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN) counselling, conduct 28 cooking demonstrations to the beneficiaries, carry out deworming for 23361 children 12-59 months with albendazole tablets, conduct monitoring visits to the nutrition sites to make sure nutrition services offered are of quality following the ministry of health nutrition guidelines, conduct testing of malaria for 2678 severe acute malnourished children under five years, provide WASH kits to the caregivers of 2678 severe acute malnourished (SAM) children, establish a kitchen gardens at every nutrition site for demonstration, stimulate play for acutely malnourished children by provision of child play, conduct Gender based violence safety audits. The project intends to support nutrition needs of 76,977(SAM, MAM, PLW, MIYCN, Vit A and deworming) beneficiaries from most vulnerable food insecure host communities, returnees and IDPs beneficiaries, mostly children under five years with acute malnutrition and malnourished pregnant and lactating mothers. This project comes at a time when Panyijiar, Leer, Malakal, Longechok and Maiwut Counties have been affected by floods causing heavy displacements and continuously experience intercommunal conflicts worsening the vulnerability evidenced by high rates of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) due food insecurity caused by poor harvests and death of livestock. Based on the recent assessment projections (PIN) and SMART surveys, the GAM rates were beyond the emergency level with Panyijiar, Leer, Longechuk, Maiwut estimated at 18.7%, 19.2%, 16.1% and16.1% respectively.

1 - Needs

In 2022, an estimated two million children and women will be at risk of acute malnutrition and will need urgent treatment. The main causes of malnutrition are directly related to inadequate dietary intake as well as high prevalence of diseases, and indirectly to multiple factors including food insecurity, sub-optimal childcare and feeding practices, inadequate sanitation conditions and hygiene practices, poor to access health and nutrition services. In 2022, some 1.46 million people, of whom 67 per cent are children, are targeted with emergency nutritional support. The Food Security and Livelihood (FSL) sectoral analysis estimates that 8.3 million people, including refugees, are expected to experience severe food insecurity by the depth of the lean season (May – July) in 2022. This is an increase of 7 per cent above the 7.7 million people in need in 2021, according to Humanitarian needs overview 2022. The communities of Panyijiar and Leer counties (Unity Sate) and, Malakal, Longechok and Maiwut County (Upper Nile state) have experienced unprecedented humanitarian crisis which has been heightened by the recent heavy floods that has seen more than 50% of the households displaced, loss of harvests, destruction of farmlands and livestock that has plugged these counties into severe food insecurity that has led to high prevalence of malnutrition. According to UNICEF 2020, only 41% of the populations have access to water and the water is entirely not safe for drinking and for domestic use. 63% of the populations practice open defecation and only 11% have access to sanitation facilities. Unsafe water consumption by the households and inadequate water storage containers that lead to inadequate water storage and this exposes children to frequent episodes of diarrheal diseases hence malnutrition. This coupled with communal conflicts, COVID-19 restrictions, inadequate health and nutrition services has plugged the county into high prevalence of malnutrition with high GAM rates way above the WHO minimum standard of 15% and enormous protection issues including GBV where the situation is not different from other counties in the region. Based on the recent assessment projections (PIN) and SMART surveys, the GAM rates were beyond the emergency level with Panyijiar, Leer, Longechuk, Maiwut estimated at 18.7%, 19.2%, 16.1% and16.1% respectively. This therefore calls for consulted effort to avert further deterioration of the humanitarian situation by contributing in provision of live-saving nutrition interventions in these counties. Alliance for Action Aid (AFAA) proposes to bridge this gap by responding to nutrition needs of 14,132(2,678 SAM, MAM 6,421, 5,033 PLWs) beneficiaries in Panyijiar and and Leer counties of Unity state and, Longechok, Malakal and Maiwut county of Upper Nile state by establishing 16 nutrition sites in these locations mostly in the payams with high population of IDPs and inadequate health and nutrition services where people walk long distances to receive nutrition services. In addition, AFAA aims to implement all the four nutrition cluster objectives( sectoral objectives 1 to 4) by providing nutrition needs to 76,977(SAM, MAM, PLWs, MIYCN, Vit A and deworming). This represents about 35% coverage of the total nutrition cluster target. AFAA will utilize the operational and static presence in Payinjiar county with field offices where its implementing mobile health services currently to make sure equitable access to quality preventive and curative nutrition services to children and women are offered to the vulnerable population. This synergy will easily contribute to the cluster objectives by reducing acute suffering and prevalence of global acute malnutrition in these counties. Due to AFAA’s operational and static presence in Panyijiar county, Leer, Longechok, Maiwut and Malakal counties, it will be easy to implement project activities and coordinate at the County and state level hence quality service delivery.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

AFAA will recruited interpreters to translate the feedback from the illiterate to English for documentation and analysis and the sign language interpreters will be availed to get feedback from the deaf. AFAA shall make sure that feedback from most vulnerable members like people living with disabilities, women and girls are considered at every level of the project implementation. The community shall be sensitized on the gender equality issues, women leaders shall be involved at every level of project implementation so that they can front the women and girls' concerns to the relevant authorities. The people living with disability shall be sensitized on the avenues of feedback so that their opinions can count at every level of implementation. AFAA shall involve women, girls and people with disability at every level of the project implementation and this will help on improving the avenues of feedback from these vulnerable groups.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

AFAA has protection officer that will constantly sensitize the beneficiaries on the protection against sexual exploitation and abuse from the inception of the project and during project implementation. The local authorities shall be oriented on protection against sexual exploitation and abuse so that they can sensitize the beneficiaries in their respective meetings. AFAA shall provide information communication and education materials containing protection against sexual protection and abuse messages and pin then at the nutrition sites and public places to make sure the beneficiaries are sensitized. AFAA shall request the beneficiaries to nominate a focal person from the community who shall constantly spearhead the PSEA activities in the community.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

AFAA always provides information to the beneficiaries about the projects and creates feed back mechanisms for the beneficiaries. In this particular project AFAA will print IEC materials to inform the beneficiaries on who to approach in case of any concern about the services offered. AFAA shall orient the community on the project objectives and targets and also sensitize the community on the avenues of community feedback through community meetings during project inception and implementation. AFAA will also engage the local leaders at every level of the project implementation and constantly orient them on the objectives of the project and the feedback mechanisms available so that they can constantly sensitize the community on the project objectives and feedback/ complaint mechanisms

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

1. Risks that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services. This is a medium risk and therefore AFAA shall make sure safety audits are conducted at every nutrition site for women and girls so that protection risks are identified and dealt with. AFAA shall also involve the local authorities to make sure women and girls are protected from violence along the way. 2. Risks of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance. This a low risk and AFAA shall make sure that the beneficiaries especially women and girls are sensitized from the inception of the project on protection of sexual exploitation and abuse and their rights. Women leaders shall be involved at every stage of the project implementation so that they are sensitized on PSEA and continue to sensitize the women and girls. A PSEA focal person shall be appointed and introduced to the community so that any early warning signs of sexual exploitation and abuse can be reported to the responsible authorities. 3. Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.). This is a medium risk since the protection services at community level are limited. AFAA will include protection awareness in our routine programming so that vulnerable beneficiaries especially women girls and children shall sensitized on the possible protection issues and prevention measures. AFAA shall work with the protection partners in the project locations so that the referral pathway is established and active to support the survivors of GBV and child protection issues among others. 4. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and Older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. This is a medium risk and AFAA shall make sure that marginalized groups like persons with disability, older persons and women are involved in the decision-making process at every level of project implementation.
