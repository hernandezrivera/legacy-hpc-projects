Project Summary

This project aims to meet the immediate, medium and long-term food security needs of the target communities of IPC level 3,4 and 5, also having the focus on restoring livelihood, enhancing food production, building resilience and strengthening capacity of the targeted population.
This will be achieved by various means. One of them is access to cereal and/or vegetable seeds’ kits and tools, accompanied by agricultural extension activities, focusing on good agronomic practices. This intervention will be delivered using lead farmers and farmer field school approaches with demonstration plots. Lead farmers and extension workers will be trained as trainers for community farmers. This methodology ensures ownership and long-lasting effects, as the capacity of trainers stays within the community. PAH will introduce different irrigation methods including drip irrigation systems together with community training on their adequate and sustainable use to enhance the copying mechanism during the dry season.
Additionally, to traditional plots, PAH will encourage setting up small-scale vegetable gardens, which can be established next to the houses. This facilitates planting and irrigation, allows higher diet diversity and minimize the protection risks in case of women walking long distances to access the plots.
Access to fishing kits and learning of fishery practices is another means to improve food security and livelihood. Fishing has the potential to immediately address food security needs. It enables people to immediately harvest fish from the rivers and swamps in the areas.
Conditional general food distribution may be considered within the project in case the targeted population is strongly affected by food insecurity. Food distribution will target the most vulnerable food insecure population with limited access to markets and having a poor road network. Contrary, in the communities with access to market and availability cash programming, will be considered. The decision will be based on initial market and community assessments, considering cash vouchers or cash for work. Food/cash for asset will be conducted in the areas where there are needs and local markets can support the project targeting the establishment of community structures and facilities like schools and infrastructures like community road projects and communal farms establishment.
Food for education and school feeding programme will be considered in the project areas where PAH carries food security and livelihood activities with seeds distribution and vegetable and cereal crops production.
For a more holistic approach, building resilience and long-lasting effects, PAH will integrate interventions with sectors of WASH, nutrition, education, protection and S/NFI through combined hygiene and nutrition promotion in addition to screening and referral to nutrition partners. Education and health will be integrated through training of school hygiene clubs and school gardening and awareness at the schools, nutrition and health centres. PAH will work with nutrition and health partners on the local level. Protection will be mainstreamed across all sectors and additional protection team will be supporting the communities.
Concerning the beneficiaries breakdown, while planning the responses PAH will follow OCHA population aggregation.

1 - Needs

In the IPC projection period of December 2020 to March 2021: a total of 21 counties may be classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, 46 - in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and 11 - as Stressed (IPC Phase 2). In this projected period an estimated 5.82 million people (48.3% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 11,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) in Pibor County, in Pibor Administrative Area. During this period, an estimated 1.79 million people are likely to face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. In the second projection period of April to July 2021, the deterioration of the situation is projected - a total of 45 counties may be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4), 27 - in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and 6 - as Stressed (IPC Phase 2). An estimated 7.24 million people (60% of the population) are likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. During the same period, 31,000 people are likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity, with 11,000 people in Akobo County in Jonglei State, 7,000 people in Aweil South County in Northern Bahr el Ghazal State, and 13,000 people in Tonj North County in Warrap State. During this period, an estimated 2.47 million (20,5% of the population) are likely to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. The most severe acute food insecurity conditions are in counties where chronic vulnerabilities resulted from a combination of shocks, including floods, insecurity incidents, the macro-economic crisis, and the effects of COVID-19 measures. Elevated levels of food insecurity (IPC Acute Food Insecurity Phase 3 and above), worse than the previous year in most counties, also contribute to acute malnutrition. COVID-19 related disruptions, including those rightfully implemented to curb coronavirus infection rates, as well as changes in SAM and MAM detection and referral protocols for children have exacerbated access to services. Furthermore, access to health and nutrition services in the Greater Upper Nile region and Jonglei has been severely disrupted due to heightened subnational conflict and flooding. A seasonal deterioration of the acute malnutrition situation is expected during the lean season due to increased morbidity, high food insecurity, and poor infant and young child feeding practices. A total of 57 counties may be in IPC Acute Malnutrition Phase 3 (Serious) or worse - of which 19 are in IPC Acute Malnutrition Phase 3 (Serious) and 38 are in IPC Acute Malnutrition Phase 4 (Critical). Based on the historical data trends, coupled with disruptions to basic health, nutrition and food security and livelihoods services, Renk may be in IPC Acute Malnutrition Phase 5 (Extremely Critical). Of the counties in IPC Acute Malnutrition Phase 4 during the projection period, nearly 70% are in the Greater Upper Nile region followed by Greater Bahr el Ghazel with 18%. The major factors contributing to acute malnutrition include high prevalence of diseases (affecting up to 36% of children under five), poor quality and diversity of food (Minimum Acceptable Diet: 7%, Minimum Dietary Diversity: 15%).

2 - Project activities

1. Fishery as an immediate source of food and livelihood: a) Fishing kits' distribution and demonstration on proper use. In case of access to market and availability – cash or vouchers modality will be used. b) Training on fishing techniques, environment preservation, risks mitigation while fishing. The trainees will be linked up with traders to encourage market linkages between fisherfolk and the supply fisher men. c) Training on the uses of improved smoking kilns and other forms of preservation. 2. Support on cereal and vegetable production a) Distribution of vegetable kits depends on preference. b) Distribution of cereal kits and culturally acceptable tools. Cash or vouchers modality may be used. c) Training in good agronomic practices d) Irrigation systems and adequate training on their use. PAH plans to provide vegetable farmers with treadle pumps and 10 to 15-litre watering cans. Drip irrigation systems together with community training on their adequate and sustainable use will enhance the copying mechanism during the dry season 3. Raising stability of crop & reducing post-harvest losses - PAH aims to introduce various technologies (e.g. silos, granary from local materials) and processing equipment training in rural areas. PAH will support identified Artisan groups of farmers through vocational skills training on the production and maintenance of appropriate farm implements and storages equipment. 4. Capacity building: Training for lead farmer’s/extension workers and setting up Lead Farmer Field Schools: a) Farmers to farmer’s exchange visits to empower the farmers and gain new skills and knowledge. b) Support in capacity on households’ level – training in groups or on the household level on preparing nutritious meals with fish, vegetable, crop, and preservation of food. 5. Support for Livestock Treatment and Vaccination/deworming to strengthen immunity status of the herd against diseases of economic importance (such as Black Quarter (BQ), Hemorrhagic Septicemia (HS), Anthrax, sheep pox, and Peste Des Petits Ruminants (PPR)). PAH proposes to vaccinate, treat and deworm animals and rehabilitate animal health posts.. PAH will offer to train women in cheese making, leak salting, Milk hygiene, silage, hay formulation and ox-plough training to support livestock expansion for the most food-insecure households. PAH will collaborate with the Ministry of Animal Resources.a) In order to Rehabilitate animal post in the vulnerable community, PAH plans to improve the livestock level of the community in IPC 3,4,5 through provision of livestock support (Cow, goat, poultry) to restart and ensure diversified production. This will reduce malnutrition and increase livestock product in the area. 6. Food for Education: a) PAH will consider school feeding programme after assessments of needs and the possibility of sustainability and ownership by the community. b) PAH will target education and sustainable health improvement by training school hygiene clubs and school gardening. 7.Conditional General food distribution - will target the most vulnerable food insecure population with limited access to markets and having a low road network. 8. Cash programming will be based on the initial market and community assessments, considering cash vouchers or cash for work. PAH will mainstream cross-cutting issues across all activities focusing on gender mainstreaming, GBV, protection, accountability to the affected population and committing to improving the effectiveness of cooperation with communities. PAH will have an integrated approach with FSL and WASH, NFI, protection and nutrition. PAH will pay attention to community hygiene and nutrition promotion on food hygiene and safe water management, to break the chain of contamination at the household level, nutrition screening and referral of SAM/MAM cases to nutrition centres, protection of most vulnerable and decrease the risks of GBV.

3 - Project indicators

MEAL plan will be developed for the project to determine the type of data that need to be collected, frequency of data collection, tools used for data collections, responsible person and track the progress against key results and output indicators and the information captured in different databases. PAH MEAL team will support the development of common data collection methodologies and it always takes part in joint needs assessments to ensure common standards. Periodic data analysis and interpretation will be made at different levels (at project implementation, location and country program level) and shared with relevant stakeholders to inform about the project progress. A mix of quantitative, qualitative, participatory and observatory data collection methods will be employed for collecting data against key indicators and the findings are incorporated into these reports. Data collection and analysis will be done separately for women, men, children and the elderly. PAH will utilise sex and age disaggregated data throughout the action. Baseline survey will be conducted to establish pre-project benchmarks for key project level indicators and will be used to measure the project progress towards the end of the project. Quantitative data collection methods will be used for this purpose. Post intervention monitoring (PIM) and Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) will be conducted in all target areas at the end of the interventions and/or after 6 to 8 weeks of each distribution to assess the short-term impact of the interventions and perception of people on the quality of items distributed, convenience of distribution location, safety of people traveling to and going back from distribution locations, etc. and inform subsequent distributions. HH Interviews, Focus Group Discussions (FGD), Key informant interviews (KII) and observations will be the major methodologies during PIM/PDM. All survey data collection will be supported through mobile-based systems. Some of indicators used will be: # Number of SAD* beneficiaries receiving food, cash transfers and vouchers # Quantity of livelihood kits delivered by IRRM (SADD)# No less than 50% women committee members, in roles mirroring that of men;# Number of HHs (female, child & male headed) receiving agricultural inputs/ vegetable & fishing kits# Number of HHs receiving cash/ vouchers# Quantity & variety of seeds distributed# Quantity & variety of crops produced (outcome)# Beneficiary satisfaction with information, participation and CFM; % women receiving livelihood kits (crop/ veg/ fish);Use of Gender Age MarkerDespite of quantitative indicators, PAH will collect qualitative data about assistance, satisfaction, changes resulting from the project. PAH conducts periodic Lessons Learnt reviews which fuels the process of constant improvement. All reports will be shared between relevant stakeholders, inc. clusters.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The vouchers will be used only after careful assessment of the market accessibility and availability of the products. Due to high inflation, the market assessment will give information on the cost-effectiveness of the activity. The voucher system supports the local economy and empowers the beneficiaries to purchase items they find most necessary. The vouchers will be used to purchase tools and non-food items for the farmers, to ensure hygienic food preparation and storage, to start livelihood activities after received training. Cash for work will be considered as a modality, as a source of short term livelihood support.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Data for assessment, baseline and monitoring will be collected through a institutions and HH survey, focus group discussions with representatives of various groups disaggregated by gender and age, people with disabilities, interviews with key informants, authorities and other NGOs present in the area. Each time PAH and/or enumerators will inform about the scope and aim of data collection. Collected data will be a base for designing activities, adjusting to the needs or external circumstances. Special attention will be given to women and girls’ voices, PWD and members of minority groups. PAH Protection officers will engage the caseworkers from the community and will be present in the community as often as possible to build trust and encourage effective psychosocial support and referral. TOR for all activities will be presented to the authorities, plans are discussed with the community. People are engaged as community-based caseworkers in GBV referrals, translators, enumerators. Regular meetings with the community representatives during intervention implementation will be organised to present plans of the activities and receive feedback on the implementation process, as well as suggestions for improvement. At all phases of the intervention, PAH staff will always consult and engage with the beneficiaries and local authorities to ensure that the decisions are made jointly with their prior knowledge. To ensure effective records of feedback/complaints from the beneficiaries for follow-up, PAH team will establish accessible CRFMs (complaint response feedback mechanisms) such as the use of complaint forms, complaint desks, hotlines, complaint boxes and complaint committees based on the area of applicability. Complaints and feedback will be registered and followed up with the communities on ongoing bases and will be used to adjust project activities.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

To ensure adequate records of feedback/complaints from the beneficiaries for follow-up, PAH team will establish accessible CRFMs (complaint response feedback mechanisms) such as complaint forms, complaint desks, hotlines, complaint boxes and complaint committees based on the area of applicability. Complaints and feedback will be registered and followed up with the communities on ongoing bases and used to adjust project activities. The records of complaints and feedback are kept and regularly analysed by MEAL team in Juba, whose officers track each case and advise/support the programme team. Under the supervision of Senior Management, the implementing programme team conducts regular review meetings and adjust the project implementation to changed circumstances or according to constructive feedback.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

PAH will conduct protection assessments and risk analysis, stakeholders mapping, and needs verification with clear AGD data to understand context dynamics, protection risks, vulnerability level for each location. The risk of members of the marginalized community’s/minority groups not being included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance will be mitigated by ensuring that they are part of the targeted group right from the assessment. Protection risks are most often higher for marginalized/minority groups’ members, so they will be the main focus in the project. All vulnerable groups will be targeted with attention to their needs and capacities. PAH has the flexibility to use innovative solutions to meet those needs, to ensure inclusion and empowerment. Complaint and feedback mechanisms are always in place to nd out about any gaps and address them. Women and girls will be consulted separately on their needs. Separate focus group discussions will be carried out specifically for women and girls by a female staff or protection officer so that they can open up. PAH works closely with local authorities, institutions and NGOs to clearly understand the cultural norms, inter-communal dynamics and potential risks. PAH will involve local actors in their mitigation. Thanks to comprehensive cooperation PAH can avoid aid diversion, ensure ownership of project results and involvement of the community in monitoring and control of the results. The project has clear risk analysis and mitigation related to access, inc. logistic and security challenges. The analysis is regularly repeated and the security situation is monitored continuously. Ongoing coordination and communication with the log cluster are done to support the delivery of project items. NGO forum and OCHA are focal points for all discussion regarding access and permission. Support from Access Working group discussed where needed. Thanks to close coordination with clusters, PAH has diversified sources of information, between others about explosive hazards, local history of cooperation with stakeholders, local regulations that may try to include taxation or influence beneficiary selections. Experienced team of PAH officers, supported by the humanitarian community, can deal with different stakeholders and solve complex problems without compromising humanitarian values and highest standards.
