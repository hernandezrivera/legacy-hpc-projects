Project Summary

-Project provides health assessment services for children (including breastfed children) and lactating mothers.
-On a public health level, the project includes initial assessment and referre for common diseases across all ages
-Malnutrition screening for children under the age of 5, lactating and pregnant women.
-Raising awareness about health and nutrition issues
-Providing of preventive nutrition aid within targeted areas for children <5 years of age, lactating and pregnant women
-Provide IYCF-E counselling for mothers with moderately malnourished children, distribute micronutrients for preventing of anemia and malnutrition among children, pregnant and lactating women, disseminate messages (IEC/BCC) on IYCF-E recommended feeding practices, and monitor distribution and use of breast milk substitutes, support health/nutrition inclusive education and promotion refer the needed cases and follow up the referred cases. Besides, they will distribute breast covers for PLWs to encourage them feeling more privacy and support the exclusive breast-feeding.
-Provision of primary healthcare services via mobile clinics to patients within targeted areas
-Target Groups:
1. Children 0-6 Months
2. Children 6-59 Months
3. Lactating and Pregnant mothers
Al-Ameen will provide these services in the following locations:
1. Idleb governorate, Ariha district, Ariha sub-district, Ariha community
2. Aleppo governorate, Afrin district, Afrin sub-district, Afrin community
3. Aleppo governorate, Al-Bab district, Al-Bab sub-district, Al-Bab community
The target population in the three mentioned locations exceeds 600 k.
Every team consists of two community health workers, the team will implement field visits to the household to reach out to the beneficiaries to do the following:
1. Nutrition status screening for all children under 59 months and all pregnant and lactating women
2. Providing nutritional supplementarity for those who are malnourished
3. Referring beneficiaries with complicated malnutrition cases
4. COVID 19 community engagement and raising awareness in regard to improving the acceptance of vaccination and commitment to IPC measures and procedures
5. Distribution of breast covers to lactating women to encourage them to continue breastfeeding
The visits will be done by household visits to reach better coverage for malnutrition.

4 - Needs

Based on the last rapid need assessment conducted by the organizations working in NW Syria between October 20th and 23rd, 2019 and under supervision of the nutrition cluster in Gaziantep, Turkey:GAM rate was 3.61%, MAM was 2.94%, SAM was 0.66%. Over 92% of the surveyed areas did not contain hand-washing basins with soap, while soap for hand washing is not available in 35% of the camps surveyed. As for the latrines locations, the assessment showed that 17% of the camps were without latrines, while 57% had latrines between tents.Concerning wastewater disposal, the results of the assessment indicated that 38% of wastewater was disposed of in pits and 20% in the streets.Moreover, Al-Ameen conducted a multi-sectoral needs assessment in many locations in NW Syria in Sep 2019. The respondents reported that 70% of the community is in need of nutrition and childcare services in Ariha while 67% in Afrin and 53% Al Bab.The assessment showed that 44% of people in Ariha get sick from drinking water, while in Afrin the proportion was 27% and 22% Al-Bab.The sample reported that it took people almost 2 hours to get water in Ariha, and Afrin while it was 7 hours in Al Bab.According to HNO 2022, Health workers must be trained on malnutrition screening and prevention and treatment interventions for pregnant and lactating women and children under 5, and accessible and safe services for persons with disabilities including communication barriers and vulnerable groups like adolescent girls

5 - Implementation of activities

Activity 1.1.1: Conduct MUAC screening for children under 5 and PLWs in Ariha, Al-bab, and Afrin sub-districts. Activity 1.1.2: Refer SAM cases, CU5, and PLWs with danger signs and medical problems to health facilities, in addition, to following MAM cases by the nutritionists.Activity 1.1.3: Distribution of 1250 breast covers to encourage PLWs for exclusive breastfeeding.Activity 1.1.4: Identify and refer to the visual abuse/violence signs for child U5/PLWs during the Nutrition screening focusing on those from the most vulnerable people with disabilities.Activity 1.1.5: Raising WASH awareness by providing key messages about (Hand Washing, Food Processing). Activity 1.1.6: Distribution of 1000 hygiene kits for children with SAM cases and the most needed cases (families and PLWs) under evaluation of the nutritionists.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project staff will mainstream protection principles in all components which include making sure that all beneficiaries have purposeful access to services prioritizing the safety and dignity of them, ensuring non-discrimination and 'do no harm' in the delivery of the services and pursuing a meaningful engagement of the stakeholders, and ensure that confidential reporting mechanisms are in place to address any potential abuse. The project will ensure reaching the most vulnerable persons and provide secondary health care services for them or refer them. The following considerations will be taken in all activities:1-Facilitate the access of the women and girls to the services in a safe manner, without being exposed to abuse or security risks.2-The teams are working in easy-to-reach, close, and safe areas in order to avoid any potential risks.3-Having a trained and qualified team that is committed to the code of conduct and PSEA principles and works on not to violate these principles knowing that there are disciplinary actions in place. The needs of people and children with disabilities were taken into consideration.The teams will be provided with measures that the needs of PWDs such asreaching out and household visits.Al-Ameen will hire localized and gender-balanced staff, and supervision mechanism will put in place to enhance the quality of services. Referral will be done for complicated cases.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Abuse or harassment by admin or medical staff towards patients and HH members.Probability: low. Risk severity: high. Mitigation measures: - Recruitment of female CHWs and limited the recruitment of male outreach workers. Organizing teams in two CHWs per household visit and conducting random supervision visits by supervisors; -Training staff on PSEA, code of conduct and ethical data collection; - Ensuring the commitment of staff to Al-Ameen’s PSEA, Child Safeguarding and Code of Conduct policies; - Contracts including a strict term of dismissal towards any misconduct of SEA; - Maintenance and enhancement of Complaint and Feedback mechanism including hot lines for receiving reports and allegationsof SEA misconducts; - Regular surveys conducted by MEAL team to monitor the conducted visits. Surveys include question about team members behaviors, misconducts and their respect of privacy; -Allegations are seriously investigated, where aid workers are subjected to contract termination and further disciplinary actions if allegations were proven. Awareness of GBV and PSEA issues will be incorporated into project activities and beneficiary satisfaction will be verified during household and facility visits which will include female employees to facilitate collecting suggestions/complaints from female beneficiaries especially in relation to GBV and PSEA. All of the CHWs under this project are trained on the PSEA policy during their initial induction.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption 1: Beneficiaries have access to centers to receive services.Risk: people have no access because of security issues.Likelihood: low Mitigation: Suspension of the work until release of risk.Assumption 2: COVID-19 prevention and response measures are in place to protect staff and beneficiaries.Risk: Lack of funding of personal protective equipment (PPE), staff and beneficiaries do not comply with the instructions.Mitigation: Encourage staff to take 2 doses of covid 19 vaccination, provide sufficient amounts of PPE and disinfection materials, raising awareness about IPC measures for COVID 19.Likelihood: LowAssumption 3: Persons with disabilities will be able to attend the HFs. Risk: lack of access for persons with disabilities, or access with being exposed to more harm because the places are not appropriate for their needs.Mitigation: provide the needed transportation to ease the persons with disabilities access to the centers.Likelihood: LowAssumption 4: Security Council resolution will be extendedRisk: Suspension of cross border resolution and supply line shortageLikelihood: HighMitigation: Looking for international NGOs and donors to cover gaps and search for sustainable solutions for funding and supply line.
