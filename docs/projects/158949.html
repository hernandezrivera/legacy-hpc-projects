Project Summary

Children in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, face severe and chronic protection challenges getting to and back from school every day. They cross Israeli military check points and go through closed military zones where they can experience harassment and even violence. On their daily commute, they pass by Israeli settlements which can also be a source of abuse, harassment and violence. The project will contribute to the 2020 HRP education objective 2: “Most vulnerable schools are supported in responding to potential emergencies through preventive measures, remedial programmes and school based psychosocial services.” It will provide vulnerable students (ages 6-17), 6,700 (2,700 girls and 4,000 boys) and 200 teachers (120 female and 80 male) in H2, South Hebron Hills, Bethlehem, South Nablus, East Jerusalem and other selected areas in need in the North of West Bank. Children and teachers facing obstacles to access schools or children exposed to risks of harassment on their way to schools will be supported to have safe access to schools. This intervention has been developed in consultation with the affected communities who express increased demand for it and will also be coordinated with the Protection Cluster. Several assessments continue to show that the realization of the right to education remains severely compromised in these locations due to violence and armed search operations in schools, harassment, intimidation, and violence towards students and teachers on the way to and from school. In response to these threats, families adopt negative coping mechanisms, such as taking children out of school to limit their exposure to violence and harassment, a practice more common among girls than boys.
The lack of safe access to education has significant and negative consequences for children’s schooling and results in a decrease of school hours, an increased drop-out rate of children, discouraging teaching staff and the displacement or separation of families as they seek solutions in getting their children in schools elsewhere. These negative factors affect children’s overall well-being. UNICEF will continue to learn about best ways to support children in their safe access to school. Communities, parents, students and teachers will be included in the study through focus groups and surveys. UNICEF team is also responsible for monitoring of the implementation to ensure quality and course-correction if needed and achievement of all planned objectives.

1 - Needs

In the West Bank, children continue to face violence and challenges in accessing schools. In the H2 area of Hebron, the withdrawal of over 60 international monitors from the Temporary International Presence in Hebron in early 2019 has left a gap in human rights monitoring and protection especially for school children. H2 was the most affected in terms of violations against education in 2019, with 35 schools in the area serving 5,561 males and 7,167 females. The majority of these children cross checkpoints daily to attend school, and parents report that their major concern is military harassment and settler violence against their children on the way to school. According to OCHA data, in H2, approximately “4,200 pupils, boys and girls, have their journey to school obstructed by checkpoints every day, frequently facing harassment, intimidation and delays that result in lost school time.” Teachers and school personnel who need to reach H2 also report frequent harassment, searches and humiliation at checkpoints. Contributing factors which put these particular children more at risk than others include having to pass through hot spot areas on the way to and from school; schools not providing a protective or supportive environment; difficult domestic backgrounds; the lack of safe places to play; and age of the child, with adolescent boys being the most vulnerable to being in conflict with Israeli Forces. In addition, there is an increased need for MHPSS support and the creation of child-friendly spaces to increase the resilience of children whilst allowing them to continue learning. According to the UN Secretary General’s report on Children and Armed Conflict, the United Nations verified 118 incidents of interference with education in the Occupied Palestinian Territory in 2018. The overwhelming majority have been in Area C, the Hebron H2 area and East Jerusalem affecting 23,188 children. In the West Bank, tear gas and rubber bullets are frequently fired at students, and Israeli forces make regular incursions onto school grounds. Frequently, children must pass through one of 59 checkpoints, where they are subjected to bag and/or body searches, and at times harassment and detention. Students also risk confrontation, intimidation and violence while walking past settlements or buildings occupied by settlers. These children can be exposed to conflict-related violence and delays in reaching schools, which can, coupled with other push factors such as weak teacher preparation, lack of remedial education support or poor learning environment in school, lead to higher school drop-out rates. Girls in particular face a higher risk of school drop-out due to the fear of parents for their safety during the school commute. Several assessments clearly demonstrate the need for protection interventions. The household survey conducted by OCHA in August 2018 found that 88 per cent of children in H2 cross checkpoints to attend their schools, which is one of the major barriers in sending their children to school, as reported by most parents. They mentioned that soldier’s harassment and settler violence against their children was their major education related concern. 60 per cent of families reported that their children are facing harassment by soldiers at a higher frequency compared to the period before October 2016. In a more recent needs assessments conducted by one Education In Emergency Working Group partner in 2018 identified the following as the most vulnerable and in need areas: 1) H2 checkpoints 55, 56, and 209; 2) South Hebron Hills villages of Birin and in At Tuwani, and As Sefer community; 3) Bethlehem -Tuqu’ schools (Al Jarmaq, Al Khansa’ and the Secondary boys schools); 4) Al Khader schools compound located near road 60; 5) East Jerusalem (Al Awqaf schools in the Old City and Issawiya schools) 6) Nablus: As Sawiya - Al Lubban Boys' Secondary School, Al Lubban Girls Secondary School located on Road 60.

2 - Activities

1. Establishment/strengthening of school management committees and training them on how to provide protective learning environments and safe access to school for children and teachers 2. Training of teachers on how to provide psycho social support (PSS) to children that may have been traumatized after being exposed to conflict going to or returning from school. This intervention will be implemented in coordination with Save the children, NRC and relevant stakeholders in order to complement what other agencies are supporting and to minimize duplication. 3. Provision of school recreational activities for children in selected schools or local community centers to serve as safe hubs for different art, sports and educational activities 4. Support Safe guarding of children’s and teachers’ commute to and from school In each targeted school, school management committees (SMCs) composed of representatives from: the school principal, teachers, students, parents, community leaders and community-based organization (CBO) will be formed and trained on how to identify and provide local solutions to provide protective learning environments for safe access to school for children taking into consideration local context. The composition of the SMCs will ensure gender representation. The SMCs will work closely with Child protection networks to ensure protection mainstreaming. Teachers will be trained on how to provide PSS in collaboration with child protection and children identified as needing protection services will be referred to relevant protection service providers. Partners will be expected to work in close collaboration with relevant stakeholders to build on lessons learnt and existing training manuals on PSS.
