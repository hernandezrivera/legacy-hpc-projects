Project Summary

While the humanitarian crisis in Iraq enters a new phase leading protracted displacement, significant needs persist across all clusters. An estimated 6.5 million people are currently in need of assistance. This includes displaced people in camps and out-of-camp locations, poor communities hosting large numbers of displaced people and returnees who are in very high and high severity hot spots, also as HRP 2018 that mentioned over 30 percent of youth are currently not in employment, education or training. Human Appeal has planned to implement a project in Ninewa and Dohuk Governorate (West Mosul, Akre and Zakho) to increase access to inclusive, protective and quality formal and non-formal education for conflict-affected children and strengthen the capacity of teachers and schools to deliver timely, evidence-based education response. Through a dual intervention of formal schools support and temporary Learning Spaces, HA will ensure at-risk children in non-camp setting have access to formal and/or non-formal education and improved psycho-social well-being through the trained teachers/volunteers programme, targeting teachers’, volunteers and government officials in Ninewa and Dohuk.

Needs

Ongoing uncertainty in relation to camp closures and continuous movements of people are causing challenges about education staff availability; lack of teachers is reported in IDP schools across the proposed areas. In addition, there is a dire need to develop a mechanism for recruitment and payment of temporary teachers to fill these gaps. Displaced Iraqi children face challenges in the classroom on a daily basis. Teachers cannot provide the psychological support the children need and schools are not able to accommodate children from different learning systems, with different languages, accents and abilities. Poverty and limitations to access legal assistance also refrains the children from accessing education. In addition, the teachers are not capacitance in identifying protection issues pertaining to the children and the absence of community structures in the targeted areas to address the child protection issues also hinder the children to access education in a protective and enabling environment.

Activities

Consultation Meetings: Human Appeal will conduct consultation meetings with the community elders/traditional leaders (religious, clergy, tribal, elder, local teachers) and use them as entry point to gain support and legitimacy in order to reach out to the maximum community members particularly females and children and to introduce the idea of Teachers’ Training Programme. Human Appeal will use Age, Gender, Diversity Mainstreaming (AGDM) Approach to ensure that these elders represents different interest groups of Person of Concerns (PoCs) residing in the community.Mapping/ Profiling: Mapping of the target areas (West Mosul (Ninewa) and Akre, Zakho (Dohuk)) will be conducted in order to verify the existing data and also to get updated information regarding the socio-economic and demographic characteristics of the area, power dynamics, existing community support mechanisms, dividers and connectors in the community, specific needs of the communities regarding child protection interventions, relevant stakeholders working with the communities in the area etc. Data that is already available with Human Appeal through its protection monitoring assessments will be verified. However, the new data will be collected through community sessions (males/females), Focus Group Discussion (FGDs) ensuring community participation and adhering the Right Based Approach (RBA). Training of Trainers (ToT): Human Appeal will hire an International Consultant to develop a module for the teachers’ training programme and conduct ToTs in the targeted areas in Dohuk and Ninewa. 20 officials (10 from each location, 05 males & 05 females) from Department of Education (DoE) will be identified and trained on child protection, pedagogy, referrals, child protection mechanism at the school level etc. Trained officials will develop training plan at the end of the trainings in consultation with the Consultant. Identification & selection of the teachers/volunteers: Human Appeal will introduce the teachers’ training programme and will identify/select the teachers/volunteers (males/females) during the community sessions and Focus Group Discussion (FGDs), moreover, nominations by the DoE, School administrations, community or community elders will also be gauge on the basis of criteria set for the volunteers, for consideration. Human Appeal will select 30 schools, both primary and secondary in Ninewa and 30 schools (15 Zakho & 15 Akre), both primary and secondary. 2 teachers (1 male and 1 female) and 2 volunteers (1 male and 1 female) already working in the schools will be selected from each school in West Mosul (Ninewa). In addition, Human Appeal will prepare a resource pool of 50 volunteers (teachers) (25 males & 25 females) who will be identified from the community and will be trained on child protection and referrals etc. These trained volunteers will be deployed in schools on need basis. Action Plan Development: The trained teachers and volunteers will develop their action plan at the end of the training sessions. The stage and process of action planning can also be a litmus test of teachers’ and volunteers’ capacity to address some of the child protection issues at the school and community level, and whether they are in a position to engage with their community, students and other stakeholders in a meaningful manner to improve the condition of their community. Establishment of Feedback Mechanism: Human Appeal will ensure the Accountability to the Affected Population (AAP) by establishing a Complaint and Response Mechanism (CRM). Human Appeal will develop a Code of Conduct for the volunteers in consultation with department of Education (DoE) and it would be mandatory for the volunteers to sign and adhere it. In addition, a separate Grievances Mechanism will be established to deal with the cases in which confidentiality of the information provided by the POCs is breached. Human Appeal will review its existing developed documents for instance, Code of Conduct on Child Protection, Whistle Blowing Policy, Code of Conduct on Principles of Human Dignity and Human Rights and Security (Confidentiality) etc. to ensure that it is line with the volunteers rights and responsibilities. Strengthening/Establishing Community Support Structures: The already established community committees will be strengthened through sensitization sessions and community mobilization sessions. However, volunteers will advocate and mobilize the community to establish new committees like Child Protection Committees at the school or community level. At the community level the volunteers and Community Committees will also help develop early response to the issue/crisis. This will built an element of self-sufficiency among the communities and will enable them to mobilize and capitalize on their skills and talents.

Protection Risk Analysis

The key protection risks facing beneficiaries in Ninewa and Dohuk are related to security, safety, and human rights violation as well as perceived affiliation with extremist/terrorist groups. Beneficiaries are still exposed to security risks posed by the presence of ISIS sleeper cells, military operations in the areas, and other armed security actors. Particular grave risks for both beneficiaries and humanitarian staff are explosions or terrorist attacks related to the presence of the sleeper cells in many of the areas, as well as high contamination with explosive hazards, improvised and unexploded ordnances. Women and children are particularly vulnerable to gender and age-based violence including domestic violence and sexual abuse in camp and out-of-camp locations particularly terrorist-affiliated women and children. Children are exposed to risks of child marriage and sexual violence as well as child labour. Children and women with affiliation to terrorist groups may face detention, denial in access to services or legal remedies, violence, social exclusion and exploitation. Many conflict-affected people still suffer from psychological distress and especially PTSD. Many vulnerable displaced and returnee populations are exposed to the risk of access to civil documentation, with HLP issues – particularly female-headed households who often cannot access the legal remedies due to the absence of a male member of the family (often in detention or deceased).

Mitigation Measures

Human Appeal has a strong element of PSEA integrated into its Code of Conduct and programming. Human Appeal Iraq has a number of measurements to report cases of sexual exploitation abuse (SEA), it is mandatory that all cases are reported. Further to that: • Human Appeal adheres to the core principles of the UN Task Force on Preventing Sexual Exploitation and Abuse in Humanitarian Crises 2002. These principles apply in any project with women, men, boys and girls, irrespective of whether there is an emergency situation or not. • Sexual exploitation and abuse by project workers constitute acts of gross misconduct and are therefore grounds for the termination of employment. • Sexual activity with children is prohibited regardless of the age of majority or age of consent locally. A mistaken belief about a child’s age is not a defense. • Exchange of money, employment, goods, or services for sex, including sexual favors or other forms of humiliating, degrading or exploitative behavior are prohibited. • Sexual relationships between project workers and beneficiaries are strongly discouraged since they are based on inherently unequal power dynamics. Such relationships would undermine the integrity of work to help vulnerable and excluded children. Concerns or suspicions regarding sexual abuse or exploitation by a colleague, whether in the same organization or not, must be reported in accordance with Human Appeal’s PSEA Policy. • Human Appeal Iraq follows the guidelines of the Iraq PSEA Network and abides by national laws related to mandatory reporting, while respecting the SEA principles of confidentiality, informed consent, and survivor centred approach. HA will ensure that its entire field and office staff are trained on PSEA, and that PSEA focal points of different genders and from different departments are trained and available to receive complaints and follow up. Human Appeal has a robust security policy and ensures all key staff are security trained to an advance level for in-the-field humanitarian interventions. Human Appeal has an international Security Officer based in KR-I that helps strengthen networking and information gathering and sharing. All new staff members and visitors are given a comprehensive security briefing within 24 hours of arrival in country. Staff will attend a Violent Environment Preparedness Training before the staff will be allowed to work outside of the Kurdish controlled areas in Iraq. Human Appeal regularly receives updates from the INSO (International NGO Safety Organization) while also maintaining contact and networking with other humanitarian agencies, the UN, local Government and local security forces on the ground, such as ISF, Peshmerga and Asayish (official security in KR-I) and Iraqi Armed Forces. Human Appeal’s Security Officer and Protection Coordinator, Programme Coordinator and Emergency Coordinator are in direct contact with local authorities and coordinate with relevant military contacts to ensure safety of staff and interventions for beneficiaries. Prior to the delivery of any humanitarian assistance, HA ensure that risk assessments are performed through internal security analysis and communication with external forums for security updates, such as clusters, local and governmental authorities, UNOCHA, INSO and other agencies as they carry out regular humanitarian access assessments. Permissions, and ongoing communication with relevant stakeholders to obtain these permissions, are key to the partners’ ensured access to the areas of intervention proposed. Human Appeal is aware of which permissions are required and how to obtain them

How is your project accountable to affected population

The project team will ensure mainstreaming of Accountability to Affected Populations throughout the project cycle including (not exhaustive of) the following elements: 1. Consultation and participationDuring the initial phase of the project cycle, meetings will be conducted with targeted groups, DoEs. International ToT consultant will run advanced training sessions for trainers (DoE’s staff). The different groups present in the schools will be identified (eg volunteers and teachers.) to ensure that all the needs are addressed. DoEs will be consulted in the establishment of mechanisms for the project including information sharing. This will ensure that mechanism is context appropriate and accessible for all members of the targeted staff, particularly the most relevant stakeholders. 2. Establishment of feedback mechanismsHA takes the accountability to affected populations (AAP) as a fundamental principle of its programming. HA adheres to the age, gender and diversity policy and treats it as a general approach throughout its programming which also enables the implementation and adherence to HA's commitment to upholding IASC's commitments on AAP and our commitment to the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) which stress the participatory approach and a strong beneficiary feedback mechanism. HA has a dedicated MEAL department which implements activities to strengthen the quality of our programming, increase the transparency and accountability to donors and POCs we serve. HA’s MEAL team established an effective beneficiary feedback mechanism, which includes complaints and feedback boxes, the nation-wide hotline with the number displayed on all information dissemination materials, and the IDP Call Center (nationwide, UNOPS established AAP mechanism). Through this mechanism, we gather views, feedback and complaints of the affected populations in addition to regular participatory assessments, needs assessments, feedback and complaints mechanism, FGDs, KIIs, etc. Project performance and activities will be monitored both qualitatively and quantitatively throughout the project period utilizing a result-based monitoring system structured to consider the overall objective, outcome, outputs and the deliverables. A sample of beneficiaries will be visited and interviewed, and MEAL team will regularly perform random satisfaction surveys by the dedicated Accountability Officer. The lessons learnt are utilized by programmes teams to improve the quality of the programming and promptly respond to community-needs making our interventions relevant, timely, and accurate.
