Project Summary

Mercy-USA for Aid and Development intends to implement a project to address the urgent health needs in Banadir, Galgadud, Hiran, Mudug and Sool regions of Somalia. Since the collapse of Somalia's central government in 1991, repeated conflict in conjunction with poor food security, limited healthcare and nutrition service availability and has resulted in Somalia having among the worst health-related indicators in the world.
According to the FSNAU-FEWS NET 2021 Post Gu Technical Release - September 9, 2021, from October to December 2021, food insecurity is expected to further deteriorate among poor rural, urban, and displaced populations due to the impacts of anticipated, below-average 2021 Deyr (October-December) season rainfall, continued insecurity and other food security related risk factors, including rising food prices and cost of living, declining availability of milk for both consumption and sale, and a likely reduction in agricultural employment opportunities during the forthcoming Deyr season. Without sustained humanitarian food assistance, 3.5 million people across Somalia are expected to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes between October and December 2021. Rural populations are experiencing multiple declines in food and income sources. In pastoral areas, large-scale livestock migration was reported from rain-deficit areas to areas that received relatively better rainfall.
Mercy-USA will deliver primary health care services through 32 Maternal and Child Health Centers (MCHs). Mercy-USA will be facilitating the provision of medicines, supplies and equipment to the health facilities through UNICEF, while working together with the Ministry of Health (MOH) in each respective region to ensure standard provision of care and to build local capacity. Mercy-USA will train and support healthcare staff and Community Health Workers (CHWs) at each MCH facility to diagnose and treat common illnesses, provide safe pre-natal and post-natal care, and support vaccination and other health services. Mercy-USA will also promote local health, family planning counseling, safe-motherhood and safe-childhood initiatives through community sensitization.
Mercy-USA will ensure that people with disabilities participate in the implementation of project activities by ensuring that they are represented in all community meetings. During project implementation, segregated focus group discussions will be held with the community to identify gender inequalities, discuss all the genders' needs, and understand the risks faced by people with disabilities in accessing health services. Activities will target to address these needs during program implementation. Access to children with disabilities and their caretakers and/or disabled pregnant and lactating women will be ensured and increased through outreach activities and behavioral change communication campaigns.
During routine and regular program monitoring and evaluation, Mercy-USA staff will collect disaggregated data by disability, age and gender to monitor the impact of programs on vulnerable groups. Mercy-USA, during both monthly and quarterly reviews and analysis of data, will seek to monitor the inclusiveness of programming by comparing achievements versus available population data.

1. - Projects activities

• Support the provision of maternal and child health services at 32 MCHs through support for staff. Each MCH will include nurses, assistant nurses, midwives, vaccinators, community health workers (CHWs). Unskilled staff will include cleaners and security guards. The project will support 32 health facilities in 5 regions in Somalia which will provide primary health care, and CEmONC service in 4 of the targeted facilities. • Support 12 outreaches to provide health and nutrition services to the hard to reach areas. • Support the rehabilitation of 3 new MCHs and 5 existing MCH facilities with inadequate water systems, sanitation facilities and incinerators, to ensure access to adequate WASH services in health facilities. The lack of WASH services puts those seeking care and those providing at significant risk. The risks of site-related or preventable infections among health staff and patients decrease with access to appropriate WASH services and proper waste management systems. Given the importance of water availability and good hygiene during childbirth in particular, WASH is considered both a precondition and an entry point for good quality of care. Mercy-USA will ensure appropriate design of the water and sanitation facilities to be accessible to people with disabilities, late-term pregnant women, the elderly, and sick people to support core universal health care aspects of quality, equity, and dignity for all people.• Build healthcare providers capacity through refresher trainings. The technical healthcare workers will be trained on IMCI, ETAT, Obstetric emergencies, CMR and MHPSS so that they can give quality health services to the beneficiaries. This will be followed up with and on job training on constant bases.• Support the provision of antenatal, reproductive and postnatal treatment, services and referrals. All pregnant mothers will be given antenatal service at the facilities and they will be encouraged to deliver at the facilities and after delivery they will be given postnatal service too.• Support the provision of communicable disease immunization, treatment and referral at MCHs and outreaches. Mercy-USA will support the provision of care for communicable diseases at 32 static Maternal and Child Health Clinics (MCHs). The focus of this program will be to treat and prevent common causes of morbidity and mortality. This program will seek to prioritize the provision of care for children <5 years of age.
