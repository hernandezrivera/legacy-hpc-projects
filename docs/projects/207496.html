Project Summary

Caritas implements a comprehensive protection and gender mainstreaming strategy, incorporating age, gender, and diversity perspectives across all stages of project execution. Additionally, special consideration is given to especially vulnerable populations within this framework of protection mainstreaming. The organisation employs the Protection Mainstreaming Framework and is dedicated to adhering to the guidance, practical tip sheets, and strategy formulated by the WASH Sector. This is achieved by integrating the principles of safety and dignity, do no harm, meaningful access, accountability, participation, and empowerment into the response efforts. In this project, CB will provide a comprehensive water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) package to individuals of concern residing in the designated camps. This package includes the operation and maintenance of facilities, the provision of water supplies, and the promotion of hygiene practises, along with solid waste management and emergency preparedness.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

As per the ISCG situation and WaSH Sector report on the Rohingya Refugee Crisis, to date, the considerable efforts made by the government, together with WASH Partners, have resulted in 86% of Rohingya refugees and FDMNs reporting having enough water to meet their household needs. 97% of sanitation facilities are also reported as functional. Challenges remain, especially in terms of the operation of water networks and solid waste management, as only 2/3 of the waste is currently properly processed and a new landfill site will need to be built in 2024.In 2024, the WASH sector’s strategic focus will be on the operation and maintenance of existing WASH services through cost-efficient solutions. Quality provision of water and sanitation facilities will be targeted, as well as solid waste management provided through infrastructure support and community engagement (especially on solid waste). Adequate hygiene behavior's will be promoted, and hygiene items will be distributed through a multi-sectoral approach.In host communities, the sector will support equitable access to quality WASH services aligned with national development plans. WASH vulnerability criteria (e.g., lack of improved water and sanitation) will also be taken into consideration when targeting neighbouring communities directly or indirectly impacted as a result of hosting Rohingya refugees or FDMNs. CB will ensure the supply of WASH items (soap, menstrual kits, etc.) if gaps are found that are under the leadership of UN agencies in partnership with NGOs for distribution. However, NGOs in 2024 will be taking on more responsibilities at the camp level as an alternative to the decline of UN funding. The neutral monitoring system will be crucial to ensuring equitable and qualitative WASH services between camps.The CB, in collaboration with WASH Sector Response, will consider environmental issues, gender, age, disability inclusion, and accountability, with an emphasis on preparedness and response for natural disasters and public health risks, including disease outbreaks.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Caritas is concerned with unique and substantial risks to the safety and dignity of Rohingya individuals, particularly those with heightened vulnerability, such as women, girls, boys, persons with disabilities, elderly individuals, and diverse groups like LGBTQ, etc. WASH facilities are strategically located for safety with accessible access roads, addressing risky slopes and isolated areas, and incorporating gender segregation facilities. Priority areas in humanitarian response include PSEA and safeguarding. Ensuring nighttime safety, solar streetlights will be installed along access roads to latrines and provided with solar lamps. The approach is community-centered, emphasising participation, empowerment, and community strengthening to reduce risks and promote resilience. The MEAL structure and CFRM mechanism will uphold AAP and community responsiveness, while the Protection team will implement a Protection mainstreamed approach for integrated and inclusive programming, including capacity building of staff to ensure Protection mainstreaming.

3.3 - Age & Disability

In Caritas Bangladesh's WASH program, prioritizing age and disability components is paramount. The organization upholds a comprehensive Child and Vulnerable Adult Safeguarding Policy, creating a protective atmosphere. Regular training on Safeguarding, Code of Conduct, and PSEA is provided to staff and volunteers. Beneficiaries receive orientation on safeguarding policies before service delivery, and safeguarding is a recurring agenda item in meetings, distributed in Bengali. The WASH program embraces inclusivity for diverse groups, particularly persons with disabilities (PWDs), offering accessible facilities with handrails and ramps. Individualized plans are tailored for PWDs, reflecting a commitment to inclusive WASH. In Cox's Bazar's emergency context, Caritas Bangladesh ensures age and disability inclusivity through comprehensive measures, including inclusive policies, staff training, accessible facilities, individualized plans, and collaboration with specialized organizations. Continuous improvement underscores Caritas's dedication to adapting responses and prioritizing the well-being of all individuals, especially the most vulnerable, in emergency situations.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The systemic MEAL mechanism ensures AAP principles, CFRM, stakeholder consultation, and periodic meetings with stakeholders are designed in the project towards improving the quality of WASH services. CB aims at capacitating staff and volunteers to facilitate cultural programming with a perspective of sustainability and potential replication and to promote the empowerment of the community. The project engages the community in all processes of project implementation. Involvement of community members in needs assessment through participatory action research (PAR) methods Sharing impact findings with beneficiaries, organising periodical meetings and consultations, and taking decisions on the ways to ensure quality WASH services. Their suggestions will be carefully considered and addressed. The proposed project keeps the door open to community participation through coordinated and inclusive mechanisms.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Caritas Bangladesh is responsible for ensuring that beneficiaries safely access their services, without causing harm, and for promoting meaningful access, accountability, and participation of beneficiaries in the provision of WASH assistance.CB is actively connected with PSEA network and has a checklist that ensures the mainstreaming of Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) in Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) facilities. It emphasizes the secure and accessible location of water points and latrines, with demarcation and separate entries for men and women. Privacy and security are reinforced through facilities that can be locked from the inside and adequate lighting. The checklist includes provisions for sex-segregated WASH facilities, monitoring of queues for security, and special measures for those with accessibility challenges. Additionally, it addresses awareness and visibility of PSEA, GBV (Gender-Based Violence), and inclusion/disability materials and reporting hotlines, promoting a safe and informed environment for beneficiaries. Staff and volunteers, stakeholders will be trained on PSEA.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Emergency preparedness and response activities are in place in the proposed project. CB will continue to provide services so that each WASH facility has DRR contingency plan and continue following the plan accordingly with community people. Capacity building of staff, Community Volunteers, user groups, WASH committees, and community stakeholders, and their active engagement in DRR program management is the crucial part of the proposed project to prevent and mitigate the crisis. Their knowledge and skills development in the decision-making process and implementation ultimately help the community to be sustainable and resilient.
