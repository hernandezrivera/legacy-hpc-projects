Project Summary

The humanitarian situation in Somalia, particularly Benadir and Galkayo, has affected enrolments and retention of children in schools, with crisis-affected school-aged children dropping out, and further exacerbated by displacement, in addition to inequality in access to education services remaining an issue (HNO, 2021). COVID-19 pandemic effects on households (HH), for nearly two years, has severely impacted on supporting children’s schooling, with worsening financial constraints, affecting their access to quality education (HNO,2021). Diakonie Katastrophenhilfe (DKH) and implementing partners (IPs) in Somalia, seek to facilitate crisis-affected vulnerable and marginalized children (boys and girls) from internally displaced persons (IDPs) HH to have access to safe and quality education through increasing their enrolment and retention in the safe learning environment. DKH aims to support the existing 2,000 (1,000 girls), while at the same time mobilizing enrolment of 1120 (560 girls) more learners, including children living with disabilities (CLWD), from IDP settlements and destitute host communities in both Banadir and Galkayo areas into existing TLS/ informal schools registered with the Ministry of Education. Where there will be no schools, DKH will construct new TLS, and rehabilitate existing dilapidated ones to enable access to school by the vulnerable and marginalized IDP children. DKH targets distribution of conditional and unrestricted vouchers for education (V4E) to vulnerable HH for EiE. The vouchers will be a package to cover school fees for the learners, learning materials, food feeding and cover dignity kits for teenage school girls facilitating inclusion in education. Vouchers for school fees will be redeemed in school in participating schools to cover teacher incentives, teaching materials, school maintenance costs, COVID-19 compliance materials and basic supply of disability and gender-friendly WASH facilities; while those for learning materials, food vouchers and dignity kits will be redeemed at through the existing vendors. Food vouchers will be conditional to school attendance and in lieu of school feeding to address the risk of dropout by the vulnerable and marginalized learners due to constrained HH income retain them in school. To increase quality of education, DKH will support training of 58 (18F) teachers which will cover the pedagogical methods as recommended by the MoE; and inclusive education, protection and socio-emotional teaching and learning, and implementation of MoE teachers’ codes of conduct ensuring safety of learners. Further, 14 Community Education Committees (CEC), each composed of 7 members comprising the teachers’ representative, parents, and members of the camp management committee (CMC), will be trained on school management including parent mobilization and awareness campaigns to increase enrolment and retention of children in schools, child protection and safety. Further, the action will promote equitable access to education by the vulnerable and marginalized boys and girls, including CLWD, and host communities. The CECs, CMCs and parents will be sensitized on the importance of enrolling CLWD and ensuring an enabling and protective environment at the learning centres. Since the existing TLS have been registered by the MoE and being transitioned into community formal schools, the project will target supporting learners’ enrolment in the same and nearby formal schools enhancing national ownership. The TLS/community schools are located at the IDP settlements in coordination with the CMCs to enhance the safety of the learners reducing the distance to school and mitigatin vulnerability against forceful eviction. Gender separated latrines and handwashing facilities are present in the schools and where they do not exist, the action contributes. DKH will mobilize resources to supplement the budget, and will also replicate the TVET project in Galkayo to strengthen humanitarian-development nexus.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The project prefers the use of cash and voucher assistance as it provides vulnerable households with a source of income through CVA to enable the learners to enrol and attend schools, in addition to enabling the learners and teachers to access the targeted services or materials specifically earmarked for learning purposes due to the conditionalities and restrictions placed on the vouchers. The process of redeeming the vouchers also enables the running of schools through redemption to pay the teachers. To ensure that the vouchers are utilised for the intended purpose, DKH and IPs have ensured that the vouchers for learning are redeemed in school to provide teacher incentives, therefore there will be monitoring mechanisms on enrolment and teacher attendance. For learning materials, the project will utilize conditional vouchers, ensuring that the vouchers will only be redeemed for the learning designated learning materials. In addition, the food vouchers are also conditional to food access and therefore will only provide food to the targeted children allowing them to be retained in school. DKH and IPs will conduct routine monitoring of the records, including verifications with the beneficiaries to confirm the utilization of the cash support for the intended purpose.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

The project has considered market functionality through conducting assessments, including market and seasonality analysis. The cash will be provided based on the minimum expenditure basket, ensuring that while the beneficiaries purchasing power is increased they will be able to get access to livelihoods from the existing markets. The results of the seasonality analysis will inform the appropriate time of the year to implement the interventions, to ensure that the markets function as normal and beneficiaries are able to access goods and services from the markets. Besides, the amount of cash being injected will be little and may not have a negative effect on the prices of goods in the local market. During the implementation of the project, the team will liaise with other stakeholders to monitor the price behaviour and supplies for commodities to inform the effects the cash intervention is having on the local economy. Decisions will be made appropriately to ensure the cash is used for the right purpose, with no or little effect on the market functionality.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Mitigation 1: - DKH seeks to ensure that all the deserving cases are selected and included to benefit from the project. - DKH will utilize existing beneficiary selection criteria meant to ensure that the most vulnerable and marginalized IDPs and host community households are selected to benefit from the project. DKH also have effective monitoring and verifications mechanisms to ensure that the selection processes meet the documented criteria.- The design of the project mainstreams protection and will apply rights-based approaches in delivering the interventions. This will entail upholding the rights of the beneficiary school-age-going children from vulnerable and marginalized households in accessing quality education in a safe learning environment, in addition to upholding the dignity of the teenage girls by giving them dignity kits to allow them to continue attending classes.- DKH Works with local community members, including local leadership, especially the camp management committees in the IDP settlements and is sensitized on the selection criteria to ensure that the school-going children from the most vulnerable and marginalized households are selected to benefit from the project. - DKH and implementing partners will also conduct sensitization of the target communities on the rights of the beneficiaries and ensure the selection criteria is used - DKH and Implementing Partners will promote community participation through working with community structures during the identification of the beneficiaries from the affected household Mitigation 5:- The project is designed with a rights approach, ensuring that the beneficiaries’ rights are protected and their rights to education is enhanced. Based on this, DKH and IPs will put mechanisms and strategies in place to ensure that the children rights are upheld at all times, including training of teachers and collaborating with other stakeholders to ensure that the schools are not affected due to land disputes, with clear measures in place to address these - DKH and Implementing Partners promote an inclusive approach in the implementation of the TLS in the IDP settlements to avoid eviction through working with CECs who engage with relevant government bodies (MoE), local authorities, and landowners to negotiate for land use for the TLS, where they enter into land-use agreements to allow the TLS to be set up and continuous checks are done on possible threats of eviction. - The implementation of the project incorporates the participation of the community members and identify with the local level dispute resolution mechanisms and will depend on the local leadership to resolve land disputes threatening the existence of the TLS- Incorporation with protection actors and CECs, the project will establish reporting mechanisms to report security incidences concerning the learning spaces to advocate against attacks on TLS. - The TLS infrastructure designs make them mobile and easy to move in case there is an eviction.
