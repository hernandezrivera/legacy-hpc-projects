Project Summary

The health system in Somalia is challenged by humanitarian crisis and a high burden of communicable and non-communicable diseases. The morbidity and mortality trends in Somalia have remained almost similar for many years with diseases such as diarrhoea, acute respiratory infections (ARI), malaria, measles and malnutrition coupled with pregnancy, labor and delivery related complications affecting the populations over the years making Somalia among the countries with poorest health indicators in the world.
Though maternal mortality rates have dropped from 732 in 2015 to 692 maternal death per 100,000 live birth, maternal mortality remains high in Somalia and at least 1 in every 1000 women aged 15-49 dies due to pregnancy or birth related complications (SHDS-2020). The survey further indicates, at least 5% of women would be expected to die from pregnancy related complications during their reproductive lifetime. This is attributed to post-partum hemorrhage, pre-eclampsia, obstructed labor, low uptake of antenatal care, postnatal care and low level of delivery at health facilities and no support from skilled health care providers. The survey indicates, 78% of birth were delivered at home, only 24% of women had at least 4 ANC visits and 32% of birth are delivered with assistance of skilled health professional. Neonatal mortality rate stands at 38% for both sexes. Women exposed to health information and pregnancy related awareness messages are more likely to seek ANC services and assisted by skilled birth attendant. According to SHDS only 31% of women of reproductive age who had a live birth received ANC from skilled personnel during their last birth and overwhelming 89% of the mothers did not receive any post-natal check-ups in the 1st two days after child birth. The main barrier that impede access to health care among women is related to out of pocket financing challenge and distance to health facilities. To address this, mobile teams/IERT for hard to reach areas and close to persons with disability, continuous awareness to promote health seeking behaviors including newborn, reproductive and sexual health at the facility levels and IDPs settings are inevitable.
Child health is critical component for child survival, productivity and development. There is a high risk of measles outbreaks and acute watery diarrhoea (AWD)/cholera outbreaks due to crowded settlements and limited access to basic sanitation and hygiene services. According to the recent survey only 11% of children aged 12-23 months are fully vaccinated meaning they received all the basic vaccinations (One dose BCG, one dose measles, 3 doses of pentavalent and polio vaccines). While DPT3 immunization coverage stands 52% in Somalia only 48% of children were vaccinated against Measles. Child mortality rate for both sexes stands at 122 per/1000 <5 live births.
To address the high morbidity and mortality trends, Services will be provided and maintained by utilizing both Mobile and facility based approaches. SFH in partnership with IOM have built Bulagaduud level 2 Health center covering 6 main locations in the newly liberated riverine areas of Kismayo and Jamame districts. The facility is estimated to covers an estimated population of over 35000 persons in the locations of (Gobley, Yontoy, wirkoy, Bulagaduud and Barsaanguni). These locations were liberated in 2018 but had very limited presence of humanitarian actors, poor immunization coverage and high rates of maternal and neonatal deaths due to lack of health facilities and skilled health service providers. Further, this program will be implemented in Hosingo, Kulbiyow and Raskamboni Health centers which are the three main fixed and accessible health centers in Badhadhe districts. The facilities served a population of over 40000 persons in Badhadhe districts. Mobile medical teams will cover the rural and hard to reach locations and establish strong referral network for the management of complicated and emergency cases.

Project Activities

As designed in the four-tier EPHS service delivery framework in Somalia, the proposed interventions aim to ensure that all vulnerable populations in hard to reach, rural, Urbans and IDPs in lower Juba including 6 newly liberated locations in Jamamme are able to access core life-saving and essential health care, reproductive health services, Mental health/disability and psycho-social support, promotion and treatment services through community based approach. This will be achieved by ensuring: -1) Provision of emergency lifesaving and essential package of quality preventative, promotive and curative services for the most vulnerable populace, male, female, boys and girls.2) Promotion of safe motherhood to improve maternal, newborn and child health, appropriate hygiene and optimal infant and child feeding practices. 3) Mothers and newborns receive an essential package of quality antenatal, postnatal and new-born care; SFH will request reproductive health/rape kits from UNFPA/UNICEF4) Children are immunized and able to access treatment for common childhood diseases - especially Measles, malaria, pneumonia and diarrhoea. SFH will request EPI vaccines from UNICEF.5) Families and communities adopt positive care and nurturing practices during pregnancy, childbirth and in early childhood; this will promote uptake of ANC services.Sexual and Reproductive Health Services includes: Adolescent sexual and reproductive health services, family planning (child spacing and contraceptive use), Antenatal care (ANC), access to micro-nutrients, delivery by skilled birth attendants in the health facilities including emergency referral, post-natal care, including post-partum family planning counselling, and early newborn and neonatal care. SFH will identify and sustain a reliable emergency Obstetric referral mechanism to tertiary facilities to ensure prompt access to blood transfusion, Caesarian sections and management of other serious obstetric complications.IDPs and returnees often suffer from various mental health problems due to violence faced before or during displacement, including rape, arbitrary detentions, separation, torture, killing, massive destruction, sexual and gender based violence (SGBV) and child soldiering. SFH will provide routine mental health and psychosocial support, counselling and referral of severe cases to protection cluster actors and other specialized service providers.In Summary, the following specific activities will be undertaken: -1). Community outreach/Mobile-health promotion and preventive services through a network of gender dis-aggregated community health workers on Malaria, Diarrhea, Pneumonia, Vaccine preventable diseases, IYCF, safe motherhood, hygiene and advocacy against GBV.2) General consultations and treatments (both communicable and non-communicable diseases) with access to essential medicines adhering the standard treatment protocols.3) Secondary or tertiary referrals for the cases meeting pre-agreed medical referral criteria such as obstetric complications and Severe chronic diseases4) Growth monitoring and immunization services (Measles and Polio) to increase the coverage of infant and child vaccinations.5) Treatment of micro-nutrient deficiencies, Family planning, Antenatal care, postnatal care and skilled deliveries. 6) Conduct facility based identification, provide psycho-social support and prompt referral for appropriate services for people with disability and mental health issues7) Identification, counseling, management and prompt referral for survivors of Gender based Violence8) Increase the coverage of mosquito nets by distributing LLITNs.9) Capacity building of 28 technical health professionals to ensure standard quality of care and patients safety concerns are met.10) Contingency plan to response common disease outbreaks (AWD/Cholera) in Lower juba and maintain a surveillance system in coordination with the health cluster and the Ministry of Health.

Mitigation Measures

1). Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance. Mitigation: a) SFH has strict policy on Prevention of Sexual exploitation and abuse that guides our humanitarian programming and assistance. In this policy, every new staff upon recruitment is required to sign PSEA policy, organizational code of conducts and Child protection statement to ensure strict adherence/compliance and its violation will lead to disciplinary actions including termination of employment contract. b) No 1 to 1 engagement will children and both fixed and mobile staff are gender dis-aggregated and women with special cases attended by female staff. c) Waiting areas will be separate for male and female at the health centers. d) Separate examination rooms with female nurse or doctors will be established. e) Consent will be sought before any operation or examination is included by any male doctor/Nurse incase a female doctor is not around.2). Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. Mitigation measure: SFH considers exclusion based on disability, gender or social status as strong protection concern and will be mitigated through the following approach:a) In the mobilization phase of the program, SFH will ensure by informing the community that persons affected with disability and those marginalized are by default the prioritized groups.b) SFH will undertake direct implementation of the program without any intermediary or third party contractor to ensure inclusive service delivery with the support of the district health committees and AAP committees representing persons with disabilities, marginalized groups and women.c) Through the mobile teams SFH will ensure person mobility impairment are reached.d) SFH will facilitate installation of appropriate hand bars and disability friendly removable seat at the health facility latrines.e) To accommodate persons with impaired hearing, vision or mental health, SFH will provide information about the availability of services through both verbal or written forms.f) SFH will ensure the location, distance and access routes to the health facilities and services are accessible to all members of the community, including persons with disability. g) SFH will collect gender, age and diversity sensitive data to ensure all age groups, disability and genders access inclusive and quality health care services.h) Where applicable SFH will coordinate with other actors in Lower Juba such as Humanity & Inclusion (Handicap International) who are specialized in working with persons with impaired mobility and disability to provide data of such individual to improve service delivery.3) Risk of unsafe and unethical management of data, especially disclosure of sensitive personal data on beneficiaries. Mitigation measures. a) SFH will assign standard officer/complaint officer to receive, record and keep the confidential and sensitive information from the beneficiaries and particularly GBV related cases. b) SFH will uses coded text to record the details of GBV survivors to protect their personal information and data. c) SFH will conduct awareness sessions on the availability of protection services and referral and share the most confidential avenues/mode the beneficiaries could share their feedback without being exposed.
