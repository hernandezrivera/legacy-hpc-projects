Project Summary

The proposed 12-month intervention will improve access to quality education, increase access to critical WASH inputs and enhance child protection mechanisms in 18 schools for conflict-affected IDP’s and host community learners in Mudug and Galgaduud regions of Galmudug State of Somalia. Education specific activities will include the provision of education supplies, emergency take-away food rations for students to meet their food and energy needs, emergency teacher incentives to motivate teachers to stay in the schools, and sanitary kits for adolescent girls to encourage their regular attendance at school. Specific WASH activities will include increasing schools’ water storage capacity through the provision of PVC storage tanks, the construction and rehabilitation of latrines, and improving hygiene practices among children through engagement with community hygiene promoters (CHPs). As CHPs will interact closely with students during hygiene promotion sessions and recreational activities, CISP will train this cadre on hygiene promotion, psychosocial support (PSS), and child protection and safeguarding. Specific Protection activities will include the mapping and sharing of existing child protection service providers and their respective services to school administrators and communities, strengthening child protection systems in schools, including the recruitment and training of child protection focal persons at each school, and awareness raising and training of community education committees (CEC) on child protection. CISP will also continue to engage with Ministry of Education Culture and Higher Education (MoECHE)
CISP will provide training to CECs on their roles and responsibilities, food and water management, disaster risk reduction (DRR), civic skills, proposal development, and child protection in the respective schools. To ensure water availability in targeted schools, CISP will provide cash grants to CECs. In return, CECs will contribute in-kind or cash to procure safe water and manage its distribution to students. Further, the CECs will be trained on DRR to build their capacity and improve school preparedness against future disasters. Disability and gender-friendly latrines in the targeted schools will be rehabilitated or, where necessary, constructed to minimize disruptions in learning for girls and boys. CISP will coordinate a enrolment drives through MoE and with CECs and religious leaders to encourage school enrolment with an emphasis on children affected by displacement. CISP education department will utilize the existing synergies with CISP protection department to will assess the existing child protection mechanisms at the start of the project, and, as determined from the assessment results, targeted schools will develop appropriate action plans to address areas of concern. To measure the effectiveness of the child protections systems implemented and capture lessons learned, CISP will conduct an end line assessment. Through the CISP protection department engagement with CEC’s and the regional Gender Focal Points (GFP’s) of MoECHE, CISP will leverage the agency's apparatus and community assets to raise awareness among children on child protection and the established mechanisms which will serve to strengthen child protection in schools.

Needs

An inter-agency assessment conducted 21-24 July 2018 in Cadaado & Hobyo district in Galmudug state established that 9,000 school-aged children lack education support. The assessment also stated that the IDP children have never had access to formal education in their areas of origin, because of sanctions imposed by armed non-state actors. The report stated the main barriers to access education is lack of learning facilities, inability of families to meet cost of education and for girl’s household chores. With the support of the GM MoE, CISP conducted a rapid needs assessment, between July 27-28, 2018 in 5 primary schools in Cadaado and 8 schools in Hobyo districts. The primary source of information was interviews with head teachers and other key informants in the two districts. The current enrollment, is 3794 (1776 girls) with 86 (12 F) teachers. The most pressing needs, as reported by head teachers, are water (100%), construction/rehabilitation of classrooms/toilets (70%), teaching learning materials (TLMs) (40%), teacher incentives (45%), food (30%), training on child protection (85%), training of teachers & CECs on PSS or hygiene promotion 95%. CISP strategy is therefore in line with the Somalia Education cluster strategy for the emergency response in proposing and focusing on admission of IDP’s, retaining current students in schools and supporting the readmission of dropouts in the targeted schools areas through the provision of safe drinking water, provision of emergency food rations, provision of sanitary kits, construction/rehabilitation of classrooms/toilets, provision of TLM, training teachers & CEC’s on child protection, establishing of referral pathways, training teachers on basic pedagogical, life-saving learning skills and psycho-social support, training CECs on PSS/hygiene promotion, provision of PVC tanks, WASH & hygiene promotion in schools and support to CECs in schools management as well as support with emergency teacher incentives.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

CISP will ensure project activities and processes through which fundamental human rights principles, including non- discrimination, ensuring meaningful access, and prioritizing the safety and dignity of our constituents are recognized and realized in program design and implementation. As per the findings of inter-agency assessment conducted in July 2018, the armed conflict of Xarardheere has resulted in increased IDP’s and household vulnerability and subsequent exposure to protection risks, particularly for displaced groups. Among the new IDPs there are 430 unaccompanied and separated children due to armed groups conflict displacement was reported across the two districts assessed in Mudug and Galgaduud regions. Eight percent of households indicated that children were involved in harsh or dangerous activities, which is likely a consequence of reduced household resilience as children are forced to contribute to household income. To address child protection issues in the targeted locations, CISP will assess child protection concerns, map existing child protection mechanisms and referral pathways, train CECs, community volunteers, child protection focal points, and hygiene promoters on child protection broadly, specific child protection mechanisms and referral pathways. Students will be made aware of children protection policies and the contact points if there are concerns. CISP has a signed MoU with Galmudug MoE under the EGEP and SETS projects to work on all aspects of school support. Ministry of education officials such as MoE Gender Focal Points, and will be engaged at the regional level in the development of the child protection referral mechanisms to address the child protection needs in the targeted locations

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

In Somalia, women are disadvantaged compared to men on all socioeconomic and human development indicators. With a Gender Inequality Index of 0.776, the country ranks fourth lowest globally. Social norms and power structures impact the lives and opportunities available to different groups of men and women. With more than 50 percent of the population under the age of 15, addressing gender inequalities is critical to maximize impact and socioeconomic development, and build resilience and sustainable peace. CISP is currently implementing the EGEP project in the same regions with a focus on girls’ education. EGEP Phase II baseline conducted in November 2017, showed that girls continue to lag behind boys across education indicators: enrollment, attendance, learning, and transition rates. The evaluation found that gender disparity is particularly acute in rural areas. CISP will ensure gender equality while implementing the project activities, focusing specifically on marginalized girls. Gender-friendly latrines will be constructed to help girls attend schools without disruption and to feel more comfortable. Sanitary kits will be provided to adolescent girls so that they are able to attend school on a regular basis. In addition, during the training of CECs/Hygiene Promoters, a gender equity component will be integrated into the training to ensure that gender equity is taken into consideration at the implementation phase both at the schools and household levels. All monitoring and evaluation data collected through the project will be dis-aggregated by gender to ensure the project is able to monitor the differing effects of activities on girls and boys.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

CISP has access and broad community acceptance in the targeted districts since we implemented EU funded project in the proposed targeted districts. However, due to the remoteness and the presence of armed groups in the catchment and the neighboring districts, access can be abruptly restricted due to the ongoing conflict between armed groups and the host community members. We will regularly monitor the security situation in the targeted locations through the engagement of law enforcement agencies, humanitarian organizations in the region and the local communities. If access is restricted based on our security assessments and/or an advisory from the International NGO Safety Organization (INSO), a revised implementation strategy will be adopted to provide uninterrupted support through the engagement of regional/district education officials and gender focal persons.
