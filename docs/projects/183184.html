Project Summary

Access to learning continues to be a challenge for over 1.2 million children in Lebanon due to the economic crisis and COVID-19 pandemic; the Ministry of Education and Higher Education estimates an influx of 100,000 children from the private sector to the public. Furthermore, a significant percentage of children with disabilities struggled to follow remote learning last year and were left out of education. The challenge to access learning becomes more complex to these children due to stigma and limited capacity of the education system to respond to their needs [Based on the consolidated analyses done by the ERP Lebanon – Education sector regarding the situation of access to education in Lebanon]. Prior to the crisis and pandemic, children with disabilities already faced exclusion. According to UNESCO (2021), children with disabilities are 2.5 times more likely not to access remote learning and to drop out when schools re-open [Consolidated information from sources such as GPE, World Bank, UNESCO, and UNICEF (2020, 2021) on how the COVID-19 pandemic has disrupted education around the world including Lebanon]. This project therefore aims to mitigate these challenges and specifically aims to provide access to learning to 3,000 out-of-education Lebanese and migrant children with and without disabilities, aged 6-14. The logical framework developed by the National Education Sector in Lebanon defines and guides the development of this project’s objectives and targets. However, HI emphasizes on targeting children with disabilities based on its institutional capacity on disability inclusion in Lebanon and globally. This then provides the organization the benefit of responding to the needs of children with disabilities who remain the most marginalized group in accessing learning. Notwithstanding, HI recognizes the importance of targeting children without disabilities as well, children from the same households or neighborhoods of children with disabilities. To achieve its objectives and targets, this project will invest on two major interventions.
First is the identification and referral to basic services including education of out-of-school children with and without disabilities. HI’s trained outreach workers will identify children at the community level. The data gathered from the identification exercise will then support HI in defining specific interventions to respond to children’s needs and barriers faced, as well as to facilitate their referral. The second intervention of providing direct assistance to children with and without disabilities, and their caregivers to support continued learning will then take place. In this project, HI will focus on direct assistance, namely: cash for education, assistive technologies, learning kits, and psychosocial support. HI will ensure continued learning by developing personalized support plans identifying children’s needs with corresponding low-cost solutions. Through outreach, these plans will then be monitored periodically via home visits and phone calls; they will include activities that provide children and parents/caregivers with training sessions on digital literacy, focusing also on practical skills such as connecting to WiFi/mobile data, signing up/creating accounts, and joining online classes. Finally, the personalized support plans will outline the provision of PSS activities to children and parents/caregivers, including the adaptation of existing PSS curriculum through theater plays, interactive learning, and training sessions. To ensure meaningful and efficient delivery of these direct assistances, HI will establish processes at different levels. Furthermore, HI’s outreach workers will follow up on learning retention among children with and without disabilities. Finally, data and evidence generation will be a prioritized under this project. HI will then ensure dissemination of these data and evidence to government and partners to facilitate scale up or replication.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

The project aims to provide access to continued learning of 3,000 out-of-education Lebanese and migrant children with and without disabilities aged 6-14. The project envisions three results:Expected result 1: Out-of-education children with and without disabilities are identified and referred to access basic services including education. Activities: a) identification of children through outreach; b) refer identified children to basic services including education internally/externally. Expected result 2: Out-of-education children with and without disabilities and their parents/caregivers receive assistance to enable continued learning. Activities: a) assessment of children with disabilities to determine needs to support learning; b) provision of Assistive Technologies to children with disabilities; c) refer externally children with disabilities to health/rehabilitation services; d) cash for education to enroll and retain in education; e) distribution of low/high tech or learning kits.Expected result 3: Parents/caregivers and their out-of-education children with and without disabilities have access to PSS programs. Activities: a) accessible & inclusive awareness sessions to parents/caregivers and children on violence/bullying/online safety; b) accessible and inclusive PSS to ensure remote learning support; c) establish accessible & inclusive self-help groups; d) train/coach self-help groups to ensure support of parents/caregivers to continued learning of their children.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

HI’s institutional policies include the protection of beneficiaries and staff from sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment and on child protection. For this project, the following interventions will support PSEA and protection mainstreaming: sharing leaflets on HI’s complaint mechanism; delivering specific key messages on PSEA and other protection topics as part of the outreach, and reinforced through PSS by including awareness raising modules. In operationalizing gender and social inclusion: targets are divided equally between girls and boys; vulnerability criteria will ensure gender and disability are prioritized. Examples include the application of the Washington Group and UNICEF’s Module of Child Functioning to identify and refer children with functional difficulties. Finally, the project will ensure that caregivers/parents activities include mothers and fathers, highlighting the role of fathers’ in the education and psychosocial development of the child.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

HI will ensure the selection of beneficiaries by applying certain vulnerability criteria, using an identification assessment. This assessment will consist of different needs analysis, vulnerability grouping and Washington Group Questions (WGQs). This will allow to determine the needs of beneficiaries and direct them to the needed services (internally/externally by referring them). The project will also ensure the participation of beneficiaries in building their support plans by allowing them to indicate their needs/preferences for the intervention implemented.HI have recently developed a new Feedback and Complaint Mechanism (FCM) enabling different stakeholders to raise their voices and concerns through different available modes of reception (complaint phone line, complaint email, complaint boxes, HI staff, FGDs, and data collection exercises). Briefing on this system as a right for the beneficiaries will be presented at the start of the implementation.
