Project Summary

The project is aimed at supporting the most vulnerable persons in Banadir particularly in the Khaxda, Daynile and Dharkinley districts with food interventions. The project will improve the lives of the IDPs by providing food vouchers to improve the immediate food security, health and nutritional well-being of IDPs, mainly by tackling the immediate food needs and reducing the percentage of people in severe acute food insecurity phases (crisis and worse). Food security interventions in camps is aimed at preventing malnutrition in the population, especially among women, young children and other vulnerable groups. Priority will be given to households with malnourished children, female headed households (widowed divorcees, separated and without adequate support from extended family members), elderly headed, child headed households and Socially excluded groups (abandoned women, intellectual/mental disability all with no economic active members. The project will use the most appropriate form of food assistance through vouchers depending on the market establishment and accessibility and vendors will be selected from within the selected target areas with close proximity to the beneficiaries to reduce protection risks.
SSWC will conduct market assessment after every voucher distribution and cash transfers to see what are the market prices for the predetermined food ratio for the beneficiaries. This will track the availability of food and the fluctuation of the market prices. SSWC will coordinate the distribution of the food through the qualified vendors who meet the standards to distribute food to the beneficiaries and on the Cash transfer SSWC will use the available e-money transfer services within Somalia. SSWC will buy SIM cards that will be distributed to the beneficiaries to receive the money. There will be complaints and feedback registers at the camps and the distribution centers beneficiaries will call a free toll number that will manned by the project staff. Immediate solutions will be given to the beneficiaries who have complaints regarding the cash transfer and the distribution of food by the vendors.
Voucher distributions will take place in the target communities, ensuring that the elderly, pregnant women and women with young children all have easy access to the selected distribution points. Post distribution monitoring will then be done to capture the exact feeling of the community. This intervention will be organized to assess and monitor the food security status of the population. This project will work closely with livelihood programmes to improve the status and promote self-reliance. Key humanitarian principles such as Care Confidentiality, privacy and impartiality will add value to the project implementation alongside active engagement of the local administration. The project will put in place a beneficiary feedback mechanism where complaints, compliments are recorded and attended to.

Mitigation Measures

Delivery of humanitarian assistance affects peaceful coexistence and spark intercommunity conflict over access to resourcesThere is expected risk of conflicts between the host communities and the selected vulnerable beneficiaries from the IDP community. The distribution of food items may trigger tensions between IDPs and host communities and increase risks of exclusion and marginalization, or even trigger violence against IDPs. Save Somali Women and Children will consult both displaced and host communities about food distributions to assess whether assistance could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the two communities. SSWC will include the vulnerable host community members as part of their beneficiaries and stakeholders in the implementation of the project. The host community will also be emphasised in the sensitization and planning meetings. Food distribution staff and committees will be representative of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, disability) as they can play a key role in identifying issues related to exclusion and discrimination and be proactive in ensuring the voice of marginalized groups is represented. SSWC will also apply a systematic outreach process in registering beneficiaries for distributions and keep lists of beneficiaries and personal data confidential and safe from unintended use while applying effective data protection measuresas well as making sure distribution points and services are safe with enough space for orderly distribution. During delivering assistance, exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance is possible and SSWC will prioritize safety & dignity and avoid causing harm. Save Somali Women and Children will ensure the location of and access routes to distribution points and services are safe with enough space for orderly distribution. The distribution centers will be central and the distribution will take place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall. Placing distribution points near military installations will be avoided to prevent threats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire or violence and weapon bearers will not be present inside distribution points. People living with disabilities may have difficulty accessing the distribution point or unable to carry the food items. Save Somali Women and Children will ensure it will work vendors that have special arrangements for persons who have difficulty accessing the distribution point or unable to carry the food items. SSWC will work in coordination with protection actors to put in place measures to prevent and respond to physical/sexual violence arising during distributions. Ensuring accountability to affected populations is important in humanitarian assistance and to counter this risk, SSWC will be transparent with the affected populations by providing them with accessible and timely information on selection criteria for targeted assistance, organizational procedures and processes that affect them. SSWC will set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Eg. feedback box at distribution site, phone lines , setting up mechanisms for submitting feedback that do not require the beneficiary exposing themselves to project staff. Responding to complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can/need to be put in place and organizing awareness raising sessions so that people know how it works. SSWC will also provide clear information to distribution actors and affected population explaining that beneficiaries do not have to pay or provide services/favours to anyone in exchange for receiving food assistance.
