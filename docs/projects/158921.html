Project Summary

The Ecumenical Accompaniment Programme in Palestine and Israel (EAPPI) is an international, ecumenical initiative of the World Council of Churches (WCC). EAPPI’s method of accompaniment falls under four strands: i) to offer protective presence to vulnerable communities under occupation, ii) to monitor and report on human rights violations, iii) to support Palestinian and Israeli peace groups, and iv) to advocate for policy change to end the military occupation of Palestine.
The Protective Presence that EAPPI is providing contributes to creating a safer environment for civilians to address their own needs, to solve their own conflicts and to protect vulnerable individuals and communities. A part of EAPPI´s daily activities is specifically intended to ensure safe access to education for schoolchildren and their teachers and to monitor attacks on schools and school compounds.
The project´s aim is to ensure a continuation of the Ecumenical Accompaniers (EAs)´presence in the most vulnerable areas of the West Bank and East Jerusalem in order to deter violence, document violations of international laws and regulations, and gather information for international advocacy work, pursuing the vision of a just peace with freedom, equal rights, dignity, and security based on international law.

1 - Needs

EAPPI monitors human rights violations and the breaches of international humanitarian law and reports on these violations to UN agencies and partner organisations providing humanitarian aid. In the period January – September 2019 EAPPI recorded a total of 607 incidents. These violations of International Human Rights and International Humanitarian Rights Law fall in three categories: restrictions of access to land and livelihood, and restrictions on the access to education which constitute 28 % of all incidents reported (174 incidents); violations of rights leading to displacement were 215 or 35 % (demolitions, confiscation, eviction, settlement expansion), and violence (harassment, detentions, physical abusage, incursions, killings) was reported in 303 incidents or 37 %. For Area C 405 incidents were reported, for East Jerusalem 95 incidents and for Hebron H2 31 incidents were documented. During the same period of last year (January – September 2018) there were 589 incidents reported. The increase in the number of reported incidents shows the importance to provide protective presence to vulnerable communities even in the coming years. In occupied Palestine, the obstacles to receiving an education are numerous. Large numbers of Palestinian children living in the West Bank and East Jerusalem experience serious harassment on their way to school, but also in classrooms and in the schoolyards. EAs witness delays at checkpoints, harassment by soldiers and settlers on school routes, and clashes between the students and the military. Teachers are also subject to harassment and controls by the military personnel. The systematic restrictions imposed by the Israeli government on Palestinian schoolchildren and teachers violate a number of international laws.

2 - Activities

EAPPI’s activities:- Providing protective presence operating from seven placements (one in East Jerusalem and six placements covering ten governorates in the West Bank). - Joining farmers in different seasons (ploughing, planting, and harvesting) when they need to access their lands in some of the hot spots: for example land near the settlement fence, or when coordination with the Israeli authorities is needed.- Joining herders; EAs accompany herders during shepherding, especially in places near outpost where settlers are trying to take over land. In addition, EAPPI responds to sleepover requests by the local communities and provides protective presence overnight after getting the security clearance from the Security Department. For example, in Khan al Ahmar the EAPPI teams slept over a number of nights in the most crucial period of threats responding to the request by the local community.- Walks and school runs on regular basis to provide protective presence against settler violence in the old cities of Jerusalem and Hebron.- Presence at non-violent demonstrations and monitoring the situation (after getting security clearance from the Security Department).- Advocacy efforts by EA’s when they go back to their home countries - Receiving international delegations and taking them for a field visit - Advocacy trips on a capitol level and to UN human rights council - Responding to emergency needs following alerts from local contacts and partner organizations- Updating the EAPPI database and referring incidents; EAPPI Incident Database is the tool used by EAPPI to refer incidents taking place on the ground to Diplomats, UN bodies, INGOs, NGOs and other actors on the ground. The incident report submitted by the EAs in the field, which is the first step in the process, include the following information: Date, Community Name, Location, Checkpoint Type (closures / barriers), Checkpoint Name (if applicable), Perpetrator, Other organizations present, School name (if applicable), Victims, Witness, Consent (To report, To use picture), Follow up by the Authorities (if applicable), Has or will complaint be filed?, Description, The needs of the Community (Legal, Psychosocial, Protective, Shelter, other), Violation and the Human Rights Affected. The second step is done by the Field Officer at the Jerusalem Office, Incidents submitted are reviewed and referred to other organizations (email Generated from the Database). The selection of who receives what is based on 1- The request from the organization itself on which incident they are interested in. 2- The organization’s field of work. 3- The location of the incident. Note: EAPPI reports on; child rights violations and access to education incidents, legal aid needed, settler violene, access to land and livelihood, road closures, need for shelter, settlement expansion, PSS, arrests, and incidents at checkpoints. UNOCHA is copied on most of the incidents because they work on documentation and the UNOHCHR is copied on incidents where there is a need for referral.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

There are various ways in which the local beneficiaries can submit complaints to the office. For instance, the beneficiaries can present their complaint directly to the EAs who will forward it to the office. Another channel that they can use is the EAPPI driver or the local contacts who are also the focal point for community-related matters. Another option is to call the field officer or the Jerusalem office or submit complaints via e-mail to the office. Currently, the WCC is developing a complaint mechanism that will ease the filing and follow up of complaints.In addition, DCA/NCA holds regular field visits and meets with beneficiaries to receive feedback from them and the beneficiaries are able to file a complaint through DCA/NCA complaint mechanism DCA/NCA have been also supporting its partners in increasing the capability of the complaints and feedback mechanism to inform decision-making, enhance accountability and transparency. The feedback will be used for; planning future activties, monitoring the sucess and for improving the activties for the future.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Risk analysis and management is covered by the Security Department, Field Officer and Local Program Coordinator. This ensures that all actions that are taken by EAPPI staff and accompaniers follow the security procedures and guidelines. The security protocols are regularly updated with the aim of minimizing the risks and creating a safe working environment. The Security Officer covers most of the security risks while the Field Officer and LPC cover the other areas of operational risk analysis.Informed consent: All EAs make sure that the rights-holders that they interact with in the field give consent to the use of photos, quotes, and other information for reporting purposes. When evaluation practices include children, consent is obtained by parents. Furthermore, EAPPI will not operate in any area without the community and its decision makers' consent. Management on data confidentiality and sensitivity: All staff members are obliged to sign a clause on Respect of Confidentiality and Data Protection. EAs have to sign the Terms of Use before starting to use the Hub and the Incident database. All manuals that the teams receive are marked with Confidentiality sign and the disposal of hard copies is taken care for by the Jerusalem Office. Furthermore, there is a limit of access to different kinds of users, e.g: the EAs have limited access compared to the LPC. For trainings, EAs get an initial training in their home countries about the history of the Israeli-Palestinian conflict, an update on the current situation in the region, training on HR and IHL, security briefings, language course, cultural sensitivity, an introduction to non-violent methods and accompaniment etc. In Jerusalem, the EAs receive a 10-day training on team building, Humanitarian Law, Stress Management, Communication, Local Security Briefing, Incident reporting and Data Management, Child Protection, and briefings on activities particular to each placement. Additionally, the new teams spend five days in their placements with the old teams who introduce them to the activities on the ground.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

EAPPI is guided by the notion of principled impartiality, meaning that it is not taking sides in the Israeli-Palestinian conflict. It supports groups from both communities working for peace and justice. Also, within the Palestinian communities, it does not engage in political action or preferential treatment of particular segments of the population. EAs are committed to hearing from as many perspectives as possible and having an open and honest conversation with the people living in the region. EAs value the work of deepening interfaith relations and are thus bridging the gaps among various communities. As a faith-based programme EAPPI stands in solidarity with churches and the minority of indigenous Palestinian Christians in the Holy Land. Additionally, EAPPI is committed to gender mainstreaming and tries to engage in women to women conversations whenever this is possible. Even in situations when EAPPI alone cannot provide assistance to vulnerable communities, the referral system to partner organization makes it possible to signal the need of particular individuals or communities to other actors that have the knowledge or expertise in a specific area.
