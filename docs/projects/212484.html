Project Summary

Across Syria, Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) has surged threefold in the past 5 years from 1.7% in 2019 to 4.7% in 2023 - R. Damascus, Idleb, Ar-Raqqa, Quneitra and Latakia surpassed acceptable to stress levels. An estim. 650,000 U5 children are stunted, with stunting rising from 12.6% in 2019 to 16.1% in 2023 and NES reporting catastrophic levels above 30%. Relatedly, below-optimal vaccination coverage & recurring diarrhea episodes, combined with growing food insecurity, currency devaluation & skyrocketing inflation, indicate worsening nutrition situation in the country.
WHO SYRIA is currently supporting integrated nutrition services across all level of the health system, including:
-23 SC’s (2000 admissions/year)
-983 HFs on Nutrition Surveillance (NS) which provided 1M services and detected 27K GAM in 2023
-31 baby friendly hospitals
-760 HCW providing IYCF
In Syria in 2024, there is a need to support centers with new equipment, supplies/therapeutic formula to treat cases in SCs, printing martial for national registry, posters, ICT for data collection, anthropometric tools, in addition to refresher trainings, monitoring visits, annual review meeting, and contract of technical focal points to follow implementation. To that end, WHO SYRIA will:
-Equip SC’s with 24 beds, 24 serum holders, 12 clipboards, 31 heaters, 24 tables, 24 breast feeding corners
-Provide SCs with 8,000 cans of each of F75, F100 and 8,000 Resomal
-Print NS registry, posters, IEC materials
-Procure 12 refrigerators to conserve formula and human milk, 32 computers for data collection
-equip nutrition surveillance centers with 1000 stadiometers,1000 scales,
-conduct 16 refresher trainings for 300 HCWs on SC, IYCF, NS, BFHI to train
-Support 2 annual review meetings
-Contract 3 technical focal points to follow the implementation.
In NWS in 2023, WHO procured medical SAM kits to ensure treatment for complicated cases of SAM, and is planning to continue supplying stabilization centers with these kits to ensure quality clinical management of SAM.
In NWS, WHO will assess the quality of care in stabilization centers for the care of acute malnourished persons with medical complications (SAM+) in NWS, so then WHO will be able to address the needs and gaps and design a comprehensive capacity-building plan for health personnel working in these facilities.
1. WHO will conduct an assessment to evaluate the quality of care provided in Stabilization Centers (SC) for children with acute malnutrition and medical complications (SAM+).
2. Introducing comprehensive capacity-building programs for health personnel in Stabilization Centers.
3. Distribution of SAM+ kits to support the stabilization centers to ensure proper and adapted medication and supplies are available for the care of children.
4. Continued coordination and collaboration with UNICEF aiming to ensure consistency in guidelines and standard operating procedures to avoid duplication or gaps in activities.
WHO utilizes the health sector PRA to ensure protection of the most vulnerable within the delivery of its services, including health care workers themselves. Additionally, both hubs have a dedicated PSEA focal point and have instituted a pillar-based approach to PSEA to ensure awareness AND response capacity within WHO and its implementing partners. These activities are further overseen by regional focal points. WHO also pursues and integrated approach by aligning GBV, PSEA and MHPSS to enable comprehensive response capacity. Finally, both hubs have developed and regularly update a risk matrix with specified mitigation measures. Business continuity plans are updated quarterly and WHO global has also rolled out readiness checklists to ensure offices are ready to respond to emergencies.
