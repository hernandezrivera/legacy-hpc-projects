Project Summary

About 1.2 million children 6-59 months are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition in 2020 in South Sudan based on the results of the Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System. CRS implements CMAM program and treatment of Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) in U5 and PLW identified in Awerial county, Lakes State for almost 5,000 children under 5 and over 2,000 pregnant and lactating women. CRS also provides nutrition programming promoting optimal maternal, infant, and young child feeding practices in the Former Jonglei Counties of Akobo, Bor South, Duk, Pibor, Twic East and Uror. These counties are projected to have 183,00 people in IPC 4 from January-April 2020.

1 - Needs

Conflict and recurrent environmental shocks have deteriorated the Nutrition situation in former Lakes and Jonglei States. For example, recent floods experienced due to heavy rains from July to October this year is already triggering crop failure in parts of these states, with the likely consequence that the harvests will be insufficient to meet individuals’ cereal requirements. Conflict and environmental shocks have also driven displacement. The risk of malnutrition continues to be high among IDPs, especially among children U5, pregnant and lactating women. Most of the IDPs do not have gardens to plant any food crops, and still entirely depending on Humanitarian Assistance. About 1.2 million children 6-59 months in South Sudan are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition in 2020 based on the results of the Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS).

2 - Activities

• Continue to support 5 centers for Targeted Supplementary Feeding (TSFP) and set up 2 additional outreach TSFP sites.• Train staff from the MoH and partner agencies on CMAM and MIYCN. • Conduct active case finding and referral of children < 5 years old and PLWs with acute malnutrition to the nutrition sites.• Provide support to 20 Mother to Mother support groups. • Build capacity and provide support to 12 CNVs for TSFP sites.• Deploy 1 trained MIYCN counselor per project site. • Disseminate information, education and communication (IEC) material and tools for nutrition promotion• Print and distribute nutrition guidelines for community nutrition volunteers• Conduct SBCC (breastfeeding campaign, social mobilization and advocacy) events• Procure and distribute teaching aids for nutrition promotion• Conduct community meetings on improved nutrition practice• Conduct training of trainers (ToT) on MIYCN for Nutrition staff, staff and partners• Conduct MIYCN and SBCC training for community volunteers, lead mothers and male community leaders• Conduct training on multisector linkages with MIYCN targeting project community agents• Promote good nutrition practices at community level through counselling and messaging• Provide facilitation support to mother-to-mother support groups (MTMSG)• Establish new and strengthen existing peer support and action oriented groups• Conduct social marketing research on barriers to adoption of optimal ANC practices• Develop appropriate strategy and SBCC manual on maternal health and nutrition• Print and distribute maternal health and ANC IEC materialsNutrition activities are integrated with WASH and FSL activities throughout CRS's projects. CRS works to rehabilitate clean water sources in areas in which we conduct Nutrition activities and conducts sensitization with parents and caregivers, teachers, and pupils on the importance of WASH practices to optimal Nutrition and Health outcomes. CRS also integrates Nutrition into its FSL activities, training families on the importance of dietary diversity and supporting families and farmers with seeds, tools, trainings, and linkages to markets to grow and sell greater varieties of fruits and vegetables. CRS uses SILC groups - our version of VSLAs - to share Nutrition messages and help families to save funds to purchase foods at the market that they cannot or do not produce themselves - for example meat, milk, and different produce. CRS is also conducting a study in its livestock interventions to see the nutritional impacts of adding goats milk to children's diets.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

CRS has established multiple different feedback mechanisms including suggestions boxes, surveys, and help desks at project sites to ensure that all beneficiaries – including those with mobility issues or low literacy – can give feedback in a way that is most comfortable for and accessible to them. These feedback and complaints mechanisms are placed at the community level, to ensure that people with limited mobility – those with disabilities, PLWs, the elderly, etc – are not excluded from accessing them easily. CRS recognizes that there may be other barriers for giving feedback, for example gender norms in some communities that make it inappropriate or uncomfortable for people of different genders to speak alone with one another. To address this, CRS hires diverse staff - men, women, people of different ages - to ensure that beneficiaries are able to speak to someone with whom they are the most comfortable. In addition, CRS provides information through Nutrition Community Nutrition Volunteers, who share information and accept feedback from community members during activities that already reach vulnerable populations, such as nutrition demonstration sessions at the FDPs and in mother-to-mother support groups. Verbal information-sharing in this way enables beneficiaries who might have lower literacy to be able to share feedback and complaints about the program. Regular visits to project areas are undertaken by diverse CRS staff to monitor and ensure that the handling of feedback and complaints are up to CRS's policy of protection and do no harm.
