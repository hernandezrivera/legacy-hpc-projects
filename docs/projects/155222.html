Project Summary

UNICEF’s nutrition intervention seeks to prevent and treat the severest forms of malnutrition, targeting children under 5 (with emphasis for children under 2), pregnant and lactating women, and adolescent women and girls through enabling preventative and curative, community-based nutrition programming. Key interventions include community-systems strengthening through social mobilization and demand-generation for malnutrition treatment and prevention programming, treatment of Severe Acute Malnutrition through in-patient and outpatient facility-based service delivery, and holistic support for mother, infants, families and communities through promotion of critical life-saving infant and young child feeding and care practices. The intervention promotes specific and sensitive nutrition needs from an integrated protection lens (including Water and Sanitation Hygiene, Health, and Gender) to ensure fulfillment of rights for each stage of the life-cycle for the most vulnerable and marginalized women and children.

Needs

"Generally the Rohingya situation appears to have stabilized and there has been no significant changes in the population, demographics except for the impacts of rains and storms during the monsoon season, which necessitated the relocation of households to new camps. The nutrition sector partners have been able to scale up nutrition interventions throughout all camps to provide the necessary nutrition services. Round 2 SMART surveys conducted in April - May 2018 have revealed significant decline the prevalence of Global acute malnutrition (GAM) in the makeshift camps from 19.3% observed in Round 1 conducted in November-December 2017 to 12.0%. However the range of acute malnutrition prevalence levels still remain on the emergency thresholds border line. In the Nayapara registered camp acute malnutrition prevalence has remained almost unchanged with 14.3% GAM observed in Round 1 to 13.6% GAM recorded in Round 2. Survey results further reveal malnutrition aggravating factors such poor dietary diversity, anemia continue to persist among young children, inappropiate IYCF-E practices, WASH indicating a severe public health problem among the Rohingya population necessitating not only to maintain, but to further scale up and improve quality of nutrition services. Furthermore, though modest gains were observed in the reduction of disease burden among children under five evidenced by the decline in diarrhea incidences, the negative impact of the monsoon season leading to potential elevated incidences of water related infections remains a critical concern. While acute malnutrition treatment was prioritised during the initial phase of response, it is increasingly important to scale up and strengthen preventive services through IYCF since the most recent SMART surveys indicate suboptimal infant and young child feeding practices. Almost 50% children in the makeshift camps and 26% in Nayapara registered camps are not exclusively breastfed. Besides 45% of infants in the makeshift camps and 24% in the Nayapara camp are not timely introduced to breast feeding at birth thus are deprived of colostrum that would contribute to their immunity enhancement.In light of the observed poor dietary diversity, nutrition sensitive measures such as livelihood generation, food security interventions, improved access to WASH and health services for the most poor will necessitate strong multisectoral collaboration to contribute and lead to better nutrition outcomes. Besides strengthening effectiveness and the quality of service provision will be prioritized, requiring hard and valid evidence to be used for a gradual restructuring of the network of nutrition facilities. As well, reconstruction of the nutrition service centers after the monsoon and cyclone seasons remains a key priority."

Activities or outputs

"1. Treatment and management of uncomplicated Severe Acute Malnutrition among Boys and Girls aged 6-59 Months through Out-Patient Therapeutic Care Programmes (OTP) 2. In-Patient treatment and managemet of complicated Severe Acute Malnutrition among Boys and Girls 0-59 Months through Stabilization Centres (SC) 3. Iron Folic Acid supplementation for Adolescent girls aged 10-19 Years for prevention of Iron deficiency anaemia and improvement of birth outcomes during reproductive age (IFA) 4. Promotion of appropriate Infant and Young Child Feeding Practices in Emergencies for caregivers of newborns, infants, and young children age 0-23 Months through establishment and strengthening of new and existing Breastfeeding Support Centers and through community-based IYCF promotion, messaging and quality one-on-one counseling and through caregiver-to-caregiver sessions care group session (IYCF-E) 5. Emphasis on programming holistically designed to promote and improve optimal Complementary Feeding, which includes service delivery that is specific (community gardens and community kitchens) and specific (dietary diversity, handwashing and cooking demonstrations) (CF) 6. Community based Management of Acute Malnutrition for caregivers of newborns and infants 0-6 months (CMAM-I) 7. Support to Government of Bangladesh for response-wide and district-wide mass Vitamin A supplementation among for all children age 6-59 months (Vitamin A) 8. As Cluster Lead Agency (CLA), capacity building, technical assistance, and supportive supervision for all Nutrition Sector implementing partners in the ongoing and overall improvement of nutrition programming, and thereby, overall nutrition outcomes (Sector) 9. Support to Government of Bangladesh for establishing and strengthening data and information managment systems to enable timely, relevant, and quality nutrition programming that is responsive to both immediate and long term needs for both Rohingya and Bangladeshi communities (HMIS) 10. Direct procurement of essential nutrition therapeutic commodities and supplies, and support for capacity strengthening of Government of Bangladesh, such as Ready to Use Therapeutic Food, Therapeutic Milks and Essential Medicines, facility equipments for facility-based prevention and treatment of Severe Acute Malnutrition (Procurement) 11. Community systems-strengthening via outreach, awareness, and sensitization for all community members to promote ownership and utilization of services, which includes active case-finding, referrals, beneficiary household follow up and defaulter/absentee tracing. (CSS)"
