Project Summary


WHO project aims to decrease avoidable under five mortality and morbidity related to Severe acute malnutrition through improved access and quality of inpatient care for the Sever Acute Malnutrition (SAM) with medical complications in 94 stabilization centers Operating in health facilities. The project includes curative, preventive and promotive Measures in the same time. The activities will address the clinical life-saving interventions in Stabilization centers targeting the patients/children with SAM and the mothers/caretakers through IYCF counseling and young children nutrition awareness to lower relapse and prevent SAM cases in the same households. The project activities and costing factored in 49 SCs are supported by UNICEF (11 SCs) and Partners NGOs (38). The SCs Supported by NGOs will require only Medical kits/ secondary level and lab investigation Support, along with IYCF training and counselling, and UNICEF will cover 9% of the Caseload through the 11 SCs, most of them in big towns. Main activities: Training of the health and nutrition staff of 94 SCs (all except the ones Supported by UNICEF and NGOs) to include IPD SAM initial training (7 days) and refresher Training (3 days), provision of guidelines, Job Aids and IEC materials to all participants from the 94 SCs. WHO will procure and distribute to 94 SCs (all except the UNICEF Supported SCs) medicines (SAM inpatient Kits – secondary level care), lab reagents for Investigation essential for case-management, medical equipment and other supplies based on the needs identified together with MOH. To improve the quality of care and cure rate in SCs, WHO nutrition and public health officers present in all targeted states will conduct jointly with SMOH regular supportive supervisions in all targeted 94 SCs conducting on the job training, coaching, and adherence to the national treatment protocols. Routine nutrition surveillance at HF level/SCs, essential for trends and gap identification and effective planning of activities will be enhanced through training, provision of recording and reporting tools for 94 SC (all except UNICEF supported), and better integration into HFs reporting mechanism to promote sustainability. WHO nutrition team will provide technical and operational support to the MOH at federal and state levels to regularly analysis the data of SAM inpatient care services (using the agreed performance indicators) to identify the trends, and challenges dis aggregated by sex and age. The information will be disseminate to all relevant stakeholders through nutrition cluster bulletins, and briefing during the meetings led by UNICEF and MOH. Effective coordination of WHO with all partners (Nutrition, Health, food security) will promote an improved referral system between the SCs, TSFP, OTP, and community. Linkages with communities will be strengthened through raising awareness and social mobilization conducted by trained community mobilizers/CHW and MSG in coordination with other UN. The modalities and subjects for these activities will be guided by the community members suggestions collected through exit interview and focus group discussions during supervisions. The project activities also includes small repairs of 20 SCs in order to ensure better environment for work and improved hygiene in the SCs.

1 - Needs

Sudan continues to have high prevalence of global acute malnutrition (GAM). The results of MICS, 2014 showed the national prevalence of GAM at 16.3%, and SAM at 4.5%, which are above the emergency threshold for intervention (WHO standards). In reference to the HNO 2020 reported that of the 2.7 million children suffering from wasting (GAM) annually in Sudan, as per sector some 471,995 suffer from Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). 11 out of the 18 states have acute malnutrition prevalence above 15% at emergency threshold. The economic challenges faced by Sudan in 2018 further destabilized the food security and nutrition status especially amongst the most vulnerable. The projections for 2019 HRP show that about 78000 children suffering of SAM with medical complications will be in need of in-patient care. Without specialized care these children risk of death is extremely high, target for 2020 set by nutrition sector around 43000 SAM with medical compilations. In spite of some progress in regard with access to, and the coverage of nutrition services over the past years, the assessments for the TFU/SC situation in Sudan result (WHO & MOH 2019) came up with a big gap in regards with access to SAM inpatient care services including capacity, quality of the services/staff qualification, availability of free of charge medicines, routine IYCF counselling for the mothers, and the quality of reporting and monitoring of SCs. In October 2019, 23,420 SAM inpatient children (with medical complications) were treated (FMoH report) in all SCs that represent a coverage of around 54% of the HRP target/expected caseload of 2019. This shows that the geographical coverage of SAM treatment services remains low, especially for the inpatient treatment. The main causes are; unavailability of proper outreach services to early detect the cases, insufficient financial and human resources investment by the Government and Humanitarian Community for scaling up SAM services through integration into selected PHC health facilities and all hospitals; poor access due to insecurity and remoteness in some of the most affected areas; insufficient health staff trained and skilled, and high turnover for SC staff. •Technical support for the State and localities for the management and coordination of SAM inpatient care ensured by the WHO in Khartoum and targeted states. •Procurement and distribution of 860 WHO SAM kits (secondary level care) for 94 SCs (UNICEF SCs and caseload excluded), and equipment for 94 TFUs as requested by MOH and partners at around USD 180/patient. The budgetary calculation excluded the costs of therapeutic milk and RUTF for 94 SCs provided by UNICEF that also covers around 10% of the caseload (11SCs). • Repairs of 20 SCs in 5 Darfurs, WN, BN, 3 Kordofans and 3 Eastern states to improve the infection prevention, and optimization of the work environment •Establishment of 8 Centers of Excellence and Professional training for SAM inpatient care in 3 states initially. •Capacity building for the SCs including staff and procurement• Support the IYCF counseling for the mothers and care taker in all targeted SCs (94)• Joint supportive supervisions with MOH (every 3 months), aiming to improve the adherence to the treatment protocols. •Nutrition surveillance in all states with focus on Eastern, WN, BN, Kordofans and Khartoum states: training, reporting and recording tools, analysis of disaggregated data for dissemination to identify the trends and challenges. •Strengthen the linkages with communities through social mobilization in coordination with other UN agencies and NGOs , referral and treatment. • Contribute to the development of Sudan Multi-sectoral nutrition plans with related sectors (include Regional and HQs support)

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Environmental protection of biological and chemical contamination is part ofaccountability to affected population. Working with patients who mostly suffer fromcommunicable diseases creates real risks for staff, other patients, care=takers,environment and thus the communities. WHO will support and reinforce strict adherenceto Universal Precautions (as per WHO/MOH guidelines) through provision of necessarymaterials such as safety boxes, self-degradable disinfectants, and personal protectionequipment. In collaboration with WASH and Health partners support for availability anduse of incinerators, pits for sharps, hand washing facilities in each SCs/HFs will beprovided to ensure the protection of the staff, patients, care-takers, environment andcommunities. During each supervisory visit the environmental protection will be checkedand the staff will be given orientation during the on job training. The training will includemessage on environmental protection from biological contamination as part of safewaste disposal and improved community level sanitation. Expired medicines will bedestroyed (incineration) as per WHO guidelines and in the presence of health authorities.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

WHO has a protection mainstreaming policy that all staff in the field have been trained in, at field level WHO partners with organizations whose programming is wholly protection focused to ensure that beneficiary protection needs are properly addressed. Equally, there is a country child protection focal person who gives the field child protection focal points remote support, this ensures WHO is equipped to capture and refer appropriately the protection issues to the best-placed protection partners.World Health Organization during the design phase, implementing SAM inpatient programs and activities, protection implies implementing projects in impartial and non-discriminatory ways that promote the safety, dignity and integrity of the SAM children receiving treatment from malnutrition with medical complications. The principles of protection mainstreaming will be incorporated during the Monitoring and Evaluation. Assessment of the project incorporates the key elements of Protection Mainstreaming, which include Avoiding-causing harm and prioritize safety and dignity, Ensuring meaningful access, Practicing accountability and Promoting participation and empowerment of the stabilization center staff and beneficiaries.A confidential complaints and protection reporting mechanism system is in place and beneficiaries know where to refer or report incidents of rights violations. Feedback on the complaints should be regularly be provided to the beneficiaries.
