Project Summary

The proposed intervention seeks to improve access to safe water and sanitation facilities and promote good hygiene practices in an effort to improve the health status of 24,100 people in Puntland, Galmudug and Hiraan regions. This will be done through the rehabilitation of solar boreholes, the extension of pipelines and water tank construction for communities and health facilities to improve access and storage, installation of desalination machines to purify water, rehabilitation of shallow wells, and provision of temporary access to water through voucher. The intervention will also promote household water treatment and safe storage (HWTs) to improve the quality of drinking water and reduce water-borne diseases (using cost-effective and context-appropriate treatment technologies such as filters and disinfectants).
The IRC also proposes to construct/rehabilitate latrines with handwashing stations in IDPs and health facilities to improve safe excreta disposal, hygiene and sanitation promotion, distribution of basic hygiene kits including menstrual management materials, promoting solid waste disposal, and training hygiene promotion volunteers to support the effective implementation of hygiene promotion activities and best use of WASH facilities.
WASH services are at risk of being disrupted by the COVID-19 outbreak; the proposed intervention will address the immediate need to mitigate the spread of COVID-19 and the public health consequences of the pandemic in target locations including Infection Prevention and Control (IPC), and focus on opportunities to strengthen recovery especially supporting local communities/actors with the tools and resources needed to maintain critical WASH services in the longer term
The proposed intervention will also take into consideration gender concerns by ensuring implementation of activities are delivered in non- discriminatory ways that promote the safety, dignity and integrity of affected men, women, boys , girls and people with disabilities. The project will be designed in such a way that the water points and latrines are constructed close to settlement areas thus reducing the distance travelled by women and children (especially girls). The project will consult marginalized groups including women and people with disabilities on site selection, establishing water management structures and will prioritize inclusion of women (60%) in water- user committees to strengthen their participation in water related decision-making. The project will ensure all the latrines to be constructed in IDP camps and settlements are gender- segregated, lockable from inside and have adequate lighting. The latrines will be equipped with assistive devices such as toilet chairs and ramp to increase access for people with disability to ensure the WASH programming contribute to greater coverage and gender equality in terms of access and use of WASH facilities and services.

Project Activities

2.7 million people in Somalia need support to access basic WASH assistance and this remains an important feature of the humanitarian landscape in the country. Inadequate access to WASH services is a leading cause of acute diarrheal diseases (ADD) and malnutrition. In 2020, a total of 5,485 AWD/cholera cases were reported across Somalia with more outbreaks anticipated in the rainy season (September – December 2020). South-central Somalia, especially the Hiraan region where heavy floods destroyed crops and water systems, still remains a hotspot for flood which will likely trigger displacement and spikes in AWD/cholera cases. Poor WASH services and lack of durable solutions expose people in Somalia to all categories of WASH-related public health threats. COVID-19 impacted health and the economy with limited resources available to fight the pandemic thus making WASH support more important than ever. Consequently, 49% of Somalis do not have access to regular and improved water sources. This undermines households to meet their basic needs for drinking and domestic use. Reports from Puntland and Galmudug suggest 40% of the existing water sources are non-functional due to high operation and maintenance costs. In addition, community structures necessary to manage the water points in a sustainable manner are either weak or non-existent. For many years, WASH in health institutions remained fragile where a significant percentage of the health facilities in Somalia do not have water connection on-premises and largely depend on water trucking. This greatly compromises service provision and undermines safe WASH practices which are important in controlling and stopping disease outbreaks. Open defecation rates in Somalia are some of the highest in the world and handwashing practices with soap are very low (62%) coupled by poor knowledge of good hygiene and sanitation practices, increasing diseases, COVID19 transmission, poor sanitation and lack of hygiene among malnourished and children under five years. An assessment conducted by the IRC in September, 2020 further indicates, 37% of people in Puntland and Galmudug prefer drinking from unprotected water sources due to dissatisfaction with borehole water whose salinity level is high. This has significant protection implications for women and girls who travel long distances to collect water putting them at risk to rape. People also buy water from local vendors at a prohibitive cost (0.2 USD per 20liter jerrican) and often carried or stored in unhygienic containers with little value placed on the importance of drinking water. Women and girls especially those that live in IDPs and in rural settings lack access to sanitary materials and facilities to manage their hygiene issues in a dignified manner. Mitigation Measures

1. Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services.• The IRC will ensure the location and access routes to WASH facilities and distribution points are safe while identifying central locations as distribution points to ensure beneficiaries do not traverse long distances to access the services. • The IRC will ensure distributions to take place in daylight hours to give beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall and information regarding frequency and mode of distribution will be communicated in advance. The IRC will consider door-to-door distribution where possible to alleviate the burden on beneficiaries and protect women and girls from violence. • The IRC will design and construct WASH facilities to preserve the safety and dignity of its users. The latrines will be gender-separated and further marked in pictorial form for illiterate users and will work with the community to ensure they are used by the indicated sex. • The IRC will ensure the hygiene kit size is appropriate for beneficiaries to be able to carry home with no physical or financial burden and without being dependent on others to carry for them. 2. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities• The IRC will consult women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, older persons and marginalized persons by collecting accurate information about their specific WASH requirements and preferences for location and design of WASH facilities and will discuss topics including privacy, using of lockable doors and walls to provide safe cover access for persons with disabilities. • The IRC will engage the community and committee representatives including women and persons with disabilities to play an active role in identifying solutions and the decision-making processes that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response. • The IRC considers women and children often collect water for household use thus plans to talk to them directly about the safety of the location and routs to the water points.• The IRC will build community capacities to maintain WASH structures and ensure sustainable provision of WASH services e.g. by establishing WASH committees and will coordinate with civil societies specialized in working with persons with impaired mobility or disabilities to help identify such individuals and use them as a resource to improve service delivery. 3. Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources• The IRC Will be transparent with the affected populations by providing them with accessible and timely information on selection criteria for targeted assistance, organizational procedures, and processes that affect them.• The IRC will provide clear information to the distribution actors and affected population explaining that beneficiaries do not have to pay or provide services/favors to anyone in exchange for receiving WASH assistance and will ensure this information is communicated to women and girls. • The IRC will ensure assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable and will identify with affected men and women the most vulnerable groups in the community to prevent discrimination or exclusion.• The IRC will identify the power dynamics in the area, who has access to water sources and who is able to influence decisions on WASH intervention and will use this information to inform the monitoring activities and identify barriers to access, discrimination and whether assistance is being diverted.

Please provide further explanation

The proposed intervention will ensure community involvement and participation at all stages, which will promote ownership and in turn ensure sustainability of the project. The program has been built on the principles of do-no harm and activities are based on the needs of the target communities and planned without discrimination. Service providers are expected to provide care to individuals based on their needs. For example; all services are provided free of charge and the trained WASH committees will continue to sustain rehabilitated water sources and hygiene promotion after IRC exit. So all users will have equal access to services. Locally available materials and designs suited to the local context will be used in order to ensure ease in running and maintaining the systems. In determining the specific equipment to be installed, including spare parts, the IRC will look at availability locally to ensure community can access the replacement parts easily. All the WASH activities are designed to support or substitute for existing services, and are not expected to create new impacts on the environment. IRC will closely plan and monitor activities to ensure negative environmental impact is mitigated. To gain meaningful participation, the IRC will consult with women and men, boys and girls and people with disabilities in the planning, implementation and monitoring of WASH activities. In case some items to be distributed by IRC are unfamiliar, IRC will ensure that the beneficiaries have access to information and training on the safe use of those items. Information on the timing, location, content and target groups for the hygiene kits distribution will be made available to the affected population. Furthermore, IRC’s staff, volunteers and incentive workers will hold regular meetings with targeted beneficiaries to get feedback and ensure that the project activities remain effective and relevant. In addition, IRC will also ensure that all data collected during surveys, assessments, evaluation and regular reporting is disaggregated by age, sex and residency status where applicable to monitor that IRC’s services are equally responsive to the needs of different groups.
