Project Summary

The project will take place in Sudan, more precisely in the States of Red Sea, Khartoum and South Darfur, and it aims at providing access to paediatric primary and secondary health care services, intended as both curative and preventive medical treatments. In addition, a referral service will be provided to children under 14 years old and patients in need of higher-level health support. Concerning the health assistance for mothers, a specific support will be addressed to women in reproductive age living in the camp of Mayo, (Jabel Awlia locality, Khartoum).
At the same time, the intervention gives priority to the teaching aspect, i.e. health education and promotion for both paediatric patients and parents, carers generically. Moreover, this preventive activities will be integrated, in line with EMERGENCY mission and principles, with training for national health and non-health workers.
The whole action will be structured by implementing activities in the EMERGENCY paediatric centres located in the cities of Port Sudan, Mayo and Nyala. More specifically the project’s purpose will be pursued in these areas by implementing or strengthening activities in the following areas of intervention:
1) Paediatric health care, through the rehabilitation and reactivation at 100% capacity of EMERGENCY Paediatric Centre in Nyala, the most important city in South Darfur State and one of the few health access for the neighbouring IDP camps. The provision of essential free-of-charge health care services to children under 14 years old will be granted in Nyala, Port Sudan and Mayo areas by implementing medical activities but also by ensuring drugs, medical supplies, equipment and diagnostic tools. The Reinforcement of the referral system addressed to patients requiring a higher-level of assistance, together with the strengthening of the outreach activities will guarantee a wider level of support to people living in less accessible areas;
2) Maternal health care, addressed to women in reproductive age living in Mayo camp, will be ensured by the provision of a comprehensive set of reproductive health services such as ante/post-natal care, family planning and constant medical monitoring;
3) Promotion and transmission of knowledge, granted by arising Sudanese population’s awareness on good practice of hygiene, health, nutrition and detection of uncommunicable diseases, ensuring also a preventive approach. While, on the other hand by improving national health and non-health workforce’s capacities at all levels, in line with international standard. EMERGENCY will increase workers’ skills by training them on the correct procedures of treatments and early warning detection of medical diseases.
The present project is tailored on Sudanese paediatric population’s needs with a comprehensive approach. More clearly, by ensuring access to medical and preventive health support to paediatric patients but also to mothers and carers in general, which represent the reference point for children. Finally, the project is designed to guarantee the widest possible area of intervention by strengthening the outreach activities and the transmission of professional capacities to national workers.

Needs

In line with 2018 Humanitarian Needs Overview, Sudan in still one of the country in which the humanitarian assistance is more urgently needed. More specifically, Sudan faces two major overlapping humanitarian challenges: large-scale population displacement as a consequence of recent conflict, food insecurity and malnutrition due to climatic and socio-cultural conditions. Together they are causing the overall 5.2 million people who are currently in need of health assistance and in particular, the 2.3 million children suffering from Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and the 820,000 under-five children needing access to healthcare, including immunization and essential lifesaving services. Nowadays only a third of the population has an adequate number of midwives providing reproductive health services, in addition some 208,391 Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) are undernourished annually, and 159,935 PLW need promotive counselling on optimal infant feeding and care practices (HNO 2018). Sudan is characterized by a very low contraceptive prevalence, resulting in a high fertility rate (4.85 births per woman in 2018) and, as a consequence, an alarming maternal mortality ratio (311 maternal deaths per 100,000 live births, WB 2017). About 36 per cent of the Primary Health Care (PHC) facilities across Sudan are not fully functional, either due to staff shortages or poor physical infrastructure. Only 24 per cent of functional health facilities offer all main service components of the PHC package, 42 per cent in Darfur is due to NGO support. Moreover, since the beginning of 2018, the Sudanese government has intensified its austerity measures, which have led to a drastic drop in local purchasing capacity, with an increase in the prices of basic goods (food, drugs and consumables) and a steady increase in the prices of imported products. The impact of these measures has repercussions on the ability of the Sudanese healthcare system to provide health services to the population, on the availability of drugs and necessary equipment in public health facilities. According to HNO 2018, shortage of staff, drugs and medical supplies results in poor quality service delivery. Finally, the salaries of local staff have drastically lost their purchasing capacity. It is worth to mention that in past years the Sudanese health workers have been constantly attracted by professional opportunities abroad (where they can get higher salaries compared to Sudan) and from the private healthcare system in Sudan. The further loss of purchasing power in the local markets has exacerbated the propensity to emigrate, contributing to the shortage of skilled health workers in Sudan.Under EMERGENCY supervision, a three-year need assessment is under implementation within Mayo camp, an informal camp not covered by international surveys and data collections, in order to obtain a clearer humanitarian needs overview.

Activities

In constant coordination with MoH and national Authorities, EMERGENCY will take into consideration three areas of intervention, in order to cover a wider and more comprehensive set of needs. Periodical meeting will be regularly attended with all the national and international stakeholders involved in the Action. With reference to paediatric health assistance addressed to 0 – 14 children: • Rehabilitation works and reopening of EMERGENCY’s Paediatric Centre in Nyala;• Free of charge curative paediatric healthcare services to 0 - 14 years old children in EMERGENCY’s Paediatric Centres, i.e. triage, emergency room, stabilization and management of critical children, outpatients’ consultations and follow up, observation ward (in Mayo) and hospitalization for urgent medical diseases (in Nyala and in Port Sudan), provision of drugs, laboratory investigations, patients ambulance referral to other health facilities;• Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI) in clinic and in outreach;• Malnutrition screening to 0 – 14 years old children in clinic and outreach. Concerning the specific action addressed to women EMERGENCY will implement: • ANC (Antenatal Care), PNC (Postnatal Care), FP (Family Planning), laboratory services for pregnant women, TT vaccination, ambulance referral for emergency obstetric care and follow up at hospital level in Mayo; • Sexual Reproductive Health (SRH) counselling and health education in weekly outreach sessions; Finally, as regards to the teaching and transmission of knowledge aspect, EMERGENCY will implement the following activities: • On the job training programme and theoretical lessons for national doctors, nurses, midwives (in Mayo), pharmacists, laboratory technicians and Community Health Promoters by EMERGENCY experienced international medical staff;• Cooking lessons with awareness activities on maternal and child nutrition in Mayo;• Health promotion activities on hygiene, health and nutrition topics in clinic and in outreach.
