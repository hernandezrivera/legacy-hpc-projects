Project Summary

Syria is entering its 10th year of devastating conflict with the situation in the northern provinces worsening with a disrupted health system and a third of its facilities not fully functional. In addition, the COVID19 pandemic has further amplified the dire needs in the health sector while acting as a risk for the continuation of essential healthcare. Additionally, in the camps, COVID19 had increased the vulnerability with 97% of the individuals in extreme poverty (SPRING 2021 REPORT SERIES - governate profiles).
THE OBJECTIVES:
- Decease the morbidity and mortality among the highest risk and vulnerable groups in the NWS.
THE PROJECTS OUTPUTS:
(1) Provision of blood bank service including donor recruiting and distribution.
(2) Provision of primary healthcare services.
(3) Provision of secondary healthcare services; surgical orthopaedic hospital
(4) provision of community COVID19 treatment services
BENEFICIARIES:
This project plans to reach about 2.5 Million indirect beneficiaries (mostly from the high priority areas in Idleb and Aleppo). At least 40% out of direct beneficiaries will be women from the age of 20-50 and 7- 11% will be people with disability. These indirect beneficiaries will be reached by supporting the primary healthcare, secondary health care, and supplying blood products to fill the gaps for the general hospitals, trauma hospital, maternal hospital, Thalassemia-NCD.
THE OUTCOMES:
(1) direct addressing of one of the top cause of death in the NWS. shortage of blood is number one of death for maternal death, one of the top 5 causes in trauma patients death.
(2) decrease untested blood transfusion done in the emergency because of the lack of central testing and availability of blood unit in the hospitals, thus decrease the transfusion-transmitted infection (TTI) rate bearing in mind the increasing of HBV, HBC infection rate (based on WATAN lab service)
(3) increase the capacity of the existing blood banks by 70% to fulfil the gaps in Idleb.
(4) decrease the disability by providing specialized orthopaedic surgical care that will reduce the duration of limb fixation and provision of limb-saving surgery.
(5) compliment the essential primary healthcare services in Azaz and Afrin including RH, Dental, child care, community outreach.
(6) increase the preparedness and responsiveness to the COVID19 pandemic in the NWS.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk Analysis (PRA): • Protection risk 1: There is a risk of gender-based violence among the affected population which can become clear during the medical consultations. The rate is anticipated to be high in camp communities (10 camps are near the hospital) due to the overcrowding and lack of personal space.Likelihood: medium. Impact: HighMitigation: 1) increase the awareness and understanding of GBV case management among the medical staff through GBV training. 2) Ensure that social workers in the hospital know how to manage cases of GBV and how to link it to more specialized protection services. 3) Violence cases must be reported to the hospital manager with respect to the confidentiality of the personal information of the patient. A reporting mechanism between the hospital management and staff and WATAN will be established, to report on any occurring cases that indicate further protection needs. • Protection risk 2: There is a risk of a lack of gender awareness among the health staff in the hospital and the hospital not being a gender-sensitive and family-/women-friendly workplace which in turn can affect the retention of female staff and hence lead to not being able to ensure beneficiaries' culturally appropriate examination by female practitioners or under the presence of female nurses.Likelihood: medium. Impact: HighMitigation: 1) Ensure gender balance among the newly hired staff. 2) ensure separate rooms for the female night shift health workers are in place

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk of security incidences: Mitigation: 1) security team to keep updating and tracking any security threat in the area and deconflict the locations. 2) develop an evacuation plan which has the following steps: STEP1: To transfer the patients to the nearest hospital in the area, STEP2: to relocate the medical staff and non-medical staff to the hospital, to where the patients were transferred. STEP3: to transship the medical/non-medical assets to WATAN central warehouse Aleppo. Risk of loss of assets and medical supplies: the risk owner: the facility manager in each siteMitigation: Monthly inventors, daily consumption tracking for the medical supplies, segregation of the duties in the medical supply management (between the logistic team and program team). The SOP of pharma management is attached. Risk of COVID19 increasing cases: Mitigation: (1) full implementation of the standard measure in the health facilities as recommended by the health cluster (disinfecting the facilities, triage, PPE, making in-advance appointments). (2) prepare the telehealth solution (another project for WATAN) to overcome the restriction of movement. (3) having always 2 months of PPE stock to overcome any shortages. (4) for the hospital, reassign the oxygen generator of WATAN to be the backup line of oxygen supply to overcome any shortage in oxygen supplies in the ORs. Risk of transfusion-transmitted infection TTI (esp for blood banks): Mitigation: (1) 100% lab testing for all blood units (2) tracking system for all units to establish a recall mechanism and feedback mechanism (3) pilot adding a pre-screening for the blood donors. Risk of medical malpractice/ errors: Mitigation: (1) having a digital medical record for each visit.
