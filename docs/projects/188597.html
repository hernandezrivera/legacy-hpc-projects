Project Summary

Overall, the Food Security Sector (FSS) estimates that 12 million people in Syria are facing acute food insecurity, which is at least 55.3% of Syria population. While the food insecure figures have slightly declined by at least 4% compared to last year’s record increase, the number of people facing acute food insecurity remains extremely high and 51% higher than in 2019, pre-Lebanese financial crisis period and before the COVID-19 outbreak. Lack of access to livelihood opportunities is a major challenge that most rural households are facing and one of contributing factors that is leading most families to leave their place of residence in search of livelihood opportunities. In this context, FAO has designed a project to assist 172,000 crisis-affected households (equivalent to 1,032,000 people) in Syria, through support to diversify their livelihoods and income sources and improve their food and nutrition security. This will be achieved through supporting vegetable and poultry production in Syria.
The overall objective of the proposed action is to improve the food security and nutrition of vulnerable crisis-affected households and the project seeks to deliver the following outputs:
Output 1: Increased household food production (family farming) for 100,000 households.
The output aims to increase vegetable/poultry production of 100,000 vulnerable households (of which 30 % are women-headed) through the provision of full vegetable production packages, which include vegetable seeds/seedlings, and organic fertilizers, or through provision of egg laying hens with feed and vitamins.
Output 2: Increased income generating opportunities through agriculture related livelihood support. The market-driven income generating opportunities include examples such as mushroom production or beekeeping, food processing, etc. for 72,000 households.
This output aims to support 72,000 vulnerable households (of which 30 % are women-headed) to generate income from agriculture related livelihood opportunities
It is expected that the project support through provision of vegetable and poultry inputs, income generating opportunities and associated training such as business skills and marketing, will diversify the diets of the targeted households and also increase household income and employment creation through the sale of products, for the most vulnerable members of the households, especially for women and youth. It is expected this will lead to improvement of livelihoods, increased local availability of food, increased self-reliance and improved food security and nutrition of crisis-affected households, especially the most vulnerable.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Risk: Beneficiaries being targeted as a result of being selected for project/Risk of community tension arising from beneficiaries selection. Mitigation measures: Inclusion of different categories of affected population (for example IDPs and host communities; returnees and refugees; men, women, boys, girls and youth) based on their specific needs and clear selection criteria agreed upon by local committees.2) Risk: Inclusion and exclusion errors. Mitigation measures: Establishing a local beneficiary selection committee with equitable representation of vulnerable groups; putting in place a clear and precise guidance on beneficiary selection criteria and process; and ensuring effective communication for guidance in local language.3) Risk of diversion of assistance/assets. Mitigation measures: Establish community-based beneficiary selection and community-based monitoring. The design of the project has taken into account protection elements, in line with the do no harm principles. To the maximum possible extent, all measures will be taken to limit the risk of beneficiaries being targeted as a result of the being selected for the project. Where IDPs are selected, representatives from the host communities will be included as well, to limit possible frictions between the two groups, and special attention will be provided to returnees to help them in their re-settlement and resuming their production activities through protecting and rehabilitating their assets.
