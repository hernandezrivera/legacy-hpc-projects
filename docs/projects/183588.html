Project Summary

The already challenged healthcare system in the oPt experiences added strain from the COVID-19 outbreak since 2020, combined with the recurring conflict. The reallocation of scarce resources has undermined people’s access to health. Direct mortality from the virus and indirect mortality from preventable diseases continue to be a major concern for 2022.
The Health Cluster estimates that more than 1.5 million people, including refugees, across the occupied Palestinian territory (oPt) will need humanitarian health interventions in 2022, two-thirds of them are in Gaza and one third in the West Bank. Of those in need, around 47% are female and children constitute almost half (47%) of the total.
Through this project, WHO will support the Palestinian Ministry of Health COVID-19 response, and management of other outbreak-related communicable diseases. At least 400K suspected cases will have access to COVID-19 testing; 500 healthcare workers will benefit from capacity building activities on laboratory testing, case management, infection prevention and control, and surveillance. Approx. 1M people will also benefit from risk communication and community engagement (RCCE) messaging. Around 10K will benefit from procurement of essentially needed items.
Planned Trauma and emergency activities will include all levels of trauma care, starting from standardizing national triage guidelines; upgrading the emergency departments and surgical wards of 7 hospitals in Gaza; upgrading emergency departments in 2 West Bank hospitals, and one hospital in East Jerusalem; in addition to supporting the Limb Reconstruction Center. More than 100K patients are expected to benefit from the proposed activities.
With contribution from 12 partner agencies, WHO will continue to document and monitor barriers to healthcare services access, including for patients, companions and health workers requiring permits to reach essential healthcare services, as a basis for evidence-based advocacy. Additionally, around 100 partners will benefit from the implementation of the Health Resources and Services Availability Monitoring System (HeRAMS).
WHO's project has been coordinated at various levels with all counterparts involved in the activities:
(1) COVID-19 response with the MoH and through the Humanitarian Response Plan,
(2) Trauma activities - with the MoH and Health cluster Trauma WG,
(3) Right to Health - with the MoH, counterparts, and partners of the Advocacy WG.

1 - Needs

According to the Health Cluster, more than 1.5 million Palestinians, including 735K females, across the oPt have been identified as requiring health-related humanitarian assistance in 2022, two-thirds of them in the Gaza Strip and one third in the West Bank. Adequate response requires promoting and ensuring equitable access for all vulnerable groups, including elderly, women and people with disability, to essential healthcare service. According to the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview, the COVID-19 pandemic has rendered an estimated 403,676 people in need of COVID-19 health humanitarian assistance, including around 198K females. The demand to scale-up laboratory testing, surveillance, contact tracing, isolation, and case management is increasing rapidly. The Ministry of Health (MoH) estimates that at least 5,000 tests are needed daily across the oPt, rising to a minimum of 8,000 tests per day during wave’s peak. In Gaza, during the peak of the third wave in September 2021, COVID-19 bed occupancy reached up to 75%, with ICU occupancy as high as 67%, according to MoH reports. This requires scaling up the health system’s capacity to early detect and manage COVID-19 cases, through the provision of critically needed supplies, including laboratory items, drugs, medical supplies and equipment; in addition to building the capacity of healthcare workers to adequately respond to the outbreak. COVID-19 awareness also remains inadequate despite the significant progress achieved (KAP Survey, June 2021). Supporting IHR core capacities is also critical to prevent, mitigate, and respond to potential future outbreaks. This includes strengthening coordination, laboratory capacity, epidemiological surveillance, including through introducing event-based surveillance, and supporting surveillance at points of entry, and improving infection prevention and control measures. Strengthening the health information system is also vital for accurate and reliable reporting on communicable diseases, allowing for a timelier response. On another note, the recent escalations resulted in an increased demand for trauma and emergency care focusing on all 3 levels of care, including emergency and pre-hospital medical services, hospital level, and postoperative and rehabilitative care. All three elements of healthcare need to be available in order for the trauma pathway to be effective. The importance of emergency preparedness and pre-stocking of supplies became evident following the hostilities on the Gaza Strip and the clashes in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, that took place between 10 and 21 May 2021 (WHO Rapid Needs Assessment, May 2021). During this escalation, over 250 Palestinians were killed and 2000 were injured, as per MoH reports. At least 370 patients are still in need of limb reconstruction surgeries, including 70 females, and more than 600 need rehabilitation, according to the 2022 HNO. To facilitate the needs, WHO plans to expand the Limb Reconstruction Center (LRC) into an advanced Multidisciplinary LRC with an educational capacity for other levels of services. This would contribute towards enhancing the quality of life for a vulnerable group of the population, including people with longer-term disabilities. Besides, no formal triage exists in prehospital settings and in the Emergency Departments of the hospitals. The absence of a standardized triage method leads to inefficient use of resources and delays in handling urgent cases. The adoption of the triage system on a daily basis will increase the resilience and provide sustainable support to the health system that is not only a protracted crisis but also now imposed with additional burden by the pandemic. In responding to the different acute emergencies, limited resources were redirected from essential healthcare services. Chronic shortages in drugs and supplies continue to burden the Gazan health system. In August, MoH Central Stores reported that 38% of essential drugs, 32% of essential consumables were at less than one month’s supply. Around 54% of the essential laboratory items were also at zero-stock level in September, according to the Central Lab Stores' report. Patients with non-communicable diseases have been impacted by the pandemic and the chronic shortages of drugs. The HNO 2022 estimates needs amongst about 110,000 people with non-communicable diseases, out of which 50K are above 60 years of old, and some of these in need of palliative care, especially those with cancer, diabetes, and cardiovascular disease. Palestinian patients and companions needed over 250,000 permits to reach essential health services in 2019, most for services within the oPt. Around 60,000 were delayed or denied: over 21,500 in Gaza and over 38,000 in the West Bank. In 2020, COVID-19 and the end of coordination between the PA and Israel have exacerbated barriers to health access and the need for effective monitoring and advocacy. Protection gaps adversely affect healthcare, with 197 attacks against healthcare, including the killing of a health worker in 2021 so far. While the number has reduced structural needs for protection and prevention of impunity remain. Health inequities have also been exacerbated by COVID-19: in Gaza, referrals remain half of the pre-COVID-19 levels, essential medicines are disproportionately depleted, and health staff receives lower salaries. Strengthening equity monitoring and advocacy is needed to reduce humanitarian needs.

2 - Activities

This project is fully aligned with the Health Cluster HNO and HRP for 2022 and will focus on three interlinked areas. WHO aims to: 1) Support the PA MoH in the containment of the COVID-19 outbreak; 2) Support maintaining the availability, accessibility, acceptability, and quality of essential lifesaving health services to vulnerable communities across oPt that were affected by the COVID-19 pandemic to achieve the universal health coverage and 3) Ensure the right of all Palestinian to health. The activities in this project are in line with the Health cluster interventions under Strategic Cluster Objectives 1, 2 and 3. Proposed activities will contribute to an equitable access to essential services for all vulnerable groups, including women people with disability, across the different age groups. To support the MoH’s efforts in containing the COVID-19 outbreak and enhance response to potential future outbreaks, WHO will strengthen the IHR core capacities of the oPt, as well as support the lifesaving interventions in the following areas of the COVID-19 response: 1. Strengthen coordination, Information, and Monitoring, 2. Scale-up COVID-19 laboratory capacity, 3. Scale-up capacity of the MoH in case management, 4. Scale-up case finding, contact tracing, and isolation, 5. Strengthen surveillance, 6. Accelerate Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE), 7. Infection Prevention & Control (IPC), 8. Operation support and logistics, 9. Support maintaining the essential health system, and 10. Support vaccines uptake. WHO estimates that more than 1M Palestinians, including 50K person with disability and 490K females, will benefit from the following interventions:1) Provision of COVID-19 testing kits and supplies, including Ag-RDT, PCR, virus mutation tests, swabs and media, and testing equipment 2) Training of 50 doctors and 100 nurses on Basic ICU and COVID-19 case management3) Provision of essential medical supplies, lab reagents and medical equipment for COVID-19 case management and vaccination3) Provision of critical medical equipment, including regular hospital beds, to MoH hospitals that deal with emergency cases, including trauma and COVID-194) Establishing two medical points at Gaza points of entry (Erez and Rafah) for response to disease outbreaks5) Capacity building of personnel working at the established medical points6) Establishing two satellite Public Health Emergency Operation Centers (PHEOC) in North and South Gaza districts7) Capacity building of PHEOC workers8) Sustaining and expanding HeRAMS, including the provision of hardware, capacity building of data collectors and decision makers on its use9) Improving the e-health information system in hospitals and primary health centers, through the provision of hardware, software, and capacity building10) Strengthening surveillance, including introducing event-based surveillance11) Training health workers on communicable disease management, IPC, and surveillance12) Training lab staff on biosafety and biosecurity13) Support RCCE activities To support the management of patients with NCDs, WHO will procure medical supplies and provide training to healthcare workers. The Trauma and Emergency Care Programme (T&E) will contribute to the emergency preparedness and response across all 3 levels of the trauma pathway (pre-hospital level, hospital level, and postoperative and rehabilitative care). More than 100K patients, including 49K females and 5K people with disabilities, are expected to benefit from the below activities: 1) Develop a National Triage system and National Emergency Medicine protocols.2) Upgrade the Emergency Departments of 7 Hospitals across Gaza through procurement of identified needs, and by training 50 health workers on Basic Surgical skills, Basic Emergency Care, WHO Trauma Checklist, WHO inter-agency Triage tool and Mass Casualty Management3) Upgrade the surgical wards and operation theatres of 7 Hospitals across Gaza through procurement of identified needs, and provision of specialized training for 42 health workers re the adoption of the WHO surgical care checklist . 4) Upgrade the Emergency departments of 2 Hospitals in WB, by training 24 health workers on MCM and the adoption of WHO Trauma checklist. Complement the training with procurement of necessary equipment and supplies.5) Upgrade the Emergency Department of 1 Hospital in East Jerusalem by training 12 health workers on MCM and the adoption of WHO Trauma checklist. Complement the training with procurement of necessary equipment and supplies. In addition, WHO will support effective coordination and capacity building of partners to strengthen evidence-based and maintain access to essential health services to protect the right to health in the oPt. WHO will:1) Monitor, document, and report barriers to health care access, including for patients, companions, health care workers and ambulances.2) Monitor, document, and report attacks against health care within WHO’s Surveillance System for Attacks on Health Care (SSA).3) Undertake capacity building activities, including training and workshops, to strengthen health care worker understanding of barriers to health access, to strengthen a human rights-based approach to health care provision and advocacy for the right to health.4) Undertake advocacy including through production of public advocacy materials, reporting through UN fora, and bilateral and multilateral briefings to strengthen respect and protection of the right to health for Palestinians in the oPt.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The centrality of protection is at the heart of WHO emergency response as mandated by the IASC. WHO works to ensure the prevention of harm to all persons it engages with through activities, including through upholding due processes of consent, the protection of confidentiality and the proper collection, communication, storage, dissemination and disposal of information on affected persons. Additionally, activities supported by WHO will contribute to ensuring that beneficiaries are not exposed to further contamination risks through adherence to IPC. WHO will also ensure that all interventions are context-appropriate and culturally sensitive. Affected people will be consulted on this where and when appropriate. The prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse has also been integrated across the WHO-supported projects through mandatory training of all staff on PSEA and seeking feedback from the affected people, which will be acted upon. WHO’s response team ensures that PSEA is integrated into the response from an early stage, to build the capacity of national PSEA focal points, and to ensure continuity of activities on the ground despite access constraints. Based on lessons learned from the independent report of the abuse perpetrated in the DRC, WHO oPt will organize a training session for staff, and has rolled out the inter-agency Code of Conduct in alignment with the national, and sub-national PSEA network and encouraged all staff and personnel to sign. WHO will participate in the GBV sub-cluster as well as the national and sub-national PSEA networks through a designated focal point. Currently, plans are under way to roll out more trainings, work on prevention, and to strengthen inter-agency feedback and reporting mechanisms, as well as case management and response services through linkages with GBV actors. In harmonization with the SOPs and ground work laid by the national and sub-national PSEA network to identify and mitigate PSEA risks, WHO is planning an agency specific risk assessment that is complimentary and identifies the specific risks associated with WHO’s scope of work, and guides a plan that is fully integrated within the IASC PSEA strategy and plan for oPt.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project activities will clearly focus on the provision of life-saving health interventions to the most vulnerable, including women, the elderly and people with disabilities (PWD) based on the latest available impartial assessments. PWD will be considered as one of the main targeted vulnerable groups in the proposed project. All indicators will be gender-age disaggregated where possible. WHO will continue to work to strengthen the empowerment of affected persons, including through the provisionof platforms to promote patient voices as well as capacity building with health service providers to promote participatory mechanisms within health services design and delivery. WHO also works with the Ministry of Health and partners to strengthen monitoring and analysis of equity and barriers to health access, focused on vulnerable groups and protected characteristics.
