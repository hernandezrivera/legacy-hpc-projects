Project Summary

CARE International planned to provide emergency food assistance using CVA modality, complemented with emergency food production and livelihoods support through provision of vegetable and fishery kits together with resilience building interventions to reach an estimated 53,900 households (32,340 female & 21,560 male), which in turn reaches 323,400 people.
The FSLC Strategy offers a clear vision and guiding principles to effectively plan, coordinate, and monitor responses to food security needs. It encompasses both the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) and Rapid Response mechanisms for sudden shocks, ensuring that interventions are timely, well-coordinated, and directed toward the most critical needs. By integrating resilience-building and sustainability into its approach, the FSLC strategy not only addresses immediate emergencies but also strengthens long-term food security outcomes. The FSLC Strategy will improve food security situation in 2026 by optimizing the response and enhancing sustainability through coordination and advocacy.
As resources are limited and FSLC partners cannot meet all needs, so rather than scattering our forces, the impact of FSL cluster interventions will be greater if we combine and coordinate complementary interventions. Integration of COI/CO2/ C03 activities will achieve the desired strategic long-term solutions to resilience building within vulnerable households rather than distribution of Food and agricultural inputs alone. The impact will be much greater if complimentary interventions are combined through appropriate response packages as well as increased local capacity that will enhance food security. To reduce the number of people in need of humanitarian assistance by 2026, the FSLC will ensure that CO1, CO2 and CO3 interventions are closely coordinated in the same location and for the same beneficiaries.
Accordingly, this project targeted to reach 25% of the target with CVA, 70% with vegetable kits ,30% with fishery kits and of all 20% will be reached with resilience building interventions.
CARE will deploy sets of proven approaches and outstanding experience focusing on linking emergency food security interventions with medium and longer-term livelihoods and market-based approaches. CARE places women and girls at the front and center of all our work to enhance impacts of all interventions conducted across humanitarian and development projects. CARE beliefs that when women and girls have equal access to resources, they lift their families and communities out of poverty. In this project CARE will focus assistance to those who need them most including child headed households, persons with disabilities, women headed households within the areas of displacements, vulnerable host communities under IPC 3+ food insecurity levels and returnees’ caseloads. Households and vulnerable populations within the severity food insecurity areas of severely flooded and conflict affected areas would be the priority locations of our project targets.
In the programme cycle, CARE would provide emergency food assistance through cash and voucher (CVA) system to populations falling in the IPC 3+ and areas of extreme concerns including in Jonglei, and Unity states complemented nutrition sensitive programming and kitchen gardening around the Nutrition facilities to promote appropriate feeding best practices. While in the areas of very high and high concerns respectively, CARE would provide emergency food production and early recovery livelihoods support and resilience programming to build absorptive and adaptive capacities of crises affected population. CARE International is a global facilitator of village savings and loan associations (VSLA) scheme for recovery and resilience building for humanitarian affected populations.

1. Needs

The acute food insecurity and malnutrition situation in South Sudan is deteriorating because of the economic crisis, climatic shocks - primarily widespread flooding - conflict and insecurity. The inflow of returnees and refugees fleeing the conflict in Sudan is exacerbating the situation, putting additional pressure on an already fragile country.In the second projection period of April to July 2025, the food security situation is expected to deteriorate with the arrival of the lean season. It is likely that an estimated 7.69 million people (57 percent of the population analysed) will be in Phase 3 or above. This will include 2.53 million people likely to be in Phases 4, and 63,000 people likely to be in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). The nutrition situation has also deteriorated, with an estimated 2.1 million children aged 6-59 months suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025, out of which 650,000 are cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). About 1.11 million pregnant or breastfeeding women (PBW) are also suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition in the same period. An estimated 67 percent of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap.As per Integrated Food Security Phase Classification report published on 18th of November 2024 and its projection for April – July 2025, 57.2% of the population will be in phase 3 or above, who need food assistance. When reviewing this by State, the two States seriously affected because of the combined effect of flood and communal conflict are Unity State, affected 71.6% and Jonglai State, affected 62.4%. Considering the resources scarcity and other partners contribution, from Unity States, Mayom and Pariang Counties are targeted, who has got 81,000 and 75,000 people in need respectively and from Jonglai State, Bor South and Pibor Counties are targeted, who has 88,000 and 200,000 people in need.The main drivers of severity of food insecurity are floods, dry spells, inflation and high food prices, and the continued disruption of livelihoods due to violence or the fear of violence and household stressors (such as death of the bread winner, gender-based violence, and morbidity and mortality due to poor hygiene and lack of access to essential basic services), which are greatly amplified by the continuous effect of shocks. In South Sudan, 95 per cent of livelihoods depend on traditional rain-fed agriculture, crop farming, pastoralism and animal husbandry. Climate induced shocks (floods events, localized dry spells and conflicts for grazing areas and farming) have increased violent conflicts among agropastoral communities and have led to an increase in livestock mortality and a decline in the agricultural productivity. Among the most affected people are child headed households, persons with disabilities, older people and women headed households. South Sudan ranks amongst the top ten countries in the world most vulnerable to climate impact, including droughts and flooding. Flooding has been a major driver of displacement followed by conflicts.

2. Project activities

Result 1, Humanitarian Food AssistanceProvide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for 18,398 vulnerable households (8085 female & 5390 male), who are in IPC 4 & above including men, women, boys, girls of different ages and people with and without disabilities.1.1 Sensitization sessions on proper utilization of CVA, all the targeted individuals will receive one day orientation on how to receive and use the CVA. This will be done by both this project staffs together with the County Agricultural department. 1.2 Provision of cash using CVA for targeted 13,475 households in IPC-4+. Even though, still quick market and risk assessment will be done before this project action, as per JMMI information of October publication from REACH, all this project operation areas market are functional and allows for partner to plan and implement any CVA programs. With this consideration this project planned to distribute $60/household with one off for each targeted household for two months, so as to quickly fix the food shortage for the most vulnerable households. This unconditional cash transfer with voucher is to fix their immediate needs, otherwise the same households will be targeted with emergency livelihood support to ensure the smooth transition from cash assistance to recovery. Result 2: Emergency Livelihood SupportTo enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support to 40,425, vulnerable host communities and returnees (24,255 female and 16,170 male), consisting of men, women, boys, girls, elderly people and people with and without disabilities. 2.1 Distribution of dry season agricultural kits (VK, FK) , this is planned for 28,298 vulnerable households to provide each of them for the upcoming dry season, the livelihood support package is composed of at least five types of vegetable seed; 20 gram amaranth, collard, eggplant, onion, tomato and 30 gram watermelon, pumpkin; 50 gram okra including at least one agricultural tool: maloda (shovel), rake, hoe, sickle. 2.2 Support to fisheries (FK); Fishery kits distribution planned for 12,128 fishery folks to provide them with two boxes of hooks (100/box), one coil of monofilament, and two spools of twine. 2.3 Establish kitchen Garden sites at Nutrition Facilities/School/Household; in this respect 63 kitchen garden will be established to serve 12,600 people (7560 female & 5040 male), with assumption of one garden serving 200 individuals in one village/boma. 2.4 Training on Agricultural practices; this capacity building training on improved agricultural practice planned for 800 individuals (480 female & 320 male), in which 200 individuals will be selected and trained from each Counties to serve as lead farmer/mother in the kitchen garden. 2.5 Training on Fisheries; 400 fishery folks will be trained on fish catching, preservation and management in which 100 fisheries will be selectee and trained from each operational County. Result 3: Resilience SupportWith overall aim of reducing dependency on food and agricultural inputs support, 12,600 individuals from host communities and returnees will have an improved ability to absorb shocks with various resilience building interventions. 3.1 Training of community-based extension service providers (CBESP); 40 extension workers (20 male and 20 female) will be selected from the community with set of criteria and will be trained for three days on basic agricultural improved practice so as to rollout the same training and support this project beneficiaries. 3.2 Develop & implement Pest Management strategies, addressing issues such as Fall Armyworm (FAW), desert locusts, and pesticide use; this is area specific strategy developed by project team and County MAFS expert in close consultation of the community, which will be used during this project period to mitigate the crop loss that comes from effect of pest
