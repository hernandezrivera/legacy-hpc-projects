Project Summary

Mercy Corps proposes a variety of shelter interventions to address various shelter needs of new arrivals, existing IDPs, returnees and the host community. This includes:
1) Provision of Basic Emergency Shelter Kits (BESK): Mercy Corps will meet critical life-saving shelter needs of new arrival IDPs and returnees in the targeted LGAs by providing 3,000 basic emergency shelter kits (BESKs). These kits will be part of a comprehensive kit provided to new arrivals which will also include NFIs, hygiene kits, dignity kits, and fuel efficient stoves. New arrivals will also receive a communal tool kit, which will be shared by up to five households, and will include necessary items like shovels, claw hammers, and saws.
2) Provision of Enhanced Emergency Shelter Kits (EESK): Mercy Corps will also distribute enhanced emergency shelter kits to 1,000 HHs in critical need of shelters, including IDPs living in the open, those living in over-congested camps, extremely vulnerable new arrivals in areas where there is available land, and returnees with completely damaged houses. These EESKs are more permanent than the BESKs described above.
3) Provision of Shelter Replenishment Kits: Mercy Corps will provide shelter replenishment kits to 2,700 HHs who are living in deteriorated shelters and are at risk of protection risks such as GBV. This kit is designed to provide additional materials to replenish the deteriorated materials such as tarpaulin to sustain for another period of 3-6 months considering the life span of tarpaulin under the heat, winds and rain.
4) Shelter Repair Kits: Mercy Corps will provide more comprehensive shelter repair kits to 400 HHs (IDPs, returnees and host community) living in poor sheltering conditions (i.e. unfinished, abandoned or damaged buildings). Mercy Corps’ protection team will help prioritize households at increased risk of forced eviction, SGBV, overcrowded families and those in critical need of additional private sleeping place especially HHs with more girls.
5) Distribution of Privacy Screens: All Mercy Corps shelter interventions focus on ensuring the privacy, security, and dignity of all household members. Mercy Corps shelters enable groups such as single women, people with disabilities, and unaccompanied children, to maintain their privacy whilst being protected from stigmatization or targeting. A total of 1,000 HHs will benefit from this intervention.
6) Shelter Repairs in Rann: Mercy Corps shall assist 600 extremely vulnerable households whose houses are critically damaged and not livable in Rann LGA to rebuild and repair. Mercy Corps has confirmed through an internal assessment, as well as a UNOCHA joint mission held on 11 April 2019, that significant proportion of houses in Rann town require major roof repairs. Mercy Corps worked with SC/CCCM technical working group partners to design an assistance package of providing a roofing and fixture kit, paired with skilled labor support and technical assistance, to compliment ongoing self-building efforts (which currently mainly consists of producing mud blocks).
7) DRR Education around Shelter & Settlement: Prior to any kit distribution, a neighbourhood/cluster level orientation shall ensure proper utilization of assistance package as well as to avoid risks i.e. fires, waterlogging, and termite attack. As per DTM Round 26, 12% of camp and camp like settlements reported fire as the single biggest natural hazard risk. Mercy Corps will educate shelter beneficiaries, as well surrounding residents on these risks using Shelter/CCCM sector IEC materials in coordination with Shelter/CCCM sector partners in respective locations.
To help bolster the local economy, Mercy Corps will work with local artisans to provide materials for shelter construction/repairs. Mercy Corps has met with the mason and carpenter unions in the targeted areas and determined that skilled labor is available.

1 - Needs

This need overview was written in May 2019 at time of proposal submission;Over 3.5 million people (53% women, 47% men) across the Northeast are in need of emergency shelter and NFI support, according to the 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO). The HNO notes that 1.4 million are reported as IDPs, 400,000 returnees, 900,000 host community and 800,000 live in inaccessible areas. Overall, 33 percent of IDPs and 14 percent of returnees are staying in poorly built makeshift shelters across Borno State, and 40 percent of IDP camps across Borno State still face challenges of over-congestion per the UNOCHA 2019 Humanitarian Situation Report. In early 2019, Mercy Corps - in coordination with LGA and national level SC/CCCM sector focal points - identified critical shelter gaps throughout Borno. In Bama LGA, a total of 1,063 households comprised of 3,013 individuals are in critical need of shelter assistance. Specifically, 297 HHs (845 individuals) are hosted in the reception centre, 559 HHs (1,666 individuals) are sleeping in the open, and 207 HHs (502 individuals) are hosted in the transit centre in Banki town. In Damboa LGA, a total of 593 HHs (2,961 individuals) are living in the extremely congested Central Primary camp. In Dikwa LGA, a total of 593 HHs are in need of critical shelter assistance to improve their self-built, makeshift shelters in the various overcrowded camps. In Gwoza LGA, 423 HHs (1,311 individuals) have been hosted in the Pulka reception centre for months and are in critical need of shelter assistance. In 2018, there were 508 HHs new arrivals in Gwoza alone. In Ngala LGA, a total of 1,092 households (3,981 individuals) are in need of urgent shelter assistance. In addition, Mercy Corps identified major gaps during an assessment in International School camp in March 2019, where currently 5,883 HHs are residing in self-built, makeshift shelters in low land parcels which consistently face flooding and waterlogging in the rainy season. In Rann LGA, 30,000 people who are in acute need of shelter and NFIs are said to have returned from Cameroon as of April 5, 2019. In Monguno LGA, out of 94 HHs interviewed during KIIs, 34% reported that their shelter was completely damaged.

Technical assistance The proposed rapid response will consist of three primary components: (1) A network of pre-contracted vendors and suppliers in strategic hub market areas (like MMC) who can be called upon to quickly provide supplies to deep field locations when needed. In this way, Mercy Corps will integrate a market-based approach into its rapid response mechanism. In areas where markets are tested and operational, such as Ngala, Dikwa, Gwoza and Damboa LGAs, Mercy Corps will use its current cash and voucher platform Nagis. (2) Prepositioning of integrated and comprehensive NFI kits (supporting WASH, S&S and Nutrition needs) in strategic locations to meet the immediate needs of new arrivals. These kits will be prepositioned in strategic locations as per the expected influx of new arrivals to enable Mercy Corps to respond immediately if the security situation permits access and markets are nonfunctional. The kit will include fuel efficient, clean cook stoves.Mercy Corps will also coordinate with other NGOs who have pre-existing warehouses in key locations and with sector to be part of the common storage. (3) A Roving Rapid Response Team will also be established to plan, assess access, coordinate and implement the emergency response in areas where Mercy Corps has no existing operational presence. Mercy Corps will keep OFDA informed of decisions to commence activities in locations other than the aforementioned LGAs. In locations where Mercy Corps does not have a permanent presence, rather than relying on Community Relief Committees (CRC), Mercy Corps will work with relevant CCCM partners for mobilization of program beneficiaries. The members of the Rapid Response Team will be present and engaged throughout the project duration by providing their expertise to ongoing project activities. The team will be comprised of multi sectoral experts including sectoral leads already on the MC emergency team who will be ready to take on a rapid response role if the need arises.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

As described above, Mercy Corps has developed and maintains Community Relief Committees (CRC) as its primary community mobilization mechanism, with representatives of diverse age, gender, ethnic, religious, and socio-economic groups, including displaced and host communities, so that their concerns are heard and addressed and to avoid community tensions. CRCs have at least two female members, helping to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse of people seeking shelter and services. Mercy Corps’ Community Accountability Reporting Mechanism (CARM) will be continued in the RAI Borno 2 project to enable a safe and effective feedback/complaint system for beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries. The CARM includes a community-based feedback mechanism developed by Mercy Corps in Gombe in 2014 that allows people (in particular non-literate beneficiaries) to give feedback verbally and with discretion. Complaints will also be gathered through designated community focal points and through the use of a logbook updated by the focal point within each CRC, with electronic recording, analysis and follow up of all complaints within a specified timeline by Mercy Corps’ M&E staff. CARM will also be used to monitor protection issues, and issues will also be monitored under the Protection-sector specific programming described below; that information will be used to reduce existing and newly identified risks. The program will monitor the security situation to adjust its implementation approach if the security situation deteriorates.
