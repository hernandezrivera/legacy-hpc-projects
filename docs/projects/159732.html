Project Summary

Project Goal
To reduce the risk posed by ERW to returning and IDP population and host communities, enabling safe movement and reducing the threat of injury or loss of life as a result of mine, ERW or improvised landmine (victim-activated IEDs) contamination.

Overall Project Objective
The project will prevent civilian injury and death caused by the presence of mines, ERW and improvised landmines through life saving mine risk education (MRE). The education will strengthen the capacity of communities to live safely in areas contaminated by past and ongoing conflicts. Targeted communities will be better equipped to avoid injury or death, and better able to report areas of suspected contamination to Somali Authorities for clearance and release. Risk education will be provided by local partners SOWMAC and JUMAN directly to target communities. The project will be made more sustainable through the training of Community Focal Points, who will continue risk education messaging after the completion of the project and provide points of contact to express needs and clearance priorities to MAG and the Somali authorities. The effectiveness and scope of the intervention will be greatly improved by local partners with field teams who are familiar with the context.
In doing so, MAG will contribute to the 2020 HRP Objectives by:
• improving risk prevention,
• providing safe access to services for IDPs, returnees and other civilians affected by conflict, or natural disasters,
• improving protective environment and intervention supporting durable solutions for IDPs,
• capacity development
• building communities resilience so they are less prone to famine shocks in future
Outputs and Outcomes
It is anticipated that the project will have the following outputs, outcomes and impact.
 Memorandum of Understanding developed and signed off between MAG and partners (SOWMAC and JUMAN).
 MRE CLM recruited and trained
 8 CL/MRE teams (four from JUMAN, four from SOWMAC) recruited and trained in MRE
 Deploy 8 roaming MRE/CL teams to operate throughout South West State (SWS)
 Development and distribute MRE materials (training manuals, leaflets, posters, t-shirts)
 Conduct 1,800 MRE training sessions for IDPs, returnees and host communities, and NGOs staff in high-risk areas of SWS
 Provide MRE to 55,000 women, girls, boys and men
 One assessment, including a best practice report, produced with any vital information disseminated to participant NGOs

Outcomes:
• Improved safety with the ERW risk to IDPs, returnees and host communities through provision of E-MRE and related awareness materials
• Reduced risk to communities of accident and injury/fatality.
• Enhanced ability to live safely with ERW/mines/IEDs, through training on the threat posed by mines/ERW/IEDs and UXOs.
• Targeted communities will be better equipped to avoid injury or death.
• Improved mechanisms are in place to support reporting of, mine/ERW/IED.
• IDPs, returnees and host communities in SWS recognise dangerous objects and able to report to appropriate authorities and mine action organisations whenever possible
• Attitudes of targeted communities changed in reference to landmines, UXO, ERWs.
• Increased awareness of how to avoid the risk of getting injured or killed by field teams and local partners staff operating in SWS
• E-MRE project presented in a format enabling it to be replicated in other programmes with similar working contexts
• A national capacity is retained enabling Local NGOs to continue to address the need for and implement attitude change with their residents on MRE.

Mitigation Measures

The scale of the problem is striking: While there has been no comprehensive survey of mine and explosive remnants of war contamination in South Central Somalia due to ongoing instability, limited surveys suggest one in ten communities are directly impacted. The scattered nature of the contamination and diffusion of IDPs throughout the country means the most practical way to mitigate the threat is through delivery of MRE. Funded mine risk education capacity in Somalia is insufficient to reach current numbers of returnees travelling from Kenya into Somalia and the IDPs moving within the country. Last year MAG supported RE teams serving returnees entering Jubaland and IDPs in Southwest State. However, due to lack of funding, the RE project was halted. RE teams would need to be deployed to ensure that the returnees and IDPs could receive RE training. Returnees who received targeted RE training tailored to the situation for instance, in Gedo and Afmadow regions will be at lower risk of injury or death, leading to a lower number of incidents, and reduced requirement to support victim assistance in the future Drought remains the largest contributor to displacement, followed by conflict in Somalia, with a complementary approach required to best support the at-risk communities. IDPs in Somalia – while at risk of lack of access to food, water and live-saving services – also find themselves in unfamiliar territory that has been scarred by conflict. IDPs travel with limited knowledge regarding contaminated areas and explosive remnants of war in Somalia (as supported by surveys of returnees and IDPs in South West State by MAG partner organisations). The lack of knowledge puts this community at increased risk of death and injury from mines and other unexploded ordnance, while IDPs that are conscious of the risks but who lack the understanding to respond adequately, find their access to necessary services limited. As part of the Protection Cluster, MAG’s intervention will mitigate the risks posed by mines and unexploded ordnance to vulnerable families. It will also improve access to the services provided by other members of this cluster. Elements of the intervention will go beyond improving basic human security, for example the creation of Community Focal Points (CFP) can enhance existing community mechanisms while establishing new community representation and improving social cohesion. This represents improved capacity within the local communities – especially that to provide sustainable risk education activities – as well as establishing focal points that both help register the needs of the local communities and improve the planning of future projects. Engaging with local focal points will inform the cluster response and that of other clusters, as well as future humanitarian mine action (clearance) and arms management and destruction activities being explored across Southwest State. MAG Somalia has experienced international and national Community Liaison (CL) personnel who have in depth knowledge of the region. MAG also has dependable national experience in CL and is conversant with its cultural and political environment that would leverage greater lobbying with both international and national NGOs to support the project. MAG has used its extensive project experience to develop best practises that can be replicated in other high-risk areas that MAG has not yet been to. This project is within the ambit of MAG Somalia that has a management team composed of a Country Director (CD), a National Community Liaison Manager (CLM), MRE CLM, a Finance Manager and Technical Field Manager (TFM) with broad range of experiences globally.
