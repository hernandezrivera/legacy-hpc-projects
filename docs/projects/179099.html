Project Summary

Sustaining and expanding essential, quality health services at the community, primary and secondary care levels remains critical to addressing the health needs of the affected population, especially with the changes in lines of control which likely to trigger disruptions in health services and may affect humanitarian access.
Areas with limited access, under-served areas, newly accessible areas, and those that recently changed control will continue to require revitalization of the health system especially in an area like Der-Ez-Zor which considered hard to reach or areas with newly displaced people which has an additional burden on its previous week health system.
The project will be focusing on providing primary health services through three primary health care centers that will provide medical consultation, diagnosis, and treatment by different clinics such as a first-aid clinic, pediatric clinic, dental clinic, and OBGYN department, that will provide 24/7 normal deliveries in addition to the OBGYN consultation and family planning.
In addition, each center will be provided with a mobile clinic that will serve the communities around the center's community and will refer consultations to the centers or to the nearest hospitals.

4 - Needs

The need for humanitarian health assistance is expected to continue in 2021. In Syria, according to HNO, 2021, 12.4 million people are in need of health services; 1.4 million of them are children U5 while 3.3 million are WRA, and over 530,000 are older people with NCD and likely to require health services. Also, the prevalence of disability is nearly double the global average, those persons not only require specialized care, but also are at a greater risk of Covid-19 pandemic outcomes, which increase the burden on the health system and their caregivers. In addition, areas of North-east Syria, particularly Deir-Ez-Zor, are considered as areas of extreme need and are at heightened risk of outbreaks due to frequently interrupted water supply from Alouk water station. Also, these areas are at high risk for an intensified crisis, a scenario which is likely to generate increases in traumatic injury, psychological trauma and the need for post-surgical care, as well as increased morbidity from causes like waterborne illness as displaced persons seek refuge in locations without reliable water, sanitation and shelter. In addition, with just 53 per cent of PHCs are fully functional across Syria, the Covid-19 pandemic has exacerbated the weaknesses in the health system and increased stress among households and health workers which place additional demand on mental health services. Linkages between health facilities and communities must be strengthened to empower individuals with knowledge and awareness to safeguard themselves and their families and know how to effectively seek health services. Outreach and support to mothers are critical to proper care for the newborn at home, including feeding.

5 - Implementation of activities

IPV will establish Three PHCCs in Thiban, Tal-Hmis and Karama subdistricts, with a mobile clinic for each PHC, which will cover the communities in the selected subdistrict after an accurate mapping with the active health partners in the field. Each PHC will have basic departments that will work 8 hours daily, it will include a dental clinic, pediatric, general medicine clinics, and a pharmacy that will provide NCD medication. In addition to a 24/7 first-aid and gynecology clinics that will provide normal delivery for women in addition to consultation, pregnancy follow-up and family planning. Each PHC will provide MHPSS and nutrition services "detailed in nutrition proposal", through CHW, PSS, and GP trained on MHgap, Each PHC will be linked with one ambulance to refer needed cases and deliveries. The PHCCs will be modified for people with disabilities, easy access at the entrance, clinics, pharmacy and WCs. Ongoing coordination with all stakeholders will be done through attending the NES forum HWG meetings, uploading 4Ws and following up with health authorities to avoid any duplication. Ongoing systems of monitoring and accountability will be in place throughout the duration of the project. These will include weekly field visits of the M&E team, as well as the provision of comments/complaints boxes that will be collected on a weekly basis, in addition to a hotline that will be available in each PHCC. Concerns, observations, and complaints will be tracked and responded to appropriately. In addition, satisfaction surveys will conduct on throughout the project. The findings will all be put through a comprehensive M&E report, which will be shared accordingly with the health department.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. The risk from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA which is referred to measures taken to protect vulnerable people from sexual exploitation by staff and associated personnel. Mitigation: -Effective community-based complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance. - Awareness-raising on PSEA amongst personnel, with training on the topic. -Provide information to communities and people affected by crisis about the organization, the principles it adheres to, how it expects its staff to behave. 2. Gender-Based Violence: health outreach services are often the first and the only point of contact for GBV survivors seeking assistance. In order to provide care, survivors must have safe access to facilities, and it is critical that health teams have the knowledge to handle the case. Mitigation: - Identification of safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases connecting health services and clinical management with social services. - Train the staff on psychosocial support , in a manner guarantees safety and confidentiality of the survivor. - Presence of female staff in outreach teams and fixed facilities increase access of women to health services 3. The health facilities and providers could face violence against them, the violence is still the main issue facing healthcare providers. Mitigation: -Set up centers in safe and accessible locations that can be easily reached by beneficiaries. -Relocate centers in relatively safe areas if anything happened. -Reporting on attacks against healthcare. 4. Staff may suffer from burnout due to excess load of work which will add on the preexisting psychosocial stress. Mitigation: - Decrease the number of working hours without affecting the service provided.

7 - Does the project include a PSEA strategy ?

Yes

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

the project includes SEA risks that could be taken place within working staff, or due to some working staff with BNFs from women or children or even within the BNFs level. SEA risks could occur during the staff or BNFs journey to the project centre location by some people from the community. IPV have set many mitigation strategies to address PSEA, which include: the PSEA mandatory training and related COC-PSEA and CSG, which the staff must align with and obtained, with the clear clarification that the IPV have zero tolerance with any SEA accidents. IPV has PSEA focal points in the working area and there will be focal points in the project centre for any reporting needed or support in PSEA mainstreaming. IPV will do awareness-raising for PSEA which include community level, working staff level and centre level which could include: brochures and banners, videos, awareness raining messages and hotlines. IPV will ensure that all the working staff is wellknown about the internal reporting and feedback mechanism taken in place in case of SEA occurrence cases. all the project's service providers or contractures who has direct contact with project beneficiaries from children or women must be trained and signed COC. IPV will ensure that during any conducted sessions there will be a female member to ensure beneficiaries safety, especially from children and women.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Other risk that may emerge, is the inability to find qualified staff, that will be mitigated by having training for the available staff on the selected needed topics. The difficulties to import the drug after the cross-border resolution through Yariboua border, the mitigation is to start using Fish Khabour border. Activities are totally in Der-Ez-Zor eastern line which considered not a stable area, and one of the less secuirties in NES, in the case of any individual violence happened "staff being threaten with weapons" as example, it will be reported instantly to a higher level, at the level of the head of field office in Der-Ez-Zor which have a very good relationship with local authorities and the level of HQ, in addition to follow the SOPs inside the facility which will be focusing on enclosing the case and not to increase tension, till a higher official solute the problems. If there was a fear of a major violence, staff safety should be a priority, therefore an evacuation process will be done according to the security personnel in the organization, and following procedures will be followed according to the emergency from HQ. All centers' staff should attend a preparedness training for the facilities and staff-care in case of incidents. In addition, continued reporting on attacks against healthcare through the Surveillance System for Attacks on Health Care (SSA), as well as NES forum Flash Updates. IPV will conduct lunching project campaign and information sharing with the targeted communities in the project area to ensure their full knowledge of the proposed activities. This will be held in safe, accessible, not far away from the centre to be safe accessed for the targeted people from children boys and girls and women
