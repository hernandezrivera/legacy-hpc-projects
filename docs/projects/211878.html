Project Summary

In 2024, Syria remains the third least peaceful country globally, as per the Global Peace Index (GPI). Persistent hostilities, including artillery shelling, airstrikes, and landmines, continue to endanger civilian lives and impede humanitarian efforts. The security situation remains highly volatile and susceptible to escalation, especially in areas of mixed or contested control and near the frontlines. The widespread presence of unexploded ordnances (UXOs) poses a significant security risk across the country. The NES region is under the control of SDF, backed by international alliances following a 15-month conflict with ISIL forces. The functionality of the health system, encompassing access to quality integrated primary and secondary health services, efficient supply chain management, and robust emergency preparedness and response capacities, has been adversely impacted by deteriorating socio-economic conditions, inadequate infrastructure, electricity shortages, and substandard WASH conditions in healthcare facilities. Food insecurity and water crises have further exacerbated the situation, leading to an increase in waterborne diseases and worsening malnutrition among children and pregnant and lactating women. The health system has been profoundly affected by hostilities, unilateral coercive measures, and a shortage of health workers, 70% of whom have left the country. According to WHO, only 59% of hospitals, 57% of PHCs, and 63% of specialized centres are fully operational. The COVID-19 pandemic, acute watery diarrhoea (AWD)/cholera, and recurring disease outbreaks such as leishmaniasis, measles, and meningitis, coupled with malnutrition, continue to be the primary causes of morbidity and mortality in Syria. These health crises, in conjunction with the declining socio-economic situation, significantly hinder the population’s ability to access healthcare. Host communities and displaced populations in NES have endured escalated levels of persecution and violence from their families and communities. In addition to this gender-specific violence, families suffer due to negative coping mechanisms. These mechanisms, in turn, can limit livelihood opportunities, restrict children’s access to education, and further undermine the already weak social position of women and girls. Inadequate living standards and insufficient coping mechanisms, highlight the multiple stressors causing significant distress and, in many instances, explicit psychological trauma among the Syrian population. Over a decade of crisis has adversely affected people’s mental well-being, with increasing reports of stress and mental health concerns, including among children and youth. Many children, having known nothing but years of crisis, are affected by mental trauma.
According to the 2024 HNO, more than 14.9 million people across Syria urgently require lifesaving health assistance, in which VIYAN plan has been designed to insure access to quality primary healthcare and MHPSS at all levels. VIYAN plan aims to bolster resilience and address the ongoing health needs of the affected population in NES. It will offer an integrated suite of services across the primary health sector, with a focus on enhancing mental health and psychosocial support. The plan includes minor infrastructural rehabilitation and operationalization of two selected PHCs, provision of comprehensive and standardized PHC services, strengthening of inter-sectorial and external referral systems, and promotion of targeted population knowledge through continuous awareness activities. A capacity-building component will be carried out throughout the project, covering topics such as PM+, PFA, EWARS, First Aid, IPC practices, SRH, and medical case management. Based on a VIYAN assessment study in Ar-Raqqa city, the plan also aims to fortify the MHPSS response and provide crucial support to individuals facing mental health challenges.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

This will be achieved through the establishment of an MHPSS center, supplemented by mobile teams, offering a comprehensive package of MHPSS services that cover the four layers of the MHPSS IASC pyramid. The interventions will be closely coordinated with affected populations, key stakeholders, national and sub-national clusters, local civil councils, and health committees. The primary beneficiaries will be IDPs out-of-camps, returnees, and host populations in high-need areas that continue to experience inadequate public basic services, including out-of-coverage and hard-to-reach areas.Protection Risk Analysis: 1) Insecurity in most target areas potentially poses a risk to women and girls and thus may not be able to access and utilize services (1A. undertake proper mechanism of feedback from beneficiaries through focus-group discussions with women, girls, men, and boys which initiate a community-based feedback mechanism in addition to suggestion boxes & hotlines 1B. participation with HWG will helps VIYAN to identified undeserved areas) 2) Other vulnerable groups such as PWD’s may not be accessed (2A. Interventions offered are provided through two different modalities (static and mobile) while static services provided a wider scope of services, the mobile modality brings basic health care and facilitate referral 2B. Community outreach network which is responsible to provide house-to-house and community awareness activities, this will allow beneficiaries to reach static and mobile services) 3) PSEA: VIYAN will enhance awareness of PSEA policy including accountability measures to the staff 4) Low quality services expose beneficiaries to harm and deterioration of their health conditions (4A. Regular supportive supervision and monitoring on quality of health care provided at VIYAN facilities).Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating Measures: VIYAN plan identifies several risks, including duplication of activities, limited medicine availability, fraud, accountability to affected populations, and deteriorating security conditions. Assumptions include effective partner coordination, stable local markets, implemented antifraud policies, community involvement in project design, and stable security conditions. Mitigation measures include improved networking, medical quality assessments, active participation in UN & Health cluster coordination meetings, key informant interviews, patient satisfaction surveys, hiring local staff, and arranging larger medical supplies to overcome service interruptions.
