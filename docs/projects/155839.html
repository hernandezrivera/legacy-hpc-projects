Project Summary

The project aims to Reducing vulnerability and Strengthening resiliency for most vulnerable households through enabling them to improve their food security, secure income and mitigates the dependence on humanitarian assistance through food production activities in Mare’ subdistrict in the northern rural of Aleppo. The proposed Project will help more than 110 households in establishing small-scale milk and dairy production in order to meet their daily needs, so they enhance diet diversity and cover market requests in order to generate income and contribute in providing goods with fair prices.
The project will target most vulnerable households including female-headed households, households with disabled members, families families that have elderly persons, families with big number of children with breadwinner facing difficulties in securing income. Selection criteria will be through detailed and well-designed scoring system and it is explained in detail in the direct beneficiaries’ section. The selection process will be in direct coordination with Mare’ local council
The project has two main components: milk production and dairy production.
In milk production component 30 households will be provided by cows (one cow per households). The household members will be provided by training on animal caring and good hygienic practice in milk production and processing. These households will have also veterinary visits by specialist veterinarian and feeding for the cows for two months.
In dairy production component every month, 8 beneficiaries will be provided with milk and trained in dairy production every reaching more almost 80 beneficiaries at the end of project. Also providing these families with equipment including ((fresh milk 200 liter,Plastic Pails (2KG capacity),Pot(stainless steel/25 Liter capacity),stove unit (gas/cylinder/burner/connectors),Large spoon,Milk filter ,Plastic container(25 Liter capacity)). The expected products will be (cheese, ghee, butter, ayran and yogurt). these products meets the exact need of the local markets and will contribute towards decreasing prices since most of these products in Mare are being procured from outside the district.
After finishing the project, these households will be able to continue generating income and contributing in re-establishment of the disrupted production process of foods and enhancing food security of the families in Mare’.

Needs

According to the statistics HNO, the Food Security Sector estimates that 6.5 million Syrians are acutely food insecureand a further 4 million people are at risk of becoming acutely food insecure due to their fast depletinglivelihoods. In total, the groups of acutely food insecure and those at risk of food insecurity represent anestimated 54% of Syria’s population.Compared to last year, the number of Syrians facing acute food insecurity has decreased from 37% to 33% ofthe population. However, the number of people at risk of food insecurity has doubled, clearly indicating furtherdepletion of livelihoods throughout the country.People’s food insecurity across Syria remains triggered by three main factors: insecurity, lack of financial andphysical access to food, and depletion of livelihoods.In particular, lack of financial and physical access to food is the result of a big drop in food production in Syria,lack of income and low purchasing power, fragmented markets and high prices. In Syria, main constraints continue to be high fodder prices, insufficient coverage of veterinary services. Multiple displacements and reduction in livelihood opportunities have contributed to an uncertain future for the majority of Syria’s population. As a result, people have resorted to multiple harmful food and livelihoods based coping strategies that have often exacerbated the protection risks they are exposed to harmful coping mechanisms include spending savings, borrowing money, and depelting productive assets. HNO 2019 reported that most affected population at household level includes, those with multiple vulnerability statuses based on gender, disability, high dependency ratio, shelter types, those that were newly displaced or displaced multiple times and returnee households who lost their assets and have to restart their livelihood, are considered more food insecure than others. According to the last update of Market Price Index issued by Assistance Coordination Unit ; the average price of milk in Syria is 0.49$, and it is in Aleppo governorate 0.5$ with difference rates of availability the prices increases to reach 0.56$ in Mare’ subdistrict with moderate level of availability.Balad M&E team conducted rapid need assessment and market assessment in Mare’ subdistrict assess the availability of dairy products in the local market and to compare the prices against the purchase power. The results of the assessment were as follows: The estimated population in Mare’ is : 54,500 individual. the breakdown of the population is as follows: (18,975 Women/15,525 Men/ 8000 boys/12000 girls). The estimated number of female headed households is (450 household).Respondents profiles:1-82% were male 18% were female. 2-40% were IDPs and 60% were locals.Major Findings: 1-93% of the respondents said that the milk and dairy products are available in the local market.2-66% said the consume 1 liter of milk per day per household, 19% said 2 liters, and the rest reported no consumption of milk. 3-For yogurt (66% consume 1 kg, 19% consume 2kg, 9% consume more than 2) .4-The numbers for cheese were different with only (33%) reported consumption of 1kg of cheese daily, and the rest reported no consumption at all.The average price of milk is 200 SYP/liter. of yogurt is 275 SYP/kg of cheese is 1200 SYP/kg (more than 2.5 USD as the average daily income is 2 USD).5-58% of surveyed said the price is high since it is not compatible with the weak salaries.6- 80% agreed that establishing dairy production will decrease the price and enhance availability.The local council members explained the higher prices in Mare’ to the transportation costs, as most of the milk and dairy products are being procured from nearest villages. These findings highlights the project goal to fill the gap and cover the need by supporting the community and most vulnerable households with food production to meet local market needs and generate income.

Implementation of activities

According to Sector Objective 2:Support self reliance of affected households by protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income generating opportunities to save and sustain lives.(Activity 3: Support to asset building and asset protection (small livestock of households targeted received livestock byand animal feed distribution) including internalization activities; and related training. It's indicators: 30 of households target will be received livestock. 30 of animals will be distributed.Quantity of animal feed distributed (18000kg)Activity 5: Support Income generating activities including vocational training.Its indicator: 110 of targeted households supported with income generation activities and training - by appropriate modality. The implementation will start by signing the MoU with Mare local council and conduct debrief meeting to explain the project to the local authorities. In the same time Balad office will announce for job vacancies for veterinarian and other direct staff. Then the team will announce the project in coordination with the local council and start registering potential beneficiaries. In that time the tender for providing cows, fodder, and all required project equipment will be announced. After that, we will establish committee for selecting beneficiaries according to the scoring system. After selecting the 60 households (30 for milk production and 80 for dairy production) and procuring the cattle and equipment, the distribution and training will start according to name list and contract with the beneficiary in presence of local council. Training will take place after that and veterinary visits. in the first two months of the project after distributing the cows, the feeding will be distributed to the households provided with cows.And the other havd, Balad Organization will buy all the assets/equipment needed for the workshop from inside Syria or from outside in coordination with the donor. At the same time, we will provide on open space for children in order to educate them and offered Psychosocial support activities by specialists for the children of beneficiariesIn addition, we will create another space for beneficiaries’ disabled children and offered Psychosocial support activities in order to permit the mother to work and train, and in the same time they are will be assured that their children are in friendly places which provide care and professional support for them Every month the 8 of Beneficiaries who will produce dairy products will be provided by 500 liters of milk daily basis to produce cheese, yogurt, ghee and liquid yogurt. at the end of each month, each trained woman will be provided with (200 $) as a one-time salary and the supporting kit which will contain 200 litres of milk+ equipment for cooking to generate income through continuing production at home. After starting producing milk from distributed cows, the milk from this source will be covered and if the amount was less than the required team will procure from the local market. Marketing and signing long-term agreements with shopkeepers and restaurants or any other potential consumers in Mare and near markets for milk and dairy product supplies.During the project training sessions and veterinary visits will be in bi-monthly basis. Also the skilled beneficiaries will participate in coaching sessions to help other households in production process.After the project finish the beneficiaries will have the ability to continue production and generate income without any assistance.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: Stability in security situation and market prices, availability of fodder and ability of cross-border procurement. Risk: Increasing feed prices - risk perceptions: likely – risk mitigation: the household will be supported by cow fodder for two months. Risk: Low milk production – risk perception: likely - risk mitigation: Cows are procured from trusty supplier with ability to change Cows. Risk: Animal illness – risk perception: fair - risk mitigation : veterinarian visits and provide veterinary drugs.
