Project Summary

The ongoing armed conflict in the Northwest and Southwest regions of Cameroon has triggered a humanitarian crisis of staggering proportions, displacing 95.7% of internally displaced persons (IDPs) from their homes. Among these, 9% are in urgent need of shelter, while 1.6% are grappling with severe economic hardships (MSNA 2024). This conflict has not only claimed lives but has also led to widespread looting, burning, and destruction of homes, exacerbating vulnerabilities and intensifying challenges related to housing, land, and property rights. As a result, communities are increasingly at risk of further displacement, conflict, and marginalization.
Many displaced families are in urgent need of safe, temporary shelters, as their homes lie in ruins. The lack of essential Non-Food Items (NFIs) such as cooking utensils, bedding, and hygiene supplies is further exacerbating their already dire living conditions. There is a critical demand for long-term housing solutions that are not only affordable and accessible but also resilient to future crises and environmentally sustainable.
The situation has been worsened by intentional acts of violence, natural disasters like storms and floods, and prolonged neglect resulting from displacement. As a result, families have been forced to exhaust their savings on rent in host communities—a precarious and unsustainable arrangement that leaves them vulnerable. This highlights the urgent need for comprehensive support to ensure that these families can establish secure and dignified living conditions.
Furthermore, displaced individuals and returnees face significant barriers in accessing land for housing and agriculture, often hindered by land disputes and unclear ownership. A streamlined process for land registration is critical to securing property rights for these families and preventing land grabbing, particularly for vulnerable groups such as women and minorities. Many lack proper documentation for their properties, complicating efforts to reclaim lost assets, while returnees struggle to reclaim homes from secondary occupants in conflict-prone areas.
Education on housing, land, and property rights is essential for both displaced persons and host communities to prevent violations and promote understanding. Currently, 1.3% of IDPs have been forced to return, 1.9% refuse to stay with host communities, and 0.4% face tensions with these hosts, further complicated by 7.2% of the population receiving no assistance during displacement (MSNA 2024).
Pathways to Resilience is a transformative initiative designed to enhance access to housing, land, and property (HLP) security for vulnerable communities in the crisis-affected regions of Northwest and Southwest Cameroon. With 74,607 households displaced—including 32,374 IDP households, 33,127 returnee households, and 9,106 host community households (MSNA 2024)—this project addresses the urgent need for secure living conditions in regions such as Boyo, Bui, Donga-Mantung, Ngo-Ketunjia, Fako, Kupe-Manenguba, Lebialem, Manyu, and Meme.
The primary goal is to empower displaced individuals and returnees to access their HLP rights, fostering a secure environment where families can rebuild their lives and establish a stable foundation for their futures, assisting them by constructing/repairing/rehabilitating houses/apartment that meet environmental criteria. By promoting resilience and sustainable development, we aim to transform the living conditions of those affected by conflict.
Target Beneficiaries
1. Displaced Families: IDPs and returnees who have lost their homes and require secure housing solutions.
2. Host Communities: Families providing shelter to displaced individuals and needing support to manage increased demands on their resources.
3. Local Authorities: Government officials and community leaders who play a crucial role in implementing HLP protections and facilitating community engagement.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The Pathways to Resilience project is dedicated to enhancing access to housing, land, and property security for vulnerable communities in the crisis-affected Northwest (Boyo, Bui, Donga-Mantung, Ngo-Ketunjia) and Southwest (Fako, Kupe-Manenguba, Lebialem, Manyu, Meme) regions of Cameroon. This initiative addresses several urgent needs identified through comprehensive assessments, including access to essential Non-Food Items (NFIs). The 74,607 displaced households in Cameroon, including 32,374 IDP households, 33,127 returnee households, and 9,106 host community households, are in urgent need of safe shelter due to conflict-related destruction. The crisis has increased vulnerability, with many facing legal challenges in securing land ownership, leading to disputes and insecurity. Teenage mothers/adolescent girls and women are particularly vulnerable to exploitation. The ongoing conflict has significantly disrupted traditional livelihoods, increasing poverty and limiting income-generating opportunities. 26.2% of internally displaced persons (IDPs) are returning to their original homes due to lack of livelihood options in host communities, and only 23.6% have access to farmland. The psychological impact has left many traumatized, particularly women, teenage mothers, and adolescent girls, and are often excluded from decision-making processes. Who face additional barriers in accessing land and housing rights. This leaves vulnerable families in urgent need of essential non-food items (NFIs), exacerbating their living conditions.To address these identified needs, the project will implement the following activities:1. Housing Reconstruction and Rehabilitation: Assess housing needs and provide materials and technical support for the sustainable construction, repair, and rehabilitation of 952 homes for displaced families and returnees. This initiative will ensure secure living conditions while meeting environmental criteria and improving community infrastructure. 2. Land Rights Documentation and Legal Support: Establish community initiatives to help families document land tenure and secure legal recognition of their land rights in collaboration with local councils and relevant ministries. This will include two day workshops on legal rights and discreet support for obtaining necessary registration documentation within each targeted division.3. Sensitization and Awareness Raising on Housing, Land, and Property Rights : Conduct sensitization and Awareness Raising campaigns to educate 1,904 IDPs, 1,840 returnees, and 2,500 host community members on their housing, land, and property rights. This will involve informational workshops, distribution of educational materials, and forming local advocacy groups to prevent rights violations and promote land dispute resolution mechanisms by 2025. 4. Capacity Building for Community Leaders: Organize two-day training workshops for 270 community leaders, including 30 women leaders from each division. These workshops will focus on conflict resolution, negotiation, and advocacy, enhancing their ability to represent community interests in housing and land issues. 5. Psychosocial Support and Trauma Healing: Provide psychosocial support services, including counseling and support, to 952 IDPs and 920 returnees, helping individuals and families cope with trauma and mental health challenges resulting from the conflict. 6. Livelihood Diversification and Skills Training: Implement training programs for 952 IDPs and 920 returnees in various livelihood options, such as agricultural practices, small business development, and vocational skills. The project will also offer rental assistance options (free rent, cash-for-rent, or subsidies) to help families afford housing. 7. Distribution of Non-Food Items (NFIs): Organize the distribution of essential NFIs, including kitchen sets, solar lamps, cooking utensils, bedding, and hygiene supplies, to 952 IDPs and 920 returnees, host community members.
