Project Summary

Nigeria, and indeed the Northeast is a conflict-prone area with deep-seated multiple crises of religious tensions. These conflicts, largely due to poverty, unemployment, and illiteracy has displaced over 1.8 million people and put over 4.8 million in crises, emergency, and famine1 and resulted in one of the worlds’ worst cases of humanitarian crises. Women and children are the most affected vulnerable groups (both in Internally Displaced Persons (IDP) camps and local communities in the Northeast and other states in Nigeria). Poor health conditions, transactional sex, human trafficking, early/forced marriage and drug abuse have been reported among IDPs.With an increase in out of school and children without an opportunity to attend school and lack of livelihood opportunities for young people makes them vulnerable to recruitment by groups like Islamic State for West African Province (ISWAP) popularly known as Jama’at Ahlas sunnah lid-Dawah wal-Jihad Boko Haram. In addition, a large number of children have become street beggars, leading to an increase in violent crime and drug abuse. All these serve as coping strategies for survival.
Center for Advocacy Transparency and Accountability initiative (CATAI) hopes to establish a Teaching-Learning Space (TLS) for affected populations to teach school-age children between the age of 6-12years the basics of literacy and numeracy as a strategy to provide them with basic knowledge on how to read and write and to also to provide them with a foundation for enrolment in formal education. 5,000 children from selected camps within Maiduguri and Jere will be targeted for this intervention. This will enable them to be active in the post insurgency and the resettlement processes. 1 - Needs

Elmiskin center camp is one of the largest camps in Borno state, with people from worse hit areas, having a population of over 8647 and 1220 household, this population is mostly children between the age of 6-17 leaving without having access to education which makes them vulnerable to social abuse and influence of radicalized Ideology. The intervention will give an opportunity for children to have basic knowledge of reading and writing and have psycho-social support to make the decision for them.the intervention will address the educational need of over 5000 children within the Elmiskin Center Camp and Transit Camp to provide an opportunity to affected population in having access to Education.

2 - Activities

Output1: Increased Access to Basic Education for Affected population. 1.1 Conduct Need Assessment Conducting needs assessment is a prime activity that will be conducted by CATAI with a focus on Elmiskin center camp and transit camp. Specific data/information on IDPs is required in order to plan and design a proper education program. Most importantly, the needs assessment requires a multi-sectoral approach in order to ensure that basic social services and welfare of the affected population is been addressed. It is also important to give priority to education so that the affected population to have access to school. Based on the results from the assessment(s), other options for some strategic interventions can jointly be looked into by education partners and the government. Thus the following will be the specific activities: Profiling of IDP children per household (to determine age, gender, level of education, etc) School mapping (to determine school needs: infrastructure-including availability of classrooms, furniture, etc, teaching/learning materials, human resources-teachers/support staff, this would include determining their qualifications & professional development needs, etc) In terms of identifying the neediest (poorest of the poor) within the camp and also verification of beneficiaries/children based on evidence-based selection criteria (must be developed). 1.2 Construct additional learning spaces in campsDepending on the findings of the needs assessment, construction of additional classrooms may be required. For such intervention, Partners working on providing safe space for children will be reached to construct the facility for the children as pointed out, this will require effort in fundraising by the EIEW Group as well individual development partner organizations. In this strategic priority, additional learning spaces will be in the form of make-shift structures, such as tents. 1.3 Provide school children with learning materials Another area of intervention is provision of other forms of learning materials such as books, pencils, and shoes in order to encourage them to contin their education. This could be a way of helping IDP children to be more motivated and perhaps even overcome the trauma caused by the displacement. However, in doing so, it is important to bear in mind that proper identification and selection of needy children from the camp will be done. Thus, the focus of this strategic priority should target the most marginalized and vulnerable group of children from both hosted and hosting communities. 1.4 Construct/strengthen water and sanitation services School children need water and sanitation services in schools. In general, WASH issues (and gender) should include sanitary products to the girls in the menstruation age (lack of which keeps them out of school).we will strengthen the capacity of the children and raise their awareness on effective Hygiene keeping to keep them clean and healthy. Output 2: Improved Quality of Education Activities 2.2 Provide Education and Recreation Materials Internally displaced children hosted in camp and vulnerable children should be identified based on vulnerability assessment of school children (there is need to agree on what this should include, i.e. parameters/criteria). The recreational service will serve as one of the psychosocial support to reduce the trauma of the affected children. 2.3 Recruit/select Volunteer facilitators. The educational intervention may need to have sustainable facilitators that can handle the learning space within the camp. Ways and means need to be found of how to do this so that it is not at the expense of focusing resources on those most in need. This includes recruitment of new teachers/ Facilitators and payment of monthly salaries, there will be need for stipends to be paid to teachers. 2.4 Train Recruited FacilitatorsTraining selected facilitators in child-centered and participatory teaching/learning methods.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

An evidence-based approach will be used to measure our impact. Through the needs assessment conducted at the beginning of project implementation, the most vulnerable community members will be identified and feedback collected from them quarterly and bi-yearly which will be used to measure project effectiveness. In addition, we will quarterly review our strategy and social impact effectiveness through the number of children taught in the learning space especially those identified as the most vulnerable and how fast they learn numeracy and literacy skills. We will assess the number of children we are able to keep off the streets or roaming the IDP camp and the cost-benefit analysis of running the programs. Consultation with parents, caregivers, and children will be key throughout project implementation. Feedback collected will be used to enhance the effectiveness of service delivery in the project. The activity plan design will guide in each step of the project monitoring particularly the curriculum that will be taught to the children.
