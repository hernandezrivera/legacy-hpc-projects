Project Summary

The project aims to support vulnerable mobile populations, including internally displaced persons (IDPs) and migrants as well as their host communities to address food insecurity and enhance socioeconomic stability through improving access to livelihood opportunities. This will be achieved through support to agricultural and livestock-related income generating activities for approximately 60,000 individuals (15,288 men, 15,912 women, 14,112 boys and 14,688 girls) and at least 15 per cent of the target group will be persons with disabilities. The target population will also consist of IDPs, vulnerable migrants, and host community members, all targeted based on their needs. IOM will apply a people-centered approach with community based planning to improve conditions in target areas and ensure that interventions are based on needs and the rights of the vulnerable migrants and communities to access food and improved livelihood opportunities.
The project will focus on communities across Blue Nile, Gedaref, Red Sea, Kassala, South Kordofan, and West Kordofan; when possible it will also focus on vulnerable communities in Darfur. IDPs, returnees, migrants, and local communities in these areas were already vulnerable to food insecurity and limited livelihood opportunities prior to the outbreak of fighting in April 2023 between Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and Rapid Support Forces (RSF), due to limited purchasing power resulting from inflation and high staple food prices. Now, in light of recent events, existing vulnerabilities have been drastically increased and many people have found themselves newly or secondarily displaced, lacking liquidity and without livelihood opportunities. According to HNO data and revised HRP information, the selected localities have been classified as Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) phase 3 'Crisis' and/or phase 4 'Emergency' further highlighting the need for timely and adequate livelihood interventions to address food insecurity, protect lives, and move towards economic self-sufficiency. Under the HRP 2023, the project is in line with FSL Cluster Objectives to support self-reliance of affected households by protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income generating opportunities to save and sustain lives.
The planned interventions will include: provision of cash assistance; provision of agricultural support including seeds to vulnerable farmers or other individuals for subsistence; provision of small livestock and veterinary services; and inputs and small business support tailored to IDPs, youth, and women in agriculture and in income generating skills such as food processing, brick making, welding activities. IOM has already established good access to most of the target localities through ongoing projects, and seeks to continue implementing life-sustaining, early recovery, community stabilization initiatives. The planned interventions will be delivered in a way to complement past and ongoing activities.

1 - Needs

The conflict in Sudan has drastically increased the impact of an existing political and economic crisis, which combined with natural shocks such as floods and droughts severely threatens the livelihoods of millions of people, now unable to engage in income generating activities. The high price of agricultural inputs had reduced harvest yields in 2022 and increased food prices, thereby undermining food security, and decreasing access to livelihood opportunities in the agricultural sector – in 2023 the conflict has severely affected supply and access to liquidity making it yet harder for individuals or cooperatives to produce food or find work. The economic crisis has also led to a drop in people’s purchasing power and reduced their ability to access enough food and essential basic items and services. The population groups most affected are IDPs and host communities affected by displacement and inter-tribal conflicts along with poor groups in rural areas including agro-pastoral and pastoral communities. The war in Ukraine and now the war in Sudan has further exacerbated Sudan’s food security crisis; reduced purchasing power combined with global price hikes in commodities such as wheat, of which Russia and Ukraine supplied 73-80 per cent of Sudan’s imports 2018-2020 (HNO, 2023), had left the majority of the population unable to cover their basic food needs. Agricultural production was also impacted by the economic crisis, and severe floods that displaced 97,227 people between May and October 2022 (IOM DTM). Sudan is facing a drastic increase in food insecurity, with an estimated 19.9 million people with food needs and 15 million of these people facing IPC Phase 3 'Crisis' and IPC Phase 4 'Emergency' level situations. According to the IPC Acute Food Insecurity Analysis, “the prevalence of the population in IPC. This represents an increase of around 3.7 million food insecure people when comparing to figures prior to the conflict, that had already increased significantly since 2022. Since armed conflict broke out across Sudan on 15 April 2023, over a million people have been displaced – more than the total number displaced in 2021 and 2022 together. Displaced people will be unable to plant crops at this essential moment in the agricultural season; those affected by the crisis economically will be unable to procure the inputs they require as stocks are low and prices high. The extreme level of displacement due to conflict, combined with the deterioration of the economy, are adding further pressure on the usage of local natural resources of host communities. IOM and inter-agency needs assessments highlight that communities in these areas rely heavily on agriculture and livestock as a source of food and income while many of them are selling local agricultural and livestock products for a short-term income to acquire enough food. These livelihood options remain a limited source of income due to current conflict preventing access to inputs in broken markets and preventing access to agricultural lands for reasons of security, fluctuating weather patterns, post-harvest losses, deteriorating grazing lands and poor farming/ livestock practices that do not meet the local market demands hindering profitability. Women's traditional sources of livelihoods such as farming and animal husbandry have been depleted as they are forced to adopt negative coping strategies due to the economic crisis or displacement – such as selling the last remaining female animal and other livestock or farming tools to pay for basic services and commodities which have drastically increase in price. – These factors lead to increased vulnerability including reduced household food intake and increasing the double burden of work to compensate for the loss of assets such as collecting firewood/charcoal for selling, fetching water, and farming.

2 - Activities/Outputs

All proposed activities will be coordinated with the relevant government authorities and the relevant partners working in the same areas. IOM will support each locality utilizing a needs-based and tailored approach including suitable interventions, inputs, and advice to communities based on land, soil, and water components. Activities: 1. Provision of multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) to support recently displaced people or host communities to protect their livelihoods and allow small investments in income generating activities – where possible, MPCA will be combined with referrals to other support including protection and livelihoods. 2. Set-up new or reactivate local livelihood management committees for each locality (10-20 members for 10 committees) to support community ownership and management of the activities. 3. Provide agricultural support to vulnerable farmers with awareness sessions and introduction sections for local production cooperatives and inputs on climate smart agricultural techniques, through: Distribution of improved seeds and farming tools for vulnerable households (HHs) per locality for the selected beneficiaries and cooperatives members. Establishment of drip irrigation farms using locally available materials for target female headed HHs per locality, including the distribution of vegetable seeds. Provision of agricultural inputs such as small water pumps for irrigation farms, and modes of transportation to local markets. The water pumps will prioritize options for alternative energy sources to reduce the impact of high fuel prices and environmental impact. Materials will be donated to the local livelihood management committees, and they will be responsible for maintaining the donated items. 4. Replace lost assets and support livestock husbandry practice and veterinary services through: Providing upgraded veterinary care equipment targeting Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) (5-10 CAHWs from each locality). Providing vaccinated healthy local livestock for target HHs and establishment of small household animal farms with business awareness sessions for target female-headed HHs per locality. 5. Small business and income generating support tailored to IDPs, youth, and women to increase household income and sustain local economies including e.g.,: Providing food processing and conservation support based on local agricultural production and addressing key components of nutritional care for pregnant and lactating women (PLWs), children, for target female headed HHs per locality. Welding inputs and support for target youths per locality. Traditional brick making inputs and support for target youths per locality and other income generating activities identified through community-based planning process. The proposed project will maintain a level of flexibility to determine and design interventions according to community priorities and results of feasibility and local market analysis and trends. 5. Monitoring and evaluation (M&E): Conduct continuous monitoring of activities to adjust support based on beneficiaries’ needs, challenges, and feedback and conduct post activity review to determine lessons learnt and best practices.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Interventions will be based on environmental trends and targeted towards local needs, for example through the selection of relevant and required agricultural inputs, seeds, and tools to farmers in order to mitigate the impacts of climate change and support livelihoods that do not contribute to environmental degradation. Communities will be introduced to alternative solutions and “water-smart” innovations for better yields and increased water use efficiency while ensuring water productivity. Interventions will be tailored to small scale farmers prioritizing simplicity and ease of maintenance and operation. Local agricultural cooperatives will also be capacitated and supported to access finance, to be able to face economic shocks and assist in the provision of animal treatments, feed, and supplements, specifically targeting herders whose movements are restricted, and supporting livelihood diversification to vulnerable communities.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

IDPs and returnees in vulnerable situations including youth and women and disabled persons will be prioritized to ensure their protection and adequate access to basic services and livelihoods opportunities. All members of the community including people with additional vulnerabilities, men and women will be separately consulted and encouraged to be fully involved in consultations, activities, and post activity processes to address their specific needs and encourage open discussions. The project will empower these groups by involving them in the decision-making processes through a rights-based, people-centered approach using community-based planning during project design and implementation, to ensure that the activities respond to the actual needs and market opportunities for target beneficiaries and promote community ownership. A broad range of mobility issues will be discussed during the community-based planning process, ranging from protection issues faced by IDPs, returnees, women, and youth and the various drivers of their displacement, additional vulnerabilities, and economic disparities to ensure that needs and gaps are addressed and challenges of different segments of the target population are considered. IOM will adhere to its gender mainstreaming and accountability to affected populations (AAP) guidelines, as they are aligned with the “do no harm” principle and IOM's Institutional Framework for Addressing Gender-Based Violence in Crises. The needs of individuals in extremely vulnerable situations will be particularly reflected in the programme design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. In these areas, IOM will ensure the following elements are considered: (1) safety, security, and dignity; (2) equitable access; (3) accountability through community-based feedback mechanisms, with information collected throughout the implementation period. Staff and implementing partners will be trained on protection topics including how to treat GBV disclosures ethically and appropriately, as well as using the referral pathways to provide essential support to community members to access these pathways. IOM will work with staff and suppliers to strengthen the implementation of the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse through awareness raising and in line with IOM PSEA guidelines. Another group that will be prioritized is households from the host communities assessed to be vulnerable and in dire need of food security and livelihoods opportunities. This will include identifying conflict sensitive locations for activities, and identifying appropriate risk mitigation measures that ensure adherence to “do no harm” including providing equitable access to assistance to avoid increases in tensions or inter-communal conflicts due to influx of IDPs and returnees in host community areas and the overall increase of needs in the area, leading to reduction of resources, and perception by the host community that they are not receiving comparable support.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The project will be guided by key principles ensuring accountability to affected populations (AAP), including host communities in all processes of project design and implementation. Vulnerabilities will be taken into consideration starting from the first phase immediate assessment of critical humanitarian needs of affected migrants including IDPs and returnees to the implementation of the interventions. The proposed project is built around a people-centred and participatory approach following IOM’s Community-Based Planning tool to ensure that communities themselves are enablers and drivers of their own recovery. AAP will also be promoted through capacity building activities for relevant stakeholders including local authorities and community leaders such as leaders from women and youth groups, etc. As part of capacity building, the project will establish or reactivate community management committees with the key responsibility to coordinate and monitor overall project implementation as well as obtain feedback from beneficiaries. IOM will support these committees to establish a complaints and feedback mechanism that is diverse and contextually appropriate as possible to enable people to provide feedback on programmes and submit complaints, that activate appropriate processes and procedures to provide timely response including: 1) Establishing an appropriate, safe, and accessible process. 2) Clear referral pathways with designated tasks and responsibilities for complaints or feedback. 3) Ensure protection of all personal data handled through the mechanisms, in terms of consent and confidentiality in line with IOM data protection principles. 4) Establish timelines for response by community, partners, IOM, or other agencies as needed. 5) Evaluate effectiveness, lessons learnt, and best practices of the response. IOM is also part of inter-agency coordination approaches and will ensure to follow the relevant guidelines on accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms as agreed by the inter-agency groups. Women’s representation and participation will be strongly encouraged within the committees to address their general lack of access to decision-making processes and to ensure that female complainants receive gender-sensitive support. All instances of harassment, sexual exploitation, and abuse reported by beneficiaries, committees, other relevant stakeholders will be referred to IOM’s PSEA focal point. IOM has Zero Tolerance policy towards SEA and has institutional guidelines on PSEA and a reporting mechanism in case of suspected SEA cases. IOM will ensure to inform its beneficiaries of the available avenues of feedback and complaint mechanisms including PSEA reporting. If needed, IOM will provide direct assistance through psychosocial support, counselling, and medical assistance to survivors, and this will be done in coordination with other qualified Protection partners and service providers.
