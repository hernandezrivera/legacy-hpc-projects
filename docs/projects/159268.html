Project Summary

Palestinian children older than ten years old living in Gaza have now witnessed three conflicts in their short lives. Children aged 11 or less have only known life in Gaza under closure, resulting in an increase in the number of children displaying high levels of stress-related symptoms, requiring additional family and community-based support and individualized interventions aiming at reducing vulnerability and increasing protection. A Child Protection Rapid Assessment was conducted by Tdh (2018) shows that violence against children is common across Gaza. The Assessment found psychosocial distress as a main child protection concern, manifested by aggressive behaviour, unusual crying, and unwillingness to go to school. OCHA (2019) has identified providing mental health and PSS to children who could resort to a negative coping mechanism or could be the victims of harmful practices, including children survivors of GBV, victims of conflict, children victims of abuse and key humanitarian response. On top of the chronic crisis, since March 2018, Gaza has been witnessing a progressive deterioration of the political, social and security environments as a result of the "Great March of Return (GMR)" Demonstrations. The GMR Demonstrations have further compound to already fragile situation of the impoverished families making them unable to provide their children with basic life necessities. Up to August 31, 2019, a total of 46 children were killed by Israeli forces since the beginning of GMR Demonstrations, along with 15 children who were killed in other circumstances. Along with a total of 7947 injuries (7394 boys and 553 among girls (OCHA September 2019; MoH, 2019). With such context, it is undoubtedly that the need for the provision of responsive and remedial protection responses for affected children and their families, such as case management and PSS are highly demanded. This Action is a response to such need and it is consistent with Cluster Objective 2 which is Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict-related violence. The proposed activities will include the provision of responsive and remedial child protection responses, including case management, psychosocial support to 600 children, including 150 injured children during GMR, 150 children survivors of GBV, and 300 children who have different child protection concerns. In total, the provided protection services will be timely and of good quality protection responses that are tailored according to children's needs and the best interest of boys and girls, equally. Under this Action, a total of 8000 persons will benefit from awareness-raising for persons at risk of GBV (5000 persons) and how to deal with traumatized children victims of the current situation and general child protection (3000 persons). The total allocated budget for this project is USD 332,100. The targeted beneficiaries, including children and adults from different age groups, including seniors were involved in the design of the project, including the proposed activities. Additionally, both men and women from different targeted localities were involved in the project by identifying the needs and activities. During the implementation, beneficiaries will be involved in monitoring the project activities. The activities of the project are tailored according to the specific needs of children, taking into account their gender. To closely monitor and follow up on the results and achievements of the project, a Project Monitoring and Evaluation Plan will be developed at the start of the Action with the participation of the five partner organizations. The plan will comprise the indicators, assessments, and means of verification tools defined in the logical framework.

1 - Needs

Children in Gaza are growing up in a society permeated by restrictions and conflict-related violence which leaves them with a deep sense of insecurity for their future. Palestinian children older than ten years old living in Gaza have now witnessed three conflicts in their short lives. Children aged 11 or less have only known life in Gaza under closure, resulting in an increase in the number of children displaying high levels of stress-related symptoms, requiring additional family and community-based support and individualized interventions aiming at reducing vulnerability and increasing protection. A Child Protection Rapid Assessment was conducted by Tdh (2018) shows that violence against children is common across Gaza. The Assessment found psychosocial distress as a main child protection concern, manifested by aggressive behavior, unusual crying, and unwillingness to go to school. OCHA (2019) has identified providing mental health and PSS to children who could resort to negative coping mechanisms or could be the victims of harmful practices, including children survivors of GBV, victims of conflict, and children victims of abuse as key humanitarian responses. On top of the chronic crisis, since March 2018, Gaza has been witnessing a progressive deterioration of the political, social and security environments as a result of the "Great March of Return (GMR)" Demonstrations. The GMR Demonstrations have further compound to already fragile situation of the impoverished families making them unable to provide their children with basic life necessities. Up to August 31, 2019, a total of 46 children were killed by Israeli forces since the beginning of GMR Demonstrations, along with 15 children who were killed in other circumstances. Also, a total of 7947 children were injured as a result of GMR (7394 boys and 553 among girls (OCHA September 2019; MoH, 2019). Most injured children come from disadvantaged families, including poverty, high unemployment rate, and female-headed households (Tdh, 2019). Thus, injured children and their families suffer from an additional burden that could push them into deep impoverishment and could lead to a long-term impact on their overall wellbeing, including physical, mental, and spiritual. According to 2020 HNO, the needs of injured children due to GMR Demonstrations are increasing, despite the provided protection services. According to 2020 HNO, about 20,000 children are in need of MHPSS responses, along with 250,689 children already acutely vulnerable suffering from severe or moderate mental health disorders (Protection Cluster 2019). Proposed interventions under this Project aim to address the current dire need of child protection services for vulnerable children including with a particular focus on injured children, and GBV case management. Although, Tdh and partner organizations have been providing child protection services to children who have protection concerns, the need for child protection services is still high as Tdh and its partners have lists of children waiting receive services, long with children have been benefiting from services over the past year/s, including injured and working children who need long-term support and follow-up including provision of specialized rehabilitative service. The Project activities will cover needs of injured children to alleviate their distress through individual counseling, PSS, and referral to specialized services. It will also cover the need of working children through provision of remedial classes, reintegration into school, and PSS. Services will take into accounts the needs of boys and girls.

2 - Activities

The complexity of the current situation in the Gaza Strip requires multifaceted approaches including the provision of comprehensive child protection services to vulnerable children. This Action aims to increase access to protection services including case management, structured psychological support (PSS), individual counseling to vulnerable children who have child protection concerns, including children survivors of GBV and injured children during the demonstrations of the Great March of Return. The proposed activities will include the provision of responsive and remedial child protection responses, including case management, psychosocial support to 600 children, including 150 injured children during GMR, 150 children survivors of GBV, and 300 children who have different child protection concerns, including all child kinds of child abuse. In total, the provided protection services will target boys and girls equally, 300 boys and 300 girls. The provision of structured PSS to children who have been recently injured and children who have child protection concerns. According to child's age, different PSS manuals will be used, for instance, children below the age of 12, Psychosocial Rehabilitation of Children Affected by Armed Conflicts and/or Violence Guide will be used and Healing and Education through Art Guide will be used for children aged from 13 to 18 years old. Additionally, a total of 5000 persons (1000 men, 1000 women, 1500 boys, and 1500 girls) will benefit from awareness-raising for persons at risk of GBV, as a remedial activity to tackle the issue of GBV. The persons at risk will be out of school children, working children, children coming from poor families, and social problems such as drug abuse. Additionally, under this Action, awareness sessions on how to deal with traumatized children- victims of the current situation and general child protection will be conducted targeting 3000 members, including (1500 men and 1500 women). Protection services will be provided to all children in need, regardless of their age groups; services will be also provided to their families regardless of the gender and age of the head of the household. Additionally, for efficiency purposes, economic support, involving both, food vouchers and conditional restricted cash support, for injured children coming from poor families will be covered from another project submitted under HRP 2020. The provided activities will be coordinated with all relevant stakeholders, including the Protection Cluster, Education Cluster, Child Protection Area of Responsibilities, and relevant Ministries such as the Ministry of Social Development.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Tdh will fully respect, protect, and fulfil beneficiaries’ safety and fully respect their dignity. Tdh will achieve this by protecting beneficiaries’ personal and sensitive data such as names and Id numbers. As a good practice for Tdh, no action would be planned and/or implemented without the child’s agreement, under the supervision of her/his legal gradient. Additionally, Tdh will not publish or report any data could reveal any identification data, all publications/report will include only aggregated data. Tdh and partner organizations will also involve beneficiaries in as many decisions as possible, including monitoring the project implementation. All interventions will seek to avoid exacerbating the situation for children by ensuring properly planned and implemented interventions that take into account children’s and their family's needs to access to humanitarian services. All services will be provided in a safe protection environment that maintains and respect beneficiaries' rights, including respecting and maintaining their privacy and not demeaning them. Tdh has zero-tolerance to exposing beneficiaries to violence and or harmful practices, all measure will be taken into account to grate this, including full compliance to Tdh Child Safeguarding Policy (CSP), PSEA Policy, Code of Conduct and confidential mechanisms. All staff members are trained on the CSP, PSEA and the Code of Conduct and have signed the policies.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In respect of equality and impartiality, all identified cases, regardless of their gender, age groups, and ability status will receive protection services according to their assessed and identified needs. All Project beneficiaries will have equal access to services, including beneficiaries with disabilities and beneficiaries living in the Access Restricted Areas. Tdh will also target equally men and women without any regard to any discriminatory factors such as age. The proposed activities are based on the different needs' assessments done by the Tdh team in which beneficiaries engaged heavily and all their needs were included in the project activities. During the project design, beneficiaries were also consulted through conducting a series of FGDs and interviews. Beneficiaries will also be part of the project monitoring. Children were also involved in the assessment and design of the project and will be actively engaged in monitoring the project activities. Finally, as any advocacy product of this project, children will propose, design, and implement such products.
