Project Summary

With Libya in a state of protracted conflict, WFP’s Interim Country Strategic Plan (ICSP) aims to introduce programmes that would support the resilience of vulnerable communities across the country. The reduction in available migrant labour, both skilled and unskilled, combined with the instability of Government salaries, has left many Libyan families reliant on coping mechanisms in order to meet their basic food needs. In this context, WFP Libya is scaling-up the Food Assistance for Training pilot, which started in 2019, matching people in need with skills trainings that prepares them for available work in the local job markets. Conditional food assistance is provided as long as minimum attendance requirements are met, giving an immediate boost to food security. After the training, if successful, food security levels should be improved, with less reliance on coping strategies. Household resilience to future shocks should also be improved due to an improved asset base through greater skills, tools, and savings.

1 - Needs

With Libya in a state of protracted conflict, WFP’s Interim Country Strategic Plan (ICSP) aimed to introduce programmes that would support the resilience of vulnerable communities across the country. The reduction in available migrant labour, both skilled and unskilled, combined with the instability of Government salaries, has left many Libyan families reliant on coping mechanisms in order to meet their basic food needs. In order to cope with these challenges, the 2018 Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment reported that 52 percent of households reported spending their savings, 35 percent borrowing money and 35 percent purchasing food on credit. In addition, 22 percent of Libyan households reduced spending in education and health and 19 percentage reported having sold household assets. This situation is mainly related to the fact that households reported increased challenges to obtaining resources and income. 87percent of households have at least an adult working in the public sector and government salaries are paid with irregularities and often delivered on bank accounts which are difficult to access due to a liquidity crisis. In fact, more than half of the households reported receiving less than 50 percent of their salary in cash while being unable to withdraw the rest of their salaries from their bank account, which is the primary reported source of household income. In addition, 58 percent reported facing issues in obtaining enough money to meet their needs over the past 30 days.

2 - Activities

WFP's Food Assistance for Training always begins with a rapid labour market assessment in the specific target location, learning what skills vulnerable people tend to have in the area and what sort of jobs they are interested in. Businesses and relevant local authorities are also consulted at this stage to explore what jobs are currently available or needed in the local job market. WFP and its partners then asses whether appropriate facilities and trainers are available for supporting trainings on the jobs that are needed. Conflict sensitivity assessments are carried out at all stages of the programme cycle, and WFP is also exploring possible contributions to peace through the Food Assistance for Training programme. Once training courses are selected and planned, participants are chose from existing lists of people in need from various local sources, with a secondary screening for likely vulnerability to food insecurity. Courses are then implemented, lasting anywhere between four and twelve weeks, and usually including a soft skills component so that participants can learn the basics of business and marketing. Throughout the training, WFP and its partners work to connect participants to available jobs. Also during the training, food assistance is provided to participants on a monthly basis, as long as they attend at least 80 percent of classes. This boosts food security in the short-term, while the income earned through jobs after the programme helps reduce coping strategies in the medium-term. The aim is that long-term resilience to shocks is strengthened.

4 - Protection risk analysis

Safety:1) Safety of participants at risk travelling to and from the training venue (including GBV risk for women/girls)2) Programme is mixing up participants from different (rival) groups Dignity:3) Dissatisfaction with the transfer level/modality 4) Late delivery of entitlements, especially in the south of the country5) General sentiment that receiving food assistance is undignified Integrity:6) Training venues not easily accessible to certain groups (e.g. women), which may lead to exclusion

5 - Mitigation measures

Safety:1) Ensure the selection of training venues is informed by security considerations. Seek security advice on planned venue location/coordinates. 2) Engage local leaders and participants to improve understanding and acceptance of the programme, and manage expectations properly Dignity:3 & 4) Improve sensitization of participants at the start of the programme in order to better manage expectations5) Explore options to make the distribution process much more discrete, so that participants don’t feel humiliated Integrity:6) Ensure the selection of training venues, and training arrangements in general, do no inadvertently discourage participation

6.1 - Other accountability mechanisms

Affected populations are involved in course selection for Food Assistance for Training through community consultations and focus group discussions. WFP also collects feedback from graduating participants to learn what improvements can be made to the programme. In addition, the common feedback mechanism can also be used and contact details are shared with all participants.
