Project Summary

This program will bring sustainable water supplies, improved hygiene, better sanitation practices and better access to education to 15,000 crisis affected students, teachers and other people Mezam and Donga Mantung
H4BFwill deliver results through an Integrated Water Resources Management (IWRM) and school management approach over a 12 months period at a cost of $0.4 million. Specifically, the programme will:
1. Establish School Managememt and Water Resources Management Committees that represent the key stakeholders, and facilitate the development of a Water Resources Management Plans through a participatory planning process and through provision of expert input to analyse the feasibility of different options
2. Provide secure access to safe water through renovation and construction of water points and infrastructure for enhancing groundwater recharge, through an effective Water Resources Management Plans.
3. Promote improvements in hygiene and sanitation practices
4. Document and share lessons learnt within and outside Cameroon
H4BF has good experience of working in the WASH sector within Mezam and Donga Mantung and can mobilize rapidly due to strong links with the communities, traditional title holders and government. H4BF has worked together previously on other projects and have proven capability in learning from, supporting and implementing other projects within target communities. To ensure sustainability, the programme will work to strengthen sector management and co-ordination across the two divisions enabling cost-effective approaches applied by the programme to be continued and shared more widely in support of national goals.
A common M&E framework which considers the requirements for payment by results will be designed during the start up phase. Monitoring and evaluation will be employed as input for progress reports, and will also contribute to internal control and learning.
Monitoring will not only track the quantity and quality of outputs, assumptions and context factors, it will also disaggregate data by gender, age, and locality.
To achieve the above objectives, H4BF will:
Provide 35,000 teachers and learners across Mezam and Donga Mantung with learning and teaching materials in three years
Establish temporary safe and protective classrooms with adequate basic WASH facilities for both formal and non-formal education, targeting 5,000 children in Mezam and Donga Mantung respectively;
Train teachers and school-based management committees (SBMCs) on WASH, Psycho-social support and life skills to cope with the negative effects of the conflict;
• Empower communities through school management committees to protect education and assist in the establishment and maintenance of clean and child-friendly school environments;
• Support parents and guardians with WASH facilities to increase enrolment and retention rates;
• Provide sanitary kits to adolescent girls to encourage them to stay in school;
Provide WASH facilities materials to school going children and support peace/sports clubs in schools;
• Empower teachers and school management committee members with knowledge on referral mechanisms for issues related to WASH, GBV and child protection.
Advocate to donors and humanitarian actors to take up education development needs manifested in North West most especially in Mezam and Donga Mantung

1 - Besoins / Needs

A total of 4.4 million people in Cameroon need humanitarian assistance because of on-going and protracted crises rooted in conflict, displacement, and periodic disease outbreaks. Cameroon continues to report daily cases of COVID-19 infections. In the midst of the crisis in NWSW, with 8,500 persons reportedly displaced in August and about 530 persons returned to their areas of origin, 28% gender-based violence (GBV) cases of the survivors being children. As humanitarian actors report Malnutrition caseloads, access to WASH remain major concerns in IDP sites and Host communities, Cameroon as the WASH cluster us underfunded. Access to hygiene is very limited, only few schools have soap and even less have handwashing facilities. This probably contributes to poor hygiene practices, only few IDP students and pupil usually washed hands with soap, especially for communities schools hard to reach rural areas, and displaced populations. In addition, one out of ten female preferred menstrual material, which is a concern. More and more systems are in disrepair, and the water supply for large portions of the country, especially in host communities, is becoming inadequate, causing many people to use alternative and unsafe water sources such as canals. The most affected communities live in the pocket of “rainfed” agricultural lands and totally depend on precipitation not only for crop production but also for drinking and other domestic purposes. Most of the host communities never had access to piped water supply and relies on open water sources. Many communities' capacity to maintain and improve water and sanitation facilities remains poor due to the excessive reliance on centralized maintenance encouraged by the former system. Former health and hygiene education in schools focused on covering theory and memorization of facts from the official school curriculum, rather than on strengthening hygiene practices. H4BF needs accessment shows formal, culturally non-specific education failed to generate interest, particularly in host communities, worse still hard to reach and remote rural areas. This topic in schools has been largely disregarded. On the other hand, because of the water shortages and infrastructure problems mentioned above, many schools are spending time and energy just to collect water for daily survival. Many International Organizations and NGOs helped to the safe water supply and basic sanitation in many schools remains poor. Large numbers of rural schools in host communities and health centres lack access to sanitary facilities like latrines and hand washing facilities. Even before the crisis, environmental sanitation in rural areas was not a priority. Less than 5% of the total rural population had access to proper sewerage systems. Pit latrines are the commonly used sanitation facility. Poor maintenance of these facilities, especially at public places as schools and health centres has resulted in unacceptable hygienic conditions. Overall the PROBLEMS can be summarized as follows: • non-existent or insufficient water supply, sanitation and hand-washing facilities; • toilets or latrines that are not adapted to the needs of children, in particular girls; • broken, dirty and unsafe water supply, sanitation and hand-washing facilities; • children with poor hygiene and hand-washing practices. Under these conditions, schools become unsafe places where diseases are transmitted. One of the major problems faced by school age children is infection by parasites and flukes. These and other diseases, often sanitation related, obviously contribute to absenteeism, but there is a more hidden aspect: Poor health of children affects their ability to learn and therefore influences their perspective in life. Good health at school is essential for now and an investment for the future. • Most children are eager to learn. Schools can stimulate and support positive behavioral change in children.
