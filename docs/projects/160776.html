Project Summary

Health Link South Sudan proposed to provide a 12 months project on equitable access to safe WASH services, to IDPs, host communities, and returnees. The strategy is to integrate WASH into Health, Nutrition, and GBV program initiatives hence improving the overall outcome of the basic packages of services being provided to the most vulnerable communities under these key thematic humanitarian response pillars. A total of 381,550 project population targeted (95,388 Males: 286,163 Females, and 60,094 Girls: 20,031 Boys) in Juba, Torit, Ikotos, Lopa/Lafon, and Kapoeta South counties. According to the cluster strategic objectives the following major activities will be done:
1 - Integrate WASH in nutrition response through famine prevention minimum package.
1.1 - Health Link South Sudan intends to establish 5 OTP/SFP sites in 2 Sites -in Lopa/Lafon and 3 Sites -in Kapoeta South Counties.
1.2 - The projects aims to treat a total of 718 targeting every month 12 cases to be enrolled through direct admission and referrals from community by CNVs. Health Link will select and train 5 CNVs per sites established on OTP/SFP in Lopa/Lafon and Kapoeta South counties.
2 - Integrate WASH in health response to control outbreaks at wider community level
2.1 - Under this objective 32,218 individuals; Women, girls, boys, and men will have enabled access to clean safe drinking water (01 borehole) and provision of safe water tanks (5,000Ltrs), at 25 health facilities.
- 25- broken hand pumps will be repaired and Water treatment supplies will be sourced from the WASH Cluster and distributed to 5,600 household for water purification practices through volunteers.
- Health Link South Sudan will also procure and distribute 20 Ltrs tapped buckets and 20 Ltrs jerrycans to 5,600 Households to facilitate the storage of clean safe drinking water.
- Fencing of open waste disposal sites at selected health facilities- 25 health facilities
2.2 - The project will procure install and provides hand washing facilities (60 Ltrs tapped tanks) with stands at 5-schools, and 25 health facilities and 5- markets in Juba, Ikotos, Torit, Kapoeta south and Lopa/Lafon counties.
3 - Provide timely/sustainable, equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs, host communities and returnees.
3.1 - This project aims to deliver to a total of 32,218 women, girls, boys and men safe and clean drinking water through drilling, rehabilitations, and installation of water tanks at target schools and health facilities
3.2 - Menstrual kits will be procured and distributed to 6,514 women and girls, while 25 toilets in health facilities and 5 latrines in schools will be rehabilitated.
3.3 - Health Link South Sudan will select and train 200 volunteers across all the locations; 75 in Juba, 25 in Kapoeta South, 50 in Torit, 25 in Lopa/Lafon, and 25 in Ikotos counties.
- A planned total of 93,007 IDPs, host community, and returnees across these locations will be reached with health promotion messages.
3.4 - Health Link South Sudan will select and train a total of 75 volunteers to take up management of water source.
3.5 Number of elevated collective water points in ﬂood zone-
- Two (2) - water points within a floods zone will be constructed/rehabilitated.
4 - Mitigate WASH-related gender-based violence
4.1 - Construction of Twelve (12) toilets in 5- markets, 5- schools and 2- health facilities, and rehabilitate a total of 25 toilets in 25- health facilities.
- A total of 6,514 girls, women will be supplied with spot light for night access to toilet and water facilities especially.
4.2 - This project will purchase and procure safe sanitary kits and distribute them to a total of 6,514 girls and women to provide safe hygiene and protections during menstrual cycle and promote well-being while on study in schools.

1 - Needs

Four years after the 2016 deadly clashes between leaders in government and opposition calm had fairly return to the country but with little hope despite the signing of the revitalized peace agreement in Sep-2018. Many population in parts of the young nation are still under fear and with limited access to basic services. The humanitarian state of access to needy locations have still remained challenging. With the displacement of over 7 million people in and out of the country in the last two conflicts of 2013 and 2016, currently more than 1.4 million people are sheltering in the various IDP camps and PoC while some of the host communities are hiding and taking refuge in locations where services are not accessible if not all. The situation of the target counties are made worst with the rampant population’s movements, insecurity, malnutrition’s, disease outbreaks, and economic crises. With the heavy rain being experienced across the country, floods have heightened the chance of further disease outbreaks that would inflict more emergency state to the general populations if nothing urgent is sought in response. Example the most recent mapping by the GTFCC- WHO on South Sudan July 2017, ranked many of the target counties to be either very high risk or medium high risk for cholera outbreaks. The increasing rain led to several locations been reported damaged to shelters, water sources, crops and crucial productive assets. The rain have also damaged essential service infrastructure like sanitation facilities in the schools and health facilities, impacting women and children in particular. The floods have rendered many roads impassable, constraining people’s access to markets and humanitarians’ ability to move across affected areas, and disease risk especially diarrhoeal and respiratory diseases will continue to rise (OCHA-SOUTH SUDAN Situation Report- October 11th 2019).In July, 2019 Health Link conducted a comprehensive WASH assessment across 112 of its various functional health facilities in Juba, Ikotos, Budi, Magwi, and Lopa/Lafon and the result indicates a huge gap in the availability and capacity of WASH related services and facilities. This will compromise general care being given to clients at the various departments in the health facilities. Secondly it also confound to the existence and increase of gender based violence and abuse in situations where services are not proportionate to the population size and exacerbating protection issues at these locations. This is expected to worsen further as its anticipated high number of returnees resettling back from their various refuge in Uganda, DRC, Sudan, Ethiopia, and Kenya. WASH would always continue to be the prevailing conditions if not address and this would eventually lead to higher Diarrhoeal disease and subsequent high risk of an unnoticed outbreaks impacting negatively onto child health and nutrition’s amongst the already vulnerable populations of IDPs, host communities, and the returnees in the target project locations. This factors has prompted Health Link SS to devise strategic approaches of working other than as a sole health partner but to integrate WASH into all its programmatic areas of Health, Nutrition’s, and Protections/GBV and collaborate with various WASH clusters to deliver this services to the 80,126 target population of IDPs, host communities, and returnees.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Per every plan being designed and used in this project there will be strict adherence to national, and humanitarian standards to inclusion and exclusion. Health Link has a guiding policy on to the rights to access and utilizations of services such as rights to care, rights to information’s for decision making. In line with this, the project will treat and handles all groups of beneficiaries with due respect especially vulnerable categories such as pregnant women, disable person, and children, and the elderly to ensure maximum access and utilizations, hence protecting their rights. Secondly the project also aims to achieve highest level of transparency and accountability through setting up of feedback mechanisms both formal and informally to ensure complaints and dissatisfaction are reported and an appropriate actions taken to minimize damage and harms to beneficiaries. Formally telephone contact for reporting, referrals of cases of abuse of right will be establish between leadership of beneficiaries and project while at the same time informal means such as probe and search would be used to find out cases that evidently violates beneficiaries rights to access and service utilization. Lastly the project will be guided by the sphere standard tool on provision of WASH related services to ensure any deviations are reported before it’s too late.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The displacement affected populations and communities will be indirectly benefiting from the project. They would have direct benefit from service of water points, health facilities, and distribution of assistance to the returnees and IDP who are integrated into the communities following their return. While they provides (displacement affected community) amble environment they would by and large own the resources created by the project especially where IDPs have settled and who shall later be repatriated back to resettle in their home areas. These kind of resources include boreholes drilled and constructed, sanitary facilities constructed, water points protected and rehabilitated.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

This WASH interventions is meant to fit into the long term development goal of the nation. In line with this the project is align to the South Sudan health strategic plan for improved health and productivity under the national health policy by involving the community’s structure in the implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of the project. The project also aims to train water management committees to ensure sustainability of the project infrastructures delivered. In line with this most proportion of the management committees would comprised of Boma Health teams, opinion leaders, and other influential elders who are already members of the indigenous community and these are the lowest unit for administration in the government system that will enables program sustainability and development in the community.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Since there is an anticipated lasting peace being expected by all citizens this project would ensure the linkage to the peace process and dialogue to enable coexistence amongst different communities in the area. It’s also a stepping stone to especially returnees from Kenya, Uganda, and other neighboring countries as it prepares for them ground for the access to basic services that would help build them resilience and recovery mechanism over the coming years.This project has the capacity to provide for basic household hygiene items such as water storage, and provision of sanitary facility supplies which gives the household capacity to have in place better hygiene practices that can limits diarrhea diseases among children since there would be better excreta disposal methods available. The involvement of women in the leadership is a way forward to meet the strategic plan for employment of genders under the public services, and it’s also a means of project accountability and equity among beneficiaries. This is because every project where women formed the highest proportion of management and leadership structure there is prosperity and children welfare that will be taken care of, equally to persons with disability.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The environment for most humanitarian response in South Sudan is fairly safe at the moment. Health Link South Sudan is currently having a running program in this areas and the addition of this WASH related activities would not encounter a great challenge to the implementation of the project. Currently the staffs implementing development project of HPF-3 in these areas are diverse and a mix of both local and international staffs and has enabled the varsity of information’s for safety during project work. Also in line with this, the local community are highly linked through health unit management committees to the existing health facility operational and this has added advantage of strengthening the security and safety of activities implementation across the various locations. This project will be implemented with due respect to views of potential opinion leaders and decision makers and they would be regularly consulted during the project period.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Once this project is initiated the formation of the management committees is sought and this is a step forward to ensure a smooth transition from the donor dependent service provision to a less donor dependent community led initiatives that fits to the sustainability components. The formation of water management committees is a roadmap to ensure the community are strengthen to take up management of services into their own leadership with full participation, hence self-reliance. While sustainability is very hard sometimes to realize in the current economic situations, this project will work along potential opinion leaders, religious, representatives of special groups (disable persons) to ensure power dynamism and transition under the project management is cyclical with every members having equal chance to become the head of the management committees. This would foster ownership, and proactive participation from each individual members.Lastly since the delivery of the project goal would be guided by the national policy, this project will work closely with relevant line ministries to ensure a continued provision of oversight and technical guidance to the life of the project is achieved.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Leadership/Governance: Health Link South Sudan is committed to ensure liaison with all relevant line ministries to coordinate activities. This project will work along the existing development plan and strategies of the different ministries to ensure a coordinated approaches to implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the project is achieved over the period. This will reduce on the chances of duplications and inefficiencies of the project outcomes to the beneficiaries. Transparency: Health Link South Sudan will ensure access to and timely information to the WASH, Health, Nutrition Clusters and partners involved in the project executions as well as the beneficiaries through regular coordination and biweekly review meetings. Health Link South Sudan will therefore work in close collaboration and cooperation’s with partners involved in WASH, Nutrition’s, Health Clusters, SMoH/CHDs operating in the proposed project locationsFeedback and complaints: Regular stakeholders meetings, Key informant interview, focus group discussions, and peer group review meetings would be amongst key strategies to help gather feedback and complaints from special categories of beneficiaries during the project period.Participation: The implementation methodology of this project will provides a platform for full participation of women; girls, boys and men as well as people with disability in the project area through local community leadership structures such as Volunteer, Health Unit Management and Water Management Committees. Design, monitoring and evaluation: Health Link South Sudan together with the County Health Departments (CHDs) conducted meetings with the affected population prior to the design of this project and this will also be done at the beginning of the project implementation. The monitoring plan will focus on achieving the targets through vigorous on site visits/supervision and reporting on progress with pictorial evidence throughout the course of the implementation.
