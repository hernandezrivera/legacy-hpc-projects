Project Summary

In coordination with relevant authorities and partners, UNICEF will address the critical humanitarian needs of women and children as identified by the Health cluster and the Nutrition working group. This will be done through supporting the provision of health, and nutrition services, including the procurement of essential medical and nutrition supplies, and improving the quality of care through enhancing the capacities of health service providers at targeted fixed health facilities in area C, H2, and East Jerusalem.
The Project will contribute to two strategic objectives of the 2023 Humanitarian Response Plan: SO2: Ensure the availability of acceptable and quality essential healthcare services to vulnerable communities in Gaza and the West Bank. SO3: Strengthen the healthcare system's capacity to respond to emergencies and build community resilience to cope with the impact of current and future crises.
The project will serve approximately 130,000 people, including 55,423 boys and girls under five years of age, who will benefit from accessing primary health care in the targeted areas. Around 13,078 pregnant and lactating women and 10,000 newborns will receive maternal/neonatal life-saving services in the targeted facilities. About 150 male and female health professionals will acquire the skills and support to deliver essential lifesaving services. Moreover, 52,482 caregivers, including fathers, will benefit from accessing counselling on maternal nutrition and positive infant and young child feeding.
As part of mainstreaming its work on disability, UNICEF will ensure that the needs of children with disabilities are addressed throughout its programming. Through consultation meetings and accountability toward affected population mechanisms, UNICEF will also ensure the engagement and participation of persons with disabilities to define responses, including services location, design, and time.

1 - Needs

The armed conflict, frequent restrictions on the freedom of movement, and sporadic outbreaks of violence significantly impact the right to life, survival, and development of children and adolescents in the State of Palestine. It also has a negative impact on access to health services. Health care in the Gaza Strip, East Jerusalem, Area C, and Seam Zones is particularly challenging, with a multitude of restrictions, including access to specialized services, entry and transport of medical supplies, equipment, and spare parts, and the construction/rehabilitation of health facilities. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022 (HNO 2022), about 1.5 million Palestinians are in urgent need of Humanitarian assistance. Particularly for the West Bank, about 150,000 Palestinians in marginalized locations in Area C, East Jerusalem, Hebron/H2, and the closed area behind the Barrier continue to suffer from limited or no access to fixed healthcare services. They are, therefore, in need of mobile clinic service provision. The same Health Cluster HNO 2022 highlighted that 33% of the boys and girls in the West Bank are without access to Primary Health Care (PHC) services. Although 61% of the population received at least one vaccine dose, the COVID-19 pandemic added challenges to the delivery of essential healthcare services and is considered to remain a burden for the healthcare system, affecting newborns, young children, and high-risk pregnant and lactating women accessing these essential lifesaving services, the most due to the scale down of services. Furthermore, maternal and child health is deleteriously affected by the inconsistent supply of pharmaceuticals and other medical items, affecting the quality of care. Considering the context and the current situation, the nutrition risks and vulnerabilities amongst children under the age of five, pregnant women and mothers/caregivers of children under the age of 2, and adolescent girls are very high. Many indicators, such as breastfeeding and responsive feeding indicators, as well as diet diversity and anemia, require special attention, particularly in the context of ongoing protracted emergency and humanitarian crises and as a country affected by the double burden of malnutrition. According to MICS, only 40.6% of children in Palestine are first breastfed within one hour of birth (44.8% in West Bank). Only 43.3 % of children 0-5 months are exclusively breastfed (39.6% in West Bank). MICS also shows gaps in diets of infants and young children, with no more than 35% of children 6-23 months of age having a minimum adequate diet and only 44.7% having the minimum dietary diversity. In Palestine, the percentages differ, with 50.8% of children 6-23 months in the West Bank having the minimum dietary diversity. Furthermore, the maternal mortality ratio in West Bank reached 47.8 per 100,000 live births, and anaemia affects 25,5 % of pregnant women, while 18% of pregnant women and 14 % of lactating women are malnourished (MOH Annual Report 2021). UNICEF supported MICS 2020, highlighting that 8.5% of children under 5, of which more than 50% are boys, suffer from stunting (moderate and severe), with the percentage reaching 12.4% in the age group 18-23 months. The 2023 Health cluster humanitarian need overview reflects the need to provide quality and essential health and nutrition services, focusing on primary health level 2 and secondary level care fixed facilities, highlighting the need to enhance the capacities of the health service providers on obstetric and neonatal care, and nutrition services during emergencies across Palestine. In addition, it emphasized the importance of community engagement and of a behavioural change component in relation to MNCH with a specific focus on engaging men and boys who have the potential to play a pivotal role in promoting optimal health and caring practices in their communities and among their peers.

2 - Activities

UNICEF and relevant partners aim to improve access to critical health and nutrition lifesaving services among the most vulnerable communities in the West Bank through sustaining and expansion of coverage to reach the most vulnerable populations with an integrated approach and build on the humanitarian-development nexus approach. UNICEF and relevant partners are committed to ensuring that women, adolescent girls, newborns, and boys and girls can access quality and equitably life-saving and high-impact health and nutrition services. Activity 1.1: Provision of quality essential mother and child-friendly health services, focusing on primary health care level 2. UNICEF will work with relevant authorities and partners to ensure the availability and usage of quality packages and evidence-based lifesaving services for mothers, newborns, boys and girls, and adolescent girls at the targeted health facilities in H2, Area C, and East Jerusalem. This will include the provision and procurement of necessary supplies (medical equipment, drugs and consumables, prepositioning of medical drugs, and other medical supplies) to support the provision of services for neonates, boys, girls under-five, and pregnant lactating women (PLW). Emphasis will also be put on improving the quality of care by enhancing the capacities of health male and female professionals and caregivers on emergency and lifesaving skills for neonate, child, and maternal care. Activity 1.2: Provision of nutrition services for PLW , adolescent girls, and boys and girls under five, including targeted outreach and early intervention to high-risk pregnant and lactating women and boys and girls with developmental delays and disabilities. This will also include the provision of nutrition supplements. With relevant authorities and nutrition technical partners, UNICEF will support the prevention of undernutrition, micronutrient deficiencies, and anemia among pregnant women, lactating mothers, and adolescent girls by ensuring that they benefit from evidence-based, high-quality services. UNICEF will ensure leveraging resources through other partners to address issues related to maternal care, such as GBV and referral of cases to relevant services. More activities with relevant authorities and nutrition technical partners will involve community engagement, social and behavioral change interventions, and awareness. Activity 1.3: Promotion of optimal infant and young child feeding practices (IYCF), hygiene, and maternal nutrition among male and female caregivers and community members. UNICEF, with relevant partners, will promote infant and young child feeding practices, including training on responsive feeding and integrating the IYCF complementary feeding bowl, Infant and Young Child Feeding in Emergency (IYCF-E), and community management of acute malnutrition. UNICEF will ensure coordination with WHO (World Health Organization), UNFPA (United Nations Fund for Population) and other related partners, hence based on their respective areas and expertise, will manage different blocks when delivering the identified interventions, aiming to utilize the complementary approach under which certain elements of the service delivery will be governed by the appropriate stakeholders. The project will consider gender- and sensitive interventions designed and implemented in direct consultation with beneficiaries and authorities. The activities will also consider the needs of women, boys, and girls with disabilities for privacy and accessibility while receiving services at maternal and reproductive health facilities. During the project implementation, communication channels for feedback, complaints, and information sharing will be established through satisfaction surveys and recording goods of services delivered to the end-user so that assistance is provided per the needs and in adherence to the principles of "do no harm. These consultations include specific awareness sessions on the management of sensitive cases such as SEA (Sexual Exploitation Abuse). Through its partnerships with specialized community-based organizations, UNICEF ensures to target and address the needs of persons with disabilities identified in the intervention design phase. The agencies conduct focus group discussions to design interventions, and people with disabilities are among the vulnerable groups that will be consulted.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

UNICEF SoP has strategically invested in advancing AAP and PSEA portfolios and addressing potential risks related to UNICEF’s operations and misconduct of personnel involved in the delivery of UNICEF’s programme. On the AAP front, UNICEF is implementing the programme under the tittle ‘Voices of Change’ which has built and internal feedback mechanism able to receive feedback from programmes and specialized channels and respond to all claims in a timely and efficient manner. This mechanism collects and classifies claims, requests for information, request for assistance, positive feedback and sensitive information such as child protection, SEAH and SGBV reports which are then channelled through a confidential and data secured platform. On the PSEAH front, UNICEF SoP has been the leader of the establishment the interagency PSEAH Network which mirrors the internal efforts exerted to prevent risk of SEAH. UNICEF SoP has been mainstreaming PSEAH in all operations, training personnel standardized good practices, and develop protocols to handle SEAH cases. UNICEF SoP has also led the establishment of a Community-Based Complaint Mechanism (CBCM) that fosters a survivor’s centred approach and connects to AAP system(s). UNICEF SoP has worked with local implementing partners on capacity development and trained them on the core humanitarian standards and AAP and PSEA principles. UNICEF SoP also engaged the private sector (by outsourcing hotline agents for an Interagency Hotline with WFP), as well as the national system hotline (SAWA, an organization used as the interagency hotline for the PSEA network). UNICEF SoP has also worked closely with partners on their internal PSEA assessments and capacity building.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

UNICEF safeguards beneficiaries' rights to safe and dignified interventions. Consent of beneficiaries is usually considered and obtained through partners and implementing agencies joining training and community interventions. UNICEF ensures applying international standards and WHO standards for medical equipment and medical supplies procured. UNICEF developed feedback mechanisms from beneficiaries to ensure accountability toward affected populations and mitigate any risks identified by end-users. UNICEF also conducts end-user monitoring field visits to ensure the safety and quality of services and supplies provided.
