Project Summary

The project proposed by Plan International is aimed at providing temporary shelter and NFIs to IDPs and refugees women in the Logone-and-Chari and Mayo-Sava Divisions to ease their suffering and build their livelihood. The project targets men, women and children with a focus on women and children through activities such the construction of shelter for vulnerable persons, cash for work programs and strengthening their capacities in conflict resolutions. The estimated beneficiaries include 12000 women, men, girls and boys in the affected areas.

Besoins / Needs

According to IOM, IDPs and Returnees women, girls, men are boys in Logone-and-Chari and Mayo-Sava Divisions live under “inhabitable conditions,” reflecting observations of the current shelter situation. While basic household items and materials for transitional shelter are available in local markets, IDPs and Returnees particularly women and youths are without livelihood to purchase food, shelter materials and basic household items. WFP reported that malnutrition was unusually high for Children Under-5 and women, noted by prevalence of anemia. This poor health condition is attributed to the lack of purchasing power specifically for women with children unable to be employed full time due to childcare. An NRC assessment found out that shelters in IDP shelters were constructed mainly of rubbish, old cloth, plastic bags and reeds (November 2019). Though shelters are constructed in a traditional style, interviewed IDPs reported that the materials did not protect them from rains, insects or snake bites, and often easily destroyed in heavy wind, rains or fire. Women specifically reported an inability to protect themselves from sexual violence at night or looting (INTERSOS, 11.19). IOM assessment found out that several groups of IDPs reside in towns, resulting in haphazard settlement delinked from the few basic services that exist, raising protection issues for women/girls forced to move between camps for goods which has often resulted to conflicts. Land is scare and often not available for refugees and IDPs especially women to do minor farming or gardening to feed themselves or for sale. There is therefore a critical need for land use planning that engages female heads of household. Land tenure is highly political, and requires incorporation of conflict resolution and peacebuilding strategies to avoid further outbreak of violence.
