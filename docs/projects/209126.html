Project Summary

According to the 2024 Humanitarian Needs and Response plan (HNRP), the humanitarian needs in Sudan are widespread and severe. As indicated by the IMMAP situational analysis (2024), currently there are a total of 5.3 million people displaced inside Sudan. The current crisis is an addition of already existing inadequate WASH coverage, low investment in new facilities, high rate of non-functionality (25%), non-affordable water points, high level of open defecation and low hygiene awareness and practices.
As per the WASH cluster severity scale most of the RI target localities in Blue Nile state (BLNS) are within severity 4 and above, while for ND, Sennar and Red sea states the target localities are within severity 3 and 4. The IMMAP report, indicated that an estimated 7.4 million children lacked access to safe drinking water, putting them at risk of contracting waterborne diseases, which, alongside poor nutrition, can result in serious illness or death. As per the National MoH cholera report, the total number of cholera cases have surpassed 9,500 with a total of 264 deaths as of 13 Jan2024. The National Cholera Taskforce that was established in September assessed a number of WASH-related risk factors present in the affected areas, identifying the need for emergency WASH interventions and hygiene awareness campaigns (OCHA, 2023).
This project aims to provide emergency WASH assistance to 434,000beneficiaries (88,536women, 85,064 men, 132,804 girls, 127,596 boys) to address the scarcity of water supply, sanitation services and hygiene promotion for populations affected by armed conflicts and to contribute to the 2024 - WASH humanitarian Response Plan contributing to the revised 2024 strategic objective:SO1: Provide safe, timely, principled and gender-responsive life-saving humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable groups to reduce mortality and morbidity in areas with high severity of need or at risk of a further deterioration of critical humanitarian needs. The proposed RI’s emergency response will be in line the 2024 HNRP strategic objectives and with the cluster priority, which focus on life saving and will ensure restoration of WASH services through time. Essential WASH services will also be provided for the health and nutrition facilities.
RI will aim at rehabilitating mal-functioning rather than constructing new water facilities for utilizing the available resources effectively and reaching the targeted IDPs and host communities in a timely manner at the target locations. Renewable energy sources, like solarization of water yards, will be given priority during the phases, of project implementation in order to avail durable solutions and ensure continuous availability of safe water supply and reduction of the operation/maintenance costs. Strengthening and capacity building of WASH staff and the Water user committees (WUCs)will be part of the proposed interventions.
By the end of the phase of project implementation, RI will provide operation and maintenance/rehabilitation support for 57water points (10 water yard rehabilitation including solarization, 21handpump rehabilitations and 26 water yards operation and maintenance support) for the targeted localities of ND, BLN, SN, AJ, River Nile and Red sea states. In addition, water provision through water trucking will be provided for a number of health and nutrition facilities based on needs and in the absence of other options. Overall a total of 165,500individuals will benefit from the water supply service, 21,720 people with the Sanitation activities, and 434,000 people (88,536women, 85,064 men, 132,804 girls, 127,596 boys) will be reached through hygiene promotion activities in the target localities of North Darfur,Blue nile, Sennar, AjJazirah, River nile and Red Sea states.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

RI will mainstream minimum protection standards to ensure equitable and safe access to WASH programs and facilities. In addition to setting locations of community water points based on hydro-geologic conditions consideration, sociopolitical considerations will be factored in to ensure the location is accessible to the entire community, and that the WUC is representative. Safety and dignity are key considerations for sanitation activities, and each household will be involved in the construction of their emergency household latrines (EHH) with technical support from RI WASH team to account for the unique needs of its members, and it is worth to mention that the hygiene kits will incorporate MHM items. All beneficiaries will be selected according to set criteria and in consultation with the local authorities and the beneficiaries' community representatives. The hygiene promoters, CHWs and CNWs will receive hygiene promotion training that considers how to tailor messaging to diverse groups. RI will ensure that protection is mainstreamed throughout the project’s WASH activities. Priority will be given to the children, the disabled, female-headed households, and beneficiaries with cases of acute malnutrition. When selecting beneficiaries for latrines construction, beneficiary households meeting the vulnerability criteria will be prioritized. In the selection of sites for the rehabilitation of water yards and handpumps, priority will be given to locations with high percentages of vulnerable households. For the training of WUCs, RI will ensure that there is representation from vulnerable community groups and that the WUCs develop processes to ensure that vulnerable community members are able to access water points easily. Throughout the project planning and implementation phases, RI will continue to monitor the communities to ensure that vulnerable households are benefiting from the proposed WASH programme.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Relief International has a standard operating procedure (SOP) that guides the proper implementation and management of the accountability to community principles including safeguarding, to address issues such as PSEA and child abuse. RI is working to strengthen the accountability system to be able to capture the needs and voices of the program recipients at each stage of the project cycle management: need assessment, design, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation. RI has also rolled out its own Child Safeguarding Policy across the operation areas in order to reinforce existing policies such as Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) within RI and therefore increase and/or maintain a protective environment for the beneficiaries including children attending all RI-supported services. All staff receives mandatory training on this policy and sign the Safeguarding policy. Adding to existing feed back and complaint mechanisms, RI also aims to strengthen its accountability system by putting a mechanism which will strengthen inter - agency coordination to facilitate learning of best practices and ensure a strong and well coordinated referral mechanisms such as referrals of GBV cases. RI will enhance capacity of staff on management of complaints and feedback to inform project designing, improve service delivery, and improve quality of program for the project beneficiaries, and encourage recipients participation in each stage of the project management cycle. As part of the existing accountability system, RI also documents community satisfaction on services which enable informed decisions on program design and adjustment, budget allocations and future programming.
