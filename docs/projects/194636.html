Project Summary

The Common Transport Services (CTS) is a free-for-user service that transports key
humanitarian supplies on behalf of humanitarian actors. CTS serves as a critical link in
the supply chain, enabling products and materials that arrive from regional and
international suppliers to move onwards to partners located deep in the field rapidly. IOM
has been managing the CTS on behalf of the Logistics Cluster since 2011. During this
time, the project has significantly grown from delivering 2,000 MT of humanitarian cargo
in 2011 to a total of 17000 MT by the end of 2021, making a yearly average of 25000 MT
since the beginning, highlighting the increase in the use of road transport as the most
cost-efficient mode of transportation as access improves. IOM has continued to maintain
flexibility and adaptability to the operating environment in South Sudan. Through CTS,
IOM works with more than 100 unique agencies in key operational locations in Jonglei,
Unity, and Greater Upper Nile Region, in Humanitarian Logistics Hubs (Malakal, Bentiu),
and Rumbek, Bor, Juba, and Wau. IOM will maintain heavy vehicles and transport cargo
from the main ports of dispatch via air, road, and river including LC beyond responses and
will continue support for common warehouse management in key operational locations.
Through the effective management of the CTS, IOM aims to support organizations'
preparedness measures to mitigate risks associated with increased insecurity,
looting/theft of humanitarian supplies, critical access impediments to vulnerable people,
and reduce expensive air and road transport costs, which ultimately supports the timely
response to the needs of the affected populations. With this funding, IOM will directly
work with 100 plus humanitarian organizations located in different areas, enabling them
to deliver critical life-saving humanitarian services and assistance items.

1 - Needs

South Sudan is one of the most complex humanitarian operating environments globally,with persistent insecurity, poor infrastructure, and seasonal hazards. Poor roadinfrastructure in South Sudan is one of the main challenges for people in need of accessto life-saving assistance and services and for humanitarian organizations to reach thepeople. Access impediments cause delays and add substantial extra costs. Humanitarianneeds are dispersed across the country, often in areas challenging to access due togeographical remoteness and lack of infrastructure. Affected populations in hard-to-reachlocations face considerable protection risks, with many not provided with consistenthumanitarian assistance. Of the 2 million IDPs in South Sudan, most live in remote andhard-to-reach areas. Due to climate variability, South Sudan experienced exceptionallyshort dry seasons in 2019, 2020 and 2021. Heavy downpours, beginning as early as Apriland continuing as late as December, led to flooding and damaged major supply routes.This resulted in a shorter window for humanitarian agencies to preposition essentialcommodities ahead of the rainy season. Access to markets, which has already beenlimited by poor road infrastructure and transport options, further deteriorates during thelong rainy season. The majority of the rural population is far from or unable to reachmarkets for a large proportion of the year. In late 2021, access to many areas affected byflooding remained challenging, with roads washed away and locations inundated withwater. As such, major roads are expected to become physically accessible later thanusual. This has shortened the window for humanitarian cargo prepositioning during thedry season. Violence and insecurity could further impact access to life-saving assistanceand services and the ability of humanitarian organizations to reach populations in need.This could result in humanitarian organizations increasingly relying heavily on costly airtransport. In 2022, the humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that 8.9 millionpeople are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 on 2021 (HNO2022). It is imperative that support continues for the prepositioning of relief items in thefield by road where possible, which remains the most cost-effective means of delivery. TheCTS project will ensure cargo is transported from warehouses to airstrip/riverside docksfor onward transport to priority locations – Bentiu, Bor, Juba, Malakal, Rumbek, and Wau– or to priority locations through the Beyond Responses, which enables time-critical, lifesaving frontline multi-cluster activities through a cost-effective and reliable logisticsresources provision at ICCG prioritized counties. This project is key in assisting more than100 humanitarian organizations to deliver life-saving and emergency materials andproducts to areas where they are most needed across the country through a fleet of 18trucks, with common storage facilities where needed. This project ensures beneficiariescan safely access services and resources with dignity, including in hard-to-reach/newlyaccessible locations, and reduction in the scale of CTS will reduce the humanitarianresponse and put already vulnerable lives in danger. The CTS fleet of trucks ensures theprovision of cargo transportation by road, while optimizing cost-effectiveness, andimproving the efficiency and timeliness of the wider humanitarian response in SouthSudan. CTS also contributes towards making the overall humanitarian response 'as localas possible' through enabling equal and impartial access to assistance and services. Inthis context, CTS ensures the timely delivery of humanitarian supplies to priority locationsserved by Logistic Cluster partners, including international and national (I/NGOs) in SouthSudan. Therefore, CTS can be considered an enabler towards frontline activities beingcarried out by I/NGOs where the capacity exists.

2 - Project activities

The CTS will continue to provide support to more than 80 agencies representing allrelevant clusters in transport, airport/river port clearing, loader management, andwarehousing. In response to the logistics challenges faced in critical areas in SouthSudan, IOM (in collaboration with the Logistics Cluster) will continue to provide reliableand readily-available assets to transport humanitarian supplies intended to provide reliefto affected populations. IOM will maintain sufficient technical staffing, office, andmechanical workshop facilities to provide adequate logistics support to partners andcounterparts, through allowing the full-time availability of 18 dedicated trucks that willmeet the cargo movement capacity necessary to transport relief items to various NGOsand humanitarian agencies serving the critical areas in South Sudan. IOM will continue toposition the 18 trucks in Bor (3 trucks), Bentiu (4 trucks), Malakal (4 trucks), Rumbek (3trucks), Juba (2 trucks), and Wau (2 trucks), reflecting the operational requirements ofhumanitarian agencies. The geographic distribution of transport assets is based onpriorities and subject to change when necessary. IOM has continued to maintain flexibilityand adaptability to the operating environment in South Sudan. IOM will also adjust theservice provision to accommodate COVID-19 and flood response preparedness efforts.CTS will encompass six mutually reinforcing activities, as follows: 1. Provide commontransport services to the humanitarian community (INGOs, UN) to facilitate the rapiddeployment of essential humanitarian cargo to field locations in coordination withhumanitarian partners (through the ICWG). 2. Manage a fleet of 18 IOM managed CTStrucks to provide timely overland transport of humanitarian cargo, including air cargohandling, and maintain effective Fleet Management System. 3. Maintain and improveHumanitarian Logistics Hubs (Malakal and Bentiu), including increasing the storagecapacity of humanitarian actors operating in these locations. 4. Provide CommonWarehouse Management in Malakal and Bentiu, and other priority locations, namely Borand Rumbek. 5. Handle cargo effectively and professionally, through consolidation andloading at hubs, and cargo handling until the final destination. 6. Ensure adequate staff toaccompany project vehicles, monitor cargo handling, and maintain communications withthe control center in Juba.
