Project Summary

Nsannkang village is one of the most remote villages in Eyumojock sub-division of Manyu division in the Southwest Region of Cameroon. Before the crisis it had a population of about one thousand inhabitants. It is found in the equatorial rain forest and the main economic activities of the people are farming, fishing and trading, since it is a border village with Nigeria. It is a village with rich historical background, and notorious in history books because it is one of the villages where the Germans lost a battle during the colonial resistance. The inhabitants of Nsan-Akang use both the Franc CFA and the Naira from the Federal republic of Nigeria as currency of exchange. Topographically, it is characterized by gentle slopes accessible to the population. The road from Eyumojock central to Nsannkang is seasonal and almost inaccessible during the rainy seasson. There is a Government primary school, no health facility and no health providers present. The population rely on traditional remedies for treating common health issues like cold, fever and others, and on Reach Out’s mobile clinic, that visits the community every few months.
A catholic church and a Pentecostal church are present. River Manyu crosses Nsan-Akang, separates Cameroon from the Federal Republic of Nigeria, and serves as a source of water for domestic use and drinking.
Water sources:
Principally, two main sources of water were identified from this community, the cross-river and rain water. The population rely on water fetched from a River which is a large river separating this community from Nigeria. The River at Nsan is quite contagious as it has a slow flow rate and the river is a junction of many streams coming from other villages having a higher flow rate and thus increasing pollution downstream. Rain water is only accessible during the wet season which lasts only for 3months in a year, thus the people from the community only use the water from the river during the dry season for drinking, defecating, washing clothes, bathing, fishing and even burying individuals in accordance with certain cultural rites.
REACH OUT’S efforts in improving water condition
Reach Out through well-wishers have succeeded in ameliorating this situation at least to an extend through the construction of a community well equipped with mechanized manual pulley. This community well with characteristic depth of 9.5m and diameter of 1.2m realized through highly rocky soils guarantees water of good quality for the population of Nsannakang. However, dependency of an extremely large population on this lone water source is in many ways not sustainable. In accordance with GOAL number 6 of the sustainable development goals and sphere standards of all people having safe and equitable access to a sufficient quantity of water for drinking, cooking and personal and domestic hygiene and bringing public water points are sufficiently close to households to enable use of the minimum water requirement, there is urgent need for more water points to be able to serve this community of a growing 800persons, largely Returnees who had escaped their home at the peak of the crisis in the Northwest and Southwest of Cameroon.
In order to ameliorate this condition and put in place the respect of the following Sphere recommendations, there is need for more water points, reason why REACH OUT is designing this project for the construction of two hand pump community wells in this locality. This will ensure that:
 Average water use for drinking, cooking and personal hygiene in any household is at least 15 litres per person per day (see guidance notes 1-8).
 The maximum distance from any household to the nearest water point is 500 metres
 Queuing time at a water source is no more than 15 minutes
 It takes no more than three minutes to fill a 20-litre container
 Water sources and systems are maintained such that appropriate quantities of water are available consistently or on a regular basis

1 - Besoins / Needs

WASH assessment conducted baseline survey on knowledge, attitude, aptitude and practices of the people on sanitation and hygiene. This embodies garbage disposal, environmental and personal hygiene, and pest control and excreta disposition. Given the low flow rate of the river it relatively contains disposables and other garbages. This has led to contamination of water sources with outlined consequences like dysenteric symptoms and gastroenteritis being the main chief complaints in children less than 5years in this community.Expressed disatisfaction on the quality of water consummed is the cry for this communty.majority of the community gave; lack of finances, lack of knowledge on household treatment methods, inadequate technical know how and absence of adequate materials for treatment, and cultural and behavoiral characters as reasons for such practices. Not withstandinding, a slim fraction of the population, about 2% demonstrated good knowledge on proper handwashing technics and the use of boiling as a method of home treatment of water for consumption.Concerning the disposition of excreta, Just about 0.00% of the population have toilets. The rest use bushes and the river as means for disposition of excreta. Direct defecation into water and washed fecal materials from these bushes during and after rainfall is an enormous source of pollution with considerable health risks attributed to this. There is also the need for community sensitization and education through Hygiene promotion campaign.uncontrolled gabage disposal paves ways for the pests growth and spread of related diseases within this community.
