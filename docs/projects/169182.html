Project Summary

The project aims to expand cash based assistance for individuals affected by the Beirut blast through a 3 months-cycle of MPCA (food portion and basic needs) based on nutrition-sensitive vulnerability criteria as entry point for targeting, coupled with guidance to safeguard nutrition security for vulnerable HHs (special focus to infants and proper use of breastmilk subsitutes). The targeted population will be from all nationalities as per needs and will focus on caregivers of children under 2, pregnant and lactating women, female-headed households, older people and people with special needs who may not have the capacity to provide healthy foods for themselves and their families as well as needed health access with the exacerbated vulnerability caused by the blast. MPCA receivers will be 900HH for a total of 4.500individuals (Disagregated 0-2 years: 100 females; 100 males/ 3-17 years: 450 females; 450 males/ 18 to 64 years: 1900 females; 1000 males/ 65+ years: 300 females; 200 males). The proposed intervention builds on a planned Emergency Cash Assistance (ECA) program under Action Against Hunger for HHs with similar vulnerability criteria residing in Karantina and Mar Mkhayel (% of existing caseload) while including areas with the highest presence of socio-economically vulnerability such as Borj Hammoud and Dawra (Data source: OCHA, MapAction 13/08/20, LRF, UNICEF UN-Habitat).

1 - Needs

The food security situation in Lebanon before the explosion and damage of the port (which affected warehouses of food products such as wheat, corn, and barley) was already concerning and rapidly deteriorating. In recent months, the dramatic economic and fiscal crises, hyperinflation, and rising prices have had devastating effects on the economic vulnerabilities of Lebanese and refugee populations, leading to serious food-related coping mechanisms. The World Bank estimates that 50 percent of the Lebanese population would fall into poverty as a result of the economic and COVID-19 crises, and a recent WFP survey found that food has become a major source of concern, with 50 percent of Lebanese respondents saying over the past month they felt worried they would not have enough food to eat and nearly 1 out 3 Lebanese have been pushed into unemployment while 1 out of 5 had witnessed a reduction in salary. Even if many restaurants, markets and businesses of all kinds are gradually beginning to open to the public, the current access to cash and thus purchasing power of the vulnerable and explosion-affected population is very low, and the immediate needs are high. It highlights the need of food, basic health care items, materials to restore their income activities and apartments´ rent money for places to live in while they fix their homes, and other basic needs. Regarding nutritional needs, breastfeeding and complementary feeding counselling support to mothers of children under two has identified a key need due to uncontrolled distribution of breast-milk substitutes during the aftermath of the blast, lack of child care and breastfeeding awareness, lack of access to necessary and quality products as well as loss of possible support that families received before the event and which has ceased due to the emergency and displacement.

2 - Activities

A1.1.Provide 3-months MultiPurpose Cash equivalent to 200 USD/mo. (total of 600 USD). for 900 HH affected with exacerbated nutrition-specific vulnerabilities. AAH is closely monitoring transfer values and coordinating with the Cash TF, of which is active member, to ensure assistance is of value. Transfer mechanism will be through money transfer with providers already working with AAH who have multiple branches, allowing HH to withdraw the MPCA at the place and time of convenience. A1.2.Assess nutritional needs of affected vulnerable HH through rapid and full assessments as needed to evaluate type of nutrition support, based on SOPs developed and validated by the nutrition sub-WG. A1.3.Provide vulnerable HHs with nutrition support as needed, including breastfeeding, complementary feeding, access to food and utilization, and malnutrition, and link to infant feeding through nutrition awareness sessions, and support from nutrition officers, linking with lactation specialists and formula IF needed, and mother-mother support groups, and referrals, as needed.
