Project Summary

There is inadequate available shelter to provide temporary as well as long-term shelter solutions for IDPs and host community. Hence, in preparation for any influx situation, it is critical for UNHCR to have an adequate stock of emergency shelter kits as well as non-food items (NFI) prepositioned to intervene in any emergency while at the same time meeting the shelter needs of persons of concern already present in Sudan.
UNHCR will continue to provide emergency shelter materials and non-food items (NFI) to people who are newly displaced as a result of conflict or natural disaster. In situations of protracted displacement faced with natural disaster such as food or conflict situation, UNHCR will provide transitional shelter or durable shelter trough cash or in kind materials according to market assessments exploring direct cash or vouchers according to what is more economically efficient for the locality and distribution. The overall objective is to improve the shelter conditions of the POC by providing them trainings on build back better and shelter upgrades to be more resilient to natural hazards like floods or fire and reduce the risk of mayor loss of basic household items and shelter destruction. UNHCR will also support returnees and protected IDPs living in substandard shelters to improve their shelter conditions with the provision of shelter upgrades/repair activities and provision of durable shelter.

1 - Needs

The 2021HNO identifies 1.2 million people in need of Shelter and Non-Food Items (S/NFI) assistance in Sudan, including protracted and newly displaced people, returnees, and vulnerable host communities across 36 key localities in Darfur, the Kordofans and Blue Nile states, in addition to supporting flood affected communities in the Rest of Sudan including Khartoum State and White Nile state. Shelter and NFI needs have increased in 2020, despite the decline in armed conflict since 2015, and corresponding reduction in new displacement. New displacement has occurred in West Darfur and eastern Sudan due to inter-tribal conflict among the Bani Amer and Nuba tribes. The estimate of the number of IDPs was revised upward from 1.6 million in 2019 to 2.55 million in 2020. New IDPs that were previously not known to the sector were found thanks to assessments that reached new areas including non-governmental controlled areas.The ongoing economic crisis continues to be a key driver of deteriorating humanitarian needs, which have been exacerbated by COVID-19. Control and containment measures increase the cost and time taken to delivering shelter and NFI aid. Flooding is a seasonal occurrence in Sudan and contributes to shelter and NFI needs. This year was particularly difficult as floods affected over 830,000 people across the country, with the highest water levels recorded in the Khartoum State, Sennar, Blue Nile, White Nile in a century. Heavy rains caused damage to houses, livestock, farmland, infrastructure, and services. Flood water and damage was record high in all States in Sudan. Inflation and currency depreciation have led to shortages of fuel and cash, price increases of imported key commodities, a a reduced household purchasing power.The need for adequate shelter has resulted in additional income opportunities including brick making, weaving of traditional mats, provision of fuel-efficient stoves and basic repairs. However, there are also negative coping mechanisms used by people in need of other services, such as health or education. There is a need for durable solutions (return and urban integration) for IDPs and returnees alike including a need to provide environmentally friendly transitional shelters, together with community training on building techniques and livelihood opportunities to promote self-reliance and resilience.While ensuring the availability of basic services in their area of choice and advocating for House, Land and Property rights, displaced people (new IDPs, protracted displaced people, returnees) need shelter and NFIs. These items help restore a minimal sense of dignity and protection against exposure to the elements, mitigate health risks and provide some privacy and security to those in need. More sustainable shelter solutions for returnees and protracted IDPs affected by not only recent conflict but also natural hazards need to be addressed.

2 - Activities/Outputs

UNHCR activities for 2021 will include overall need assessments, verification of beneficiaries with partners and government counterparts, planning , dispatch and distribution of shelter &NFIs and post distribution monitoring through PDM partner or counterpart. First) preparedness will be ensured by timely procurement and pre-positioning of key NFI kits in strategic locations by UNHCR as the manager for the NFI Core Pipeline on behalf of the Shelter/NFI Sector in Sudan. This will support front line responders in hot-spots receiving IDPs, and in newly accessed locations and communities affected by conflict, floods or fires. Second) UNHCR will also support beneficiaries in those areas chronically affected by floods to organize preparedness committees and do sensitization and awareness on disaster preparedness like techniques and guidelines to improve their settlements against fire outbakes or floods. This could also include trainings on shelter construction techniques that could support the communities to improve their shelters to reduce the risk of fire or include their water collection and build back better techniques. Third) UNHCR comprehensive response is to ensure timely emergency shelter and NFI assistance is provided to people affected by crisis or natural disasters. Emergency shelter and NFIs materials are purchased internationally and/or locally by UNHCR supply unit and delivered to the UNHCR warehouses in Khartoum (Central Warehouse), Kosti(supporting White Nile State),El Obeid (supporting South and West Kordofan), and Nyala (supporting East Darfur) and onward to partners at the various distribution locations as and when required. Given the limitation of resources, UNHCR will strengthen coordination of resources with partners to ensure coverage of emergency shelter & NFI needs. As sector lead UNCHR is flexible with the localities targeted to be able to reach everyone in need in case a new displacement of crises ocures and there is lack of resources or parnters in the area. Also, UNHCR is presence in the main IDP camps and settlements that receive regular influx of displaced. While looking for shelter support IDPs shelter themselves in gathering points that usually are government facilities increasing the tension with the host commuinty and afecting the functionality of the institutions like schools. In orther to be able to support to government caunterparts when this situations occure, UNCHR is willing to build collective shelters in the key displacement localities like for instance Kriding camps in El Geneina. Fourth) UNHCR will continue ongoing activities that support durable solutions and support the transition from dependency to self-reliance. This includes income-generating and livelihood activities, including training on basic construction, skills, production of domestic items and construction of fuel-efficient stoves. The need to continue recovery and longer-term development activities in Darfur is considered a priority and therefore shelter upgrades/repair and construction of durable shelter will be needs based and will prioritize identified people with specific needs (PSN) and most vulnerable individuals (EVIs) in line with the protection vulnerability criteria. The modalities of the distribution can be either in kind or cash depending on the context of the locality, specific response plan and market assessments. Feasibility analysis will consider prevailing market conditions; the availability of financial services; the preferences of the affected population; the safety and security of beneficiaries and host community; and collateral effects on social cohesion. Simultaneous community training on building techniques will be conducted in line with humanitarian early recovery activities, to promote self-reliance and provide some livelihood opportunities.

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

UNHCR aims to strengthen protection, gender mainstreaming and accountability to affected populations as they link with the principle of “do no harm”. The particular needs of such individuals will be reflected in NFI assessments, programme design, implementation and monitoring and evaluation. In these areas, the sector will ensure the following elements are taken into account: (1) safety, security, and dignity; (2) equitable access; (3) accountability through community-based feedback mechanisms, with information collected based on on-site and post-distribution monitoring that will feed into the sector’s program cycle; and (4) sharing information, consultation, participation and community empowerment. The sector will work with partners to strengthen the implementation of the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse through awareness-raising for both partners and suppliers. UNHCR within its capacity as Shelter/NFI Sector lead will advocate to draw attention to shelter issues such as housing, land and property rights, particularly for returnees and/or integrated IDPs. HLP due diligence tools will be set in place with cooperation of the HLP WG to harmonize the shelter responses among partners ensuring that before building durable shelter partners will ensure in cooperation with the communities that beneficiaries’ land if free of claim and refer those conflictive cases to protection partners providing ICLA support through a referral pathway. This is to reduce the risk of further harm and mainstream protection on the shelter construction programs.
