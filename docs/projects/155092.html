Objectives

Objective 1: To reduce excess mortality and morbidity among boys and girls under 5 years old, PLW and other vulnerable groups through provision of life-saving interventions to treat Severe and Moderate Acute Malnutrition. Objective 2: To reduce the burden of malnutrition among boys, girls, PLWs and other vulnerable groups through the strengthening and scale up of malnutrition prevention interventions.Objective 3: To strengthen the collective nutrition sector response through timely collection and analysis of nutrition data, information management and effective coordination

Needs

High level of undernutrition observed among the refugees are triggered by pre-existing vulnerabilities such as consistent high level of stunting and anaemia, food insecurity. Furthermore, inappropriate hygiene and sanitation conditions, limited access to safe water as well as poor living conditions contribute to recurring communicable disease outbreaks resulting in increased risk of negative nutrition outcomes among the boys, girls and other most vulnerable young boys, girls and adolescent girls in all camps. Round 2 nutrition survey findings showed that the GAM prevalence of both Nayapara Registered camps and makeshift settlements has been decreased between October 2017 and May 2018 from 18.2% to 12.0% (source: SMART survey) but remains above the UNHCR standard of <10% GAM prevalence. Equal GAM prevalence was found between girls and boys. The survey revealed furthermore that gaps remain in appropriate IYCF practises, only 50% of women are breastfeeding exclusively in makeshift camps. Similarly, anemia in children aged 6-23 months is with 52% above the emergency threshold in all camps. Furthermore, negative coping mechanisms have also been reported by the food security sectors across all camps setting. These include selling of assets s and humanitarian relief items to obtain cash, a practise that is likely to negatively impact the household food security when the assets are depleted. The nutritional needs of older adults are often overlooked in a crisis situation. Though no specific assessment was conducted, BSFP programmes implemented by UNHCR are including older adults with chronic diseases or specific needs. UNHCR is currently implementing nutrition programmes through 22 OTPs, 4 TSFP, 4 BSFP and 3 SC. In the first 10 months of 2018, a total of 15,672 acute malnourished children were treated. Cure rate reach above 90%, a key concerns remains however low programme coverage. The SLEAC coverage assessment revealed that only one third of all eligible children are enrolled in nutrition programmes (27% OTP, 34% TSFP). No significant difference was found in enrolment rates for girls and boys. Key underlying causes for low programme uptake are lack of awareness on malnutrition and service availability which can be directly attributed to weak community outreach activities. In 2019, UNHCR will therefore strengthen the prevention activities like community outreach activities such as mother to mother support groups, infant and young child feeding practice. A total 164,804 under five children, 71,614 adolescent girls and 46,366 pregnant and lactating mothers needs different nutritional support as per the sectors need analysis. UNHCR is targeting 68801 boys and girls aged 6-59 months, 27380 pregnant and lactating mothers and 25800 adolescent girls through treatment and prevention related nutrition activities in different camps which will be implemented through three international nutrition partners.

Activities or outputs

Project Activities: The project activities will be implemented by UNHCR in partnership with three partners 1) Action Against Hunger (ACF/ AAH) 2) Foundation Terre Des Hommes (Tdh) and 3) Save the Children International (SCI). The project will seek to meet the needs identified under the Nutrition sector needs assessment and is aligned to the sector strategic objectives. Specifically, project activities include;Treatment of acute malnutrition (objective 1): 1. Screening, detection and referral of acutely malnourished children aged 6-59 months to the nutrition centre.• Hiring and training of 600 community nutrition volunteers (CNV) to conduct community based screening at the household level. • Monthly nutrition screening during growth monitoring points• Screening of new arrivals at transit centre• Procurement of Equipment and supplies for screening 2. Implementation of stabilization centres for inpatient management of severe acute malnutrition with medical complications and outpatient therapeutic feeding program (OTP) and targeted supplementary feeding program (TSFP) for the treatment of severe and moderate acutely malnourished children under 5.• Construction and rehabilitation of 2 OTPs • Operation and maintenance of existing 20 OTPs, 4 TSFPs and 3 SCs including hiring of 420 staff, capacity building • Home visits by nutrition staff for non-responders, defaulter tracing through CNVs, • Procurement of equipment and therapeutic Nutrition product supplies including plumpy nut/RUTF , therapeutic milk (F100 and F75 ) , resomal Prevention of acute malnutrition (Objective 2):1. Implementation of Blanket Supplementary Feeding Program (BSFP) targeting non-malnourished children 6-59months and pregnant and lactating women (PLWS) and persons with specific needs (e.g chronic diseases, older adults).• Referral of beneficiaries to other preventive services (e.g. ANC, PNC, EPI) 2. Behaviour change communication through community- and facility based nutrition education, provision of culturally appropriated IEC materials 3. Micronutrient supplementation (MNP) for children aged 6-59 months and Iron folic acid (IFA) supplements for PLWs and adolescent girls. 4. Implementation of IYCF-E activities in established in the IYCF-e corners located in the nutrition centres and community to ensure awareness and adoption of appropriate feeding practices. Through the following :• Individual counselling sessions for PLW• Health education and group counselling sessions. • Increase awareness of IYCF through Action oriented sessions with community groups, family influential members and PLWs on 1000-days approach. • Establishment of community based mother to mother support groups (MTMSGS) to enable experience sharing among the pregnant and lactating women and caregivers of children under 2 years. • Cooking demonstration using locally available food to enhance and support complementary feeding among children 6-23 months. • Support the behaviour change communication strategy to promote essential nutrition actions on “basic nutrition, IYCF, care practices and hygiene promotion” in centre and community.5. Provision of hot meals in transit centre to all new arrivalsCoordination, nutrition information management and Nutrition Assessments (Objective 3): 1. Ensure reporting of nutrition data and analysis of the trends in admissions of SAM and MAM. 2. Conduct the annual nutrition survey based on SENS/SMART guidelines to strengthen effective nutrition emergency response planning, implementation, monitoring and come up with recommendations to support nutrition programming. • Hiring of the survey consultant and survey teams. • Technical support to the survey methodology and survey results validation. 3. Conduct IYCF assessment to understand contributing factors for acute malnutrition.
