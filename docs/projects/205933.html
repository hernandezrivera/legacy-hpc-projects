Project Summary

South Sudan is grappling with a profound health crisis driven by a combination of factors, encompassing climate change, protracted conflicts, inter-communal fighting, outbreak of infectious diseases and regional conflict. Thousands have been displaced within the country while the number of returnees from Sudan has exceeded 360,000 individuals of whom over 50 percent are women and girls.
According to the Humanitarian Needs Response Plan (HNRP, 2023), 8.9 million individuals are in urgent need of humanitarian assistance. This assistance is particularly crucial in regions that have been severely affected by these challenges. The health cluster, in collaboration with its partners, has set ambitious goals to reach 3.2 million people with life-saving health services of whom 384,00 are women of reproductive age. This target population includes those internally displaced, refugees from neighbouring nations, and over half a million returnees from Sudan.
UNFPA and its implementing partners intend to reach this target population using two main objectives:
1) Improve equitable access to comprehensive, quality health care services covering maternal, child, and adolescent health, as well as Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH).
2) Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen the recovery of the health system and coping mechanisms.
UNFPA through implementing partners will provide integrated quality services to 320,000 women of reproductive age, with 55,682 expected live births, among which 8,352 are anticipated to occur in health centres. Additionally, UNFPA will target 64,000 adults living with a sexually transmitted infection (STI). The full implementation of MISP and universal access to Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (EmONC) services is projected to avert 504 maternal deaths.
In the course of the implementation, UNFPA will champion the delivery of the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) in acute emergencies and tailoring sexual and reproductive health (SRH) care to the specific needs of protracted crisis situations. The focus of this will revolve around the six key components of the MISP, which include (1) leading the coordination of MISP activities at both the national/field level in collaboration with the Ministry of Health 2)prevention of sexual violence, and catering to the needs of survivors (3) preventing the transmission of and reduce morbidity and mortality due to HIV and other Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs) (4) prevention of excess maternal and newborn morbidity and mortality (5) Prevention of unintended pregnancies and (6) Planning for comprehensive SRH services.
The main interventions to achieve the project purpose include, among others:
1) Delivering MISP services, which involve providing clinical management for rape for survivors of sexual violence, case management, psychosocial support (PSS) through individual and group counselling, HIV/STI prevention and treatment services, maternal healthcare services including antenatal, Labor, and postnatal care services, facilitating referrals for obstetric services, and increasing access to reliable contraception. These efforts will also encompass community health education and awareness campaigns.
2) Procuring and distributing interagency reproductive health kits to ensure access to essential medical supplies.
3) Enhanced management and coordination between humanitarian and development programs to aid the delivery of comprehensive services.
UNFPA and its implementing partners will also address the cross-cutting issues including inclusion of age and disability; mainstreaming protection and Gender-based Violence (GBV) including Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA); Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MPHSS); localization and community engagement.

1. Needs

South Sudan is significant vulnerabilities, particularly in terms of its social indicators for women and girls. The maternal mortality ratio is estimated at 1,150 per 100,000 live births according to United Nations data from 2017. The United Nations Cooperation Framework report for 2020 highlighted that only 19 percent of births in the country were attended by skilled health personnel, and there was a notably high rate of home deliveries at 87 percent. Access to emergency obstetric care services is limited, with only 40 percent of health facilities in operational condition, and international organizations play a significant role in providing healthcare services.These poor health indicators can be attributed to various factors, including an underfunded and weak healthcare system, with less than 2 percent of the national annual budget allocated to health. Additionally, there are challenges in implementing effective health policies, and severe shortages in healthcare personnel, particularly doctors (with just one doctor per 65,574 people) and midwives (with one midwife for every 39,088 people).Climate change has impacted the country's already fragile health system. Climate change has led to more frequent and severe weather events, such as floods and droughts, which disrupted healthcare services and infrastructure making essential medical care inaccessible. Additionally, climate change has exacerbated the spread of diseases. The influx of returnees from Sudan and Ethiopia in large numbers has also placed additional strain on the country's already overburdened health system. The returnees face inadequate healthcare facilities and limited access to essential services, contributing to the country's poor health indicators. This led to overcrowding in health facilities, making it challenging for the existing healthcare infrastructure to meet the increased demand. GBV continues to be the biggest protection concern in South Sudan. Factors contributing to GBV include economic stress, isolation, and increased tension within households due to the challenging economic situation. Societal norms, gender inequalities, and inadequate legal and support systems further compound the issue. The health cluster has identified approximately 8.9 million individuals in need of healthcare services. However, the health cluster's specific target is to provide services and information to approximately 3.2 million people in need. This target encompasses 768,000 women aged 15-49, 544,000 adolescent girls, and 192,000 adolescent boys aged 10-19 who require healthcare in 2024. UNFPA anticipates that only one-third of these women and adolescents will have access to high-quality sexual and reproductive health services. UNFPA plans to reach 256,000 women in the reproductive health age group (15-49 years) and 181,333 adolescents (comprising 181,333 girls and 64,000 boys), totalling 245,333 beneficiaries.To address the healthcare needs of vulnerable populations, UNFPA will maintain its pivotal role in coordinating sexual and reproductive health services during emergencies across the nation, utilizing both national and subnational coordination mechanisms. This collaboration with other sexual and reproductive health partners is geared towards ensuring the provision of high-quality services. There will be a focus on enhancing skilled birth attendance services by providing Interagency Reproductive Health Kits and enhancing the expertise of healthcare professionals. Additionally, the referral systems for EmONC will undergo strengthening to enhance the efficiency of referrals to Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (CEmONC) centres. UNFPA will also integrate GBV services into routine healthcare, with a particular focus on the treatment of survivors of sexual assault. Furthermore, as the core pipeline lead for the inter-agency RH Kits, UNFPA will support with Health Cluster partners with inter-agency RH Kits.

2. Project activities

The objective of this project is to enhance the equitable accessibility of essential, high-quality healthcare services for populations affected by crises. These services encompass maternal, child, and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, the treatment of common illnesses, disability care, mental health, and psychosocial support (MHPSS), as well as GBV healthcare. The following activities have been designed to guarantee the availability and accessibility of reproductive health (RH) and GBV services across all affected locations:1. Deliver life-saving essential integrated GBV and sexual and reproductive healthcare services through both static healthcare facilities (hospitals, primary healthcare centers, and units) and mobile healthcare services. This will involve procuring, distribution and pre-positioning inter-agency Reproductive Health Kits; training healthcare workers on their use and reporting, supporting referral services, and conducting community education and awareness programs. 2. Offer technical and operational support to healthcare facilities by conducting supportive supervision visits to ensure the quality delivery of healthcare services and accountability to the affected population. Comprehensive technical and operational support to implementing partners will be provided through regular supportive supervision visits, focusing on enhancing the quality of healthcare services and fostering accountability to the affected population. This will involve collaborating closely with facility staff to identify and address any operational challenges, offering guidance on best practices, ensuring compliance with established protocols, and promoting patient-centric care. These visits will also provide a platform for open communication, feedback, and capacity-building, fostering a culture of continuous improvement and responsive healthcare delivery.3. Strengthen the coordination of SRH Technical working group at national and sub-national levels. UNFPA will enhance SRH coordination by actively engaging in preparedness measures to ensure readiness for potential new crises. This will involve sustaining a robust SRH In emergency coordination network and communication channels to facilitate the seamless delivery of MISP services, including reproductive health, GBV, and other essential care, during crises. Ensure that all stakeholders, including government Ministries at national and state levels, NGOs, and healthcare providers, are well-trained and equipped to respond swiftly and effectively to emergent situations. Continuously updating and adapting response protocols and resources based on lessons learned, emerging threats, and evolving needs, to ensure a timely and comprehensive MISP service provision in crisis-prone regions.
