Project Summary

The overall objective is:“To improve the health of vulnerable mothers and children in AreaC in Hebron and Nablus through community preventive health and nutrition interventionservices.”The specific objectives: • Vulnerable children <5 , neonates , pregnant andlactating women have access to life sustaining interventions Mental health andpsychosocial services are provided to vulnerable mothers and to vulnerable children atschools The project responds to the First cluster objective “Ensure the availability,accessibility, acceptability and quality of essential lifesaving health services to the mostvulnerable communities in Gaza and the West Bank /EJ including through health systemstrengthening, preparedness and community resilience building.” The selectedinterventions will be implemented at two levels; households and community; using a multidisciplinary health approach. WV community health workers, and MOH service providerswill join their efforts for optimal outcomes. At community level, WV community healthworkers will work with mothers to change behaviors and practices mainly in infant caresuch as breast feeding practices, complementary feeding, supplement intake , recognitionof danger signs and providing psychosocial support to mothers showing signs of distress.In partnership with MoH, child friendly facility approach for infant and young child caringand feeding practices will be integrated at the field level to provide further technicalsupport to mothers and children. This project will complement the services provided by the mobile clinics service providers, the provided resources will improve the quality of services , WV community health workers will target mothers and their children with responsive and ongoing support , they will reach to those in the hard to reach areas , they will be the linkage between their communities and the service provides ( MOH,PMRS) to ensure proper service utilization ,ensure referral when needed, support services providers and provide individual support to those in need (ex :support mother who are unable to breastfed).At school level, psychological first aid interventions will be done through training of teachers to provide assistance to affected children.Extracurricular activities will be done at school level to overcome psychological stress ofaffected children. The project will ensure proper utilization of health and mental healthservices provided by different partners, mainly the MOH.Building in WV experience in the health sector ,we are proposing new expanded services , part of the proposed approach within project was evaluated by WV (specifically the targeted interventions to the mothers) ,results showed significant changes among mother not only in regards to their knowledge but also critical lifesaving behaviors such as illness management ,feeding practices ,service utilization and supplementation , ( evaluation results are available to be shared ,data was shared recently at the global nutrition cluster meeting ) ,those changes were reflected on mothers and children’s health.

Needs

Needs for this project were identified through referring to the following sources:• WV health and nutrition assessment for 25 area C communities in Nablus and Hebron , a quantitative assessment was conducted through household survey for a sample of 427 on; Mothers Knowledge, attitudes and practices on issues related to maternal, newborn and child health and nutrition critical issues, and on Mental health for mothers. Support on data collection and analysis was provided from consultants form Al Quds University, the collected data included the communities that we will target under this project. • Data base from WV health interventions in the targeted communities in Area C and data collected from WV trained community health workers•Review data available at MoH on topics including anemia prevalence among mothers and children, supplementation availability and others. • WV school assessment in cooperation with the MOE ( September 2018 ) it was conducted for area C communities in Hebron, that included data on number of affected students, availability of counselors, students with disabilities, protection issues and violations . The selected areas are specified as the most in need due to limited interventions from organizations and PA. Number of these villages, mainly in Hebron, are either adjacent to settlements or by bass roads that makes access to health services difficult mainly during political escalation, closure of roads, demolition orders and other Israeli regimes, Access to essential health services is very limited, the 17 selected communities that will be covered under this project have no access to structured health facilities, they only receive limited services through mobile clinics. Such limitation contributes little knowledge and poor practices on issues related to mother care, poor infant and young child feeding (IYCF) and new born caring practices. In Hebron; exclusive breastfeeding is practiced by only 3.4%, only 8.1% are practicing the correct management during diarrhea, 47% of the mothers are not giving their children iron supplements on regular basis and 54.4% are not giving Vit A,D regularly , only 26.8% of mothers are taking iron on regular basis . In Nablus communities 45.6% of mothers are not taking folic acid, only 4.8 % followed proper correct management during diarrhea among children. In Nablus communities only 5% of mother are practicing exclusive breastfeeding , only 8.8% are practicing diarrhea correct management ,37% of mother are not taking iron, folate on regular basis. Anemia prevalence among mothers and children in all the targeted areas is high, among children it’s 40.7% in Hebron and 48.3% % in Nablus whereas among mothers it’s 39% in Hebron and 29.6% in Nablus (WV assessment,2018). The majority of the interviewed mothers (95%) mentioned that they were not aware of maternal and infants danger signs and don’t seek any care once these signs appear .They also mentioned introducing unappropriated complementary meals and at very early age (WV assessment,2018). According to WV recent research regarding mental health, the data showed that the prevalence rate of postpartum depression in Nablus communities is around 20% (WV assessment,2018). Based on OCHA vulnerable school mapping and recent WV assessment a number of schools are frequently being affected by different kinds of violations , those schools were selected and prioritized they will be provided with psychosocial support ( this has been coordinated with the protection cluster )

Activities

The proposed activities are designed to respond to the high needs of the selected communities, knowing that current WV services cover only part of those needs, additional support is still needed to achieve better health outcomes, last year we managed to cover only 20% of needs. Output 1.1. Pregnant and lactating women and children <5 receive targeted health and nutrition interventions. In cooperation with MoH enrichment of infant and young child caring and feeding practices will be strengthened at community level through establishing Child friendly facilities, those facilities will be rehabilitated and supported with needed tools and supplies to enhance the services offered especially CWDs. Selected health facilities will be assessed, to identify exact interventions to make them disability friendly, this will including infrastructure, provision of special tools and equipment’s (HCI will be consulted). MoH field staff will utilize those spaces to provide the services supported by WV trained CHWs (training was delivered by the MOH specialized staff). CHWs (21), will target 500 mother and their children at household level using a social and behavioral communication change approach (an evidence based approach that was tested by WV and published in Lancet), trained CHWs will visit the mothers and address issues mainly related to infant care such as breast feeding, complementary feeding practices, assuring the supplement intake, recognition of any danger signs, pre-post natal care. CHW will be the linkages between their communities and service providers to ensure proper utilization and timely referral of mothers to services, all the identified CWDs will be prioritized (30 CWD out of the 500)1.1.1 21 CHWs will conduct home visits (2 visits /month /households) 1.1.2 Establish 17 child friendly facilities and support them with tools/supplies 1.1.3 Rehabilitate health facilities to be accessed by CWDs; 1.1.4 Conduct advanced training for 38 CHWs and health providers on special nutritional and caring needs for CWD including first aid. Output1.2: Teachers, children and mothers’ mental health psychosocial status is enhanced Psychological first aid (PFA) training and activities follow up will be done jointly in partnership with the Palestinian counseling center (PCC) a specialized in this field. The training will be provided to school counselors and community health workers in partnership with PCC. Activity is to equip them with skills needed to deal with affected children. 9 teachers will receive an intensive related training. Extracurricular activities will be designed to help provide students with essential skills to increase their resilience. Counselors and Community health workers will be equipped with techniques and comprehensive tool kit to sustain interventions. For critical cases, a referral mechanism will be developed and activated 1.2.1 Train 9 counselors/teachers (4M/5F) on PFA.1.2.2 Conduct extracurricular PFA related activities in 9 targeted schools for 1940 children (880M/1059 F &23 CWDs)1.2.3 Train 38 CHW and service providers on mental health and PFA 1.2.4 Insure referral mechanisms are in place Coordination with the protection cluster in regards to MHPSS took place. Emergency preparedness& first aid training supported with basic tools will be provided to volunteers in each of the targeted communities. Active community participation and engagement in implementation and monitoring will be ensured and maintained as a way to ensure quality of work and draw lesson learnt for future interventions. Needs were discussed with communities, recommendations received from communities and partners were considered. Satisfaction surveys and FG discussions will be considered taken into consideration while preparing the second year of intervention. Services will be accessed by all while ensuring safety, mothers with neonates will be reached at households and CHWs will be from the same communities to avoid travel risk.

The action will establish strong monitoring and learning mechanisms to assess and track progress against program objectives. The monitoring of the action will be conducted in accordance with existing WV JWG monitoring systems under the local responsibility of the field team in close liaison with the country office (Jerusalem) and partners. To improve the monitoring process at the results' level, a Detailed Monitoring Plan (DMP) will be developed, which details all monitoring tools and monitoring time frames. Monitoring tools will be developed taking into consideration age and gender aspects. Tools will include Observation report, clinics observation report, baseline and end line of trainees to measure demonstration of knowledge, attendance sheets of trainees. The action monitoring approach will follow key principals: 1 Joint monitoring practices through collaboration with the implementing partner, communities and MoH, MoE . WV has strong relationships with the action's stakeholders creating a foundation for shared responsibilities and accountability in M&E tasks.2 World Vision field team and in coordination with community members and project partners will be oriented on using the monitoring tools. It’s worth mentioning some of the action monitoring tools builds on existing monitoring mechanisms aiming at strengthening them and achieving better impact.3 Progress reports will be regularly collected on weekly basis and shared with key stakeholders including communities. 4 WV team will follow up with the training and extracurricular program in cooperation with implementing partner and MoH to ensure quality implementation of all activities. 5 End of project report will be shared with cluster and partners.Targets in relations to the indicators: # of neonates receiving quality health and nutrition services Target 500 ( 250 females -250 males ) #of <5 children receiving targeted health and nutritional interventions Target 1000 ( 500 females -500 males) # of people with access to primary healthcare services in the West Bank Target 1500 ( 1000 females, 500 males )#of acutely vulnerable boys and girls ,men and women receiving MHPSS Target 2440 ( 500 women – 880 boys- 1059 girls)#of communities received preparedness training Target 17#of pregnant lactating women received reproductive health services Target 500 women#of health workers trained Target 38 females #of counselor /teachers trained on psychosocial Target 9( 5 females ,4 males )# of targeted home visits conducted by the CHWs Target 12000 visit #of mother child friendly facility established Target 17#of CWDs reached 53 ( 30 girls , 24 boys)
