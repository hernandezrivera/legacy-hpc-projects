Project Summary

SPEDP is proposing activities to increase access to safe, protective and inclusive education for 21,800(12,394 girls) children and youth including 125 teachers and 286 SMC/PTAs members affected with crisis in Western Bar El Gharzal, Eastern Equatoria and Central Equatoria states in the counties of Wau, Raja, Magwi, Morobo, Kajokeji and Yei. The project will target IDPs, Refugees, returnees and host community. The education sector is characterized by poor learning outcomes and high numbers of out-of-school children. According to UNICEF, 2.8 million children of which 53% girls are out of school, particularly those affected by conflict and other emergencies, girls, children with disabilities and those living in pastoralist communities and in hard-to-reach areas. According to the South Sudan Education Sector Analysis (2018), school coverage rates in South Sudan remain below the regional average with Gross Enrolment Rates of 10% at pre-primary level and 72% at primary level. Primary Net Enrolment rate is low at 42.3% while for secondary school it is 3.3%. Female enrolment is low with the Gender Parity Index (GPI) going from 0.92 in pre-primary to 0.68 at primary and, ultimately, 0.46 at secondary level. Provision of quality education is affected by many factors including critical shortage of qualified and motivated teachers and non-availability of teaching and learning materials. There is a significant challenge of infrastructure with 2,042 schools operating under a tree, 1,134 are temporary. SPEDP will establish/rehabilitate 22 learning spaces to accommodate OOSC in the targeted locations. South Sudan is also impacted by climate change, with flooding in some locations causing damage to school infrastructure and learning materials, displacement and increased health risks for teachers and learners.
The ENA (p. 36-37) found that there has been a decline in back to learning enrolment due to a sharp increase in protection risks, particularly for girls, as well as economic pressure on families.
Most schools lack adequate facilities to safely accommodate girls and boys in a conducive learning space. A lack of WASH facilities is particularly challenging. According to Girls Education South Sudan (GESS), 40% of schools they assessed had no toilets at all. Where there were toilets, just 40% of those were accessible to leanings with disabilities. Further, only 45% of schools had boreholes to provide direct access to clean water in the vicinity of the school. Other schools may have had access to water, but it may have been poor quality, or outside the control of the school. Just 33% of schools assessed had hand washing facilities, dropping to 23% in rural areas. The lack of WASH facilities contributes to discomfort especially for girls and may ultimately lead to lost learning times. In a worst-case scenario, a lack of accessible WASH facilities may create protection risks as children must go outside of school bounds to seek such facilities.
Therefore, To address the several challenges faced by the education system, SPEDP is proposing activities such as training of teachers on safeguarding and GBV risk mitigation, referral mechanism, EiE subject matter and pedagogical skills, and Psychosocial support(PSS), Support volunteer teachers with incentives, Establish/rehabilitate temporary learning spaces/Gender sensitive latrines and changing rooms, training of SMC/PTA and Education authorities on school management, governance, climate change and Disaster Risk Reduction(DRR) and conduct community mobilization and awareness raising on key issues of education e.g. Back to Learning especially for young mothers and children living with disabilities and resources mobilization to increase access to safe, protective and inclusive education for children and youth affected with crisis in Western Bar El Gharzal, Eastern Equatoria and Central Equatoria states in the counties of Wau, Raja, Magwi, Morobo, Kajokeji and Yei.

1. Needs

Conflicts in South Sudan mainly intercommunal fighting and political instability has led to a severe and prolonged state of humanitarian crisis and underdevelopment. This has impacted the education sector both at service delivery and system level. The education sector is characterized by poor learning outcomes and high numbers of out-of-school children (2.8 million of which 53 per cent are girls), particularly those affected by conflict and other emergencies, girls, children with disabilities and those living in pastoralist communities and in hard-to-reach areas. According to the South Sudan Education Sector Analysis (2018), school coverage rates in South Sudan remain well below the regional average with gross enrolment rates (GERs) of 10 per cent at pre-primary level and 72 per cent at primary level. Primary net enrolment rate (NER) is low at 42.3 percent while for secondary school it is 3.3 percent. Female enrolment is low with the Gender Parity Index (GPI) going from 0.92 in pre-primary to 0.68 at primary and, ultimately, 0.46 at secondary level. Provision of quality education is affected by many factors including critical shortage of qualified and motivated teachers and non-availability of teaching and learning materialsthe ongoing conflict, violence and insecurity frequently resulted in damage to school infrastructure and teaching and learning materials, and drives displacement of teachers and learners.The education system in south Sudan has been constrained by chronic crisis conditions over the last decade. Most recently, the Covid-19 pandemic has had a significant impact on education services – schools in South Sudan were closed for over one year. The ENA (p. 36-37) found that there has been a decline in back to learning enrolment due to a sharp increase in protection risks, particularly for girls, as well as economic pressure on families. South Sudan is also impacted by climate change, with flooding in some locations causing damage to school infrastructure and learning materials, displacement and increased health risks for teachers and learners. There is a significant challenge of infrastructure with 2,042 schools operating under a tree, 1,134 are temporary structures, 1,115 are permanent, and 848 are semi-permanent . The continued influx of returnees from Sudan has continued to stretch the already dire resources. In August 2023, 243,940 individuals were recorded at entry points close to 50% are children aged 0-17years, while close to 30% are Primary and Sec school aged (6-17 years).Most schools lack adequate facilities to safely accommodate girls and boys in a conducive learning space. A lack of WASH facilities is particularly challenging. According to Girls Education South Sudan (GESS), 40% of schools they assessed had no toilets at all. Where there were toilets, just 40% of those were accessible to leanings with disabilities. Further, only 45% of schools had boreholes to provide direct access to clean water in the vicinity of the school. Other schools may have had access to water, but it may have been poor quality, or outside the control of the school. Just 33% of schools assessed had hand washing facilities, dropping to 23% in rural areas. The lack of WASH facilities contributes to discomfort especially for girls and may ultimately lead to lost learning times. In a worst-case scenario, a lack of accessible WASH facilities may create protection risks as children must go outside of school bounds to seek such facilities.All the above challenges are not exceptional to the proposed States and Counties

2. Project activities

1. SPEDP will provide Training to 125(60% female) teachers on safeguarding and GBV risk mitigation, referral mechanism, EiE subject matter and pedagogical skills, and Psychosocial support(PSS). This will target both IPD, returnees and Host community teachers in secondary, ALP/ASEP, Primary and ECD schools.2. SPEDP will Enrol 21800 (12394 Female) learners to ALP, ECD, Primary, TVET and Secondary schools3. Support 125 volunteer teachers(60% female) with incentives to improve quality of teaching by daily attendance 4. SPEDP will Establish/rehabilitate of temporary learning spaces5. Establishment of Gender sensitive latrines and changing rooms 6. SPEDP will Conduct training for 286 SMC/PTA members and Education authorities on school management, governance, climate change and Disaster Risk Reduction(DRR). 7. Conduct community mobilization and awareness raising on key issues of education e.g. Back to Learning, resources mobilization to support schools etc. 8. SPEDP will Distribute teaching and learning materials, recreational materials, dignity kits, ECD kits, hygiene kits and assistive devices for children living with disabilities in the targeted schools9. Establishment of environmental clubs and school gardens in all schools targeted.
