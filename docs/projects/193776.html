Project Summary

The action will contribute to improve the access to agriculture and farming for vulnerable Palestinians in the Gaza Strip enhancing food security levels and contributing to the potential and sustainability of livelihoods. The proposed intervention aligns with SDG 2 Zero Hunger and the HRP 2023 Strategic objectives 3, therefore complementing FSS objectives. The resilience of vulnerable households (HHs) is protected, and their livelihoods, food security and productive capacity are restored/enhanced, and community-led advocacy actions, including awareness raising, are planned and implemented in a coordinated manner, in fair partnerships among concerned parties, including civil society organizations, key national ministries and concerned donors. The action has been informed by the main findings collected through the CPA-RESCO Integrated Territorial Approach by WeWorld-GVC (WW-GVC) and the needs assessment conducted jointly by WW-GVC and Al-Fukhari Association for Rural Development with farmers in Khan Younis & Middle Area. The proposed activities have been built upon the FSS-eligible interventions for HRP 2023. The intervention has been coordinated and endorsed by the Ministry of Agriculture (MoA) and the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD). The action will contribute to local plans for mitigating the risks faced by communities and support resilience building towards threats and vulnerabilities. Within this framework, WW-GVC and Al-Fukhari designed a complementary action that capitalizes on each partner's expertise and resources. Throughout the action, safe access to resources will be enhanced to alleviate poverty indicators and sustain income generation for HHs with disrupted livelihoods due to climate change, economic shocks, the blockade and Covid19. Specifically, the intervention will foster the agricultural sector through the provision of adequate and sustainable inputs and the rehabilitation of the assets. Lastly, advocacy actions will sustain marginalized communities’ contracting power on rights and needs in front of duty-bearers. Priority will be given to women, youth, and persons with specific needs, including PwD. Hence, the action unfolds on two pillars: (1) The resilience of 340 vulnerable HHs in the governorates of Khan Younis and Middle Area is protected and their livelihoods, food security, and productive capacity are restored/enhanced by the (1.1) Rehabilitation and provision of productive assets and inputs for farmers, including promotion of technical capacities, with 170 dunums of greenhouses rehabilitated (170 direct beneficiaries); (1.2) 170 dunums of the open land made productively or with improved production capacity (irrigation system, tolerant seeds etc.). (170 direct beneficiaries). (2) community-led initiatives, including awareness-raising sessions are planned and implemented in a coordinated manner, in fair partnerships among concerned parties, civil society organizations, key national ministries, and concerned donors. Specifically, (2.1) 5 community-led FSS advocacy actions (awareness initiatives/campaigns conducted with a focus on small-scale producers’ rights.) (5,000 beneficiaries). The action will benefit a total of 340 HHs in the governorates of Khan Younis (140) and Middle Area (200) of 8 communities. Indirect beneficiaries will be 1,904 (439 women, 452 men, 474 girls, 487 boys, 52 elders including 180 PwD). Moreover, it is estimated that the advocacy actions will reach 5,000 people recipients in a year. WW-GVC is a member of the FSS Palestine and of the WB Protection Consortium, holding solid expertise in promoting socioeconomic development through enhancing access to food and natural resources through innovative and sustainable interventions including open land and greenhouse rehabilitation. The action serves beyond mere material needs by addressing broader issues of safety and dignity to promote gender and age-responsive protection, equitable participation and PSEA.

1 - Needs

Food security in Palestine continues to be affected by prolonged conflict, economic stagnation, restrictions on trade and access to resources, and high unemployment and poverty rates. According to FSS HNO 2023, among the drivers of food insecurity are lack of means to afford nutritious and healthy food and a significant consumption gap due to the uneven economic recovery from COVID-19 pandemic and widespread supply chain disruption caused by the Ukraine/Russia crisis. In 2022, an estimated 1.8 million individuals experienced moderate to severe food insecurity in the oPt. Moreover, 1.54 million people are expected to need humanitarian assistance, 77% of those are in the Gaza Strip. The key sectoral findings of the latest Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) facilitated by REACH show 50.5% of HHs interviewed in Gaza rely on NGO or charity assistance as primary income sources, due to a lack of resources. More specifically, 60.3% reported having at least one HH member unemployed and looking for work at the time of data collection (May-July 2022). Unemployment rates showcase a gender dimension, with 29.0% of HHs reporting lack of opportunities for women as the main obstacle to finding work. Furthermore, 70.0% of HHs reported difficulties meeting essential food needs, while an estimation of 55.7% of their expenditure (in cash or credit) was reportedly spent on food in the 30 days prior to data collection. As highlighted in the Protection cluster’s Protection Analysis Update (August 2022), the situation in the restricted areas on land and at sea raises serious human rights concerns for residents or who are dependent on those areas for livelihoods (fishermen, farmers, shepherds or rubble collectors) and remain vulnerable to threats to life and security, arbitrary arrest and detention, loss of livelihoods, restricted access to services and potential displacement. Recurring hostilities -especially in August 2022- jeopardized the profitability and sustainability of agricultural activities for many farmers. As prioritized in FSS HPC 2023 needs analysis, female-headed HHs constitute one of the most vulnerable amongst the AGD groups, at greater risk of poverty and hunger. In addition, women, especially pregnant, suffer from a lack of access to nutritious food and awareness on the issue; the same applies to children under 5 years old, elders and PwD. In the framework of a 3-year EU-funded project, during 2022 WW-GVC applied a community-based participatory territorial approach (CPA-RESCO) consisting of a multi-sectoral needs assessment conducted in the Strip, as well as an analysis of the most promising value chains aimed to build sustainable strategic plans for development. The approach consists of multi-layered set of engagement mechanisms ensuring that participation and agency of community members is governed by an Age, Gender and Diversity approach, along with civil society, private sector and institutional representatives, with instruments to analyse social and power dynamics. The results, including the consistent process of engagement of different age, gender, and diversity groups fostered through CPA-RESCO and specific gender variations highlighted in the target areas for the action, Khan Younis and Middle Area, in coherence with the priorities identified by the FSS , informed the proposed HRP. Among the most relevant are challenges in accessing or non-available markets, sustainable income-generating projects and/or funding, specialized technical assistance and capacity building (improper use of chemical fertilizers, difficulties in terms of regulations for the creation of cooperatives, lack of knowledge on the proper treatment of agricultural diseases), products' diversification, adequate information/marketing/networking, supporting programs for farmers. The assessment conducted in September 2022 by the partners, in coordination with the MoA, informed further the proposed action from a technical perspective, particularly for OL & GH farmers and supported the selection of the targeting areas and communities (Khan Younis: Bani Suhaila, Abasan, Khuza’a, Qararah, Al Fukhari; Middle Area: Wadi Al-Salka, Dail Albalah, Zawaida. The farming-related livelihood rehabilitation for GH and OL farmers was planned based on the results of the focus groups which highlighted the most important issues faced in the agricultural sector in the target areas, including the persistence of a coercive environment, increasing protection threats and restricted access to basic natural resources, livelihood inputs and services. The interviewed farmers, including women, reported the high cost of inputs, along with the need for irrigation networks, seeds and fertilizers. Hence, there is an urgent demand for an immediate response via agricultural/land rehabilitation, access to water for agriculture, provision of inputs for production, irrigation networks, and access to more sources of water and markets in order to enable farmers to access quality products and essential resources to sustain their livelihoods. Multiple issues are relevant, such as lack of accessibility to markets, unavailability of food, and the need for prioritization of vulnerable population groups (elders, PWD). Funding, technical assistance, income-generating projects, and capacity building are essential (improper use of chemical fertilizers, difficulties in forming cooperatives, lack of knowledge for treatment of agricultural diseases), as product diversification, adequate information, marketing and networking services, and farmer assistance programs. Hence, WW-GVC and Al-Fukhari identified 3 main priorities: 1) 170 dunums of open land rehabilitation and agricultural inputs; 2) 170 dunums of GH rehabilitation; In terms of capacity building, there is a need to raise awareness about climate change’s impact on agriculture with complementary advocacy actions to support the sustainability of farming activities and small-scale producers’ rights.

2 - Activities

The project aims at reducing the deterioration of food security and livelihoods experienced by vulnerable farmers and families in the governorates of Khan Younis and the Middle Area. The action was designed in close collaboration with local authorities, MoA, MoSD, and the FSS and it will be delivered through service delivery (100%) for activities 1.1 and 1.2 and 2.1. The activities will be implemented to ensure the participation of men, women, boys, girls, and PwDs in analyzing their needs through AGD-tailored interviews and focus groups. PSEA messages and calls for feedback will be integrated into the programming. Technical assessments will be conducted before the intervention to design appropriate interventions that match their needs in compliance with food security sector SOPs for PSEA. Staff will include awareness activities on PSEA, including complaint mechanisms (including how to report SEA-related complaints). The activities are clustered under two main components: 1. Provision and rehabilitation of meaningful access to productive assets and inputs to increase the safety and dignity of farmers. Open land (OL) and greenhouse (GH) selection will be coordinated with FSS and MoA to ensure targeting the most vulnerable farmers and avoiding duplication and overlapping with other local and international actors. The selection criteria will be based on joint discussion with relevant stakeholders such as MoA, FSS, local actors, farmers committees etc. A specialized technical team will work in the field with the farmers to identify the specific inputs and interventions needed for the targeted farmers. (1.1) Rehabilitation of 170 dunums of GH (full or partial land rehabilitation, piping, and irrigation system, seeds and seedlings distribution, habilitation, piping and irrigation system, tolerant seeds, etc.) (1.2) 170 dunums of the OL made productively or with improved production capacity (full or partial land rehabilitation, piping, and irrigation system, seeds and seedlings distribution, habilitation, piping, and irrigation system, tolerant seeds, etc.) WW-GVC and Al-Fukhari Association will target 170 small-scale GH farmers, and 170 small-scale OL farmers (340 *5.6 Avrg. = 1904) affected by the hostilities providing them with rehabilitation services, to help them to cope with negative impacts and ensure a stable supply of the essential vegetables (as tomato, pepper, Eggplant, Cucumber) to the local population. Specifically, the target communities will be: for the GH (Khan Younis: Abbassan (30), Bani Suhaila (20), Al Fukhari (20) ); Middle Area: Wadi Al-Salka (30), Dail Albalah (40), Zawaida (30). Regarding the OL (Khan Younis: Khuza’a (20) ), Qararah (20), Al Fukhari 30), Middle Area: Wadi Al-Salka (40), Dail Albalah (30), Zawaida (30)). GH and OL farmers will be supported to rehabilitate and cultivate one dunum only. The value of the delivered services for OL and GH was unified and agreed upon between FSS and MoA as follow: Open land (OL) is 1500 NIS and greenhouse (GH) is 3000 NIS. Accordingly, and in compliance with the FSS and MoA standards, each farmer’s HH will receive rehabilitation services that are equivalent to 1500 NIS for OL rehabilitation and 3000 NIS for GH rehabilitation. Selection will be based on the hostilities-affected and vulnerable farmers list prioritized by MoA. Verification will be done by the partner and MoA’s representatives to prioritize the most vulnerable HHs. Criteria will include: A) Small-scale farmers who own ranges (1-3 dunums) B) Have an affected GH due to last hostilities C) Resident\Property in one of the targeted areas D) Registered as affected farmers E) Breadwinners for big families of at least 5 people F) Farming is the only source of income G) Did not benefit before from similar interventions. Priority will be given to large HHs with a high number of children/dependents, female-headed HHs, HHs who have members with disabilities and/or chronic diseases, HHs who have elderly persons, HHs who have pregnant and/or lactating women (more affected by food insecurity), HHs with high dependency ratio. Orientation meetings will be organized by WW-GVC and Al-Fokhari to present the details of the action, and discuss the intervention nature, modality and potential activities. A comprehensive assessment of the selected farmers will be conducted to identify the specific needs of each farmer. The assessment, delivery and rehabilitation activities will be followed and monitored by a specialized agronomist to control, orient and monitor the farmer’s rehabilitation activities. The Building Back Better (BBB) approach will be encompassed to rehabilitate vulnerable farmers' agricultural assets, to increase productivity, and business longevity and improve overall food security 2. Awareness-raising sessions and national and international advocacy initiatives to promote the sustainability of farming activities and the livelihood of the families, favoring community agency, participation and empowerment (2.1) 5 community-led FSS advocacy actions, with a focus on small-scale producers’ rights. Community based-Food Security Advocacy Team (FSAT) will be formed (male and female farmers, youth, community leaders, PwD and local actors). FSAT will receive capacity building for the elaboration of advocacy strategies to empower a rights-based approach toward FSS issues. Once the FSAT is identified and trained, FSAT will define, with the support of WW-GVC and Al Fukhari, a detailed advocacy plan, selecting the most effective actions according to the target audience. FSAT will be supported to organize 5 advocacy actions, in coordination with relevant national and international FSS partners and CBOs, with a focus on small-scale producers’ rights. Such actions can include press releases, campaigns on social media, training, multimedia-related products such as infographics, videos and animation, in addition to FSS meetings with relevant stakeholders, including line ministries.

3 - Indicators

WW-GVC and Al-Fukhari Association for Rural Development will coordinate the progress of activities and the monitoring of the indicators with the MoA and MoSD, strengthening the involvement of the latter and its directorates in all project activities. This will create interactive relationships and synergies between the project beneficiaries and MoA and MoSD, which will also contribute to guaranteeing project interventions' sustainability. Moreover, the project beneficiaries will be engaged in providing continuous access to technical support during project life and after. Furthermore, Al-Fukhari Association for Rural Development and WW-GVC will update both MoA, MoSD and FSS members on the project's progress and achievements. The project partners will appoint one Monitoring & Evaluation focal point each for the project. The delivery and quality control will be ensured through regular field visits to the sites of the project. Staff will monitor progress on agreed indicators and verify the validity of reported data through a regular review of means of verification. These measures will consider beneficiaries' participation and reporting throughout implementation and monitoring. Additionally, the monitoring implies a participatory approach that includes group feedback sessions and individual interviews (with family members with and without disability) to assess to which level the identified activities were suitable to single beneficiaries as well as communities’ needs and match with the Agricultural directorates in the targeted Governorates. After the completion of the intervention, the qualitative and socioeconomic impact of the initiative will be further assessed through focus groups, interviews, and surveys, collecting the feedback of different vulnerable groups (women, men, youth, elderlies, PwD, and their caregivers). The proposed intervention is expected to improve the protection of the targeted communities by enhancing their access to livelihood resources and inputs that will contribute to food security sustainability and resilience and build their awareness and mechanisms of the approach regarding group advocacy towards duty-bearers. Lastly, both direct beneficiaries and community members will have access to a Feedback and Complaint Mechanism jointly managed by the Al-Fokhari Association for Rural Development and WW-GVC throughout the duration of the intervention and beyond. 9.1c - How will feedback be used?

All feedback and complaints coming from beneficiaries will be given high consideration. Beneficiaries are informed that the complaints will be kept confidential, specifically for sensitive issues (such as GBV and SEA), and will be dealt with accordingly. GBV cases identified or self-reported during the implementation will be referred to specialized GBV-response agencies, in full respect of the survivors' wishes and consent. Al-Fukhari and WW-GVC will jointly scrutiny the content of the complaints and/or feedback and, if necessary, will further investigate the matter to assess whether an adjustment to the programming or disciplinary actions are needed. Also, the two partners commit to addressing the complaints and feedback within a reasonable timeframe.

9.1b - If 'Other', please specify :

WW-GVC and Al-Fukhari and will arrange a dedicated hotline for complaints coming from beneficiaries and their communities throughout the duration and up to 6 months after the end of the project (as both organizations are implementing such a mechanism as part of the responsibilities of their M&E departments). This will complement WW-GVC’s Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM), accountability, and commitment to guarantee transparency, fairness, and equity toward all our beneficiaries. It aims to establish a system that allows individuals and groups to securely report complaints, suggestions, and inquiries and receive a response on time. All complaints/feedback are reported to the WW-GVCs and Al-Fukhari Monitoring and Coordination manager by field staff, project managers, and/or focal points who refer the case to relevant staff according to its sensitivity. If the complaint requires follow-up, the complainant will receive an answer within 15 calendar days. Once the issue is solved, the complainant will receive a phone call from WWGVC and Al-Fukhari Monitoring and Coordination manager. Upon registration of the complaint on the database, the admin officer analyses complaints and classify each of them according to the internal classification system; then, the complaint should be resolved within 15 days by WW-GVC and Al-Fukhari /or CRM team.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

Every member of the field staff is obliged to inform and explain to all direct and indirect beneficiaries of the presence of a complaint-response mechanism (CRM) and the referral pathway for their complaints, as well as provide the contact information/hotline for the CRM focal points depending on the type of the complaint (female focal point for GBV and PSEA; a male focal point for other types of complaints). Also, the beneficiary agreements will contain a particular part about the complaint mechanisms, including the dedicated number for this subject. The complaint mechanism will be in-person, at any time during the field visits of the staff of the two organizations, or remotely through the dedicated hotline. The latter will ensure an anonymous and discrete means in a complaint, ensuring that all population groups may access the mechanism.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The response is carefully designed to ensure that interventions will not impair beneficiaries’ safety or expose them to further risk. A thorough assessment will be conducted with each community to investigate further and understand the needs, risks, and vulnerabilities of the diverse social groups, assuring compliance with the Do No Harm principle so that the rehabilitation works are based on the preferences and needs expressed by the members of the HH, considering the different threats that affect girls, boys, women, and men of all ages including PwD. Additionally, livelihood support and land rehabilitation intervention will be designed to comply with protection principles, such as meaningful access and accountability towards beneficiaries, taking into account the diverse needs, roles, and dynamics of women and men, age and disability status, in each community as well as PSEA SoPs. Furthermore, household members will work in rehabilitation when possible, so the sense of dignity and ownership will also be improved. The field team participated in the protection cluster GBV training to enhance their capacities in detecting and referring cases. Moreover, the field team received capacity building on how to identify multi-sector risks affecting the safety and dignity of the populations and design relevant actions to address them. WW-GVC is now mainstreaming the AGD and PwD toolkits in the community-based protection methodology. During COVID-19 outbreaks, protection measures (wearing face masks, hand sanitizers, and physical distances) will be followed by WW-GVC and Al-Fukhari teams during all project activities to prevent the disease spread.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

An environment conducive to protection is obtained when individual and communities’ multi-sector humanitarian and development needs are met in a dignified and safe manner. As such, multi-sector needs are identified and ranked based on the contextual protection risks of communities and individuals. A right-based analysis is used as an entry point to elaborate local protection strategies to be used as a programming framework for the definition of needs-based responses. The CPA was developed according to IHL, Humanitarian, Do No Harm, and the following fundamental principles: 1. The safety of the affected population (both groups and individuals); 2. Restore the dignity of the affected people, putting a stop to emerging or established patterns of violations (and/or alleviating their immediate effects); 3. place the empowerment of communities at risk at the core of any action to enable self-protection and realize their rights. Using focus groups, interviews, and transect walks ensure the population's involvement in terms of location, age, gender, ethnicity, diversity, profession, and power. According to people’s access, level of trust built, and other do not harm considerations, multiple specific techniques will be applied to get deeper insight (bias reduction and/or data gap filling) into a particular group, a specific situation, or a particular household. Throughout the assessment and planning phases, all community members' needs will be considered. The proposed food security and livelihood interventions address the urgent need of the targeted communities, accounting for non-discriminatory practices and considering the needs of every age, gender, and level of ability. Providing a voice for each community member, and requesting their direct action in the project’s implementation, contribute to preserving the dignity of each beneficiary and fosters community empowerment and ownership of the action. The distribution of Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) cards in the communities will ensure the accessibility to all beneficiaries to provide their feedback and complaints about the implemented project.
