Project Summary

The living conditions in Area C of the West Bank is still deteriorating resulting in various negative impacts to the whole population especially for vulnerable communities and families. The Palestinian Authority is struggling to contain the outbreak and its effects. Most of communities in Area C continue to suffer the social and economic impact of this pandemic in many ways. In the West Bank, tens of thousands of families fell below the poverty line due to COVID-19 which is an additional challenge to the communities as well as to the humanitarian response. In this context and in line with the Food Security (FS) Cluster 2021 HRP Framework, the proposed intervention led by Action Against Hunger, Palestinian Agricultural Relief Committees (PARC), and the Union of Agricultural Work Committees (UAWC) will target the food security and livelihoods needs of the most vulnerable communities affected by protection threats in Area C of the West Bank. In addition, the proposed project will provide specific and tailored assistance to targeted communities in response to COVID-19 outbreak and its negative consequences. The proposed intervention will thus have two major implementing dimensions and activities: non-agricultural and agricultural activities.
Specifically, the activities will contribute to protecting vulnerable communities in Area C from forcible transfer and COVID-19 negative consequences by improving the main factors involved in food production (roads, water, livestock shelter), income generation, and agricultural inputs. By doing so, the proposed intervention will contribute to improving the food security of marginalized families living under the poverty line. The project will also participate to the increase of income of vulnerable women and men in non-agriculture sectors. This will include cash for work schemes as well as support to existing small businesses and different Income Generation Activities (IGAs) aiming to assist targeted communities to face the COVID-19 negative impacts.
The project activities will benefit all members of the household and will be carried out on two levels:
1. At household and even individual level through the rehabilitation of animal shelters, provision of cash support (cash for work), and providing support to existing IGAs as well as through the provision of agricultural inputs.
2. At community level by improving the access to water and the communities’ accessibility (road rehabilitation).
The non-agriculture-based activities will be designed to serve the targeted communities including individuals with focus on women affected by the COVID-19 pandemic.
The proposed intervention will deal with the protection threats to the livelihoods of the targeted families. Fomenting a community participation approach in the assessment, design and implementation of the activities, all the community’s sub-groups will participate in different focus group discussions, technical assessments and in the implementation process. The communication with communities (CWCs) will mitigate potential security and protection risks and, ultimately, empower the beneficiaries at community, family and individual level. As outlined, the proposed intervention ensures protection mainstreaming as it is guided by protection principles and considers safety and dignity; meaningful access; accountability to affected population (AAP) as well as participation and empowerment.

1 - Needs

The conditions in the West Bank are characterised as a protracted crisis, with repeated shocks and continued restrictions on freedom of movement, constrained productive capacities and lack of economic opportunities resulting in high unemployment and low household incomes especially in Area C. This, together with the high cost of living leave 600,000 Palestinians in the West Bank (175,000 of them in Area C representing over 30% of the total food insecure estimates in the West Bank including East Jerusalem) in urgent need of assistance (HNO 2021). In addition, the COVID-19 outbreak has added extra burden on the economy, communities and families following the Palestinian Authority’s (PA) resolution to declare the state of emergency and a full lockdown to contain the virus spread. The restrictions had a severe disruptive impact on the entire food system, including producers, processors, marketers, transporters and customers leaving thousands of people unemployed. The vulnerable communities in Area C rely mainly on handcrafts, small businesses, agricultural activities, and herding as main activities for daily income generation. Based on a recent comprehensive survey for Area C conducted by Bir Zeit University (Addressing the Needs of Palestinian Households in Area C of the West Bank, 2019), families in general do not have a stable source of income; 25% of households in Area C rely on herding and agriculture as a main source of income, 40% rely on irregular daily wages, less than 25% rely on stable wages, and almost 75% rely on agricultural products and animal products as main part of their income. Affected by the pandemic restrictions, small famers and producers have been particularly hit by the reduced demand, the unprecedented challenges in securing basic inputs, and the impediments in accessing credit and markets. The World Bank estimates that the share of poor households in the West Bank will double by the end of 2020 reaching a level of 30% (14% in 2019) driven by the impact of the pandemic. Families who are already food insecure and have limited resilience are unlikely to be able to retain (or rebuild) their livelihoods with the expected loss of the last of their remaining productive assets.Additionally, communities in central West Bank are under threat of forcible transfer (OCHA). Structures owned by Palestinians in Area C are also subject to demolition; between January and August 2020, 322 Palestinian structures were destroyed or seized including livelihood infrastructures (OCHA monthly report, August 2020). 20-2021 winter could potentially bring high winds, storms, flash flooding and frosts, exacerbating the already poor humanitarian status for large segments of the targeted population, namely women, girls and boys. The ongoing assessment of AAH’s interventions in 2020 enabled AAH to identify communities requiring priority emergency support due to their vulnerability (unemployment rate, income, demolition/eviction orders, and sensitivity of their location). These families lose their assets due to demolishing incidents or natural hazards and/or have lost income due to the COVID-19 pandemic. Female headed households were identified as particularly vulnerable due to limited access to livelihood opportunities. Women and children are the most disadvantaged regarding food security status, women are responsible for cooking, children are affected by malnutrition. An animal shelter assessment was conducted by AAH, updated in March 2019, covering 6 Governorates in Areas A, B and C through interviews with MOA directorate in each Governorate and questionnaires. This confirmed that herding is the main source of income for most herding families (90% of the targeted families) - especially in Area C. The study highlighted that most of the animal shelters in the targeted areas are not adequate for the flock size resulting in overcrowded shelters. In addition, a proportion of the flock spent extensive periods of time in the open thus resulting in high abortion and mortality rates (25% in most of the targeted communities).

2 - Activities

AAH, PARC and UWAC will coordinate the final selection of localities and beneficiaries with MoA, WASH Cluster, the Palestinian Water Authority (PWA) MoSD and MoL directorates, the FS Cluster and with organizations working in the same area. To ensure diversity and a balanced gender and age perspectives, women children and elderly family members will participate in all stages of the project. The local partners will be involved in all phases of the intervention: in conducting pre-implementation assessments, project design, setting up specific criteria, selection of localities and beneficiaries, and implementation. The role of our local partners will be a crucial role to cover all targeted locations. The approach will adopt a geographic distribution among partners, where each partner will cover certain areas and AAH lead the entire intervention. This will allow for better coverage, effective implementation, and experience/information sharing. The interventions will be divided into two categories: agricultural-based and non-agricultural based activities. Partners will adopt the protective measures to protect staff and communities against the risk of COVID-19 transmission and avoid doing harm. Agricultural-based interventions: A.1 Water infrastructure rehabilitation and provision of water storages systems to improve access to agricultural water for vulnerable farming communities under threat of forced displacement (Hebron, Jerusalem, Ramallah, Jericho and Tubas);the intervention will reduce the farming input costs. The interventions will cover construction and rehabilitation of water pipes (20 Km), 80 agricultural cisterns and bonds, and rehabilitate 3 springs (2 springs in Batteer and Artas and 1 spring in Halhoul) in coordination with the relevant organisations. For springs, the type of rehabilitation includes: rehabilitation work including construction/cleaning of the spring source and conveying system, channel maintenance, water Filtration system, and water distribution system (main and submain polyethylene pipe) and accessories (valves, elbow, T-connection and other fitting).A.2 Construction/Rehabilitation of livestock shelter for 200 vulnerable herders (Hebron, Bethlehem, Jerusalem and Tubas). This emergency intervention will help ensure hygienic conditions and maintain production levels in the context of COVID-19 pandemic. A.3 Supply of agricultural inputs (agricultural tools and material for rehabilitating 260 green houses and 50 irrigation systems) to improve productivity of farming activities (Jericho and Tubas).The rehabilitation will include all required work and materials to assure that a greenhouse is fully utilized, specifically by providing all required materials such as the metal and plastic components (e.g., solid plastic coverage, plastic pipes, and the metal skeleton). The type of greenhouses to be rehabilitated; abandoned greenhouses, most affected by weather conditions and winds, main source of income for beneficiaries, and those located in most affected areas. The intervention will target vulnerable farmers with low incomes and under the poverty line impacted by the COVID-19 pandemic and who are not ensuring the maximum benefits of their agricultural plots due to limited financial capacities and Israeli restrictions against construction in Area C. A.4 Rehabilitation of road to improve the most vulnerable herding and farming families’ safe access to livelihood assets and services (20 km roads in Hebron, Bethlehem and Ramallah), allowing them to reach their agricultural lands and grazing areas. The proposed activity will also protect their livelihoods assets from confiscation for settler expansion or road construction. A.5 Rehabilitation of lands located in areas under threat of confiscation (adjacent to settlements and bypass roads) in Hebron and Bethlehem (rehabilitation of 100 Dunums); This activity will support vulnerable farmers to protect and improve lands productivity. A.6 Provision of drought tolerant seeds to improve access to locally produced pastures (20 dunums per herder, for 500 herders). Female herders and farmers who own livestock, land, greenhouses will be supported to improve the productivity of their land and to increase household income. The project activities will prioritise them to promote their role within the HH and to decrease aid dependency of the women headed households (from both relatives and humanitarian aid). The non-agriculture-based interventions will target communities in five governorates (Bethlehem, Jericho, Hebron, Jerusalem, and Tubas) to address COVID-19 related impacts and will include; A.1 Cash based support (cash for work) & employability for families with limited access to income opportunities in Hebron, Jerusalem, and Tubas. The project will mainly target workers in most affected areas who lost their jobs due to closures and restrictions followed the spread of COVID-19. This component will assist them in securing basic cash assistance to support the household income and, therefore, contributing positively to the entire household’s food security. The planned work activities will include participating in constructing and rehabilitating public buildings (school rooms and facilities) within the community, roads, agricultural roads, land rehabilitation, and any other required work in the community. 50 individuals will be supported for 75 days period based on a daily salary rate of NIS 80 for unskilled workers and NIS 120 for skilled workers. A.2 Support deteriorated IGAs and small-scale businesses affected by COVID-19 in the five governorates by assisting 40 small-scale businesses headed mainly by women. As elaborated above, IGAs who have been affected by the coronavirus are facing hard times: limited access to markets, less market demands, and stocks/products/goods are ruined. Additionally, they are facing continuous setbacks in their daily business life due to the restrictions on access, closures, and other types of threats.

3 - Indicators

A baseline survey will be conducted at the start of the project by the three partners targeting a representation of all gender and age groups, which will be updated and compared to an end line survey after the activities’ completion. Information will be collected through semi structured interviews, household visits and field observations. Focus group discussions with female family members and female head of HHS will take place separately to eliminate any cultural and social barriers preventing them to actively participate in the project activities. Selection criteria will be identified by the partners in coordination with MoA, MoSD, MoL and the related local councils and the partners will coordinate with the related directorates in each governorate to benefit from their assessments and data. Particular attention will be paid to beneficiary selection based on diversity including their economical and displacement vulnerability, methods of working with village councils, mechanism of asset distribution and gender sensitive approach. Ongoing monitoring will allow AAH, UAWC and PARC to evaluate methodologies and the impact of the activities, to identify challenges and best practices. Accountability and transparency will be ensured through the establishment of AAH’s standard beneficiary feedback mechanism. . In general, the project will benefit a diversified population in terms of age and gender. Output 4: 200 herders (980 individuals: 479 females and 501 males) receive technical and material assistance to rehabilitate their animal shelters. Output 5: 500 herding HH (2,450 individuals: 1,196 females and 1,254 males) received climate adapted (drought tolerant) seeds for locally produced pastures (20 dunums per herder); Output 6: 35 farmers (172 individuals: 84 females and 88 males) benefited from the rehabilitation of 100 dunums. For the non-agriculture-based activities; Output 1: 50 families (245 individuals) mainly women receive support through the cash for work component. Output 2: 40 small businesses and IGAs (196 individuals) affected by COVID-19 negative impacts mainly women will be assisted. The expected objective verifiable indicators and targets from the project are as follows: - Outcome 1: The livestock mortality and abortion rates for the households targeted by livestock accommodation decreased by 30%; - Outcome 2: The agricultural water cost for the communities targeted by water interventions will decreased by 35%; - Outcome 3: The targeted farmers targeted by agricultural inputs will improve their productivity by more than 25%; - Outcome 4: Iincome generation activities (agricultural and non-agricultural) will be supported to assist individuals in the targeted areas to cope with the current challenges. To further ensure protection is mainstreamed throughout the project implementation, the indicator “% of communities/project beneficiaries reporting that humanitarian assistance is delivered in a safe and dignified manner” is added and will be measured through an end line survey of the beneficiaries.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Through the whole project cycle AAH, PARC and UWAC ensure protection mainstreaming is guided by protection principles of Do No Harm. The partners consider safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability as well as participation and empowerment of paramount importance. Overall AAH seeks to complement its own actions with for instance legal support to inform risk mitigation measures especially in Area C and consequently to increase complementary humanitarian action. For activities that include physical interventions (including constructions of animal shelter and roads, water pipes rehabilitation), AAH will coordinate with the relevant legal entities to evaluate how this intervention will affect the legal status of the targeted communities. AAH, PARC and UWAC will do the hard construction works during Fridays and the holidays of the ICA to attract minimal attention from them, using low visibility implementation and coordinating with beneficiaries (especially animal shelter rehabilitation) to cover the new structures while ensuring that the new works will not change the overall appearance of the communities. In order to inform its assessments and implementation AAH, PARC and UWAC will continuously work on understanding gender roles. By understanding gender roles and household dynamics, AAH believes that the organisation mitigates the risks of doing harm. AAH has in place mechanisms to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse by all staff. All AAH staff has the obligation to adhere to the organisation code of conduct. AAH has in place focal points at base and mission levels. as well as safe and confidential ways to report abuse. Moreover, all staff received specific in person training on the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

AAH has conducted in its Middle East country offices a Protection Mainstreaming Internal Evaluation. Particular attention was paid to the way AAH includes vulnerable groups in its work, for instance through inclusive participatory assessments. As one of AAH’s core values, AAH thrives to prevent discrimination and to ensure equal and impartial access though transparency and accountability to affected populations. AAH aims for all community members to be involved and consulted as much as possible in project design and implementation, as well as evaluation, including through household and community consultations and focus group discussions, accessible to all. In order for their participation to be meaningful, AAH focuses on ‘qualitative inclusion’ meaning safe spaces for beneficiaries to participate. Also, AAH started engaging with Humanity & Inclusion as inclusion experts to sensitise and inform AAH’s programming. For instance, instead of asking directly if people have a disability, AAH is increasingly using ‘The Washington Group Short Set of Questions’ to detect people with disabilities. AAH is reporting under the HRP disaggregated data by sex and age; though pending the reporting format, AAH usually includes further disaggregation on vulnerability such as data on people with disabilities. In addition, AAH started in its assessments to consider ‘barriers’ of particularly vulnerable groups and ‘enabling factors’ for people with specific needs. AAH has safe and accessible beneficiary feedback mechanisms in place which allow beneficiaries to provide their inputs, complaints, and satisfaction regarding the activities implemented. Beneficiary knowledge of this system are promoted during field assessments and monitoring visits during which AAH and partners staff explain how the mechanisms work. With regards to access to assistance and in this specific project the partners will 1) Use their respective data and knowledge of the areas to prioritize in a transparent and relevant manner more at need communities2) Consult widely with community members, community leaders and MoSD, MoL and MoA directorates3) Call for applications with a fair competition mainly for the income generation activities to select most relevant and promising business to be established and access business ideas based on criteria made public.
