Project Summary

The project aims at building resilience of children to the continuous protection crisis that they face as a result of the climatic chocks and conflict that has prevailed in the country over time. The action will directly benefit 28,200 people through community interventions and case management addressing individual child abuse cases such as child labour, gender based violence just to mention a few. A multi sectoral approach will be used to ensure maximum benefit to prevent further escalation of violence against children. About 21,000 (13,000F, 8000M) children will be reached with life saving child protection in humanitarian action interventions. An additional 9000 people (5400F, 3600M) will be targeted with child protection capacity building to strengthen community based child protection structures to enhance prevention of violation as well as increase reporting, quality response and referral to service providers. The project envisions case management for survivors of sexual gender based violence, alternative care services for unaccompanied and separated children and children involved in worst forms of child labour. In addition, the proposal takes into consideration the education needs of the children as a measure of protection and includes a component of cash based assistance to boost their economic status to provide for their children. Household livelihood support will also prevent further family separations as families go out in search of improved economic status. The school will also be used as an entry point to offer child protection training to teachers, parents and children. Further psycho social support will also form part of of this project's focus in order to reduce the level of distress phased by the children living in conflict zones. This project therefore will target IDPs, host communities, and refugee returnees.

Mitigation Measures

3d. External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: Delivery of humanitarian assistance affects peaceful coexistence and spark inter community conflict over access to resources Using ‘Do no harm principles’, WV will seek to avoid physical or psycho-social risks that would be detrimental to project beneﬁciaries, and will serve all people irrespective of age, sex or clan. Given vulnerabilities associated with issues related to clan dynamics, WV has done a thorough analysis of the context and has conducted the Do- No-Harm Assessments for target districts. Host communities have also been included to avoid any conflict with the IDPs. 2a. Exclusion and marginalization: Members of marginalized community’s/ minority groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance To avoid provoking fraction among communities, the project will closely engage them throughout the project cycle, ensuring they are involved in beneficiary selection by formulating a criteria in an open and participatory process. 3b. External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance. The project will ensure all beneficiaries are fully informed of project details, and put in place accountability system to allow beneficiaries to voice their concerns and complaint regarding any threats or risks of sexual exploitation or abuse to access aid. Further WVI will ensure that all project staff and partners have been taken through the Child and Adult Safeguarding Policy and signed acknowledgement of orientation including a commitment to adhere to the policy requirements.
