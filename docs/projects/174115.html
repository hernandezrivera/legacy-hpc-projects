Project Summary

The proposed project to support livelihoods stabilization and peace building among IDPs, returnees and host community will target 6900 women and girls, men and boys, from 6 Local Government Areas in Borno and Adamawa States. The project has three component:
1. For the agricultural component of this program, 900 direct beneficiaries will be selected from rural communities hosting IDPS who have fled from their homes due to conflict. The beneficiaries will be selected regardless of tribe or religion. The women selected for participation in the program will be those who have experienced violence and/or are heading households independently. The beneficiaries will range from ages 18 and older with 3,600 indirect beneficiaries.
2. 3000 males and females from ages 11 and over will be engaged as beneficiaries through our Gender Norms Intervention. Male and female leaders from the host and IDP communities will be invited and trained as facilitators, who will in turn conduct dialogues in their communities with males and females of all ages. Family members of the participants in the agricultural and entrepreneurship will be actively encouraged to participate in the GNI initiative in order to maximize the collective impact of all program components.
3. The entrepreneurship component of the project will train 1000 selected youth (at least 50% IDPs and 40% female) in a 6-week business and entrepreneurial course and also provide training in reconciliation and participation in workshops/activities that provide spaces for psycho-social support and the strengthening of life skills. Among this number 100 selected youths formed into cooperatives will be mentored by a business coach through the formation of startups determined by them and needs of a market assessment and labor market survey. A total of 4000 indirect beneficiaries will be impacted by the intervention.

1 - Needs

Damages to several infrastructures such as market and lack of access to arable land for farming as a result of the insurgency in the northeast have led to loss of livelihoods by many. OCHA in her humanitarian situation update for August, 2019 edition reported that out of 5.5 million people that are in need of early recovery and livelihoods assistance and 4.6 million targeted, only about 267,000 people were reached leaving a lot of needs to address. Many internally displaced people, returnees and members of host communities have lost their houses and means of livelihoods to insurgency and could not provide basic needs such as food, water, shelter clothing for themselves and their immediate family.The internally displaced people living in host communities now find themselves living in a new environment where they experience difficulties sometimes in relating or coping in their new environment.

2 - Activities

Income generating activities (IGA) targeted at young women and girls. These beneficiaries will be provided with training in farming techniques and business with financial skills. Also, agricultural inputs such as improved seeds, pesticides and fertilizers will be provided after adequate training/demonstration on their use have been also determined. Research has shown that growing cash crops would have significant positive outcomes for female farmers. As such, JRS endeavours to provide the skills and inputs for female farmers to expand their source of sustainable income. The ultimate goal is the emergence of a Savings and Internal Lending Community (SILC) (commonly known as VSLA) among these women geared towards sustainability. Awareness and dignity-promotion: JRS will also distribute Dignity/hygiene kits to vulnerable females and males in the target communities. Adolescent girls will benefit from the dignity kits as most girls have been known to stay away from school during their monthly periods, and this amounts to approximately 15 days in a three-month school term. This absence from school affects their school performance. Gender Norm Intervention (GNI): Gender Norm Intervention creates opportunities for participants to share ideas and experiences with a view to changing the behaviours of participants over time. An individual is said to be reached with GNI if he or she have participated in GNI sessions for at least 10 hours over a 40-day period. A session can last one or two hours, depending on what participants agree on. GNI sessions are facilitated by trained community influencers with the aid of GNI manual or guide. Issues discussed during subsequent sessions will be generated during the first session where participants are free to mention negative gender or societal norms that affects them. The main aim of GNI is to change the behaviours of participants and the community at large as it regards negative norms that affect a group of people.The Entrepreneurship component is in three phases which include:Business Training – participants will complete a business training course in a classroom setting which will include, but not be limited to, the following topics: Introduction to Entrepreneurship, Business Plan Writing, Project Cycle Management, Marketing and Sales, Product Development, Customer Service, Access to Finance and Internal Financial Management, Human Resources Management, Social Enterprises and Collective Business Principles. In addition to the business topics, life skills and reconciliation activities through capacity building and experiential learning will also be included in the curriculum. Business Development - After the 6-week business training, a total of 400 selected participants from different LGAs will form a group and work with a business coach to elaborate a collective business concept in detail over the course of 4 weeks. Participants will be encouraged to develop business concepts that focus on generating maximum profit while also addressing a social, environmental or financial need in their community. Business Incubation - each group will present their business concept to a committee which will determine the business concept as being approved, approved with minor adjustments or in need of further revision for second review. Business supported through the project will have access to the JRS Nigeria Business Incubation services for 3 months after receiving the initial seed funding.
