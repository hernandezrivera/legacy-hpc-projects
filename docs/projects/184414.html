Project Summary

The report of FSNAU 2021 approximate that 1.2 million children will experience malnutrition and another 3.5 million people will face acute shortage of food.This has been attributed acute food insecurity in Somalia include the combined effects of poor and erratic rainfall distribution, flooding and conflict.
A delayed start, early end, and erratic rainfall distribution characterized the April to June 2021 Gu rainfall season. As a result, cumulative rainfall was below the 40-year average across much of the country. However these resources are inadequate to support normal livestock production until the start of Deyr season rainfall in October. Furthermore, floods caused further population displacement and damaged crops and farmland in riverine areas of Hiiraan, Shabelle, and Juba regions. Based on the results of household surveys and field assessments conducted in June and July 2021, more than 2.2 million people face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes in the presence of planned and likely humanitarian assistance during the July to September 2021 period. An additional 3.4 million people are Stressed (IPC Phase 2), bringing the total number of people experiencing acute food insecurity to 5.6 million. A majority of the estimated 2.9 million Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) across Somalia are poor with limited livelihood assets, few income-earning opportunities, low communal support and high reliance on external humanitarian assistance. As a result, a significant proportion of IDPs (in both rural and urban settlements) face moderate to large food consumption gaps through late 2021. Some of the urban poor across Somalia also continue to face moderate to large food consumption gaps over the same period, partly due to a slowdown in economic activities in urban areas and the rising costs of food and other essential non-food items.
Physicians Across Continents (PAC) proposes to have interventions on reduction of severe malnutrition in children (under 5yrs) the crisis affected and returnees, vulnerable population of women of reproductive age who face also nutrition deficiencies as a result of food scarcity and other cultural perspectives The project will focus on provision of curative, preventive and promotive approaches in an effort to ensure children and Pregnant and lactating women are provided with nutritious food to thrive.
The interventions for curative will be in case finding, screening, nutrition assessment and provision of treatment for complication and therapeutic feeding. PAC will deploy trained Service providers in a fixed center to receive and care until stable for discharge home. In this intervention also there will be 1 fixed center to accommodate all the sick and malnourished, that will be supported by the 2 mobile teams and 2 outreach services.
The interventions for preventive will be in deploying 20 community health workers after training Infection Control, Focused Antenatal Care,BEMONC, Screening in Malnutrition and referral pathways. On key messaging in the prevention of covid-19 at village level. Of this 20 –two third will be female health promoters, for gender empowerment and increasing facility utilization in skilled birth deliveries. During the training the community health workers will be utilized. PAC will monitor and work closely with local authority to ensure that CHWs have access smoothly to surrounding villages and referral community smoothly to fixed centre.
The interventions for promotive will focus on community engagement and dialogue in promotion of positive culture to enhance that children and PLW are provided with opportunity to seek for healthcare. PAC shall engage the local community regularly for decision making on contact tracing, supporting young girls delay in marriage to enhance future positive maternal health outcomes. PAC will also work closely with partners in protection and WASH as holistic.There will be 2 surveys conducted to inform the trends of nutrition in the community.
