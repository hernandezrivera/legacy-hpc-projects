1. Needs

HNO-the health cluster plans to reach 3.2 million people with essential lifesaving health services. This includes people who are displaced within the country, refugees from neighboring countries, and more than half a million recent returnees from Sudan. They need interventions to prevent and respond to disease outbreaks and other public health emergencies. They also need support from other sectors, such as nutrition, water, sanitation, hygiene, and food security. They also need community health services and engagement to bridge the gaps in accessing health care. The national prevalence study in 2022 found that violence against women is common in South Sudan. Physical, sexual, psychological, and economic violence and forced marriage affect many women. Depression rates are also high among the post-conflict populations. Technical support and coordination to provide quality humanitarian health services will be needed at all levels. Particularly women and children are facing elevated risks of sexual violence, sexual exploitation and control by smugglers, and are unable to move safely and freely in cities to access public areas. Sexual, reproductive, and GBV health services are facing alarming accessibility, availability and quality issues. The birth spacing component of family planning services remains non-existent in public facilities but is mainly offered by the private sector. Access to public GBV services by female refugees is highly challenged. Reproductive health services are available only in 15% communities. More than 80% of functioning PHC facilities in the west and in the east do not provide the required antenatal care services. In 2023, thus far, no health workers have been trained in clinical management of rape (CMR). Mental health services Are non-existent. The predominant number of mental health consultations is reported for the South Sudan area while absent in 80% of other districts in the country. COVID-19 led to the closure of the main psychiatric hospital in South Sudan and the suspension of outpatient services. Non-communicable disease treatment becomes less available as almost 100% of communities report hypertension and diabetes as two of the most commonly reported diseases and causes of death. Disability.
