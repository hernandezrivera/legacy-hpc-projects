Project Summary

IMC will continue its program through a community center in Jaramana and a mobile team deployed in Damascus and Babella and Yalda; this latter will be redeployed once two centers (one community center and one WGSS) will be established in the area. IMC will provide: non-formal education, skills development, social and recreational activities; rehabilitation and inclusion program targeting children with disabilities and their parents; speech therapy services ; parenting skills programs and peer to peer support groups; Youth Empowerment Program (YEP); information and awareness activities on GBV and CP topics; training to community members and frontline staff. IMC also plans to establish community-based committees to facilitate the identification of children at risks and/or survivors, raise awareness and discuss main CP concerns and solutions. Regarding GBV prevention and response, as mentioned, IMC is in the process of establishing a WGSS, which will work as entry point for identification of individuals at risk or survivors of GBV and work a safe space to access confidential support through case management, psychosocial support and referrals services. The center will also offer structured and non-structured activities to enhance awareness, prevention and response about GBV concerns. Women and girls and the communities will be consulted to better understand the main risks and the existing community support systems. WGSS will offer the following activities, aimed at building resilience, fostering social interaction, reducing GBV risks and improving beneficiaries’ safety and wellbeing: PSS activities and basic life skills programs; Conflict resolution education, a new program targeting girls and focusing on understanding rights and identify solutions, applying different techniques. The healthy life style program, a pilot to respond to the needs of elderly women with chronic diseases and exposed to violence and abuse. Health and legal services will be provided at the WGSS (through IMC and SARC staff respectively) to ensure access to safe and comprehensive services for GBV survivors, in line with the survivor centered approach. Dignity kits will be provided, as well as Individual Protection Assistance (IPA) to address the immediate consequences of GBV incidents and protection risks. Case managers will provide the assistance through assessments of each specific case and established criteria. Information and awareness sessions, in addition to capacity-building activities to community members and volunteers will also be provided in the center. IMC protection intervention will also be linked with health and livelihoods activities, specifically: 1) GBV and CP case management services are provided in static and mobile health facilities. When dealing with CP cases, the case managers will focus on the needs of the child and his/her family, in consideration of the best interests of the child and building upon the child and his/her family resilience and ensuring the child’s meaningful participation and family empowerment throughout the process. 2) Vocational training courses and related start-up kits will be provided to selected beneficiaries, at risk or survivors of CP and GBV, with the aim of reducing protection risks and increase wellbeing. Courses will be selected based on market surveys and discussions with targeted communities and beneficiaries. Furthermore, IMC plans to expand its work in Hama (exact locations tbd); most likely the intervention will be launched through mobile facilities, and focus on activities where IMC has expertise, based on needs.
IMC will continue attending relevant sector and sub-sector coordination groups, and bilaterally coordinate with actors working within the protection sector to reduce risk overlapping, enhance complementarity and enhance effective and safe external referrals. IMC protection team maintains a service mapping to facilitate external referrals for services and assistance not provided by IMC.

4 - Needs

According to the preliminary estimates, 14.2 million people are in need of protection humanitarian assistance, including over 4 million PLWD and over 6.2 children. The deterioration of the economy is forcing families to adopt negative and harmful coping mechanisms. Children continue to be exposed to a variety of risks and forms of violence and their prevalence is reported at increasing levels in all governorates. Child labor was reported as occurring in 84% of the assessed communities, family separation in 36% and psychosocial distress was reported by 26% of the surveyed households. Girls are more at risk of early marriage (reported in 71% of the assessed communities, against 62% in 2021), harassment, sexual abuse and movement restrictions; boys are more exposed to heavy child labor, negligence and harmful discipline methods, recruitment, detention, family separation and engagement in illegal activities. Women and girls are disproportionately affected by GBV and despite under-reporting (due to stigma, social and traditional norms, lack of awareness and support services), humanitarian partners and IMC teams confirm they are on the rise. IMC data for case management services shows an increase of 110% in the number of GBV survivors seeking support in 2021 compared to 2020 and of 44% for CP cases. Another worrying trend is related to the increased violence against older women, mostly psychological and physical violence, neglect, denial of resources and IPV.

5 - Implementation of activities

IMC will implement the following activities: -CP and GBV case management at IMC PHC health and protection facilities (static and mobiles), with facilitation of internal and external referrals as needed and in the respect of the principles of safety, confidentiality and survivor-centered approach. -Non-formal education, life skills and empowerment programs, social and recreational activities at IMC protection facilities (static and mobiles).-Rehabilitation and inclusion program targeting children with intellectual disabilities and developmental delays and their caregivers; speech therapy services as needed. Implemented at IMC static protection facilities. -Parenting skills programs at IMC static facilities (health and protection).-Information and awareness raising activities for beneficiaries and community members, on topics selected based on the most recurrent risks reported by each target community. This will be implemented at all IMC sites and vocational training centers.-Training to IMC and other organizations staff, and community members on different topics as per needs assessed in each community of intervention.-Vocational training courses (through private institutions) and provision of start-up kits. -Distribution of dignity kits.IMC community feedback mechanisms will be adapted to the specific context of implementation and will allow beneficiaries to express their feedback and level of satisfaction on the services, submit complaints, and suggest improvement.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Lack of/low quality of services including case management and related external referrals, might expose survivors to further harm, discrimination, stigmatization. Mitigation measures include: training and supervision of case managers, frontline staff and volunteers, according to relevant minimum standards and protocols including those related to confidentiality.Resistance from the community on GBV/CP messaging and activities, resulting in low participation, stigmatization or harm to target groups. Mitigation measures: community and leaders’ engagement on all phases of the project cycle; adapting awareness and information messages to the specific context; only female staff working in WGSS; engagement of volunteers from the target communities.Risks related to the location of service provision (safety of movements for beneficiary, contamination from remnants of war, proximity with sensitive buildings). Mitigation measures: choice of locations done in close consultation and agreement with the community, conduction of safety audits. COVID 19: Not adherence to preventive and IPC related measures can increase the risk of transmission to beneficiaries. Mitigation measures include: monitoring of adherence to preventive measures; reduction of beneficiaries numbers for group activities; provision of PPE; trainings/refreshers; suspension of service provision if increase in the number of staff with confirmed/suspected infection occurs.PSEA: please refer to the dedicated section.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SEA related risks include abuse and exploitation both towards staff and beneficiaries seeking services at IMC facilities, either in exchange for services/assistance or not. At organizational level, all acts falling under the definition of SEA are prohibited and considered a gross violation of IMC Code of Conduct, which all IMC employees must sign and commit to adhere to. The Code is supported by IMC Safeguarding and Whistleblower Policy. All employees and volunteers are required to complete a mandatory training on the Code of Conduct & Ethics and Safeguarding Policy, and on reporting mechanisms, with refreshers provided on annual basis. IMC HQ has established a Safeguarding Task Force while at country level there are designated focal points in charge of training and awareness to staff and beneficiaries and ensuring safe and appropriate referrals in case of any incident being reported and confirmed by investigation. Furthermore, IMC Syria MEAL team conducts mandatory and refresher trainings on the community-based feedback and response mechanism for all IMC staff and volunteers. Awareness and information sessions to beneficiaries regarding PSEA and reporting mechanism are also provided, together with information on how to safely report any incident.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1: Delayed/lack of approvals. Mitigation: Regular follow up with relevant authorities, advocacy for uninterrupted access. 2: Resistance and non-acceptance of IMC activities from the community. Mitigation: Community engagement and awareness through culturally and context adapted tools and communication strategies, especially for new areas of intervention. 3: Delays in international bank transfers. Mitigation: Maintain more than one bank account; advocate for dedicated channels for humanitarian transfers; info sharing with partners and donors. 4: COVID 19 limit capacity to deliver services either because of lockdowns or infection among staff, as well as reducing opportunities for monitoring and supervision activities. Mitigation: waivers to allow movement of staff and mobile facilities; adherence to preventive and isolation measures; temporary suspension of services to protect beneficiaries from transmission, remote case management services; establishment of remote monitoring modalities based on the specifics of the activities. 5: Deterioration of the security situation. Mitigation: monitoring and info sharing with relevant stakeholders and temporary closure of facilities if risks raise; security/surveillance at facilities, premises.
