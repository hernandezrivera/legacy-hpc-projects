Project Summary

CCS is a project for direct implementation that aims to improve access to health and nutrition services to the most vulnerable communities, towards preventing the occurrence of acute malnutrition among under-five age children, pregnant and lactating women in Ancuabe, Balama and Namuno and ultimately contribute towards stunting reduction in the province.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Overall, Cabo Delgado is a province with lots of vulnerabilities: second highest rate of stunting (53%) per the last DHS and recent food security and seasonal assessments conducted by SETSAN been consistently identifying different districts with prevalence of acute malnutrition above acceptable ranges (<5%) according IPC for AMN protocols, very low IYCF practices and poor sanitation practices. With the on-going situation (conflict, massive and daily increasing exodus), it increases even higher the vulnerability of the affected population to life threatening situations, such as malnutrition. In this regard, this project was developed with the uptmost aim to prevent that the conflict affected population in the target areas of this project die from malnutrition and protected their wellbeing throught provision of life-saving health and nutrition services and empowering them to be resilient. The key target groups of this project are children under five age, pregnant and lactating women (even if adolescents pregnants) in IDPs and government sectors with nutrition activities at province and district level for advocacy related activities.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Primary prevention- Create partnership with health cluster and the nutrition program at DPS to support on the routine program immunization, vitamin A supplementation, deworming for children and iron folate acid for pregrant and lactating women. These activities would be implemented in strict collaboration with the Servicos Distritais de Mulher e Accao Social (SDMAS). The modalities would be through integrated mobile teams to IDP locations and hosting communities organized in collaboration with local health authority and partners. Delivery would also be at health facility level. Secondary prevention- Regular MUAC nutrition screening for early detection of acute malnutrition for children under five age as well as pregnant and lactacting women, and referral as appropriate for treatment services. In line with the delivery approach described under the primary prevention, MUAC screening would be integrated in the mobile clinics too.- Training of APE and ACS in the identification of cases of acute malnutrition at the family level through the use of MUAC.Educational and behaviour change approach- IYCF couselling activities to caregivers on how to feed their child, breastfeeding promotion and culinary demonstration using local language, community theaters with key information, risk factors to acute malnutrition and how to avoid. Local radio stations may be used and regular meetings with pregnant and lactacting women and caregivers in the IDP setting in close collaboration with the SDMAS and local partners. APEs, ACS, traditional midwives and Mother models approach will also be used. - Empowerment approach- Training of mothers or key child caregivers to screen their children for acute malnutrition throught the MUAC-family approach to early detect acute malnutrition and refer the child to a health center for treatment as appropriate. In collaboration with the local leaders in IDPs settings and hosting communities, there will be a mapping exercise to identify caregivers who have children under five age then, in collaboration with the SDMAS, plans to train mothers in MUAC-family approach will be done and delivered. Coordination with the SDMAS will be negotiated to establish a mechanism to identify and account caregivers who referred children acute malnourished identified via MAUC-family approach.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The ABS and AMODEC consortium will follow the "Do no harm" principle when collecting data and feedback, which will be done confidentially to avoid exposing the target population to additional risks.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

Mechanisms and approaches will be adapted to the context, such as the use of the local language; ensuring everyone's participation and inclusion. The Consortium will address comments as appropriate, forward to relevant teams as needed and communicate with communities about the results during ongoing activities or subsequent consultations.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project includes good practices for safe, non-discriminatory and supportive care and learning environments, regardless of any disabilities. Before implementation, an assessment will be carried out to identify people with disabilities (hearing, visual, cognitive, physical), where they will have more special attention, so that they can access assistance and obtain their benefits.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The CCS Project is environmentally friendly. The activities have no negative influence on the preservation of the environment.
