Project Summary

Needs:
After 13 years of ongoing and escalating hostilities, along with the continuous economic crisis, the situation in Syria worsened significantly, especially when a series of earthquakes hit northern Syria in February 2023. This led to the displacement of hundreds of families and caused severe damage to the infrastructure already weakened by previous conflicts. Many families lost their main breadwinners due to death or injury, at a time when the economic situation was already dire, exacerbating the vulnerability of hundreds of thousands of people who previously struggled to meet their basic needs. The ability to provide essential services continued to deteriorate, with water systems, sanitation, and public health services coming under immense pressure in a context where there is hardly any developmental investment. The recurring outbreaks of diseases, waterborne illnesses, prolonged drought, water crisis, and food insecurity contribute to increased mortality and morbidity rates, including higher levels of malnutrition and humanitarian needs.
Idleb Governorate has transitioned from acceptable levels to severe levels of comprehensive malnutrition. By October 2023, the cost of the food basket had doubled compared to January of the previous year and quadrupled over two years. The crisis in Idleb creates ongoing cycles of vulnerability disproportionately affecting the most vulnerable individuals, including persons with disabilities. The situation is characterized by an increasing number of people in need of unavailable basic assistance, making them more vulnerable and putting pressure on social cohesion. The population in the area experiences almost complete food insecurity, with 90% living below the poverty line. The widespread displacement of most indigenous populations, destruction of infrastructure and homes, crop destruction, and damage to agricultural land have led to increased poverty levels and decreased income. Food indicators have also declined due to economic sanctions imposed on the country, weakening purchasing power and increasing the difficulty in providing agricultural inputs and food supplies.
Activities:
• Conduct training courses for 150 beneficiaries on successful rooftop farming techniques.
• Distribute agricultural kits containing necessary tools for implementing this step, including plastic planting containers, soil, various vegetable seeds, pruning shears, soil turning tool, pesticide sprayer, watering hose, and a 200-liter plastic barrel.
The implementation of the above activities aims to achieve self-sufficiency for families and secure a small profitable project for those involved, with the goal of improving the financial status of low-income households.
