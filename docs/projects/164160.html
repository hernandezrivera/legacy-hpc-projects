Project Summary

In response to the Objective 1: Provide life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support, Field assessments showed that the situation in East Ghouta is rapidly unfolding while uncertainty over the precise numbers of returnees in the different towns of Duma, Hosh Nasri, Hosh Eldawahreh, Shafuniyeh, and Hosh Elfara where severity is 5. The current HNO estimates put the number 46,497 returned to their towns, majority is women and children (60%). The inhabitants are in dire need for any kind of support because they are under restricted movement measures including ban access for market supplies. People have settled randomly despite the damaged houses with open windows and walls and without any access to a proper living condition or privacy. This number is subject to be increased based on the movement of displaced people back to East Ghouta during the harsh winter season, based on UN OCHA most recent report that states that 26,000 are returning from other governorates, while some 25,000 are returning to their homes from the same governorate after long and multiple displacement. ONG Rescate conducted field assessment in East Ghouta (Duma, and its surrounding towns) with Syria Trust (ST) support. Beneficiaries asked for household items to be able to establish primary living standard for cooking, and sleeping, especially that they returned to looted houses; besides, they were unable to bring these items with them nor have the resources to buy them. These families are in urgent need of the Sector Basic NFI Kit in addition to items as provided by potential beneficiaries during field visits. It contains at minimum all essential items (5 Blankets; 5 bed sheets; 5 Mattresses; 5 sleeping bags, Kerosene Cooker; plastic sheet, 2 Solar Lantern; and 1 portable heaters) will be the kits content due to extra hardship during the harsh winters in Syria to 2500 families in the five targeted towns. Rescate focused on Duma and the surrounding towns because these areas are under-served with limited access for the inhabitants.
Rescate will maintain monitoring the NFI delivery, and will work through an established network of NGO partners such as ST and SSSD to ensure distribution as follows:
•Determine the NFI distribution mechanism in recognition of the availability of goods, the urgent needs of the returnees, and the operational network of NGO partners;
•Assemble the NFIs Kits target assistance to total of at least 12,500 beneficiaries;
•In cooperation with partner NGOs, will identify vulnerable individuals, and prioritizing assistance for most vulnerable populations, including women and children. The distributions of NFI will take place in the geographical areas of the selected beneficiaries which will be determined in cooperation with the partner NGOs, to the most affected and vulnerable population.
•Rescate team will produce progress reports on implementation and share with the donor. Progress will be measured against indicators listed in the narrative and quality assurance of services delivered will be assessed through internal program reviews, including site visits, data and records review, and beneficiaries' interviews to address bottlenecks.
•Rescate will provide overall oversight and technical backstopping for this operation. Rescate will maintain detailed records of all beneficiaries. Data will be disaggregated according to age, gender, nationality, previous location in Syria. All data will fall under Rescate’s Data Protection policy accordingly.
•Monitoring will be part of an on-going process implemented throughout the course of the project.
•In addition, Rescate will conduct post-monitoring distribution for 15% of the total distributions conducted under this action. The teams with local NGOs staff carry out those post-monitoring visits two weeks after the distribution or completion of works to verify final use of commodities and assess satisfaction.

4 - Needs

Rescate carried out field assessments to multiple sites in East Ghouta. Results of the visits revealed that there is dire need for mattresses, high thermal blankets, and cooking utensils. Beneficiaries left their homes seeking safety leaving behind all their living commodities and are now unable to restore the missing items. The targeted area is under limited access measures, adding to the vulnerability of the population there as they have depleted their resources to feed their families under lack of livelihood opportunities. Therefore; organizations’ support is very important to help people live in minimum normalcy by securing cooking and warmth utensils and items. Rescate will coordinate to combine efforts with other actors providing other items to the same areas, such as food parcels (WFP). In coordination with the acting NGOs, stakeholders and communities, Rescate had discussions and most of the persons met asked for home utensils and warmth covers and mattresses. Hence, Rescate will address the urgent needs of 12,500 most vulnerable returnees, IDPs and families who did not leave their homes during the events, including women, children, elderly and people with disabilities, in Syria through the provision of NFIs in coordination with established networks with NGOs partners. • The total project targets assistance a minimum of 12,500 vulnerable individuals. • Targeted beneficiaries are: • Returnees living in collective accommodations such as unfinished buildings, and damaged houses. • Returnees living with host families, IDPs and families who did not leave their homes. • Households facing financial insecurity, often unable to leave the areas due to lack of resources • Families hosting returnees who are stretching their resources • Minimum of 12,500 most vulnerable population will improve their living conditions by received NFIs assistance. ONG Rescate has access to the intervention zone due to its local partners working already in the zone (ST and SSSD).

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk (PR): It is likely that activities might create some inter or intra-community tension due to service delivery locations or service types if one community is served and another is not. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. PR: misunderstanding with the community has been identiﬁed in rural areas where there is lack of services. If the population doesn't understand the purpose and goal of the activities proposed, it might create a negative effect and prevent the quality of the implementation of the project. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High.Mitigation measures for both PRs: Rescate and Partner SSSD who is a registered NGO, and is already working in Duma will both ensure consultations with the targeted communities and neighbouring ones, create a mapping of services and involve the community in the activities’ design. Based on lessons learnt, Rescate and IP will create and support the process to undertake explanation to the community and encourage acceptance. In addition, Rescate/IP will monitor population movement and community tensions to be aware of the situation in the ﬁeld and design the program respecting the “Do No Harm” approach. Speciﬁc tool will be designed for data collection where field staff of Rescate & Partner will conduct a questionnaire to follow up and monitor the situation through regular ﬁeld visits and data updates through focus groups, interviews, observation and surveys. PR: SEA & GBVLikelihood: Medium. Impact: High.Mitigation: a training will be provided to service providers and staff members to ensure knowledge and commitment of the Code of Conduct. Rescate ﬁeld based staff already communicate on regular basis and will continue to explain to the community about the services available and the activities implemented. In addition, the community focal points and stakeholders are consulted and can call Rescate team within the frame of the complaint mechanism set up in the course of the project’s activities.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

This project is likely to obtain approval because it is designed in response to MOSAL priorities. The targeted areas are nominated by stakeholders and local communities. There is a risk of discrimination or perceptions of discrimination if a service is provided to specific groups or individuals at the exclusion of others. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High. Mitigation Measures: In order to ensure protection mainstreaming, Rescate will work with ST and SSSD based on their long experience in the same services and sites. We will share with the communities’ leaders the assurance of the comprehensive approach toward vulnerability of the beneficiaries. Rescate/partners will work with community leaders in transparency sharing the selection criteria, its intended target population information, and the organization’s core values, mandate and vision during the allocation of the areas/beneficiaries. Rescate will also ensure active involvement at all times of the wider community, ensuring that the activities are accessible for examination and inspection and will assist beneficiaries to actively involve throughout the projects’ life cycle. During the registration, Rescate staff will ensure to take clear information about the status of each family member so that special need, if required, will be directed to those in need such as, elderly, persons with specific needs, GBV survivors, and FHHs who are considered among the most vulnerable groups. Rescate will ensure mitigation measures are taken in consultation with other partners from the sector so nothing will go wrong during the implementation of the project. Rescate will provide NFI items in consultation with stakeholders and will coordinate with relevant partners to ensure do no harm. The intervention will focus on returnees to Duma and the surrounding towns reaching to (12,500 individuals) based on the local communities’ information that these areas are under-served by local and international actors.
