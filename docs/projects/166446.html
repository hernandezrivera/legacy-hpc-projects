Project Summary

Oxfam’s COVID-19 response aims to meaningfully engage affected women, men, girls, and boys in the design and delivery of communication resources adapted to their different needs to facilitate community solutions within the changing context of the crisis. The project will employ Oxfam’s community engagement approach which focuses on the active participation of women, men, girls’ and boys and especially of at-risk groups among them (elderly, with medical conditions, and low immunity, and persons with disabilities). Oxfam staff with community-based volunteers (CBVs) will build on its existing networks and collectives in the community among women, men, and youth. In addition, engagement with religious and other local leaders with influence at the community level will act as links and drivers for COVID-19 prevention, mitigation and response, and its relevant advocacy actions. This includes addressing different concerns based on gender, age, and disability, and translating those concerns into achievable actions. By working with and through a community network, community capacities are strengthened, advocacies are responsive, and respect for local culture and beliefs will be ensured, which are essential to enhance the appropriateness of COVID-19 interventions. Based on the identified gaps and epidemiological realities over time, the CBVs will be trained and supported to facilitate quick referrals of COVID-19 suspected cases to the health facilities, as well as referrals for other services (e.g. GBV, NFI, etc) as appropriate.

Needs

Vulnerability and capacity to cope in this COVID-19 crisis are largely defined by age, medical conditions, socio-cultural norms, gender, disability, and immunity status in both Rohingya and host communities. In recent studies in the Rohingya response (done by BRAC University & UN Women), women were reported to know less about COVID-19 than men – a result of disproportionate gender access, gaps in message delivery and socio-cultural norms. As with the nature of gender and protection issues in refugee camps, deep-seated socio-cultural gender beliefs are conflating with religious beliefs surrounding the COVID-19 disease, which put the blame on women. This will likely worsen the stigma already experienced based on gender, age, sex and disability, and prevent health-seeking behaviours needed for COVID-19 prevention. Further, barriers to implementing basic hygiene measures, such as hand-washing (e.g. handbasins or sinks may be physically inaccessible, or a person may have physical difficulty rubbing their hands together thoroughly) increase for those with disabilities and that barrier is compounded for persons with disabilities that are women or girls. Community networks we work with share that such risks increase with the anxiety and reduced humanitarian access – with reported increases of intimate partner violence and unpaid care work. In addition, both people with disabilities and the elderly population have limited access to information and this is again compounded for elderly women or women with disabilities. Both older men and older women have limited knowledge about the how COVID-19 is transmitted, its symptoms and how they could protect themselves against the disease. In addition, women’s access to information is limited and often male members of their families are their only source of information. Largely excluded from community decision making, women, girls, and vulnerable persons have lesser means to participate in the response. Barriers to participation are seen to worsen given the reduced mobility, telecommunication blockage and reduced humanitarian operations. Women and girls as primary care givers of children and ill persons at home and role as water collectors in crowded water points prevent them from social distancing while persons with disabilities also may have difficulty in enacting social distancing because of additional support needs. Despite these issues, a growing network of CBOs of women, men, and youth are active in funding solutions within their communities, even during COVID-19 crisis. This includes women’s self-organized collectives in both Rohingya and host communities – but humanitarian actors miss the opportunity to meaningfully engage them for a better response. Therefore, the approach, design, delivery and reach of communication resources, including COVID-19 messages are not widely adapted to equitably reach women, men, girls and boys and the most vulnerable among them. Unequal access to information are likely likewise experienced by elderly, persons with medical conditions and with disability.

Activities or outputs

Assessment/ Researches/ Studies: Support local design improvements of handwashing devices in collaboration with DPHE to reduce likelihood of contamination at touch points; encourage handwashing by incorporating nudges and additional attributes that make them user-friendly, at a relatively low cost. Test the suitability for various users (children, women, elderly, PWDs) and report the community feedback. Study the impact of handwashing behaviour in both the refugee and host communities with these improved devices, including Oxfam’s handwashing device.Communication Resources: participatory design, translation, production and distribution of IEC materials in line with the guidance by the RCCE committee on COVID-19 signs/symptoms and prevention behaviours. Integrate gender and protection messages to mitigate corresponding risks of COVID-19 to effectively engage and reach women, men, girls and boys and vulnerable groups among them. Gender and protection analysis to inform the design, approach and delivery of the RCCE resources for the project. Also, stakeholder mapping and power analysis of communities to inform communication plans and channels. Consultations will be done to ensure content of materials used are technically sound and appropriate (e.g. varied messaging for women/men and elderly vs. Youth), including the preferred mode of delivery (audio, language appropriate text, pictorial representations etc). Capacity development of the staff and community groups being engaged on COVID-19 awareness and prevention practices: provide joint trainings (WASH, EFSVL, Gender, and Protection) and continuous dialogues with communities (particularly the CBVs) to strengthen their capacities to prevent COVID-19 and effectively do referrals of suspected COVID-19 cases, as well as other services (such as GBV). All the CBVs & casual labours will be supplied with a mobilization kit (comprising of PPE) to ensure they’re protected as they carry out activities in the communities. Key stakeholders will also be oriented on how to provide feedback on their concerns and supported in their proposed community-led solutions. Community Engagement: identify community groups that are at high-risk of infection with COVID-19 and vulnerable (such as the elderly, women, PwDs) and those that are influencers (such as religious leaders) in the targeted communities to understand their perceptions, coping mechanisms, current behaviour, options for health seeking, rumours and misinformation on COVID-19. These community groups will be trained to improve their leadership capacities on prevention activities through community-based plans to address pertinent gender and protection issues such as stigma and any other misconceptions. They will also be engaged to strengthen the link between communities and health centres to ensure rapid and appropriate referrals. Mass awareness through megaphone and miking will be done to reduce face to face contact between staff, CBVs, and beneficiaries in view of physical distancing.Information and Feedback Service centers/Facilities: conduct public health promotion campaigns using appropriate sex-segregated communication channels in consultation with women, men, girls and boys; and ensure safe space for two-way discussion (where physical distancing allows); to increase knowledge of the differential and gendered risks and preventative measures; and dispel unhelpful rumours and myths. Oxfam will use the Community Perception Tracker (CPT) to collect, analyse and close the feedback loop by either provide answers to community questions and needs.
