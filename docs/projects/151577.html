Project Summary

The project is responding to humanitarian needs of drought affected community in target locations of Puntland and Somaliland through cash injection to address the immediate food needs of the people. Cash transfer programming will be used to reach households that are in IPC 3 and above. While focusing on life-saving interventions, the project will also support recovery and resilience building of the people where possible.
Improving food availability through support to agricultural production in Awdal, and introducing it in Sool and Sanag regions of Somalia is seen as an entry point to diversify pastoral livelihoods of those two regions. This plan involves also supporting communities’ recovery and livelihoods diversification interventions through livestock support with vaccination and treatment and Improving Agricultural Production/Food Security.
Livestock production was severely affected by the drought leading to substantially reduced herds; which is the country’s prime investment especially in rural communities. The mains reasons leading to the death were water shortages and lack of pasture. In view of this situation the project will explore fodder production combined with campaigns on livestock vaccination and treatment. These include promotion of carrying capacity for sustained production. These actions will be coupled with Cash for Work activities which will be used for communal asset development linked to crop and animal husbandry.
In improving agricultural production/ food security, the project will use conservational agriculture model as a climate smart agriculture technology to facilitate regeneration of agricultural production among agro-pastoral households. The beneficiaries will be given seed to improve seed security for the coming farming season. Farmers will be trained in collaboration with the Ministry of Agriculture on agricultural techniques to obtain optimal yields from their fields. Upon harvesting farmers will be able to reserve part of the harvest as seed for next season.
In times of shock and more critical food insecurity, Oxfam will utilize multi-purpose cash transfers to meet the immediate emergency needs of at risk communities. More structurally, Oxfam will help to setup mobile early warning on dissemination of national early warning info to communities to enhance preparedness measures.

Needs

Food Availability and Access: According to FAO-FSNAU Post-Gu Assessment (2018), Food insecurity is a major concern in Awdal, Sanaag and Sool regions, with a total of 747,000 Acute Food Insecure People at IPC 2 or above in August 2018 (306 000 in Awdal, 227 000 in Sanaag and 214 000 in Sool) . This translates to 48% of the total population of three regions being acute food insecure. Most Households have little or no food stocks due to low production and limited purchasing power due to depleted income sources. Acute food insecurity is prevalent among populations who lost most of their animals and who became destitute during the 2016-2017 severe drought. In addition, those affected by Cyclone Sagar in April and May 2018 and others who were affected by large-scale and protracted displacement due to drought continue to face large food consumption gaps .If the 2018 Deyr season performs below average, access to typical sources of food and income, including agricultural labor, crop production, and livestock sales, will remain strained throughout the projection period. Pastoralists in northern and central regions lost a large number of livestock in 2017 and the recovery of herds to pre-crisis levels will require several consecutive, favorable seasons . The recurrent drought has affected the livelihoods of the pastoral community as livestock, the main source of food and income, has predominantly perished affecting the coping mechanism of the people. This plan will support immediate food needs of the people for a period of two months through provision of MPCT for target IDP, pastoralists who lost their livestock and are in the rural area to improve access to food. Complementary activities are planned to ensure community short term and medium livestock and agricultural support tis given in an effort to support livelihoods options of the people.

Activities

1. Through Multi-Purpose Cash Transfer, 20 400 HH will receive $85 each per month for 3 months. This will be transferred through ZAAD mobile money transfer which has a is reliable network coverage.2. Cash for Work (CFW) will be rolled out for the construction and/or rehabilitation communal assets identified by Awdal, Sool and Sanag communities. Beneficiaries will be paid a sum of $6 per head per day through mobile money transfer. Beneficiaries will work for 10 days per month over two consecutive months. 3. Strengthen crop production through distribution of drought resistant inputs, provision of tools to and training of 2 000 Farmers (benefiting 12 000 people) in post-harvest management. 4. Support 3 333 farmers (20 000 people) to pilot fodder production and preservation. This will increase livestock access to biomass, thereby minimizing the risk of livestock death associated with scarcity of food. 5. In partnership with the Ministry of Livestock, Oxfam will reach out to 6 000 pastoralists through two livestock vaccination and treatment campaigns. It is critical to note that Oxfam does not have an animal vaccination license, therefore it will provide logistical support to Ministry of Livestock who will directly vaccinate and treat livestock. 6. Facilitate income generation and vocational skill training (in entrepreneurship, financial literacy and basic marketing) for 1 100 youths ( 600 females and 500 males).7. Pilot mobile early warning system on dissemination of national early warning information to 27 000 people so as to enhance community disaster preparedness mechanisms.8. Development of self-help groups such as Voluntary Savings and Loans Associations (VSLA), for 5 000 youths (2 500 males and 2 500 females) so as to diversify their livelihoods options. This will be complemented by a comprehensive training program on VSLA methodology and entrepreneurship modules.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The response plan proposed is addressing resilience and recovery of the target community to help communities improve adoptive capacity to drought both in host and IDP settlements, offering different approaches based on the preferences, vulnerabilities and needs identified by the communities. Oxfam will work with IDP and host community to define food security and livelihoods interventions that consider people’s choices, for example vocational skills training for those in IDP camps, and agriculture for host community in rural areas who have access to land. Oxfam will explore working with IDP’s on how to tackle the problem of access to land when it comes to livestock rearing and agricultural livelihoods. This response plan will bridge short term humanitarian response to medium and long-term livelihoods diversification. Disaster risk reduction and climate change adoption activities are part of the commitments of Oxfam. Somaliland government priority under the NDPII (National development plan II) include resilience of the local communities to withstand shocks and stresses. Somalia government is similarly focusing resilience and sustainable food of its communities to avert and tackle effects of recurrent drought on Somali people. This project contributes to meeting government’s efforts on saving lives of the people facing food insecurity situation primarily and promote resilience endeavors of the government and take steps in developing a social protection programme in Somaliland.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

RISK 1: Overlap of resources might take place in specific location, leading to beneficiary duplication. MITIGATION MEASURE 1: Ensure that Oxfam does coordination with other actors operational in the area and ensure overlap of same activities does not take place. This could be achieved through strong participation of FSC meeting, Regional FSCL and Somalia cash working group. Ensure that accountability hotline and beneficiary verification process (at Household and community level) is in place to limit duplication. RISK 2: Conflict ignited when some villages receive UCT and others do not. UCT may escalate latent clan conflict, for example between host and displaced communities MITIGATION MEASURES 2: Encouraging use of feedback and accountability system carry out proper analysis of village dynamics before the intervention, close consultation with authorities, participatory community engagement and verification of beneficiaries. RISK 3: Unwillingness of target groups to participate in project activities (use the resources / establish plots / scale up operations to harvest produce for local markets / be trained. MITIGATION MEASURES 3: Ensure target group involvement in project design, implementation and monitoring / evaluation; ensure downward accountability / feedback loops . RISK 4: Beneficiaries of UCT may be targeted and forced to share amount received or pay commission to community leaders authorities. Community gate keepers influence selection process/nepotism. MITIGATION MEASURES 4: Proper mobilization of communities, dissemination of Oxfam complains mechanisms, ensure fair selection process. Involvement of Oxfam staff in selection and verification process.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

RISK 1: Drastic increase in number of female, youths and children IDPs and/or shifts in geographic locations for priority IDP relief. MITIGATION MEASURES 1: Monitor situation regularly; close coordination with other agencies and UN OCHA and NGO Security Forum; respond in accordance with the Oxfam Emergency Response Plan, ensure that gender and protection analysis forms the start-up of any intervention RISK 2: Unsatisfied female and youth beneficiaries Excluding child headed HH, female headed HH Disability and elderly people that have no families support, minorities and marginalized groups. MITIGATION MEASURES 2: Close consultation with different parties of community’s women, men boys and girls including disabilities, develop proper selection criteria, dissemination of Oxfam community feedback mechanism Adequate communication with and involvement of IDPs / host community leaders; clear identification and selection criteria; transparency with relevant stakeholders; joint planning and meetings, donor visits and assessments. RISK 3: Exclusion of Vulnerable women and girls due to limited access to community decision making process e.g widows, divorced. MITIGATION MEASURES 3: Ensure that vulnerable women groups participate community engagement process have access to registration, community committee are briefed vulnerability criteria and protection issues to avoid risk of exclusion. RISK 4: Excluding the right people affected by drought, not identifying the most vulnerable MITIGATION MEASURES 4: Oxfam is going community targeting approach. However during beneficiary selection, a joint team of Oxfam and community representatives will conduct the selection of the target beneficiaries. The beneficiaries will be selected based on level of vulnerability. Criteria for selection of beneficiaries to be done and shared with community representatives. Thus Our team will ensure that right people are included/selected.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

RISK 1: Deterioration of security situation (including political instability/renewed fighting). MITIGATION MEASURES 1: Continuous coordination with government, local authorities, partners and key stakeholders; monitor situation regularly; work closely with OCHA/ UN and INGOs; security support in place; regular context/conflict analysis; continue to update contingency plans; temporarily suspend or withdraw staff to safety as necessary. RISK 2: Limited Humanitarian Access in target areas due to insecurity. MITIGATION MEASURES 2: (Joint) risk assessments will regularly be conducted and all movement to project sites will be coordinated with other humanitarian actors. Close coordination with the UNDSS, INSO, UN clusters to negotiate access with the government and non-governmental forces in order to reach people in need. Staff will adhere to Oxfam security protocols when implementing project activities. Oxfam security team will provide support to staff in terms of security updates and appropriate measures should there be security and accessibility challenges. RISK 3: Sporadic clan conflict might limit access to specific locations. MITIGATION MEASURES 3: Oxfam has field offices in Burao and a satellite office in Garadag and Oog making it easier for our national team to work and travel in remote areas. We continue to monitor the flare-ups in clan conflict, and ensure that conflict sensitive approach is taken in selection of project sites to not feed into clan conflict. RISK 4: Clan conflict ignited when some villages receive UCT and others do not. MITIGATION MEASURES 4: Transparent information sharing with beneficiaries around selection. Use of a mixed section approach, consulting with communities and authorities as well as a detailed needs-driven assessment.
