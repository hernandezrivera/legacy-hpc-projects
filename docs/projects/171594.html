Project Summary

This proposal is fully in line with the strategic objective 1 and 2 of Education Cluster, SDG4 and CRC art.28 and 29, aiming to guarantee accessibility to quality education for children living in Area C communities. TCC in partnership with WeWorld-GVC aims to reach children in the most vulnerable communities in the West Bank, through working in direct rehabilitation of 16 educational infrastructures (schools and KGs) along with improving their preparedness and increase their capacities to respond to emergencies, and through improving access to educational services in 6 communities located in Area C by providing facilitated means of transportation.
TCC and WW-GVC are active members of the Education cluster and are committed to promote the quality education in Palestine, supporting the MoEHE in providing adequate education services as rehabilitation of education infrastructure, responding to the lack of foreseen implementation by the duty bearers, due to the fiscal crisis and financial cuts from major donors.
According to OCHA oPt, the protracted conflict and the occupation have compromised the access to education in the occupied Palestinian territory. In the West Bank, access to education is undermined due to the detention of children, military operations and settlers-related incidents; the shortage of physical infrastructure due to building restrictions in Area C; and movement restrictions such as the checkpoints and the Barriers. Additionally, on top of conflict related needs and implications on the education system in oPt, COVID-19 pandemic caused school closures (3161 schools including UNRWA and private schools, and 2017 KGs), resulting in new challenges to the functionality and accessibility to education. Therefore, the proposed interventions aim to increase equitable safe access to inclusive and quality education in 23 communities in Area C, through a combination of hard and soft components in line with the mandates, strategies, and expertise of TCC and WW-GVC.
Based on that, the project proposal includes soft and hard components as follows:1) Renting of school vehicles to assure safe access to Education for students obliged to walk on rough roads in winter and summer, operation and monitoring of the vehicles are under MoE responsibility 2) rehabilitation/ Upgrading/ or yard rehabilitation of 16 primary schools and KG infrastructures to ensure a safe and protective environment for quality education; 3) Provision of IT assets for schools staff (laptops) to support the targeted schools in responding to COVID-19 pandemic through providing the basic tools for remote education; 4) Advocacy activities to allow the bus to take the children from the community, and also for monitoring of daily passage of students and teachers on seam zone checkpoint. 5) Provision of school-based psychosocial support to school staff, students and parents affected by the education-related violations by several trainings and workshops in coordination and with support of MoE.
The actions will target 2724 students (986 boys, 1509 girls), 229 Teachers and counsellors (102 men, 127 women), including 41 PWDs, and reach official decision-makers, media and donors through advocacy actions, and at least 11840 individuals living in the targeted communities.
TCC and WW-GVC will implement the project proposal in coordination with MoE in line with signed agreements that facilitate the entrance and interventions in the government schools selected.

1 - Needs

According to MOE and Education Cluster, it was estimated that over 400000 children across the oPt need humanitarian assistance related to Education in emergency, of whom 9780 are children living with a disability (CWD), who face challenges in accessing quality education in a safe, child-friendly environment, in addition to 14917 teachers (6008 M, 8910 F). Students in Area C are facing restrictions and violations on their right to access to safe education. Through the Community-based Protection Approach, WeWorld-GVC has been collecting quantitative and qualitative data in West Bank about 16 sectors including Education sector, to collect and systematize protection related information on vulnerabilities, threats, capacities and coping strategies, with a community engagement method. The assessment targeted most vulnerable communities in area C, conducted by WW-GVC in February 2020, underlines the persisting diﬃcult environment, continuously challenged protection threats, restriction of movements and access to education. Girls and boys students are considered a vulnerable group due to the long distances they are forced to walk in order to reach educational services, especially for the communities that are adjoined to bypass roads or use deteriorated roads to access the centre of services; in addition, the high exposure to Israelis attacks, IF and settlers, while accessing to education services and schools. They are generally not safe due to exposure to the threat of tear gas and sound bombs thrown by the IF, and at risk of inspections in cases of security escalation. The IF violence actions faced by the girls and boys students include: detainment, access restriction, several schools’ evacuations; physical attacks; regular verbal harassment and running people over in the street. Girls and boys accessing schools or even staying inside are always intimidated and threatened by the high Israeli presence. Different challenges are faced by boys and girls: especially male teenagers who are more exposed to IF attacks as a way to increase the violence not only among the students but among their families. Girls face challenges related to social norms such as harassments. Communities that are served by school buses indicate that no continuous maintenance is carried out for the school bus, which leads to instability in transferring children to their schools, and delays in reaching the educational services, along with the small number that a bus could transfer, giving priority to younger children and forcing older children to go on foot. In regards to the internal environment of the existing schools in the targeted communities, the joint assessment carried out by TCC and WeWorld-GVC with close coordination with MoE during the proposal elaboration phase showed that some schools in Area C, are in a critical condition and need urgent rehabilitation: the schools’ interior environment is very poor, lacks proper lightening and ventilation, the classrooms are small and overcrowded and suffer from water leakage, the humid atmosphere in the school imposes health concerns on both children and teachers; additionally, there is lack of laboratories or multipurpose rooms for any educational activity (including remote education and online courses), in addition to their lack of adaptability to meet the needs of children with disabilities. As for the external environment, some schools are lacking healthy sanitation units for students, and no play area for the children. Moreover, the lack of space leads the children to express their energy through violence, as stated by the schools’ principles. Moreover, TCC has published in September, 2020 a research paper on the status of education sector during the pandemic of Covid-19 and identified a gap in the interventions in area C which affected the children in the mentioned areas of accessing the education system due to double exclusion caused by; weak infrastructure\technology infrastructure and Israeli violations and threats of demolishing schools. To tackle the challenges and diﬃculties mentioned above, families often adopt a coping mechanism that have a negative impact on girls and boys, including withdrawing children from school, or skipping classes. To avoid the increase of dropouts and the negation of the right to education (CRCart.28-29), schools need to guarantee a safe and protected environment for access to quality education, where children do not feel threated or intimidated, besides improving the internal environment of the existing schools, or upgrading them in order to accommodate higher educational levels.

2 - Activities

During the design of the project, the different needs of boys, girls, men and women and PWDs identified through the field assessments that followed the recommendations of the MOEHE were generally related to improving the infrastructures of existing schools and KGs in the targeted communities, or improving the means of transportation to reach the educational services outside the community. Therefore, along with educational infrastructure rehabilitation activities, school vehicles will be provided to transfer students safely in the primary levels to their schools in the nearby villages in order to improve their accessibility and reduce the risks they face during the trip, The schools and KGs that will benefit from the rehabilitation/upgrade activities will also be supported through provision of IT assets for schools staff (Laptops), in order to increase the school’s capacity to carry on online teaching in the light of the COVID-19 closure or any other restriction that prevents the students from accessing schools for prolonged time. Advocacy actions will also take place in two communities located inside the Seam Zone area, in order to guarantee that the school bus/vehicles transfer the children to their schools with proper safety and dignity, and to monitor the daily passage of students and teachers on seam zone checkpoint ensuring their safe access. The project aims to increase equitable safe access to inclusive and quality education services for vulnerable children, through a combination of hard and soft components in line with the mandates, strategies, and expertise of TCC and WeWorld-GVC. The action will entail 5 general integrated activities that include: 1) Provision of essential and basic emergency rehabilitation to schools, to ensure that school environments are appropriate and safe for students and teachers. This will be carried out through the following interventions in the targeted communities: rehabilitation of 8 elementary schools and 2 KGs; upgrading and addition of new classrooms for 3 elementary schools and 1 KG; school yard rehabilitation for 2 elementary schools; provision of WASH facilities to 1 school. 2) Same communities are targeted for the infrastructure rehabilitation/upgrade, will be provided by IT assets for schools’ staff (Laptops).3) Provision of protection services while commuting to schools for children in the most vulnerable areas, this will include renting of four (4x4) cars to assure safe and dignified access to education for students who use very rough roads in winter and summer: the vehicles will be shared by students of 6 communities, MoE has the mandate to employ the drivers for these vehicles, track and monitor the daily routs of them, and provide the needed cost for their operation (as fuel and maintenance). 4) Strengthening documentation, monitoring, reporting to the Cluster, to generate responses and advocacy (including dialogue with national and international authorities, and media outreach) on education-related violations which is implemented through Advocacy actions in 2 seam zone communities: A Seefer and Daher Al Maleh, in order to allow the bus to take the children from the community, and to carry on monitoring of daily passage of students and teachers on seam zone checkpoint5) Provision of school-based psychosocial support to school staff and students affected by the education-related violations, by the application of the Social emotional learning (SEL) theory among the school communities in the targeted areas, this will be through organization of 6 raising awareness workshops for 60 parents linked to 6 school community, organization of 6 Psycho-education workshops for 60 caregivers on creating understanding on “psychosocial”, The organization of the one TOT training aims to build the capacities of those teachers in the provision of training courses for other groups of teachers in the fields of how to identify and assess the socio-emotional learning challenges experienced by children with weak socio-academic performance, Screening of children facing socio-emotional learning challenges; following the training courses at school settings, target teachers will be required to screen their classes to identify children suffering from socio-emotional learning challenges that affect their socio-academic performance, developing SEL intervention plans for 92 student Trained teachers will implement the intervention plans with the students screened in the earlier stage of the projectAll of the aforementioned activities and the targeted schools and communities have been preselected based on the MoE recommendations, joint field visits and needs assessments by WW-GVC and TCC, and through protection response plans created within the Community Protection Approach (CPA) implemented in the targeted community throughout the years.

3 - Indicators

The purpose of this action is to address the communities’ humanitarian and protection needs while improving their access to education and contributing to safety and dignity. Specifically, the action will lead to the achievement of the specific objective following these indicators: - # of boys, girls and CWD in vulnerable communities benefiting from safe accessible transportation to schools; # Advocacy initiatives including events and briefings on education related violations; # of female and male students and teachers benefiting from emergency school rehabilitation, and # of male and female students and teachers benefiting from the provision of education supplies.# Of male and female students including CWD improved their educational achievement after remedial education classes, # of male and female students and educational cadre who participated in SEL (socio-emotional learning) The achievement of the indicators will be measured through an in-depth baseline technical assessment. An evaluation will be conducted to measure the impact and benefit related to the activities’ implementation for the different target group (including teachers, students and their caregivers) with a gender-age sensitive approach, The evaluation will include quantitative data collection (post specific survey) and qualitative analysis through separated focus groups (women/girls and men/boys) focusing on access to education/ safety/ dignity and integrity.MEAL officers jointly with the social team in WWGVC and TCC will be responsible in collecting and analyzing data in assessment and monitoring phases.The action responds to educational needs and looks beyond the mere material needs to the broader issues of personal safety, dignity and protection. The designed activities will assure: an improvement of the safety of all students and teachers, with specific attention to girls and boys students who are under threat of drop out or have history of dropping out; good learning environment and subsequent improved health conditions and more resistance to COVID-19 and other diseases for all schools attendees especially CWDs during cold and hot seasons; increased security and safety of all students during their ways to schools.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

To avoid negative impacts, TCC and WeWorld-GVC analyze the context and the interaction between the intervention and the context where the action will be implemented. The organization implements projects in line with the principle of Do No Harm, improving the effectiveness tailoring specific context action; the accountability responding to any unintentional negative impact created by the intervention. The eﬃciency is guaranteed by incorporating the Do No Harm principle encouraging local actors and community involvement on the overall steps of the suggested intervention and prevent unintended negative impacts. The proposal elaborates a remedial action to hazards that caused a change in educational needs and affects the ability of girls and boys to attend school. Which has an impact on the levels of literacy, numeracy and wellbeing of the affected population. TCC and WW-GVC aim to reduce the vulnerability of the beneficiaries caused by the systematic settler and IF abuse violence creating an increase of protection risks. The core action aims to create a safe, secure and inclusiveness environment in schools and realize supporting actions that focus on the need for physical protection, resilience building and psychosocial-emotional protection. The Do No Harm principle is assured during all the project cycle, guaranteeing that every project- phases and steps are endorsed by the beneficiaries themselves or their families, to prevent -in so doing- any potential negative consequence. The assessment lead by WW-GVC in Area C and the needs assessment carried out for the project writing focus on the understanding of the needs of the communities specifically girls and boys. Particular attention is given to input/feedback provided by vulnerable groups such as women, whose participation is strongly encouraged to any decision-making process, and girls and boys in line with article 12 of the Convention on the Right of the Child. Specific attention will be also given to the lessons learned coming from previous similar projects of both partners. The elaboration of each single activity will take into account the different needs and investigate how girls and boys are differently affected. In the implementation phase, monitoring mechanism will guarantee direct interaction with the beneficiaries to make sure that their concerns and needs are heard and to guarantee their right to participate and be agents of change. Moreover, WeWorld-GVC adopts a well-designed and established complaint mechanism system, allowing beneficiaries and affected population to provide feedback and complaints, which will then -if needed- influence management decisions and concrete implementation. Safety and dignity have been taken into consideration in the design of tailored activities to address the different needs and risks that girls, women, boys and men are currently facing.Required protection measures will be taken into consideration during schools rehabilitation works to assure safety of students and staff by fencing the rehabilitation area, schedule rehabilitation works after schools attending hours and at the weekends, all WWGVC and TCC contracts have an article about preventing child labor or any kind of child exploitation during all project phases.COVID-19 protection measures (wearing face masks, gloves, hand sanitizers, physical distances) will be followed by WWGVC and TCC teams during all project activities to prevent the disease spread.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project follows a sustainable humanitarian approach aiming to provide remedial and environmental-building action, ensuring equity, sustainability and impartial access to education. Impartiality principle is carried throughout the action on the basis of needs, exclusively, giving priority to the most urgent cases of distress and making no distinction on the basis of nationality, race, gender, religious belief, class or political opinion. Impartiality principle will be recognized throughout the actions' entire phases: design, planning, implementation and monitoring. This is illustrated by the fact that assessments have been conducted through the same tools and methods in every school targeted, collecting the data directly from the duty-bearers as a way of accountability and transparency. On another note, schools targeted for needs assessments have been collected through trustable sources, as public data from OCHA or the Ministry of Education, avoiding random targeting. During the design phase, several schools have been assessed by TCC and WeWorld-GVC staff in coordination with the Ministry of Education, to identify the different needs and potential activities and beneficiaries. The identification of the beneficiaries and activities followed the criteria of higher need and higher vulnerability. The proposal targets girls and boys students and KG children, besides the educational staff, as they are considered as vulnerable groups due to the high exposure to Israelis attacks while accessing to education services and schools, they are generally not safe as are exposed to the threat of tear gas and sound bombs thrown by the IF, and the risk of inspections in cases of security escalation. Children with disabilities will be also targeted by the project and their specific needs will be analysed and addressed. During the previous needs assessment, no cases of severe disability have been identified but to guarantee the right to education for all, the activities will be implemented with an inclusive approach.Parents and caregivers of children will be essential part of several project phases including assessments, implementation as well as the monitoring of the impact of the project activities, in order to assure their satisfaction about safety and dignity of their children during access to all educational services.
