Project Summary

The project goal is to respond to and address protection risks faced by conflict and natural disaster crises affected communities and empower persons with disability through protection service delivery. This will be through timely identification of protection risks and safe referrals for GBV and child survivors through functioning referral pathway. The identification assessment of protection needs and provision of assistive devices for persons with disability to promote access to basic services. To improve their participation in social activities, rapid response assessment including head counting of people with disabilities, types of impairments, protection risks faced will be done to draw possible mitigation measures in all project site. HI organizing inclusive and holistic community awareness raising on key protection messages including on human rights and the right of persons with disability. HI will establish Community Integrated Centre to facilitate the MHPSS group counselling and rehabilitation for children with disabilities. HI will establish / strengthen community based protection committee which will be disability inclusive. HI will provide capacity building training for community based protection committee, community based organizations and service providers on protection mainstreaming, disability and inclusion including sign language for the service providers. HI will conduct monthly protection monitoring assessment to ensure the protection environment is maintained and mitigate protection risks affecting the protection environment and to inform the protection response plans, update the 4Ws matrix and regular service mapping in all project sites. HI will conduct baseline and endline survey.

1 - Needs

Since after the independence and last revitalized peace agreement, the people of South Sudan faced a devastating and catastrophic humanitarian situation. Indiscriminate violence against women, men, boys and girls including but not limited to person with disability, conflict induced displacements and flooding has affected approximately 72%(8.9 Million) of county’s population out of whom 5.6milion are dire need for protection and 15% 1.3 million( according to WHO ) are persons with disability.The over decade old conflict and civil war in South Sudan affected 15 % (1.3 million populations who sustained at least one type of disability if not all i.e. physical, visual, hearing and intellectual impairments with limited support to cope and overcome that new challenges. Persons with disability who were disproportionately affected by the protracted crises nation wide, remained a primary victims for institutional, attitudinal, environment and structural barriers that hinder person with disabilities to access basic services. Women, girls, children and older persons are often subjected to discrimination and marginalization due to Intersectionality of Gender and disabilities. These make individuals more vulnerable to protection risks including but not limited to life threatening GBV and protection forms of violence, coercion, harassment, sexual exploitation and abuse. People with or without disability and other most vulnerable population have minimum livelihood opportunities insufficient material assistance, unable to travel long distance to access services due to lack of enabling assistive devices, lack ramps at facilities, fear of stigma and coercion- forcing persons with disabilities and most vulnerable population to seek more dangerous work in the informal economy and low levels of social acceptance of disability and mental health issues, leading to discrimination even extending to service providers. People with disability are not of part community leadership and more importantly, they are not part of the community and family decision making. Between December 2021 to February 2022, HI conducted an assessment and identified 2500 people with disabilities who needed urgent functional rehabilitation and assistive devices and urgent referrals to other service including health, MHPSS, food distribution and NFIs. In response to this, HI supported 560 persons with disability with assorted assistive devices, more than 1900 person for safe referrals to other services. Among the referrals, there was over 200 children with disabilities who needed urgent MHPSS counselling in Aweil south and provided. As such, there is need for a continuation of protection monitoring assessment to better understand the protection needs faced by people with disabilities particularly children for regular MHPSS groups counseling and regular referrals to other service. Need for establishment of Community Integrated Centers to facilitate the MHPSS and rehabilitation activities for children with disabilities and other vulnerable population. Need of assessment to establish the number of persons with disability as per their age group and types of impairments( based on Washington group classification) for evidence based response and mitigation measures. HI will organize community awareness raising activities which are disability inclusive. In addition, the project will seek to strengthen community based protection committee focusing persons with disability. HI will conduct capacity building training for Organizations of person with disabilities and Service providers.

2 - Project activities

These project activities aim to mitigate and the respond to immediate and long term protection risks, enhance the protection and well-being of the conflict& floods induced IDPs, returnees and affected host communities. Furthermore those activities will empower person with disabilities and other vulnerable people affected by the crises, focusing on increasing the ability to access safe and improved protection services in a rights based manner, through context specific approaches that take into consideration the limited access for basic service. The project will collect accurate protection and disability inclusion data, to form evidence-based advocacy and mainstreaming inclusion in the wider protection response. This project will provide direct protection and rehabilitation services to at risk persons including person with and without disabilities identified through protection monitoring, rapid rehab assessments and referral. *Protection needs assessment will be conducted to better understand the protection environment and magnitude of the protection risks so as to address and deliver timely evidence based protection response. *Capacity-building: HI will provide capacity building training for staff, Community based protection committees, Organizations of people with disabilities, Community leaders, Service Providers (implementing partners staff) and duty bearers. *Establishment of Integrated Community Centre: the integrated community center will be safe space for MHPSS group counselling, Functional rehabilitation and Referrals, and targeted awareness raising for women, boys, girls and men with disabilities. *Monthly protection monitoring assessment: will be conducted across the project sites to identify multi-sector humanitarian needs, such as food, healthcare, shelter, NFIs, protection risk including but not limited to GBV incidents, child protection, civil documentation, child forced recruitments in the armed forces, barriers faced by people with disabilities, Risks associated with travel long distances to access services, firewood collection and when fetching water. HI will share the protection monitoring finding with protection cluster and relevant members for evidence based response and advocacy. * Safe referrals: In collaboration with protection cluster, HI will develop functional referral system and will keep updating referral system where it exists to ensure to respond to protection and GBV cases through safe and survivor centered referral linkages to lifesaving service in a timely manner. *Rehabilitation assessment: Identification and assessments of persons with disability to promote meaningful access to services and improve the full-fledged participation in the social activities, including head counting of people with disabilities, types of impairments (based on Washington group classification) to draw possible mitigation measures and inform our response in all project site. *Community awareness raising: HI community workers in collaboration with community based protection committee will organize inclusive community awareness raising, where the discussion topics will be selected through inclusive consultation and proper pretesting before activities commenced. This is to ensure cultural appropriateness of key protection messages through various community mobilization and outreach strategies including but not limited to home visits, mini campaigns, mass campaigns at international events, dialogue sessions, panel discussions and conveying messages at community entry points. *Establishing/ Strengthening community based protection committee: Identification and training of the community based protection committee with focus on person with disabilities. This is to empowerment person with disabilities to actively participate the community based leadership and come to the decision making forums on issues that affects them.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

HI has both global and country level feedback complaint management system to ensure beneficiaries feedback are incorporated with the project and share the assistance provided to the beneficiaries. Beneficiaries feedback are very essential part of monitoring and analyzing potential risks and all actions and decisions taken are focused on the best interests of the target populations. These system includes Any complaint related to HI programming and areas of operation that is non-sensitive is categorized as an operational complaint. This includes but not limited to complaints such as:• Dissatisfaction with the provided goods and services • Quality and quantity of the distributed items• Failure to provide timely assistance or request for additional assistance• Issues related to eligibility, registration, or distribution• Lack of general project information Non-serious, yet impolite or irresponsible behavior of HI staff or partner staff Operational or a program relevant complaints are critical for the FCMS focal point as they need to track them until they are resolved and closed. For operational complaints, the procedure is as follows: 1. Operational complaint is forwarded as email to the relevant project/program manager, copying area manager or Senior Project Liaison Ocer (PLO) and where appropriate technical specialist of respective sector (with MEAL Manager in cc) on the same day of receiving the complaint 2. Complainant receives a written receipt (see External Acknowledgement Form) in their preferred modality: paper copy, email, or SMS text message within max.7 calendar days 3.Relevant Program staff reviews the complaint, brings forward recommendations for action, and shares these with Complaints Focal Point and/or MEAL Manager. Some complaints may require Country Director oversight or consultation. 4. Complaint Desk provides feedback to the complainant in his/her preferred modality: paper copy, email, or SMS text message, and informs about the appeals procedure within max. 21 calendar days The complainant has the right to appeal the feedback provided by HI within a period of maximum 30 calendar days. Appeals shall be dealt with on a case-by-case basis unless case-type precedence has been established. Complaints may require immediate action, for example an issue that poses a security threat to beneficiaries, HI staff, HI resources/assets or HI reputation should immediately be acted upon. In such a scenario, the complaint focal point needs to contact the relevant person, either by phone or by visiting them (If they are in the same once) such as area manager, safety advisor or his/her replacement and country director, deputy country directors etc. These managers should decide the future course of action by assessing the severity and the nature of the complaint. Following this, the complaint focal point should write an email containing all the details about the complaint provided by the complainant to ensure the formal submission along with adding this to FCMS centralised data sheet. All non-programmatic HI related complaints should be forwarded to the relevant staff head of the department by copying their immediate supervisor. The relevance of the complaint will be determined by the nature of the complaint as explained above. Operational complaints will be processed and delegated internally by the complaints and response focal person by forwarding the complaints and response log to the Area Manager. If the complaint entails a senior member of staff or is about the Area Manager/PLO, the complaint will be sent directly to the CD in Juba. If the complaints is about the CD, the complaints will be sent to the HQ via email : alert@hi.org However for most other programmatic complaints.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

HI has a robust feedback and complaints mechanism for beneficiaries that combines both unsolicited communication channels such as suggestion boxes, text and phone line as well as an anonymous reporting email service, with solicited forms such as monthly focus group discussions, interviews and post intervention monitoring surveys. Detailed sensitization of communities on when, how and why to report is critical for the functioning of the system, as is HIs internal review, response and feedback protocols – designed to close the loop. To ensure the most vulnerable community members can access feedback and complaints mechanisms, HI will include considerations such as the use of local languages, reasonable accommodation for persons with disabilities (i.e. accessible venues, sign-language interpretation, sighted guides, accessible information materials), as well as gender specific mobilization and messaging to minimize any barriers these key groups encounter. Moreover, HI is proposing many entry-points for feedbacks/complaints to reach all: 1. In-person once visit to FCMS Desk 2. HI community & group meetings 3. Complaint/Feedback Box4. Toll-free telephone Hotline and SMS 5. HI email address 6. Beneficiary Satisfaction Surveys

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The risk that beneficiaries may be exposed to violence due to the distance particularly for women and girl’s men and boys with disabilities is there. However, HI will mitigate those risk in inclusive consultation with beneficiaries incorporating their feedback in the project implementation and in bring project activities in close and safest places in the beneficiary’s residents and will keep possible and passible distances. HI also well trained protection and MHPSS staff who quickly provide urgent response to individual survivors in all project locations. HI will work in close collaboration with other humanitarian partners and cluster members and local authority when providing materials and making registration. Access to humanitarian assistance is very challenging, due to insecurity resulted by the inter communal conflict, gang groups and unknown armed forces are all a risk factors that might put people at risk when accessing services. However, HI will mitigate through conducting monthly protection monitoring to assess the protective environment and access to services at all times, providing intensive community awareness raising and capacity building training the service providers and government agencies on PSEA in the field, putting up complaint mechanism in place and promoting community feedback about the protection environment. HI has PSEA and child safeguarding protocols as part of the code conduct and compliances of all employees to abide by. HI will provide regular PSEA training for the staff, HI will produce Information Education Communication materials, HI will use GBV referrals pathway to raise the awareness of the staff and community at project sites. And HI will provide open door policy of the beneficiaries to come forward and report any PSEA cases committed by HI staff and that of other partners or agencies through the pre-established complaint system. HI will coordinate closely with GBV partners, camp management (if available), UNICEF and UNHCR and health providers to ensure t support to survivors are supported and referred in timely manner, as well as to develop solutions to address risks with protection partners. Given that the project is intending to support persons with disability and most vulnerable beneficiaries it is essential that needs based selection criteria are adhered to. HI will create holistic environment where everybody particularly person with disabilities, older persons without support boys and girls able to actively participate the projection consultations and feedback sessions. Reporting tools will also capture gender age disability disaggregated data on regular basis. HI will ensure people have safe access to the available complaint mechanisms in place and will promote people at participate other open feedback sessions. HI has safe and password encrypted data bases. HI staff will be trained one data protection principles, national cluster endorsed data sharing protocols and other related confidentiality of collected datas. The field forms use none identifiable information and use coding instead on names. To mitigate external interference, HI will utilize standardized vulnerability and targeting criteria, and consult all local stakeholders on this process to avoid any bias in the process. To mitigate this, HI will coordinate with HLP and Mine action partners will consult about the risks associated with HLP and Mines in the project location. HLP issues and the presence of explosive hazards in communities would heavily impact the delivery of activities.
