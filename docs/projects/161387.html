Project Summary

This project will target a total of (2500) beneficiaries (375 Men, 675 Women, 625 boys and 825 girls) will be address directly with lifesaving Health Services. Health interventions will focus on Curative consultations, Immunization, ANC, and referral patients with complicated health condition to high level health facility. These essential health services will be provided through 1 PHCC, 1 PHCU and two mobile clinics. CRD will screen children with severe and moderate acute malnutrition among under-five children and PLW and will send them to Nutrition centers.
A total of 12 Community Health Volunteers will be train on Protection and Promotion. A total of 32 health personnel will be trained on Health emergencies including; IDSR, and Infectious diseases.
This project will have three objectives; namely, (1) Reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic prone diseases and health insecurities among target population in Manyo and Panyikang Counties (2) Improve access and scale-up response to integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable populations in both Manyo and Panyikang. (3) Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled and mental health disorders.
CRD has a good understanding of the geographical area and movement of host communities in the trend of crises such as drought, conflicts and sporadic fights

1 - Needs

According to IPC (May 2019) report Manyo County is among Counties classified in crisis (IPC phase 3), and Panyikang County has been classified among 25 Counties in emergency (IPC 4). In these two Counties Health Cluster estimated that a total of (41,669) people are in need of essential Health services. Kitkwan in Manyo County has highest number of IDPs and Returnees in which it worsening Humanitarian. This led to deterioration of health Infrastructure and health status of the population. Moreover, according to humanitarian response plan monitoring report for January – March 2019 indicated that Manyo County under major problem classification and Panyikang under catastrophic classification. These two classification indicate acute and immediate need of humanitarian assistance in these two Counties. Inter cluster assessment to Kitgwang, Manyo County in August 2019 found that a total of 18467 IDPs, Returnees and host community are in dire need of health services. The report highlighted that there are no functioning health facilities in areas under opposition control. In kitgwang there is only one PHCU supported by Community but its luck drugs, immunization and antenatal care services. The most prevalent health condition in kitgwang includes malnutrition, malaria, respiratory tract infection, acute watery diarrhea, skin infection, eye infection, intestinal worms and suspected measles casesAccording to Reach report January – March 2019 indicated that most causes of morbidity and mortality in Manyo and Panyikang Counties were Malaria (45%), Typhoid 24% and Diarrhea 12% respectively.WASH services are non-existence as health services in the area. Some of the existing health facilities lack basic health care services including immunization and antenatal. Some facilities have been constructed by the community, but there are no medical supplies; this further aggravated the already dire situation. Disease burden is very high with malaria the major cause of morbidity in these counties; this has also lead to increased cases of water borne diseases like cholera and acute watery diarrhea. During this crisis, a lot of violence against women, boys and girls have occurred. Several women and girls have been raped and went without noticing. Services that could be rendered to such victims are not available. There is no health protection including CMR services. There is lack of infection Prevention Control, waste management, WASH in health facility, this cause high risk to the community and health facilities staff. REACH and SMART survey conducted in Manyo and panyikang Counties among the host population in March 2018 highlighted the detrimental impact of conflict on children <5yrs and pregnant and lactating women.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Primary intend of this project is to save-life among affected and vulnerable People including host community, IDPs and Returnees affected by natural and man-made disasters.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

This project is linked to South Sudan Basic Packages of Health and Nutrition which it primary intend is to promotes, maintains healthy nutrition status of children under five years, pregnant and lactating women. In the course of the implementation plan, CRD ensure that the programs are well implemented by ensuring that it adheres to the BHPNS and work plan. The organization will work with the CHDs, Nutrition cluster at State and national, government and the core pipeline partners to ensure that medical supplies are delivered in time to ensure that health services reach the vulnerable population.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, this project is aligned with National Health policy of South Sudan and HRP strategic objectives. The project is aligned with object one and two of Nutrition cluster. CRD will also report to financial tracking system to ensure that the money received from the donors is well accounted for. CRD ensure that there are no duplication of activities but complimentary services are offered to the beneficiaries for them to benefit more. CRD will also build capacity of the government staff to ensure that they are capable of offering the health services even after the project ends. This will be done in consultation to the community, government and other stake holders in areas.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The project is tailored in a manner that encourages the participation of, local authorities CHD, SMOH and have ownership for health care services in the counties. CRD will work strongly with other humanitarian actors who are implementing protection activities to ensure that the rights of the beneficiaries are well protected. CRD will be ensure that, there will be clear referral pathway for GBV survivors to access assistance and this will be well communication through consultation with stakeholders and addressed by a female staff

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Together with the community, local authorities CRD will draw out exit plan that will be shared with the county authority, community leaders and other partners. The idea is to draw the attention of other actors who might take up and sustain the project. Throughout the live of the project CRD will maximally engage the community at all levels such that they will be able to facilitate and sustain the Primary Health Care activities in the community when the project closes out. The formation of community mobilization group and their work become very crucial and as the project phases out they will remain in the community and act as community monitors, continue with their activities especially among the Payams and Boma’s who have existing institutions and jurisdictions.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Integrating protection in Health will be central to Health programming through appropriate Health response mechanisms that protects beneficiaries from increased insecurity risks, training protection staff on Health promotion and referral of children with protection concerns to Health sites. CRD Health staff will be trained on non-specialized protection package to identify and refer children with protection concerns for specialized care. Also Special attention will be given to prevention of neglect among vulnerable groups to accessing Health services among care givers and Health staff in Manyo and Panyikang Counties. CRD feedback system will be use for information sharing and feedback through project implementation. In addition, stakeholders meetings and exist interview and focus group meeting will be used. Moreover, Consultation meetings with Communities in these counties will be conducted before project implementation, in these meeting communities will identify hard to reach areas and to select services locations. However, outreach activities will be include in this project to offer Health services to population in hard to reach areas.
