Project Summary

The project aligns with the Shelter-CCCM Sector strategy and specific objectives. UNHCR and partners will continue to strengthen and improve a wide range of camp management components including; enhancing the capacity of camp management actors through targeted modular training, maintaining and improving camp facilities, facilitating access to services, advancing community participation, promoting adherence to camp management standards and practices, improvement of settlements infrastructure, combined with site planning to address vulnerability to natural hazards and foster natural resource management. In collaboration with the Office of the Refugee Relief and Repatriation (RRRC), IOM and Shelter-CCCM partners, UNHCR will work towards strengthening coordination structures, promoting good governance and civilian administration of the camps. The Capacity Sharing Initiative (CSI), undertaken with the RRRC, camp-in-charges (CiCs) and partner staff will advance the understanding and application of standards relevant to the sector. To encourage community participation and ownership, UNHCR will continue consulting and working with refugees to implement community-led projects under care and maintenance through cash for work (CfW) and material support. UNHCR will partner with other stakeholders to foster initiatives to promote peaceful co-existence amongst refugee and host communities in the camps. The Common Feedback Platform (CFP) will be harmonised and strengthened to increase access and speedy resolution of cases by respective sectors.. Recognizing Bangladesh's susceptibility to climate change, UNHCR will continue its efforts to enhance the resilience of both refugee and host communities. Results from the J-MSNA show that 96% of the population is satisfied with how humanitarian agencies have responded to natural hazards. UNHCR will also sustain its commitment to strengthening preparedness by ensuring the timely activation and deployment of personnel, providing necessary equipment, conducting relevant disaster risk management (DRM) trainings, drills and simulations and prepositioning essential relief items ahead of potential disasters. Adjustments to the LPG refill cycle, informed by data collection, underscore ongoing efforts to optimize efficiency. Advocacy and promotional activities, including the ongoing promotion of pressure cooker usage across the camps, aim to ensure higher utilisation rates. Recognizing the temporary nature of approved shelter material and the impact of seasonal hazards in Bangladesh, regular maintenance of refugee shelters throughout the year is deemed essential. In coordination with CiCs, UNHCR and its partners actively support refugee households in maintaining their shelters using bamboo, tarpaulin, rope, and wires. Additionally, tie-down kits are being considered for emergency distribution to reinforce shelters in anticipation of strong winds during monsoon and cyclone seasons. UNHCR remains committed to prepositioning contingency stocks and conducting distributions based on needs assessments and validation, drawing insights from the Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (J-MSNA) and UNHCR's post-Distribution Monitoring Reports. Similarly, due to the temporary nature of site infrastructures developed with non-durable materials, UNHCR commits to regular maintenance to ensure the longevity and functionality of these infrastructures, fostering a sustainable and resilient environment in the camps. This includes maintenance of pathways, bridges, drains and slope stabilization works. In addressing environmental concerns, UNHCR seeks technical solutions which are aligned with the natural environment, ensuring safety, supporting good living conditions and promoting climate-friendly practices. Initiatives encompassing rehabilitation, disaster risk reduction, and biodiversity conservation will be sustained. Refugees will continue to access non-food items (NFIs) and renewable energy solutions, including LPG refills.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

UNHCR conducts regular, sector-specific, and context-sensitive needs assessments within its Area of Responsibility (AoR) to guide targeted responses and priorities. Strengthening the capacity to coordinate comprehensive service provision at the camp level remains a priority, necessitating further enhancement. Sustained support is crucial for the Government (RRRC, CiCs) to enhance camp administration and management. Acknowledging refugees as primary responders to disasters, the Capacity Sharing Initiative, implemented in collaboration with IOM will play a pivotal role in offering direct technical support and training to the RRRC and partners. The majority of shelters, constructed with bamboo and tarpaulin sheets, are highly vulnerable. Currently, only 11.5% of households have received improved shelter materials, with the remaining 88.5% still in need within UNHCR’s AoR. Weak shelter structure and leakage in the roof and wall are the prime concerns of the refugees as indicated in the J-MSNA 2023. Therefore, there is a growing necessity to explore and pilot alternative shelter options using fire and weather-resistant materials. UNHCR aims to support regular maintenance and emergency repairs for 28,500 shelters, construct 1,800 new improved shelters and address emergency incidents affecting 11,000 shelters. Environmental degradation exacerbates vulnerabilities to natural hazards given the coastal location of the camps, which are susceptible to disasters such as cyclones, floods, landslides and fire incidents during different seasons. Responding to ongoing climate change requires specific mitigation and adaptation activities. Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), environmental restoration and holistic watershed management are paramount. Community-led interventions for slope stabilization through nature-based solutions will contribute to environmental rehabilitation, preserving ecosystems, biodiversity and ensuring long-term resilience. The J-MSNA 2023 findings indicate that the community identified steep or damaged pathways and inadequate drainage as barriers for safe physical accessibility. Therefore, UNHCR will maintain its capacity to implement site development interventions as well as to complete the necessary repair and maintenance of existing site infrastructure based on prioritized needs in close coordination with relevant stakeholders, e.g., the sector, Government officials, partners and refugees. Despite notable progress in addressing non-food item (NFI) needs for refugees, as identified in the 2023 J-MSNA, gaps still persist. Refugees identified mosquito nets, kitchen sets, solar lamps and blankets as top NFI needs. Therefore, continued distribution of NFIs including solar lamps and LPG, along with provision of training on energy-efficient cookware usage, remains essential as well as drain cleaning (primary and secondary drains) which remains a priority in UNHCR's AoR. The strategic pre-prepositioning of shelter and NFI materials will improve the efficiency of large-scale emergency responses. Findings from the 2023 J-MSNA, identified that 37% of households accessing the common feedback (CFP) desk reported that their complaints had been resolved (within an acceptable timeframe). As outlined in the response strategy, UNHCR emphasizes inclusive engagement, accountability and community empowerment through participatory approaches and feedback mechanisms. Community inclusion will be strengthened through involving community groups in the decision-making processes, common feedback platform and mobile services targeting marginalized groups.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

UNHCR's commitment to enhancing the effectiveness and efficiency of its response is evident in its ongoing emphasis on rationalization. The objective is to create a more predictable and accountable overall response, ultimately rationalizing and improving services for both refugees and host communities. Through strategic alignment, coordinated partnerships and innovative approaches, UNHCR strives to deliver improved Crosss-cutting themes services, minimizing duplication and ensuring a streamlined and accountable overall response. Notable progress has been made in aligning activities such as cash for work non-food item (NFI) distribution, Liquefied Petroleum Gas (LPG) refill schedules, and optimizing drain cleaning efforts. These achievements result from increased community engagement, the strategic realignment of volunteers based on camp demographics, topographical considerations, and the understanding of operational complexities. The project places emphasis on regular risk assessments, allowing adaptive adjustments in implementation to optimize outcomes and achieve goals. Actively involved in service monitoring, incident reporting, post-distribution monitoring (PDM) and regular needs assessments, UNHCR aims to establish baseline data, prioritize the needs of the affected population with a focus on age, gender, and diversity (AGD), and address the requirements of individuals with specific needs. With a comprehensive understanding of the roles and activities of various actors in camps, UNHCR’s ongoing efforts include strengthening inter-sectoral coordination mechanisms at the camp level through focal points and continued collaboration with Camps-in-Charge (CiCs) and other Government authorities. UNHCR is dedicated to exploring innovative and integrated multi-sectoral approaches with other sectors; Protection, Education, Child Protection, Gender-Based Violence (GBV), Health and WASH.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Protection mainstreaming is at the core of SCCCM interventions ensuring that the proposed response contributes to saving lives, safety and security, alleviating suffering and providing dignity for women, girls, boys and men, particularly marginalized groups such as older people, people living with disabilities and minority groups. UNHCR and its partners will use gender-sensitive quantitative data analysis to track levels of beneficiaries’ satisfaction, participation, acceptance, knowledge and practices. UNHCR will ensure maximum protection mainstreaming in its response by prioritizing the most vulnerable groups affected by protection issues. Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), access to safe cooking energy, reaforatation and climate change actions contribute to the overall protection of the populations and sustainability.

3.3 - Age & Disability

In all project planning and implementation, UNHCR establishes infrastructure and mechanisms with the aim of creating equitable access for all, especially children, elderly and disable persons, recognizing that children and elderly persons have the same right to site management services as everyone else. At the same time, UNHCR recognizes that children may have specific needs. Improperly built and poorly maintained facilities, especially in crowded refugee camps, may expose children to risks of sexual exploitation and abuse. Therefore, UNHCR in coordination with child protection and community-based child protection structures will ensure that shelter facilities are safe and related information provided through communication interventions, including on safety, is disseminated in formats that children, adolescents and caregivers and persons with disabilities can understand and also promote safeguarding.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Systematic and meaningful engagement of the affected population in the design of projects represent key steps in ensuring successful programmes and achieving quality output and outcome results. UNHCR will enage affected populations throughout the project cycle; during assessments, design, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of programmes; establishing open channels of communication for feedback, complaints and information sharing; and facilitating a participatory process for decision making and mutual learning. The Community-Led Projects and Community Maintenance initiatives have provided affected populations with platforms to identify, prioritize and contribute to the improvement of community infrustructure and enhancement of ownership and sustainability. Community volunteers are first responders and have been equipped with skills on emergency preparedness and response as well as community awareness. UNHCR, through site management focals and partners, will conduct regular meetings with community representatives in order to inform and update communities on the planned project activities, as well as receive feedback from beneficiaries.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

The provision of safe site management services to address the specific needs of refugees represents one of the most fundamental principles of protection and gender-based violence (GBV) risk mitigation. Assessment of the needs of the various communities, strengthened communication with the community through various thematic groups will focus on gender-based needs to determine appropriate referral and response in coordination with various service providers. UNHCR will continue to follow the established referral mechanisms i.e. toll-free hotline, e-mail, complaint and feedback boxes, in-person reporting and Inspector General’s Office (IGO). Assistance to persons with special needs and risks associated with gender, age and diversity will be assessed through participative and inclusive risk mapping focused on the provision of basic services, as well as safety audits. UNHCR will ensure ongoing information sharing between site management, shelter, protection and GBV field staff in the targeted areas, and ensure appropriate training of the partners and community volunteers on gender and age equality and GBV mainstreaming. As member of the PSEA Network UNHCR will support and facilitate capacity building of partners and refugees on the established PSEA reporting mechanisms to ensure cases are timely reported and addressed.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

UNHCR continues to implement its activities while considering existing climatic and environmental risks and vulnerabilities, given that the refugee camps are prone to climate related disasters, as well as explore programmes and initiatives contributing to climate change mitigation and resotration of the fragile environment. Through the continuous delivery of training and simulations, the communities continue to be equipped on disaster risk management (DRM) and disaster risk reduction (DRR) elements.
