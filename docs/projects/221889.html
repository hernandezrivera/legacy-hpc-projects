Project Summary

The northern districts of Cabo Delgado, particularly Macomia and Muidumbe, continue to experience insecurity, with ongoing attacks forcing many families to seek refuge. In response to this humanitarian crisis, PRONANAC is committed to providing essential support to vulnerable populations through effective Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) strategies. This project focuses on improving the living conditions of internally displaced persons (IDPs) by ensuring they have safe, equitable, and dignified access to humanitarian services. The initiative aims to enhance the protective environment for IDPs and host communities by addressing gaps in protection and ensuring effective multi-sectoral interventions at the local level. Key activities will include the establishment and maintenance of safe and well-managed camps, along with coordination of services to meet the immediate needs of displaced populations. The project will also support the recovery process by strengthening community self-management, building resilience, and empowering local structures to ensure sustainable solutions. The operations will reach multiple locations, targeting IDPs, host communities, and returnees with solutions tailored to their specific needs. The project emphasizes partnerships with local volunteers to build capacity and ensure that interventions are responsive and culturally appropriate. The goal is to provide long-term support and promote recovery and resilience among affected populations, contributing to a safer, more sustainable environment for communities in northern Cabo Delgado.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

An estimated 446,611 displaced people, including returnees and host communities affected by displacement, are expected to require Camp Coordination and Management (CCCM) services in 2025. This also includes 177,744 internally displaced people on protracted displacement who are unable to return to their homeland due to ongoing conflict in some potential situations. 124,860 people have returned to their homelands. Areas of return and those whose homes, land and property have been destroyed or occupied. There is no significant decrease in the 600,000 people who required assistance in 2024.However, data from CCCM Cluster 5W and camps show a significant need for strengthened camp management structures across Cabo Delgado displacement sites. While community governance structures exist in collective sites, these remain highly under-capacity, with a persistent lack of accountability to the people they were formed to serve. A weak response mechanism among some IDPs makes the situation difficult and causes frustration and hopelessness. Sporadic conflicts in northern Cabo Delgado, Macomia and Muidumbe in particular may further exacerbate protracted displacement and the associated burden on the displaced population, returnees and host communities, especially given the uncertainty about the continuation of humanitarian services, safety and security in the areas of return. This uncertainty and the inconclusive The peace process may lead to most of the currently displaced people leaving prematurely to find shelter and livelihood opportunities elsewhere, but they essentially remain displaced.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

PRONANAC EDUCACIONAL, in coordination with the Protection cluster, ensures the integration of protection aspects into all activities, emphasizing staff training and education of community workers. The organization prioritizes safety and security to prevent GBV by providing culturally sensitive CCCM services to vulnerable populations. Accessibility concerns are addressed for individuals with disabilities, chronic illnesses, the elderly, and marginalized individuals. PRONANAC EDUCACIONAL’s design strategy emphasizes the integration of protection, safety, and dignity by conducting a multi-sectoral risk analysis, promoting equitable access, participation, and transparency. PRONANAC EDUCACIONAL is committed to training staff in protection, humanitarian norms, and principles to prevent harm and maintain ethical and safety standards in the execution of activities.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

PRONANAC EDUCACIONAL guarantees significant participation involving women and the elderly from the initial stages, through discussions in focus groups. They will be involved in the design and coordination of the program, and actions such as the commitment to a minimum representation of 50% of local volunteers for CCCM work will empower women and elderly people in the community.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Throughout the project cycle, PRONANAC EDUCACIONAL ensures active participation and soliciting of ideas and opinions from people with disabilities. The team actively involves people with disabilities in the design of other infrastructure, incorporating suggestions to improve access. PRONANAC EDUCACIONAL disseminates information related to disability to the community, improving understanding of needs, and shares progress with the working group for continued input on inclusion. To remove barriers, a community survey will determine the number of people with disabilities.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

PRONANAC EDUCACIONAL ensures environmentally friendly solutions with locally sourced materials for necessary activities, promoting a system of reduction, recycling and reuse. Rapid impact assessments and environmental screening will be carried out at various sites, aligning with country regulations and maintaining coordination with relevant authorities.
