Project Summary

The political instability combined with 13 years of closure of Gaza, and the recent COVID-19 outbreak have resulted in the lack of access to adequate WASH services and restricted movements for people and goods in Gaza. The existing scarcity of natural resources and already limited access to basic services are constantly exacerbated by recurring escalations of the conflict in Gaza. In addition, a lack of income opportunities with a minimal room for economic recovery continues to make the majority of Gaza’s population dependent on humanitarian assistance.
The Gaza population is subject to a chronic electricity deficit that are provided from Gaza Power Plant (GPP), Israeli lines and Egyptian lines, which is less than half of the estimated requirement and undermine already fragile living conditions. The fragile increase in the electricity supply due to funding from the Government of Qatar – from 6.6 hours in 2018 to 12.1 hours in 2019 and 2020 on average – have improved slightly the operation of water facilities, but this support is not guaranteed for the future, and could be interrupted any time Furthermore, the precarious situation in the last two years in the Gaza Strip, characterized by escalations of violence and regular Great Return March demonstrations, hindered operations and slowed the implementation of the WASH response.
As a result of the worldwide coronavirus pandemic, governmental restrictions, including movement restrictions, were introduced and applied in the Gaza Strip to prevent contagion. The health crisis comes in the context of a serious deterioration in the economic situation in Gaza, severely impacting the ability of vulnerable households to survive.
This project is an immediate response to ensure that women, children and men in vulnerable households in Gaza have sufficient storage capacity for domestic and drinking water, so that they could store water whenever it becomes available from different sources, such as municipal water, private water wells and private vendors, whose water supply may be intermittent deriving from the power supply shortage mentioned above. The project also aims to increase the access of beneficiaries to safe water and safe sanitation facilities through the rehabilitation, construction and upgrade of existing wastewater infrastructure, and to promote proper daily hygiene practices through increased advocacy and awareness raising. It will ensure that vulnerable households receive information on the measures to put in place to reduce the risk of COVID-19 transmission and have the minimum infrastructure to be protected in case of further contagions and movement restrictions that could follow.
Through this project, UNICEF aims to improve access to water and adequate sanitation at household level for (in different vulnerable areas in Gaza through distribution of 1,500 drinking water tanks and 1,200 domestic water tanks, rehabilitation of 600 household sanitary facilities, installation/ rehabilitation of 300 sewage connections, and rehabilitation and upgrading of water and wastewater networks in the targeted localities.

1 - Needs

During the last 6 years, UNICEF led multiple interventions at household and community levels for vulnerable families with different implementing partners in Gaza. This vulnerability is the result of a protracted crisis that continues to increase. The social safety nets are also very weak with the population resorting to negative coping mechanism to survive. Furthermore, the deteriorated conditions in Gaza have a significant negative impact on basic family health, mainly of women and children. Children are considered particularly vulnerable and most exposed to water borne diseases such as diarrhea and skin infections. Without proper sanitation facilities, the basic hygiene needs of children cannot be covered, which have long lasting and adverse impact on their health status. A recent need assessment conducted by different WASH partners in July 2019, and more recently in 2020 specifically after the spread of COVID-19 in Gaza, these partners estimated that there are more than 8,000 households in need of rehabilitation of their sanitation facilities. The assessments were conducted through questionnaires and focus group discussions in addition to field visits. The collected data for more than 15,000 applicants were analysed and shortlisted households were visited by social workers and technical teams for the rehabilitation of household sanitation facilities, including the provision of domestic water tanks distribution and installation. Another assessment conducted specifically after the spread of COVID-19 in Gaza shows that more than 40% of interviewed households reported that their houses are not prepared for the COVID-19, due to overcrowding, humidity, weak ventilation, absence of tiling, inadequate sanitation facilities including absence of toilet chairs or windows inside the toilet, or toilet and kitchen are located in the same space. In addition to lack of financial ability to afford hygiene materials on regular basis to maintain house cleanliness and protect themselves from the COVID-19. In addition, more than 50% of the contacted households are suffering from shortage of domestic water that dropped to 58l/c/d and do not have sufficient storage for drinking water as well due to the interruption of well water supply and shortage of power. This data confirms there remains a significant gap in meeting the water storage and sanitation needs of the Gaza population. The assessments were conducted, taking into account the specific gender needs of men, women, boys and girls with the collection of disaggregated data. Another assessment on sewage connections in different areas in Gaza was conducted and updated in 2020 in coordination with relevant authorities. Based on this assessment, Jort Al lout and Satar in Khan Yunis in addition to AlSalam in Rafah are selected to improve their connections to the wastewater network. Furthermore, since 2018, in collaboration with the Child Protection Networks (CPNs) and through home visits, UNICEF and its partner have recognized households’ poor access to adequate and gender sensitive WASH facilities. Detailed assessment was conducted to address the WASH needs to meet the special needs of people with disabilities, including children affected by the Great March of Return (GMR) and the Israeli military attacks. Using updates from WASH cluster partners, cluster partners’ 4Ws records, and the needs assessment findings, it is clear that there remain significant unmet needs UNICEF to implement this WASH HRP project in 2021.

2 - Activities

The proposed intervention is an immediate response to ensure women, children and men have sufficient storage capacity of domestic water and have better access to safe water and safe sanitation facilities and services. It further aims to address the significant gap in humanitarian needs, especially female-headed households and households with war widows. The project will ensure that the needs of women and girls are taken into consideration. Trainings will focus on the specific needs of men and women (e.g. menstruation and childcare) with appropriate locations and timing. Separate training sessions with gender specific trainers may be required to ensure messaging is presented in a gender sensitive manner. UNICEF and its implementing partners will maintain close coordination with relevant authorities, WASH Cluster, area focal points and other organizations working in the targeted areas to avoid duplication and ensure complementarity.1. Household Water Supply 1.1 Provision and installation of 1,500 new drinking water tanks and 1,200 domestic water roof storage tanks equipped with all fittings installed for families whose water storage tanks were not filled due to deteriorated power supply or for those families that are consuming less than 60 l/c/d. Priority will be given to large families living in critical conditions, such as families benefiting from household sanitation rehabilitation, female-headed families and vulnerable families who have children with disabilities in Gaza North, Khan Younis and Rafah.2. Household Sanitary Services 2.1 Rehabilitation of sanitation facilities for 600 households to meet SPHERE standards including GMR affected people in different areas in Gaza 2.2 Rehabilitation of wastewater connections for 300 households in different areas in Gaza, in addition to wastewater network and associated works in North Gaza, Gaza City and AlSalam area in Rafah 3. Household Hygiene Promotion 3.1 In close collaboration with communities, to improve the quality of life in the targeted communities and public health, focus group discussions will be conducted with communities’ representatives and other key informants, interviews with household representatives, and women from the areas. 3.2 Targeted households will benefit from home visits whenever possible and with hygiene awareness campaigns through the Communication for Development approach and specific messages for COVID-19 prevention measures including IEC materials, messages and workshops for behavioural change to improve their knowledge, attitude, and key practices for water handling and menstrual hygiene for adolescents and women. 3.3 Distribution of 1,000 hygiene kits to targeted families affected by the COVID-19. 4. Water network4.1) Upgrading of water network (4,500 metres) in AlSalam area in Rafah

3 - Indicators

UNICEF and its implementing partners will supervise the projects on a daily basis to ensure timely and effective implementation. Women and men’s participation in workshops will strengthen social relations between family members and unify them in their efforts to assure their long-term water and sanitation needs are met. Focus group discussions will ensure separation of women and men to create an open environment where women are free to discuss key issues. A detailed survey, field visits and water analysis will be conducted to supervise, monitor and evaluate the activities. A Knowledge, Attitudes and Practices (KAP) survey will be conducted to assess and evaluate the impacts of the activities on women, men and boys, girls, persons with disabilities and the elderly. UNICEF and its implementing partners will contribute to the exchange of information/lessons learned, fact sheets and stories among the cluster community, which are to be used for advocacy purposes. Project activities will be monitored by a Project Management Team composed of key stakeholders responsible for monitoring and evaluation, and the results will be communicated to humanitarian actors and project beneficiaries. Accountability and transparency will be ensured through establishment of a beneficiary feedback mechanism. The following results will be achieved: 1.1) 9,005 people (2,273 men, 2,173 women, 2,248 boys, 2,234 girls and 77 persons with disability) will benefit from distribution of 1,500 water tanks 2.1) 2,720 people (671 men, 623 women, 673 boys, 657 girls and 96 persons with disability) will benefit from rehabilitation of 303 sanitary facilities2.2) 11,247 people (2,855 men, 2,772 women, 2,761 boys, 2,730 girls, and 129 persons with disability) will benefit from wastewater connections and wastewater network3.1, 3.2) 3,300 people (858 men, 825 women, 790 boys, 794 girls and 33 persons with disability) will be benefitting from hygiene awareness sessions 3.3) 5,645 people (1,468 men, 1,411 women, 1,355 boys, 1,354 girls and 57 persons with disability) will benefit from distribution of 1,00 hygiene kits4.1) 1,800 people (468 men, 450 women, 434 boys, 430 girls and 18 persons with disability) will benefit from upgrading of water networks

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

UNICEF is leading the child protection working group, and is member of the protection cluster, applying the principles of child protection to different projects. Through the whole project cycle, UNICEF and its partners ensure protection mainstreaming is guided by protection principles considering safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability as well as participation and empowerment. Gender will be mainstreamed, ensuring that protection concerns of men and women, girls and boys are addressed in line with gender in emergencies minimum standards and gender mainstreaming strategies. UNICEF ensures that due consideration is given to key humanitarian standards and protection principles, especially the do-no-harm principle, which is particularly important in Gaza’s context where social and political instability and conflict are evident in many areas with conservative gender norms. Local community committees (with equitable gender and age representation) will be established to ensure their direct engagement and ongoing consultation with targeted communities and beneficiaries throughout the project, thus enhancing community engagement and reducing any potential protection risks or exclusion at community level. As a specific protection mainstreaming component, UNICEF and its partners wherever applicable will coordinate with other protection and human rights agencies to ensure their engagement in the project activities. Hence, this HRP project is designed in a manner that avoids harm and risks to the targeted communities.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

This HRP project is designed in a manner that enables equal and impartial access to assistance and services to the targeted communities. It was developed through a participatory process that included representatives from the affected communities (all age, gender and diversity groups), including them in all project stages with regards to priority setting and the design of monitoring, evaluation and reporting systems. The planned proposed interventions will improve the access of the most vulnerable communities including children and PWD to safe water and sanitation through the provision of safe water, tankered water, increasing the HH storage capacity, and rehabilitation of WASH facilities in Households. While lots of households in Gaza need adequate sanitary facilities, yet, girls and women are the most vulnerable and in need of such services due to female personal hygiene needs. The project will target some of these households, and provide the most needed WASH services. UNICEF and the partners respect a non-discrimination approach through ensuring the equality in the access of the outcomes and benefits of the project, some of the activities will directly promote the gender equality and the rights of children as a key element in the development, safety and dignity of the community itself. This approach will have a crucial role in the direct contribution to the beneficiaries’ coping strategies development. In this sense, some of the activities will promote these coping strategies in which is expected that young people have a crucial role. Coping strategies will not only be promoted through beneficiaries’ involvement with local councils and stakeholders, but also in a family and individual level acting through the different trainings and awareness campaigns as direct agents of change spreading the importance of the different roles in the community and sharing the load of care-work needed in order to prevent the spread of the different diseases.
