Project Summary

The intervention aims to respond to the significant need of implementing public infrastructure rehabilitation, road rehabilitation, and the creation of short-term job opportunities in NWS,
based on the previous experience in implementing similar activities, Global Communities will provide critically needed upgrades/rehabilitation/construction to the neglected and crisis-affected infrastructure as follows:
- Rehabilitation of 6km of the main roads including a new asphalt layer with all the manholes and drainage infrastructure and lighting required.
- Implementing sidewalks through cash for work approach next to the newly rehabilitated roads
- Establishing and training 16 rescue teams during the rainy season to assist in responding to the floods risks
- Rehabilitating 5 bakeries in NWS.

4 - Needs

In 2022, ERL sector estimates 13.8 million people in need of early recovery and livelihood support. 50 percent are estimated to be women, 50 percent children, and 27 percent persons living with disabilities (PLWD). the reported PIN figures refer to 6.5M PIN for rehabilitation of basic community and social infrastructure (800 Km of roads need to be repaired), 2.7M HHs reported a gap between their income and MEB), 2.1M in-need for creating of long or short-term work opportunities.According to HNO 2022, only 4% of the HHs in NWS reported the ability to meet all their basic needs, while 55% reported as insufficient, 38% adequate, and 3% as completely unable, while the "lack of income" come as a second biggest reasons limiting HHs from meeting basic needs. Among WoS figures, the NWS sub-districts are at a noticeable level of vulnerabilities and severity scale. The subdistricts of Dana, Maaret Tamsrin, and Azaz come at the highest level of the ratio of IDPs and with an advanced severity score. The high ratio of IDP, as a result of the large numbers of displacement waves that these areas received, put pressure on infrastructure, public facilities, and services, this casts a shadow on the most vulnerable and marginalized groups meanwhile the exacerbation of the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic and limited livelihood opportunities.

5 - Implementation of activities

Global Communities will implement:- Rehabilitation of 6km of the main roads including a new asphalt layer with all the manholes and drainage infrastructure and lighting required.- Implementing sidewalks/Road rehabilitation through cash for work approach- Establishing and training 16 rescue teams during the rainy season to assist in responding to the floods risks- Rehabilitating 5 bakeries in NWS. The activities will include; 1. Community/facilities selection and outreach: collecting detailed information about the specifics of intended infrastructures that require rehabilitation, through FGDs, KIIs, Observations with all the stakeholders. 2. Topographic surveys for the selected locations, developing the BoQsand the technical specification. 3. Procurement process: through a transparent procurement process, advertising and receiving offers from the bidders, opening bids, TEC, and signing the contract with the selected contractor. 4. Implementation for the proposed rehabilitation and construction, and supervising the implementation of the rehabilitation and construction works. Along with the implementation of the activities, we will have ongoing monitoring for the implementation.Meanwhile, the selection of CFW workers will be through the beneficiaries selection approach and modality while the process will include registration and verification in light of the preselected selection criteria, considering the most vulnerable cases, their dues will be transferred in cash.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

There will be a probability for the following risks;Increased risk of inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages across members as well as the probability of Tension between host communities, IDPs, and returnees. This risk can be mitigated through 1. Use the Cluster guidance on minimum standards and recommended content. 2. Use recommended minimum standards in quality and specification of items. 3. identify and exclude from variance any items particularly prone to create tensions. 3. Ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs and not based on status. 4. The selection of the facilities is transparent, and groups are fully informed. Distribution partners engage with the communities to sensitize the population. 5. Establish a feedback mechanism and conduct follow-up visits if needs be. 6. Involve the local stakeholders throughout the process. 7. Make effort to target a % of the vulnerable host community in every intervention that aims to support the IDPs. HLP Risk-Related can be mitigated through:Abide by the HLP guidance document that was developed by the SNFI Cluster and Do not proceed with the site preparation unless with the consensus of the ownerConsistent with our standard approach to humanitarian programming, the Program will follow Sphere Guidelines and the "Do no harm" principle and humanitarian principles.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

In order to mitigate is a high risk of Abuse of Authority by actors (PSEA, GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion). This can be mitigated through 1. Ensuring that GC’s and its implementing partners’ staff have read and signed their Code of Conduct. 2. developing a range of measures and internal procedures to identify staff who are perpetrators of abuse and exploitation and act accordingly. 3. Ensuring active inclusion of complaint mechanism in place. Meanwhile, there is a high risk of Crowded living conditions and lack of lighting and gender segregated sanitation facilities can lead to increase in GBV and sexual exploitation as well as communicable diseases. This can be mitigated through 1. Advocate for provision of alternative accommodation/expansion of existing sites, 2. Provision of gender segregated/appropriate privacy in mass shelter in case of large displacement/lack of space to provide individual family shelter unit, 3. Provision of gender segregated sanitation and hygiene facilities sufficiently close to dwellings, 4. Provision of lighting around shelter and WASH facilities, 5. Work closely with protection and other sectors to understand the issues and take necessary actions together to address them, 6. Extending consultations such as on siting of facilities with all groups of population including women, girls and PWD.
