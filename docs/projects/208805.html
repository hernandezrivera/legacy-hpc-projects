Project Summary

Medair aims to deliver multi-sectoral lifesaving basic services in health, nutrition, basic Mental Health and Pychosocial Support Service (MHPSS), and water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH), emergency shelter & non-food items (ES/NFIs) along with strengthening local preparedness and response capacities in delivering humanitarian aid, responding to crisis and strengthening resilience.
In Blue Nile, with underlying conflict for decades, has seen an estimated influx of 118.093 IDPs in the State since April. Medair conducted a mass MUAC screening in target catchment areas in October 2023, with 10.828 CU5 and 2.896 PLW screened. 3,7% of CU5 were identified as having severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and 11,1% with moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) and 14,7% of PLW had a MUAC < 210mm. Sexual and gender/based violence (SGBV) is sensitive to address, and is further complicated by continuous displacement. Suspected measles and chilera cases were also reported in Blue Nile.
In White Nile, current data estimates 303 active gathering points (GPs) providing refuge to 43,493 IDPs. In Tendalti there are 20 GPs with 230 households and 1000 IDPs integrated in host community. Average clinic utilization rate is 0.4 in assessed areas along with cholera outbreak and poor response measures to contain it.
In West Darfur, approximately, 123,835 IDPs have so far sought refuge. Kereneik locality, identified at severity level 4 (HNO Sudan 2024) and predicted to be at IPC level 4 (IPC Oct 2023 to Feb 2024), has been hardest hit by the most recent fighting. 5 out of the 6 HFs visited have been looted and are temporarily closed for services (Dawr Shari, Ramaliya, Hajr Tama, Mandi and Konjoltori) and 1 has been destroyed (Um Ashwush). Main mortalities of CU5 were reportedly malaria, diarrhoea, and malnutrition. Screening for malnutrition amongst PLW is only available at 20% of the HFs assessed and unavailable for CU5 in any of the HFs. OTP was available at 3 HFs prior to the crisis, but TSFP only available at 1 HF. Together with non-functional HFs, the influx of IDPs have increased the burden of the health services.
Therefore, this project addresses critical needs in hard-to-reach areas of Khartoum, West Darfur, White Nile, and Blue Nile.In West Darfur, White Nile, Blue Nile, through supporting Primary Health Care Centres/Units and mobilising the Mobile Health and Nutrition Teams (MHNTs), Medair will deliver integrated health and nutrition programming which includes the provision of Integrated Management of Childhood Illness (IMCI) for children aged under five (CU5); reproductive health (RH) services (antenatal and post-natal care, family planning, safe delivery and sex and gender based violence (SGBV) management); support of outbreak responses; Community Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) and IYCF; integrated community support through Mother-to-Mother Support Groups (MtMSGs); and MHPSS programming. Basic WASH packages will be provided at supported health facilities to reduce the risk of the spread of communicable diseases. Medair will also conduct distributions of cash assistance and/or non-food items (NFIs) to cover the basic shelter and household needs of the affected populations. Medair has established Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) with IOM and so core pipelines will be mobilised to save cost and increase efficiency.
And in Khartoum, Medair will support primary health services with supporting operational costs through local partners. This will include essential child and maternity health services.
Protection will be mainstreamed across all activities to ensure safe and equitable access, beneficiary participation, empowerment, and dignity.
By targeting the most vulnerable (esp. PLW, CU5, the elderly, people living with disabilities), this project will help reduce excess morbidity and mortality and decrease the risk of communicable illnesses among the affected populations.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection will be mainstreamed across all activities to ensure safe and equitable access, beneficiary participation, empowerment, and dignity. Safe and equitable access - Risk assessments are conducted to identify risks and establish mitigating measures. Considerations of timing, location, layout, function, and support are included when planning the delivery of services. Locations of the service delivery points are safe and accessible to girls, women and people with disabilities (PWDs). By ensuring privacy and confidentiality, all beneficiaries feel safe accessing the health and nutrition services.Beneficiary participation - All proposed activities and target project areas for the needs assessments have been selected in consultation with relevant local authorities and line ministries. At the start of the project, Medair teams will consult with community leaders and other key community representatives (e.g. women, youth groups, PWDs) to consult on specific needs of the community, describe the planned services, ask for feedback, and gain acceptance. Regular communication and consultation will take place with community leaders and other key community representatives throughout the project to share proposed plans, ask for feedback, and address concerns regarding project activities. Girls, women, boys, and men will be consulted throughout the project about concerns, specific needs, protection risks, opinions, and feedback and solutions about key issues, and will be involved in the design and implementation of activities, such as hygiene promotion sessions. For NFIs distributions, targeted communities will be asked what priority items they need in the kit. These items will be based on the Clusters (WASH/ES-NFI) list. Empowerment - WASH in health facility (HF) improvements will be carried out through the establishment of a WASH-FIT Committee at each targeted HF. Following the WASH-FIT methodology, committee members will include members of the community, as well as HF staff. Men, women, and youth and PWDs will be selected as committee members, with at least half of the committee members being female to ensure gender balanced views are heard. The committee will be trained on the WASH-FIT methodology by a Medair staff, and will be actively involved in prioritising the HF improvements required, and monitoring their implementation. The committee’s role is to also seek the input of the wider community to ensure a transparent approach. Mother-to-mother Support Groups (MtMSGs) are established to disseminate behaviour change communication messages. When possible, staff for mobile health and nutrition teams will be recruited locally. Dignity – When support basic WASH at HFs, protection needs are considered. At specific sites, Medair will repair existing latrines, and construct additional latrines, ensuring at least one gender-separated toilet with PWD adjustments in each HF. All toilets will be fitted with interior locks for dignity and safety.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

Medair will conduct regular meetings with community members, leaders, MoH representatives, and local authorities. These meetings will serve as forums to provide updates on project progress, as well as receive feedback from communities. Action points from these meetings will be tracked to monitor accountability. Exit interviews are conducted at supported HFs and mobile sites to understand the feedback of the services delivered. Feedback received from the community will inform the project design and adaptation so that the quality of services can be improved. Considering the current fluid and volatile situation in Sudan, Medair will uphold “do no harm” principle in the planning and implementation of the response. Medair will ensure that the response will not cause any further harm to the population. For example, Medair will take guidance from the community when determining mobile service locations and referrals to other services to address any safety concerns.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Medair will implement Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) as a means of involving affected populations and target communities in the project. AAP provides a simple process that allows communities and people affected by crisis to voice their feedback. Medair has a feedback Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) to promote beneficiary feedback through project meetings, consultations, and focus group discussions (FGDs) with community representatives, key informants, and direct beneficiaries, which will continue throughout the project.Medair maintains robust feedback mechanisms that encourage beneficiaries to freely share their concerns. Feedback and complaints on fraud, corruption, and safeguarding issues (i.e. sexual exploitation and abuse, and child protection) will be addressed promptly and relevant policies and procedures will be applied. Feedback from beneficiaries and other key stakeholders will be continuously used to address individual concerns and improve programme quality.As mandated by the Core Humanitarian Standards, Medair is committed to providing an accessible complaints and response mechanism. Programme staff will meet with communities at the start of the project to communicate programming expectations, engage them in programmatic decisions, and share how to provide feedback. Medair will always ensure there is more than one available feedback channel, and the beneficiaries are informed about the mechanisms and have access to them. The teams will conduct FGDs and exit interviews and there will be lockable feedback boxes in each clinic, with paper and pens provided. Medair has also focused increasingly on accessing feedback and complaints via phone calls, SMS, and WhatsApp to the Medair hotline. Leaflets will be provided with the hotline number, together with key information about Medair and how to provide feedback and complaints. For NFIs or CVA distributions, on the day of distribution, specific staff (male and female) will be solely responsible for hearing feedback and complaints.Feedback received through these mechanisms will be logged. Where possible an appropriate response given on the day in person or during the call or referred to the correct member of staff for follow-up. All complaints will be put in the feedback/complaint online register to be managed through the standard reporting process. Sensitive complaints will be managed with appropriate confidentiality.Where appropriate, feedback or suggestions will be discussed by relevant teams and incorporated into the decision-making processes that directly affect the nature of support to beneficiaries and future programming.Medair will continue to review the demographics of beneficiaries providing feedback to determine whether there is a sub-group of the beneficiary population who is not engaging in feedback systems. If any group is identified, Medair will review the existing Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) mechanisms and adapt to ensure balanced and thorough feedback.
