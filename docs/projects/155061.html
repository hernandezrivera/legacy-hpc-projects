Project Summary

The prolonged and multi-hazardous nature of the crisis in Cox’s Bazar calls for a multi-sectoral response. DCA takes an integrated, gendered approach where all activities lead back to DCA’s strategic goal to save lives, protect people from violence and abuse and to build more resilient communities with access to quality services. Increased physical safety in the camps through site improvements facilitates access and enhances protection and safety of women, girls, children and other vulnerable groups at risk.

Needs

Since August 2017, more than 900,000 Rohingya individuals have fled violence and persecution in Myanmar. Now the second year into the crisis, the scale, severity and complexity of needs across the hazardous conditions in the camps remain overwhelming and services continue to be insufficient or of poor quality. The settlement areas for Rohingya refugees are extremely vulnerable to natural hazards and disasters, particularly in the monsoon (June-Sep) and cyclone (Apr-May and Oct-Nov) periods. This leads to risks of landslides and flooding while storms and cyclones have devastating effects on infrastructures such as pathways, bridges, land, shelter and communal spaces. Technical initiatives and strengthening of infrastructures are urgently needed and are in particular linked to protection and GBV risks (DCA risk mapping sessions). Since the onset of the crisis, deforestation has been massive due to tree cutting for cooking fuel as well as to make space for shelters. Sector assessments highlight the need of scaling up environmental friendly fuel alternatives, which are planned for 2019.
