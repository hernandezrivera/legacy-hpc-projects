Project Summary

According to HNO 2024, which revealed a 4% increase in the sector PIN, indicating a pressing need for emergency education services, UNICEF highlights the dire state of the education system post-conflict. Schools are damaged or destroyed, hindering access to accredited educational services for over two million children, with another 1.6 million at risk of dropping out.
Challenges persist in making learning environments conducive, including overcrowding, dilapidated infrastructure, teacher shortages, and inadequate facilities. Particularly concerning is the exclusion of over 60% of children with severe disabilities from any form of education, due to physical barriers, and lack of resources.
the MSNA in northern Syria shows that the percentage of children with disabilities not enrolled in school stands at 72 per cent, compared to 40 per cent for children without disabilities. The data also shows that enrolment rates for children with disabilities drop by about 10 percentage points after the age of 14, reaching 81 per cent for children aged 15-17.
Such conditions contribute to low attendance and learning outcomes, exacerbated by the risk of communicable diseases in schools and communities. Limited investment in diverse learning pathways, especially for adolescents, raises concerns of a "lost generation".
Urgent actions are needed to repair schools, provide educational materials, implement specialized learning methods, and incentivize teachers. Ensuring physical and mental well-being for both students and educators is paramount to sustaining learning opportunities and preventing long-term educational setbacks.
In response to the urgent educational needs of children and youth in conflict-affected areas of Idlib and Aleppo governorates, ATAA is launching a comprehensive project aimed at regenerating and enhancing educational opportunities. The project targets vulnerable populations, including children and youth aged 3 to 24, with a particular focus on those in Early Childhood Care and Development (ECCD), Basic, Secondary, and Vocational Education.
The communities of Dana, Harim, Maaret Tamsrin, and Teftnaz, in Idlib governorate, and A'zaz, Al-Bab, Jarablus, Atareb, Daret Azze Jandairis sub-districts in Aleppo governorate are prioritized based on severity rankings. These areas have been deeply impacted by frequent displacement and challenging living conditions, leading to a significant number of dropped-out children and those at risk of dropping out.
The project through 2024/2025 will reach 31,824 beneficiaries, including 14,320 females, 17,504 males, including 375 female children with disabilities (CWD), and 419 male CWD. Additionally, 1471 educational personnel, consisting of 624 females and 847 males, will be supported.
Educational interventions will encompass both FE and NFE programs, tailored to meet the diverse needs of the target population. FE will follow the SIG curriculum, while NFE will include remedial classes, catch-up programs, and SLP for OOSC.
Moreover, TVET opportunities will be provided to OOSC aged 16 to 24, aiming to mitigate the risks of recruitment into armed groups, early marriage, and child labor.
To ensure a safe and conducive learning environment, the project will undertake minor rehabilitation of schools to make them disability-friendly. TLCs will be established, and school furniture provided. School safety plans will be implemented, including fire extinguishers, first aid kits, evacuation drills, and information-sharing initiatives.
Collaboration with the ED will facilitate the recognition of NFE achievements for transition to FE. Children with disabilities will receive tailored support, including recreational activities and the rehabilitation of WASH facilities and classrooms.
During winter, heaters and required fuel will be provided to ensure the comfort of students and staff.
Summer programs will feature catch-up and remedial classes, as well as SEL activities.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

ATAA employs a scientific risk management approach by developing a risk matrix during project design that considers all potential hazards and mitigation measures. As part of the management of this risk, the S&S department depends on the strategy of achieving community acceptance during all phases of projects and building a trust relationship with the local authorities by clarifying for the local authorities the impact of the project on the region. Due to the unpredictable nature of conflicts, such as shelling or armed clashes, the S&S department closely monitors the project area, conducts daily risk assessments, and communicates potential scenarios and appropriate responses to the crisis cell. In addition, the department updates evacuation and contingency plans and provides field staff with training on expected scenarios and adherence to approved SOPs for a secure operational environment.ATAA is considering the PSEA in the top of priorities by active membership and participation in PSEA networks activities, and developed internal framework and control system to enhance PSEA including all the core standards, in addition to include the prevention measures in all the humanitarian interventions of ATAA.Additionally, ATAA created a solid response system starts from the active and accessible reporting channels, and investigation planning and procedures with qualified and trained staff and network of the focal points with gender balance to enhance access to survivors and provide the response interventions based on survivor based approach.Ataa is updating PSEA assessments with partners and received a UNICEF assessment result as full capacity low risk partner since mid of 2023. the project is designed based on solid integration of protection risks especially GBV related risks on female children and adults in the project locations, in this regard ATAA will establish specialized service mapping including GBV related services and provide safe identification and referral training for the education personnel to support the referral mechanism and conduct survivor based approach to respond to any case of GBV during the implementation. Protection mainstreaming is a core concept of the education intervention in ATAA and each education facility will be supported with protection specialized person with considering the gender balance to reach all gender groups especially most vulnerable and marginalized groups (PWDs)
