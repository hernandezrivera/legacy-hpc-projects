Project Summary

The ENRICH II project will utilize a multi-sectorial approach to reach the overall objective of improved nutritional outcomes of PLW and CU5 (and a special focus on CU2) addressing the gaps in the targeted areas.
ADRA has funding approved for supplementary food/hygiene assistance in Qusair on a monthly basis for 6 months through a voucher to 500 PLW in line with food and agriculture sector guidelines. The voucher amount will be between $44-$50 / month (variable based on inflation) and redeemable through local vendors for a variety of fresh foods (vegetables, fruits, meat and dairy products) and hygiene items (female sanitary pads, soap, washing powder, dishwashing detergent, cleaning clothes, shampoo, toothpaste, toothbrushes, cleaning liquids, gloves, etc.). ADRA’s partner, the Syrian Arab Red Crescent (SARC) already has a list of PLW in the targeted area and ADRA will undertake 100% verification and prioritization of potential beneficiaries. In addition, identified cases (by ADRA or other organizations) of severe or moderate acute malnutrition (SAM or MAM) in the targeted areas will also be prioritized for nutritional sensitive agricultural or livestock grants and technical training which will be provided to 120 HHs to help ensure the sustainability of a diversified diet.
The proposed project will complement the activities above. Those receiving the supplementary food/hygiene assistance or nutrition sensitive grants, as well as PLW and CU5 in Rustan will receive a series of awareness sessions encompassing topics designed to prevent malnutrition including the need for a diverse diet, IYCF practices as well as safe water and hygiene practices. During the verification for the supplementary food/hygiene assistance and awareness sessions, and as part of the SARC mobile and fixed health teams, PLW and CU5 will be screened for malnutrition and referred to either the SARC fixed health clinic in Rustan with nutritional services planned to be established through this project and supplies provided by existing agreements with UNICEF and WFP, SARC mobile clinic in Qusair or to local NGOs Al Bir and the Syrian Family Planning Association (SFPA), all of whom have established programs to treat severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) cases.

3 - Needs

The increase in the burden of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) cases among children aged 6-59 months across Syria is concerning, from 51,013 recorded in 2021 nutrition surveys to 75,726 (48% increase) recorded in 2022 (HNO 2023).The sub-districts of Qusair and Rustan, Homs Governorate experienced heavy damages during the conflict to homes and basic services. Malnutritional rates, food insecurity (severity 4) and WASH needs remain high with limited humanitarian assistance. Both locations are classified overall as severity level 4 with severity level 4 for Stunting, and EBF, as well as level 3 for MAD. Qusair in particular has unmet needs (severity 5).In Rustan, food insecurity remains high with 67% of the population moderately or severely food insecure and 56% of households experiencing stress, crisis or emergency livelihood coping strategies. The area is considered to be at extreme risk of food insecurity (level 4) according to the FSA 2023.In Qusair, a total of 1,709 PLW and over 7,000 children under 5 are present in the area according to Ministry of Health centers statistics in June 2022 with Kareem (a local NGO) covering only between 600-700 individuals with no nutritional screening. The area is considered to be at extreme risk of food insecurity (level 4) according to the FSA 2023.

4 - Implementation of activities

Volunteers from ADRA and SARC will be trained to undertake MUAC screening for PLW and CU5. The training will consist topics of IYCF (how to asses child growth, IYCF in emergencies, importance of breastfeeding and best practices...) and CMAM (overview of the types and causes of malnutrition, the nutrition situation in Syria, how to take measurements and indicators for malnutrition...).MUAC screening will take place during the verification process for the supplementary feeding as well as during visits by the SARC mobile health teams in the targeted areas, during awareness sessions and through the fixed health center in Rustan. Screening will target around 4,000 individuals (PLW as well as CU5 with a special focus on people with disabilities). Those measured as at risk and malnourished will be referred to the SARC mobile clinic in Qusair for treatment and follow-up, or to local NGOs working in the area. It is anticipated that 4% of those screened will be referred for treatment. Those individuals will be prioritized to receive nutrition sensitive agriculture and livestock support through ADRA’s other funding source. ADRA will monitor the project through visits, review of daily and month reports, case studies and respond to feedback through the feedback and complaints mechanism.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

The targeted community withdraws its support of the planned activities. Likelihood: Low, Impact: High. The project will work closely with community members, including beneficiaries to ensure that the materials used for the awareness are culturally appropriate, suitable to the context and will not cause offence to the population. The selection criteria used for all activities will be clear, equitable and target the most vulnerable, thereby reducing any potential community tensions.Risk of GBV against participating beneficiaries: Likelihood: Low, Impact: High. All staff and volunteers are trained in PSEA and sign the code of conduct. There is a complaints mechanism that can be utilized anonymously if desired and the project will work closely with community leaders and local authorities to address any protection concerns raised.Planned activities overlap with other nutrition partners, causing confusion and tensions in the community and inefficient use of resources. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Moderate. ADRA will coordinate all activities through the nutrition sub-sector in Homs which has already been consulted about the planned activities. In addition, bilateral meetings will regularly take place with partners working in the same area and comparing beneficiary lists for the purpose of avoiding duplication.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: The field staff or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they should be serving, likelihood low, impact high. As a mitigation measure,ADRA Syria will stand firm on zero tolerance for (sexual) misconduct, and will regularly refresh and train its staff and partner staff on the importance of maintaining an environment thatprevents sexual exploitation and sexual abuse. Project staff at all different levels, partner staff, contractors, and suppliers will be encouraged to read and understand the ADRA code ofconduct and safeguarding, the protection manual and commit to abide by this principle. ADRA protection officer will ensure to have systems in place for activating the system, gatheringinformation, and conducting an investigation where necessary to make decisions together with ADCOM for taking any necessary actions towards any misconduct reported.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Operational:Project activities will require extensive coordination with all actors in the targeted areas and permission from SARC.Complaints from the community about the project leading to a withdrawal of support.ADRA maintains strong connections with all stakeholders and has already obtained necessary permissions and approvals, including coordination with relevant sectors and partners. The project has clear selection criteria and complain mechanism to raise any issues with ADRA and SARC staff. Security:The security situation may deteriorate, preventing access and increasing displacement. Sensitive data is leaked / released to unauthorized sources. ADRA closely monitors the security situation through local contacts and UNDSS to ensure the protection of staff, beneficiaries and stakeholders in accordance with the ADRA Safety and Security Plan. ADRA has a data protection policy that will be implemented to protect sensitive beneficiary information and along with SARC have established complain mechanisms to address the communities’ needs. Financial:Devaluation of SYP may place project activities outside the planned budget The procurement of supplies and materials / vendors could be subject to corruption or conflict of interest.ADRA has financial policies and procedures that adhere to internal and donor regulations but prioritize the quick payment of invoices from vendors and suppliers to help mitigate against any exchange rate variations.
