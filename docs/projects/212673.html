Project Summary

After 13 years of conflict, repeated displacements, and economic decline resulting in protracted humanitarian crisis needs remain high and widespread in Northern Syria. According to the latest findings from the Northern Syria Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA), released by REACH in February 2024 almost three quarters of households’ report that they were unable to secure basic necessities. Food, livelihoods, and health assistance were reported as the top unmet needs. In response to these needs, People in Need's (PIN) project proposes to contribute towards saving and sustaining lives of targeted conflict-affected communities in Northern Syria through the provision of unconditional and unrestricted cash assistance aimed at fully or partially covering a household’s (multiple) survival needs to reduce protection risks and reliance on negative coping strategies.
Under the assistance PIN target to cover 14,190 individuals in NES and 40,000 individuals in NWS. Both hubs provide 130 USD in emergency assistance and 105 USD in regular assistance.
Activities will be underpinned by emergency preparedness and response plans developed in coordination with relevant clusters, working groups, and partners to promote timely, efficient and joined-up responses that seek to maximize geographic and sectoral coverage and minimize gaps.
PIN is committed to ensuring inclusivity, ‘’do no harm’’, and accessibility throughout the project. The selection of women, children, people with disabilities, and the elderly as primary target groups is grounded in a deep understanding of the distinct vulnerabilities these populations face in conflict-affected settings. Women, often disproportionately impacted by conflict as well as social barriers, confront elevated risks of gender-based violence, encounter obstacles to accessing essential services and have much less opportunities to sustain income. Prioritizing their needs is not only a matter of addressing immediate challenges but is also critical for advancing gender equality, community resilience, and long-term sustainable development. Children, being amongst the most vulnerable in conflict zones, experience disruptions in education, psychosocial trauma, and heightened exposure to various forms of exploitation such as home retention for girls due to safety and culture-driven reasons or child labour where school-aged boys are the primary group impacted. By bringing strong emphasis on the needs of children, PIN aims to safeguard their well-being and contribute to their holistic development. People with disabilities encounter unique challenges, including discrimination and physical barriers, limiting their access to critical services. Tailoring our interventions to address their specific needs aligns with the principles of inclusivity, ensuring that humanitarian assistance is accessible to all members of the community, regardless of ability. The approach is embedded in all PIN policies and technical manuals that govern the implementation of all the activities under this action. The elderly, often overlooked in crisis responses, face heightened health risks, isolation, and increased vulnerabilities. By specifically targeting this demographic, the action recognizes the importance of addressing their unique needs, fostering social cohesion, and contributing to a more comprehensive and effective humanitarian intervention. The intervention will be carried out in consultations with community members using a participatory and inclusive approach.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

PIN intervention will observe any chance of tension between IDPs and the local population, PIN is always circulating the project information across all communities ensuring that all the vulnerable beneficiaries have equal and possible access to the information and are aware of the selection process, PIN is ensuring that through project mobilization and the CFRM channels, this approach is gender sensitive which means that gender is well considered in the responsible team structure. The CFRM channels are also highly sensitive to any claims of abuse and exploitation and hold the needed investigation by the accountability and take the right actions once confirmed. PIN interventions ensure that no one shall be left behind due to gender, race, religion, ethnicity, or geographical area, PIN is working closely with the local authorities and working group to help ensure equal access to the services and exclude any possibility of exclusions. And PIN will make sure to train staff on Do No Harm, protection from SEA (PSEA), and child safeguarding; and sign and adhere to code of conduct that respect and uphold the rights of beneficiaries and PSEA . Set up communication and complaints mechanisms or desks that are clearly marked; for ex. feedback desks, face-to-face interviews with beneficiaries, and have teams on ground to monitor distributions and respond quickly and fairly to safety concerns including coercion, intimidation, cheating, violence, or exploitation, including actions perpetrated by project staff, and employ female staff to be part of the project field team.
