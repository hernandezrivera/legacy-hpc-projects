Project Summary

The project aims to provide improved quality and protective learning environment for 8120 Syrian boys and girls in 10 schools in Idleb and Aleppo Governorate. These schools are located in the areas where there is high proportion of out of school children. As per the non-structured interviews with school management, 30% of primary school age children (5-17 years) are out of school. Non-availability of education supplies (school bags, stationary, uniform), child labor, increase trend of early marriages and inappropriate learning facilities are common factors contributing to high percentage of out of school children.
A complete Back to School campaign will be run in the targeted communities. Community volunteers (teachers) will be recruited and will receive monthly stipend to facilitate this campaign. This campaign will sensitize the parents and caregivers about the importance of education and will bring back 300 children into targeted formal schools. All the children in the targeted schools will be provided with student kit. The sensitization campaign will continue throughout the project period to mitigate the risks of school drop outs.
The targeted schools will be provided with student kit and education supplies and furniture as per the need. 120 teachers and school management staff will be identified for monthly stipend. These teachers will also include psycho social trainers. The teachers and administrative staff will also be trained on child protection and psychosocial support. The teaching staff will conduct regular classes in the targeted schools and individual/group psychosocial sessions and recreational activities will be conducted. In additional minor school repair and rehabilitation will be conducted specially to improve WASH facilities in schools. Provision of prefabricated classrooms to ensure that education space is suitable for learning.
The provision of conducive learning environment, improved enrolment rates, psychosocial support, education supplies, student kit, school rehabilitation and regular classes are expected to contribute positively in the overall child development as well as attendance and school performance.
The project is aligned with the objectives and priorities as set by Humanitarian Response plan. This project will also contribute to the No lost generation initiative by enrolling the out of school children and providing them with protective learning environment.

Needs

As the conflict continues, effective teaching becomes increasingly challenging due to mixed-aged, mixed-ability, over-crowded and under resourced classrooms. In addition, having been exposed to the psychological trauma of hostilities, displacement and family upheaval generated by the crisis, many children and teachers are affected by psychological trauma. A 2018 survey across multiple governorates indicate 1 in 8 children per classroom have psychosocial support needs requiring specialized interventions for effective teaching and learning, affecting the learning not only of the individual students affected, but also other pupils and teaching staff. The barriers to accessing quality and relevant learning for children inside Syria are complex and include a wide range of protection and socio-economic issues, extending beyond the education sector. These complicating factors include the prevalence of child labor, child marriage, lack of identity and education attainment documentation, lack of safety both at home and in school, the cumulative psychosocial effects of the protracted crisis and extensive displacement. In some areas, over half of children with a disability have an unmet need for education, and over two thirds require specialized health services which are not available in their area. After years of crisis, the number of school aged children who have been out of school for more than five years is on the rise resulting in complex learning needs. More than 40 per cent of the out-of-school children in Syria are between 15 and 17 years of age and have differentiated learning, skills development and livelihoods needs. The education system is fragmented and overburdened with multiple curricula taught. This has significant repercussions on the provision and certification of learning. Protection risks have been observed during official examination periods for students crossing active frontlines to sit for official exams. Teaching capacity remains overstretched. Over 140,000 teachers and education personnel are no longer in their teaching posts. The remaining are overwhelmed, resulting in an overall poor quality of education. Quality of teaching is key to students’ performance, and teachers’ capacity is fundamental to children’s experience of a quality education. Reliance on unskilled and unspecialized teachers with limited financial remuneration is impeding learning outcomes of students in public schools, with important disparities amongst regions and between IDPs and host communities. Across Syria, 2.1 million children, over one third of children in Syria, are out-of-school and a further 1.3 million children are at risk of dropping out. 2.6 million school-age children are displaced as a result of the crisis, many displaced multiple times, while 360,000 children remain in hard-to-reach areas as of October 2018. Displaced children are more likely to be out-of-school and the dropout rate is higher in displaced communities. The vulnerability of school-age children with no access to learning opportunities has increased over the almost eight years of the crisis, putting children at higher risk of being exposed to exploitation, abuse and rights violations. The intensification of hostilities in some areas has negatively impacted thousands of children who lost access to education opportunities. Access to education is lost when children are forced to suddenly leave their area of residence, schools close or have no teaching personnel available and do not provide certified education.

Implementation of activities

The project will be implemented in strong coordination with the local authorities, the education directorate involving all relevant stakeholders. The project is designed to contribute to the overall objective of contributing to the improvement of the education and individual development of the Syrian children affected by the on-going crises and is set jointly by the humanitarian community under the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP). Detailed meetings will be conducted with the school management to list down the furniture and education supplies /running cost for each school. Based on which procurement of furniture, education supplies will be conducted. To ensure teachers and the school management staff remains committed for their regular attendance, monthly stipend will be provided to 120 teacher and school management staff over a period of 9 months. IR in coordination with the local authorities will identify training institute to provide TOT trainings on INEE minimum standards for education. 120 teachers and school management staff will be trained as master trainers. These trained group of teachers will conduct monthly workshops in their respective schools. These workshops will provide an opportunity to disseminate their learning to PEER and colleagues. Also through these workshops plans will be developed to improve the teaching quality and conduct Psychosocial support activities. This platform will be used to share the ideas among the teachers and school management staff to further improve the learning environment for children. Back to school campaign will be organised to enrol out of school children in the targeted community. Open days including interactive theatres will be organised to raise awareness about importance of education. Broachers, banners and other visibility material will be distributed. Further community volunteers (teacher) will be recruited to facilitate the campaign in the targeted community. These will primarily work as a strong link between the targeted community and the school. These will work with parents and caregivers of out of school children. Door to door visits will be conducted. Each teacher will educate at least 20 out of school children by accelerated and remedial learning for 2 months. These children will then be mainstreamed in targeted formal schools after determining suitable grades for them. The children in the targeted schools will also receive student kit. The kits includes all the required learning items such as books pen student bag.. School rehabilitation including the WASH facilities rehabilitation (segregated by sex and age, separate space for boys, girls and adult as well as children with disability), installation of prefabricated classrooms, schools expansion and rehabilitation. IR team will continuously monitor and follow up the project activities to ensure the attendance of the children and their school performance.

Protection Risk Analysis

• Changes in the security situation, particularly in project target areas, affects the safety/mobility/ displacement of beneficiaries/staff and results in being unable to participate in project. This includes the risk of targeted attacks and bombing of schools/ communities and the risks of mines, airstrikes and cross fire. (IR will closely monitor the security situation in coordination with partners and relevant stakeholders and conduct a security assessments. IR will ensure that staff members and teachers are trained in safety and security best practices, including mine risk education.)• Protection risks of mixing different age groups in the same class IR will ensure that the school management create separate times of attendance for each age group.• Resistance to gender mainstreaming from male leadership structures. (Programme activities which engage women will be endorsed by community and religious leaders to gain support from male community members to reduce the likelihood of resistance to these activities)• Child abuse in school (Training of partners and local teams on Child safety, development of schools specific child safety policies; establishment of complaint mechanisms in school.)• Security situation affecting the project cite (no access) IR will keep working with the relevant partners on other means of access• Risk of not having enough WASH facilities for boys and girls. This might cause lack of privacy to the children. IR will work in collaboration with WASH section who will establish WASH facilities in schools.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

• No access to project location. In coordination with all relevant stakeholders IR can change the project location if access to the planned location become impossible • Closure of Turkish border will hinder the access of some material and staff, we will look for other alternative to get the material from the Syrian market or coordinate with the Turkish authority to deliver the items through humanitarian border• Interference of armed groups in the project areas ( In order to ensure that armed groups do not get involved in programming, IR will engage with relevant stakeholders and make sure that the local councils and local authorities are aware of the projects and have provided adequate access and space to conduct activities.)• Risk of children dropping out of formal education ( Identify specific criteria to all implementing partners for accepting children into the program; meeting with parents to encourage them send their children to school)• Market fluctuation changes in prices. Prior to start of implementation, a market assessment will be conducted in order to see what is available and what is not, and establishing contingency plans for materials that can be replaced with alternative resources, including determining purchase inside or outside Syria;
