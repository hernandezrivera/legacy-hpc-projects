Project Summary

Zimbabwe is experiencing a deepening humanitarian crisis, resulting from a combination of climatic shocks and economic crises. According to the World Bank (October, 2019) inflation, with attendant consequences on the population’s purchasing power, has been increasing since October 2018, with annual inflation reaching 230 percent in July 2019 as compared to 5.4 percent in September 2018. As at July 2019 food prices had risen by 319 percent (Ibid). It has been noted that the proportion of household expenditure on food rose from 70% of disposable income in 2019 as compared to 55% in 2018. Extreme poverty is estimated to have risen from 29 percent in 2018 to 34 percent in 2019 (from 4.7 to 5.7 million people) (WBG October, 2019). These factors, coupled with food insecurity and drought, has resulted in a number of risks, arising from negative coping strategies, conflict and violence. Currently a projected 1.2 million people, mostly women and girls are facing the risk or experiencing gender based violence, and require urgent protection measures to be implemented by all humanitarian actors.
Humanitarian crises are deeply gendered and increase existing socio-economic vulnerabilities, more so for women and girls. For example, poverty levels among female-headed households are higher than among male-headed households, and any shocks disproportionately affects them. During crises, such as conflict or disasters, women often endure extreme hardships, such as increased violence and insecurity, restricted mobility and additional care, domestic and livelihood responsibilities. It has been noted that during natural disasters the likelihood of rape, sexual exploitation and risky behaviour greatly increases, and unwanted pregnancies, sexually transmitted infections and complications regarding reproductive health. Studies have also shown that women disproportionately suffer the impacts of drought because of cultural norms and the inequitable distribution of roles, resources and power. This was borne out in a UN Women Rapid Vulnerability Assessment of the Impact of Drought on Women and Girls in Chiredzi and Mbire Districts Zimbabwe (2016). According to the 2016 ZIMVAC Supplementary Gender Report more female headed households (4.5%) reported experiencing marital and other social disharmony/gender-based violence when compared to male headed holds (1.9%), added households unable to recover from such situations.
The effectiveness of humanitarian responses is undermined by the lack of adequate gender analysis based on accurate and timely sex and age disaggregated data. This is compounded by limited or no consultation with women on forms of assistance and protection they need and the role they can play. Further, women’s leadership roles as early responders and promoters of community resilience continues to be underutilized. It is thus essential that humanitarian actors who are responsible for ensuring the humanitarian response system is effective underpin all their work with a gender perspective, as this enhances access to assistance and is critical in preventing and mitigating protection risks.

1 - Activities

Needs780 273 Zimbabwean women and girls in 46 districts continue to require some form of protection from gender-based violence in its various forms: sexual, physical, emotional, intimate partner violence, sexual exploitation and abuse. The economic situation, including food insecurity impacts on the ability of women and girls to access basic services, including education and health. This also compromises their sexual and reproductive health rights. The humanitarian situation is not confined to cyclone and drought affected areas alone, women and girls in urban areas and informal settlements, amongst others, are also in need. This requires that those at the frontline of humanitarian assistance are well equipped with targeted and women centred responses to the multiplicity of crises. 675 humanitarian workers have been capacitated to with GBViE mainstreaming and PSEA capacity building initiatives (OCHA 2019). The integration of broader gender equality and women’s empowerment perspectives, tools and strategies beyond this is required, to ensure emergency response plans, monitoring and reporting tools, are addressing the complex nature of gender issues in humanitarian response. This includes the equal consultation of women and girls so that their needs and vulnerabilities are understood and addressed. Further mobilisation and information efforts shall be required, including supporting the collection of sex disaggregated data which will inform intervention efforts. Capacity building of humanitarian actors across the clusters encompasses equipping them with the necessary information supported by rapid gender and vulnerability assessments, supporting their respective response strategies, undertaking monitoring including setting up gender monitoring mechanisms, and providing the necessary information to communities for an effective humanitarian response. One of the key tools for capacity that Un Women shall utilise is the “Gender Handbook for Humanitarian Action” produced by the Inter Agency Standing Committee (IASC) in 2018. It is a practical tool that provides guidance to frontline humanitarian workers on how to integrate gender equality and the empowerment of women and girls throughout the humanitarian action cycle. The planned activities are as outlined below:-•Rapid gender and vulnerability assessments in affected districts (including reviewing other sectors’ tools to ensure gender is taken into consideration)•Train humanitarian actors to integrate gender in their humanitarian response plans, and implementation strategies •Training on gender sensitive monitoring and evaluation in humanitarian programmes including •Training & establishment of community outreach groups, including women groups to monitor gender in humanitarian response•Develop information and advocacy materials to strengthen the gendered response to humanitarian disasters
