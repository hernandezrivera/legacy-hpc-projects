Project Summary

For - 2023, the Syria project will focus on providing health and nutrition services in 4 locations in high IDPs location in Aleppo and Idlib governorate including those most at risk. The proposed 4 expanded PHCs will provide integrated primary health care services, CMAM and IYCF services, and protection support for people in need.
This integration will be as recommended by the health and nutrition clusters, to ensure well and safe access for PLWs and children. However, the PSS and protection activities will work as a supportive package and referral point to provide basic PSS services, and mot-to-mother support, and also refer advanced cases to specialized mental health clinics in the nearby area. This project will run in, Al-Bab, Dana, TalAbyad, and Jarablus Districts.
Med global will use the ESHP as guideline for service provision through pedatric clinic, GYOB clinics, and GP and CHWs services.
In order to provide life-saving nutritional support to PLWs, CU5, through the qualitative implementation of CMAM and IYCF guidelines supported with awareness-raising, referral, and COVID-19 prevention measures, targeting people in need as IDPs and Host Communities in 7 different sub-districts in NWS, and to support the cluster coordination activities, facilitate the nutrition actors access to supplies, and follow-up the malnutrition trend in NWs.
The following activities will be done in this project.
• Increase access to lifesaving and life-sustaining primary health care services.
• Enhance the pandemic control and prevention measures through good application of C19 and cholera awareness and protection measures in the served communities.
• Run 4 Outpatient therapeutic feeding programs (OTP) and Mother-Baby Areas (MBAs) through Static OTPs located in high GAM rates and coverage gap areas. These centres will provide outpatient CMAM services by screening all the 6–59 months children, PLWs, treatment of SAM, and MAM children and PLW. In contrast, the centres will provide IYCF services for PLWs and CU2. the needed supplies will be received through UNICEF and the WFP supply chain systems through their local partners. Med-Global will follow the nutrition cluster TWG recommendations and guidelines for safe service provision.
• 8 RRT lined to these 5 OTP&PHCs through well-trained CHWs and IYCF workers will serve in the target area to enhance the IYCF best practices, CU5 and PLWs screening, and awareness raising These 8 RRTs will provide multisectoral services by integrating the CMAM program and infant and Young Child Feeding in Emergency (IYCF-E) Program that directly works for with;
A- Health Services through screening, referral of CU5 and PLW with medical problems to the nearby health facility disseminate of awareness of self-hygiene messages, child protection, and COVID-19.
B- WASH awareness: Support hygiene promotion by raising WASH awareness by providing Key messages (Hand Washing, Food Processing, and access to clean water). COVID-19 hygiene, Risk Communication, and Community Engagement (RCCE) related messages.
C- Child Protection: Identify visual signs of abuse/violence against child U5/PLWs during Nutrition screening.
• All these facilities have access to the nutrition supplies needed for MAM and SAM case treatment.
The target population is almost 175000 including 45% host community and 55% IDPs. Beneficiaries from both genders and target ages will have equal access to project services.

3 - Needs

In health, only 59% of hospitals and 57% of public health care centres are fully functional and active. Also, COVID-19 and Cholera outbreak has created an overload on the current fragile healthcare system. The population in NWS is still in deep need of life-saving health care, and through the provided PHC services, Medgloabl will address this gap. The overall nutrition situation in NWS is deteriorating. Children under 5, pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) are the most susceptible to malnutrition since their nutritional requirements are relatively high. According to the SMART survey conducted by PAC in July 2022, the prevalence of chronic malnutrition among children aged 6-59 months increased was 22.3 per cen.The prevalence of combined Global Acute Malnutrition GAM was 5.0 per cen in July 2022 in Northwest Syria in Aleppo and Idlib governorates, and the prevalence of GAM by MUAC was 4.3 per cen.Among PLWs, the prevalence of acute malnutrition across northwest Syria is 12.9 per cen. There are also significant differences across northwest Syria, witIdleb more than Aleppo.According to the same survey, Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices were found to be sub-optimal. The high burden of stunting in children is linked to sub-optimal uptake of appropriate IYCF practices, which are crucial for healthy child development and survival. Only 56% of children under six months are exclusively breastfed whilst only 4.62% consume the Minimal Acceptable Diet (MAD) in NWS.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

In MedGlobal(MG) Organization protection main principles are mainstreamed into all the Organization’s project cycles and in all humanitarian activities and work aiming at enhancing MG’s programmes quality to enable the most vulnerable members of communities to access the services that MG seek to provide; contributes to creating a safer, more protective environment for the people we serve, ensures that MG is aware of the possible harm that its activities can cause and that MG take steps to avoid this; and empowers staff and communities to support and protect themselves thereby making our interventions more sustainable, this is can be clearly seen through two key underpinning of our integration approach. First, linkages and integration of services (in health, WASH, FSL, and child protection) where feasible and with the establishment of community based referral mechanisms where appropriate to ensure accountability. Second, through the integration of technical training material and resources between the key sectors to raise and up scale the staff capacities . MG’s approach provides an optimal mechanism for the identification GBV cases of the most vulnerable children and women providing referrals pathways mechanisms that are available to the most appropriate service to be tailored to their individual needs. In the proposed project, MG will make sure that within the PHC Center the beneficiaries privacy and their dignity through keeping all medical personal information confidential.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

MG is an active member in the NWS PSEA network and maintains close monitoring activities over the PSEA in its ongoing and planned projects. For this project, the PSEA strategy can be summarized as follows; Risk #1; Beneficiaries and community members have no access to the PSEA complaint and reporting system:Mitigation: MG has clear signs and a feedback system in its fixed facilities that show how the reports can be provided through email, phone, social media, and feedback boxes. Also, the mobile team will distribute IEC matriels to encourage the BNFs to share their feedback Risk#2: MG newly contracted staff have a poor experience in PSEA. Mitigation: MG has a clear policy presented to every staff that shows the PSEA mitigation mesuears. Also, the MG PSEA code of conduct is well written and all the staff signed on it. In addition to that, MG has mandatory online PSEA training that should be completed by every newly contracted staff training link: " https://agora.unicef.org/course/info.php?id=18168", and all MG staff should do annual refreshment training. furthermore, MG has PSEA focal points in the field to support the field workers in full adherence with PSEA guidelines. Risk #3: The investigation process in the reported PSEA is not well managedMitigation: MG has well-trained PSEA officers. Also, any PSEA case reported to MG team will be processed.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Implementation of the project activities is prevented/ stopped by regular clashes, violence, or an upsurge in criminality including theft or kidnapping.Mitigation 1: MG will monitor the security developments and reported criminal instances/ violence in its areas of operation. If an increase in criminality/violence is noted, the project activities will be suspended and alternative areas/activities might be considered under the planned intervention with the approval from health Cluster. Risk 2: staff and patients are at risk due to potential outbreak mitigation measures 2: PPE Kits always are available for staff, regular sterilization for facility and equipment, distribution of surgical masks for patients, and ensure physical distance condition.. risk 3: Loss or damage to project documentation Mitigation measures 3: Maintaining soft copies of documents - Storage of hard copies in safe places and Communication with partner and donor directly Risk 4: Limited access to target areas due to security situation mitigation measures 4: Coordination with community representatives and local authorities, where possible - Presence of staff, offices, and volunteers in target areas Risk 5: Airstrikes or military attacks targeting the project sites Mitigation measures 5: Development of safety and security plan and training - Identification of security focal point in each project team - Establishing communication plan.
