Project Summary

Women, men and children in conflict and disaster affected communities are able to meet their basic household needs and protect their livelihoods, despite shocks and uncertainties. This will be achieved through a combination of emergency, recovery and resilience projects. The aim of emergency projects will be to provide lifesaving unconditional cash and voucher transfers to prevent famine, improve food consumption, improve nutrient intake and dietary diversity. Targeted groups will include recently displaced populations, host communities, households in locations under IPC 4 and 5 in areas where markets are functional. Relief and resilience projects will aim to increase household food production promote and protect rural and urban livelihoods, through public works, training and provision of agricultural inputs through support to market systems. Target groups will include IDPs, returnees, host communities in locations under IPC 3 and 4. Emergency, recovery and resilience projects will all contribute towards improving coping strategies of vulnerable households and reduce their reliance on general food and in-kind distributions. Women and youth will be prioritized as most the vulnerable groups.

Needs

The context analysis of South Sudan identified conflict and insecurity as some of the main shocks affecting South Sudan populations resulting in the disruption of livelihoods and high levels of food insecurity in the country. The political crisis has resulted in massive fighting among rival military groups; inter communal conflict causing insecurity countrywide, sporadic displacement of women, children, youth and men and loss of livelihoods assets. In Aweil North and East, the counties bordering Abyei, Gogrial and Sudan, often experience an influx of people fleeing inter communal clashes due to competition for grazing land. According to REACH February 2018 assessment, clashes between armed groups in Raja County during the first quarter of the year have caused displacement resulting in affected populations fleeing to Wau and Aweil West. Meanwhile, in Wau urban displaced households have started gradually voluntarily leaving PoCs and returning to areas of residence in Wau urban. In Jonglei, cattle raiding, revenge killings, child abduction occurs in most areas, in particular Duk, Bor, Twic and Akobo. In Warrap inter communal clashes between Apuk and Aguok communities resulted in the government issuing a state of emergency covering areas of Aweil East and Gogrial West. Flooding in Twic East, parts of Warrap, Aweil West and East also contributes towards food insecurity as crop production is affected due to excessive rain. The displacement has resulted in affected populations missing the main cropping seasons as some of them cannot access their fields, preventing them from meeting their food and basic household needs. The September 2018 IPC report indicates that an estimated 4.4 million people (43% of the population) are projected to be in Crisis (IPC phase 3) or worse between October and December 2018. In January-March 2019, the number of people in IPC 4 and IPC 5 will increase to an estimated 1,475,000 and 36,000 people respectively. In Jonglei 65.2 % of the population is expected to be in IPC 3, 4 and a concerning 25,000 people are expected to experience catastrophic (IPC 5) food insecurity. In Northern Bahar el Ghazal and Western Bahar el Ghazal 54.6% and 43% of the population are projected to be in IPC phase 3,4 and 5. Findings from the 2018 NRC Gender analysis carried out in Wau and Akobo indicated that as a way to cope, female-headed households engage in negative coping strategies such as reducing number of meals, eating less preferred foods, sending children to beg and selling livelihood assets. Income generating options are scarce and unprofitable due to inflation and most vulnerable households engage in casual jobs, selling firewood/charcoal and petty trade. The chronic pattern of food insecurity in South Sudan has eroded livelihoods, disrupted social safety-nets and negatively affected the resilience of affected populations. This is compounded by already existing factors of low seed and tools access, mono-culture, flooding, dry spells, low area under production, lack of land use planning, weak state institutions and poor governance. It’s expected that with low yields and ongoing economic challenges prices of food commodities will increase, reducing the purchasing capacity of households throughout South Sudan especially in Greater Bahar el Ghazal where households are dependent on markets. The action proposes unconditional cash distribution, to support poor vulnerable households affected by conflict, displacement, and experiencing high levels of food insecurity (IPC 4, 5). In locations where populations are in crisis IPC 3 targeted households will be supported through recovery activities to protect their livelihoods and prevent them from deteriorating to IPC levels 4 and 5.

Activities

Emergency activities will include the following; Unconditional cash and voucher based transfer with food and nutrition objectives. The delivery mechanism will be through a financial service provider (FSP). The FSP conduct direct cash transfer to identified beneficiaries. NRC will be responsible for community sensitization, beneficiary identification and registration. Awareness creation on the use of cash and household gender dynamics will be addressed through consultative focus group discussions before and after distributions. This will be complimented by post distribution monitoring and establishment of a complaints and feedback mechanism. The activity will target recently displaced populations, food insecure households and vulnerable groups during the lean season to cover a percentage of their annual household food gap. Recovery activities will include the following; food production main season agriculture seeds and tools distribution, (fish, vegetable and main crop kits). Training on improved farming practices for crop and vegetable production. Conditional food assistance through cash/food for assets. Asset creation, public works and management of natural resources and engaging local traders. NRC will be responsible for community sensitization, beneficiary identification and registration. NRC will also purchase and distribute the inputs for hard to reach locations where markets are not functional. A high percentage of beneficiaries will be women especially beneficiaries of gardening activities while men will be targeted for fishing activities. Households who are willing and able to participate in asset creation and public works will be targeted. Households who are labour poor will be considered for unconditional cash or they can participate in light works such as tree planting and participation in awareness training. Post distribution monitoring will be conducted complimented by participation country wide FAO led assessments i.e Seed Security Assessment and Post-Harvest Assessments. Resilience activities will include the following; support to seed fairs using local traders, seed security and multiplication strengthening, support to markets. Conditional cash transfers for safety nets and investments in assets and skills training. Support to value chain addition e.g milk, groundnuts and fish, preservation and marketing. Training on farm and off farm activities such as small business management skills, life skills and vocational training, small livestock fairs, seed and input fairs. Diversification of restoration of livelihoods, small grants for petty trade, village saving and loan scheme. NRC will be responsible for community engagement and sensitization with consecutive meetings with local authority to ensure they take ownership of the projects for sustainability. The implementation methodology will be based on group formation for value chain activities and seed multiplication. The project will promote the use of supply markets for agricultural inputs and services. This will be complimented by intensive skills training and knowledge transfer for relevant on and off farm topics. The project target women and will also have a specific focus on youth to reduce their engagement in negative activities. NRC’s main role will be to facilitation a linkage with private actors and beneficiary communities for sustainable transformation. All activities will be implemented through a gender and protection lenses. Housing land and property awareness in relation to women and access to productive assets will be integrated into the interventions. Women groups will receive legal support on formation of associations.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes the project provides beneficiaries with information. NRC will be responsible for consultation, sensitization of local authority and beneficiaries to inform them about the project objectives, expected activities, community participation, NRC's role, local authority role and the role of communities which include beneficiary selection, activity implementation and monitoring.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages are activity specific in relation to the context. Gender assessment were conducted in some of the project sites and findings indicated that men are the main decision makers in the household. The project will have specific messaging for men and women in relation to targeting, beneficiary selection, prioritization of women, gender and protection measures. Awareness creation on prevention of domestic violence sexual exploitation in relation to the various intervention in particular cash based transfers will also be conducted. During the awareness sessions women focus group discussions will be conducted separately from men focus group discussions. Contributions and feedback from women will be Incorporated in to the projects throughout the implementation cycle.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

NRC protection officers recently train all casual laborers, incentive staff, project management committee members in PSEA, and all NRC staff have been previously trained in PSEA and prevention of gender-based violence. Basic do no harm principles and accountability to affected populations will be upheld ensuring all aspects of the project take into consideration the needs of PSNs, the elderly, the sick, people with disabilities, women, youth men and children. Throughout the project, NRC protection officers will receive support and mentoring from the Protection and Gender Adviser.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

The appropriateness of the activities to each of PSN groups will be assessed during the Community Based Project Planning. NRC has experience conducting CBPP and women are represented during this process through women leadership in PMC committees. Moreover, as outlined in the M&E section NRC will set up a complaints and feedback mechanism where complaints and feedback from affected populations will be captured and addressed using various mechanisms. NRC plans to strengthen internal capacities for the collection and analysis of protection data so as to be able to share information on observed trends with greater confidence, as well as to refer and follow-up on individual cases with specific protection actors.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

A simple participatory M&E system and Complaint, Response, Feedback mechanism (CRFM) will be established at community level to collect information on project activities and beneficiaries. CRFM and Monitoring of project activities, outputs and outcomes will be an ongoing process from the start of the project to the end. M&E team will be responsible for setting up a complaint, Response, feedback mechanism, compiling and analyzing data, and producing monthly and quarterly data reports to share back. A set of user-friendly data collection tools will be developed for the routine monitoring activities and CRFM. A simple database will also be developed to CRFM, track activities, output data and capture lower level outcomes.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes the project includes community engagement in monitoring and evaluation. This will be captured at planning stage during the CBPP. Activities will be outlines, priorities will be set and roles and responsibility of all stakeholders will be outlines. This includes the role of the community in M&E for example all incentive staff such as extension agents, nutrition champions, will be identified from the community and they will be responsible for monitoring and recording the day to day activities. this will be complimented by tracking of the overall project outputs and outcome indicators, a comprehensive Output Indicator Tracking Table (OTS) will be used. Digital mobile technology will be used to collect data using Kobo mobile app and uploaded into Kobo database and analyzed using simple excel pivot table sheets. The verified and analyzed data will be uploaded to NRC database. The database will include a dashboard that will allow implementer to see results overtime.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

A gender analysis was conducted in Wau and Akobo however NRC has protection staff operational in the proposed geographical areas. Project staff have also been trained on protection and safe programming and the needs of different population groups are embedded in the project design and implementation for example public works will target community members willing and able to participate while women will be consulted on the works they would like to participate in. Cash transfers will be conducted in a way that ensures do no harm principles are upheld with considerations on transfer amount, cash recipient and location of distribution in relation to distances households travel to receive the cash. Agriculture activities will include an element of HLP awareness especially in relation to women's access and ownership of land. While youth will be targeted for most interventions it will be important to ensure there is no child labour and youth above 18 will be considered to participate in asset creation and public works activities.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes through collaboration with NRC ICLA core competency department who will operating in the same locations conducting group and individual information sessions, community outreach (mobile clinics, monitoring et al), sessions on securing land tenure through legal documentation, advice clients on available options, case management (mediation, accompaniment, representation) and legal assistance (documentation, business registration etc.).

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

In recognising that every individual faces a unique array of protection risks, depending upon their own vulnerabilities, capacities, and external threats, NRC conducted a gender-sensitive needs assessment, consulting more than 100 women, girls, men and boys in targeted areas, including older men and women, female-headed households, and persons with disabilities of both sexes. The assessment identified a range of individualised protection threats and potential inequalities that NRC is considering in programming interventions and approach modalities. An example of one such consideration is that that men must be explicitly targeted and significantly engaged. In all target locations, a majority of respondents reported that men enjoy sole control over assets and decision-making in households At the same time, respondents across gender and location reported high rates of GBV. A failure to proactively engage men will likely result in significant harm to women and girls.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Through CRFM and PDMs beneficiaries can highlight their perception of the project in terms of targeting process whether the most vulnerable have been reached, entitlements, access to assistance and NRC's performance.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

NRC prioritises the safety of beneficiaries in all activities. Thus, all assessment and monitoring tools take questions of community and individual safety as mandatory. Likewise, all M&E tools will ask about improvements in safety generally, and related to the intervention. Beneficiaries will be asked, before, during, and after an intervention what steps would contribute to feelings of safety, and if they felt that NRC was failing to account for safety in meaningful ways. Beyond perceptions of safety, protection monitors will be asked to report on specific instances of rights violations, both related to NRC interventions and those otherwise occurring in targeted communities. NRC will also include an internal incident reporting mechanism for staff to monitor our own observations regarding beneficiary safety.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

As with issues of safety, NRC prioritises the dignity of beneficiaries in all activities. Thus, all assessment and M&E tools take questions of community and individual dignity as mandatory. Beneficiaries will be asked, before, during, and after an intervention what steps would contribute to feelings dignity and self-worth, and if they felt that NRC was undercutting feelings of dignity in meaningful ways.
