5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The proposed project will adhere to the Do No Harm principle. This begins at the beginning of the project cycle, with project design being informed by various analyses conducted by CARE including a recent Conflict-Sensitive Gender Analysis and the needs assessment to be conducted at the beginning of the project to ensure a relevant and appropriate response and identify potential risks. CARE will regularly collect data through our Feedback and Accountability Mechanisms (FAMs), as well as complement them with secondary data, to best examine how our intervention may interact with the conflict and social context. CAREs 4 FAM entry points include Linha Verde, a toll-free hotline that serves as a feedback mechanism for humanitarian assistance recipients, Complaint Boxes, Beneficiary Registration/Post-Distribution Monitoring (PDM) and directly with project focal point during activities. The results will inform any adaptation of activities to prevent negative impacts. Finally, CARE has robust safeguarding policy in place, with accountability holders throughout the organizations, ensuring safety and dignity are prioritized. In the proposed project, CARE plans to conduct a training on PSEA to all staff and key community stakeholders so they can identify, respond to, and prevent protection risks.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

To ensure accountability to affected people and communities, CARE will utilize its established Feedback and Accountability Mechanisms (FAM). CARE will encourage the use of its established FAMs through verbal messaging and IEC material during project activities and especially at WGFSs. The FAMs allows all groups including men, women, boys and girls of different ages, illiteracy abilities and other vulnerabilities to provide feedback at different points during implementation. The 4 FAMs entry points include Linha Verde, a toll-free hotline that serves as a feedback mechanism for humanitarian assistance recipients, Complaint Boxes, Beneficiary Registration/Post-Distribution Monitoring (PDM) and directly with project staff at distribution exit points. CARE will be using all 4 channels noted above but will consider any communicated preference by the target population. The availability of these CFMs ensures at least one accessible option for all. For those who cannot access the hotline or are illiterate, PDM and direct communication with CARE staff provide an appropriate option. CARE is currently (November 2021) undertaking need assessments in Mueda, which includes participants of different ages, sex, and ability; this will inform CARE’s planned interventions in 2022. Their needs and preferences will inform project design and implementation.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

During the needs assessment planned at the beginning of the project, CARE will be able to identify and assess the needs of People with Disabilities (PwDs). This will allow CARE to consult PwDs to determine how assistance can assistance can be delivered in an appropriate and accessible manner. During WGFS activities, CARE will accommodate PwDs to remove barriers to participation. This includes setting up a ‘buddy’ system for PWDs to access and have support in the WGFS and adapting the WGFS to be accessible and safe (e.g., removing tripping hazards, etc.). In the proposed awareness raising, CARE will highlight the specific protection risks PwDs face and how they can be responded to and prevented. Messaging dissemination will come in a variety of formats, to account for different disabilities (radio, use of images, speakerphones, etc.).

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Overall, the project will have a minimal impact on the environment. As this project has a material support element through dignity kits, CARE will make smart procurement decision to reduce the environmental impact of humanitarian operations and keep packaging at a minimum (or have it be recyclable/reusable). In CAREs programming, aid packaging waste has minimal environmental impact as nearly all materials/packaging will be re-used by beneficiaries in some way. For example, at the CARE warehouse, pallets will be reused (all pallets made of wood which upon end of lifespan can be utilized by community members); items such as sacs distributed alongside family kits are used by community members to sell charcoal and other goods. For aid packaging, plastics straps can be used to secure tarpaulin in shelter and plastic bags have been used to transport and plant seedlings and wrap food in, such as cereals and beans. Resourcefulness of the recipients of aid and CAREs own reuse of material/packaging in its own warehouse, ensures resource efficiency. During project activities, CARE will monitor the use of material and packaging, to ensure no environmental damage occurs.
