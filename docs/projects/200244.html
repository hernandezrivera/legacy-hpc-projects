Project Summary

Since October 2019, students in Lebanon had lost valuable learning time due to school closures, whether be it due to Beirut port explosion,COVID restrictions or public-school teachers’ strikes.It is estimated that 1 million children are out of schools(SC,2023).In 2021-22,public schools opened for only 34 days as of March 31st,including days when schools were “only half operational”.As for 2023, public school teachers have been on an open-ended strike since January.Due to the further deterioration of the economic crisis,many children dropped out of education, with some being forced to work due financial pressures.Those who remained in schools reported a decline in learning outcomes.
With insufficient access to inclusive e-learning tools,students were deprived of education for almost 2 academic years.Hence,children have been experiencing poor learning outcomes which carry serious implications for human capital development, economic growth as well as significant protection risks such as increasing possibility of dropping out.Safe learning spaces and schools not only support children to achieve academic learning outcomes but also play a critical role in children’s social,emotional and physical development.
Despite low tuition fees, access to public education in Lebanon includes other financial demands that make education out of reach for many. These include costs for transportation, stationery, clothes and other indirect costs(Lebanon Education Sector,2021).Approximately 45% of school drop-outs are students from difficult social and economic backgrounds (Ibid).With extreme poverty increasing from 10% to over 34%, the number of schools drop-outs will likely rise as families struggle to cover education costs(UNESCWA, 2021).
In response to the current educational crisis,ISWA is proposing to carry out a contingent project to ensure that children in Lebanon can adequately return to school in the upcoming academic year. The remedial catchup summer program aims at enhancing the educational outcomes of 1,200(grades 1-12)children in Saida which was chosen as it has huge poverty hotspots and hosts many refugees- including Palestinian camps. The city's large population exhausts its overstretched public services. The program will be launched at the beginning of May 2022.The program will be composed of 4 cycles, each cycle will be targeting 300 students in different grades.In June, cycle I will be launched targeting grade-12 students,cycle II will be in July for grade 1-3 students,cycle III will be in August for grade 4-6 students and cycle IV will be in September for grade 7-11 students.
The program will be carried out in 2 educational centers in Saida where qualified teachers and PSS facilitators will be implementing the program.The centers have 20 classrooms with 15 students/classroom. Students from each cycle will be identified 1 month prior to the start of the cycle through outreach activities in collaboration with schools. The cycle includes the provision of remedial classes for 2.5 hours/day,4 days/week for 1 month as well as 1 PSS session/week.To ensure that the children are provided with comprehensive services, during the period of their enrollment, they will be provided with age-appropriate stationery kits,cash for education($35 as a lumpsum to cover fees like transportation),and daily refreshments.
Through carrying out this program, children's educational outcomes and wellbeing will be enhanced. Hence,improving their ability to concentrate and motivating them to learn.The selection criteria of the students will take into consideration vulnerability according to well-established criteria.Providing remedial classes to vulnerable children will allow them to bridge their learning gaps and catch up with their peers,decreasing their chances of dropping out.As for the PSS,it'll help the children cope with the struggles they have been facing due to their engagement in negative coping strategies by teaching them skills to face challenges.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

This project is in line with the education sector outcome 1, activities 1.2 and 1.4 in the ERP 2023 log frame. As the aim of the project is to improve the academic preparedness, enhance their resilience and well-being, and reduce dropout amongst vulnerable Lebanese, Palestinian and Syrian students in Saida. This is done by offering remedial support to 1200 students 4times/ week over the period of 1months for each cycle, Hence, decreasing the probability of them dropping out due to academic underperformance. Moreover, the project addresses key hurdles to students’ access to education, namely the increasing transportation and stationery costs which were reported as the two main reasons for not attending school(VASy2, 2022).As for the provision of refreshments, it addresses the grim reality of food insecurity which reflects negatively on students’ academic performance, where each student will receive a daily snack during their attendance of the sessions. Moreover, this project is in line with the 3rd activity 2nd outcome under the education sector “Children and youth participating in PSS, life skills, organizational skills through recreational/extra-curricular activities.”As the students will be provided with PSS sessions, 1 time/week over the course of their 1month enrollment period addressing the increasing rate of violence and aggression among children by teaching them positive coping mechanisms, hence improving their mental health and wellbeing.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

ISWA has its own gender, protection, and safeguarding policies that abide by the core humanitarian standards. Moreover, ISWA staff are always trained on these policies and are entitled of spreading awareness among the beneficiaries which in return will encourage them to later talk about it. A complaint/Feedback mechanism is in place where any complaint, especially those with sensitive content will be tackled immediately.Among ISWA’s child protection policies, that we take to address gender, the inclusion of children with disability or critical medical conditions are the following:- Equal opportunities for accessing our services- Zero tolerance for any kind of bullying and discrimination, whether coming from staff or from students towards each other.- Creating an enabling environment by trying to eliminate the barriers (themcenters are student-friendly)- Awareness and integration of inclusion principles within the policies and implemented activities

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

ISWA adheres to the 9 commitments of the core humanitarian standards, ensures beneficiaries’ access to information, and allows their involvement in decisions that affect them. To ensure APP, ISWA will-Establish 2 channels for complaint/feedback (one is anonymous). Along with a complaint box, a hotline is established for collecting the caregivers' feedback throughout the project-Organize focus groups with the students to collect their feedback.-Hold staff surveys for educators to elicit their feedback on the program.-Organize teacher-parent conference meetings to inquire regarding their feedback on the project and following their children’s progress.-Outputs of feedback will be taken into consideration to identify areas of improvement- Ensuring beneficiaries access to information by providing them with adequate information regarding the program's outcomes-Monitor attendance so that any issues related to the drop in attendance are addressed in a timely manner
