Project Summary

The project titled "Strengthening Sexual and Reproductive Health Services in Conflict-Affected Communities in Borno and Adamawa States" aims to improve access to essential sexual and reproductive health (SRH) services for vulnerable populations, particularly women, adolescent girls, and young people, in communities severely impacted by the ongoing conflict in Northeast Nigeria. The region's healthcare infrastructure has been significantly weakened by years of insurgency, leading to limited availability of SRH services, including maternal health care, family planning, prevention and management of sexually transmitted infections (STIs), and gender-based violence (GBV) response.
The project will focus on strengthening existing health systems by providing capacity-building for healthcare workers, improving the availability of essential SRH commodities, and enhancing community awareness and demand for SRH services. Through strategic partnerships with local health authorities, community-based organizations, and women’s groups, the project will also establish safe spaces for women and girls, where they can access information, counseling, and care in a confidential and supportive environment.
Key activities will include: Capacity-building of healthcare providers on SRH service delivery in humanitarian settings, with emphasis on trauma-informed care, adolescent-friendly services, and GBV case management, Provision of essential SRH supplies, including contraceptives, maternal health kits, and post-rape care kits, to conflict-affected communities, Community outreach and sensitization campaigns to increase awareness of the availability and importance of SRH services, Establishment of referral pathways for survivors of sexual violence to access timely medical and psychosocial support, Strengthening partnerships with local authorities and humanitarian actors to ensure coordination and sustainability of SRH services.
The expected outcomes of this project include increased access to SRH services, reduced maternal and neonatal mortality, enhanced protection and care for GBV survivors, and improved health-seeking behaviors among conflict-affected populations in Borno and Adamawa States. By addressing these critical needs, the project will contribute to restoring dignity and improving the overall health and well-being of vulnerable women and girls in these conflict zones.

1 - Needs

A deteriorating health infrastructure, limited access to health services in remote areas and the lack of resources and skilled personnel continue to pose challenges to affected people. Volatile security conditions, repeated displacements and socio-economic challenges will continue to erode access to health care for already struggling populations, such as IDPs, returnees and refugees. The health sector has identified over 5 million people in need, with 19 LGAs in the BAY states having the highest needs. Four of these LGAs are in Adamawa, four in Yobe and 11 in Borno. MMC and Jere have the highest number of people in need, over 600,000. HNO 2024Women and girls are the most affected, with more than a million children and over 600,000 adolescents identified. Women constitute 13 per cent of the population in need (almost 700,000) across the BAY states. Around 960,000 older persons were also identified as requiring assistanceAccording to HNO 2024, more than 5.3 million people across the BAY states were identified as requiring health assistance. The affected people are primarily concentrated in rural and hard-to-reach, security-compromised areas. Around 1.5 million returnees are in areas of relocation in the three states, and about 1.8 million IDPs are living in camps and host communities.Due to the destruction of health facilities, the displacement of healthcare workers, and insecurity in conflict-affected areas, access to critical SRH services is extremely limited. Many women and girls are unable to access basic maternal health services, antenatal care, and family planning, increasing the risk of maternal mortality, unsafe abortions, and unintended pregnancies. There is a need to strengthen the availability and accessibility of these services. The conflict has disrupted the supply chains for SRH commodities, leading to shortages of essential supplies such as contraceptives, maternal health kits, STI treatment drugs, and post-rape care kits. Without these supplies, health facilities are unable to meet the needs of the community, and women and girls are left vulnerable to preventable health risks. Many healthcare providers in the region lack the skills and training needed to deliver quality SRH services in emergency settings. There is a need for capacity-building to ensure that healthcare workers can offer trauma-informed care, manage GBV cases, and provide adolescent-friendly services. This includes training on the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) for reproductive health in crises, which is essential for addressing SRH needs in humanitarian contexts.

2 - Activities

Activity 1.1: Conduct training workshops for 200 healthcare providers on delivering sexual and reproductive health services in emergency settings.Target Beneficiaries: 200 healthcare providers (doctors, nurses, midwives, and community health workers)Sub-activities:• Training on the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) for reproductive health in emergencies.• Trauma-informed care for women and girls, including managing cases of sexual violence and psychological support.• Adolescent-friendly SRH services, including counseling and contraceptive methods for young people.• Management of GBV cases, including post-rape care and referrals.• Ensuring the integration of GBV response into SRH services.Activity 1.2: Distribute 5,000 essential SRH commodities and supplies to healthcare facilities in conflict-affected areas.Target Beneficiaries: 5,000 individuals (including women and girls of reproductive age, youth, and survivors of GBV)Sub-activities:• Provide contraceptives, maternal health kits, and STI treatment drugs.• Supply post-rape care kits, including emergency contraception, antibiotics, and HIV prophylaxis.• Distribute menstrual hygiene products for adolescent girls and women.• Support mobile clinics with necessary SRH supplies to reach remote and inaccessible areas.Activity 1.3: Set up 10 safe spaces in targeted communities for women and girls to access SRH services, information, and psychosocial support.Target Beneficiaries: 2,000 women and girls (approximately 200 beneficiaries per safe space)Sub-activities:• Provide confidential spaces where women and girls can receive individual counseling, peer support groups, and GBV services.• Organize community-based workshops on SRH, GBV prevention, and rights awareness.• Ensure these spaces are equipped with supplies for menstrual hygiene management and basic healthcare services.Activity 1.4: Implement 20 community-based outreach programs to raise awareness of available SRH services and promote health-seeking behaviors.Target Beneficiaries: 10,000 community members (including men, women, and youth)Sub-activities:• Conduct sensitization campaigns using radio programs, community meetings, and posters.• Organize male involvement sessions to challenge social norms around SRH and GBV.• Provide information on GBV prevention and how to access services for survivors.Activity 1.5: Strengthen referral systems to ensure that survivors of GBV receive timely and comprehensive medical, legal, and psychosocial support.Target Beneficiaries: 500 GBV survivors (victims of sexual violence and gender-based violence)Sub-activities:• Develop referral pathways linking healthcare facilities, GBV service providers, legal aid services, and psychosocial support programs.• Train healthcare providers on referrals and managing cases of sexual violence.• Establish mobile referral systems for women and girls in remote or hard-to-reach areas.Activity 1.6: Develop and implement SRH programs tailored to adolescents and young people, with a focus on menstrual hygiene, family planning, and GBV prevention.Target Beneficiaries: 5,000 adolescents and young people (ages 10-24)Sub-activities:• Provide adolescent-friendly services, including confidential counseling on reproductive health, menstrual hygiene, and family planning.• Set up peer education programs in schools and community centers.• Distribute youth-friendly SRH information materials (posters, leaflets, booklets) and host information sessions in schools and community spaces.Activity 1.7: Establish a robust MEL system to track the project’s progress and measure its impact on SRH outcomes.Sub-activities:• Develop and implement a monitoring framework that tracks the delivery of SRH services.• Conduct periodic evaluations to assess the effectiveness of training programs and availability of SRH supplies.• Gather feedback from beneficiaries (especially women and girls) to improve service delivery
