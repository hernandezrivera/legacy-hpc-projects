Project Summary

Women, men and children affected by disaster and conflict will have access to appropriate and acceptable WASH services that enable them to mitigate public health risks in a dignified way. Vulnerable and disadvantaged groups will be actively identified; the response will meet their specific WASH needs and will give them a greater voice in how the response is designed and delivered. Oxfam will continue to be a leading player in humanitarian WASH, demonstrating rapid and high quality emergency response, influencing the WASH sector with evidence of our effectiveness, supporting community resilience in disaster prone and fragile contexts and enhancing national capacity to improve WASH coverage for the most vulnerable. Oxfam’s impact and profile in WASH will be increased through good quality programming in large scale emergencies, working with international and national NGO partners and institutions, state actors and the private sector. Direct implementation of WASH responses will continue where this is considered critical to achieve positive outcomes for communities affected by emergencies.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The goal of the project is to improve access to food, WASH infrastructure and deliver gender sensitive protection services for targeted communities. With this project, Oxfam and its partners aim to reach a total of 68,500 people (women, men, boys, and girls) affected by conflict and climate changes in Mozambique. Oxfam will directly manage and implement this multisectoral project in partnership with local organizations. A key component will be safe programming, which will also raise awareness on gender, protection amongst communities affected by conflict, food and nutrition insecurity in Mozambique. WASH Hygiene Promotion: Support to girls and women, PSN andIDPs and hosting communities in hygiene NFI in the resettlementand or hosting areas by (i) distribution of women’s dignity andhygiene kits, and hygiene promotion as preventive measuresagainst diarrheal diseases, (ii) access to water inquantity and quality for families, access to appropriatesanitation and hygiene facilities, including management of solidwaste and (iii) safe programing.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Water quality monitoring.- Distribution of point-of-use water treatment materials / products, including training and monitoring on proper use at household level.- Rehabilitation and disinfection of existing water points in host communities, accommodation centers, and resettlement areas, including support for water committees.- Construction of new water sources for host communities and resettlement areas- Expansion of piped networks to affected communities, resettlement sites, and hosting communities.- Installation and management of temporary sanitation and bathing facilities in accommodation centres/transit camps and/or resettlement sites (first phase)- Solid waste management (including collection, removal, disposal and drainage) in accommodation centres / transit camps or resettlement sites.- Promotion activities for self-construction of household latrines and garbage pits in resettlement sites and hosting communities (including the provision of slabs)- Dissemination of WASH related messages. - Procurement and/or distribution of WASH related Non-Food items, including menstrual hygiene health materials.- PSEA trainings and feedback to communities.- Gender based violence prevention safety audit for WASH planning- Market based WASH service provision. Note: all those activities will be implemented in Meconta District (Nampula province), Ancuabe, Metuge, Mecúfi, Montepuez, Mocímboa da Praia (Cabo Delgado Province).

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

• Oxfam and its partner will implement a Safe Programming approach based on conflict sensitivity and protection risk analyses that include capturing gender and power relations and ensures community participation and inclusion. Oxfam and its partner are guided by IASC GBV and PSEA Guidelines and Oxfam's Minimum Standards for Gender which defines our strategies to (i) promote equality; (ii) conduct gender analysis that informs the project; (iii) promote dignity, participation and empowerment; (iv) work on GBV; (v) prevent sexual exploitation, abuse, and harassment (PSEAH).• We recognize that there are unequal power dynamics across the organization, our partnership relationships, and in relation to those we serve, and that we face risk of some people exploiting their position of power for personal gain. • Considering this we commit to minimizing any negative effects that may arise as a result of any of our interventions by implementing and monitoring our zero-tolerance policy towards sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse and then core standards on safeguarding throughout the project cycle.• The project will ensure that all personnel will undergo induction on PSEA, CP and Code of Conduct prior to engage in activities.• The project will have a PSEA and CP focal person.• During the meetings communities will be informed about expected staff behavior and how to report SEA and CP concerns.• Regular Protection and PSEA risk assessments will be conduct for all activities.• Ensuring that all Oxfam and partner’s staff, contractors, volunteers and related personnel in the project go through Safeguarding training/ induction, and all sign the code of conduct.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Gender analysis and inclusive consultation with all Community members informs the design, implementation and measurement of impact. Implementation will be by trained, gender balanced teams. Beneficiary targeting will be based on gender sensitive vulnerability criteria with payments made to female heads of household as key decision makers in management of household WASH. Monitoring/evaluation will assess qualitative and quantitative sex-disaggregated information.The project will target the most vulnerable population group living in hosting communities, accommodation/resettlement centers, based on selection criteria for different project activities and local context. It is important to emphasize that the selection of beneficiaries will be done in coordination with government institutions and stakeholders, in addition to involving local community authorities and in line with the standards of the different clusters. The beneficiary selection process will prioritize people with disabilities, the chronically ill, lactating and pregnant women, households headed by women and/or children and the elderly, all with limited ability to acquire livelihoods confirmed by the members of the community where they live.Men, women, boys and girls will be involved in the project activities in different focus groups, with a greater focus on women and girls who will be our greatest attention to meet their priority needs within the project. The project will also conduct auscultation sessions with girls and women aimed at understanding their priorities and basic needs. Identify the most vulnerable groups and include them in them as active members of the hygiene promotion teams as well as in the decision centers. The construction of public emergency sanitations will consider the specific needs and conditions of the disability groups.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Oxfam and its partners will conduct and inclusion analysis during the first months of implementation to inform the appropriate interventions for people with disability, and other forms of inclusive approaches. In addition, attention will be paid to representativeness of actors participating in these processes and activities, to ensure inclusion of disability persons organizations/associations. Consultations will be done individuals with individuals living with disabilities to identify specific adaptation needs for infrastructures. Infrastructures will be adapted for these needs.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Sanitation and waste disposals management: the construction of public emergency sanitation, promotion of hygiene practices while dealing with waste disposals will help, not only the health of people, but also to protect the environment around the accommodation centers. We will promote creation of waste disposals collection points within the resettlement area.
