Project Summary

This project aims at strengthening OCHA's role in the leadership and coordination of humanitarian action in South Sudan based on a people-centered approach. Specifically, the project will bolster OCHA's coordination role (at national and sub-national levels), information management, needs assessment and analysis, leadership on access and cash coordination, humanitarian financing and advocacy. The direct target of this project is the humanitarian community in South Sudan who benefits from OCHA's services while the indirect target is affected people whose needs have to be addressed in a principled and effective manner.

1. Needs

Conflict and insecurity are key drivers of humanitarian needs in South Sudan leading to widespread violence, displacement, and constrained access to vital services. South Sudan grapples with a huge displacement crisis with over two million internally displaced persons (IDPs) and nearly two million refugees hosted in neighboring countries. With the onset of the Sudan crisis in April 2023 and a combination of violence and the cessation of food distribution in parts of Ethiopia, thousands of people returned to South Sudan, many of whom are highly vulnerable and arriving in critically underdeveloped areas. Food insecurity situation has deteriorated due to multiple shocks, including flooding, ongoing conflict, displacement, and a high cost of living. The Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) of October 2023 projects over 7.1 million out of the total population of 12.4 million to face crisis-level or more severe acute food insecurity during the lean season from April to July 2024. Malnutrition rates remain high among children and women in South Sudan with the latest estimates of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels above the World Health Organization (WHO) emergency threshold of 15 percent in 46 out of the 79 counties. Contributing factors to this include high food insecurity, poor water and hygiene conditions, high child morbidity rates, GBV concerns, and poor child feeding and caring practices. Eighteen counties experience extreme deprivation, further compounding the nutrition crisis. In addition, South Sudan faces severe climate vulnerabilities, ranking as the second most vulnerable country globally to natural hazards (floods and drought) according to the 2023 INFORM Risk Index. Access to basic services is very limited and Government investment in key sectors providing basic services remains dismal.

2. Project activities

This project aims at:-strengthening strategic, operational and inclusive coordination at national, state and field levels to enable an effective response to people with humanitarian needs, including through improved accountability to affected people (AAP), localization of the response, protection and gender needs.-Enabling coordinated and strategic planning through an enhanced Humanitarian Programme Cycle aligned with global policy guidance, informing critical decisions on prioritization of the response.-Supporting annual multi-sectoral needs analysis, coordination and facilitating rapid multi-sector assessments to inform situational awareness for evidence-based response.-Providing timely communication, information management, informed analysis and information products to communicate people’s humanitarian needs and gaps in the response.-Enabling humanitarian access through guidance, advocacy, dialogue, civil-military coordination and monitoring informed by access incidents data and best practices.-Ensuring a smooth and timely transition to the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Cash Coordination Model in South Sudan and maintain an accountable, efficient and effective Cash Working Group (CWG): Advancing collective action and innovation in sourcing and delivering catalytic financing for humanitarian action that is sufficient, timely and coordinated to meet the needs of crisis-affected people:-Promoting respect for International Humanitarian Law (IHL), by ensuring the protection of civilians. OCHA will continue to ensure that all parties are constantly reminded of their IHL responsibilities.
