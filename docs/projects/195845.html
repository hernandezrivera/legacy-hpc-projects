Project Summary

In fragile South Sudan, protection issues such as sexual violence has increasingly become normalized on both the local level and national level. Further, harmful gender norms and traditions limit the meaningful participation of women which further compromises the effectiveness of peace building processes among communities. Therefore, the project is aimed at addressing the challenges of High rate of general protection issues by promoting awareness session on human rights violations, gender equality, economic empowerment and participation in decision making process. The project also has the component of local peace building social cohesion, root cause analysis of inter and intra community conflict, child protection including general protection, early warning and response mechanism about conflict and disaster.

1 - Needs

South Sudan continues to face one of the worst protection concerns since the outbreak of civil war, intercommunal conflict, disaster, sexual exploitations and abuse has been widespread and was widely used as a weapon of war. While the precise number of incidents remains unknown, it is clear that sexual violence, physical abuse has increasingly become normalized at all levels. In addition, harmful gender norms and traditions limit the meaningful participation of women and children which further compromises the effectiveness of peacebuilding and establishment of social cohesion processes among communities. It was noted that men often lure women and girls from returnee, IDP, and host communities into sexual exploitation through grooming in exchange for food/money. A gender analysis conducted by Islamic Relief in 2022, revealed that there are still high incidences of child abuse, human right violations, displacements, insecurity, and general violations of human rights in Kajokeji, Kapoeta East, Kapoeta North, Yei, and Tonj North. According to Protection Cluster HNO these counties have been rated as severity level 4. This requires a bottom-up approach to child and general protection responses and risk mitigation for the affected population. For example, insecurity, peace, gender inequality, and child abuse are the key protection issues faced by women, adolescent girls and children in the targeted counties. Displacement reinforces pre-existing discrimination and socioeconomic disadvantages. The effects of displacement depend on its duration, but immediate manifestations include family separations, exposure to gender violence, trauma associated with the deaths of family members, impaired health, and the loss of the home and possessions. The project aims to improve and promote community response and prevention of protection risks related issues such as child abuse, and human rights violations. This project will be strengthening both returnees/IDPs, and host communities through participation in outreach, advocacy, and peace dialogue organized and facilitated at grassroots level to conflict-affected communities. The project will also establish and building the capacity of Community Based Protection Committees on gender equality, and rights of children and people with disabilities in the target areas. Through the interventions, the project will address the challenges of high rate of human right violations, peace building initiative, women economic empowerment, participation in decision making process, and cultural practices that are harmful to women/girls in the society. The project will be implemented following a Channel of Hope Methodology (CoH) and will address the following:(i) Strengthen local capacities of chiefs, community leaders and local authority on gender equality, conflict prevention, mediation skills, and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR); (ii) Support communities’ efforts in peacebuilding, protection, conflict transformation, and awareness raising.(iii) Create increased awareness on child and general protection to ensure improved knowledge and access to services for refugees, internally displaced persons and host communities.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will reach the persons with disabilities, illiterate women and girls through: (i) Using pictorial media and communication materials for the illiterate individuals; (ii) Developing inclusive communication materials using local languages; (iii) Consultation with the beneficiaries; (iv) Female health workers will be in place for collecting feedback of the women and girls particularly on maternal and reproductive health issues; (v) Protection and nutrition workers will be in place for collecting feedback from affected population; (vi) Following the principles of privacy and confidentiality during disclosure.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The Islamic Relief South Sudan will follow its Complaint Response mechanism to ensure collection of beneficiaries feedback systematically. Complaint/Suggestion boxes will be installed at the implementation sites. The proposed action will include inception meeting, monthly and quarterly review meeting with stakeholders, including target population and marginalized groups. The field team will ensure that persons with limited access or illiteracy equally take part proactively in monitoring, evaluation, and learning mechanism.As part of accountability and transparency, various project learning and accountability forums will be organized with community members where they will be given time to share and provide feedback, which will be one of the key forums for lessons learnt. The process will be facilitated by a MEAL coordinator and Head of Programmes.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

IRSS is committed to promote gender equality and women’s empowerment across all operations, programs and activities, and it will seek to enhance meaningful access to, participation of, women, men, girls, and boys throughout the project. The IRSS will follow its own policy for protection against sexual exploitation, safeguarding and Child Protection. The CHS and international standards will be followed during implementation of the proposed action. As described in the proposal, gender mainstreaming as well as targeted action has been taken into account in all sector responses. For instance, in EFSL women’s participation will be ensured in various community resourcepositions (such as CAEWs) and gender equality awareness will be conducted on household’s decision making on food security related aspects. In WASH, women and adolescent girls are represented in all WASH related functions and the specific WASH needs of women and girls, such as MHM kits, have been ensured. Under Health/Nutrition, SRHR, malnutrition and mother-to-mother groups are giving preference to the specific needs of women and girls, and inProtection, CP, and general protection, prevention, response and awareness are prioritized. Before implementation of any activity, consultation will be made with all target groups to remove any barrier to equal participation (such as issues of location, time, schedule), and to bring forth gender related needs, capacities and aspirations ofwomen, men, girls and boys separately.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The Project will follow the Complaint Response mechanism of the IRSS as mentioned earlier at 7.4.1. Once receive the feedback from the beneficiaries the focal person of the CRM will go through on the suggestions and complaints made by the beneficiaries and will be addressed in consultation with the beneficiaries. The Citizen led monitoring and follow-up mechanism will also be in place to ensure optimum use of beneficiaries' feedback during implementation of the project as well as future design of the similar action.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The project will have a risk mitigation plan with several alternatives to mitigate risks and implement timely. The following measures will be taken to mitigate the risks: (i) Regular follow-up of the security information with the UN and INSO, NGO Forum, UN OCHA regularly; (ii) Follow-up with the Security Focal Point Persons of the Islamic Relief South Sudan Juba office and Field Offices. (iv) Revision of Risk Mitigation Plan regularly in consultation with the UN OCHA and UN Clusters. IRSS has a risk management policy in place. For this project, IRSS considers the risk of political insecurity and general insecurity (particularly due to theon-going war) to be the largest risk factor. Major roads for transporting project materials, the supply of which, if disrupted, could negatively impact project implementation. These conflicts could also badly affect local and national markets. While structural solutions to these problems are largely beyond the scope of the project, IRSS has put in place a system that will monitor the situation if these risks unfold and will put in place mitigation measures to address them. IRSS is a fully developed country office with appropriate, effective, and efficient systems. The country office has written accounting policies and procedures. The country finance manager implements these procedures with oversight being provided by IR East Africa RegionalFinance Manager. IRSS uses IRW’s policy, records and documents keeping procedures; all records are safely kept under key and lock within the IRSS office. Finalized documents are scanned and stored electronically in the IRSS document archival system. IRSS Finance, Programmes and Country Management Teams operate independently to ensure that they are providing controls and oversight to each other but at the same ensuring that they are working for the common goal according to the Islamic Relief values. Additionally, Islamic Relief East Africa office provides strong managerial and technical oversight to ensure quality programme delivery and management. IRSS is licensed to work in the proposed project location and has an office in project locations.
