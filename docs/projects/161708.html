Project Summary

INTERSOS will implement the full CCCM component on behalf of UNHCR. The beneficiaries of this project will be both the old case load and new arrival IDPs, returnees and host communities, in application of an approach fully compliant with the international humanitarian community strategy aligned to the Government’s Economic and Recovery and Growth Plan (2017-2020) and the Nigerian Humanitarian Response Strategy for 2019-2021. INTERSOS intends to integrate a community participation approach for all beneficiaries, to acquire more community ownership and empowerment in the project using local materials and personnel.
). This intervention aims to cover about 297,693 IDPs (68,949 IDP Households), who are spread out in Borno state LGAs of Banki, Ngala, and Monguno. This CCCM project aims to bridge the gap between the people of concern and service providers to ensure quality, dignified and equal access to humanitarian assistance as well as protection within the camp and camp like settings. It will also coordinate the several humanitarian partners within the camps in collaboration with Borno State Emergency Management Agency (SEMA) officials and local authorities to harmonize service delivery and avoid duplication of services by operating partners. As CCCM partners, the project will further carry out site facilitation, camp management and its structures, reception centers as well as provide a channel for complains mechanism where the beneficiaries also receive feedback to their queries.

1 - Needs

The alarming rate at which new arrivals have accessed the safer LGAs and camps/camp-like settings since 2015, has significantly overwhelmed the humanitarian capacity to meet the IDPs basic needs. This result is congestion of camps/sites and a pile of unmet needs registered by most settlement sites, as well as the stretching to breaking point of the national authorities’ capacity to facilitate the CCCM component. As such, duplication of services, insufficient advocacy for services, lack of proper coordination among implementing and operating partners to provide appropriate and timely assistance, and lack of quality information to help in joint planning of beneficiaries’ services was registered. Additionally to this, it has been detected a poor site planning and high congestion of beneficiaries in the camp sites, untimely response to natural hazard occurrences such as fires, flooding and storms that destroy camp residents property, unmaintained, abandoned, damaged or dilapidated camp structures and infrastructures that need urgent decommissioning (for example filled up latrines, blocked water drainage system or the lack of drainage channels). In addition, The reception center strategy which is used to receive new arrivals, registration and monitoring, needs continuity to carry on its duties, but its faced with lack of a proper “exit strategy” to find and provide shelter for the new arrival, therefore ending up becoming a very big congested shelter in some of the locations with high numbers of new arrivals, such as was the case in Banki, Monguno, and Ngala in 2018, for a specific period of time. According to this situation, INTERSOS intends to build on its existing strong foundation of CCCM project implementation and presence in Banki, Ngala and Monguno (Borno state) to bridge the gaps in coordination, collecting information on needs, supporting service delivery of stakeholders, monitor the movement of the populations coming in and leaving the camps as well as support the Reception Centre Strategy in all its areas of implementation mentioned above. INTERSOS’ main objective in this project is to refine and improve camp management, coordination, strengthen partnerships and community mobilisation and facilitate the provision and distribution of shelter materials for construction and maintenance as well as facilitating the provision of sufficient basic and domestic items for core relief, according to UNHCR standard operating procedures.
