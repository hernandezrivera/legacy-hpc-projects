Project Summary

Given the scale of needs in Puntland, the effective ability of NGOs to reach the community level, and the lack of alternative providers, it is expected that a significant amount of relief and development resources will be channelled through LNGOs in the years ahead. The role for LNGOs in service delivery and involvement in advocacy and public accountability is likely to grow. A number of key requirements must be met in order for LNGOs to fulfill, their expected role in Puntland in the years ahead: Coordination: To reach their maximum collective potential, the efforts of this increasingly diverse range of LNGOs must be effectively coordinated. Coordinated action is recognised in the Humanitarian Reform agenda as vital to ensure effective humanitarian response. Development actors however consider building inclusive partnerships as necessary to achieve desired results. While programmatic coordination needs for humanitarian efforts are generally filled by the UN through OCHA and the Cluster System, there is a notable lack of coordination for development initiatives. LNGOs as distinctively separate organisations must be able to collectively and autonomously voice concerns over issues of general interest and be represented to stakeholders. Security: NGOs must be able to safely operate and have access to diverse areas of intervention in order to address targeted needs. Although duty of care for staff lies with individual agencies, and ultimate responsibility for the safety of aid workers rests with the host government, inter-agency initiatives (such as information sharing, joint analysis, access to training and common advocacy) provide aid improved security for all. Operating Environment: Broader operating environment issues can often hinder the effectiveness of LNGO operations. Development interventions are most useful when they take into account the views and needs of the communities they are seeking to assist. LNGOs, given their connections to the grass-roots level, can play a vital role in this, informing and shaping the approaches taken by other development actors. Effective funding mechanisms are also a pre-requisite for successful development. Lessons can be learned from the pooled funding mechanisms trialled in Somalia; many failed to take into account the needs and constraints of implementing NGOs, and thus failed to achieve intended results. NGOs must therefore feed into the development and use of future funding mechanisms in Somalia. Government also has an important role to play in coordinating and facilitating the work of NGOs. Any legislation framework for NGOs must facilitate legitimate humanitarian and development activities in an un-obstructive way. LNGO Capacity Building: The LNGO sector in Puntland is rapidly evolving. New LNGOs are forming, and agencies seek expanded roles in service delivery, civic education, media and civil justice. As Somalia develops, the need for a strong local non-governmental sector is vital to ensure accountability, national identity, and to lay foundations for long term humanitarian work and development. Capacity building is therefore required to adequately support LNGO’s fulfil their expected roles and meet the fiduciary requirements of international community.
PUNSAA will also work with women led organizations (WLO) that are around 35% of its network and Persons with disabilities organization to ensure inclusion. PUNSAA will conduct a vulnerability of gender-differentiated groups disaggregated data according to age and sex which will necessitate to assess the specific situation or needs of men and women of different age groups and will make an effort to obtain information specific to men, women, girls and boys through its member networks to minimize the possibility of overlooking specific needs related to age and sex to set a baseline to track changes in living conditions induced by the project activities.
