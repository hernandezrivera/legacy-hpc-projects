Project Summary

IMC will continue implementing its integrated health, nutrition and WaSH interventions in
ve states, West, Central and South Darfur, South Kordofan and the Blue Nile covering
around 1,250,645 people. The proposed intervention will address gaps in the provision of
primary health care and nutrition services. Under the health intervention, International
Medical Corps will be providing basic primary and nutrition services, including outpatient
consultations, reproductive health services (antenatal care, delivery service, post-natal
care, and family planning), and immunization through 6 health care facilities across the
targeted 5 states. In addition to facility care, community-based health interventions
including; health and hygiene education, referral of cases, and campaigns will also be
provided. In collaboration with SMOH, WHO, and HAC, IMC will respond to seasonal
outbreaks such as AWD, measles, and malaria in all targeted areas. Health facilities will be
prepared with necessary human resources and equipment (including laboratory
equipment and materials) to enable them to respond to outbreaks and related
emergencies. IMC will be implementing community-based interventions such as the
promotion of child and maternal health, health education, community mobilization for EPI,
mass MUAC screening and other activities through local partners.Under the nutrition
intervention, IMC will support SCs, OTPS, and TSFPs, IMC (in conjunction with the Ministry
of Health) will provide nutrition services to the most vulnerable groups- children under 5
and PLWs- among the IDPs, returnees and host population and refugees in the targeted
localities. The active and passive screening will be done to determine the children that
should be enrolled for treatment. Treatment will be providedbased on the national
guidelines for the management of acute malnutrition

(MAM). Children 6-59 months found to have SAM without complications will be treated in
the OTPs and those with medical complications or children below 6 months will be
transferred to SC where they will receive medical and therapeutic nutrition.
To promote optimal IYCF practices, including exclusive breastfeeding, timely
complementary feeding, and dietary diversity, individual counseling and group sessions
for caretakers of children 0 to 23 months will be provided. Nutrition, health and hygiene
education will also be provided for caregivers of all children admitted in the CMAM
program, women attending antenatal and post-natal services, and when immunization
services are provided. Key messages will be on recommended feeding and child care
practices, improving dietary diversity using locally available foods, and improved water,
sanitation, and hygiene practices. Community outreach activities will result in improved
timely access to treatment, program coverage, and community engagement and
contribute to the prevention of malnutrition.The health facilities will be made fully
functional by the rehabilitation of the structure, provision of drugs, nutritional supplies,
medical equipment and capacity building of State Ministry of Health (SMoH) staff on the
provision of nutrition-specic and health care services. The aim is to facilitate access to
quality health and nutrition services for all, with a focus on the most vulnerable groups.

1 - Needs

The health systems in Darfur, South Kordofan, and the Blue Nile remain weak andinadequately prepared to provide basic life-saving health services and responds tooutbreaks. In Central Darfur, it is reported that 26% of the health facilities are notfunctional. Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BEmONC) coverage in healthfacilities is only 30% in Central Darfur. Only 40% of Clinics in Central Darfur States provideIntegrated Management of Childhood Illness (IMCI) Services. The coverage of measlesvaccination is only 55.9% while only 38.3% of children are fully vaccinated. Only 54% ofthe clinical OPD cases are managed according to the protocol despite training andsupportive supervisions, which signals the need for strengthening support and follow upto ensure adherence to protocols. Detection and referral of pregnant mothers is weakbecause of low coverage of community outreach activities. The clinics are not yet fullystaffed and still lack the necessary equipment and physical structure to providecomprehensive health services. Water supply, medical waste management and infectioncontrol practices are identied as a gap in the clinics. The community outreach activitiesneed expansion to more villages. Staff need to be trained as they are relatively new to theareas for intervention. Dispensaries do not have enough shelves and storage capacity.Health workers in targeted clinics need training on IMCI which is an important skill forbetter treatment of childhood diseases. According to the Ministry of Health, in Sudan,some 2.2 million children suffer from malnutrition annually (Global Acute Malnutrition)out of which over 573,000 suffer from Severe Acute Malnutrition.. South Kordofan andBlue Nile States have been affected by persistent conict between government forces andrebel groups since 2011. Two million people are affected by the conict since then andmore than 250,000 refugees ed to Ethiopia and South Sudan. The United Nationsestimates that armed movements in South Kordofan and Blue Nile resulted in additionaldisplacement of 545,000 people in areas under their control. The conict has disruptedtraditional livelihood activities and eroded community resilience to withstand shocksthough the areas were known to have good agricultural productions before the conict.Among displaced people, women and children are the most vulnerable and at the greatestrisk of being exposed to violence. Healthcare facilities were destroyed in both states as aresult of the ghting. Lack of basic services and food scarcity perpetuates displacementand migration of people out of the country, mainly to the camps in South Sudan. Thehumanitarian context in South Kordofan is further complicated by arrival of SouthSudanese refugees in the state increasing the humanitarian need and straining thealready compromised basic services infrastructure, including health services. There arenew arrivals of around 5000 refugees in South Kordofan in December 2019.According toS3M II 2018 survey result, seven out of the 18 states have malnutrition prevalence rates ofabove the critical threshold as per the WHO standards (15%). Nationally, GAM prevalenceis at 14.1%, whereas some states have higher GAM prevalence rates than the nationalsuch as GAM prevalence rates of acute malnutrition in U5 children in South and CentralDarfur States have remained above the emergency threshold (15%), which is recorded at16.43% and 15.28%, respectively, High rates of acute malnutrition is recoded in SouthKordofan (8.13%) and the magnitude of aggravating factors such as poor feedingpractices (low rate of exclusive breastfeeding (61.12%), continued breastfeeding (77.04),dietary diversity (23.58)) (S3M II 2018, Similarly, prevalence of stunting in South Darfur,South Kordofan and Central Darfur States is as high as 23.85%, 36.80% and 43.67%,respectively.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Health: Deliver minimum basic package of primary health care services (includingmaternal and child health) and support referral to secondary health care. Support andconduct routine or acceleration interventions for immunization. Strengthening EarlyWarning, Early detection and reporting of AWD(EWARS). Procurement, storage, anddistribution of drugs and medical supplies. Conduct health education training for healthstaff. Conduct awareness/orientation sessions at the health facility for the community.Nutrition: Provision of CMAM services for SAM and MAM cases of U5 children and PLW(incl. refurbishment of OTP/TSFP, provision of RUTF/SUTF, MUAC screening, referralservice for SAM with complication case etc.). Conduct training for nutrition workers,community volunteer on CMAM, IYCF etc. Conduct community awareness campaigns onCMAM, IYCF etc. Establish a mother support group for the promotion of IYCF. Through atailored approach to service delivery based on the needs of the different vulnerablegroups, the proposed action specically attempts to address disparities and differences inaccess to health, nutrition services among vulnerable populations, including women,children and the elderly. Children and women's health and nutritional needs are a specicfocus of this Action and women, in particular, are solicited as catalysts for behaviorchange in the communities through their participation in various community committees.IMC's ongoing collection and analysis of health and nutrition data are disaggregated byage and sex. IMC will continue to ensure the impartiality of program services whileenabling the identication of individuals and families with specic vulnerabilities relatedto age, sex, disability, family size, health status, socioeconomic status, or other factors.International Medical Corps will continue to work with federal and state-level MOH andactively participate in federal and state-level relevant sector clusters (Health, Nutrition)meetings. WaSH: Provision of safe and sufficient amount of water, medical wastemanagement, provision of sanitation facilities and hygiene promotion in the healthfacilities and catchment population
