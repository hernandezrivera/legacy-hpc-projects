Project Summary

The project aims to support the IDPs, Returnees, and vulnerable host communities in North, South and East Darfur to improve food security and livelihood, water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH), Nutrition NFIs/ shelters and protection assistance. The project aims to assist 190,064 beneficiaries (78,880 in North Darfur, 75,220 in South Darfur and 35,964 in East Darfur). The target beneficiaries include internally displaced persons (IDPs) in the camps and rural areas with focus on the newly displaced people and their host community, returnees and nomadic pastoralist community. The project will be implemented in close coordination with the sector leads and other actors from the INGOs and NNGOs to ensure collaborative and complementarity coverage of the needs and avoid overlap or duplication.
The project activities incorporate humanitarian response by a provision of assistance to save lives and alleviate suffering, and building the target communities’ resilience through livelihood restoration and building household productive assets and develop productive and managerial skills to enable the target beneficiaries to sustain the provided assistance and cope with future changes and reduce vulnerability.

Needs

Darfur continues to be amongst those areas with the highest levels of vulnerability in Sudan. According to 2019 HNO 1.76million Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) still live in camps and settlements in Darfur out of them there are 1,068323 IDPs in Oxfam operation area in Darfur. In addition, more than 1 million residents and nomads are affected by the conflict and are in need of support for their lives and livelihoods. In the absence of durable solutions, the situation of long-term IDPs, while relatively stable, depends heavily on continued humanitarian assistance. Permanent returns of IDPs and seasonal displacements are hindered because of prevailing insecurity and unresolved issues of land ownership, the confilect cycle conunite the needs for NFIs/shelters and protection services remains high. The conflict in South Sudan continue to force more people from South Sudan to cross the border, and becoming another contributing factor that will exacerbating the humanitarian crisis in Darfur. In South Darfur, Oxfam health and nutrition team reported serious concerns on the malnutrition rate in the Kass IDP camps, Katayla and Sharg Jabel Marra(E JM). It has increased alarmingly due to the decrease in food rationing by the world food program (WFP). Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) rate has reached about 5% while the Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate reached 20% particularly in Bilel IDP camp, in South Darfur. Moreover, the on-going influx of refugees from South Sudan in East Darfur putting more pursuer on the overstretched WASH infrascture and increase the risk of water borne disease. According to 2019 HNO, there are 84,859IDPs people living in Ed Daein, Sheiria and Yassin with limtied access to WASH and livelihood services. According to the WASH sector, small margins have adequate water for drinking and domestic consumption. Food prices have decreased households purchasing power and reduced their access to food among the IDPs and their vulnerable host groups. In North Darfur, the needs assessment covered six localities (El Fasher Rural, Dar Elsalam, Kalimendo, Um Kadada, Saraf Omra and Kebkabiya including Sortony IDP camp). The findings indicate that 20 % of boreholes and 60% of the hand pumps are not functioning and people travel a long distance to collect water, 75% of the assessed population are not practicing proper hand washing; more than 80% of the people do not have access to adequate WASH services. The IDPs in Sortony suffer from the shortage of safe drinking water within the camp and water is trucked from Kubie boreholes which is difficult to sustain. Sanitation facilities are not enough and inappropriate hygiene practices prevail. Despite the good harvest in 2018, large numbers of most vulnerable HHs have cultivated a limited area due to limited access to land and farming inputs. Impact on environment/natural resources

Oxfam recognizes that environmental considerations must be integrated into the planning and operation of projects to ensure any activities implemented are environmentally and socially acceptable.While carrying out the planned activities, Oxfam will take into account a variety of environmental considerations such as the management of wastewater, environmentally sound solid and liquid waste management (e.g. proper draining of water from tap stands so that it will not create pools of stagnant water that could contribute to the spread of disease.) Similarly, individual and communal latrines will be properly drained once they become full and effluent will be safely treated. All WASH-related infrastructure design and construction will take into account environmental issues such as the distance of shallow and deep wells from latrines and residential blocks, materials used for the construction of the hardware facilities, the existence of proper drainage systems form sanitation facilities, etc. The project will make the maximum effort to use environmentally friendly materials and options in WASH and livelihoods activities and will advocate with the UN and local government on how better to protect the environment in the midst of the crisis.Oxfam will also try to innovate the water supply with the application of solar power (green technology) which is friendly to the environment and at the same time have a very insignificant running cost. In Sanitation, we started using mud bricks in latrines fencing in Kalma camp and by this, we avoid using plastic sheeting, palm leaf mats and wood for fencing, which are more environmentally sound as well as long lasting compared to plastic sheeting and local tree materials.When drilling boreholes Oxfam will take care to avoid depletion of groundwater. After the drilling of the well, a pump test will be conducted to check the yield and the water drawdown, this testing helps to see the ground water recharging capacity to avoid any future risk of groundwater depletion. Distance from any household and public latrines and waste sites will be considered so that potential underground contamination is prevented. Oxfam will train the animal health workers on the issue vaccines and animal veterinary treatment waste, caring safe box in the field and in the animal gathering area by the community animal health worker and collection of the waste and safe handling and disposal in central incinerators to ensure the safeguarding of the environment as well as animal and human protection will be major part of the training package.
