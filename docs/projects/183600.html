Project Summary

The project provides critical humanitarian information to the Somali population, especially vulnerable groups, and ensures ongoing communication between communities and responders. It is based on information needs assessed through an audience survey conducted in 2019, supported by smaller local studies of audience views conducted in 2021 and 2021. As there is no national broadcaster and the local FM radios are largely centred on urban areas, significant geographical areas are uncovered by media leaving many rural, pastoralist, farming, displaced, and poor communities without adequate access to information. The local media provides a surfeit of political and current affairs news but far less consistent, reliable issue-based programming that informs and amplifies community voices. As well as the pastoralist and farming groups named above, in particular women, youth and children, IDPs, and the disabled, lack coverage and involvement in media content. Radio Ergo’s broadcast strategy is to reach as many people as possible, using shortwave and FM combined. The purpose of this project is,1. to provide critical information through daily radio broadcasts on lifesaving and life-enhancing issues to Somalis across the country, to enable them to make better informed decisions; and 2. to amplify the voices of local communities, including the most marginalized and excluded, through a trusted feedback platform to enable them to participate in humanitarian planning and response. The original content will be produced by Radio Ergo, a dedicated humanitarian radio with an established reputation among listeners and partners for quality and professionalism. Radio Ergo’s mission is to provide relevant humanitarian and public interest information for listeners (men, women, boys and girls) across all areas, and to facilitate a participatory communication cycle between the listeners and the humanitarian community and local/national governmental authorities. The broadcasts contain original content based on a number of complementary sources. First, Radio Ergo’s unique network of around 25 locally based Somali reporters gathering interviews, reports, features and information from their areas. The local reportage will reflect needs, views, and developments from different genders, age groups, social backgrounds and livelihoods. Second, practical humanitarian and developmental information and advice, aligned with cluster key messaging and advocacy, is incorporated into the radio programming in various formats including interviews with experts, discussions, question and answer, dramas, and advisories. The topics covered includes health, COVID19, agriculture, livestock, protection, displacement, etc. Secondly, Radio Ergo runs an feedback platform which is the only independent voice-based channel open continually to callers free of charge across all regions. The platform has become a popular way for people living in rural and remote areas to voice their concerns on the key issues affecting their lives, hoping to connect with the wider world including responders. Many callers are pastoralists, farmers, displaced, returnee refugees, among others. Radio Ergo compiles weekly feedback analysis reports to share with the humanitarian clusters and others as information for action. Some calls are followed up journalistically by reporters, and others are included in radio segments such as Radio Doctor, farming and livestock programmes. Relevant calls are referred to clusters and/or agencies for response. The feedback forms part of collective efforts in Community Engagement and Accountability (CEA). The project aims to harness the power of radio as the most popular means of mass communication in Somalia to save lives, raise awareness and enable informed decision-making; and to ensure the inclusion of voices that would not otherwise be heard in forums for service planning and provision.

1. - Projects activities

The project has four main activity components:1. Gathering information2. Broadcasting and other information dissemination channels3. Community feedback platform4. Local partner radio supportFurther details within each component are as follows:1. Gathering informationUsing its unique network of around 25 locally based Somali correspondents, as well as its team of staff producers, Radio Ergo reports on humanitarian and development issues from regions across Somalia and Somaliland. The local correspondent network provides a key source of information. This is supported by information from sources including humanitarian and development actors (including cluster members); government and other official sources; relevant experts and specialised agencies or bodies.2. Broadcasting and other dissemination channelsDaily one-hour programmes containing news and information in a wide range of formats (interviews, features, reports, dramas, advisories, expert Q&As, etc.) are broadcast nationally on shortwave, and on FM via a network of around 20 local Somali FM radio partners. The programming is also disseminated via Ergo's social media sites. Web stories based on the radio programmes are posted in Somali and English.3. Community feedback platformCalls from Somali listeners across the country are gathered on an independent toll-free mobile phone platform called Freedom Fone. The calls are collected and analysed weekly. Weekly reports summarising callers' needs and views are shared with the wider humanitarian community for response and action.4. Local partner radio supportThe projects editorial and technical support and capacity building training to local Somali FM radio partners, aiming to strengthen locally produced humanitarian content.
