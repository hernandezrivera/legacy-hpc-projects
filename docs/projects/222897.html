Project Summary

BRAC’s WASH initiative will emphasise community engagement, sustainability, and cost-effective solutions to address the growing needs of the Rohingya and host communities. This plan will directly respond to challenges posed by population growth, ageing infrastructure, and limited funding, aligning with the WASH sector’s objectives to deliver inclusive, accessible, and safe services across Cox’s Bazar camps and the surrounding host areas. To meet increased demand, BRAC will expand and maintain the water supply system. Efforts will include the repair and maintenance of deep tubewells(DTWs) and water distribution networks, ensuring consistent access to safe water. Strengthening water quality monitoring will also be vital in reducing health risks, involving the treatment of non-chlorinated sources and regular checks across BRAC and operational partner-managed areas as needed. Recognising environmental pressures on water resources, BRAC will adopt rainwater harvesting systems for drinking and wastewater recycling for non-potable uses. These sustainable solutions will help alleviate pressure on groundwater, with particular attention to acute needs in host communities. In response to the rising demand for sanitation services, BRAC will prioritise the regular maintenance and functionality of latrines and bathing cubicles and regular desludging of the latrines will be ensured.
Additionally, constructing new latrines and bathing spaces in underserved areas will improve accessibility, focusing on safe, inclusive environments for women, adolescents, and people with disabilities. Climate-resilient designs will be incorporated to withstand risks like landslides and floods, ensuring continuous service even during extreme weather events. BRAC will promote community-led hygiene campaigns with targeted hygiene education and preventive measures to address health risks, such as acute watery diarrhoea(AWD), dengue, and scabies. For waste management, BRAC will implement door-to-door collection and foster community involvement to reduce visible waste around households. Plastic waste management initiatives will minimise plastic use and promote upcycling to address plastic waste challenges. BRAC will also support the current sanitary landfill’s operations. These efforts will contribute to improved public health conditions, reduced environmental impact, and long-term resilience for camps and host areas alike. Public health risks associated with poor WASH conditions will be a priority, with a focus on AWD and dengue prevention. BRAC will promote water quality, sanitation, and hygiene while equipping community members with skills in facility repair and maintenance. Training will be provided to enhance local capacity, with a focus on women’s leadership within WASH committees to foster ownership, promote gender mainstreaming, , and ensure sustainability within the communities. Climate-resilient infrastructure will ensure continued service access amid environmental stressors. Emergency Preparedness and Response along with contingency plans will activate early warning systems to manage disease outbreaks and natural disasters, engaging the community to ensure proactive response and enhance resilience. To meet the unique WASH needs of the host community, BRAC will construct new water points, latrines, and bathing facilities, improve waste management in marketplaces, and construct wastewater treatment plants. These efforts aim to ease tensions from shared resources between host and refugee populations. The wastewater treatment plant at the camp periphery will promote shared benefits and foster harmony between communities through equitable essential services. A robust monitoring system based on BRAC’s project implementation quality (PIQ) indicators will track progress and align with sector goals. To avoid duplication and optimise resources, BRAC will coordinate with different stakeholders, including RRRC, CiC, DPHE, UN agencies, and NGOs.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

As the Rohingya crisis enters its eighth year, the need for daily WASH support continues to grow due to increasing population, ageing facilities, and shrinking funding. In the 2024 JRP, the total funding requirement for the WASH sector was $70M, whereas funding secured $13.5M only , 19.3% of the total appeal as per the financial tracking services of UNOCHA. This funding gap has hindered service improvements, reducing both the reach and accessibility of services. Although a water supply system exists in the Cox’s Bazar camps, gaps remain that impact daily life for the population. According to the WASH sector overview and monitoring data (Round 5, September 2024), 15% of Rohingya households lack sufficient water for daily needs. Moreover, an average of 32% of the Rohingya population (ISNA 2024) still depends on non-chlorinated water sources, increasing the risk of cholera outbreak and health complications. Cases identified in 2024 across the rohingya camps highlight the need for extending the treated water supply system with a strengthened monitoring system to ensure water quality. Safe drinking water remains scarce in the host areas, with almost half of the targeted population lacking sufficient access. On the other hand, expanding alternative water infrastructure, such as rainwater harvesting for drinking and wastewater recycling for cleaning and other household chores, will be essential in the coming years to preserve groundwater levels. For the sanitation component, maintaining the functionality of existing facilities will be crucial. The growing population is placing increased pressure on existing t facilities, resulting in frequent use and repair needs that often render them inaccessible. Increased usage of the latrine facilities has heightened the need for desludging ,placing additional pressure on faecal sludge management facilities. Moreover, some under-served areas in the camp still lack sanitation facilities like latrines and bathing cubicles. Climate-related challenges, such as landslides, flash floods and other natural disasters, make the facilities vulnerable, disrupting regular services. Greater engagement of the community in the facility repair is also necessary. The sanitation situation for persons with disabilities remains challenging, with significant gaps in inclusive facilities. The situation for women and adolescents remains inadequate, as 32% of Rohingya women do not feel safe accessing latrines at nighttime. Moreover, the routine incidence of dengue and scabies in the camps indicates a need for improved sanitation services. WASH sector overview and monitoring data (Round 5) indicate that 8% of households still have visible waste in their surroundings. Therefore, the door-to-door waste collection system needs to be strengthened with increased community engagement along with proper management of waste at Material Recovery Facilities. Plastic wastes in the camps are temporarily managed at the sanitary landfill , but initiatives are needed to reduce the amount of plastic waste generated. Solid waste management in the host community significantly lags behind that in the camps, requiring additional support.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

BRAC will mainstream protection by ensuring the participation and representation of marginalised groups, reducing discrimination based on gender, disability, sexual orientation, and other contributing factors while upholding dignity. Protection, safety, and privacy considerations will be integrated into WASH services to ensure safe access to the WASH facilities for all beneficiaries and to prevent physical and psychological harm while accessing these facilities in both camps and host communities. Messages targeting menstrual stigmas will be disseminated among the community people to eliminate the stigmas regarding menstrual practices in the community.

2.3 - Age & Disability

In 2025, BRAC will focus on making WASH facilities more accessible and comfortable for people of all ages (especially for children, youth, women and older adults) and persons with disabilities. BRAC will host community sessions to reduce stigma around age and disability, train staff, volunteers and caregivers to provide safe, comfortable support and implement practical facility changes like ramps, lower taps and tactile paths for easy access. BRAC will also include the laundry facility inside the bathing spaces, particularly for pregnant women. Through advocacy and inclusive engagement, BRAC will work to ensure everyone feels valued and connected in their community.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) represents BRAC’s commitment to responsibly using organisational resources and influences for the greater good of the targeted population. BRAC will prioritise transparent and regular engagement with the affected population, ensuring active community consultation so they are informed about selection criteria and processes. Unified feedback and response mechanisms will continue to address community needs aligning with the WASH sector strategy. Community based groups, including imams, majhis, respected community members, older adults, youth, persons with disabilities, and women, will help spread and mobilise the feedback system. Program participants will receive clear communication about the project’s goals, outcomes, and the services they will receive throughout the project period.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

BRAC is committed to strengthening Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) within WASH programme, enforcing a zero-tolerance approach to prevent exploitation and abuse throughout the planning and implementation. To uphold safeguarding standards and protect vulnerable groups, all WASH staff and volunteers will undergo regular, mandatory training and orientation on key policies, including safeguarding, PSHEA, child and adolescent protection, whistleblowing, prevention of workplace bullying and violence, and adult protection policies. By integrating these policies, BRAC aims to create a respectful, safe environment for all participants.A structured response process will be maintained, ensuring that participants can access trained safeguarding focal points within camps. BRAC's system guarantees confidentiality and respect when addressing issues, and Complaint Feedback Response Mechanisms (CFRM) will be established at all WASH facilities, featuring complaint boxes and PSEA focal contact information for reporting across all demographics. Staff and volunteers will receive specialized training on gender-sensitive complaint mechanisms and GBV (Gender-Based Violence) referral processes to enhance compassionate, effective response.BRAC will further extend PSEA awareness by conducting community sessions with leaders and youths to emphasize shared safeguarding responsibilities. Distribution of informational materials will reinforce the importance of safe reporting practices.In 2025, BRAC will continue prioritising PSEA and safeguarding, building on its 2024 efforts. This will involve ongoing training for staff, volunteers, vendors, and other service providers, participant awareness sessions, and standing agenda items on PSEA and safeguarding in staff meetings. A strong response mechanism will support survivors, and SEA risk assessments and dissemination of awareness materials will remain ongoing priorities.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The BRAC project will focus on addressing disaster risk reduction (DRR) and environmental issues through climate change adaptation and mitigation. Project interventions will emphasise the use of renewable energy in operating water networks and the promotion of rainwater harvesting. Additionally, BRAC will work exclusively on plastic waste management, aiming to reduce plastic waste and promote recycling in the camps. Furthermore, BRAC will collaborate closely with relevant agencies for regular drainage system cleaning to prevent potential flooding during heavy monsoons. BRAC will also strengthen its WASH facilities with necessary protections to mitigate the risk of landslides during the monsoon season.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

BRAC has a Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) for Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) and a Business Continuity Plan (BCP) to address various emergencies, including natural and man-made disasters, as well as nationwide shutdowns. Additionally, the BRAC project allocates a significant contingency fund to manage situations in line with the EPR SOP and BCP.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

BRAC actively engages youth as volunteers (Age minimum 18) and participants, harnessing their potential to improve community well-being and resilience. By including youth perspectives in program planning and assessment, BRAC will address specific needs, like adolescent girls' access to Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) facilities within WASH facility designs.Youth engagement will be central to hygiene promotion, where they will share insights, influence sustainable practices, and act as community role models. Through awareness, BRAC will equip young people for future decision-making roles, while empowering them as current agents of change. BRAC will remain vigilant about the gender-based violence (GBV) risks associated with WASH facilities, especially for adolescent girls whose education is often impacted by inadequate sanitation options. Engaging youth as WASH promoters will lift project impact, delivering enduring benefits to their communities.BRAC’s approach will include initiatives tailored to youth interests, such as campaign, volunteerism, and skills training, communication skill-building and foster an interest in green technologies, cooperative learning, and teamwork. To support adolescent girls, BRAC will distribute MHM kits, addressing their unique needs directly.Community training sessions engaging youth will enhance their understanding of WASH principles, reinforcing both facility sustainability and positive behavioural shifts. BRAC will collaborate with youth groups, addressing specific areas where young girls participate in MHM and hygiene practices, while young boys will receive guidance on hygiene practices and GBV awareness. Regular meetings with these groups will ensure sustained engagement, fostering positive practices in the community.This comprehensive youth-focused approach will not only meet the immediate WASH needs but also will cultivate resilience and well-being in the communities.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

BRAC’s WASH project will foster self-reliance among Rohingya communities by equipping them to manage their own water, sanitation, and hygiene needs. The project will emphasise community empowerment through restructuring and training of WASH committees and user groups, enabling them to oversee facilities, handle maintenance, and conduct minor repairs. This approach will not only build technical skills but also foster ownership and accountability, encouraging community members to protect these essential facilities. By training refugees in community-managed repair, BRAC will minimise reliance on external support and build confidence in local capabilities, promoting sustainability. Additionally, BRAC will prepare communities for emergencies by providing disaster response training, enabling refugees to protect WASH resources during crises. This project will support a shift from dependency to resilience, empowering the Rohingyas to lead their journey toward sustainable WASH management.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

BRAC’s WASH project will focus on host communities in Ukhiya and Teknaf, aligning with district development priorities and contributing to SDG 6. By enhancing WASH infrastructure, the project will address both immediate humanitarian and development needs, improving public health for the local community. These initiatives will support the Government of Bangladesh’s strategy to integrate humanitarian efforts with national development, promoting social cohesion and reducing tensions. BRAC’s emphasis on capacity building and community engagement will ensure local stakeholders participate in planning and implementing WASH services, fostering ownership and sustainability. Additionally, the project will address environmental challenges through sustainable WASH practices and wastewater management, promoting eco-friendly solutions and reducing pollution risks. Ultimately, BRAC’s efforts will meet immediate needs while contributing to a resilient, inclusive, and thriving Cox's Bazar district.
