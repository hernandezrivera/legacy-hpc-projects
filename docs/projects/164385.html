Project Summary

The project aims to enhance the affected population’s coping mechanisms by creating safe spaces where they can express themselves and interact with others freely. On the other hand, providing PSS services for their possible emotional problems, due to past traumatic experiences and adaptation process to the new social environment is the other equal part of the project, and in conclusion, their social and emotional well-being is improved through qualitative individual and/or group counselling. Throughout the social services provided by this center, it will become a recognized and accepted actor for both IDPs and host communities as well as public authorities. The activities which will take place in this center are identified in accordance with a community-based approach, based on the needs and resources of the affected population.
The multi-service community center ( MSCC ) regularly will organize awareness group sessions and psychosocial support activities for children, youth and adults, along with with Turkish, Arabic and English language courses and referral systems for the affected population.
The overall objective of the project is “To contribute to alleviating the suffering of psychosocial issues among IDPs living in the clusters (Der Hasan and Sarmada) and to enhance functionality and contribute to the reduction of loss of dignity caused by war.
On the other hand, additional activities will not change the essence and main goal of the project, which is providing PSS services and support emotional well-being. The language courses will be used as a tool to reach more beneficiaries, as potentially PSS services beneficiaries, and also as a tool to assist in improving social and emotional well-being.
Psychosocial support is considered as the main priority of the community center from life skills to early childhood development to awareness-raising events to household and community outreach. This ensures that all activities take the specific needs of the vulnerable population into account, including the integration of age and gender appropriate support for trauma and conflict-affected individuals, such as art and music therapy.
Therefore, this community center will provide support for them through PSS activities that will help the integration process between different groups (women, youth, adolescents, adults), and give many opportunities to work with a holistic approach and the referral mechanism also will be an essential part of the project by referring the cases which need specialized services to benefit from either IYD's projects or other partners' projects.
The MSCC personnel is composed of 1 head of MSCC and 5 staff members for facilitation (1 team leader, 4 psychological workers (2 male - 2 female) who undertake camp visits, awareness-raising sessions and PSS activities, in addition to 3 teachers for language courses. All these personnel will receive at the start of the project a general training on PSS, PSEA, SGBV, PFA…..ect.

4 - Needs

The conflict in Syria – triggering what is now the worst humanitarian crisis since World War II– is in its ninth year, with no end in sight. Millions have been forced to flee their homes to neighbouring countries and camps inside Syria, their futures are uncertain. People in need continue to pay the highest price and bear the heaviest burden of this crisis: their health, education, emotional well-being, and even their lives, are all at risk, and much more must be done to prevent them from becoming a ‘lost generation’. The clusters of Deir Hassan and Sarmada are of the top crowded areas in northwest Syria, host the highest number of IDPs in Idlib, According to CCCM figures, including 156 camps with a total number of 38,373 HHs. The limited access to health,education, MHPSS, and legal assistance ) remain as protection concerns to be addressed there, as well as the lack of the availability of community centers and services, IDPs lack the basic support from public facilities due to insufficient services and the requirement of protection. After the operations' recent interruption of INGOs in the field, the number of available community center services have been dropped drastically. The targeted clusters are overcrowded and have unsafe conditions, so IDPs there need a social environment that accepts and empowers them through appropriate channels to avoid possible heightened risks of self-harm, isolation, committing suicide, discrimination, economic and sexual exploitation, early marriages, and child labor. The top four MPHSS complaints reported by the 42 participants in FGD conducted by IYD in January 2020, were: (1) behaviour-related problems 31%, (2) anxiety-related problems reported by 24%, (3) physical complaints 14%, (4) age-related problems 10%, (5) family-related complaints reported by 7%. The mood-related problems 5% and other serious mental health disorders 2%.

5 - Implementation of activities

Activity 1: Conduct psychosocial activities for youth and adults at the community center 1.1. Coping mechanisms and functionality are strengthened through individual and group sessions. – Group sessions (focused on woman sessions, men session and girls /youth sessions) - Coordinate and monitor clear referral pathways to specialist services. The PSS activities will last for 2 months per group (2 times a week). All in all, 16 sessions of psychosocial support will be conducted per group. In total, 10 types of activities are offered in parallel out of which they can choose. 1.2. Community members achieve and sustain personal and interpersonal wellbeing, build capacities and skills and increase awareness on PSS services. - Provide skills-building activities. -Create, implement and monitor a plan of psychoeducation sessions- Active screening and case identification through PSS activities Activity 2: Deliver language courses in the community center for adolescents and adults to develop their knowledge skills. M&E Plan: The MEAL plan will apply random sampling to obtain sex and gender-segregated data, ensuring the equal participation of women, children, elderly people and people with disabilities to have comprehensive feedback among the beneficiaries.The verification of data quality provided by staff at the field level will also be done by project staff and MEAL teams. The data will be used to issue the monthly reports, which will highlight lessons learned and challenges.The IYD team will conduct (baseline-endline) study; whereas the baseline study will be with representative sample out of the chosen beneficiaries before starting the implementation and the endline study will be at the end of the project with the same chosen beneficiaries in the baseline, to measure the improvement through the intervention.The MEAL team will carry out random field visits (on-site monitoring visits) during the implementation to monitor the quality of implemented activities.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk1: discrimination or a feeling of discrimination if service is provided to specific groups or individuals and others are excluded.Likelihood: Medium Impact: HighlyMitigation: Printed guiding instructions clarify that services and courses cover everyone without any discrimination. Activating the complaint mechanisms to get feedback from people who haven't benefited from center services. Risk 2: sexual exploitation and sexual abuse by the project personnel against female and girls beneficiaries who regularly attend awareness-raising sessions and language courses as well as exploiting the beneficiaries' vulnerability points.Likelihood: Medium Impact: HighlyMitigation: Providing training on PSEA to all staff, and establish complaint mechanisms, and addressing these complaints. The Code of Conduct will be signed by all employees. Risk 3: Project staff or beneficiaries face a safety hazard in the project location.Likelihood: Low Impact: HighlyMitigation: We will train our staff on emergency preparedness and PSS activities closure SOPs. The situation will be monitored closely with the field staff and the local authorities. Risk 4: Low interest of beneficiaries to project activities. Likelihood: Medium Impact: HighlyMitigation:We will have an effective outreach period to reach the potential beneficiaries that are most in need of or interested in the services provided by the project. Risk 5: Beneficiaries will not attend the language courses regularly.Likelihood: Medium Impact: HighlyMitigation: Beneficiaries will be consulted on the topics, the pace of the course and they will be able to give feedback. The reasons for their lack of commitment will be analyzed and addressed accordingly.

Assumption 1: the security situation remains stable and the area is accessible.Risk: the local community refuses to participate in the assessments or share information.Mitigation: they will be briefed about the aim of this activity and the expected outcomes, they will be assured of the confidentiality of the information collected.Assumption 2:Risk: Security instability that affects the prospects for integration of IDPs into host communities.Mitigation: We will closely monitor the security atmosphere and will adjust our activities accordingly if any sign of instability emerges. Assumption 3: the host community acknowledges the critical needs of IDPsRisk 2: a tension between IDPs and the local communities due to the center services prvided which mainly target IDPs.Mitigation: awareness-raising sessions will include both IDPs and host communities to alleviate the tension and enhance cohesion.Assumption 4: staff is capacitated to conduct activities.Risk: beneficiaries have high expectations of PSS activities that cannot be fulfilled.Mitigation: the activities -centered approach will be followed and staff will be transparent and realistic about the available assistance.
