Project Summary

This proposal aims to ensure that 2200 vulnerable students, both male and female attending grades 7th to 12th, from 50 vulnerable schools in the West Bank and Gaza Strip, who were unable to pursue their education due to implications of COVID-19 and the digital divide, are able to resume their education. Moreover, 300 teachers of both genders teaching the most essential subjects will be targeted in this project.
The targeted students and teachers will be provided with laptops necessary to continue the educational process while in lock down and will be supplied with internet connection. In addition, the project is foreseeing to provide training for students and teachers on the use of ICT equipment purchased within the project to ensure best delivery of quality education.
Moreover, considering the psychological impact of COVID-19 on both teachers and students, the project will provide psychosocial support through group and recreational activities to students (drama and storytelling). Meanwhile, teachers will receive training on child-friendly teaching methods, supportive communication skills, self-care on how to stay positive during the crisis, and how to keep their students engaged and enthusiastic during online sessions as part of the psychosocial support component. They will also be trained to recognize signs of child's anxiety and depression and, if required, upon the consent of students, in consultation with their parent to refer the students to the professional counseling as part of the psychosocial support component.
The Ministry of Education (MoE) will provide the list of 50 of the most vulnerable schools from the West Bank and the Gaza Strip. This project will be implemented in close cooperation with the MoE as the main partner in addition to selecting implementing partners who have the necessary expertise in distance learning and psychosocial support. The interventions are sought to conduct an assessment on the need of ICT equipment with clear eligibility criteria in order to ensure transparency and to reach the most vulnerable student.
The project follows a blended approach of educational and psychosocial support, promoting the use of technological and non-technological solutions. This project will also mitigate the risk of dropout rates from the schools and will save the children from exploitation through any form of work that deprives them of their right to education, mitigates the risks of falling into crime, joining extremist groups, and child marriage.
Project Goal and Objectives:
The goal of this project is to enhance access to education for students from the most vulnerable groups in the context of Covid-19 through:
• Equipping students and their teachers with digital technology literacy and skills
• Providing them with laptops to ensure their access to quality education
• Addressing the psychosocial needs of students and teachers to guarantee their engagement and achievements.
The activities proposed for this project align with the priorities of the Government of Palestine’s education sector response during the pandemic. The response was presented in the National Response Plan (NRP) for COVID-19 and the Distance Learning Taskforce co-chaired by UNESCO, the Ministry of Education among others. Access to distance learning including infrastructure and psychosocial support are among the top three priorities of the Palestinian Ministry of Education's National Response Plan to mitigate the effects of COVID-19.
The project objectives also aligned with the objectives of the education sub-cluster's Humanitarian Needs Overview (objectives 1 and 2) aiming to ensure vulnerable children have access to formal and informal education services. The target population consists primarily of vulnerable students who risk dropping out of school due to the disruption in the education system and the economic fallout caused by the implications of the COVID-19.

1 - Needs

The preliminary data of the education survey for the academic year 2019/2020 conducted by the Palestinian Central Bureau for Statistics revealed that the number of school students in Palestine is approximately 1.313 million students. The current situation of the occupied Palestinian Territories due to the socio-economic hardships caused by the ongoing COVID-19 crisis showed students were forced to stay at home as early as March 2020, following the declaration of the state of emergency, which disrupted the normal setting in which children were expected to grow and learn. The occurred disturbances affected families, relationships, daily routines, and communities. The current scenario since the first quarter of 2020 has had a negative consequence on children's well-being, development, and protection, resulting in child labor, dropout, and early marriages of girls. The consequences were experienced among children, especially those who suffered from digital divide, who had no access to online education since their families could not afford laptopsAs per the Ministry of Education's latest estimated figures, and the figures provided to UNESCO through the governing entities in West Bank and Gaza: 216,932 students in the West Bank and 87,724 students in Gaza Strip were reported not owning digital educational tools such as laptops. To enable them to access education through the current blended learning approach as part of the Back to School campaign, students in the West Bank and Gaza Strip will require digital educational tools (i.e. laptops). According to the PCBS household survey, 44% of the households in Gaza and West Bank suffered from a lack of devices (36% were in Gaza while 50% were in the West Bank). Past experiences demonstrate that education along with gender inequalities are first to be neglected as a response to disease outbreaks. In addition to the lack of access to education, the economic impact on households likely extended the inequities in education attainment. Many of these issues have been designed in previous policy briefs and include, for instance, food insecurity, economic instability, and violence against women and girls.According to the experiences due to previous pandemics, closures of educational institutions due to COVID-19 increased risk for women and girls, as they had been one of the most vulnerable segments, prone to multiple types of abuses, such as domestic violence, sexual violence, and early and forced marriages. The closures of institutions such as education have also affected the normal work pattern of many parents. Apart from the economic hardships that many parents had to face since the start of the crisis, affecting their children's education is a greater challenge for parents as a significant share of working parents relied on childcare and schools.The areas that the project targets are some of the most vulnerable due to political unrest, remoteness, difficulties faced during student commute to schools, and numerous other problems that predated COVID-19, but were exacerbated by the pandemic. Specifically, the COVID-19 crisis worsened pre-existing education disparities, by reducing the access to opportunities for many of the most vulnerable children, youth, and adults, especially those living in poorer areas, people with disabilities, and girls, among many other vulnerable groups, from continuing their education.These children do not have the required devices to access the remote learning opportunities, currently being used by the educational system to provide education during the COVID-19 pandemic. The schools in poor communities further face challenges in maintaining their schools safe from COVID-19 infections as the communities in these schools are not able to afford cleaning and disinfecting materials as per safe school operations protocols that have been put in place by the government.Lack of education and learning threatens to go beyond the current generation and has the power to erase progress at many levels that may have been achieved through decades of hard work. As financial pressures increase, and development assistance is drained, financing to education faces major challenges, which leads to widening education funding gaps that preceded COVID-19. If not supported, vulnerable students are at high risk of dropping out of school, the well-being of children and parents will be harmed, and the inequities between the rich and the marginalized segments will further be aggravated.To address this, UNESCO is requesting funds to provide vulnerable children from the most vulnerable schools with access to the educational system at times of crisis through the provision of laptops and enabling the teachers and students to connect online for virtual classes. Particular attention will be given to children with specific needs and children who have been exposed to gender-based violence and grave child rights abuse. The funds requested through this proposal will be used to build on current ongoing interventions to reach more vulnerable children from the most vulnerable schools and to ensure continuity of their education and mainstreaming of psychosocial support and social and emotional learning in the education program. This proposal will target the most vulnerable students in the most vulnerable schools. This project in the initial phase targets specific students from grade 7th to 12th in 50 schools and aims to expand to other grades so that enables all the vulnerable students to get connected online to their schools at the time of crisis.

2 - Activities

UNESCO's experience and long-term fruitful partnership with the MOE in supporting the right to education and psychosocial services to students will contribute to the successful implementation of this project which will be conducted through several activities. The implementation of the programmatic activities will be the responsibility of UNESCO and the selected implementing partner and will be carried out by UNESCO in accordance with its own applicable policies, procedures, rules and regulations including those relating to procurement. The following are the proposed activities:Activity 1: Provide a list of the most vulnerable schools in the most vulnerable areas in the West Bank and Gaza Strip that were not targeted before for these types of activities. The list will be reviewed and analyzed by a committee comprised of the MoE, UNESCO, and selected implemented partner, and 50 schools will be selected.Activity 2: Develop a selection criterion for eligibility of targeted students and teachers through a committee comprising of members from MoE the distance learning taskforce and parent-teachers associations of Gaza Strip and West Bank. The committee will also be involved in the implementation, monitoring, and evaluation phases of the project. Activity 3: Assess the total number of students and teachers who need laptops at each school. Activity 4: Provide laptops to 2200 most vulnerable students at 50 schools to access online learning platforms and ensure their right of entry to active participation in the school programs. The implementing partner under the supervision of UNESCO will make sure to connect the selected teachers with the selected students through the online teaching method. Through this methodology, this project will gap the bridge between school and students at the time of lockdown or another crisis. Activity 5: Provide 300 teachers at the selected schools based on the most essential subjects to be taught to targeted students through digital platforms.Activity6: Provide training to the students and teachers on how to use the laptops on distance learning methods and take part in online school sessions.Activity 7: Share the list of the students and teachers whom were provided with laptops under the project with the Palestinian Ministry of Telecommunications and Information Technology to provide them with free Internet (DSL) connection.Activity 8: Build capacities of 300 teachers through providing training on child-friendly teaching, supportive communication skills, and self-care to better cope in times of crises, for example recognizing signs of stress, common reactions, building resilient, coping skills and how to stay motivated and positive at the time of crisis as a basic component of the psychosocial support training. This will also give a space for teachers for debriefing and sharing their experiences. Activity 9: Conduct group and recreational activities with 2200 students to improve their well-being and this will be done through sports activities, storytelling, and drama activities. The children will be given an opportunity to select the activities they like most around the value of education, friendship, positively handling isolation, depression, and anxiety. With support from the implementing partner staff, the children will identify the topics of most concern for children during the pandemic and elaborate on them and make theatre or puppet play. It is also possible that the play will be recorded and shared with other schoolchildren during teachers' weekly offline and online sessions. Also, sports events will be organized among the students in the open air to improve team spirits and improve their physical and mental health.Activity 10: Undertake a major fundraising initiative to approach donors at the local and international level to scale up the project to the larger target groups in the second phase (since the current allocated budget can reach a comparatively small number of students and teachers). Other partners of UNESCO including the distance learning taskforce partners, as well as local businesses that pass the UN's due diligence for the business entry point, will be targeted to cooperate on visibility, as well as support during later phases.

3 - Indicators

The 50 targeted schools will be identified as the most vulnerable schools situated in high-risk areas in the West Bank and the Gaza strip will benefit from this project. Whereas 2200 vulnerable students from these schools will receive laptops, Internet connection, and training on the usage of modern technology, as well as 300 essential teachers. Furthermore, 2200 students will receive group and recreational activities to improve their well-being (this will be done through sports activities, storytelling, and drama activities); and 1000 teachers from the 50 schools will be trained on supportive communication skills and self care so they stay motivated and positive during the crisis as a part of the psychosocial support component through practical methods intended to support the wellbeing of teacher and students. These trained teachers will conduct weekly online or offline sessions to keep students motivated and positive at the time of crisis such as COVID-19.To ensure timely and effective achievements of the project outcome; UNESCO in partnership with MoE will select an implementing partner through a competitive process based on a review of technical and financial proposals submitted by participants. Through the intervention of UNESCO, the selected implementing partner will target the most vulnerable schools in West Bank and the Gaza strip. The project team will work closely with the Ministry of Education and Teachers-Parents committees. The project team will also be responsible for implementing the activities, which include the assessment of the children in need of equipment; the selection of students based on the eligibility criteria that will be developed, and they will also provide psychosocial support activities to teachers and students. The proposal follows a blended approach in education and psychosocial support, promoting the use of technological and non-technological solutions. Protection will be mainstreamed throughout the project by the incorporation of protection principles and promoting meaningful access, safety, and dignity in all activities. UNESCO will be accountable to ensure that a system for "Accountability to the Affected Populations" approach, which will be developed and part of the M&E plan. Students who will benefit from this intervention will be involved in consultations and dialogues with UNESCO partners to influence the implementation of the proposed intervention. UNESCO and the selected implementing partners will communicate and receive feedback from beneficiaries throughout the project implementation through its complaint and feedback mechanism, which will be designed and developed to respond quickly to concerns raised.Project monitoring at all stages will be done jointly by a team comprising of MoE, UNESCO, the implementing partner, teacher association, and the education cluster. UNESCO through its implementing partner will ensure monitoring and evaluation of the project based on outputs and outcomes delivered. Also, the focus of UNESCO's M&E efforts will include analysis of the reports and information provided by the implementing partner combined with physical monitoring directed at the school level.The implemented partner to have complete control of the track of the project implementation, under the supervision of UNESCO need to establish ground rules for the students and teachers to ensure that these target groups understand that they are as responsible for doing their work as in a traditional classroom. Students and teachers should know the importance of responsible, respectful, and honest participation. Students should be reminded from time to time that their goal should be to gain a valuable education. The implementing partner should provide a monthly progress report of the implementation of the project, and UNESCO along with its implementing partners in this project should take timely actions to ensure the success of the implementation of this project.The implementing partner should provide the complete list of beneficiaries that received the equipment and the services. They should also, provide information about the success level of connecting the selected students with the selected teachers through a successful online session. They are obliged to keep UNESCO informed about the obstacles that occur in conducting the online teaching sessions so that UNESCO along with other partners could take timely actions to fix or prevent future disruptions.UNESCO monitoring team will contribute to the development of a complaint mechanism where beneficiaries could always access the monitoring team through this mechanism and put their complaint that could help UNESCO and its partners to act upon the issues promptly. Furthermore, UNESCO will conduct teacher/community focus groups related to perceived satisfaction and the effectiveness of the project.Monitoring and evaluation will be achieved by systematic tracking of the project objectives and indicators. Reporting mechanisms are in place to ensure regular reporting on defined project-indicators as well as to verify that the financial reporting requirements are met.The project will follow the "Do No Harm" approach and will consider the principle of equity. UNESCO will ensure that all staff is trained in the "Core Humanitarian Standards for Quality and Accountability and PSEA" method, as well as the inclusion of all vulnerable groups. Gender parity will be attained through the assessment phase. Furthermore, at the end of the project, UNESCO will carry out post-distribution monitoring to analyze the result of the intervention, the level of appreciation from beneficiaries, and collect feedback. Besides, UNESCO and the selected implementing partner will discuss any substantive revisions to the project, and promptly inform one another about any significant circumstances and major risks, that interfere with or threaten the achievement of the outcomes outlined in the Project document.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

This project will be implemented according to the Do No Harm approach in all stages of the project cycle. The project needs assessment, as well as project design and evaluation are based on community involvement and feedback, ensuring input from all stakeholders during all processes of the project cycle. This is to further understand internal social processes, develop an analysis of the possible negative consequences during implementation, and taking into account the needs of all stakeholders. Feedback mechanism: A project review committee will also be formed comprising of different stakeholders (distance learning taskforce, support organizations, MoE and parents/teachers) that will review the feedback received through monitoring, evaluation & Preventions of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mechanisms and will send the recommendations to UNESCO for possible implementation.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

This project aims at providing access to education and psychosocial support activities to nearly 2200 students of all genders. These students are unable to access education facilities since the closure of schools in early March 2020 due to a lack of capacity to buy digital educational tools (laptops, tablets, …etc.), while the rest of the students may continue their education online. The project will target girls (substantially contributing to gender equality), children with disabilities, and those belonging to the most vulnerable families and do not have access to online education due to a lack of devices/laptops. UNESCO will ensure that all staff is trained in the “Core Humanitarian Standards for Quality and Accountability” method, as well as inclusion of all vulnerable groups. Gender parity will be attained through the assessment phase.
