Project Summary

The project total number of beneficiaries is 54,942. This number is dis-aggregated as (Idp
13,735 – host population 38,460 – returnees 2,747) 53,821 children (28,283 Boys and
25,538 Girls) 1,121 (532 women and 589 men) (Male 28,872, female 26,070). BINAA aims
to reach 53821 children (28283 Boys and 25538 Girls) in approximately 160 schools or
temporary learning space (TLS) and support them with fuel, heaters in addition to the
learning supplies (backpack, school in box kit and recreational kits), develop the capacity
of 1121 (532 women and 589 men) teachers through training sessions during the school
year, and awareness campaigns in targeted communities on back to school and dropout.
The project also aims to rehabilitate 50 damaged schools (including the maintenance of
windows, doors and ceilings, water facilities, rubble removal and lighting) and equip the
50 schools with the necessary furniture (desks, tables, boards, laptops and projectors).
The project also includes preparation of child friendly spaces in 50 schools, and operate
the 50 schools to deliver quality education services to the children in these schools by
securing educational staff incentives, schools’ operational costs and coal for heating
during winter.
Gender has been inbuilt within the design of the project by ensuring that both men and
women are targeted by this action. In particular, girls and boys will be targeted to ensure
their access to adequate education services. Representation of both men and women to
ensure their voices and particularly those of women are heard in consultation and
awareness campaigns matters that are pertinent to their gender. People with disabilities
will be targeted as well through adding disable-friendly facilities such as handrails, ramps
and toilets.
In tracking and monitoring the results accrued from the project, the project has developed
gender dis aggregated indicators to demonstrate the extent to which both girls, boys,
men and women have benefited from the action.

4 - Needs

The humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2020 An estimated 2 million school-aged children have been internally displaced and an additional 2.5 million children have fled to neighboring countries There are nearly two million school-aged IDPs and over 70,000 school aged returnees . These children have particularly limited availability of, and access to, education. Additionally, child labor disproportionately results in IDP children not attending school The impact of displacement has affected hosting communities where larger number of students further over stretch limited resourced comprising access and quality of education services for residents and IDPs. Overcrowded and under-resourced mixed-aged mixed-ability classrooms reduce the likelihood that learning can take place and increase the risk that schools will not be able to adequately accommodate children with special needs. BINAA provided full education services to more than 125000 children during 2019. Through the intervention fuel,educational materials, school support bags and recreational kits. In order to increase access to Life saving education for war affected children in Syria, BINAA will complement all responses to sustain students' involvement in the learning process by providing school with fuel, heaters in addition to box materials and recreational kits to 58000 children in elementary, middle and high school, including children from IDPs and host communities, and rehabilitate, operate and follow up the education process for 50 schools, and provide psychosocial support and mine risk education, and provide water and sanitation facilities as they are key interventions to achieve this goal. BINAA conducted rapid consultations with the educational directorates and selected subdistricts conforming with HNO needs to be targeted with high severity scale and will conduct a field and detailed surveys

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk-01: Interference from armed groups or the Camp management in the works. The likelihood of occurrence is high and its impact is high as well. this risk will be monitored by Binaa field staff in addition to the report received from the security department. Clear agreement with and consent form signed by the civilian management bodies. However, such kind of risks when happen the activities should be held and the incident will be reported to the relevant stakeholders. Risk-02: Unintentionally, misapplying Do no-harmprinciple: the likelihood is low and the impact is high. This risk will be monitored basicallyby Binaa M&E team. Community mobilization in addition to ensuring proper and adequate need technical and verification process. The verification process should cover both side of the intervention, vulnerability in addition to the technical needs assessment. Feedback and complaint mechanism is adopted to contribute in solving the issues. Risk-03: Access restrictions to the targeted areas active fighting: the likely hood of occurrence is moderate and its impact is high. The risk will be monitored by Binaa field staff in addition to the report received from the security department. Contingency plan in addition to reasonable exit strategy is prepared. Once this type of risk is happened the all works willstalled in the low-security locations and being prepared to apply either the contingency or exit plan. Risk-04: GBV, sexual abuse or facilities are not disability-sensitive in the rehabilitated schools’ facilities. the likelihood is low and the impact is high. This risk will be monitored basically by Binaa M&E team. Referral path system and Feedback and complaint mechanism is adopted to contribute in solving the issues

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk-01: Interference from armed groups or the Camp management in the works Mitigation action: - Signing the consent form adopted by was signed by the camp management to ensure the independency to access, select and implement as well without any potential external interference. Risk-02: Unintentionally, misapplying Do no harm principle Mitigation action: - Enhancing the community mobilization. communication representative in addition to camps management and camps people representative and all interventions details were explained very clearly to the inhabitants there. Risk-03: Access restrictions to the targeted areas active fighting. Mitigation action: Based on the intel provided by BINAA security department, whenever a fighting occurred,the field team is directed to change routs, suspend work or take required precautions. Risk-04: GBV, sexual abuse or facilities are not disability-sensitive in the rehabilitated schools’ facilities. Mitigating measures: Adequate amount of easy to maintain lockablelatrines (special units for boys, girl and teachers); Adequate amount of hand washing facilities near the latrines and inside the school A well-constructed and well-maintained school building that protects pupils during classroom time; Ramps and handrails for thedisabled. Ramps and handrails for the disabled. Assumption The main assumptions of this project are: - Fund will be delivered on time. - There will be no change in control of the targeted areas. - Prices of inputs remain stable during the project duration – Security situation allows access to beneficiaries.
