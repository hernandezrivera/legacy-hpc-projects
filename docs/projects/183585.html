Project Summary

The project aims at emergency support for Gaza farmers in order to improve their economic situation through rehabilitation of their agricultural facilities . The project targeted groups will be vulnerable affected households whom agricultural facilities are damaged during repeated Israelis wars launched against Gaza, especially the during last one in May 2021. The targeted groups have to be engaged in greenhouses and open land farming and animal breeding. Farmers with limited financial capacity to cover the costs of the needed production inputs and needed materials for the rehabilitation of their agricultural facilities will be supported through conditional restricted cash assistance and service deliver for the rehabilitation of their damaged agricultural facilities ( OL, GH and poultry farms ), the cash assistance will be delivered to the beneficiaries in 2 installments. AL’ARD focuses also on the infrastructural challenges for protecting agricultural livelihoods in the Gaza Strip such as; damaged agricultural land , greenhouses and poultry farms through land , greenhouse, poultry farms rehabilitation; shortages of electricity through provision solar PV system for Poultry farms . The Action will enable vulnerable households to maintain their agricultural activities and enhance their coping capacity to future shocks.
The project will contribute to the following overarching country strategic objectives: SO3: The resilience of vulnerable households are protected, and their livelihoods, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced (CO1) and Households suffering from limited access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs.(CO2). The planned action was closely coordinated with the Food Security Sector, ministry of Agriculture (MoA), ministry INGO and NGOs
The project activities are described as follow:
1. 200 OL rehabilitation in north and Gaza governorates; 100 farmers will be supported through conditional and restricted cash assistance needed for the rehabilitation of 2 OL Dunms for each farmers, each farmer will receive 950 USD in 2 installment
2. 100 GH rehabilitation the north, Khan Yunis and Rafah; 100 GH farmers will be supported through the rehabilitation of their greenhouses.
3. 20 poultry farm rehabilitation with PV systems in the North, Gaza and middle area, 20 poultry breeders will be supported through service delivery assistance needed for the rehabilitation of the poultry farm.

1 - Needs

There are two million Gazans who are living under an Israeli blockade, and who also face an electricity crisis as well as an unstable political and security environment. These challenges continue to prevent Gazan’s access to a decent livelihood and limits the free movement of people, goods, and services. The situation became even more diﬃcult with the spread of COVID-19 in the West Bank and the Gaza Strip and the deterioration following the declaration of a state of emergency and the frequent lockdowns. As a result of these factors, roughly 73% of households are severely or moderately food insecure in Gaza Strip (HNO2022). Furthermore, the May 2021 conflict had a direct and severe impact on the agricultural sector, which contributes to nearly 7% of the GDP and informally employs 20 percent of the Palestinian workforce in Gaza (WFP, June 2021). According to OCHA reports, large agricultural areas and several agricultural facilities such as animal farms, greenhouses and water wells have been damaged or exposed to disruptions, which has consequently decreased agricultural production (OCHA, Escalation in the Gaza Strip, the West Bank and Israel, May 2021). The Ministry of Agriculture (MoA) in Gaza assessed that the damage caused by the May escalation cost US$ 56 million, which was in line with the World Bank’s estimate which stated that damages and losses in the agricultural sector totaled $55-60 million During the months of September and October Al'Ard conducted . Needs Assessment of the Livelihood of farmers /breeders and fishermen in the Gaza Strip 2021. The assessment followed a qualitative inquiry approach which was based on rapid participatory assessment techniques and guided by standard data collection tools that were developed at the onset of the assessment in consultation with the FSS. The following points provide a overview of the methods and tools that were used:• Desk review: A review of the most recent reports covering the humanitarian, food security, agriculture and market conditions was undertaken to get a snapshot of the food security and livelihoods situation in the Gaza Strip prior to and in the aftermath of the recent crisis.• Focus group discussions: A total of 5 focus group discussions were conducted with 175 farmers engaged in plant production (81), livestock breeding (86) and fishing (8) in a purposive sample of 10 farming communities. Targeted categories invited to participate in the discussion were selected in consultation with farmers’ Commissions based on their main livelihood activity (i.e., plant production, livestock production, and fishing), most of them, and except for fisherfolk, had mixed agricultural holdings. Main findings of the assessments This part of the report presents the findings of the assessment. 5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

Al'Ard will use a combined modality for implementing the project. Al'Ard will use direct assistance (unconditional and restricted assistance) to the selected beneficiaries for the following activities: open land and CFW activity The main reasons for selecting this modality are: 1. Fast and efficient. 2. Flexibility 3. Give power of choice to the beneficiary 4. Dignity and limit sexual exploitationFor the rehabilitation of greenhouses and poultry farms and installation of solar systems to light the poultry farms, Al'Ard will use a direct service deliverymodality. The main reason for this is that those two activities require technical specifications and design requirements to ensure efficiency and effectiveness. It would be extremely difficult for small-scale farmers to come up with high quality designs and technical specifications. Al'Ard will procure the service of specialized consulting companies or qualified individual consultants/engineers to develop the technical specifications and the design requirements. Moreover, Al'Ard will advertise the tenders for those activities in the newspapers in order to give the suppliers the opportunities to participate in the tender.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

As most project activities will be carried out in a sensitive area, a risk analysis and security incidents will be discussed and updated in a participatory approach with the target population. Consequently, mitigation measures will be carried out to reduce potential risks adhering to the principle of “Do No Harm”. UAl'Ard support for vulnerable farmers is based on the humanitarian principles (humanity, neutrality, impartiality and independence), the ‘do no harm’ concept and other Al'Ard values, such as non-discrimination ,Al'Ard support is guided by a people-centered approach, which means that the interests of beneficiaries are at the center of operations and humanitarian assistance is tailored to their differentiated needs. The most important principles that believes in is providing service to the target groups without causing harm that matters or exploiting them in exchange for providing these services, as Al'Ard through its follow-up team and through its Committee follows up on that, and in the event that one of the employees breaches it, the appropriate penalty is inflicted upon him according the existed code of conductTechnical assessments will consider the safety, dignity and confidentiality of the served population while also protecting data and humanitarian actors uncompromised. Preserving dignity is essential to maintaining self-esteem and confidence, vital to coping in stressful and potentially overwhelming humanitarian situations. Supporting women’s self-esteem and confidence will reflect positively on them and will enhance the roles of each member of the household and will result in improved wellbeing of the family, especially children as care receivers.Before implementing activities, project staff will attend a training session on the overall protection principles and how to detect GBV. This should equip them with knowledge and tools to conduct a risk analysis that identifies risks, risks level, likelihood and mitigation measures to be taken into consideration in advance. Most importantly, activities will be designed and implemented in close coordination and full engagement and consultation of targeted communities in liaison with relevant authorities whenever needed and appropriate. Specifically, the following steps will be implemented to integrate do no harm throughout the project: 1- Project risk assessment will be prepared at the project start to identify clear measures to mitigate any risks with beneficiaries, contractors, and Al'Ard staff, community committees and the broader community by the project. 2-Active engagement with targeted communities, including their representatives, through the project community committees that will include farmer representatives (women CBOs and local committees in target localities). This approach will be the basis for all steps of the project cycle, ensuring meaningful representationof women and youth whenever possible to ensure that the project’s design is responsive to project beneficiaries' different needs and concerns. 3- Supporting women in accessing cash for work activities by providing safe and appropriate work opportunities in terms of location and time. All activities will follow a quick risk assessment which will identify any potential risks related to women’s participation in the activities and identify means to support them within the project period or beyond through a referral mechanism. 4- Cash for work activity will be aligned with the child protection policy prohibiting child labour. It will also promote women participations in agricultural typically un-accounted for, employing the equal access principle of protection and considering preserving the dignity of targeted women. This will be conducted by tailoring activities according to what is most suitable for them in terms of timing, form, location, payment method, and implications of HH and childcare burdens. 5- Activities will be adapted to COVID19 related preventive measures.
