Project Summary

In line with Cluster guidance, the objective of this response is to save and sustain lives through the provision of timely, targeted and appropriate shelter assistance and relief items. PIN plans to provide different shelter and NFI kits to meet the priorities and different needs in North East Syria, notably distributions of Sealing-off & partition kits (SoK) and emergency shelter kits, New arrival & kitchen kits Winterization kits/cash. PIN will also contribute to sector SNFI sector objective - Reinforce an enabling protection environment and community cohesion by improving housing and related community/public
infrastructure through repair/rehabilitation of housing and related community/public infrastructure.
PIN’s response will be grounded in needs assessment data, contextual understanding and scenario planning.
PIN will target 338,200 individuals with Shelter and NFI support across Northern Syria. Under the first Cluster Objective: Provide life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support, PIN will target 82,800 individuals with emergency shelter and NFI support. Under the second objective: Reinforce an enabling protection environment and community cohesion by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure, PIN will target 255 400 beneficiaries, with the majority expected to benefit from the Repairing/rehabilitation of community/public infrastructure and facilities.
Under the objective 1:SOKs will be distributed in informal settlements, collective centers and damaged houses of returnees/IDPs and will be provided according to the level of damages (light and medium). Winterization will provided to most vulnerable HHs, where feasible targeting the same beneficiaries PIN is supporting already with Food security or another programming to maximize impact. NFI support will target primarily newly displaced HH with a specific focus on the most vulnerable groups.
Under the objective 2:PIN will target vulnerable households living in inadequate shelter with the mix of eligibility (HHs with damaged house, Returnees, remainees with HLP or IDPs with tenancy agreements in place, Shelter that has been damaged due the conflict and is repairable) and vulnerability criteria (HH hosting IDPs/returnees, chronically ill, PwDs, FHH, single women, elderly, etc.). The selection of the public infrastructures will be done based on criteria of safety and accessibility, acuate humanitarian needs, Essential infrastructure with Light to Medium Damage and sustainability/continuity of services.
PIN will select locations based on needs assessment data, highlighting the most critical needs, vulnerabilities and gaps. In areas where security, access, and market functionality permit, and needs assessments indicate cash-based approaches would provide a more appropriate response, PIN may shift NFI/Winterization assistance to value vouchers to assist the targeted population. Value voucher will only be provided in cases where needs assessments show that NFI/winterization items are priority needs, in order to mitigate the risk of these being used for other purposes and where possible, PIN will strive to provide them together with other aid such as food assistance under complementary donors to ensure that all key priorities are met.
PIN is aware that needs are different amongst gender and age groups, and other vulnerable groups such as people with disabilities. PIN aims to provide most needed assistance to such groups through: Employing a wide spectrum of different activities to meet distinct need, Establishing beneficiary selection criteria to priorities inclusion of those most vulnerable and adapting its assistance delivery to promote access for the most vulnerable people. PINs programmes are carried out in coordination with local authorities at various levels – from cross-border, to hub, to district and village level. PIN engages in regular dialogue with authorities to ensure transparency, accountability, and protection mainstreaming.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2020 draft, the overall shelter severity has increased across Syria, with a corresponding increase in PiN from 2019 by 20 per cent to 5.65 million people. Of this, 3.8 million (69 percent) are in areas of severity 4 or higher. Geographically, these areas are concentrated in Idleb, Aleppo, Rural Damascus, Raqqa city and camps in the north-east. Shelter needs are particularly acute in more densely populated urban areas, including in areas with high levels of returnees and high levels of damage and destruction of residential buildings, such as in Ar-Raqqa city). Al Hasakeh Governorate (Al Hassakeh sub-district) has one of the highest number of people in needs of shelter and NFI in targeted area (231,842). The targeted areas were selected in line with the Cluster prioritization. The sub-district which PIN plans to target have total of 495,600 in need of shelter assistance and 407,356 in need of NFI (HNO draft, 2020). IDPs, returnees and host populations in urban areas are often living in inadequate shelter, including unfinished buildings and in buildings that are not adequately protected from the elements. Private shelter and NFI needs are acute in all of the above contexts, whilst likewise, neighborhood services have in many cases have been damaged, destroyed, or become neglected as a result of the conflict. UXO contamination is likewise an issue of significant concern. Al Raqqa area remains a priority for private repair interventions and distribution of NFI and winterization support. Al-Hasakeh has absorbed a high concentration of new IDPs. Likewise, in Aleppo governorate, particularly areas in close proximity to the Turkish conflict and in areas where IDPs are fleeing from Idlib and Western Aleppo, risks of mass influxes of IDPs remain high and conflict-affected populations are very vulnerable.

5 - Implementation of activities

The proposed PIN’s response under Shelter/NFI sector will be implemented with 2 objectives: 1)Provide life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support - Provision of core and essential NFI items - New arrival & kitchen kits (16,800 ind) will be provided to IDP and returnee HHs who have not been able to travel with NFIs. Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs and shelter. Winterization kits/cash transfers (42,000 ind) will be provided to IDP and returnee households in pre-identiﬁed camps, informal settlements and housing units during the winter of 2020–2021. Cash handouts for winterization are particularly eﬃcient and effective since they empower people to make decisions regarding their priority needs; whilst reducing delivery time and costs. SoKs (9000 ind.) will provide thermal comfort and protection from the climate, as well as ensure privacy, safety, health, and enable essential household and livelihood activities to be undertaken. Provision of emergency shelter kits (15,000 ind) will address acute shelter needs in camps and informal settlements; both in response to ﬁrst phase displacement, and prolonged sub-standard shelter situation in camps and informal settlements. 2) Reinforce an enabling protection environment and community cohesion by improving housing and related community/public - Support to sustainably repair/rehabilitate. This will be achieved through 5,400 people assisted by rehabilitated damaged houses and 250,000 people assisted by repaired/rehabilitated community/public infrastructure and facilities. Selected beneﬁciaries will be supported with light and medium repairs of private houses, including support with HLP. Based on discussion with communities and LA, PIN will also rehabilitate public infrastructure (health clinics).

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

As per PIN’s protection mainstreaming strategy for 2019-2021, PIN carries out regular protection risk assessments, which feeds into the development of protection risk and mitigation matrices. The following major risks (R) and respective mitigation measures (MM) were identified with Impact (I) and Likelihood (L). Additionally, PIN will ensure constants monitoring of all the identified risks for which necessary resources will be allocated: 1)R: Most vulnerable are not selected for assistance, tensions between IDPs/Host (I/L: H) 1)MM: needs based targeting criteria shared transparently with the communities; use of Washington groups Questions (WGQs), FGDs with representatives from a cross-section of community, community sensitization, CFRM, 2)R: inter-groups tensions over assistance (I/L: high); 2)MM: Community stakeholder meetings, harmonization of the packages across partners, Provision of assistance to host communities and IDPs. 3)R:PSEA, Abuse of authority by distribution actors (I/L: H/H)3)MM: Trainings of all staff on CoC; Gender balanced teams, PDM contains protection related questions, Clear process to swiftly address PSEA case, Signs are posted throughout project sites stating that assistance is free. 4)R: Distribution sites are not accessible/safe (I/L, M-H/high) 4)MM: Internal distribution procedures in line with Sphere, collaboration with Security teams for timing and place of distributions, vulnerable people are fast-tracked; bypassing queues, distribution sites with sheltered waiting areas, with seating (if possible); Different queues for men/women, PIN home delivers assistance to beneficiaries with mobility/other difficulties if needed; kits are grouped to lighter boxes to facilitate easier transportation. 5)R: Shelter infrastructure is not accessible to all/safe (I/L, M-H/M) 5)MM: Structural assessment of buildings by qualified personnel, accessibility, support to HLP for private owners.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

(A1/R1): The security situation in all target areas remains conducive to implementation; (MM1): Suspend/relocate activities to areas where access is upheld; and/or switch to remote management, potentially through pre-identiﬁed local partners. (A2/R2) PIN’s staff, oﬃces and warehouses in Syria are not subjected to targeting/abductions/raiding by armed actors. (MM3): rigorous security monitoring and management mechanism. If speciﬁed risks occurred, PIN would temporarily suspend activities; carry out internal/external investigations; advocate for dialogue with relevant actors, inform relevant counter-terrorism agencies/authorities/partners of the incident; and seek to relocate the intervention ;(A3/R3): LCs/others continue to collaborate in line with principles. (MM3): Temporarily suspend activities; engage in dialogue to re-sensitize on principles of engagement. If such action fails, PIN would relocate resources to other target areas.(A4/R4): Armed actors present in areas of operation facilitate safe and principled humanitarian access; (MM4): Suspend operations, engage in advocacy with local authorities and armed actors (jointly with others, including UNOCHA, NGO forums, and/or Access Working Groups) in an effort to restore free and unhindered access. If such efforts were unsuccessful, relocate activities to other target areas facing acute needs; (A5/R5): Local markets remain functional across target areas; (MM5): If border crossings to Syria were closed/restricted or PIN was prevented from transferring goods, PIN would suspend activities; advocate with UNOCHA/local authorities to facilitate access; purchase locally if possible; and shift funds to Cash-based programming. (A6/R6): PIN is able to retain registration in respective countries; (MM6): If PIN’s registration or visas in cross-border response hubs, PIN would adopt a phased approaches to withdrawal.
