Project Summary

The goal of this project is to provide emergency shelter and related non-food items for the IDPs in Al Amal, Al Forkan, Ahl Wafaa, and Al Bayan located in Harim and Al Teh Camp in Ma’arrat Misrin district of Idlib. This will benefit approximately 10,000 individuals (1,800 HHs) in total in the targeted 5 camps.
However, this will exceed the current overall budget; we will therefore for now address only 2 camps, Al Bayan and Al Teh, total target of 7,267 individuals (1,294 HHs). Subject to funding, Olive Branch can scale up to reach approximately 10,000 individuals (1,800 HHs) in total in future by including Al Forkan, Ahl Wafaa and Al Bayan camps.
According HNAP’s MNM report of January 2020, Dana sub-district witnessed 63,735 IDPs moving in.
Project will be in 4 phases, with 2 main activity categories – Activity1.1-Core/Essential NFI and Activity1.2-Seasonal/Supplementary NFI. There will be one distribution in each phase comprising of different items. Core/Essential NFIs will be distributed in phase 1, 2,3 and 4, while Seasonal/Supplementary NFIs will be added in phase 4 only.
Outcomes are in four domains - camping stability, family preservation, adult well-being, and child well-being. The outcomes capture intermediate and longer-term results rather than the experiences of families while they were receiving the shelter aid. Impact of the project can be seen in: Living conditions improved for 7,267 IDPs; Increased chances for families to move to more settled areas in or outside current locations; Improved well-being of the most vulnerable, children, infants, disabled, elderly; and Increased number of families staying together thus protection for children.
Olive Branch can deliver this project on an estimated budget of 567,944 USD over 12 months with 6 field workers to coordinate and manage operation with Management support from HQ in Gaziantep/Turkey.
This project will provide NFI provisions to the residents of the targeted camps in Idlib province. The data breakdown as per the ISIMM report of January 2020 for all 5 camps are as follow:
Al Amal: 6,105 Individuals, 1,100 HHs, 1,860 Girls, 1,493 Boys, 1,552 Women, 1,200 Men
Al Forkan: 601 I, 108 HHs, 166 G, 194 B, 127 W, 114 M
Ahl Wafaa: 533 I, 94 HHs, 167 G, 188 B, 103 W , 75 M
Al Bayan: 1,438 I, 226 HHs, 408 G, 432 B, 330 W, 268 M
Al Teh: 1,162 I, 194 HHs, 387 G, 322 B, 242 W, 211 M.
In the current plan, we are targeting only Al Amal and Alteh camps.
The project is designed to benefit all population of the camps including women and girls. The aid will be provided to all households without discrimination. For the older and persons with a disability, the aid will be taken to their place of accommodation. The project should reduce the risk of vulnerable women and girls from being exploited in anyway due to lack of basic life-saving aid.
Basic needs will reduce domestic violence within the family and tension within the camp. Women will be able to stay safe in their own tents and feel less vulnerable. In general, OB will ensure that there is sufficient monitoring conducted by OB to avoid any kind of harm done to the beneficiaries in the distribution. Proper measures will be in place to ensure there is no abuse of power by any members of the implementation team, the volunteers or the IDPs themselves. OB will involve the women participants in the operation to decrease the protection risks in dealing with gender sensitive matters. Also, there is no risk of tension between inter-IDPs or with host communities as all beneficiaries are IDPs and there is no host community involved. OB also have a strict PSEA and CP code of conduct for their staff to adhere to in the course of the operation.

4 - Needs

According to the 2020 HNO, with the crisis in its eighth year, staggering levels of need persist for people throughout Syria. Whilst there has been a reduction in violence in many parts of the country over the past year, an estimated 11.7 million people require multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance. Amidst ongoing hostilities and significant protection needs, the population continues to look for safety. According HNAP’s MNM report of Jan/2020, Dana sub-district witnessed 63,735 IDPs moving in. In Maaret Tamsrin, 38,375 IDPs have moved in the month of January 2020 alone. Idlib subdistrict has seen 28,852 IDPs influx so far. Dana, Maaret Tamsrin, and Idlib feature in the Top 5 sub-districts experiencing new arrivals recently. Of the IDPs, 58% are in inadequate housing; 43% of whom are in emergency shelter. The project will begin with a reassessment activity to confirm the needs. Olive Branch plans to reach approximately 10,000 individuals (1,800 HHs) in 2020 as per the current IDPs in Al Amal, Al Forkan, Al Wafaa, Al Bayan and Al Teh camps. However, the current estimated budget will cover 7,267 individuals (1,294 HHs) form Al Amal and Al Teh camps. According to our own assessment conducted in September 2019, Al Teh camp is relatively new and 1,162 IDPs are currently taking shelter there. This camp is not covered by any NGO and is desperate need of basic items. The camp is too isolated for the residents to reach the nearest shops; Olive Branch will therefore arrange to bring the goods to the camp. 90% of the communities report Female-Headed Households as the top vulnerability. The project will take into consideration their protection needs as well as participation in the implementation to address the gender sensitive issues. Project will include women participants to volunteer in distributing to women headed households.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will distribute core/essential NFIs (Activity 1.1) and seasonal/supplementaryNFIs (Activity 1.2) over the course of the project which is divided into 4 phases, JAN-FEB-MAR, MAY-JUN, JUL-AUG-SEP, and OCT-NOV-DEC. They are further subdivided into sub-activities as follows: PH-1: Activity 1.1.1: Distribution of Mattresses, Winter Blankets, Plastic sheets, Jerry Cans, Pillows, Floor Mats. PH-2: Activity 1.1.2: Distribution of Summer Blankets. PH-3: Activity 1.1.3: Distribution of Kitchen Sets and Solar Lamps. PH-4: Activity 1.1.4: Distribution of Winter Blankets, Plastic Sheets, Jerry Cans, and Floor Mats. & Activity 1.2.1: Winter Clothing and Heating Fuel. The NFI materials will be provided to 7,267 IDPs who are living in Al Amal and Al Teh Camps through a voucher/coupon mechanism that the recipients can use according to their requirements. Each HHs will be issued with one coupon every quarter of the year starting from January 2020. Depending on the composition of items, the value of the voucher varies for each round, 1st round: $301.75, 2nd Round $15, 3rd round $32, and 4th round $189.25. Al Amal camp is located within reasonable reach from local supplier outlets and the camp residents can reach them and use their voucher (coupon) to purchase the items of their needs from the prescribed list. Given the different accessibility challenges of the camps, Olive Branch will deploy separate approaches for Al Amal and Al Teh camps. For the Al Teh camp, the aid will be brought into the camp and distributed to the residents according to the requirements. Al Teh camp is not covered by any NGO and is desperate need of basic and winterization items. The project will begin with a re-assessment activity to confirm the needs. This will be followed by the main activity which is the distribution of the NFI items at start of each quarter of 2020. Post distribution assessment will be carried out by Olive Branch team to ensure all documentations are complete.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

By following the cluster guidelines, the project will address potential protection risks with mitigating measures appropriate to the intervention modality of this project. Oliver Branch assesses the risks to be in following areas- A: Inter-IDPs tension due to varied selection of items – Med/MedSince the aid is provided through cash/voucher approach, and all HHs getting the same amount of aid, and the content is based on cluster’s guidelines and standards. B: Tension between Host and IDPs – Low/MedThe camps are sufficiently distanced from the nearest host communities.C: Abuse of authority by distribution actors – High/HighOlive Branch applies Code of Conduct policy/procedure on all staff along with complaint mechanism to prevent abuse. D: Risk of Physical Harm distribution points – Low/LowIntervention is based on cash voucher hence greatly minimises such a risk E: Risk to humanitarian nature of the intervention by armed actors – Med/HighStrong coordination with local civil authorities, camp management, and community actors to prevent interference by armed actors.F: Lack of documentation may exclude certain IDPs – Med/MedClose coordination with Camp management authority to ensure no IDPs are excluded. Alternative documentation will also be considered depending on the camp policy and processes. Reconciliation will be done between data provided by the camp management and our own assessment from data collection/analysis. G: Cash Voucher mechanism can entice corruption – Med/High Staff will be trained on our Code of conduct and anti-fraud policy to ensure no corruptive practices occur. For accountability and transparency, M&E will be responsible to monitor and document at all stages of the operation, in particular, from the supplier end as well as the beneficiary end to ensure the quality, quantity, and efficiency, in order to avoid risk of corruption. OB also have a strict PSEA and CP code of conduct for their staff to adhere to.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Olive Branch have assessed the risks to the proposed intervention and can be summarised in terms of Operation, Financial, Security in addition to Protection discussed in the previous section. In terms of security risks, we estimate this to be low in that the district is sufficiently close to the Turkish border and is considered safer with easy access to the border and markets. However, the situation can change if the current escalations continue for next six months, in which case there is likelihood of the IDPs moving into Afrin and Euphrates Shield. Should this happen we would move our operation to these areas since we already have capacity on the ground to operate directly. OB will also coordinate with the local civilian authorities and the camp management to avoid any kind of interference by any armed groups in our operation. Financial risks are low as we have a well-established financial control, procurement and anti-fraud policy to ensure the duties are sufficiently segregated for the benefit of transparency, control and avoidance of any frauds. Since the materials will be sourced in the relatively stable local markets or across the border if required, there is low risk of impact from inflation and price fluctuations. In terms of disruption to our operation due to shortage of required items, we estimate the risk to be low in that the required items and tools are available in the market. In addition, the risk is minimised by having more than one supplier for the items.Distributions will be supervised so as not to cause any harm or tension. The project team will organise a schedule considering the place, time, items and manner of distribution in advance. The time-table will be made known to the residents in advance. For safety and well-being of the vulnerable beneficiaries, distribution points will be tented and guarded.
