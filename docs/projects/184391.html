Project Summary

Explosive weapons pose one of the most prevalent challenges to security and stabilization in South-Central Somalia. The proliferation of explosive remnants of war (ERW), improvised explosive devices (IED), and mines not only threatens lives in Somalia but also limits access to basic services and livelihoods/economic opportunities for most of the population. As a result, explosive contamination represents a serious impediment to stability, security, and ultimately recovery and development. Furthermore, EO causes loss of life and damage to the property creating a climate of fear within which effective humanitarian operations can be prevented. The presence of EO and unsecured Small Arms and Light weapons SALW poses threats to the lives of IDPs, host communities, and returnees, and acts as a source of instability through providing components to build improvised devices and resources for armed belligerents. On an almost daily basis, disparate insurgent elements terrorize Somali communities through the use of IEDs on city streets, targeting AMISOM security forces as well as Somali civilians, which results in deaths and injuries.
Al Shabaab has reoriented its campaign towards asymmetric warfare, with increased use of IED against the military. Flood-affected communities and IDP are highly vulnerable to EO threat: they can encounter IEDs along the roads when intending to return, be exposed to attacks targeting AMISOM and SNA, but also due to the presence of EO contamination in their areas of origin.
