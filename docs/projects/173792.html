Project Summary

Mercy Corps with funding from Bureau of Humanitarian Assistance (BHA) plans to implement a multisector humanitarian response to Address Diverse and Acute Primary Threats (ADAPT) to human security in North Eastern Nigeria. ADAPT responds to the urgent humanitarian needs of 129,584 displaced, returnee and host households living in 5 LGAs i.e. Damboa, Dikwa, Ngala, Gwoza and Bama LGAs and covers essential humanitarian needs across a range of essential sectors, including Food-Assistance, Nutrition, Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance, WASH, Shelter and Settlements and Protection.
The nutrition response supports both nutrition specific and sensitive activities that contribute to prevention, detection and management of malnutrition. It builds on achievements in the current USAID funded Food for Peace and OFDA programs. It supports continuity, scale up best practices, filling existing gaps in CMAM services as well as ensure an integrated holistic nutrition approach through mainstreamed agriculture, WASH and protection activities. The planned activities include: a). Promotion and support of appropriate Maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition in Emergency through use of multi-pronged approaches that foster contextually appropriate and sustained social and behavioural change for improved nutrition. This includes establishment of support spaces for IYCF and peer to peer support groups (Mother to Mother, Father to Father and adolescent girls’ groups) to serve as enabling environments for MIYCN protection, promotion, counselling and education. Mercy Corps will also support CNMs to sensitize target participants on integrated nutrition, health & WASH and as well disseminate high quality nutrition information through mass platforms. b). Provision of supplemental nutrition assistance to facilitate regular consumption of diversified nutritious diets by Young Children, pregnant and lactating women. This includes distributing fresh food vouchers (FFV) to increase access to vegetables, fruits and animal source protein food items and also supporting kitchen/ backyard gardening and small-scale poultry production to foster long-term diet diversification. In addition, Mercy Corps will provide supplemental food assistance to children 24-59months, pregnant and lactating women with Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) to ensure that malnourished participants living in chronically food-insecure Households meet the required caloric and protein intake. c). Lastly, Mercy Corps in coordination with the Nutrition Sector Technical Working Group will support Outpatient Therapeutic Programs (OTPs) to fill existing gaps, strengthen community level nutrition surveillance through enhanced nutrition screening, referral and follow-up of acute malnourished children by CMNs and also support surveys/ assessments to evaluate program contribution to reducing prevalence of malnutrition and coverage of CMAM programming.

1 - Needs

The nutrition and food security assessment, round 8 indicated GAM levels based on Weight for Height (WFH) at 8.1% (Medium Severity/Poor) for Borno State; however, the situation was serious (10.1%) in Eastern Borno domain, which includes Damboa, Bama, Dikwa, Ngala and Gwoza LGAs. These LGAs are also projected to be in a serious nutrition situation (Phase 3) in the Acute Malnutrition Integrated Phase Classification (IPC) recently conducted. The survey also showed inadequate dietary intake as evidenced by extremely low minimum acceptable diets; only 0.5% of children 6-23 months had a minimally acceptable diet in Borno. Minimum meal frequency and minimum dietary diversity were also poor. Exclusive breastfeeding was low at 45.6% and similarly, the practice of early initiation and sustained breastfeeding up to two years was low. Only 34.1% of women of reproductive age met minimum dietary diversity requirements and consumption of iron and vitamin A rich food was as low as 21.0% in some areas. Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) practices were equally poor which affects food safety and utilization. However, these were projections/ assessments conducted before COVID-19 pandemic, the pandemic restrictions are likely to have further aggravated the situation i.e. increased food insecurity and malnutrition. A final evaluation assessment conducted under the Mercy Corps FFP funded program in Damboa estimated practice of exclusive breastfeeding of children under six months at 63.7%, Minimum Acceptable Diet (MAD) among children 6-23 months at 11.6%, and Minimum Dietary Diversity among women at 31.1%. These figures suggest considerable progress when compared to the program’s baseline, however, there is still much scope for improvement. For example, it was concerning that less than one mother and child pair in every 10 (9.38%) met both measures of food security in the same HH at the same time. Furthermore, whilst knowledge on exclusive breastfeeding increased by 46.4% and practice reportedly increased from 58.6% to 63.7%, knowledge of other key aspects of IYCF were still relatively poor. For instance, only 53.6% of mothers/caregivers fully understood the term “exclusive breastfeeding”, casting some doubt on the veracity of the 63.7% EBF rate. Finally, it was also noted that among mothers/caregivers of children 0–23 months only 7.2% knew at least two critical times for handwashing, “after defecation” being one of them. Based on these findings, there is a need for continued effort to promote and foster adoption of recommended practices. Additionally, current program data on community nutrition screening, referral and follow-up shows that some malnourished cases identified and referred are lost in referral due to limited coverage of Outpatient Therapeutic Program OTP services. Specific to Gwoza LGA, the Nutrition in Emergency Technical Working Group (TWG) projects gaps in CMAM services in 2021 following reports that Medecins Sans Frontiers (MSF) – a key partner providing SAM treatment – plans to scale down its nutrition activities and this is likely to further worsen the already difficult situation in most areas of Gwoza/Pulka.

2 - Activities

Promote and support MIYCN including counselling to ensure good nutrition status among women, infants and young children through various SBC strategies that foster nutrition-related behavior change, especially among women of childbearing age, adolescent girls, and children. This will include: supporting Community Nutrition Monitors to provide key multi-sector SBC (integrated Health, Nutrition and WASH) i.e. support recruitment of men and women, their trainings, equip them with IEC materials, data collection and reporting tools and also provide monthly stipend. Mercy Corps will establish supportive Spaces for IYCF-E, Peer to Peer Support groups (MtMSG, FtFSGs and Adolescent girl groups) as enabling environments for MIYCN promotion, counselling, education and CMAM service promotion. Additionally, to ensure linkage of MIYCN activities with on-going SAM and MAM treatment services, following the “Family MUAC approach” the project will build the capacity of mothers and other caregivers on nutrition screening to enhance their knowledge of acute malnutrition, facilitate timely case identification, referral and improved coverage of treatment programs. Mercy Corps will also support immediate and long term sustainable approaches that facilitate increased availability, access and regular consumption of diversified nutritious diets by Young Children, pregnant and lactating women. For example, distribution of fresh food vouchers to households with children U2 and pregnant women and supporting participants to start up simple SMART agronomy and livelihood techniques i.e. equip households with tools and skill-building on agronomy to facilitate kitchen/ backyard gardening and small-scale poultry production. Mercy Corps will also provide supplemental food assistance to children 6-59months with Moderate Acute Malnutrition. The program will also support and strengthen nutrition surveillance systems and gap filling for CMAM services through support CNMs to conduct nutrition screening, referral and follow-up of acute malnourished children, support transportation of Identified SAM & MAM Children to CMAM Centers in some LGAs to further improve coverage and access. Mercy Corps will also support establishment of Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) centers in Gwoza LGA to respond to the current/ expected CMAM service gaps. lastly, to evaluate the effect of activities on the prevalence of malnutrition and coverage of CMAM, Mercy Corps in collaboration with the Nutrition Sector will support SMART survey and SQUEAC assessments in targeted LGAs.
