Project Summary

It is estimated that 6,023,576 South Sudanese women, men, boys and girls face multiple protection threats and vulnerabilities resulting from active conflicts, displacement, and breakdown of community protection systems and structures exacerbated by economic decline, lack of basic services and disease burden (Protection Cluster HRP 2019 Strategy). 192,574 of the people in need of protection assistance are from Western Equatoria (Nagero and Tambura) and Lakes (Awerial and Yirol East). While 65% of these are IDPs, 61% of the affected people are children. Crises affected children are exposed to serious threats and risks including separation from families, psychosocial distress, armed recruitment and sexual and physical violence. Without appropriate care and family-based support, affected children are forced to process their conflict experience alone, which may prevent them from being able to fully engage in daily activities such as playing, learning, or basic social interaction. Of the children in need, 16000 children and their caregivers (7000 girls, 6500 boys, 1500 women and 1000 men) will be targeted for integrated life-saving child protection in emergency services in Nagero, Tambura, Awerial and Yirol East in 2019.
Nagero experienced fighting in April 2018 and populations have been displaced to Tambura. Mingkaman IDP camp in Awerial hosts the largest caseload of IDP in the country. Military forces clashed with civilians in Rumbek causing a protection crisis in Yirol East in May 2018. In all these contexts, children have been severely affected and the poor child protection indicators include high caseload of separated and unaccompanied children (UASC), lack of psychosocial, recreational and learning opportunities for affected children, precarious incidents of sexual and physical violence against (SGBV) children and adolescents, use of children by armed forces and armed groups (CAAFAG), and increase in child marriages and child exploitation.

To ensure that boys and girls of high protection risks receive critical child protection services, CINA will provide comprehensive case management including for separated and unaccompanied children, children associated with armed forces/groups, child survivors of SGBV, severe psychosocial distress and children in conflict with law. CINA will support caregivers and caseworkers to provide the critical protection care and structured psychosocial support services through emergency CFS, PSS in the community and in schools through support to teachers/PTAs, CP helpdesks and life skills and resilience services for adolescents. In order to increase access to, and strengthen resilience to well-coordinated age and gender-sensitive child protection and preventive services, CINA will conduct child protection risk awareness sessions, CP campaigns and provide support to child protection structures and adolescent peer to peer groups to prevent and respond to violence against children.
To ensure centrality of protection, this project will address risks facing UASC, OVC, CAAFAG, child headed households, adolescent girls, and children living with disabilities, etc. through strengthened community safety audits, community protection mechanisms, etc. CINA’s staff will sign and adhere to code of conduct and CP policy to ensure safety, dignity and avoid causing harm. The team will conduct community and caregivers’ consultations on sites for CFS, etc and conduct monthly beneficiary feedback mechanisms on quality of CP services delivered by CINA as a means for Accountability to Affected Populations. Adolescents and children with disabilities will be particularly empowered with self-protection and life skills and involved to ensure meaningful participation for all children. CINA is currently implementing CP activities in Tambura and Awerial. To avoid overlapping in Tambura, CINA has agreed with WV for CINA to focus its CP activities in Nagero, Mapoi and Jerusalem areas in Tambura town.

Needs

Nagero experienced a heavy fighting in April 2018 which led to displacement of about 26,000 persons, some fled to Namutina and others to Tambura. 18,500 of the displaced persons settled in Mabia 15km East of Tambura town and about 8,000 settled in Namutina 30km West of Tambura town (Nagero and Tambura IRNA reports June 2018). There are respectively 12,888 and 1,539 IDPs settled in Tambura and Nagero as of September 2018. Mingkaman in Awerial remains the largest IDP camp in the country hosting 102,413 IDPs. Inter-communal violence in Yolakot and Kalthoc in March 2018 increased the IDP burden, straining the already limited protection services for the displaced children in Mingkaman. Government’s soldiers in Rumbek clashed with civilians in May 2018 resulting into a protection crisis which displaced about 8,750 IDPs to Yirol East currently settled in the areas of Pagarau, Nyang, Adior, and Billing, in a mix of cattle camps and host community villages (IPMT Report Yirol East 1 September 2018). As a result of this military offensive, a number of people was killed, women raped and 13 children abducted and 5 children got drowned in the river. It was also reported that many who fled to the bushes during the attack were killed by wild animals. CINA’s FTR caseload for September 2018 shows 303 UASC awaiting FTR case management (95 UASC and other vulnerable children from Tambura and Nagero), 120 UASC and OVC from Yirol East and 88 UASC from Awerial). SGBV incidents against adolescent girls are being reported in Nagero and Yirol East and survivors lack referral services. Boys in particular have been seen caring guns and wearing military uniforms in Nagero and Namutina. Conflict affected children suffer loss, fear, panic and anxiety and lack recreational and learning facilities. Family and community support systems have been devastated and harmful cultural practices such as child marriages child abduction and violence against children have heightened. While child protection programming is usually not funded/ severely underfunded, it is worth noting that South Sudanese children face one of the worst humanitarian child protection crises and therefore their needs cannot wait.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

The project will provide awareness sessions on the availability of child protection services within the community, particularly through establishment of CFS as entry points. Awareness activities on prevention and response to child protection issues by the community members will be presented on the banners, leaflets, and posters and displayed in the CFS, schools, churches, and during community gatherings. Preventive messages will be passed through local radios (especially in Mingkaman). Community volunteers will be trained to relay specific messages in local languages to children and their caregivers and entire community.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Specific messages on prevention of family separation support to child survivors’ referral services, dangers of child recruitment, child marriages and child abduction practices, and prevention of psychosocial distress will be produced/printed and widely disseminated to boys, girls and their caregivers. Messages on where boys and girls can find protection assistance will be provided through the CP helpdesks. How and where to report grave rights abuses and violence against children occurring within the community.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

CINA staff and volunteers will be trained to ensure adherence to IASC PSEA guidelines throughout the project cycle. Each will sign a Code of Conduct. All CINA staffs are subjected to enhanced reference checks up on taking job offer. A commitment statement on PSEA is found on CINA’s job adverts. CINA will ensure that staff are trained and uphold humanitarian principles including, of neutrality, impartiality and participation, and where feasible, CINA team will remain diverse as much as possible to achieve this.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

CINA is currently implementing child protection activities in Awerial and Tambura. CINA also participated in the IPMT response to Yirol East in September 2018. During the situation and response monitoring and IPMT missions within these communities, community members have been consulted on their perceptions on prevailing child protection issues, particularly in Yirol East. Women and men participated as KIIs and adolescent boys and groups were involved via Focus Group Discussions, as well as caregivers of UASC. In particular, child-headed households in Yirol East and Awerial were much involved in the FGDs.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Beneficiary Complaint mechanisms will be established at the community level and feedback will be collected using a form, complaint box or hotline number. Banners on SEA awareness and reporting will also be displayed at the CFS sites and in the schools. In particular, CP desks in the schools will be used to receive feedback from community members

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project will involve community members during setting up and design of CFS centres for children. Community members will be tasked to check the relevance of CFS activities to their children. In particular, they will interact with CFS animators on specific PSS activities, including sensitive CP issues according to context. Caregivers will be involved in PSS activities to check that they gain the necessary parenting skills and support other children. Adolescent peer groups will be established to support other children and community members will monitor effectiveness of peer support activities.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project activities will be implemented in line with Child Protection Minimum Standards. Specific needs of boys, girls and adolescents will be considered, including needs of 0-5, 6-12, 13-18. The project will ensure that UASC boys and girls and other vulnerable children are supported based on vulnerability criteria. Information for and about the children will be kept confidential, and shared on need-to-know basis when password-protected. PSS needs of children rescued from armed forces and groups will be considered and ensure that they are included in socio-economic reintegration activities. Needs of child survivors of SGBV and other harmful cultural practices will be well coordinated, to ensure that they get urgent and appropriate case management services.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project will ensure that children living with disabilities, children living with HIV/AIDS, children living and working on the streets, and child-mothers and children in conflict with law, etc are identified and included to benefit from CP services. In particular, awareness raising messages will be disseminated to ensure that no children are left behind from attending CFS activities. Adolescent girls will be targeted and included as well.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project will monitor and respond to any violence, coercion, exploitation, deprivation or neglect against children, particularly to UASC, child survivors of SGBV, CAAFAG, child-mothers, children living with disabilities, children living and working on the streets and children in conflict with law, and to ensure that all children are considered equal, the animators will be supported/mentored on this.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project will consider monthly situation and response monitoring to check whether children have been accessing child protection services especially through CFS monitoring, FGDs with caregivers and community based child protection committees. CFS will be established and will be monitored to ensure that it meets minimum standards including being in locations that are safe, have shades, not near markets and within the closest open space within the community, that can be accessed by children.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

CFS activities will be monitored regularly to ensure that the location is safe for children in terms of security, the surrounding is clean, free from flooding, have toilets, drinking water and, shelter and a big shade. Where applicable, security will be provided during the CFS activities. Overcrowding will be avoided, and a CFS will have a maximum of 200 children per day.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

The project will monitor that the animators, volunteers, staff and clients respect children’s dignity by not sharing information about children or taking children’s photos without their consent. In particular, children will not be exposed physically or emotionally. Toilets will be ensured in the CFS sites, marked separately for female and males. Female volunteers and staff will be recruited to ensure that sensitive concerns of girl’ child are kept confidential, when they chose to share.
