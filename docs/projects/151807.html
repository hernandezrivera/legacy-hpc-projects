Project Summary

The vision of IRC Libya is that people whose lives and livelihoods have been impacted by conflict and political turmoil are safe from harm, and able to access necessary services in the face of ongoing instability. This includes IDPs, returnees, and vulnerable non-displaced Libyans, as well as refugees and migrants who, while fleeing from conflict or economic deprivation, have found themselves in a similarly unstable environment. To drive progress towards this, the core focus of IRC Libya’s programming will be an integrated health and protection approach to improve the health and safety outcomes of all populations served. The intervention will mainly target women and children through the provision of specialized services (case management and PSS activities for women and children Libyans and non Libyans) based on current lessons learnt by promoting community based approaches (already established integrated outreach activities and existing community led activities which will facilitate dissemination of essential information on available services, risk reduction measures, support for referrals to specialized services, improving the protection environment and the well being of target groups in three Mantikas - Tripoli, Misrata and Sirt.

Needs

Humanitarian conditions in Libya have further deteriorated as the ongoing disorder and fragmented political situation in Libya weight heavily on the country stability and causes collapsing of infrastructure impacting Libyan and the other people living in the country (non Libyan nationals). Nevertheless, Libya continues to be both a transit and destination country for migrants in their way to reach Europe. Base on the Protection sector PIN by mantika 2019, specifically in Misrata (with 4,390 girls, 7,276 boys and about 7,898 women) Sirt (with 2,180 girls, 2,756 boy and about 1,576 women) and Tripoli (with 10,534 girls, 16,109 boys and more than 8.000 women) where IRC implements its program there is an high number of women and children in need of safe and qualified protection services. GBV and Child Protection problems have been raised as a big concern for effected people as mentioned by the HNO 2019; while a majority of women and men reject violence against women as a norm, yet it is acceptable for a husband to abuse his spouse because of mobility outside of the home without permission, disobedience of a husband’s wishes, neglect of children, participation in political activities and refusal to have sex. Social norms condone violence against women. Lack of quality services, collapse of the judicial system and absence of rule of law women and girls are even more unlikely to report crimes of GBV. Migrants woman and girls also face specific risks of being targeting by criminal gangs conducting human trafficking and have specific vulnerabilities linked to their status and, in some instances, nationality and race. The protracted armed conflict, multiple displacements, economic crisis and widespread violence take a heavy toll on children’s lives too. Not only do children suffer from the direct consequences of war and armed violence, but they are also indirectly affected by displacement, worsening of economic conditions, weak referral system, their psychological distress as a result of witnessing violence in the house and in the community, but also as a result of their caregivers stress. Based on a service mapping exercise in Misrata and Sirte and Protection monitoring visits conducted by IRC since July 2018 the main gaps and needs were identified as follow: - very limited number of safe and confidential spaces (health facilities, children's spaces) with appropriately trained case workers to provide case management, PSS and referral services; - low capacity of service providers, local authorities and other key stakeholders with respect to IDP's and refugees/migrants rights, GBV guiding principles and child protection approaches, etc. - Inadequate Community based protection mechanisms to be able to reach women and children migrants combined with prejudice and misconceptions of the hosting community.

Activities

IRC will adopt a wider mixed approach (Protection/health) that covers all target populations project and will allow to respond to unpredictable escalations of violence in the wider community, whilst retaining the capacity to intervene where specifically women and children are particularly exposed to risk. The main activities of the project are the following: A. Women-centered activities 1. GBV case management and referrals provide to women and girls through 3 women and children safe spaces (existing spaces will be prioritized such as health facilities where IRC provides Clinical Care of Sexual Assault Survivors (CCSAS) services and by supporting women group organization). IRC will adopt the Women and Protection Empowerment Model. This will also include Caring for Child Survivors (boys and girls). (Objective 2, Indicator 2)2. Women, girls and GBV survivors will have access to group PSS activities, including recreational and skills development activities at safe spaces; (objective 2, indicator 1) 3. Distribution of cultural sensitive dignity kits to women and girls in need through case management or safety audit assessments (Objective 2).B. Child Protection: 1. In suitable health facilities in Tripoli, Misrata and Sirt where IRC is providing Primary health care designated areas will be equipped for children to provide PSS group activities by adapting the IRC safe healing and learning space approach (SHLS) and other psychosocial activities to the Libyan context. IRC psycho social kits will be also distributed to children based on their age, during PSS group activities or through the outreach activities. Psychosocial kits are designed to give children the opportunity to express their feelings and emotions through drawing and play. (objective 2, indicator 1) 2. Brief parenting tips sessions for parents/caregivers coming to the health clinics while their children are engaged in recreation activities will also be organised, adapting the IRC "Families make the difference" modules. (objective 2, indicator 1). The activities 1 and 2 will be implemented where there is referral system in place (referral to CESVI, HI and other NGOs) and CP case management will be included just for cases identified through activities 1 and 2. C. GBV/child protection: Community events will be organized to foster social cohesion such as child rights day, 16 days of activism, etc with the engagement of community focal points/authorities and through existing safe spaces for mothers and children. D. CSOs, service providers and Ministry of Social Affairs female staff train on GBV core concepts, GBV risk reduction/mitigation, and other related topics. (Objective 3, indicators 1, 2). E. In Misrata and Sirte Mobile protection outreach team (2 team of 4 members), trained on emergency contingency plans through protection monitoring missions will be able to provide life-saving services including distribution of emergency kits, dignity kits for women, psycho-social kits for children and urgent confidential referral. (Based on vulnerability criteria)IRC Protection mobile teams (jointly with the community health workers) will facilitate information dissemination based on the needs of the target groups (legal documentation, GBV/CP responsive services), will ensure emergency referrals (for children too) in a sensitive and appropriate way. Existing Migrants focal points will be engaged in capacity building on Community-based protection follow IRC developed and field-tested CBP guidance, to ensure adherence to Do No Harm, conflict sensitivity and other key principles.Conflict sensitivity analysis exercise will generate findings and best practices to inform social cohesion initiatives and advocacy actions towards women and children especially migrants living in urban areas at risk of violence, abuse and exploitation. While developing referral pathways, IRC will make sure the process will be through GBV WG and will adhere to GBV case management approach.
