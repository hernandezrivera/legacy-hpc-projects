Project Summary

Ongoing armed conflicts and insecurity, with recurring climate shocks, has resulted in a protracted economic vulnerability and internal displacements across Somalia.Somalis are vulnerable to various shocks such as drought and flood that contribute to poverty and displacement. The Center for Global Development places Somalia at the top of the list of 167 countries for overall vulnerability to climate change adjusted for coping capacity.
The Cyclical climatic shocks and ongoing conflict are challenging traditional rural livelihoods and causing
growing number of urban poor and internally displaced people in Somalia. Displacements due to armed
conflict and political tensions, drought, and lack of livelihoods are expected to continue in 2022, further
exacerbating food insecurity in many areas.
According to FSNAU,out of the total 3.5 million people in need of urgent food assistance, 640,000 people face significant food consumption gaps. In addition, 3.7 million people are classified in IPC phase 2 (Stressed), engaging in negative coping strategies to meet their food and non-food needs, bringing the total number of Somalis experiencing acute food insecurity to 7.2 million by late 2021.
Furthermore,COVID-19 pandemic and related containment measures seem to have affected the urban poor population more than rural poor population. As a result, the urban poor face moderate to large food consumption gaps, partly due to a slowdown in economic activities in urban areas and the rising costs of food and other essential non-food items.
World Vision proposes a multi pronged interventions to reverse the current trajectory at which Somalia is going.The interventions would include immediate access to food needs by vulnerable households facing hunger and starvation, improving livelihoods and creating opportunities for diversified livelihoods by protecting key livelihood assets .
The proposed project intends to address the food insecurity situation, loss of livelihood strategies, poor HH and community livelihood assets, as well as minimum interventions integrated to address the environmental degradation on which most livelihoods hinge upon.
These activities will target IDPs, pastoral and agro-pastoral communities across Somalia, in districts where World Vision operates in.

1. - Projects activities

Cash Transfers: The project will support vulnerable rural and IDP communities through short-term income support. This will be through multipurpose cash assistance to the most vulnerable persons in the community. A community-based targeting using agreed upon selection criteria will reduce potential conflicts through transparency and collective ownership of the list of program participants selected. Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance allow program participants to get the required food and non-food items needed faster. Each of the targeted 4000HH will receive cash transfer of approximately $70/month(considered average for all regions WV operates) for 3 months based on CWG recommendation on minimum expenditure basket (MEB) for every region which is done on a quarterly basis based on Somalia's standard minimum expenditure basket (MEB).The project will also engage 2000 HHs identified by the project and with at least one able-bodied member in cash for work (CFW) activities for 3 month. Cash for work mechanisms will be to rehabilitate and build key livelihood assets, will kick start the local economy and livelihoods of poor households while laying a foundation for longer term food security and environmental resilience. By targeting women for cash for work and training support, the project supports women’s economic empowerment and increases the financial, social and economic resilience of households. Program participant selection will be conducted after the selection criteria is agreed on among communities and is implemented as part of the HH vulnerability assessment that is delivered through a participatory process with the community. Considering participants may need to engage in other livelihood activities and/ or need to attend to their HHs’ needs, especially for women, the daily working hour for CFW is usually 4 to 5 hours per day; and 15 days a month. This also considers that the weather in Somalia is hot and not appropriate to let participants’ work outside for long hours. The current rate for CFW in the area is at USD 4/ day. The number of suggested CFW days (15) and proposed daily rate ($4) will see program participants earning $60/month.conditionality of the transfers will be reviewed regularly to respond the the emerging vulnerabilities so as not to expense the targeted beneficiaries to further suffering in times of unprecedented shocks. Support Own Food production: To improve HHs own crop production, the program proposes to provide 3200 targeted HHs with a restricted seed and tools voucher of $40. The seed and tool pack comprises of maize/sorghum, legumes sufficient enough to plant one acre plot together wit hoe ,shovel and machete. The voucher will be redeemable from identified seeds/inputs suppliers who are recognized by the ministry of agriculture as certified seeds suppliers. The seed toolkit will be provided for one major planting season. The proposed project will provide training to the farmers on good agricultural practices (GAP), climate-smart technologies, train farmers using establish demonstration plots/farmer field schools and train on post-harvest management/ agro-processing and marketing. To sustain enhance and sustain the HHs’ livelihoods and food security the project proposes through CFA to support pastoral and agro-pastoral communities participate in feed/fodder production, rehabilitate damaged feeder roads and other damaged productive public infrastructures like market places, animal troughs and bridges. The project will provide adequate training to the community based animal health workers (CBAHWs) on the commonest cause of livestock morbidity and mortality in Somalia so that they get required skills and knowledge to diagnose sick livestock and link the pastoralist with World Vision/other partners implemented livestock project so they are able to get right treatment for their sick animals on time.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Voucher or cash distributions are the most appropriate modality in all the targeted areas, because there are functional markets. Low purchasing power of vulnerable local inhabitants dampens market incentives and limits the incentive of traders and wholesalers to expand markets. Through the provision of food vouchers, this project will assist in stimulating these markets. Through established mobile transfer mechanisms, mobile cash is fast in boosting the purchasing power of the disaster-affected communities and minimise physical contact which deems appropriate when COVID-19 threats sustain. Cash, as compared with in-kind support, can also ensure the appropriateness of interventions as communities will be able to access different kinds of goods and services available that fit their households needs.Cash transfer is the preferred modality by the people in Somalia based on the experience that World Vision, and other organizations implementing emergency projects. A community-based targeting using agreed upon selection criteria will reduce potential conflicts through transparency and collective ownership of the list of program participants selected. The use of cash reduces visibility of program participants when spending their money. Robust monitoring and feedback mechanisms will also help mitigate the risk of an increase of GBV caused by the cash received.More over Mobile Money Transfers are widely used by other INGOs, UN Agencies and some governmental organizations and reduce protection and security risks associated with collection of cash at a distribution point, by removing the need to accept and carry physical cash, as well as travelling to and from distribution sites. For transparency and accountability, WV will use its proven Last Mile Mobile Solutions (LMMS) to register program participants, ensure they receive their entitlement and accurately report on assistance provided.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

WV has conducted Market assessment in the target locations to understand the market situation and provide a snapshot of market functionality, capacity and availability of different commodities across the target locations. The market assessment adopted the Rapid Assessment for Market (RAM) Approach to provide a basic understanding of the capacity of selected markets in providing people with key commodities in the aftermath of a shock/disaster. The market assessment focused on market systems of staple food commodities, fresh produce, sanitation supplies and agricultural inputs including seeds. These commodities are essential to the communities and the program and are mostly affected by shocks. WV teams conducted Key Informant Interviews with traders in key markets in the propposed project areas.The Assessment revealed that all districts had functional markets with the capacity to adequately serve the communities. Subsequent market monitoring and analysis is done monthly in all the target locations using a structured tool, observation of markets and reference to secondary sources showed that different scenarios were experienced that in one way or the other impacted the market situation leading to the different trends exhibited in the market with regard to the availability and pricing of commodities.WV also receives market updates from FSNAU and CWG which would be used to inform programmatic decisions on cash and or vouchers.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

1.Ensure that both men and women have equal and fair access to work opportunities 2.Ensure that marginalised and disenfranchised groups have equal and fair access to livelihood/agricultural assets 3.conduct a Do no Harm,and gender assessments and put in place appropriate mitigation measures 4.Set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct 5.Reinforce the capacity of the community - encourage collective livelihood projects over those that benefit individuals. Collective practices contribute to community protection and resilience, particularly in rural community economies which are closely linked with social networks, community structures and cultural practices.5.For GBV risks WV will Monitor any cases of GBV during community meetings and during ODM/PDM and refer any cases to GBV specialist if picked. 6.If there arises cases of discrimination of the host community-WV will Target a small percentage of the host community households who are more vulnerable and food insecure
