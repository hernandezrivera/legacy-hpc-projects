Project Summary

The Beirut Port explosion affected the built as well as the social environment of major parts of the capital and its northern suburbs, namely infrastructure. In a multi-sectoral approach to respond to this crisis, UN-Habitat, along with relevant local and international partners, has engaged with several sectors such as shelter, information management, protection, and WaSH. After conducting several field assessments and coordinating with the Municipality of Beirut (current custodian of the sewage networks of the city) and the Governor of Beirut, it is deemed critical to thoroughly investigate the impact of the explosion on underground wastewater networks, particularly as sewage and rainwater networks are not separated. According to the Head of Municipality of Beirut Engineering department "the explosion caused most of the sewage manhole covers to pop open in the immediate surrounding areas, indicating the underground network is severely impacted". Subsequent to a desk review exercise, the total estimated length of affected lines is 90 kilometers, ranging from dislocated pipes, broken connections, leakages, etc. In view of these potential sanitation problems if not addressed (high possibility of inundation and sewage flood will occur as first rains arrive), coupled with increased number of COVID-19 cases, and the necessity to enhance adequate hygiene practices in this current complex health and emergency context, it is critical to investigate the unseen WASH damages likely incurred by the explosion. As such the project entails identification of wastewater networks deficiencies and propose immediate/medium/long term solutions in order to prevent potential sanitation and public health emergencies, including the spread of COVID-19, particularly during the upcoming autumn/ winter rains.

Plan fields

1 - Needs

1- The first need is to identify and assess all deficiencies in the sewarage network such as leakages, damages pipes or manholes, and disconnected buildings. 2- The second need is to train municipal workers to monitor the situation and findings from the assessments, to share with relevant partners and actors, and analyze priority interventions in the network. 3- The third need is to fix urgent damages and avoid the spread of potential sanitation problems, such as flooding. 4- The fourth need is to enhance hygiene practices and awareness against pandemics, particularly COVID-19.

2 - Activities

1. In collaboration with Global Water Operators Partnerships’ Alliance, a program of UN-Habitat, the Compagnie Intercommunale Liégoise des Eaux (CILE), the water operator from Liège, and the SPGE, the sanitation company of the same region, UNHABITAT will Conduct a diagnosis mission to wastewater public network in the affected area, this is to assess the impact of the explosion on the nework. In adition, a governance diagnosis will be conduct, discussion with the main stakeholders in the wastewater sector which results in sharing recommendations for the upgrade and rehabilitation of the wastewater public network. Furthermore, UNHABITAT will support the municipality of Beirut in the planned technical assessments for the networks. 2. Conduct CCTV surveillance to the most urgent sewage pipes in Rmeil, Medawar, Saifi, and Achrafieh impacted by the explosion. 3. Apply immediate fixation and urgent rehabilitation / upgrade to 1,500 meters of wastewater networks (manholes, main sewer pipes, house connections) based on the assessment findings' priorities.
