Project Summary


A 12 month project addressing immediate basic food needs of the poorest populations in hard to reach and most vulnerable rural communities in Kariba and Mt Darwin districts in Mashonaland Central and Mashonaland West Provinces of Zimbabwe)The proposed project will support 13,400 most vulnerable HHs (67,000 individuals).
In response to the acute food insecurity situation, World Vision (WV) is proposing a 12 month project (1 January 2021 to 31 Decembper 2021) to increase access to food for vulnerable populations in rural communities in Mt Darwin and Kariba districts of Mashonaland West and Mashonaland Central provinces of Zimbabwe.
The proposed project will support 13,400 most vulnerable HHs (67,000 individuals) with agricultural inputs in the form of 50 kg top dressing and 50 kg basal dressing fertiliser, drought tolerant small grain seeds for pulses (5 kg cowpeas) and an option of either 5 kg of Sorghum or 5kg millet per household. Benefiting households will also receive appropriate trainings in climate resilient agricultural practices includuing conservation agriculture and improved post harvest practices. The project will provide 200 kgs of fodder seeds for the establishment of 20 fodder production demontration plots i.e. 10 plots in each of the 2 targeted districts with each plot being established using 10 kg of fodder seed of their choice i.e. lab lab, velvet bean. Increasing the capacities of small holder farmers to manage their livestock and grow fodder crops will increase access to feed for their small and large livestock during the dry season thus increasing livestock productivity and reducing livesock deaths.
The project will train small holder farmers on improved small livestock production/husbandry practices with a focus on goats, sheep and poultry which are key in contributing towards increasing the food and nutrition security of targeted vulnerable households.

1 - Activities

The following activities will be implemented in the 2 districts i.e. Mt Darwin and Kariba. • The project will provide households with 50kg top dressing fertiliser, 50 kg basal dressing fertiliser, small grain and legume seeds (5 kg of each per HH) • The project will train small holders farmers with trainings in conservation agriculture, nutrition sensitive agriculture, Integrated Pest Managent and improved post-harvest management practices and technologies. • Inputs need to be available for distribution to targted households by end of October 2021 and trainings will be carried out as from September through to Mid November 2021; and post-harvest management trainings will be carried out in April 2021. • The project will provide fodder seed for the establishment of 20 community based demonstration plots which will be used by extension workers and identified Lead farmers to train small holder farmers on fodder production and improved small livestock husbandry practices.

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

Accountability mechanisms will be put in place to include suggestion boxes, help desks, toll free lines to ensure that feedback is collected and given back to the recipients. World Vision will train all personnel, partners and agents on these issues and ensure that they conform to the highest standard of moral and ethical conduct. Regular community meetings will be held with community groups to discuss issues coming out of the project as well as a platform for community feedback.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

World Vision together with the Environmental Management Agency and AGRITEX will ensure that crop production trainings for small holder farmers will emphasise the importance for soil and water conservation. The project will focus on training farmers on conservation agricultural practices that promote minimum soil disturbance, mulching and crop rotation, all of which are key contributors to soil and water conservation while maximising crop production.
