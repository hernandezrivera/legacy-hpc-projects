Project Summary

Communities in Jonglei and Upper Nile continue to face adverse effects of displacement, economic collapse, floods, disease, as well as localized conflict, the effects of COVID-19, limited access to basic services, and the cumulative effects of prolonged years of asset depletion and loss of livelihoods. have greatly impacted the food security situation of households in the counties targeted.
The agro – pastoralist nature of populations in the counties targeted has exposed population to severe food insecurity and malnutrition due to low food availability, diversity and access. Loss of livestock which is the major source of livelihood due to low vaccination and treatment reach, loss due to insecurity, as well as the compounding effects of floods.
Medicair seeks to integrate her present nutrition programming with FSL activities, aimed at improving the well being of households. The project will target persons through cluster objectives CO2 (Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19 (compulsory use of new SOP & guidelines) and CO3 (Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19 (compulsory use of new SOP & guidelines)
Enhancing household and communal production and value addition will enhance communal resilience and enable communities withstand future shocks.
A total of 31,250 persons (6,250HHs) are targeted throughout 2021 in the counties of Nyirol Jonglei State

1 - Needs

Food Security and nutrition deteriorated further in the counties of kkk in Jonglei and Upper Nile, according to the latest Integrated Phase Classification report and the South Sudan Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring Survey (FSNMNS). Y% of HHs in Jonglei and Upper Nile are food insecure, as a result of constraints in food availability, access to food and food utilisation. Latest figures of cereal production indicate that stocks will most likely not meet the country’s cereal needs because of flooding, dry spells, crop pests and diseases, insecurity, and displacement of farming households which affected most agricultural activities. Production in Greater Upper Nile regions is however expected to decline compared to last year. The ongoing economic crisis and the effects of COVID-19 continue to make it difficult for majority of households to access food from markets because of loss of sources of livelihoods, reduced income, and high food prices. The seasonal deterioration of road infrastructure during the rainy season will also affect market functionality by disrupting the timely restocking of markets. The effects of insecurity in parts of the country will also lead to displacement, depletion or loss of assets, and disruption of livelihoods, further contributing to reduced income for purchasing food and essential needs. Food utilisation is affected mainly amongst women, children and the elderly because of the chronic nature of waterborne diseases, low use of latrines, poor personal hygiene and living environments, and limited access to hygienic materials. Access to health services is also poor which leads to high incidences of diseases that not only affect the health of the population, but also negatively affects availability of labour and leads to reduced income at household level. About 1.4 million children under five years are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition in 2021 based on same season historical data of food security and nutrition monitoring system, SMART nutrition surveys and admission trends for 2020. COVID-19 related disruptions, including those rightfully implemented to curb coronavirus infection rates, as well as changes in SAM and MAM referral protocols for children have exacerbated lack of access to services. During the lean season period in 2021, the food security situation will deteriorate in Upper Nile with an estimated 1,034,000 people (70.8% of the population) likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 688,000 people will be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, and 346,000 people will likely be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. The food security situation is expected to deteriorate further throughout the entire Jonglei, with an estimated 1.68 million people (84.7% of the population) likley to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 876,000 people will likely be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, 792,000 people will likely be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, and 11,000 people will likely be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Akobo County. Low levels of livestock treatment and vaccination continue to lead to livestock deaths, with communities in Jonglei and Upper Nile constantly suffering loss too due to intercommunal rustling.

2 - Project activities

• Provision of Emergency food & livelihood support through distribution of seeds, tools, fishing kits/ vegetable kits; rapid response kits (fish kit/ cow pea & three variety of fast maturing vegetable seed)• Livestock treatment and vaccination; livestock disease surveillance, Community Animal Health Workers Training and inputs support, cold chain establishment and maintenance, prevention & treatment. • Support to seed fairs using locally sourced seed; seed security & multiplication strengthening/ Support market functionality. • Cash & voucher-based transfers with food security/ nutrition objectives. • Support value chain addition e.g. through agriculture, fishery and livestock products/ by-products preservation & marketing• Training & capacity building: both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production (farmer/agro-pastoral field schools, demonstration plots), income generation (business skills), vocational training etc.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

PSEA information will be provided through maximum utilization of all information materials, such as fliers, banners, T Shirts, workshops, focus group gatherings, interviews as well as assessments. Key trainings will be organized for all stakeholders directly involved in project which will have a key focus on PSEA protocols according to humanitarian standards.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Focus group discussions will be utilized to obtain maximum feedback from the most vulnerable members; as well as house to house interviews, interviews with key informants. Medicair will assign key staff to follow up households with persons with special needs, inorder to ensure maximum input from all.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Clear feedback pathways have been designed by Medicair, through which dissemination and input of information from key stakeholders will be done. All stakeholders will be trained/briefed on the importance and mechanisms of feedback. Information on key achievements against the targets will be provided through public dissemination using local community publicity forums, community gatherings, and use of IEC materials. Focus group discussions will be organized, suggestion boxes fixed at key field sites, interviews with key informants, as well as house to house surveys. Toll free hotlines will be set up; to enable maximum input from beneficiaries and key stakeholders.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Project design will be greatly influenced by input from beneficiaries and key stakeholders. Feedback will be documented, and utilized in project review meetings to inform design and implementation.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Safety audits will be carried out to mitigate risks associated with delivery of humanitarian aid. Services will be brought closest to the beneficiaries; avoiding women and children traveling long distances to access aid. Beneficiary selection will be done in consultation with all stakeholders, with priority of response given to the most vulnerable based on gender. Clear feedback mechanisms and pathways will be emphasized to all stakeholders, while working with local authorities, other humanitarian actors to mitigate incidences of bureaucratic impediments. Coordination will be done with the mine action working group to ensure reduced risk of harm from UXOs.
