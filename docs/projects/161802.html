Project Summary

The overall objective of the proposed project is to provide emergency food assistance and stabilization of conflict-affected population. This will be achieved through two sets of activities:
- Improved Food Security, through e-voucher programme:
o 5,200 beneficiaries for previous project will receive 5 monthly disbursements before transitioning to livelihood support (see below),
o 38,500 beneficiaries will receive 12 monthly disbursement (35,000 selected trough food security criteria, 3,500 trough protection criteria)
o 7,000 new arrivals in Bolori II will be supported for 2 months, upon their arrivals, along the project duration.
The monthly disbursement will cover 70% of the food security needs, as per sector guidance, and will be divided into 50% food items to be retrieved from selected vendors and 50% to be received in cash.
- Livelihoods Restoration and creation of economic opportunities through the establishment of Income Generating Activities (IGA) to promote self-reliance and reduce dependency on humanitarian assistance for 650 beneficiaries. Each beneficiary will receive 250 USD and be supported and trained to create its IGA.

1 - Needs

PUI’s strategy within Nigeria follows an evidence-based, integrated approach with the proposed intervention aimed at enhancing the overall living conditions of affected persons - both vulnerable host communities and IDPs – through complimentary Health, Nutrition, MHPSS, Food Security & Livelihoods and Protection activities. In Bolori II, Maiduguri, it seeks to transition to more sustainable programming by promoting self-reliance – while continuing to meet the acute needs of the populations.Access to food in Borno State remains the most urgent concern for more 1,380,597 (June-August 2019). Between 45% and 80% of households are food insecure (FCS<35), and 96% of food insecure households resort to potentially damaging coping strategies (CSI >22). In Maiduguri MMC, commodity prices are high compared to the rest of Borno State though markets are in the process of recovering and becoming more accessible. Recent harvests remain substantially below average as conflict and climate change have increasingly impacted the cropping season . The preliminary findings of a MUAC assessment conducted by PUI in July 2018 in Bolori II showed a proxy Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate of 17%, with 3% of children between 6 to 59 months reported to have severe acute malnutrition (SAM). These numbers classify the severity of the situation in Bolori II as critical as per WHO standards, highlighting the need for community management of acute malnutrition in the area. The ward is hosting a large number of IDPs amounting to 61% of its population according to information from community leaders. Displaced people are primarily dependent on humanitarian food assistance and limited income earning opportunities. The recent Cadre Harmonise (June 2019) confirmed that an elevated number of households continue to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity and humanitarian needs remain high for the more than 1.7 million IDPs and poorest host populations. To meet basic needs, HHs resorted to potentially damaging coping strategies with 93% of them reporting borrow food or money to buy essential items, 35.5% sending children to beg, and 32.7% engaging their children in child labor. Given the context, and persisting needs of the population, it is critical to empower affected populations to gradually re-establish their means of livelihoods. Bolori II is particularly suited to kick-start livelihood interventions since the access to and security within this area allow for humanitarian interventions with early recovery activities to sustainably address the looming food and livelihoods crisis, further encouraging stabilization.

2 - Activities

Livelihood Restoration: PUI aims to assist 650 households to restore their livelihoods through Income Generating Activities (IGA). These selected Households will be supported to rebuild their livelihoods based on skills, experience and motivation in their livelihood of choice. Beneficiaries will attend a business skills training course conducted by a certified trainer with facilitation and monitoring conducted by PUI (Introduction to business ; Business Management ; Business Planning ; Marketing ; Risk management ; Record keeping ; Costing and pricing). Once selected, awardees will receive a cash grant not exceeding 250 USD per individual through e-vouchers modality aligned with the productive assets’ lists developed during the business planning stage.Food security: The improvement of food security will be achieved through a 12-month program of voucher distribution (to be redeemed in food items and cash). Beneficiary targeting will be determined through consultations with local stakeholders including community committees and leaders, partner NGOs and the food security sector as necessary. PUI will follow a mixed (qualitative and quantitative) targeting approach based on needs and vulnerability criteria (Coping Strategy Index, Food Consumption Score, HHS and HH Diversity Diet indicators) to identify beneficiary. All beneficiaries validated will be issued with e-voucher card and all beneficiaries who receive an e-card will attend sensitization session. Non-verified beneficiaries after 2 rounds of verification due to failure to present themselves at verification stations will be terminated from the program. 3 different beneficiary caseloads will receive monthly disbursements:- 5,200 households for previous project will receive 5 monthly disbursements before transitioning to livelihood support (see below), - 38,500 beneficiaries will receive 12 monthly disbursement (35,000 selected trough food security criteria, 3,500 trough protection criteria)- 7,000 new arrivals in Bolori II will be supported for 2 months, upon their arrivals, along the project duration.The monthly disbursement will cover 70% of the food security needs, as per sector guidance, and will be divided into 50% food items to be retrieved from selected vendors and 50% to be received in cash.The assistance package is in-line with the food security sector per capita recommendations. In modelling the food basket, several factors where considered including beneficiary preference, availability, seasonal price fluctuations and the ease with which households can prepare the items in the food basket compared to availability of fuel. The assistance package is calculated based on 70% of the food basket value which includes Cereals, pulses, fish, tubers, oil, pasta/spaghetti, sugar, fruits and vegetables. Market prices were used in determine the voucher value through the minimum expenditure basket (MEB) as per the food security sector. The project will allow beneficiaries to withdraw half the value of the assistance in cash. This is due to beneficiary preference as per PUI’s experience and PDM exercise 2018 and 2019. The cash transfer aims at complementing food items retrieved in the selected vendor shops with other food items (fresh vegetables, meat,). PUI will use RedRose platform for cash transfers capitalizing on operational efficiency. This maintains transparency to ensure that funds reach the most vulnerable persons targeted. PUI will carry out monthly price monitoring exercise and agree on a list of fixed prices with the vendors.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

PUI has a strong mechanism in place that relies on various channels. Feedbacks are mainly captured through:(1) Community Committees Meetings that are organised once a month with community leaders, elders, women and PUI program staff to discuss project activities, gaps and needs; (2) Community Volunteers Network which comprised all outreach workers who directly link PUI to targeted communities; and (3) PUI Complaint Mechanism which consist of helpdesks in the community and a toll-free helpline managed by dedicated trained staff.
