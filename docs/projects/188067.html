Project Summary

The project aims to respond to urgent winter needs to support the most vulnerable groups in identified underserved areas and provide urgent shelter needs through upgrading of sites and provide innovative shelter solutions to ensure better-dignified shelter and measures to limit flood damages and address current emergency needs and critical gaps identified of where GAN is already intervening, it is also consisting of three main components:
1-Provide winterization assistance to vulnerable families in the targeted sites:
A- GAN aim through this activity to provide timely life-saving winterization needs for 10000 families (5000 HH in 2022, 5000 HH in 2023) to help them cope with the harsh conditions and secure their needs in a dignified way by distributing 3 rounds of cash for winterization needs, so 50$ will be allocated per family per round as agreed with SNFI and CWG for cash winterization assistance, so 150$ will be allocated per family as agreed with SNFI and CWG, since cash modality gives beneficiaries flexibility in choosing heating items they need and to give the project an ability to meet all changes in beneficiary needs as all targeted areas have access to the markets and heating items according to the assessment, so blanket distribution will be applied in camps and the prioritized criteria will be applied out-off camps as follows: Female and/or child-headed households, disabled members, Elderly-headed households, Household with more than 5 children, Families without a breadwinner, Families with pregnant and lactating women. (41% of this activity for 2022 is secured)
B- Provide heating means items (firewood, coal, diesel, and Perrin) with 150$ value 1500 HHs (750 HH in 2022, 750 HH in 2023) using (e-voucher) during the project period (where the markets are not functional). (20% of this activity for 2022 is secured)
2-Better dignified shelter with associated infrastructure and cooking facilities:
The project is tailored to provide a new durable shelter unit, which is prefabricated unit (sandwich panel), to be set up as a substitution for the normal tent. The project aims to target 400 HHs (200 HH in 2022, 200 HH in 2023) of IDPs living in randomly, underserved, and in bad condition camps, to be placed in a new organized camp targeted by a full package of infrastructure works.
3- Infrastructure improvements to mitigate the risk and prevent flood damages: this intervention targets the identified gaps in the assessed location where possible considering the HLP and other critical issues, so the sites are targeted under which include 1000 families (500 HH in 2022, 500 HH in 2023), the main works are:
A- Graveling of the roads and raising the tents’ grounds.
B- Rain drainage system and channels.
These sites exposed/could be exposed to floods to mitigate and protect tents from the driftage by water rain or from being partially/totally destroyed during rainstorms, in addition, road graveling /compacted layers of graded gravel for internal roads (sub and/or base layers based on the camps’ needs to be laid down and compacted), so this will improve the access to /within camps and prevent camps disconnecting with its surroundings during the heavy rainstorms and facilitating the living conditions and the access of humanitarian aids to population and vulnerable people
C- Provide and install solar-based points to facilitate access to wash facilities (latrines, showers) for the population especially women and children, and mitigate protection risks. In addition to maintaining lighting for the existing system.

4 - Needs

Long-standing needs of an estimated 6.9M IDPs remain staggering, particularly for over 2M people in camps often hosted in inadequate shelters Overstretched host communities frequently continue to face inadequate living standards 28% reside in finished residential buildings classified as damaged and/or displaying inadequate conditionsThe deteriorating economic situation, compounded with the implications of COVID-19 has led to further intensification of the severity of need for NFI supportMany of the 2M IDPs living in overcrowded sites/camps have exhausted their financial capacity to cope following repeated episodes of displacement 67% of the population reported that they are unable to afford NFIs available in the marketplace. The top items reported are solar panels, fuel (heating/cooking), clothing, winter heatersTwo-thirds of the IDP population living in inadequate shelter types which have particularly high needs for continuous shelter assistance/repair and NFIs to keep them warm. Primarily lack insulation, privacy/space, electricityOvercrowded informal sites with no proper site planning, limited or no access to services, or camp management structures pose serious protection and GBV risks for the most vulnerable, who are mainly diverse women and children. 1.18M people in the northwest were found to be living in sites with no lighting on the main roads80% are women, girls, and boys who face significant GBV risks due to inadequate living conditions across IDP sitesS:HNO22

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

GAN is a member of PSEA network, attending a monthly meeting and following the guidelines, so to mainstream the protection from sexual exploitation and abuse in the project, GAN have developed a policy stating standards of conduct, including acts of SEA, and a work plan to implement the policy in place, GAN will ensure all staff members are signed and adhered to a code of conduct policy, PSEA and will support the members to access relevant pieces of training (PSEA, GBV), ensuring that non-humanitarian stakeholders who will be in touch with beneficiaries such as (suppliers) will be informed and adhere to the humanitarian principles and code of conduct, PSEA posters will be available at distribution points. GAN recognizes the rights of all stakeholders, particularly the vulnerable, to access humanitarian programs without the risk of sexual exploitation or abuse. GAN intends to prevent abuse by implementing procedures to protect beneficiaries through best recruitment practices, staff induction, creating an open and aware culture and assigning clear management responsibilities, ensuring that victims are supported, and perpetrators held to account. Information on the referral pathway will be circulated through posters and banners at the distribution points. The distribution points will be inside the camps itself, during daylight hours, and safeguard that there is appropriate segregation for women to create a culturally sensitive and safe operational environment
