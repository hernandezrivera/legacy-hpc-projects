Project Summary

World Vision Sudan is currently gradually resuming operations in the Sudan area. The resumption of operations is guided by the regular security assessment focusing on the safety of the staff who are supposed to be deployed at the different duty stations. The gradual opening is currently starting with States that have relative peace and or have the highest IDPs and hence in need of services that are lifesaving. World Vision plans to preposition its mobile clinics with integrated Health and Nutrition services including MIYCF-E in Blue Nile, Algereeza and Sinar states (Planned through our Emergency fundraising). With a plan in South Kordofan currently dealing with multipurpose cash assistance (MPCA) and in East Darfur where 14 PHUs are currently operational with remote support. World Vision’s biggest challenge is South Darfur due to access and communication issues.
World Vision is currently benchmarking a number of possibility to support focusing on the utilisation of mobile clinics to offer services and show humanitarian presence The Key Project Objective as adapted is aimed at improving equitable access to quality life-saving curative and preventive services through the systematic provision of nutrition among vulnerable children under-five and PLWs in the mushrooming IDPs, vulnerable host communities, refugees in some places and our traditional states once the security situation permits. This will target 9,835 CU5 and 5, 817 PLWs to be reached with community based lifesaving nutrition interventions.
World Vision proposes offering counselling for maternal and young child nutrition, work in an integrated way to offer micronutrient supplements to include Vitamin A, and Iron folic for PLWs. Support the community based MUAC assessment working through the Mother support groups for the mother MUAC. Management of acute malnutrition will be done through the continuum of care approach from the SC, OTP and then counter referal to the TSFP. World Vision will also support data management through the SMART and earl warning (EWARN).
World Vision plans to work through a collaboration with the community structures for example capacity of the Mother support groups, and the community health workers to deliver social behavioural change communication for mothers and young children nutrition. Further still working with the states ministries of health to ensure treatment and preventive nutrition services can be accessed and utilize services at the treatment centers and in the community.
World Vision Sudan has plans to leverage on the experience and the very good relationship with state authorities to be able to facilitate implementation. Working in collaboration with the state ministries of Health, and HAC to ensure mobile clinics are operation in the assigned IDPS, and vulnerable communities.

1 - Needs

The Country Sudan has plunged into a political turmoil with at least two factions fighting each other in the capital Sudan and almost all states in the country. The clashes are accompanied by heavy fire and artillery. This has caused spillover to death of the population triggering displacement and movement from deeply affected crisis areas to those that have relative stability and peace. The situation has led to an increase in the population in need (PIN) generally by a 57%. This displacement and insecurity in Sudan has resulted in to the deterioration of the nutrition situation leading to an estimated 30% increase in the number of children with acute malnutrition in hotspot areas, a 15% increase in IDP hosting states and a 10% increase in other localities. This high malnutrition rates under pins the role played in the cycle of nutrition though life. A malnourished mother will deliver a malnourished child and a malnourished child will grow to be a problematic adult. With this surging increase in the severity of the nutrition situation, it is definitely worrying that if interventions are not suggested to respond, there will be increase in morbidity and eventual mortality among PLWs and CU5. Due to the security situation in the country and the different states, there has been an increasing trend in the IDPs in states like Blue Nile, Al gezera, Sinar, East Darfur and South Kordofan, and hence more households are going to be dependent on food and nutrition handouts that are now not sufficient. The insufficient of these lifesaving commodities is attributed to poor communications, life threatening insecurity on the roads and hence refills cannot be done, some states have closed entry and exit boarders, and the general curfew imposed by the clashes. This therefore calls for an innovative way of providing community management of acute malnutrition services coupled with MIYCF-E preventive services ensuring every household is reached with lifesaving nutrition services. It is important to further note that even before the advent of these fresh country wide clashes, the states have been grappling with an inadequate structural and institutional capacities. The State Ministries of health have provided guidance on the seconded staff in the nutrition units. They have further guided on how the CHWs and the MSGs should be selected and utilized. However, there is need for capacity building of these staff to be able to offer quality services. Offering of quality services is only effective if there is timely active case finding and referal. The weak link between the community and the nutrition units contributes to the late admission into the program.Regardless of the current situation, there is a noticeable increase in the care of a malnourished child with a 37 to 75% inflation on a dollar. The qualified medical nutrition staff willing to be included on a mobile team or a PHU has kept deteriorating with those willing to work state new terms that include high secondment fees. World Vision is currently popularizing the MPCA initiative for households in shock to be able to buy nutritious foods with in their catchment without waiting for handouts through food distribution, however this needs to be strengthened by “do no Harm” gender sensitive mechanisms to ensure harmony at the household and appropriate proper utilisation of the resources for the actual procurement of nutritious foods.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Lifesaving prevention and protection interventions focusing on pregnant and lactating women, and CU5: This will be part of the SBCC approaches that will be bundled on the mobile teams that will be established. The MIYCF-E, establishment of breastfeeding corners at the IPD receiving points as part of triage for PLWs. This will include: IYCF/E message dissemination, Nutrition Screening, growth monitoring, and referral, Nutrition Counselling for pregnant and lactating mothers and IYCF one to one counselling for mothers and caregivers with children 0-23 months. Further still there will be improvements in the mother to Mother and Father to Father support groups, strengthen and scale up breastfeeding corners at primary and secondary health care levels and care of children in special circumstances; Support for the non-breastfed child, LBW, HIV and Infant Feeding including the baby friendly hospital intervention. Improved treatment of severe and moderate acute malnutrition for PLWs and CU5: The mobile clinics will have an integrated care team that includes care for the malnourished. This will also include onsite active case finding for the PLWs and CU5 to assess for malnutrition. The early active case finding will ensure access to outpatient management of severe acute malnutrition without medical complications for children 6-59mo. Quick and efficient referal system will improve the linkages to inpatient management of severe acute malnutrition with medical complications for children 0-59mo. Strengthen the role of community cadre in active case finding and referral (CNVs, MSGs & mothers of SAM children). This will include capacity building of the structures to ensure they can offer the appropriate nutrition services at the community level. Nutrition evidence generation for effective programming: This will be strengthened through the periodic technical support supervision for the community structures and the nutrition units and service points. The services availability and readiness assessment of the CMAM units will be the basis to ensure needs are identified to be able to implement the minimum nutrition care package. There will be monitoring core supplies availability on monthly basis and provide update to the sector. Further still there will be conducting the early warning and early warning reporting and analysis of evolving nutrition situation feeding programs World Vision acknowledges the fact that there is need for increasing investment into resilience. This this situation, or a war ravaged country, it’s more like investing in locally accepted survival. As stated earlier, there is going to be a strong linkage between the MPCA initiative and the nutrition SBCC. For Two reasons;- 1) To ensure that the money distributed to the households is actually going to the search and procurement of nutritious foods for the households. Further still that the entry into the MPCA is triggered by the households with PLWs and CU5 who are at the verge of malnutrition (boarder line). 2) That is are SBCC initiatives in the Nutrition interventions through CHWs that promotes “do no harm” to ensure harmony in the households while this money is being put to use. Issues around community identification of gender-based violence and networking into the PSEA framework for Sudan have also been considered as part of resilience in this multistate project. World Vision as suggested 24 out of the 54 activities to focus on Resilience and this accounts for 44% of the total project plan.
