Project Summary

Basmeh and Zeitooneh for Relief and Development proposed intervention is in line with the education cluster’s strategic objectives 1 & 2, in which the proposed intervention will focus on increasing access to non-formal education for children and youth and enhancing the quality of non-formal education for children and young people affected by the crisis.
In urban settings, B&Z will provide classes for out of school children and youth. To achieve this, B&Z will work in 2 existing Learning Centers in Ma'arrat Tamasrin and Atarib providing classes for primary education 2nd phase (for 12-26 year old) targeting a total of 1000 children/youth.(545 girls, 455 boys) out of which 77 CwD.
B&Z has already trained education staff in these two centers who have received several training on education, dealing with children, and more importantly are training on child protection, and safeguarding.
Additionally, B&Z will utilize its new Learning Center in Jenderis providing basic primary education (7-15 year old) targeting a total of 500 children/youth (275 girls, 225 boys) out of which 33 CwD.
Through classes in its centres, B&Z will provide remedial education to primary school/high school students/secondary school students on English, maths, , Physics and Arabic. This will be offered to primary school/high school/secondary school students aged 12-26 who are enrolled in general schools in opposition held areas and need assistance in these subjects. These remedial classes aim to help students achieve higher grades and pass their exams in order to be more eligible to enroll in the universities or start work.The courses will be provided in 6 months cycles each. The first cycle will start in January 2024 and will end in June 2024; the second cycle will start in July 2024 and end in December 2024. In each location, it is expected that with each cycle 250 students will benefit from this activity. Classes will have an average of 25 students of the same age group or level. The national formal curriculum will be followed including subjects such as Math, Physics, English and Arabic.
Selection criteria will be communicated with communities and B&Z will conduct the selection of children/youth depending on vulnerability criteria. Priority will be given to 12-26 year old children. Children with disability, girls and IDP children and youth will be prioritised. A pre-test conducted for applicants will indicate their knowledge level and will inform enrolment within particular groups.
Remedial classes will be implemented as a part of GvZ community centers.
This will be through physical attendance in the centers which are accessible by the participants.
Children participants will be divided into groups according to their age and educational level,which will be measured at the beginning of the cycle through a Pre-test. Gaps will be identified through this primary test in order to provide the needed classes and support.
GvZ always tries to maintain small groups which encourage more effective participation from the children and more attention from the teachers to the needs and gaps.
Methodology for learning :
Interactive education: It is based on the interaction between the teacher and the children, according to the following:
Answering children’s questions, dialogue, and discussion. Even if the children are not able to attend the lesson because of the availability of the internet they leave their questions and the teachers answer them and discuss with them.
Duties, activities, and worksheets. This is proof that the children benefit from the lessons and can evaluate the effect of remote learning.
Asynchronous education: It uses non-interactive and non-synchronous education tools, which are available at any time, and tools such as:
Slide shows (PowerPoint).
Videos related to the topic of the lesson.
Other files that support the idea of the lesson.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

More than 1 million school-aged children need education support and are at risk of being out of school due to the earthquakes and at least 25,000 teachers affected by the earthquakes are in need of mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), appropriate compensation and capacity building, especially in recovering from learning loss. A minimum of 452 primary and secondary schools have been reportedly damaged to varying degrees. This number is expected to increase as the Education cluster’s school assessment is underway. There is a critical need to invest in an overstretched, under-resourced and severely impacted formal school system. According to Rapid Needs Assessment conducted by World vision in NWS 2023, 42% of respondents reported that the education facilities/services were damaged in their neighborhoods; of these, 90% were partially damaged and 10% were destroyed. 84% of respondents felt that the earthquake affected their children’s ability to access education services [WV,RNA, 2023] Protection Risk: Learning facilities could be attacked. Attacks may include: airstrikes, bombs/explosions/shelling, fighting/gunshots, arson/fire, looting/theft; kidnapping/arrest of a teacher or students, direct attack on teacher or student, intimidation of school staff / students or military use of the school.Mitigation Measures: Reduce time/distance traveled by decentralizing learning facilities.Consider sunrise and sunset times when setting learning hours.Provide students, their caregivers and school personnel information on safer routes/times to travel. Engage community leaders/members and change makers to support awareness raising interventions to highlight the importance of safety and related solutionsPSEA protection Risk: Aid workers or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they should be serving.Mitigating Measures: Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policies should be in place, practiced and monitored. Policy should cover standards of behavior for the organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and include accountability measures. Policies should address risks at the organizational level and the program level.The targeted communities and learning facilities should be aware of the organization’s policies and procedures. The organization’s staff and targeted communities should have access to safe and effective reporting systems including knowing when they will get a response to their report and having the right to report anonymously.Organizations should ensure a survivor centered approach that ensures the safety and confidentiality of the affected person. The survivor should have a say in the process.
