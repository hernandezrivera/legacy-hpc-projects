i. Mother to mother support groups, Father support group, cooking demonstration on complementary feeding with MTMSG and nutrition education

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"As malnutrition is high in both the host community and in the Rohingya Refugee camps. In the Rohingya refugee camps the results from the 2022 UNHCR nutrition survey global acute malnutrition (GAM) was 12.4% and stunting was estimated at 41.2%. This was a slight improvement from the situation in 2021 but recent ration cut worsen the situation of nutrition. Infant and young child feeding practices are sub optimal as indicated by only 22.7% of the children meeting the minimum acceptable diet, early initiation of breast-feeding was only 85% and exclusive breastfeeding estimated at 62.2%. All this data indicates there is a huge challenge of both acute and chronic malnutrition in the target area and hence the needs for activities to prevent malnutrition in the target areas.Nutrition sensitive activities including awareness sessions, livelihood activities including home gardening, livestock rearing, skill development and self-empowerment can contribute to improve nutrition and food security status for both in Rohingya and host community.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

For all activities, Concern will ensure that in the staff and volunteer we have women to ensure that female beneficiaries have free access to the services freely discuss some sensitive topics for both men and women. Concern will support male involvement especially on childcare and other activities, which traditionally left for women and girls. In our current projects, Concern has support groups also targeting men. Concern has an Equality Strategy, which guides all programming across nutrition, livelihoods, health and education sector. During the process of beneficiary selection, identification of community assets and communications with communities, Concern ensure protection mainstreaming. Having women in the committees help ensure that their concerns are taken into account and that their dignity and protection is preserved.

3.3 - Age & Disability

The project targets age specific groups such as child, adult and person with disability people for the services. Concern has strong safeguarding policy, which protect children from child labors, abuse & harm and also ensure rights of the children.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

To foster active participation and inclusivity of the affected community, Concern Worldwide will adopt a multi-faceted approach. Concern places a strong emphasis on involving affected community in project design and implementation, regardless of age, gender and diversity. Concern has a robust Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) establishing in every nutrition facility and a dedicated AAP/CFM staff will be deployed to facilitate the process. Accessible communication, and regular feedback loops will be in place to engage affected people throughout the project cycle and work as a change maker. Concern will ensure required training for the service providers to improve feedback collection and analysis system. The collected data will be analysed in the light of age, gender, disability, and diversity. Insights will be shared with nutrition and food security sector as well as other relevant stakeholders and contributing our expertise to develop best practices for an effective response. Independent monitoring and evaluation along with vibrant two-way feedback and response mechanism will help the project prioritize humanitarian needs of those affected population
