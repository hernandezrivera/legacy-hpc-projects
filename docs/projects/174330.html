Project Summary

Implemented in partnership with Search for Common Ground (SfCG) and the national NGO Pastoral Resolve (PARE), this project aims to improve food security in Katsina State through the parallel modalities of Permaculture Design & Regenerative Agriculture, adopting ACTED’s Flagship REVIVE/THRIVE approach.

1 - Needs

Following unabated waves of displacements recorded throughout 2019, ACTED conducted a needs assessment in January 2020 to determine the magnitude of food insecurity amongst IDPs and host communities within Katsina State. Executed in partnership with the local NGO Pastoral Resolve (PARE), the needs assessment involved significant stakeholder consultation in Katsina State, sampling 165 individuals through a mix of community-based focus group discussions (FGD; n=11) and key informant interviews (KII; n=16) in two of the most severely affected districts: Batsari and Jibia LGAs. Firstly, the needs assessment elicited information on the prevalent conflict dynamics and their impact on agricultural livelihoods and food security needs by obtaining opinions from local leaders and chairmen from local farmer and pastoral associations. Interviews with several directors of local government ministries enabled a broader understanding of the ecological challenges of the agricultural sector in Katsina, underpinning the large-scale surge in food insecurity. This was followed by a series of in-depth gender-separated focus groups, to identify specific vulnerabilities faced by women and girls. Participants from different communities facilitated open discussion on the livelihood challenges and food security needs, whilst allowing farmers to discuss areas of support to improve agricultural production modalities. A considerable amount of female community members expressed distress over the extensive unaffordability and unavailability of food, reduced nutritional intake and dietary diversity, as well as heightened cases of acute child malnutrition rapidly extending amongst communities. Beyond determining immediate food insecurity concerns, a number of consistent problems were identified throughout these diverse stakeholder consultations, including: reduced agricultural productivity; the inability to access traditional farmland; the lack of access to farming inputs, including seeds, fertilizers and utensils; inadequate to inexistent government support to stimulate agricultural production and meet immediate food needs; and calls for training to improve farming practices and increase yields on reduced sizes of land. These concerns have directly informed ACTED’s programmatic approach with direct beneficiary loops permitting a participatory design of activities and informing project needs. Food Insecurity Resulting from Reduced Agricultural Productivity and Income Opportunities During focus group discussions, a large majority of farmers expressed pervasive food scarcity as a result of a stark decline in agricultural productivity and rampant criminality in Katsina State. The heightened insecurity has restricted access to cultivatable land and prompted the abandonment of farmlands, with many farmers fearing the imminent risk of abduction, assault or even death. Looting, seizure or destruction of farm assets, including houses, field crops, seeds, livestock, grain silos and utensils, have further impaired farming activity, affecting yearly harvests and placing farmers under financial strain. Compounded by ecological degradation, climate variability and out-dated agricultural practices, the loss of agricultural assets and inaccessibility of traditional farmland for cultivation, has further reduced overall agricultural productivity amongst rural communities. This has invariably impaired household access to income and overall availability of food, unleashing severe food insecurity amongst the Katsinan population, historically dependent on agriculture for subsistence and livelihoods.

2 - Activities

1. Conducting baseline assessments on the state of food insecurity to inform site selection and beneficiary identification A one-month assessment phase will be crucial to the first month of project initiation, in order to identify 4 target communities facing food insecurity within Katsina State (see work plan in Annex C). This activity will be led by PARE to capitalize on their intimate familiarity of the proposed area of intervention and longstanding engagement with local communities, ensuring that the proposed Action exploits synergies with other actors and delivers maximum impact. Communities will be selected based on food insecurity levels, land use and land degradation, to ensure inclusion of the most vulnerable and food insecure communities within accessible areas. The results of this assessment phase will inform household-level beneficiary selection, as well as the development of contextually sensitive perma garden and regenerative agriculture training toolkits.2. Conducting a conflict sensitivity assessment in target communitiesSearch’s community platforms and early warning systems regularly report issues related to the activities of humanitarian and development actors, particularly in their approach to the distribution of aid materials, which communities often find random or unfair.3. Training ACTED and PARE staff on conflict-sensitivity and Do No Harm by SearchGiven that ACTED and PARE staff will be working with communities affected by on-going violence and displacement, and a thus facing breakdowns in social cohesion, Search will train frontline staff on conflict-sensitivity and Do No Harm ahead of project implementation. To ensure a highly contextualized training, Search will tailor the training based on the findings from the conflict assessment. The training will support the ability of these actors to lead conflict-sensitive initiatives as well as constructively engage with project beneficiaries in ways that do not cause harm or exacerbate conflict, increasing community acceptance of the project as well as building trust between ACTED, PARE and the beneficiaries more broadly.4. 8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

ACTED takes seriously the responsibility of addressing all forms of marginalization, exclusion and inequalities in access while upholding the principles of gender equality and non-discrimination. ACTED will therefore address the susceptibility to marginalization in participatory processes of vulnerable groups by prioritising inclusive policies in program design and implementation. Women and children, boys and girls, are particularly vulnerable to the risks of malnutrition and food insecurity. Specific attention has therefore been allocated in the design of the present intervention to prevent and reduce those vulnerabilities. ACTED will promote the crucial role of women as producers and providers of food by ensuring that gender is mainstreamed throughout food security activities. Indicators of gender equality and involvement will be included for monitoring and evaluation purposes and ACTED will collect gender disaggregated information. Moreover, all staff will be made clearly aware of, and remain in adherence to, ACTED’s stringent protection policies and standards such as neutrality, independence and Do No Harm throughout project implementation. Finally, considerations will be made to ensure that participation is not prohibitive or exclusive in terms of financial or time constraints. To minimise disruption to livelihoods, activities will be carefully planned in line with inputs garnered during community consultations.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

In accordance with ACTED’s Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) policy, ACTED has established a beneficiary Complaints & Response Mechanism (CRM) for beneficiaries and wider stakeholders to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback in a secure way, and receive a response in a timely manner. Throughout the project, targeted communities will be sensitized on the CRM. The CRM is established and implemented in all ACTED’s intervention sites and led by ACTED’s AMEU, under the direct supervision of the Coordination team in Maiduguri. ACTED staff will centrally log each complaint within 5 days of receipt and aim to address the complaint within 15 days. All complaints will be categorized based on relevance, sensitivity and urgency to determine the required response. Standardized CRM tools, including set SOPs and sensitivity levels will be utilized. The CRM will also ensure accountability to beneficiaries’ and build trust between ACTED and the community, while continuously informing program quality
