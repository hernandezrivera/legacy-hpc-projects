Project Summary

Plan International will implement a vital 12-month project aimed at delivering life-saving nutrition services to the most vulnerable children suffering from moderate and severe acute malnutrition (MAM and SAM). This critical project will be operational in outpatient therapeutic program sites, targeted supplementary feeding program locations, and stabilization centers within the Dikwa Local Government Area (LGA) in Borno State, Madagali LGA in Adamawa State, as well as the Nguru, Yusufari, Karasuwa, and Nangere LGAs in Yobe State.
To ensure the long-term success and sustainability of these efforts, the services will be seamlessly integrated into the existing government health facilities. Within the project framework, Plan International will provide comprehensive treatment options for both complicated and uncomplicated forms of severe acute malnutrition at designated outpatient therapeutic programs (OTPs) and stabilization centers (SC) in these targeted areas. Furthermore, maternal, infant, and young child nutrition (MIYCN) promotion and support will be emphasized, particularly in the identified LGAs.
The project will also establish targeted supplementary feeding program (TSFP) sites to effectively address cases of moderate acute malnutrition. A significant focus will be placed on the early identification and treatment of malnutrition through proactive case finding while enhancing and strengthening referral pathways within the LGAs to ensure that children in need receive timely care. To support cognitive development, brain stimulation services will be offered to children diagnosed with severe acute malnutrition.
Recognizing the importance of gender equity, gender-responsive programming will be woven throughout every stage of the project. This will include the incorporation of gender-responsive messaging and child safeguarding practices within community engagement activities, fostering dialogue, and addressing the gender-related issues that affect optimal Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices.
To further combat malnutrition in the community, Plan International will provide comprehensive MIYCN promotion and support through various strategies. These will include counseling sessions, the formation of support groups for mothers and parents, cooking demonstrations to promote healthy feeding practices, and effective behavioral change communication aimed at optimizing infant and young child nutrition.
Additionally, to bolster local capacity and ensure lasting impact, Plan International will invest in training government health workers in the integrated management of acute malnutrition (IMAM) as well as in the inpatient management of severe acute malnutrition with medical complications. This training will empower healthcare professionals to conduct assessments and deliver interventions that promote the well-being of children in their care, ultimately ensuring a sustainable approach to combating malnutrition in these vulnerable communities.

1 - Needs

The 2024 Humanitarian Response Plan indicates that approximately 1.53 million children under the age of five are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition, including around 511,800 who are likely to be severely malnourished. Additionally, it is projected that 207,000 pregnant and breastfeeding women and girls will also experience acute malnutrition throughout the year.Without sustained nutrition assistance, the risks of mortality and lifelong disabilities among survivors may significantly increase. This deterioration is attributed to worsening food and economic crises, a lack of access to healthy diets, conflict-related displacements, poverty, outbreaks of communicable diseases, childhood infectious diseases, and dysfunctional health systems.According to the Round 14 Nutrition and Food Surveillance report, the Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) and Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) rates in Borno are 15.7% and 3.1%, respectively. Adamawa has a GAM and SAM rate of 6.6 and 0.7 while Yobe has a GAM rate of 11.9 and 2.2. The GAM and SAM rates at the LGA level are alarming with eastern Borno (Dikwa) having a GAM and SAM rate of 14.1 and 3.7, Northern Adamawa (Madagali) 6.4 and 0.6, Northern Yobe (Nguru, Karasuwa, Yusufari) 14.9 and 3.5, and Southern Yobe (Nangere) 11.4 and 2.2.The Integrated Phase Food Security Classification (IPC) analysis report, published on November 27, 2024, paints a concerning picture of food insecurity and malnutrition affecting vulnerable populations. It estimates that an alarming 5.4 million children aged between 0 and 59 months are currently suffering from acute malnutrition. Disturbingly, this situation is expected to deteriorate further through 2025. Among these children, nearly 1.8 million are struggling with Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM), a life-threatening condition that requires immediate medical intervention.The report also highlights the plight of pregnant and lactating women, revealing that approximately 787,000 of them are acutely malnourished and in urgent need of treatment. This increase in malnutrition is striking; the number of acutely malnourished children requiring help has surged by 23 percent, and cases of severe acute malnutrition have skyrocketed by an astounding 69 percent compared to the previous IPC analysis conducted in 2023. Furthermore, the number of acutely malnourished pregnant and lactating women has risen by 35 percent, underscoring a widespread crisis that extends beyond just the children.Particularly concerning is the situation in Eastern Borno (Dikwa) and Southern Yobe (Nangere), which has been classified in Phase 3 (serious) during the lean season, a period marked by diminished food availability from May to September. The report warns that this dire situation is anticipated to persist throughout the harvest season (October to December) and into the post-harvest period (January to April), leaving countless families in a state of vulnerability and uncertainty as they struggle to meet basic nutritional needs. The situation in Northern Yobe (Nguru, Karasuwa, and Yusufari) is very alarming with the IPC report revealing a phase classification of 3 (serious) during the lean season. Most importantly, the situation is expected to get worse during the harvest and post-harvest season revealing a deterioration from Phase 3 during the lean season to Phase 4 during the harvest and post-harvest season.Malnutrition in under-five children is a major cause of morbidity and mortality. If it is not treated timely, the likelihood of severe acutely malnourished children dying is 12 times higher than their healthy peers, while children with moderate acute malnutrition are 4 times more likely to die. Children suffering from prolonged undernutrition who survive may become locked in a cycle of recurring illness and faltering growth, with irreversible damage to their development and cognitive ability.

2 - Activities

Strengthen the existing government hospital facility for inpatient management of SAM with medical complications by providing the necessary support as identified by the hospital staff and/or Plan International staff which may include staff training, distribution of SAM treatment guidelines, rehabilitation of building, provision of medical infection prevention items and nutrition supplies as well as technical support and capacity development.Admit and treat children with SAM with medical complications and/or failed poor appetite in established Stabilization centers.Provide gender-responsive and safeguarding social, & behavioral change communication activities in the Stabilization center for SAM children, caretakers, and mothers on maternal infant and young child nutrition and feeding practice, early stimulations, sanitation and hygiene promotion, and other health topics. This includes male engagement sessions for positive IYCF practices. Provide psychosocial support and counseling for SAM children and 743 SAM children caretakers, mothers, and fathers using playing materials and one-to-one counseling to improve the recovery period in the hospital and improve their cognitive development.Organize orientation sessions on GBV and PSEA, safeguarding rules and reporting mechanisms as well as referral pathway and referral of GBV survivors for nutrition staff and Hospital associates in the stabilization center.Ensure and strengthen a good referral system from SC to OTP and vice versa. Provide ongoing monitoring and Joint supportive supervision of program performance indicators on a weekly and monthly basis with Hospital board management, UNICEF, Donors, and SPHCDA.Conduct Refresher training for 20 nutrition staff and government health workers on SAM with medical complication treatment in the Stabilization center (inpatient at Hospital) for six days in Adamawa and Yobe States.Distribute WASH items for all cured/ recovered Discharged complicated SAM cases children from SC to OTP to promote the hygiene and sanitation practices at the Household level.Procure of SC medical supplies and Medicines to treat the medical complications of children admitted with complicated SAM in hospitals/SCs in two LGAs government hospitals.Provide integrated nutrition service with WASH, child protection, SGBV/PSEA, and Health at Hospitals.Conduct weekly and daily MUAC screening, and checking Oedema of children under five in IDP camps and host communities. Establish OTP sites for the treatment of uncomplicated SAM.Enroll children with SAM without medical complications in decentralized OTP every week by the Nutrition officer and health workers.Distribute Ready Therapeutic Food (RUTF) weekly to children with SAM as per the child’s weight as well as prescribe and provide routine drugs as per the national CMAM guidelines.Conduct training on CMAM and IYCF for Plan International nutrition staff and government health workers.Conduct behavioral change activities for children under five, caregivers, and mothers on proper feeding, maternal nutrition, IYCF, key hygiene messages, GBV/PSEA, etc. as well as provide psychosocial support and counseling for children and their caregivers/mothers.Provide training for Community nutrition volunteers on community mobilization, anthropometry, causes of malnutrition, signs and symptoms of malnutrition, hygiene promotion, and referral linkages.Provide Tom Brown treatment for children with moderate acute malnutrition in Madagali in Adamawa State.Establishment of mother to mother and father to father support groups in the community.Cooking demonstration at the community.Training of mothers and caregivers on family MUAC approach.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Plan follows the principle of “Do No Harm” which will be considered in all of its activities, it requires the organization to take specific steps and measures to protect and reduce the risk of harm from all intervention processes and activities, such as inclusive and participatory beneficiary selection, community involvement throughout the implementation, openness about the intervention’s aims and activities, informed consent collection, conducting a risk assessment prior to engagement, as well as ensuring the existence of a functioning and responsive feedback and complaint mechanism at the community level that allows timely action to address issues of concern that may cause or put beneficiaries at risk of harm, reputational risk or create conflict in the beneficiary communities.Plan understands that it is vital that beneficiaries have a strong sense of ownership of the project and that men and women have the ability to assess their own situation including safety and take actions to improve their own and their children’s nutritional status. In line with this, and building on Plan's ongoing experience, sensitization on gender, child protection, inclusion, and safeguarding concerns is included in any training and awareness-raising activity. Other sensitization measures include the mandatory onboarding signature of the Safeguarding Policy and Code of Conduct by Plan Nigeria staff. Nutrition staff and Hospital associates will receive orientation on GBV, safeguarding, etcPlan International ensures protection mainstreaming throughout its intervention. The budget allocation for protection will be utilized for capacity building for Plan International staff as well as partner staff on protection, gender, and safeguarding and the development of sensitization messages and materials that challenge or address these norms. Additionally, Plan International will sensitize the community on safeguarding, GBV, gender equality, PSEA, and SRHR. The estimated budget also includes the budget for a Gender, Psychosocial, and Protection Officer who will be providing support to all the project locations. The project will be implemented through a safeguarding lens and will also focus on challenging gender norms that may exacerbate the deficiency in nutrition found in pregnant and lactating women as well as infants.GBV is a prevailing issue in the communities where the project implementation will take place and affects the mothers and children who are suffering from malnutrition as a result of the norms that exist in these communities. The project will carry out activities of awareness raising on the risks of GBV as well as equip staff with the expertise on what to look out for when addressing GBV and the role they play in its mitigation. The project will also engage different stakeholders in the community to discuss the relationship between GBV and malnutrition and come up with possible actions to address the various causes including the norms existing in the communities. Plan will sensitize and also create awareness among fathers in the community on the linkages between GBV and malnutrition and how it can be addressed. Plan International will create a working relationship with other actors implementing protection in the LGAs to establish/strengthen referral linkages for protection-related concerns. Plan will ensure safe access to nutrition services for women and girls.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will establish a complaints and feedback mechanism that utilizes different types of mechanisms to solicit feedback and complaints from different groups (including child-friendly mechanisms). Child-friendly Mechanism will be used through listening to children, young people, and communities using simple language and illustrations to communicate key messages about the feedback mechanism. PLAN International has a toll-free line for Complaints and Feedback which will be communicated to beneficiaries. This is to ensure the confidentiality of the complaints and feedback. The project participants will be sensitized on the channel of providing feedback. Plan will also develop a feedback register that will be used at the facilities to track feedback received by the project participants on the services rendered in the facility and suggested areas of improvement. This feedback will be used to strengthen the quality of services delivered. Plan will conduct joint project monitoring and supportive supervision along with government sector officials and community representatives to provide stronger evidence that implementation is on the right track and take corrective measures in a timely manner. In addition, project staff will sign the Code of Conduct (CoC) and Plan will provide a comprehensive orientation to all project staff on the principle of accountability and feedback/complaints handling and code of conduct.
