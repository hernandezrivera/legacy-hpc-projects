Project Summary

This project targets 141,000 beneficiaries (30,000 men, 50,000 women, 20,000 boys and 21,000 girls). The proposed interventions will be implemented in 10 Counties of Uror, Twic East, Pochalla, Morobo, Kajo Keji, Yei, Lainya, Aweil East, Aweil Centre, and Aweil South of the 4 states Jonglei, Greater Pibor Administrative Area and Central Equatoria, Northern Bahr el Ghazal respectively. The needs across all three states continued to escalate throughout 2021, with poor harvests, prolonged floods and conﬂict resulting in depleted stocks. Uror, Twic East and Aweil East, Aweil South are in an In South Sudan, where 95 per cent of livelihoods depend on traditional rain-fed agriculture, crop farming, pastoralism and animal husbandry, climate shocks are likely to lead to an increase in livestock mortality and a decline in the amount and viability of land farmers can cultivate. The project has been designed to address the deteriorating food security in the project locations. The proposed intervention will achieve the HRP Strategic and Cluster objectives in line with the sector objectives:
SO1: Life saving:
SO2: Centrality of protection:
SO3: Resilience/ Coping mechanisms vulnerable people withstand & recover from shocks, have their resilience to shocks & stressors built and seek solutions to displacement which respect their rights
CO1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5, 4 & some 3) in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19 (compulsory use of new SOP & guidelines)
CO2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19 (compulsory use of new SOP & guidelines)
CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19 (compulsory use of new SOP & guidelines)
This will be done through the provision of lifesaving assistance to conflict affected communities by providing agricultural inputs and providing training to vulnerable households to increase food production through good agricultural and fisheries practices to improve food security. Emergency livelihood kits distribution, and increasing access to local markets through seed fairs will be organised for local farmers and the project will provide direct distribution of food rations to the most vulnerable communities. Tearfund will also look at ways of resilience-building that reduce recipient’s future risk and vulnerability to negative shocks. The project will engage Community Agricultural Extension Trainers to train the groups on community managed disaster risk reduction; organic manure preparation and basic conservation agricultural practices; local production of climate resilient seeds (cereals and tubers); improved agronomic practices for identified crops; post-harvest handling; marketing and record keeping; soil and environment conservation; farming as a business; improved sustainable agronomic practices; early warning systems.
The proposed project activities will enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, and fishing support in a way that prevents the spread and transmission of COVID 19 with adherence to and compulsory use of new Standard Operating procedures (SOP’s) and guidelines by the Ministry of Health/WHO. The distribution of vegetable seeds will provide the households with a variety of vegetable food for their consumption in a span of 3-4 months, in addition, surplus harvest will be sold in the market for income or preserved for future consumption during the lean season. The Fishing kit’s will help the households get the much needed proteins almost immediately after they receive them and engage in fishing activities.

1 - Needs

There have been several stressors and shocks ranging from drought, floods and conflicts in South Sudan and these have prolonged in the areas of Jonglei, GPA, Central Equatoria and Northern Bahr el Ghazal. The high levels of food insecurity drive humanitarian needs in most parts of the country. Severe food insecurity is expected at the height of the lean season of 2022 due to the impact of conflict, climatic and economic shocks across various locations in South Sudan. In 2022, an estimated 8.3 million people (60% of the total population), including refugees, are expected to experience severe food insecurity by the depth of the lean season (May-July) as shocks appear to be intensifying. In addition, an estimated 2 million people, including 1.3 million children under the age of 5, and 676,000 pregnant and lactating women, are expected to be acutely malnourished in 2022. Uror is ranked among the 13 counties of extreme concern in 2022; Twic East is ranked among the 18 counties of severe concern; while Pochalla, Morobo and Kajo Keji ranked among the 15 counties of high concern. According to the FSL Cluster, Counties of extreme and severe concern will need immediate emergency livelihood assistance across farming, agricultural and pastoral communities, for the medium- and longer-term to build both absorptive and adaptive capacity. Women and girls continue to face extreme levels of gender-based violence and psychosocial distress. People’s coping mechanisms has weakened because of the cumulative shocks, leading families to adopt negative practices such as forced labour and child marriage. Besides the high levels of acute food insecurity there is declined crop production, diseases and pests, the economic crisis, the effects of COVID-19, limited access to basic services,and the cumulative effects of prolonged years of asset depletion and loss of livelihoods. An End of Project Evaluation conducted by Tearfund and CRS in December 2021 showed that 43% of women aged 15-49 achieved minimum dietary diversity across Uror, Pochalla and Twic East counties. Overall, 74% (CI: 66.9-79.4) of the children were exclusively breastfed; 72% (CI: 68.4%-74.4%) of the respondents mentioned at least three critical times to wash hands; and 64% have no latrine. The findings also showed that boreholes (64%) and unprotected sources (28%) are the most common sources of water used by targeted communities.The findings from the June 2021 SMART survey conducted by Tearfund in Uror revealed a Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence of 14.4 % (11.1 - 18.5 95% C.I.) and a Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) prevalence of 2.8 % (1.7 - 4.7 95% C.I.) indicating a ‘high concern’ nutrition situation according to WHO cut off values. The current key livelihood opportunities include cultivation of vegetable and field crops, rearing of cattle, goats and sheep. They provide the most needed vegetables, grains, milk and meat for family meals and some are sold for income for other family needs like clothing, medical and foodstuff. Key crops grown in the areas of Uror, Twic East, Pochalla, Morobo, Kajo Keji, Yei, Lainya, Aweil East, Aweil Centre, and Aweil South include sorghum, maize, Cassava, Kudra (Jute mallow), Cow peas and other vegetables are grown for household consumption. Introduction of other varieties of vegetables by Tearfund through FAO supported programming will enable households to diversify their diets by growing tomatoes, kale, okra, eggplant, onions and watermelon. Based on the findings of a seed security assessment conducted by Tearfund in December 2021, it was observed that seed sources for most farmers are reserved from their harvest. To a large extent except in cases where one depletes their whole produce, the farmers always find comfort in using their own seeds reserved from the preceding season. Alternatively, they may purchase from markets or obtain through social networks.

2 - Project activities

The main activities will include the food and cash distribution, including distribution of complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, and fishing support in a way that prevents the spread and transmission of COVID 19 with adherence to and compulsory use of new Standard Operating procedures (SOP’s) and guidelines by the Ministry ofHealth/WHO. The distribution of vegetable seeds will provide the households with a variety of vegetable food for their consumption in a span of 3-4 months, in addition, surplus harvest will be sold in the market for income or preserved for future consumption during the lean season. The Fishing kit’s will help the households get the much needed proteins almost immediately after they receive them and engage in fishing activities, the households will be encouraged to preserve and or sell surplus fish for future use or earn extra income for the household to address other household needs. The distribution of kits will be conducted across the Bomas in agreed upon distribution sites with local community leaders and local government actors. Project staff, community volunteers and leaders will facilitate distribution and ensure crowd control. Post distribution monitoring meetings will also be implemented in all the selected Boma’s to identify key learnings,record statistical distribution data and confirm receipt on behalf of the communities. To Ensure efficient utilisation of kits by individual recipients, appropriate training on vegetable production, agronomy, post-harvest and sustainable fishing and conservation methods will be delivered. Training sessions will be facilitated by Tearfund’s Food Security And Livelihoods Project officer in collaboration with Government extension workers. Leadfarmers will also be targeted during vegetale kit and seeds training so as to disseminate key learnings to communities. The project will be implemented by a team of staff who are already established and currently implementing crop kit distribution food security programmes, supported by an Advisor with specialist technical knowledge. Tearfund staff in Juba, such as Grants Information Coordinator, will support report writing, monitoring visits and financial transactions throughout this project.The community and other stakeholders will be sensitised on the project and informed of the beneficiary criteria, which will be agreed by community members. During community sensitisation the importance of prioritising women and vulnerable groups will be emphasised in order to gain community acceptance of the selection criteria. Vulnerable HHs will be prioritised for assistance, including female, elderly and child headed HHs, those with malnourished children and people living with disabilities. Beneficiaries will be asked their preferences regarding seeds through FGDs, which will be held separately for men, women and specific vulnerable groups to ensure that vulnerable groups are able to fully participate in and contribute to the discussions. Community members, including representatives of vulnerable groups, will be involved in the selection and verification of beneficiaries.Assessment of physical security and safety risks associated with provision of agricultural inputs and livelihood assistance will be in consultation with all beneficiary groups representing all segments of the community to address their specific needs, capacities and vulnerabilities. Special attention will be paid to groups that are more vulnerable to GBV risks such as women, PLWDs, and people with intellectual and mental disabilities.Recruitment of staff and will take into consideration different ethnic groups that are accepted by the community. The local authorities, community leaders, GBV and protection actors will be engaged to ensure protection issues related to SGBV are addressed and communities are able to have access to productive resources. Access to information on knowledge and tools through innovative approaches will empower women and youth.

5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Tearfund is committed to evidence-based delivery, continuous improvement, value for money, and providing appropriate, flexible aid to communities. So cash and voucher assistance is a key component of our programming as it not only dignifies the beneficiaries, but also enables them to prioritize their own basic needs, to make choices for themselves and do what is best for their household. Where the context allows, cash prompts a private sector response, helps local businesses survive a crisis and be part of a community’s recovery.The usage of Cash or Vouchers is to cater for the various goods and services needed for the program which may involve different supply chains with different logistical issues for commodities, shock affectedness, and cultural contexts including willingness and choice considerations in a dignified manner for the different modalities in consideration of the appropriateness and value for money.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Tearfund has been participating in Joint Market Monitoring Initiatives in the various target counties. The information gathered, plus the analyses from various market assessments showed that there is evidence of sufficient food and seed supplies in some markets hence the ability of the market to participate in the proposed project. Beneficiaries are able to get supplies from their usual suppliers when need be. They are also able to get commodities from other suppliers either within the market or other locations to meet increased demand. A majority of traders restock most of the commodities every one month. They are however able to restock weekly depending on demand, mostly from their alternative suppliers. The major disruptors of market functionality in the target locations include: floods (that make access a challenge), poor/impassable roads, especially during the rainy season, Insecurity, mostly along the Sudan -Uganda border, High taxes which mostly affects those that have to import commodities from neighboring countries. The project will continue with the JMMI activities and market assessments to inform all the Cash and Voucher assistance interventions in the target locations.Tearfund will do systematic analysis of market information to understand how the markets in the various counties function, and how they have been impacted by the crisis. Tearfund is already a member of the Inter Agency Cash working group and the interventions will be in line with the Interagency Cash working guidelines and Joint Market Monitoring Initiative where Tearfund is taking part. Other activities will include analysis of information on supply chains and levels, demand, price changes and income data. Conducting regular project monitoring during implementation and conducting regular market monitoring during implementation including post-distribution monitoring and evaluations of Cash and Voucher Assistance.

Tearfund will collaborate with protection cluster to provide information that seeks to prevent and respond to abuse, neglect, exploitation and violence against women, men, boys and girls, and ensure that a comprehensive complaint mechanism is in place and sagefuarding reports and concerns are investigated. Tearfund will develop Information Education Materials (IEC) and disseminate information to the beneficiaries at the host communities/IDPs/Returnees on the available service providers, locations and services available. The IEC materials will also convey knowledge and skills to young men to adopt gender equitable behaviour.Tearfund has safeguarding policies in place, the staff will be trained on PSEA to undertake safeguarding initiatives to strengthen local capacities and assure their full compliance and understanding on safeguarding policies and practices. PSEA messages together with entitlement cards will be translated into local language to ensure transparency and accountability. Tearfund will embed a robust and timely referral mechanism and form linkages with national child protection actors to refer any identified child protection case.

To support better performance and WASH outcomes at the community, Tearfund and partners will ground their response in the needs, priorities and expectations of the beneficiaries through a number of approaches for example, by engaging in dialogue and joint analysis with beneficiaries on feedback and complaint mechanisms to empower the communities in ways to communicate their needs and concerns and provide the space to make decisions that affect them to enable beneficiaries to act on their own independently. Mapping of existing mechanisms will identify challenges and best practices. The PMC will orient the communities on feedback and complaint mechanisms to ensure their timeliness and responsiveness.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

The project will provide sustainable and high-quality standard services with a disaster risk reduction component. There is a need to provide climate-proofing market infrastructure, drainage systems, services, and behaviours to build community resilience against flood-related displacement. Tearfund will enhance beneficiaries coping mechanisms and local individual measures to build resiliencies to the concurrent shocks through identifying and implementing various flood mitigation measures to reduce risk and exposure during flooding, working to create flood friendly environments, improve the community’s ability to respond during a flood, and identifying strategies for recovery after a flood. This project will address live-saving needs in disaster contexts in restoring immediate access to basic services either temporarily or permanently. The project will also target locations that were affected by flooding in the past three years. It aims at addressing the needs of the most vulnerable with durable solutions and community engagement in finding solutions and maintenance of facilities.Tearfund will implement the proposed activities incorporating Disaster Risk Reduction in order to reduce flood related displacements. Agriculture interventions will be done in consideration of regulations on how land is used, limiting the degradation of environmental resources as possible approaches to reducing the exposure and vulnerability of people. Early warning information will be included in interventions.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

While a majority of HHs in the three Counties rely on markets as a source of food in the lean season, markets are performing at suboptimal levels, access is constrained, and the variety of food and other goods available is limited. Tearfund will integrate FSL with ongoing BHA funded food security and livelihood projects and provide agricultural inputs along with training on field preparation, sowing and transplanting, weeding, use and conservation of resources and pest control to reduce crop losses to vulnerable HHs to increase HH food production. It will focus on organic agricultural practices that have access to proper resources and learn best practices that are more likely to generate much higher crop yields. This allows farmers to not only secure enough for their own needs, but also to sell in local markets. In turn, this enhances the family’s livelihood, strengthens community resilience and furthers the local economy. It will also enable the local communities to earn money and provide for the basic needs of their family. Furthermore, Tearfund will strengthen MIYCN counselling and hygiene promotion activities to boost their knowledge on the importance of diversified and healthy diets for better nutrition.Integration with WASH: Providing safe access to potable drinking water and sanitation facilities including good hygiene practices, will improve the quality of food being consumed at household level, it as well reduces the prevalence of the acute watery diarrhoea case in Children. It gives the mother and caregivers an opportunity to spend more time doing food security and livelihood activities than looking for water. Thus lowers water related diseases, improves nutrition and decreases the IPC level.As poor access to WASH services have a high impact on the health of children, resulting in high malnutrition rate, providing safe drinking water to the community, dissemination of safe hygiene and sanitation practices to the mother/care givers will automatically reduce malnutrition and improve the health of children. Services provided will break the transmission cycle of main communicable diseases (i.e. diarrhea), thus improving food intake of malnourished children and contributing to the sustainability of health and nutrition objectives.Tearfund will distribute emergency food and livelihood kits which will increase food production which will lead to greater food availability, access, and ultimately improved food intake and diets. While distribution of food alone is life saving, Tearfund will collaborate with WFP or GFD implementing partners to see to it that the emergence livelihood support will collaboratively address malnutrition issues. Tearfund will also use the community engagement and training platform for nutrition education and other nutrition-related services. Tearfund also works with UNICEF and WFP in addressing malnutrition in Jonglei, this avenue will be used to refer to acute malnutrition cases.
