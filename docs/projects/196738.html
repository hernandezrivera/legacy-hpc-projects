Project Summary

This project will contribute to preventing and treating malnutrition by providing infant and young child feeding interventions, Micro-nutrient deficiency prevention, control, and improve equitable access to high-quality, lifesaving, curative nutrition services through early identification, referral, and treatment of acutely malnourished children and PLW.
The project will be implemented by nutrition Rapid Response Teams (RRTs) and nutrition facilities.
These distinctive RRTs will conduct HH visits and orientation sessions in areas populated with IDPs and affected people in both Idleb and Aleppo governorates. These RRTs will screen, refer and follow up children 6-59 months, pregnant and lactating women who are malnourished and provide emergency IYCF counselling for mothers whose children are moderately malnourished. Moreover, RRTs and nutrition facilities will distribute nutrition supplements and micronutrients for preventing anemia and malnutrition among children, pregnant and lactating women. Also, they will screen the children regarding general danger signs and educate their mothers about them.
They will do hygiene promotion through their activities and will distribute IEC materials for targeted PLW, girls, and boys. There will be 17 nutrition RRTS teams bound to each of the following health facilities:
PHC centers: Mastumeh, Sijer, Atma, Hamama, Deir Siwan, Sandara, Qabasin, Ar-Ra'ee, Zoghra, Suluk, Tal Abiad, and Hammam At-Turkman
Hospitals: Tell Abiad hospital, Afrin hospital, AlBab hospital, Ar-Ra'ee hospital
The 17th RRT will be based in a new pediatric hospital in Afrin City that will additionally have a ward for nutrition admission to deal with complicated SAM cases.
Each team will provide basic nutrition services (MUAC screening, distribution micro-nutrient, high-energy biscuits, and case referral for treatment). The project duration is 12 months and the target beneficiaries are 175000 from IDPs and Host communities including 4000 PWDs. Beneficiaries’ segregation: children <5 years (115000: 57500 girls + 57500 boys) and PLWs (60000).
On the other hand, this project is an important foundation for integrated primary health and nutrition services and community-based outreach activities, it will include a network of RRT, to provide integrated health and nutrition services focusing on IYCF counseling and screening of children under 5 and referral for treatment of malnutrition at a community level. These RRT will be linked with 17 health facilities. This project will also contribute to treating malnutrition cases in 17 facilities in Idleb, Aleppo, and AlRaqqa.
The project will also focus on building the capacity of the health and nutrition staff on IYCF counseling to provide integrated health and nutrition services. The health facility staff and RRT will be trained in aspects related to child health care and nutritional screening and referral, they will also be provided with sufficient education to help to raise the awareness of the related communities. RRT will cover a serious gap resulting from poor coverage of nutritional services in health facilities. QC will provide training on IYCF principles for all staff in RRT and health facilities, and this training will be conducted for 2 times, each time will include 40 personnel and it will be conducted in Syria.

3 - Needs

After 10 years of conflict, the humanitarian situation in Syria remains alarming as violence and displacement continue to have a devastating impact on millions of people's lives. Immense humanitarian needs persist, including but not limited to WASH, food, health, and nutrition. According to HNO 2021, an estimated 4.3 million people (1.7 million pregnant and lactating women and 3.3 million children under 59 months of age) required nutrition assistance. More than 1.8 million people have 'critical' needs whilst almost 1.9 million have 'severe' nutrition needs. According to SMART survey2022 Chronic malnutrition (stunting) rate was found at 24.5% (20.1 - 29.5 95% C.I). Thus, 220,000 children U5 in NWS are stunted increasing the risk of death, disability and poor cognition and educational performance and The combined GAM rate showed a prevalence of 5.0% (3.3 - 7.5 95% C.I) with a combined SAM rate of 1.7% (0.6 – 4.4 95% C.I).

4 - Implementation of activities

The project activities will be implemented by supporting 20 nutrition Rapid Response Teams "RRTs" in high severity communities and IDPs hosting areas. Each RRT will consist of " a nutritionist, 2 CHWs, Data entry". The teams will provide basic services such as screening of children, pregnant and lactating women for malnutrition signs, and referring them to the mobile clinic or SC, in addition to PSS cases. Moreover, the RRTs will disseminate IYCF-E messages among mothers of children under five through individual counseling and group sessions, facilitating IYCF-E campaigns and integrating them into the Child Health Days, registering all screened cases, and reporting to the nutritionists and data entry team members. M&E team will conduct field visits to ensure the quality of services, verifying the raw data before preparing monthly report, conducting beneficiaries’ satisfaction surveys, Observational checklists-following complaints. QC will share the requested reports including 4Ws and others with doners and cluster. QC will ensure from good coordination and communication with targeted location during all the project title to ensure from smooth work. QC will ensure from sharing 4W and other required reporting with the cluster and donor also, the MEAL team will do their work to ensure from the quality of the services provided including using quality checklist, satisfaction surveys, observation checklist, IPTT ,daily and weekly and monthly MEAL reports.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Gender-Based Violence (GBV) affects accessing nutrition services. The likelihood of this risk is low, but the impact will significant if took place. Mitigating Measures: Provide nutrition services inaccessible easy to reach areas. Training of nutrition RRTs on Prevention and management of sexual violence. The presence of female staff in the nutrition teams increases access of women and girls to the nutrition services. Risk: Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) of women: The likelihood of this risk is low, but the impact will significant if took place. Mitigating measures: Establish effective community-based complaints mechanisms, awareness-raising on SEA amongst nutrition team members, with continuous training on the topic. Performance management and monitoring of all team members. Risk: Lack of Duty of care for Nutrition RRTs. Likelihood is low, with a significant impact if took place. Mitigating measures: DOC policy in place and applied for all staff, measures ensuring regular and consistent delivery of salaries, bearing in mind the ethical and logistical imperative to go further than basic labor rights. Risk: Inequitable access to nutrition services by Vulnerable Groups. Likelihood is low and impact is moderate. Mitigating measures: Implementation of standard-essential services package across the sector interventions, including improved quality reporting and robust monitoring through standardized SOPs. Addressing the specific needs of vulnerable groups.

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Increased malnutrition rate due to increasing the opportunity of diseases and medical conditions that needs professional intervention. Mitigating measure: - Hire qualified medical staff - Distribute nutritional supplies and rations, QC assumes northwest Syria areas will be stable for the next year. The risk of increasing pressure on health services influx of IDPs is very high. Program activities will be negatively impacted when the number of IDPs in some areas suddenly increases.Risk: COVID19 and Cholera pandimic, mitigation :strict applying IPC. Risk: non renewal of SCR,mitigation: QC has channels of funding and has cross-border access to pass commodities to Syria with its wide local coordination with local authorities. Risk/Threat: More Cholera cases in NW Syria Likelihood: High, Impact: high Mitigating measures: • QC is applying recommended IPC measures at the health facility • Screen and isolate all beneficiaries with suspected cholera case at the entrance of the facility and refer them to cholera treatment facilities • Enhance wash activities and ensure periodic chlorination of water tanks of the hospital. • Disseminating cholera awareness messages about cholera to the community through outreach community health workers. • Activate oral rehydration points taking into consideration all needed WaSH criteria • Applying periodic chlorination and other IPC procedures within the hospital
