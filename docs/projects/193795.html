Project Summary

The project will contribute to the Sudan humanitarian response plan and specifically the food security and livelihoods (FSL) sector as it focuses on addressing life threatening, life protection and life-sustaining needs through timely provision of life saving cash/vouchers assistance for the purchase of agricultural inputs and garden tools to reduce mortality and morbidity of the affected people while supporting and protecting livelihoods activities. The project will support a target of 18,000 individuals (10,800 females and 7,200 males) comprised of 10,000 internally displaced persons (IDPs) (6,000 Females, 4,000 Males), and 8,000 non-displaced vulnerable people (4,800 Females, 3,200 Males) in Gedaref and White Nile States prioritizing new displacements due to the ongoing armed conflict.
NRC focuses on needs-based approach to reach the most in need locations and groups while promoting the Do No Harm principle and protecting target beneficiaries, staff and other stakeholders from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). The project will make use of NRC’s community engagement toolkit to communicate and engage with the target communities in deciding on the beneficiary selection criteria and the implementation of the activities. Socio-economic vulnerabilities based on age, gender, disability, and nutrition status will be considered in the beneficiary selection criteria for the life-saving interventions. Children under the age of five, pregnant and lactating women, all gender types, the youth, women, girls, people with disabilities, the elderly and chronically ill people make up a special target group for NRC’s food security projects.
Analysis of the situation in terms of gaps, capacities and market functionality will precede response to inform the project on the most suitable modality for project interventions. Nutrition awareness messaging with special focus on food hygiene and diet diversity will be developed and shared with the target groups. The project will also include market support interventions through the provision of cash grants to identified small scale traders to enhance their capacity to stock food commodities in their shops and to sustain their businesses while increasing food commodities on the local markets for displaced people.
To ensure accountability to affected populations (AAP) the project will use NRC’s locally tested complaints and feedback handling mechanism (CFM) that has multiple channels that the affected people can use to register their complaints and feedback. The system allows for recording and follow-up of sexual exploitation and abuse cases related to the project implementation. The monitoring and evaluation activities capture achievements on project objectives and target beneficiaries’ satisfaction of their engagement and treatment during project implementation.

1 - Needs

Sudan is currently undergoing a turbulent political period compounded by a myriad of challenges including, high levels of food insecurity, an economic crisis, armed conflict and protracted internal displacement without viable durable solutions prospects and increased insecurity. Efforts to advance the political transition and peace process faced significant challenges culminating in a military take-over that overthrew the transitional government in October 2021. Over 1 million people have been displaced since the armed conflict between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) began on 15 April, fleeing to safer locations inside and outside Sudan. Over 1,361,308 million people have been newly displaced, including 1,042,114 displaced internally and 319,194 have fled to neighbouring countries i.e. Central African Republic, Chad, Egypt, Ethiopia and South Sudan, according to the UN Refugee Agency (UNHCR). The highest figures of displacement have been reported in West Darfur (23.86%), White Nile (19.65%), River Nile (15.64%) and Northern States (13.93%). According to the revised Sudan HRP for 2023, updated needs analyses indicate that there has been a 57 per cent upward revision in the number of people estimated to be in need, from 15.8 million to 24.7 million, as well as a significant deterioration in humanitarian conditions. According to the Sudan FSL cluster, food insecurity forecasts indicate that as rural areas enter the hunger gap period and if the conflict continues, the number of food insecure people in Sudan is expected to increase by up by 2.5 million to 19.1 million people across the country in the next three to six months. About 40 per cent of the population in phase 2 of the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) could slide into phase 3. Disruptions in supply chains and markets has caused the price of food and other essential commodities to increase by 400 per cent compared to before the conflict. If the current conflict continues, the price of the local food basket is projected to increase by a further 25 per cent in the next three-six months. This increase in food prices will, in turn, result in over 18 million people being unable to afford the local food basket. Existing insecurity has eroded livelihoods, damaged farms, and triggered widespread unemployment. This critical situation has constrained pastoralists, agro-pastoralists, and farmers to abandon their crops if they do not receive immediate emergency livelihood support. This situation has left grave consequences not only for their food security but also on food availability in Sudan and may lead to more conflict and displacement.The internally displaced (IDPs) are among sections of the population impacted the most among the people facing high food insecurity in Sudan. Such identified, yet unmet, priority needs for the Internally Displaced and their geographical location in areas in IPC 3 and above, will guide the NRC in prioritization when planning for cash/voucher support to increase vulnerable small holder farmers’ access to garden tools and nutritious seeds.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Community engagement activities will be conducted before the start of project activities to ensure that critical information about the project activities reaches all targeted displaced populations especially the minority gender groups, the elderly and people with disabilities. The project will reach 18,000 individuals (10,800 females and 7,200) males) with cash/voucher support for the purchase of fast maturing nutritious agricultural seeds and garden tools in addition to Market Support Interventions through the provision of cash grants to 30 small-scale traders. Project activities will be implemented to enable targeted households to access food, diversify diets, and reduce negative coping strategies. A market-based approach will be used to identify relevant modalities which will include cash/voucher transfers. The project targets 18,000 individuals with cash/vouchers to cover their food needs during the crisis and the peak hunger season in 2023. In addition, 30 small scale traders will be supported with cash grants to enhance their capacity to stock their shops and sustain their operations while increasing access to food commodities on local markets for displaced populations. The 18,000 individuals who will be targeted by the action will include 8,000 vulnerable non-displaced people, and 10,000 internally displaced people. The intervention will contribute and utilize the monthly market monitoring information coordinated by the World Food Program (WFP). Where necessary, this information will be used to adjust the transfer modalities and the value of transfers to prevent negative impacts on the local markets. Awareness creation on the use of cash and household gender dynamics will be addressed through consultative focus group discussions before and after distributions. This will be complimented by post distribution monitoring and the establishment of a complaints and feedback mechanism. Project staff will be responsible for community engagement and sensitization with consecutive meetings with local authorities. The protection of target beneficiaries will remain of prime importance and will determine the approaches and the way the project activities will be implemented to foster the Do No Harm principle and creation of safe spaces for target beneficiaries. Monitoring of project activities will be done by the project team, target beneficiaries and other stakeholders with support from the NRC monitoring and evaluation (M and E) department and Digital Community Hub (DCH). Needs, baseline and endline assessments will be conducted at appropriate times to inform the project design and understand the impact of the project. Project monitoring activities will include exit interviews during distributions, post distribution monitoring and post implementation monitoring.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Assessments and field observations during interventions that NRC has conducted in Gedaref, South Kordofan, White Nile states from 2020 to 2022 have indicated that displacement-affected communities are particularly affected by the rising scarcity of natural resources and, therefore, prone to adopt non-sustainable coping strategies exacerbating the already existing tensions with host communities. Due to their relatively cheap and ease of access, natural resources have become the most extensively used resources for energy and income (charcoal and firewood), wild fruits and foods, farming and grazing of livestock in communities affected by displacement leading to their depletion and prone to causing conflicts. This finding was supported by secondary data that shows that in Sudan, land and other land resources such as water, forests, and rangelands have historically been at the heart of conflicts. In this backdrop, climatic changes and environmental degradation in Sudan are stressing the country’s rain fed agriculture and pastoralist livelihoods. This has led to increased food insecurity, worsening poverty, protracted displacement, and renewed conflict. Unsustainable (and unlawful) natural resource management by authorities, businesses (including mining industry), formal and informal armed groups and communities are exacerbating tensions. Raising awareness on effective and environmentally sustainable practices, will not only foster peaceful co-existence but will create new livelihoods opportunities for the affected communities. NRC plans to raise environmental management awareness for target populations during project community sensitization sessions towards improving natural resources management.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

This project will actively contribute to the implementation of the NRC’s global Safe and Inclusive Programming (SIP) Minimum Standard in its activities which ensures protection mainstreaming in all projects. The project will actively and timely incorporate SIP related requirements in its technical tools for community engagement, assessment, monitoring and evaluation. The protection environment is not sufficiently safe or stable. The project has therefore taken into consideration risk analysis and mitigation measures to reduce the impact. The target communities were involved in the identification of protection and security risks in their locations and the analysis has been captured in NRC's area and country strategies. These risk assessments remain living documents as they are continuously updated during specific periods of the year and when certain events take place in an area. NRC practices SIP across all its projects through training of project staff to increase their capacity to engage with communities in identifying protection issues for the different gender, age and socio-economic minority groups. Awareness raising of communities is carried out to enable them to understand protection issues that can affect different people by gender, age, socio-economic and displacement status. Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) training is mandatory for all NRC staff and this is cascaded to all casual laborers, incentive staff and project management committees. The Do No Harm principle and accountability to affected populations (AAP) will be upheld ensuring all aspects of the project and takes into consideration not only the needs of the elderly, the sick, people with disabilities, women, youth, men, and children but ensuring understanding of the risks that might negatively impact them as being part of the project. Information about the CFM will be provided to target communities to build their “speak out culture” while ensuring confidentiality especially for sexual exploitation and abuse complaints. A two-way communication system will be established between NRC and the target communities using diverse methods to capture issues from all target groups. The CFM in place took into consideration the different channels of choice that are accessible to target communities. The project will continue to actively support the monitoring and evaluation team in the establishment of effective complaints and feedback response mechanisms in communities and will ensure that complaints received are timely addressed by relevant staff according to the type of complaint as guided by NRC's standard operation procedures.Help desks, exit interviews, post distribution monitoring and end line assessments will provide the target communities and target beneficiaries with the opportunity to provide their feedback on processes and procedures regarding project activities.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

NRC designed and trained its staff in using community engagement tools that allow for a continuous interaction between its projects and the target communities. NRC has a systematic sharing of information at specific periods of the project that is monitored to ensure timely sharing of relevant information with the communities. This interaction starts with the understanding of needs through assessments that will be conducted before implementation of the project activities in order to inform the design and scope of the project in each target community. The project will conduct community mobilization and sensitization sessions to inform and provide details of the assistance to the target communities. NRC has put in place a community feedback system that allows target beneficiaries to assess and comment on the performance of the project. This includes sensitive matters including sexual exploitation and abuse, fraud, corruption and all forms of discrimination and exclusion. NRC's system has capacity to provide timely responses to the issues raised by communities and beneficiaries as these are tracked and supervised by senior management.The complaints and feedback handling mechanism has multiple ways or channels of collecting information from target community members including people living with disabilities, those who are illiterate, women and girls. The mechanism provides for anonymous complaints and feedback to be captured and uses different media and methods that the affected can choose to use. The feedback mechanism will allow the project to adapt project assistance based on information received. Monitoring and evaluation activities will measure if beneficiaries felt well-informed and respected during project delivery and their overall satisfaction with the project. The evaluation will reflect how the project tracked and responded to beneficiary complaints and how the feedback shaped the project delivery. NRC will contribute to the food security cluster reporting mechanisms and participate in any other AAP efforts by the humanitarian sector including referral systems for SEA and other abuse complaints.
