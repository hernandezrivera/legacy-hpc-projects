Project Summary

POINT has implemented many infrastructure rehabilitation activities since 2015, the last project was a rehabilitation of a local market inside Idleb city, and POINT has active activities related to the ERL sector. This project delivers a strategic and integrated set of interventions to restore the local economy and increase the resilience of vulnerable communities in the Afrin sub-district in the aftermath of protracted conflict further aggravated by the deteriorating economic situation. The project’s main objective is to increase resilience and access to livelihood opportunities for vulnerable communities through diverse livelihood opportunities and rehabilitation of critical infrastructure facilities in the Afrin sub-district. The project proposes a comprehensive, livelihood intervention where the most vulnerable households (HHs) (those referred from Protection actors and PwDs) will be directly targeted to enhance their resilience and promote their access to employment. The project has 2 components and all of them are solely ERL. They aim to enhance access to livelihood opportunities and increase the resilience of conflict-affected and vulnerable households and communities. The project formulation is based on needs identified in RNA/REACH/HNAP data. POINT team has conducted a rapid need assessment to identify the needs in the targeted areas in addition to holding a meeting with the local council and relevant stakeholders in the Afrin Sub-district.
The project aims to achieve the following:
Equitable access to basic services is improved through rehabilitating basic economic infrastructures ( industrial zone) in the Afrin sub-district linked with creating short-term livelihood opportunities.
Access to inclusive livelihoods and entrepreneurship support is enhanced through an innovative and unique three-phase scheme (one-month vocational training, one-month apprenticeship, and four-month job placement).
Local workshops owners are supported to enhance their capacities to train and host the three-phase s
The first component is infrastructure rehabilitation in the Afrin district in Aleppo governorate which was prioritized during the rapid assessment and FGD held with the LC and local community representatives, the area is public property and belongs to the LC. The rehabilitation will cover the rehabilitation of the sewage systems, water network, roads inside the industrial zone, lighting, sidewalks, and rain umbrellas for shops. the percentage of the damage is estimated to be 40 %. about 300 workshops are working inside the industrial zone of Afrin, those workshops will be enrolled in the second component of the expansion and business support activities. It’s worth mentioning that POINT has good experience in rehabilitation and worked in several rehabilitation works like market and road rehabilitation with UNDP, and light and heavy rehabilitation activities in 40 camps with IOM and SCHF - OCHA in NWS.
The project's second component will identify 200 individuals with a specific focus on severe economic vulnerability to provide them with an integrated and innovative three-phase scheme encompassing: 1) vocational training at fully-functional workshops, 2) apprenticeship, and 3) job placement. The three-phase VT will be provided by local workshop owners who will be targeted in the business expansion component. Thirdly, the project will target 60 local business and workshop owners from the rehabilitated industrial zone by providing a holistic approach to capacity development and business support, including business management and leadership, business expansion cash grant, labor force, business mentoring, coaching, and supervision to expand their business and create sustainable long-term employment opportunities that will continue to impact the local community well beyond the completion of this project. These same workshop owners will host the three-phase training scheme mentioned in the previous component.

3 - Needs

Syria remains a complex humanitarian and protection emergency characterized by over 10 years of ongoing hostilities and their long-term consequences including widespread destruction of civilian infrastructure, explosive ordnance contamination, and the largest number of internally displaced people in the world. More than ten years of crisis have inflicted immense suffering on the civilian population, who have been subject to massive and systematic violations of international humanitarian and human rights law. More recently, the accelerating economic deterioration and impacts of climate change have increasingly become additional key drivers of needs, compounding vulnerabilities even further. In 2022, 14.6 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 1.2 million from 2021.Long-standing needs of an estimated 6.9 million IDPs remain staggering, particularly for over two million people in 1,760 informal settlements and planned camps, often hosted in inadequate shelter. Specific need assessment for this project has been conducted by the POINT team which aimed to assess the local markets and livelihood conditions, civilian infrastructure, and productive and income-generating facilities that were damaged during the war and need to be rehabilitated to get back to production phase and secure more livelihoods opportunities, and the needs for vocational training and financial support in the market to secure more livelihood opportunities for small scale businesses

4 - Implementation of activities

The project aims to participate in improving access to livelihood opportunities and increasing the resilience of conflict-affected and vulnerable households and communities in order to reduce the risk of resorting to negative coping strategies in the context of the Covid-19 emergency. Moreover, this project aims to help the targeted communities to overcome the severe economic deterioration situation and enable them to get back control of their lives and reduce their reliance on humanitarian assistance. Besides that, the project aims to increase social cohesion through working for and with communities by including them in the need assessment and the project design phase, and later, by targeting both host communities and IDPs in the project activities. On the other hand, the project aims to mobilize local resources by rehabilitating one of the public facilities (industrial zone) and linking it to the other activities in this project. income-generating activities will be through the creation of long-term job CFW and apprenticeship for more than four months inside the workshops and PWDs will be included in all activities and will be given priority in the selection criteria with consideration of disability and gender disaggregation, in addition to women HH and protection referrals from other protection service providers at the same targeted location. The MEAL team will follow up on the activities during the project period according to the MEAL plan and logical framework.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Collaboration from local councils in Dana for approval from the local community and availability of rehabilitation needed resources.Risk1: intervening with local authorities and local council:Mitigation: coordination with the local council and local authorities before starting the rehabilitation and getting required approvals and signing an agreement identifying each party's role and responsibility, additionally prepare the handover plan of the shops after the project end. Risk 1: COVID-19 and Cholera spread in target communities: Mitigation: POINT will take all necessary measures to avoid the transmission and spread of the disease infection, such as wearing a face mask, and gloves and securing them for each participant, providing hand sanitizers, and measuring the temperature of the beneficiaries before entering the training halls.Risk 2: Local community discourages women's participation in CFW.Mitigation: Selection criteria will be followed and well explained to the local community, and the project will enroll women in culturally accepted tasks such as attendance sheets, distributing and maintaining workers’ safety tools, and tasks inside the facilities.Risk3: Apprenticeship may cause disruption to local labor markets. Mitigation: Wages have been set below the average level and the number of days that workers work on the project is less than in the private sector


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

POINT is strongly committed to protecting children and women from abuse, a commitment that is reflected in their Child Protection and PSEA Policies and is a member of PSEA network and follows the guidelines. Gender is mainstreamed in the design of proposed project, ensuring that the needs of women are considered and properly addressed. During the beneficiaries’ selection phase, specific emphasis will be put on women-headed households and their specific vulnerability to protection concerns and reduced access to employment opportunities due to their gender, ensuring women have equal access to the provided services. POINT will closely monitor protection risks throughout the implementation period and will ensure coordination with Protection Cluster partners. POINT has developed mitigation measures set in place to ensure that protection is mainstreamed in all project activities and GBV risk is minimized to the minimum level: Ensuring that protection principles are incorporated throughout all project activities, including the promotion of meaningful access, and ensuring the safety and dignity of beneficiaries. POINT trains its field staff to safely identify and refer protection concerns to the correct unit(s) and external actors to reduce risks and support safe and dignified access for all affected populations. Ensuring that staff members are trained on following do not harm principles in interventions and signed a code of conduct, receive PSEA training, and have a reporting system
