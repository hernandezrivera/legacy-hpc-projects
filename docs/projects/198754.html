Project Summary

The response to Mental Health and Psycho-Social Support (MHPSS) needs remains insufficient in the Cabo Delgado conflict. The number of victims is enormous, access to survivors is difficult due to insecurity and long distances, and the capacity of stakeholders to provide quality MHPSS, child protection and GBV services is limited. There is a clear lack of resources and technical capacity in MHPSS of the existing local actors. Also, women and girls may remain vulnerable to GBV due to lack of support from community structures, gender stereotypes and difficult access to integrated support services for women and girls victims of GBV.
Few survivors have access to comprehensive case management (psychosocial support, health care, legal and social services, and protection). Socio-cultural barriers such as stigma and lack of community awareness also hinder victims and particularly women from seeking help. Many of the local associations that implement support activities for displaced people are local development actors who do not have experience in MHPSS and GBV programs. There is a need to strengthen the capacities of community health workers and health professionals (training, technical manuals adapted to the context) to avoid providing an inappropriate response that could harm victims.
Armed conflicts provide situations of violence with different types of impacts (psychological, physical, health, economic and social) on the lives of survivors such as trauma, fear, anger, shame, loss of self-esteem and self-confidence, guilt, aggression, panic attacks, loss of confidence, feeling of vulnerability, difficulty sleeping, difficulty concentrating, difficulty maintaining love relationships, post-traumatic stress disorder, behavioural disorders - Psychological; increased number of disabled or incapacitated women, - Physical; impaired reproductive capacity, proliferation of sexually transmitted diseases, malnutrition, proliferation of diseases due to lack of medical treatment and medication - Health; hunger, practice of prostitution for survival, unemployment, increased rural and urban poverty, homelessness, lack of livelihood - Economic; the collapse of their families and communities, several homeless or homeless women, many women unable to return to their families, orphans or widows, single mothers, refugee or displaced women, illiteracy and education, stigmatisation, social rejection, ill-treatment - Social.
The strategies carried out by DSF in the context of past projects show that through the MHPSS to people presenting the aforementioned manifestations, it is possible to obtain satisfactory results. With the action of local partners trained by the technical team of DSF, an optimized and holistic monitoring of the beneficiary is carried out to provide integrated support and contribute to building the resilience of victims.
The General objective of the project is “to improve access to MHPSS and GBV services in the context of the armed conflict in Cabo Delgado” by: 1: Improving access to assistance and care services for victims of conflict and host communities (including children, women, and men, GBV victims, people with disabilities) in Cabo Delgado province, enabling a more significant number of victims to have access to complete, safe and inclusive MHPSS, Child protection and GBV services by international standards and coordinated with other institutional and humanitarian actors; 2: Mitigating the risks of GBV in targeted communities, IDP camps, and households.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The General objective of the project is “to improve access to MHPSS and GBV services in the context of the armed conflict in Cabo Delgado” by: 1: Improving access to assistance and care services for victims of conflict and host communities (including children, women, and men, GBV victims, people with disabilities) in Cabo Delgado province, enabling a more significant number of victims to have access to complete, safe and inclusive MHPSS, Child protection and GBV services by international standards and coordinated with other institutional and humanitarian actors; 2: Mitigating the risks of GBV in targeted communities, IDP camps, and households.There are many accounts of victims of the conflict (children, women and men) traumatised by the events they have experienced. The need for MHPSS is indeed high among vulnerable people affected by violence, who have witnessed extremely violent events, have suffered multiple losses and live with constant stressors and fear. Trauma is deep and care is needed to restore resilience to crisis situations, dignity and an "almost" normal life. Protection and referral mechanisms need to be strengthened.As in any large-scale humanitarian crisis, existing evidence suggests that GBV has been a key feature of the conflict, especially for women and girls. It is a life-threatening health and protection issue that often continues beyond the initial emergency phases. The crisis has compounded multiple forms of GBV including intimate partner violence (IPV), physical and sexual violence, abduction, sexual trafficking, sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), early and forced marriage, and economic violence. Children should be an obvious target of MHPSS activities. Many have experienced traumatic events that can impact their growth, their socio-educational capacities and their general quality of life if the symptoms they develop are not taken care of. Similarly, children are still likely to be victims of GBV and/or physical and social violence in IDP camps, due to the harsh socio-cultural parameters (economic vulnerability of families). Although there are more than 15 actors implementing MHPSS activities, the needs remain enormous and MHPSS services usually provides only one session or "awareness raising" on wellbeing and resilience. At the community level, local organisations that implement support activities for displaced persons are local development actors with little concrete experience in MHPSS. It is necessary to strengthen the capacities of community health workers (training, technical manuals adapted to the context) in order to avoid providing an inappropriate response that could harm victims.The province has only 14 psychologists and 2 occupational therapists working in the health facilities. According to an ICRC survey (2021), MHPSS medical staff from the Ministry of Health report that they have not received any mental health training or technical supervision after graduation, except for some training on HIV-related topics, and clearly lack the skills and experience to support people affected by violence. On the other hand, medical staff working in health facilities are not prepared to recognise and care for people in need of MHPSS, and therefore referrals to MHPSS services are not made according to existing needs. DSF will reinforced the HNS. Thus, inspired by the IASC pyramid, DSF is largely in line with a desire to transfer skills. The referral mechanism that will be put in place will allow us to offer access to health services and increasingly specialised psychosocial follow-up depending on the problems encountered. Because of its mandate and past experience in emergency, also thanks to the diversity of situations and needs encountered in the field, DSF has enriched its methods of action in MHPSS, allowing it to position itself as one of the most experienced actor in Mozambique.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Outcome 1.1: Victims of conflict and host communities (including children, women and men, GBV survivors, people with disabilities) have access to quality and coordinated MHPSS services. To meet the needs of victims and improve access to care, a team of 5 psychologists, based in Pemba, will implement various care and support activities for victims in the camps and host communities of the closest districts. DSF will provide direct care for 1100 people in needs of MHPSS services including 400 children and 400 women and girls who suffered GBV. DSF offers patients a healthy framework of listening, neutrality and benevolence, while respecting the notion of confidentiality. The methodologies are adapted to the needs of the victims in order to take care of them without risking further injury. For each boarding, monitoring tools are developed to track the psychosocial care of patients. A1: Provision of MHPSS to Victims of conflict and host communities in “BIAP” (Information and Psychosocial Support Balcony): DSF will provide MHPSS through a mobile BIAP in IDPs camps and communities and through a stationary BIAP located in Pemba. BIAPs are safe and confidential spaces.A2: Establish technical partnerships with MHPSS, child protection and GBV actors.A3: Sensitize and refer victims to MHPSS, Child Protection and GBV related services: to refer and accompany victims in accessing legal, social and health services. Outcome 1.2: Service providers working with Victims of conflict and host communities are trained and adequately resourced.During an evaluation carried out in March 2022, DSF identified a great need to support local organisations, but also health professionals, to gain experience in providing care adapted to the needs of patients. Capacity building will be carried out through training, but above all through technical monitoring in the field and/or at a distance of the trained actors, to ensure that they apply the methodologies and techniques learned during the training.A1: Study context-specific MHPSS methodologies (GBV, socio-cultural parameters) and development of IEC material and MHPSS technical guideline adapted for different target groups.A2: Training and technical support to Community Health Workers (CHWs) from local partners (OSC/OCB): DSF will strengthen the capacity of 200 CHWs to provide psychological first aid to 4,000 victims (including children and survivors of GBV) through training and technical supervision. The CHWs will also be able to identify victims in need of reinforced MHPSS service and will be able to rely on the established referral systems to direct them to DSF, to the Health Units or to other partners.A4: Training and technical support to health professionals: Similarly, DSF will build the capacity of 100 health professionals and 100 psychology students to strengthen the responsive capacity of the National Health System to allow more than 2000 victims in need of MHPSS services to be catered for.Outcome 2.1: Women and girls' risk of GBV is reduced in target communities, IDP camps and within the family (20 000 persons sensitized on GBV – 5000 women and girls trained on GBV prevention). A1: Sensitization and mobilization of the community, community and religious actors on the different forms of GBV: Through advocacy and awareness-raising campaigns that will influence the change of behaviour of the population, and in particular that of men, with regard to GBV.A2: Creation of support groups among women and girls, including GBV victims and carers of people and children with disabilities: with a view to developing resilience. Members will be sensitised to become peer educators, to raise awareness of the target audience about GBV and disabilities as a way to ensure access to information and quality health care, breaking barriers in the context of stereotypes.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

In order to reduce potential risks, we will analyse the cultural context and customs in order to gain a detailed understanding of the customs that can play a role in the perpetuation of GBV. We will also identify the social and community structures that have been identified as an unfailing support for victims. We will also study the collective dynamics that will allow us to refine our individual care. The evaluation phase of the case management process will enable us to propose an individual care pathway. This involves understanding the situation, priorities and immediate health and safety needs of a survivor.In order to avoid causing harm, our team will be trained in stigma reduction. Respect for confidentiality and consent will be central to our psychosocial care. In view of the risks that this may entail, a victim will never be forced to follow a course of treatment with which he or she is not comfortable, or to go to legal aid. Our listening will be based on respect and encouragement. The locations of the activities will be safe and their location will allow victims to avoid travelling too far in order to reduce the risks involved. Services for survivors of GBV should be open to all, including men. However, we will ensure that we provide these services without compromising the integrity of women's spaces. It is important to provide a space in which women can speak freely, without risk of censorship or reprisal. Ensuring that men and boys also receive psychosocial support will help to ensure that the benefits are better understood and accepted so that women and girls benefit as well. Alleviating psychological distress among boys and men should also have a positive impact at the household level, particularly with regard to marital interactions, as well as at the community level, by promoting social cohesion and thus helping to reduce protection risks, including sexual violence. Awareness-raising and psycho-education activities on issues related to gender equality or the promotion of women's and girls' reproductive rights will help to consolidate community commitment. By also targeting community health workers, traditional healers, community and religious leaders, community resilience and overall understanding will be strengthened.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Our psychosocial support activities will develop a personalised action plan in which the counsellor and the survivor work together to identify and link her to appropriate services in a safe and supportive manner. Confidentiality and informed consent will be present throughout the process. Within this framework, a regularly updated mapping of available services will be our working tool to link survivors with existing services. The actions should also help the survivor to consolidate or develop social links, to undertake activities that develop her sense of autonomy. These activities are important to help her regain a sense of well-being and control over the situation, and to remove the guilt of being a victim again. Sexual violence has serious long-term consequences for women's physical, sexual, reproductive and mental health. It is a deeply transgressive and painful experience for the survivor. Because of the stigma, a victim of GBV has fewer people to come into contact with who can help her regain her confidence, feel accepted again and know how to make the right choices for herself. Our intervention will therefore counteract this damaging dynamic that impacts not only on the survivor and her family but also on the community and society as a whole. There are many barriers to accessing care: lack of transportation, lack of money to pay for services or transportation to access services, lack of childcare options, lack of awareness of services, isolation, stigma, which are some of the many reasons why a survivor may not want to disclose what happened to her. Our intervention will therefore provide information on accessing and facilitating access to health services, as well as important messages on what sexual violence is and the emotional reactions to it. It is essential that victims regain a place in this situation that is beyond their control, and that they become agents of change. We give them the opportunity to express themselves while respecting those who do not wish to say anything. Our response in terms of psychosocial support will be adapted to the age of the patient. Indeed, the problems and psychic reorganisations differ according to the period of life. While adolescent girls are in the midst of a maturational crisis and may be confronted with the issues of early marriage and potential GBV victims, older women are no less affected. The relationship with the body is different according to age and the discourse must be adapted to this in a simple and clear way. The technical capacities of psychosocial workers will therefore be strengthened in this respect. Specific training on dealing with emotional distress and traumatic symptoms, including case management, will be provided. Participants will also be sensitised to the prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA).

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Survivors with disabilities may be particularly vulnerable to violence, because of the power dynamics associated with dependency on caretakers. But also because of the limitations they may have if they have to flee from danger. Our communication will be adapted to the survivor's knowledge and abilities, especially if they live with a disability. It is important to involve the responsible persons (family carers) of the survivors, especially if they are children. However, this also entails a certain risk, as they may also be the aggressors. DSF pays particular attention in MHPSS sessions to the dynamics between the survivor and the carer.It should always be assumed that a survivor can provide informed consent, but DSF ensures that the survivor understands what is happening throughout the process. If the survivor is unable to provide informed consent and/or when their carers are involved in the case management process, DSF uses the principles of safety, empowerment, cost-benefit assessment and therapeutic benefit to determine whether an action is in the best interest of the survivor.Creation of support groups for carers of people with disabilities (mothers) can, under the guidance of CHW or DSF psychologist, create emotional support groups to exchange positive experiences and technical advices, provide emotional support, carry out recreational activities, or receive support from health professionals (therapeutic training, group therapy by a psychologist). In this way, women carers will have their mental strength and technical knowledge reinforced. Similarly, the establishment of these groups is a form of combating the stigmatisation of disability in communities, revaluing the role of people with disabilities.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

DSF took part in the cyclone season preparation exercise in Sofala province, prepared by the protection cluster (UNHCR) and the INGC, positioning itself as one of the main actors in the MHPSS response for potential victims of a natural disaster in Sofala province. DSF has in its MHPSS packaged an emergency psychological first aid kit, consisting of a technical guideline and training modules for local actors to improve their capacity to provide psychosocial care to victims of natural disasters. DSF could, in the event of a cyclone emergency, participate to preparedness plan and implement MHPSS activities for the victims of the disaster.
