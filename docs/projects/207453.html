Project Summary

The project is designed to contribute to a reduction in WASH-related public health risks through improved access for 367,220 people living in 14 UNHCR-managed camps.
The WASH response will be based on the following three sector strategic objectives to ensure; 1) regular, sufficient and equitable access to safe water for drinking and domestic needs; 2) adequate and appropriate sanitation facilities allowing safe management and disposal of solid and human waste; and 3) the standardized distribution of hygiene items and target health-compromising behaviour and gaps through community engagement with a particular focus on contagious diseases.
UNHCR and its partners will ensure continuity of essential WASH services through a combination of service provision activities involving the operation and maintenance of an existing 95 water supply networks and tube-wells which will be regularly monitored to ensure the continuous supply of safe drinking water. In addition, UNHCR will continue to support the operation and maintenance of facilities providing sustainable sanitation infrastructure. In this regard, UNHCR will seek approval from the Government of Bangladesh for the adoption of integrated household latrines and bathing areas as standard in all new transitional shelter designs covering 3,500 households. Shared household latrine coverage and bathing areas, especially for women and children, will be increased and coverage rates approved according to UNHCR standards. In 2024, faecal sludge treatment capacity will be increased through the operationalization of a third large-scale treatment plant in Teknaf. This plant will be linked with the piped collection network which will be expanded in camps 24 and 25 and is currently operated by IOM.
In relation to solid waste management, UNHCR is committed to enhancing the collection, transport, segregation, and treatment of solid waste through integrated planning. This involves prioritizing the implementation of a household collection system, emphasizing source segregation and attempting to reduce waste littering, particularly in drainage areas. Simultaneously, UNHCR will start to develop a data-driven waste management strategy to improve and optimize sustainable waste management practices. To achieve this, UNHCR is dedicated to embracing a multi-sectoral approach and increasing community involvement fostering a sense of responsibility within the refugee population.
Finally, a particular focus will be given to emergency preparedness and response to disease outbreaks (e.g., acute watery diarrhea/cholera/dengue) as well as climate-induced disasters such as monsoon and cyclones. UNHCR and partners will explore innovative approaches for community mobilization and engagement for hygiene behaviours ensuring the provision of a minimum package of WASH NFIs including soap and menstrual hygiene management kits. Other priority interventions will include water quality monitoring, provision of adequate WASH services in learning centres, health and nutrition facilities, child friendly spaces and other public spaces within the camps. In addition, UNHCR will advocate for more long-term solutions to be considered and funded for both refugee and host communities. In all cases, UNHCR’s WASH response will consider gender, environmental, age and disability inclusion and ensure equal access for persons with special needs.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Despite extensive efforts undertaken by UNHCR and its partners in 2023, access to adequate water and sanitation in the refugee camps has not yet reached the required minimum standards. Among the Rohingya refugees in Kutupalong and Teknaf, the primary source of domestic water differ. In Teknaf, surface water collected in small temporary reservoirs which is treated and then distributed through a network with tap-stands represents the main source. However, seasonal water scarcity remains in Teknaf, impacting the refugees and surrounding host communities. The Teknaf water situation requires significant investments in water infrastructure. In Kutupalong, the primary water source is groundwater accessed through hand pumps and solar-powered chlorinated water networks established during 2019-2021. While the most recent UNHCR Knowledge and Practice (KAP) survey reveals that on average each refugee receives more than the minimum 20 liters of potable water per person per day in the 14 camps managed by UNHCR, upgrades are still needed to enable solar-powered networks to remain operational after four years to ensure sustainability. Sanitation coverage within the camps remains an issue, as indicated by the latest KAP survey, showing high rates of open defecation among children under five years old. The WASH needs of persons with disabilities (PWD) have been only partially addressed in 2023, with gaps still remaining. Communal or shared households latrine usage averages 20 individuals/latrine in the 14 UNHCR-managed camps, just reaching the post-emergency target of 20. The usage of bathing facilities exceeds post-emergency targets, putting a strain on women and girls who report difficulties in accessing these facilities, leading to makeshift alternatives inside the shelters causing contamination and structural damage. The large and increasing stock of latrines requires frequent desludging, posing health risks and increased costs, as sludge must be pumped, transported and treated. In 2023, the third large-scale faecal sludge treatment plant began operating in Teknaf, processing approximately 40% of its total capacity (120 cubic meters of faecal sludge per day), with some components requiring completion to make it fully operational. Once the treatment plant in Teknaf is ready to operate at full capacity, it will potentially be able to cover additional camps in Teknaf (camps 24 and 25, currently managed by IOM).In 2023, the communal waste collection strategy highlighted shortcomings in segregation and collection frequency, making daily household collection one of the critical needs. Across 11 camps managed by UNHCR’s implementing partners, a shortage of 233 waste collectors was observed, falling below the required standard of one collector per 1,000 persons. The 2022 survey showed that only 30% of refugees have household bins, and not all were making effective use of them. This lack of attention resulted in widespread unauthorized dumping, including drainage, prompting the need for external monitoring. Both market and public waste management remain challenging, and requires attention and streamlining. To address this, an emphasis on community engagement is essential to gradually shift the behaviour of refugees in waste management practices.Moving forward in 2024, the WASH response will focus on implementing more durable solutions, replacing non-compliant emergency facilities, ensuring sustained WASH services for refugees and host communities, including sufficient quantity and quality water, desludging of wastewater, solid waste management, access to WASH/NFI and hygiene behavior change and operation and maintenance of WASH facilities.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

UNHCR conducts regular needs assessments to identify assistance required and prepare for the necessary provision of support considering age, gender and diversity (AGD) and the prioritization of persons with specific needs. It also ensures regular risk assessments and adjusts implementation as deemed feasible to obtain the desired outcome of the programme. UNHCR will continue to strengthen inter-sectoral camp level coordination through camp focal points and the continued engagement of Camps-in-Charge (CiCs) and other Government authorities, as well as through participation in inter-agency and inter-sectoral fora in its 14 managed camps. Through improved information sharing, mapping of partners’ roles and responsibilities among organizations operating in the camps and camp-level coordination, the overall response will be more predictable, with increased accountability helping to rationalize and provide improved services to refugees and host communities. Projects will complement the activities of other operational partners working in the same areas to avoid duplication. UNHCR’s WASH programme will explore opportunities for more innovative and integrated multi-sectoral approaches with other UNHCR units such as protection, education, child protection, gender-based violence (GBV), health, nutrition, shelter/NFI, energy and environment and Site Management/Site Development. UNHCR is implementing a progressive rationalization process within its partnership agreements with shelter and WASH partners, aligning with the implementation of both programmes in the same camps. This will contribute to effective coordination at the camp level and efficient and integrated service delivery, as well as strengthening strategic relationships with different stakeholders including the CiCs and refugees which have developed over the course of time. Joint planning and partner review initiatives will be implemented, as well as field supervision through existing multi-functional teams.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Protection mainstreaming will be at the core of this intervention ensuring that the proposed response contributes to saving lives, ensuring safety and security, alleviating suffering providing dignity for women, girls, boys and men, particularly marginalized groups such as older people, people living with disabilities, and minority groups. UNHCR and its partners will use gender-sensitive quantitative data analysis to track the level of beneficiaries’ satisfaction, participation, acceptance, knowledge and practices. UNHCR will ensure maximum protection mainstreaming in the WASH response by prioritizing the most vulnerable groups affected by protection issues. As in previous years, WASH and partner staff will complete the annual prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) full day training delivered by the UNHCR protection team. A PSEA assessment has already been conducted for all UNHCR partners with satisfactory results.

3.3 - Age & Disability

Children have the same right to clean water, sanitation and hygiene as everyone else. At the same time, UNHCR recognizes that children may have specific needs. Improperly located and poorly maintained WASH facilities such as latrines and water points, especially in crowded refugee camps, may expose children to risks of sexual exploitation and abuse. During repair and maintenance activities, appropriate health and safety measures will be undertaken to ensure children are safe by ensuring that they are not engaged in labour and porter activities. The project will also consider youth engagement and training to support WASH facilities maintenance activities within their communities. Specifically, elderly persons will receive WASH support, mobilizing households to support repair, maintenance and construction work including the collection of WASH NFIs from distribution points.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Systematic and meaningful engagement of the affected population in the design of projects represent key steps in ensuring successful programmes and achieving quality output and outcome results. UNHCR will continue to promote compliance with AAP commitments on participation and partnership and information, feedback and action. Community consultations will be conducted during the planning, implementation and post-project phases through house visits, focus group discussions (FGDs) and key informant interviews (KIIs). Trainings will be provided to beneficiaries of all programmes to explain and clarify eligibility and the process, as well as to provide beneficiaries with the necessary knowledge to ensure proper utilization of NFIs. Household-level post-distribution monitoring (PDM) will be conducted. Through the Common Feedback and Response Mechanism, the project will promote information sharing and the community feedback mechanism among the community.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

UNHCR continues to implement its activities while considering existing climatic and environmental risks and vulnerabilities, given that the refugee camps are prone to climate related disasters, as well as explore programmes and initiatives contributing to climate change mitigation and restoration of the fragile environment This is a strategic priority for UNHCR, given that the refugee camps are prone to climate-related disasters. WASH facilities will be designed and constructed, while considering existing climatic and environmental risks and vulnerabilities. To date, the following measures have been taken by UNHCR in terms of the climate resilience of water infrastructure in Cox’s Bazar; • Preliminary topographic studies prevent the establishment of WASH infrastructure in heavily-flooded areas.• Water points such as tap stands and handpumps include a concrete apron to limit erosion, stagnating water and destruction caused by floods.• Water supply and sanitation pipes are buried at a sufficient depth to limit erosion caused by water runoff. • Productive boreholes are subject to a long duration pumping test to ensure that the aquifer tapped will not dry up during the drought period, and the that the system is designed to exploit the aquifer in a sustainable way.• Whenever possible, pump stations are solarized, to increase profitability and reduce vulnerability to fuel price variation and carbon print.• Proper source segregation, especially of organic waste, plays a key role in mitigating climate change by reducing methane emissions from landfills and promoting recycling efforts.• Awareness on avoiding the littering of drains helps to prevent clogging, reducing the risk of flood occurrence due to excessive waste accumulation.• Above mitigation measures will continue in 2024 to support the long-term objective of ensuring safe and sustainable WASH services."
