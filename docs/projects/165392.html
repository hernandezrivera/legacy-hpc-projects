Project Summary

This project aims at promoting cohesion and recovery by reducing shelter vulnerabilities of IDPs inside of camps as follows:
1- Establish a transit center in Sarmada in Idleb for 250 individuals (52% female - 48%) to support saving lives, alleviate suffering and increase access to humanitarian assistance for IDPs, vulnerable people and those with specific needs. Priority is given to women and girls and the most vulnerable groups throughout protect (Women, men, girls, and boys) from harsh weather conditions by delivering the emergency shelter in the temporary transit center,
2- Establish planned camps including key administrative and critical humanitarian infrastructure: including firebreaks & fire points, admin offices, distribution points, and warehouse, safe spaces for women and children in the three areas as following:
- Aleppo (Jarablus)
- Idleb (Dana, Atma, Selwa, Harim, Kafr Takharim, Batenta, and Kelly)
1900 HHs (9500 individuals, 52% female - 48%). WASH services costs are not included in the budget, It will be provided in coordination with the WACH cluster and CARE organization.
3- Providing camp operational and regular maintenance activities for camps that managed by ATAA in Atma and new camps in Sarmada and Jarablus (15 camps established and managed by ATAA before and new three camps, the total 18 camps), 3000 HHs (16000 individuals): including repair of lighting, fence and other camp facilities (replacement of broken parts, welding, painting, reinforcement), and graveling of communal areas and roads, especially after heavy rains. cleaning of sanitation and communal facilities, provision of fire extinguishers and fuel for running generators and other maintenance costs.
ATAA will collaborate to identify critical needs for targeted maintenance. These discussions will be driven by FGDs and key informant information interviews done by previously made projects with both elderly and female community members. As water collection and other essential daily tasks are most often carried out by women, ATAA will ensure their involvement in the identification phase of all mentioned activities.
Conducting the PSEA/GBV awareness-raising sessions to the beneficiaries for protecting them from sexual exploitation and abuse.

4 - Needs

According to (Situation Report No. 8 - As of 13 February 2020) People in northwest Syria are living through some of the worst crisis since the war in Syria began. More than 800,000 people have been displaced since 1 December, due to intense conflict in freezing weather. The humanitarian community is doing everything it can but is overwhelmed by the scale of needs. An immediate cessation of the violence is critical. More resources, including funding, is immediately needed to save people’s lives and alleviate their suffering. Of the more than 800,000 people who have been displaced in northwest Syria from 1 December 2019 to 12 February 2020, some 60 percent are estimated to be children. Women and children who are 81 percent of the newly displaced people are again among those who suffer most. Several children are reported to have died due to the freezing temperatures. The number of people who are displaced since 1 December 2019 over 800,000 individuals (158,212 families).• There is a need to establish new camps and reception center/s as part of the preparedness plan for the new wave of displacement.• A’zaz Reception Center has a population of 4,434 individuals that exceeds the camp’s capacity at 2,000 individuals maximum. There is an urgent need to expand and/or establish the reception center to provide temporary accommodation.According to January update of ISIMM 2020. As of January 2020, there are 603 IDP sites listed in the CCCM database, which host a total of 973,861 IDPs/ 186,339 families out of them 9,569 IDPs with special needs, 80 % out of the population are women and children. 16 new sites have been added to the list in January 2020. In addition, One reception center in Idleb was inactivated in ISIMM list since the IDPs were evacuated to another reception center.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project was designed in a manner that benefits all of the categories, including women, children, people with disabilities and the most vulnerable categories. The Principals of protection in Sphere Project standards will be implemented in all project life cycle. Equality will be achieved in the recruitment process and personnel selection. Also the participation of the women will be enhanced in the project designThis Project include a safe spaces to provide PSS services and other to support the women and children categories The risks: 1- Sexual exploitation and abuse and child abuse by ATAA personnel, its partners, services providers/suppliers toward the beneficiaries especially women and girls. The mitigation: - Mainstreaming PSEA policy, in addition to CSG policy including training ATAA`s personnel. - requiring ATAA`s personnel, partner, services providers/suppliers to sign a commitment of the policy. - Activate the complaint mechanism and penalty procedures. - Awareness raising sessions to the beneficiaries about sexual exploitation and abuse, and aware them about complaints mechanism. - Activating the mechanism of referring survivors of SEA/GBV to the specialist services 2- The risk of sexual abuse incidents among the beneficiaries due to the nature of housing in the camps:The mitigation:- ATAA will assign protection workers in every camps to monitor the protection principals, prevention any abuse, and for interaction with it if that done- We will conduct an awareness sessions to aware the beneficiaries about this incidents

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Protection Risk1: Eviction from camp due to lack of approval from property owner and/or exposing beneficiaries at risk due to lack of HLP documentation for the camp rehabilitatedMitigation: 1. Abide by the HLP guidance document that was developed by the SNFI Cluster, 2. Do not proceed with the camp rehabilitation unless with the consensus of the owner, proof of registered HLP ownership, verified ownership claims. If this won’t be available, feasible, refer to the HLP DD, 3. Include HLP caveat into the MoUs or agreements signed, 4. Have an HLP section in the M&E assessment form.Protection Risk2: Risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment) of women and girls.Mitigation: 1. Establish a confidential complaint mechanism 2. Train staff on code of conduct 3. Code of conduct in all staff contracts Address any PSEA complaints Protection Risk3: Not having an exit strategy might lead to transform a temporary shelter solution (CSs) into a long-term accommodation,Mitigation: 1. Ensure an exit strategy in place, in coordination with the other NGOs acting in the field to provide NFI and house rental for leaving IDPs, 2. Keep the communication lines with the authorities open to inform them of the rationale of the interventions and the aim of the programmeMonitoring: Monitor IDP situation,Protection Risk4: Access restrictions to geo area due to active fighting and/or shifting of frontlines would prevent the humanitarian actors to provide timely emergency relief to the affected population.Mitigation: 1. Constantly monitor the security situation on the ground, 2. Develop contingency planning, 3. Set-up multiple back-up distribution centers, 4. Liaison with the local authoritiesMonitoring: 1.
