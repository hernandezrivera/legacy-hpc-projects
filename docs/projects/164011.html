Project Summary

The recent escalation and intense air strikes by Government of Syria (GoS) and Government of Russia (GoR) since the 1st of December 2019 led approximately 390 000 people to move civilians to ﬂee from their villages in eastern Idleb countryside to NW Syria and areas close to the Turkish border in northern Idleb governorate. Out of those displaced people, 16,425 of them moved to A’zaz. Due to recent displacements to A’zaz, rents for housing is skyrocketed. The displaced people have sought shelter near the Turkish border in densely populated areas where humanitarian response is over-stretched and humanitarian needs are mounting. The more the offensive over Idlib advances toward the North, the more people are forced to ﬂee to Northern Aleppo including the district of Azaz.
Azaz is host to over 265.000 internally displaced persons (IDPs), which amount to almost half of the total population of the district. Given the high number of IDPs, safe and habitable shelter is scarce. According to the Shelter Cluster, up to 103.000 people are in need of shelter assistance in Azaz, making Azaz one of the districts with the highest shelter needs in North-West Syria. According to the last WHH desk assessment from July 2019, from the shelter available 56% is damaged and 21% uninhabitable.
With this project WHH aims at responding to the most acute needs of the population in Azaz district, focusing on the most vulnerable including newly arrived IDPs, women and youth.
In order to address the needs regarding damaged and unﬁnished buildings, WHH will provide low and medium level shelter repair for 565 HHs. 200 HHs will be supported for low level shelter repairs by provision of cash up to 400$ per HH and 365 HHs with medium level shelter repair needs will be supported through a contractor. Each HH will receive repairment services up to 600$. Thus, in total 2825 people in need will get access to digniﬁed living conditions. At the beginning of the action, WHH will assess the level of vulnerability of households in a general assessment considering the rights of the landlords and tenants in order to ensure security of tenure, prevent (forced) evictions and disputes over shelter in accordance with the HLP Rights in Shelter, Due Diligence Guidelines . In a second step, a technical assessment will identify the speciﬁc shelter repair needs and classify them based on the extent of repair required in order to comply with minimum standards for shelter (Sphere) appropriate to the context in accordance with the Shelter Repair and Rehabilitation Guidelines developed with the SNFI cluster. The technical assessment will also include an estimate of the costs for the repair based on a pre-established bill of quantities. Based on these assessments, each household will be supported in the most adequate manner to meet their needs. For households with low level repair needs and/or with experience in shelter repair, cash assistance will be provided. In order to ensure the assistance contributes to the objective of the project, half of the assistance will be provided at the beginning of the cycle and the second half after the shelter repair has been completed as previously agreed. Thus, the people assisted are required to take responsibility and develop a feeling of ownership instead of being pure beneﬁciaries. In order to ensure the quality of the repairs, WHH will agree with the beneﬁciaries on the repairs needed before the action and WHH staff will provide training, monitoring and support as required until the ﬁnalisation of the repair. The success of this action is also dependent on the commitment of the beneﬁciaries. Thus, the modality will be adjusted on a case by case basis. At the same time, in order to address the needs of the people who reside in shelters with medium-level damages, an agreement with a contractor will be signed and these HH will be assisted with up to 600$. In total, 2825 individuals (460 male, 600 female, 690 boys and 1075 girls will be assisted).

5 - Implementation of activities

In line with the SNFI cluster objective of providing life-saving and life-sustaining shelter, WHH aims at responding to the most acute needs of the population in Azaz, focusing on the most vulnerable including newly arrived IDPs by prioritizing female-headed HHs, PLWs, separated children and youth. Light and medium level repair assistance for people living in inadequate shelters will address the needs regarding inadequate buildings. 200 HHs will be supported for low level shelter repairs by provision of cash up to 400$ per HH and 365 HHs with medium level shelter repair needs will be supported through a contractor. Each HH will receive repairment services up to 600$. Thus, in total 565 HH/2825 people in need will get access to digniﬁed living conditions. At the beginning of the action, WHH will assess the level of vulnerability of HHs in a general assessment considering the rights of the landlords and tenants in order to ensure security of tenure, prevent evictions and disputes over shelter in accordance with the HLP Rights in Shelter, Due Diligence Guidelines. Secondly, a technical assessment will identify the speciﬁc shelter repair needs and classify them based on the extent of repair required to comply with minimum standards for shelter (Sphere) in accordance with the Shelter Repair and Rehabilitation Guidelines developed with the SNFI cluster. The technical assessment will also include an estimate of the costs for the repair based on a pre-established BoQs. For HHs with low level repair needs and/or with experience in shelter repair, cash assistance will be provided. In order to ensure the assistance contributes to the objective of the project, half of the assistance will be provided at the beginning of the cycle and the second half after the shelter repair has been completed as previously agreed. Enhancing shelter conditions do not only include physical upgrades, but it provides protection to women and girls by providing individual space and privacy.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

WHH has a program participant protection policy (P4) and requires all project participants including partners, suppliers and staff to understand and comply with this policy. The policy ensures that all project participants understand the power imbalances and the potential protection risk from implementing projects to help people affected by crisis and conflict. The P4 policy also clearly states the expected behavior of staff, the staff of partner organizations and suppliers in relation to the people affected by crisis and conflict.WHH is committed to mainstream protection into all of the projects and does so through the use several mechanisms. These mechanisms include the creation of feedback and complaint mechanisms for projects, capacity building of all staff in relation to protection and mainstreaming protection throughout all phases of implementation and creating a work environment that encourages the consultation and active participation of women and children throughout programming. Collection of beneficiary information is kept internal and only shared at the request of beneficiaries. Furthermore, WHH's policy of documentation, including photography and videography, strongly considers protecting the rights of the beneficiaries and maintaining their dignity.The project is designed to contribute significantly to gender equality. Improving the resilience of female-headed households and IDP households is one of the major objectives of this project. Protection needs to be seen as a cross-cutting issue and protection outcomes are also considered in this project. Home gardening activity can have not only positive protection outcomes, but also for cross-cutting issues such as gender, age and disability. Within the vulnerability criteria, households having women headed households, PLW, disabled people shall be able to benefit from the assistance.
