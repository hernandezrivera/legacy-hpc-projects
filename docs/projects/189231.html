Project Summary

ADA plans to implement a multi-sector intervention, integrating Shelter and Non-Food Items responses with child protection and WASH activities in Ayod, Nyirol, Pigi/Canal and Fangak Counties of Jonglei State, Mayendit county of Unity state and Nasir county of Upper Nile State.
According to the (Humanitarian Needs Overview -HNO, Feb 2022), a deterioration of people’s physical and mental well-being, living standards and coping mechanisms is expected in 2022. Findings from the various data review processes indicate that 8.9 million people are estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2022, compared to 8.3 million in 2021. This increase is largely driven by compounded shocks triggered by continued conflict, widespread flooding, deepening food insecurity, inflation, high food prices, and lack of access to basic services greatly contributed to the increase of needs. Those in need of help are 24% women, 53% children and fifteen percent persons with disability.
2.4 million people are in need of SNFI assistance and services countrywide (HNO 2022 Report). Of this, the SNFI sector target is 1.4 million people. Hence, the most in need are women 24%, girls 26%, boys 27%, men 23% and persons with disabilities 15% people will be targeted by ADA through this plan (HNO 2022 report) to receive the assistance. According to the inter-sectoral severity of needs analysis, humanitarian needs
are most concerning in Fangak, Ayod, Pigi/Canal, Nyirol of Jonglei State and Nasir county of upper Nile State. A continuation and increase in sub-national violence and other forms of crises including floods reported in the HNO 2022 will likely continue to disrupt livelihoods and trade and contribute to an increase in humanitarian needs in 2022. Unprecedented continuous flooding impacted children and their caregivers` physical well-being, living standard, and coping mechanisms are expected to further deteriorate in 2022, as a result households’ populations have increased to more than 6 people, creating congestion and overcrowding, exacerbating the spread of COVID-19 among the affected population. Most affected Households lack basic household kits including plastic sheets, rubber robe, mosquito nets, blankets and kitchen sets.
ADA through this plan, will target to reach 37,500 individuals’ beneficiaries with in-kind SNFI assistance- 32000 individuals new displacement (conflict and flooding-85%); 5500 individuals Protracted Displacement-15%; SNFI target per location: Ayod – 11,250 individuals; Fangak – 13,125 individuals; Nyirol – 3,750 individuals; Pigi/Canal -3,750 individuals; Nasir -5,625 individuals.
The proposed interventions will be integrated with child protection and WASH responses in Fangak, Ayod, Pigi/canal, Nyirol and Nasir counties. Housing, Land and Property (HLP) mainstreaming. The project will target elderly persons 5% (1,875 individuals) living in crowded communal shelters to de-congest them through the distribution of plastic sheets, rubber rope, blanket and mosquito nets to reduce transmission of Covid-19. In line with SNFI cluster best practices, ADA will mainstream its responses through implementing AAP (Accountability to Affected Populations), HLP assessments, and safety audits activities during the four (stages) of SNFI response methodology, i.e. Needs assessment, Registration and verification of needs; Distribution of supplies and post distribution monitoring. In Ayod, Pigi, Fangak, Nyirol and Nasir contexts, unexploded ordinance (UXOS) and landmines issues or presences will be highlighted through awareness sessions especially during community sensitization and mobilizations meetings. An inter-sectoral referral system will be established to strengthen the SNFI synergies with others sectors like WASH, Child Protection.

1 - Needs

South Sudan is among the poorest and least-developed countries in the world, and in 2021 was the seventh least economically resilient country in Africa. The economy is heavily dependent on oil, which accounts for 70 per cent of Gross Domestic Product (GDP) and more than 90 per cent of public revenues. The COVID-19 pandemic has severely affected the economy as a result of the sharp decline of international oil prices that subsequently had a knock-on effect, resulting in worsening existing vulnerabilities of people, and long-term implications for economic growth and development in Ayod, Fangak, Pigi, Nyirol and Nasir counties. More than 2 million internally displaced people, almost a quarter of whom live in displacement sites (over 440,000 including 34,000 in the Protection of Civilians (PoC) site as of December 2021), 1.18 million IDP returnees and more than 505,000 spontaneous refugee returnees have been recorded since the signing of the peace agreement in October 2018. In addition, South Sudan hosts nearly 338,000 refugees, mainly from the Republic of the Sudan, who are not included in the 12.4 million population baseline (HNO 2022). HNO 2022 indicated that, women and girls hold primary responsibilities for farming, collecting water and firewood, cooking, cleaning, and childcare. Men and boys are traditionally the decision-makers within communities and their families and this is driven by many factors including limited or lack of access to adequate humanitarian services including shelters materials, mosquito nets, coking sets, rubber ropes, blankets and kanga for protection of women and girls. As such, women and girls tend to take responsibilities for such activities which places them at the risk of sexual and gender-based violence, while they carry out the tasks. Similarly, last year, Ayod, Fangak, Nasir, Nyirol and Pigi/Canal counties were hit hard by the triple shock of intensified sub-national violence resulting from impact of cattle raiding and revenge killings, also a third consecutive year of major flooding in the country, and the impacts of COVID-19, all are compounding factors, sliding the needs of vulnerable persons to worse (HNO 2022). Affected people’s coping mechanisms are weakened as a consequence of the cumulative shocks and lack of access to resources, leading families to adopt negative practices such as forced labour, sexual exploitation and child marriage. Much of the service infrastructure like shelters were destroyed either by inter-communal violence or floods or abandoned due to displacement in 2021 (HNO 2022). Despite huge humanitarian aid relief provided in 2021, vulnerable populations in the target areas continue to slide to poverty as a result of increased violence among communities which prevented thousands of vulnerable people from practicing their livelihoods and accessing the assistance they require. This negatively impacted vulnerably persons physical and mental well-being, living standards and coping mechanisms. Therefore, those affected persons well-being is anticipated to further deteriorate in 2022. HNO 2022, indicate that 8.9 million people are estimated to be in need of humanitarian aid in 2022, compared to 8.3 million in 2021. This increase is largely driven by compounded shocks triggered by continued conflict, widespread flooding, deepening food insecurity, inflation, and lack of access to basic services greatly contributed to the increase of needs. Of the above numbers, 2.4 million people are in need of SNFI assistance countrywide (HNO 2022 Report). According to inter-sectoral severity of needs analysis, humanitarian needs in Ayod, Fangak and Pigi/canal are in IPC4 and Nasir and Nyirol are IPC3 Counties. These effects of shocks weaken affected persons' health and security and affect their dignity and well-being too. Therefore, ADA HRP is designed to response to these identified needs in 2022.

2 - Project activities

Project activities:1) Mobilization and sensitization of the affected populations of Ayod, Fangak, Nasir, Nyirol, Nasir and Pigi/Canal counties through appropriate community channels including recruitment of community mobilizers and volunteers. The activities will be carried out in line with covid-19 preventive measures (install hand washing facility, sanitizers, social distancing). Local authorities including women, youths and church leaders and persons with disability will be engaged & consulted on the needs survey, and be used as focal and entry points for the project activities. 2) Needs Assessment of 37,500 beneficiaries will be conducted in Ayod, Fangak, Nasir, Nyirol Nasir and Pigi/Canal counties: ADA will assess the needs to determine the vulnerabilities of the target populations. Local enumerators and volunteers will be identified with the support of authorities and trained. Data will be collected from all categories of the affected populations across gender and age group like women, girls, boys, men, persons with disability, analyzed and findings presented for response. The project will employ various data collection techniques such as household questionnaire, FGD, KII and observation. ADA will use the cluster standard tools to collect, analyze and present the data through the cluster reporting template.3) Registration and verification of 37,500 beneficiaries needs. The registration and verification of beneficiaries needs will be done through consultation and coordination with local authorities, community project committees, enumerators, volunteers. The project will target 32000 individuals’ new displacement (flooding & conflict-85%); 5500 individuals Protracted Displacement-15%; target per location: Ayod – 11,250 individuals; Fangak – 13,125 persons; Nyirol – 3,750 persons; Pigi/Canal -3,750 persons; Nasir -5,625 persons. Also, women 24%, girls 26%, boys 27%, men 23% and persons with disabilities 15% people will be targeted for the SNFI assistance. This targeting aims at participation across gender, age and persons with disability. The project will target 5% (1,875) with awareness sessions on flood risk reduction. SNFI cluster tools will be used during the registration, and priority will be given to the most vulnerable communities such as elderly, pregnant and lactating women (PLWs), female and child-headed households, and persons with disability. Tokens will be issued to the registered beneficiaries to facilitate quick and easy identification in the distribution sites. 4) Distribution of SNFI supplies to 37,500 beneficiaries: 28300 will be targeted with flood kit; 3700 with individuals SNFI and 5500 protracted kits. ADA will identify appropriate distribution points and consult its protection team to conduct safety audit, to ensure affected persons’ dignity and rights are protected. Also, women, boys, girls, men and persons with disability opinions will be consulted and considered in the selection of the distributions points. Trained community mobilizers will mobilize and sensitize the beneficiaries about the distribution dates, venue and time. Prior to the distribution, subsequent consultation will be conducted with local community to ensure participation. The distribution points will be selected not more than 30 minutes’ walk to mitigate protection incidences during return home. Due to covid-19 pandemic, social distancing will be maintained in queues. 5) ADA will administer a post distribution monitoring tool after three weeks of distribution to evaluate the project impact and collect feedback from the beneficiaries on their level of satisfaction with the project received. Local enumerators and volunteers, will be identified with support of authorities, trained and deployed to the field to collect data. Different data collection techniques will be employed including FGD, KII, household questionnaire and observation to ensure wider information.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The feedback from the beneficiaries will be applied during selection of distribution locations. Thisconsultation shall be done during the beneficiary registration and verification time. Also, beneficiaries will be consulted during the needs assessment to provide at least their top three (3) priorities items (needs) for consideration during the recommendation of supplies for distribution. The different opinions of beneficiary will be harmonized during FGD to ensure the most top three (3)items cuts across the priority needs of all affected communities. ADA will engage community leaders including chiefs, RRCs, church and women leaders and youthgroups to mainstream HLP issues into the project activities. This engagement will ensure, that the IDPs or returnees have access to land to construct their temporary shelters for settlement and cultivation of crops. ADA will ensure that authorities provide written MOUs between the host community and the IDPs/Returnees on the land that the beneficiary will settle on. This aims at fostering community cohesion thus, minimizing sub national conflicts. During the community engagements, ADA will provide information on HLP guidelines in accordance with the South Sudan commission for land rights. Also, ADA will train community project committees on grievances reporting procedures, especially linked to complaints mechanisms, the community and volunteers mobilizers will translate and pass key messages with aid of megaphones to read information printed on fliers or reports to the community. The feedback and complaint mechanism shall be based on community preferences and cultural norms to ensure that they are accessible, safe and easy to use. All feedback will be recorded, and responses given to the community members or groups, besides a monthly report of the feedback received and responses given is sent to ADA head office in Juba for documentation, filling and refining into implementation matrix. The project team shall include staff with specific responsibility for supporting the complaint/feedback loop.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

ADA will employ participatory methods and processes for needs assessments, program design, monitoring and evaluation; accountability frameworks and mechanisms; complaints and response mechanisms; listening exercises between project team and beneficiary including persons with disabilities; conduct social audits to ensure protection and GBV mainstreaming in its activities and story-telling sessions, among others. ADA will receive and provide information to beneficiaries through focus groups discussions with women, boys, girls, boys, elderly and persons with disability, personal interviews with selected respondents especially government authorities and local partners, open community meetings, trained community mobilizers will go door-to-door, establishing listening exercises and appointing reporting lines, community monitoring field visits, existing community liaisons contact persons appointed by beneficiaries, weekly coordination meetings and community project committees. Locally employed enumerators, community mobilizers and volunteers will be identified trained to participate in the project. Documents in English will be translated into the local languages in fliers and read to the communities for better understanding of the information.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

ADA staffs have signed the child protection and other key GBV code of conducts documents, affirming their commitment to protect Beneficiaries (women and girls) from any form of abuse and exploitation in all project areas. This project will employ inclusive consultative methods with various community members; while considering gender and age groups. ADA will conduct separate community meetings including FGDs with women and girls alone, and men and boys also separately ensuring persons with disabilities are engaged in each of their gender group, which allows for easy speak up of sensitive issues of disadvantaged groups for consideration. This HRP is design to recruit local enumerators, volunteers and community mobilizers -and consideration will be given to those who can speak the local languages to be selected to participate in the project, to ensure adequate translation of project activities to those who are illiterate and cannot hear the language due to their physical or mental barriers. The project shall hold meetings with authorities and disadvantaged groups to share assessment findings, verify needs and triangulate data to ensure comprehensive mapping of PSNs is done, and that physically or mentally impaired persons are reached, consulted and involved into the project design and implementation and monitoring and evaluation.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will use the beneficiary feedback/complaints mechanism to enhance participation of affected communities and empower rights-holders, build trust and stronger relationships with project stakeholders. A project community based committees will be established in each county comprising of 20 members (beneficiaries; women, boys, girls, elderly and persons with disability and local authorities). The community project committees will help to strengthen project ownership in their project locations, improving the chance of long-term impact. Not only that, it will provide ADA with feedback on what is working well and not so well, hence, will strengthen the organizational understanding of the context in which the project operates, including identifying unintended consequences that are impacting negatively on some stakeholders, allowing for quick action to be taken so that these do not become a risk to the project’s success. Essentially, when positive feedback is received, it motivates staffs to put more efforts to assisting the affected communities, and also feedback help to pick up on issues such as fraud and misconduct, thus places ADA in position to know who to keep in the organization and who not to keep. Finally, feedback gives empowerment and voice –capacity to challenge and hold decision makers to account for their actions against the affected communities being served.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

ADA will conduct capacity building approaches, to enhance knowledge and networks of primary stakeholders (e.g. individuals, households, communities) through awareness creation and trainings sessions to strengthen the ability of affected persons to withstand adverse impact from external shocks such as armed conflict, sub-national violence and disaster like flood. The project designs has process of incorporating protection principles and promoting meaningful access to services, safety and dignity of affected persons while receiving SNFI assistance in the project locations. It is the responsibility of ADA and partners to ensure safety audits is done at all its distribution points; and that beneficiaries walks for less than 30 minutes to receive their items, distributions are conducted at morning hours (from 10 am-3pm) as late or early hours are always prone for armed robbers. Distributions points will be established where there is shade and at least minimum WASH facilities to reduce exposure of beneficiaries to dangers of robbery, rape and diseases. The project design prioritizes safety and dignity, and with strategy to avoid causing harm. It will prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of SNFI intervention which could increase affected people's vulnerability to both physical and psycho-social risks. Also, the project aims at promoting equity and meaningful access; the approaches take pro-active steps to ensure beneficiaries’ meaningful access to impartial assistance and facilities -in proportion to need and without any barriers (i.e. discrimination). Besides that, the project approaches pays special attention to lobby for inclusion through ensuring community infrastructures like public toilets, community centers, training halls, NGO offices, water and supplies distribution points have facilities that enables disable individuals and groups to participate like any other persons. These groups may include the disable persons, lactating and pregnant mothers, female and child headed house, elderly 60 years+, unaccompanied minor and person with ill health condition.
