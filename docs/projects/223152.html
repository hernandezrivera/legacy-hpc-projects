Project Summary

Guided by IASC Minimum Standards, UNFPA will promote, coordinate & provide lifesaving, comprehensive, multi-sectoral & inclusive GBV prevention & response services in line with the human rights approach, survivor-centered approach, and principle of value-for-money.
Recognizing GBV as a serious human rights violation, life-threatening health & protection issue, UNFPA will provide integrated & age-responsive GBV/SRH services including case management, MHPSS, CMR, voluntary family planning, STI syndromic treatment and referrals, in 53 Women Friendly Spaces. 3 WFS will integrate legal services with UNHCR & Blast support. UNFPA will support GBV response services in 3 One Stop Crisis Center/Cells in Ukhiya, Teknaf & Cox’s Bazar Sadar hospitals as well. Case workers will be deployed in 12 health facilities, including a Wellness Center for gender diverse population, for providing integrated GBV services. Women & girls affected by disasters and GBV will receive Dignity Kits, MHM Kits, and voucher & cash assistance.
UNFPA will promote gender equality, women empowerment & social behavior change through male engagement in 9 Women-Led Community Centers and a Multi-Purpose Center providing GBV awareness, PSS and self-reliance training. Adolescents & youth will receive SRH/GBV information, life skills and PSS in 7 Adolescent & Youth Centers (AYC). Communities will be mobilized to address gender-biased social norms & harmful practices and prevent GBV; in particular, SASA Together phase III will strengthen community skills to promote social behavior change through structured sessions for influential, community & religious leaders, and caregivers.
Adolescents will receive structured life-skills empowering programs to prevent GBV & child marriage. The Girl Shine program for girls will run in Community Based Learning Centers (CBLC), AYC and be scaled up in 53 WFS. Boys will engage to challenge harmful social norms that perpetuate gender inequality and redefine masculinity through the Champions of Change program in CBLC and AYC. The programs engage caregivers as well.
UNFPA will contribute to the Peace & Security Campaign to strengthen refugee women and girls’ voices by integrating peace & security awareness raising sessions and messaging in its activities. Community groups affiliated to GBV facilities will be supported to better recognize protection & GBV risks and provide referral. IEC materials will be printed and distributed as well.
Through an intersectional approach, the project will address the needs of persons with disabilities and gender diverse population, and facilitate their access to integrated GBV/SRH services. New arrivals will benefit from existing lifesaving GBV-SRH services at no cost and without discrimination; additional GBV case workers will be deployed on a need basis. UNFPA will strengthen strategic partnerships with organizations led by women, PWD & GDP.
UNFPA will advocate, provide technical guidance & capacity development on PSEA to partners in coordination with PSEA Network to ensure safeguarding for beneficiaries and that PSEA referral, reporting mechanisms & policies are in place.
The UNFPA-led GBVSS, comprising over 50 members, coordinates the inter-agency GBV response in Cox’s Bazar as per IASC Minimum Standards. GBVSS engages with & enhances the capacities of members, other sectors & government stakeholders for effective, coordinated & integrated GBV prevention, mitigation & response. Focus will be on ensuring continuity, quality & sustainability of comprehensive GBV services, case management & multi-sectoral referral systems while enhancing data collection, regular community-based GBV risk monitoring, joint safety/service audits & advocacy for a survivor-centered approach across sectors. Resource mobilization efforts will support accountability, sustainable impact and responsiveness to community needs.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

In the 7th year of the crisis, GBV continued to be a major concern for the health & wellbeing of refugees and host communities, putting their physical & emotional well-being at risk. In 2024, women & girls - 51% of the refugee population - encountered increased GBV risks due to the deteriorating security conditions, while women of reproductive age continued to be significantly affected by domestic violence. As per GBVIMS and UNFPA community consultations, the most vulnerable groups include adolescent girls, younger women, female-headed households and PWD. Women & girls declared feeling unsafe in 58% of camps, while 71% GBV Camp Focal Points perceived an overall increase of GBV incidents (GBV Risk Monitoring, Q3 2024). As per UNFPA FGDs, women & girls’ concerns revolve around GBV, domestic violence, exploitation, and GBV threats from organized criminal groups. This scenario reveals that lifesaving GBV services continue to be much needed across the affected population. The 6% decrease of GBV reporting in Q2/2024 highlights growing barriers for women & girls in accessing lifesaving services due to the deteriorating security situation, including fear of GBV in relation to the presence of organized criminal groups (GBVIMS). Restrictions to women & girls’ mobility led to underutilization of GBV services - UNFPA observed a dramatic 14% reduction in access to our GBV service facilities from Q2 to Q3. GBVIMS also registered a steady increase in sexual violence incidents (10.4% in Q2/2024, 8% in Q4/2023), spotlighting the vulnerabilities of women & girls to this more egregious form of violence, which remains still severely under-reported. As per ISNA, WFS are identified as the primary provider of GBV services by 70% refugees and are the first choice to refer a friend who has experienced GBV. CiCs remain the second option, showing a need for continued awareness on GBV, available services and survivor-centered approach. Integration of SRH services in WFS contributes to access to basic SRH and voluntary family planning, with 87% of households reporting that women & girls had access to these services. Ensuring accessibility & awareness for marginalized groups, such as PWD, GDP and new arrivals remains crucial. While food insecurity of refugees decreased in 2024, 73% of households reported buying food on credit and 14% reduced expenditure on health, female hygiene products and education to meet basic needs (ISNA). Lack of livelihood opportunities increase stress and exacerbate existing GBV patterns. This suggests a need to enhance GBV community awareness, community-based outreach and peace & security campaigns. Engaging men & boys can help reduce stigma around seeking GBV support and foster a more supportive environment for survivors. UNFPA community consultations highlighted that lack of education and poverty increase vulnerability in the current security context, particularly for adolescent girls who face specific GBV risks. Emerging risks include technology-facilitated GBV such as cyberbullying, exposure to explicit contents, dissemination of adult contents of refugee adolescent girls and grooming (UNDP). Life skills programming remains a key evidence-based tool to equip youth with information & skills to make informed decisions and prevent GBV. Targeted awareness programs for GDP, PWD or new arrivals can bridge gaps in knowledge on services. Regular assessments of the effectiveness of outreach interventions and an expansion of GBV service networks to underserved areas will enhance access to vital support. (ISNA) Bangladesh being a natural disaster-prone area. Considering the 2024 extensive monsoon season, there is continued need for prepositioning Dignity and MHM Kits, and enabling PERUs & other first responders, including UNFPA’s PFA volunteers. GBV case workers need strengthened capacities to provide remote case management, while volunteers shall be capacitated to manage facilities when service providers’ movement is impaired.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

UNFPA will ensure that protection mechanisms and principles are in place by applying protection mainstreaming assessment tool kits, safety audits & related IASC protection standards. UNFPA will provide GBV lifesaving services, which incorporate protection mainstreaming principles & approaches, and timely update referral pathways at facility level. By applying the “do no harm” principle to affected populations in facilities & outreach, UNFPA will provide services to the Rohingya refugees and host communities while minimizing harm and maximizing the positive impact of aid and assistance to affected communities. Through regular needs assessments and participatory discussions with beneficiaries, UNFPA will ensure that services are tailored to changing protection needs, security and safety concerns; and regular M&E of service quality. UNFPA applies a survivor-centered approach in programming, specifically through GBV case management. All service providers in UNFPA-supported facilities will abide by this principle and provide services based on survivors’ consent, needs and wishes while maintaining confidentiality. Service providers will share GBV-related information with other providers on a strict need-to-know basis while managing referrals to specialized protection-related services for legal assistance, safe shelters and advocacy. Three WFS will also integrate legal services with UNHCR and BLAST support. Service providers share aggregated non-identifying GBV-related data to GBVSS/Protection Sector according to ISP and data protection protocols. Within the heightened protection and GBV risks in the camps, UNFPA will continue to coordinate closely with the GBVSS and the Protection Sector as a current key member of both SAGs, and contribute to the Peace & Security Campaign.

2.3 - Age & Disability

UNFPA and partners will follow a twin track approach to ensure GBV services are disability inclusive and accessible. We will:1. Engage PWD throughout the project cycle. Ideally, we will engage at least 3.5% in our activities (2.4% in 2024); 2. Develop and implement a disability accessibility plan for our GBV facilities, based on the audit done in 2024;3. Use the Washington Group Questionnaire across facilities; 4. Provide disability-accessible GBV case management and strengthening referrals from/to disability-focused organizations; 5. Organize GBV awareness & WFS service information sessions for PWD & caregivers;6. Provide dedicated “tom-tom” referral services & cash; 7. Enhance capacities of partners, service providers & volunteers on GBV, PSEA, referrals, case management for PWD & caregivers; 8. Gather Sex Age Disability Disaggregated Data (SADDD) to ensure programmatic improvements are made. UNFPA’s GBV interventions target all age categories and will adopt an adolescent- responsive & age-sensitive approach to information & service provision to adolescents and youth (A&Y). The project will:1. Support life skills education programs (Girl Shine, Champions of Change) for A&Y and caregivers to equip them with clear understanding of gender equality, safety, child marriage & ASRHR;2. Provide GBV case management, referral & services to adolescent girls, child marriage survivors, & married and/or pregnant adolescents, through case workers trained on “Caring for Child Survivors” modules. Through WFS, girls will have access to integrated ASRH services;3. Include older women in programming, particularly through intergenerational dialogues and activities with younger women and adolescent girls in WFS;4. Provide PSS sessions to adolescent girls GBV, SRHR, PSEA, DRR & emergency referral tailored to A&Y needs.5. Support A&Y community activism & peer support groups at community level part of SASA! 2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

UNFPA embeds a Human Rights Based Approach across its programming, ensuring participation, accountability, non-discrimination and empowerment. To ensure inclusive community engagement, UNFPA involves diverse community members, including persons with disabilities and gender-diverse populations, in all phases of its interventions. UNFPA is and will continue to conduct extensive consultations & assessments among target populations & key stakeholders to plan, implement, monitor & evaluate interventions, i.e. assessments & surveys; community consultations & FGDs with relevant community groups and stakeholders; bilateral dialogues with government officials & community leaders. Consultations will inform & involve community members in all project phases; proactively identify & address risks; and ensure transparency & AAP by implementing community recommendations in a reasonable manner. UNFPA utilizes various tools to facilitate community feedback such as Community Feedback Response Mechanisms, Client Satisfaction Surveys, Complaint Boxes, and hotlines including UNHCR’s and Alapon. We provide direct support to Alapon, focusing on adolescent & youth services, ensuring timely response and quality improvement. Regular FGDs with affected populations in WFSs, WLCCs, MPC gather critical feedback on service quality. All staff, including partners, receive PSEA training. Community awareness on PSEA is raised through an integrated approach, including Community Watch Groups and Women's Support Groups. UNFPA Investigation Unit (OAIS) has established an Investigations Hotline as a confidential mechanism for individuals wishing to report fraud or other wrongdoing, accessible to all. This channel is disseminated across our Partners. We are recruiting an International Specialist on AAP. Furthermore, a Social and Environmental Safeguarding personnel worked with UNFPA and partners in 2024 to adhere to the organization's Social and Environmental Safeguards policies & procedures.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

UNFPA upholds a zero-tolerance policy towards Sexual Exploitation, Abuse & Harassment, extending this commitment across organizational levels and stakeholders. We prioritize SEA prevention and provide immediate assistance to victims, collaborating with partners to foster a safe workplace. As an active member of the ISCG PSEA network in Cox's Bazar since 2017, UNFPA has appointed PSEA Focal Points and hired the national-level PSEA Coordinator who oversees the CXB Network. PSEA training is mandatory for all UNFPA personnel, emphasizing reporting wrongdoings and survivor-centered support. The Office of Audit & Investigation Services (OAIS) facilitates reporting of misconduct through an online platform and hotline, accessible to all. PSEA focal points are tasked with receiving complaints & ensuring rapid reporting to OAIS & the Country Representative. UNFPA has a retaliation policy in place to protect survivors, victims & whistleblowers from any form of retaliation. UNFPA is dedicated to enhancing staff capacity on PSEA and empowering partners, service providers and volunteers to raise community awareness through diverse modalities. Regular monitoring ensures compliance with PSEA policies across all supported community-based facilities, complemented by quarterly meetings among UNFPA & Partners to address challenges and strategize. Periodic capacity assessments are conducted to support partners in upholding necessary PSEA protocols. To align with global standards in SEAH investigation, UNFPA partners participate in specialized training organized by the Network. Certified personnel may join SEA investigation units of other organizations. UNFPA contributes to technical groups of the PSEA Network, including PSEA Communication with Community Task Team, Technical Review Team for SEAH Investigation ToR, Drafting the Sexual Harassment Law Task Team. In 2025, UNFPA will train its deployed case workers on the new IASC GBV Case Management Responses to SEA Module.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

UNFPA will ensure full adherence to DRR/DRM standards, including Bangladesh’s National Plan for Disaster Management (2021-2025) & Action for Disaster Risk Management Towards Resilient Nation. GBVSS contributed to revise the Emergency Preparedness & Response Protection Plan which includes preparedness planning with 57 GBVSS partners & identifying GBV focal points for PERU. UNFPA partners are part of PERU and are trained, including on GBViE. Social mobilizers are also trained to respond to disaster-related emergencies to support community members during the acute and post-disaster phase. A pool of PFA providers (130 case managers & case workers) from WFS is ready to be deployed in case of emergencies to provide PFA & facilitate emergency referrals. 210 community volunteers are trained on PFA & referral pathways to assist in emergencies. 424 members in Women Support Groups and 401 in Community Watch Groups are trained on DRR & GBV referral to support their community during post-disaster emergencies. Case workers & social mobilizers received ToT on Fire Safety, Security & Risk Mitigation and will cascade it to other staff and community members. UNFPA prepositions, stores & distributes within 48 hours customized Dignity Kits to reduce vulnerabilities of affected women during acute emergencies. UNFPA is managing a pilot Voucher Shop in the WFS in Camp 9 to provide key hygiene & wellbeing items usually included in Dignity Kits to better target women needs, while ensuring that waste is minimized. The model will be reviewed and scaled up based on its efficiency & effectiveness, and client’s satisfaction. UNFPA actively promotes eco-friendly practices by discouraging the use of plastic and polythene banners. Solar panels are installed in all WFS, WLCC & AYC, helping reduce reliance on fossil fuels. UNFPA provides community-based awareness programs on climate change through art-based activities and audio-visual content, and DRR training to adolescents & youth.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

UNFPA provides lifesaving SRH/GBV services to the affected population and has in place strong measures to always ensure the continuity of such services, while taking into account staff security & safety. UNFPA has an updated business continuity plan, latest updated in September 2024, to ensure the smooth continuity of UNFPA operations at all times. All UNFPA-managed facilities have in place preparedness measures for fire response, including fire extinguishers and other emergency fire tools. Emergency exits are available in the majority of facilities - whenever the space makes it possible - to ensure the safety of the GBV survivors who need to exit the facilities without being noticed, as well as for the overall safety of the staff and beneficiaries in case of emergency. UNFPA facilities can also be used as shelters in case of natural or human-made emergencies that require the population to leave their own shelters. Due to the restrictions on access to the WFS, those spaces in particular host women and girls, and boy children 5 years of age or under. For GBV case management services, UNFPA has a contingency plan in place to ensure to protect data, confidentiality, and to not lose important data on the GBV cases managed. In order to strengthen this approach, UNFPA will also provide a refresher on the provision of remote GBV case management as a lesson learned from the civil unrest in 2024. Volunteers update the referral pathways every 2 weeks, to ensure that all contact details of service providers are updated as much as possible to ensure quick action in case of emergency referrals. Please also refer further to the “Climate change adaptation & mitigation” Section which highlights some preparedness and response measures adopted by UNFPA, which overlap with this section as well. For emergency funding, UNFPA relies on the provision of CERF funds as part of the UN System to respond to time-critical SRH and GBV needs.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

UNFPA commitment to advocate for young people’s rights is enshrined in our mission to deliver a world where “every pregnancy is wanted, every childbirth is safe and every young person's potential is fulfilled”. Aligned with UNFPA’s global strategy for adolescents & youth and IASC guidelines on working with and for young people in humanitarian and protracted crises, considering that one out of three refugees is young, UNFPA will provide critical SRH & GBV information, life skills and opportunities to support young people - particularly adolescent girls - in reaching their maximum potential. Young people in both refugee camps and host communities will have access to youth-responsive safe spaces: WFS, WLCC, AYC and Community Based Learning Centers. To promote youth rights, the facilities will promote peer-to-peer knowledge transfer on SRH, GBV & health issues through educational and recreational programmes. They will also empower youth and enhance their community participation through life skills programs (Girl Shine, Champions of Change). Young people will participate in decisions affecting them, and strengthen their ability to advance human rights and development issues such as health, education & employment. In WFS, activities will aim at ensuring that participants include 30% young women & girls and 5% elderly women (out of all participants). Inter-generational dialogues in WFS will engage adolescent girls in conversations with elderly women. As co-lead of the Youth Working Group (YWG) at ISCG, UNFPA ensures that the project aligns with best practices for coordinated programming aimed at refugee and host community youth. The YWG is committed to advocate for the youth agenda and integrate youth needs and capacities at strategic & operational levels across various sectors. It promotes the implementation of IASC Guidelines in relevant sectors and prioritizes meaningful youth participation in its activities & decision-making processes.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

The project supports self-reliance by: 1. Providing incentives to community volunteers who support the GBV facilities under this project. UNFPA supports 424 volunteers in WFS (215 camp, 209 host). In 2025, volunteers will receive training and mentorship to enhance their skills to provide GBV-related awareness raising sessions and be increasingly engaged in facility management.2. Supporting Women Support Groups, Community Watch Groups and Self-Help Groups in GBV facilities, to boost community leadership and social cohesion. 3. Providing self-reliance training through WFS, WLCC and MPC, as part of the GBV prevention and psychosocial support activities to women and men, under the Livelihood Sector. 4. Planning to open one display corner in Ukhiya host community to generate income for the participants of the self-reliance trainings. Efforts will be made to explore similar options further.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

UNFPA project aligns with Cox’s Bazar District Development and Growth Plan , which highlights a need for holistic and inclusive development targeting both the Rohingya refugee and host communities. The document stresses that the district is affected by both humanitarian and development challenges and prioritizes: i) strengthening the economic resilience, ii) improving human capital and productivity, iii) promote social cohesion and emphasized that all the initiatives will be gender inclusive, addressing the needs of children, youth and elderly, and gender based violence concerns, which are fully in alignment with what have been proposed in the proposal. The plan is also committed to contribute to SDG5 “Achieve gender equality and empower all women and girls”.
