Project Summary

The right to education is not being fully realized for girls and boys living in conflict affected and fragile areas of Hilawa District in Banadir region. The expulsion of al-Shabaab from the city and the consolidation of FG control of all the districts of Banadir
region, created positive optimism and widespread sense of improved security, mass return to settlements once deserted including Hilwa. Hiliwa district is undergoing changes and increasingly becoming a choice location for the poor communities residents due to its relative peace and cheap housing. According to rapid assessment carried out by SOSCV revealed that most of these residents are from poor background and the right to education of girls and boys are not being fully realized. However, despites the districts inhabited by poors families there is little sign that international development and relief partners have done much in education in this districts.
The families interviewed indicated that they do not send their children to school. The bulk of this families lack access to basic services including education. Most of the schools in the area are public schools and most the schools lack adequate learning space and learning materials. SOS children villages school is currently in full capacity and have no space to provide opportunities to these girls and boys from the poor family background.
The community leaders indicates the needs to provide vital education services, since these are severely lacking, SOS CV understand in such vulnerable situations, girls and boys have have different vulnerabilities and capacities and, therefore, different protection needs. SOS CV also understands that education can both be empowering and protective for girls and boys, and this intervention will therefore be designed to be protective for all children affected by the crisis in Hilwa districts and provide them access to relevant and inclusive education through existing public and private schools.
The proposed Project aims to ensure that vulnerable children and youth, particularly girls and young women, affected by conflict or rural/urban inequality, and those that are disabled, have access to relevant and inclusive education. The proposed education project will work closely work with the ministry of education and the community and local district leaders to identify children out of schools and provide them an opportunity to be enrolled in schools. SOS CV with its vast experience in quality education and has been active since 1985 in providing basic and high education to most vulnerable children as well
education quality improvement will will propose to provide key components on teacher training, CEC training, PHAST and CHAST training and sanitation campaigns in the targeted schools.
SOS CV Somalia has, in collaboration with the government, implemented special initiatives when it comes to IDPs and children who lost parental care and at risk of losing parental care through innovations alternative care at SOS children villages in Somalia and since over 800 children were supported through this program. It is imperative to have interventions on provision of immediate and integrated assistance to education services for vulnerable girls and boys in Hilwa District. This proposed intervention will target 5000 (2500 girls and 2500 boys ) vulnerable IDPs and vulnerable host communities in Heliwa district. To ensure inclusivity, the project will ensure 500 (10%) of the target group with be children with disabilities which will be provided access through inclusive education approach and improve access to schools for children with disabilities. SOS CV will design this project to ensure that services and aid delivered will protect all segments of the target population and will not put some at risk, especially girls. As a strategy, gender will be significantly entrenched in this project, to ensure protective environments for girls and boys. The project will also establish CFS at targeted schools.

Project Activities

Set-up 12 temporary learning spaces and renovate/rehabilitate existing public schools spaces in target primary schools. Each learning space will also be equipped with furniture. Provide basic teaching and learning supplies and recreational materials for 5000(2500 girls and 2500 boys) vulnerable girls and boy(including learning resources for learners with disabilities, dignity kits ) in 12 targeted public schools. Identify and support a total of 20 volunteer teachers (30% female) with monthly emergency incentives. Provide in service training for 60 teachers (30% women) with a focus on improving equity and sustainability in the education profession and integrate life-saving messages on key issues on education in emergencies (such as risk reduction, gender based violence and peace education) and on child centered, inclusive teaching, and fundamental basics of pedagogy, child protection and psycho social support, in an emergency setting in Hilwa districts. Construction/rehabilitation of 24 temporary gender sensitive latrines and hand washing facilities (in consultation with WASH cluster) in 12 target public and private schools in Hilwa districts. • Train school children on Participatory Hygiene and Sanitation Transformation (PHAST) and Child Hygiene and Sanitation Training (CHAST) and conduct interactive hygiene and sanitation campaigns in 12 schools, to include messages on COVID 19, hand washing, drinking safe water and environmental hygiene within the school and its surrounding, among other important sanitation & hygiene messages. The messages will be formulated using the WASH cluster guidelines. provide awareness creation on COVID 19 adopting UNICEF and WHO IEC and communication material with local languages. Establish CFS in target schools.
