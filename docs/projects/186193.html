Project Summary

The Anglophone crisis which escalated since 2016 due to socio-political issues causing tension, threats, killing, has drastically displaced hundreds of people in affected areas. The crisis has also increased violence against children, neglect, and exploitation especially the North-West and South West Regions (NWSW) of Cameroon. “Acts of Wickedness”, barbarism, child slavery, and abuse are among the critical risks faced by children at the family, and community levels in the NWSW regions in Cameroon. However, insufficient child protection mechanism or programming to prevent and respond to violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect of children in NWSW affected by this crisis has been identified. Issues related to physical abuse, either deliberate or symptoms, child sexual exploitation, persistent emotional maltreatment that impacts a child’s emotional development. Emotional abuse actions towards children including restriction of movement, degrading, humiliating, bullying, threatening, scaring, discriminating, ridiculing, hostile and rejection are all the various forms of emotional abuse suffered by children due the crisis in the NWSW regions of Cameroon.
Commercial exploitation which involves exploiting a child in work or other activities for the benefits of others to the detriment of the child’s physical or mental health, education, moral or socio-emotional development. E.g. like, child Labour, abuse, neglects, exploitation and violence against children is also a problem. Also, there is sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA).
Statistics indicate that there are hundreds of children who are displaced due to gun shut and some of the children are unaccompanied children, separated children’s, Orphans and more vulnerable children in the host communities in which some of these children saw one or both of their parents been shut.
CAMHELP is currently carrying out case management for Child protection GBV survivors and also advocated for unaccompanied children, separated children,Orphans,internally displaced children and vulnerable children with high risk of insecurity and sexual violence in some communities including children living with disabilities in our area of interventions in Fako , and CAMHELP also advocate for Re-unification of separated children, Orphans, displaced children into their families and foster home where they can be able to bounce back to live and live together like before in Peace and Harmony for a better tomorrow and future.
Due to the Anglophone crisis, parents have abandoned their internally displaced children (IDPs) to caregivers whose husbands, uncles, etc., use these children as sex workers.
Strengthening of child protection in emergencies especially in hard-to-reach areas in Cameroon involves preventing and responding to violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect of children during times of emergency, disasters, conflict or other crises. Other factors which have aggravated these factors include cultural behaviours.
It is therefore important to strengthen Civil Status Documentation and Child Protection Case Management in Emergency programmes in Emergency (Limbe 1 and Idenau) at the family, and community levels to children living at the brink.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Every child has the right a) to be registered at or soon after birth, and to have a name and nationality; b) to basic nutrition, clothing, shelter, sanitation and health care; c) to family care, protection and guidance, which includes the equal responsibility of the child’s parents to provide for the child i) whether or not the parents are, or have ever been, married to each other; and ii) whether or not the parents are living together, have lived together, or are separated; d) to be protected from abuse, neglect, harmful cultural practices, any form of violence, inhumane treatment and punishment, and hazardous or exploitative labour; and e) not to be detained, except as a measure of last resort, and when detained, to be held i) only for such period of time as is necessary; and ii) separate from adults, and in conditions that take account of the child’s sex and age. 2) The best interests of a child are the primary consideration in every matter concerning the child.
