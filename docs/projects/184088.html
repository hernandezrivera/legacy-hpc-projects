Project Summary

Under the HRP 2022, ZOA is planning a multi-sectoral response (health and nutrition) in 13 localities in North Darfur, South Darfur, East Darfur and Gedaref states to respond to critical WASH, and Health & Nutrition gaps. This action is joint with ZOA' other HRP 2022 submitted project for an integrated FSL, WASH, health and nutrition action (code: HSDN22-FSC;WSH-183775-1). The main areas of focus are providing the targeted population with essential primary health and nutrition services. Nutrition services will be delivered with CMAM approach together with other preventive intervention including proper IYCF promotion.
Localities selected are: Mellit, Kuma, Dar el Salam and rural Al Fasher (ND); Damso and Bileil (SD); Bahr el Arab, Assalaya, Adila and Abu KArinka (ED); Al Fashaga, Al Mafaza and Galabat Ash Shargi (GED). Localities selected are mostly (with the exception of 3) in IPC Phase 3, and have high WASH severity ranking related to open defecation and poor access to drinking water, resulting in potentially catastrophic health outcomes.

1 - Needs

The nutrition situation in Sudan in 2022 is projected to remain at elevated high levels with overall GAM levels of 13.6 percent. The situation is more critical in 64 localities that are classified with very high prevalence of acute malnutrition of 15% and above as per WHO emergency levels classification of which 9 localities fall into catastrophically levels i.e GAM 30% and above. The projected worsening of drivers of malnutrition: food insecurity, deterioration in provision of Health and WASH services in 2022; increasing inflation while no significant improvement is projected on caring and feeding practices for under-five children. Vitamin A supplementation coverage is estimated at 39.87%. The age-appropriate dietary diversity is only 24.11% indicating that malnutrition is not just caused by lack of food. The health systems in Darfur remains weak and inadequately prepared to provide basic life-saving health services and respond to outbreaks. In Darfur, it is reported that 26% of the health facilities are not functional. The coverage of measles vaccination is only 55.9% while only 38.3% of children are fully vaccinated. Only 54% of the clinical OPD cases are managed according to the protocol despite training and supportive supervisions, which signals the need for strengthening support and follow up to ensure adherence to protocols. Health promotion, disease surveillance and referral remain poor due to low coverage of community outreach activities. Health facility delivery rate is also low (11.5%) which requires an increased effort on identification and referral of pregnant mothers to give birth at facility level. There are outbreaks of AWD, malaria and measles that occurred frequently. Recently, hepat with a hepatitis E outbreak has beenrecently reported. The epidemic response mechanisms are weak resulting in inadequate surveillance and epidemic control activities. Facilities are not yet fully staffed and still lack the necessary equipment and physical structure to provide comprehensive health services. Health workers in targeted clinics need training on IMCI and other topics which is an important skill for better patient care. Most facilities lack the skills and supplies to diagnose and treat Non-Communicable Diseases (NCDs), mental health problems and traumas.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

ZOA and partners adopt a protection mainsreaming approach and will ensure the following during the project’s design and implementation: Gender sensitive latrines, with separate stalls for men/women, with locks on the inside for safety. Gender sensitive latrines will also be constructed in schools while in public social places, separate latrines will be constructed for men and women. Basic facilities including lighting will be installed in public places to ensure safety to women and children when using them even at night. In the water infrastructure rehabilitation and development activities, the project will ensure to put in place the following protection measures: Ensure that the planning of newly constructed water points will be jointly determined iwth the community to avoid any inter-communal tensions; the water points are at a central and safe, well-lit location easily accessed by most users; and the timing for access; if regulated, is convenient for majority of users. Further, water pumps will be well positioned so that even children, the elderly, and pregnant women can easily use; separate watering points for animals.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

As part of accountability, ZOA has a complaints reporting mechanism in place in all its programming that is adopted by all implementing partners. The reporting mechanism is part of a broader three-pronged organizational integrity framework (IF) made up of i) code of conduct (coc)signed by all staff and consultants; ii) complaints reporting mechanism for staff, partners and beneficiaries and iii) Addressing complaints. ZOA will also ensure nutrition services are available in the clinics supported. Under the CoC are the child protection policy, PSEAH and fraud & corruption, among others. Each new ZOA project develops a beneficiary reporting mechanism that is shared with beneficiaries at the beginning of the project through sensitization so that they know their rights on where and whom to report to incase they have complaints.
