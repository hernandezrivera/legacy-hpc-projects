Project Summary

The overall goal of the Project is to reduce the threat from explosive ordnance (EO) to IDPs, returnees, and host communities, enabling safe returns, increased access to villages, land, and infrastructure as well as safe livelihood and stabilization activities. Further, MAG aims to contribute to building a substantial national demining capacity as well as laying the foundations for future training facilities that can be utilized by local authorities and other HMA NGOs.
MAG’s strategy in NES aims at developing an integrated response focusing on Humanitarian Mine Action which has been designed to incorporate the necessary skills and technical capacity to address the contamination threat in North East Syria (NES). This approach is an essential pre requisite for early recovery, stabilization, and safely consolidating humanitarian efforts across NES. MAG will conduct non-technical and technical surveys as well as clearance of land contaminated with explosive ordnance in NES, and delivery of Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) messaging directly as well as by training Community Focal Points.
In addition, as existing national mine action capacity remains extremely limited, and the scale of the contamination is very extensive, training of new national clearance teams together with advanced training in management for existing national demining staff members will be a key component throughout the project. Furthermore, MAG will continue to work with national partner organisations, to enhance additional efforts of national capacity building through delivery of Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) and contamination mapping. The main capacity of the project will be focused and deployed across Hasakeh and Raqqa governorates, with additional resources deployed in Deir Ez Zor to support the overall protection and stabilisation response in NES.

4 - Needs

IDPs, community members and returnees have been placed at risk of death or injury from explosive ordnance (EO) in the form of improvised landmines, cluster munitions, booby traps, and other explosive remnants of war. 6.7 million people remain internally displaced within Syria. 448,000 spontaneous IDP return movements were reported in 2020, and 38,200 refugees returned to Syria. These EO are particularly dangerous when transiting or returning to areas which have witnessed high levels of violence and conflict. UNOCHA’s 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview for Syria estimates at least 1/3 of populated communities are contaminated by explosive ordnance. Such findings correspond to MAG’s database, where a total of 3,186,986 sqm of land surveyed in Al-Hasakeh Governorate in 2021 was found to be hazardous. Although MAG has already cleared over 6km² of land, large portions of the targeted areas require further survey and clearance. In 2020 alone, an average of 76 explosive incidents per day were recorded. Furthermore, on average, 25 people are killed or injured each week by EO, with approximately 1 in 3 survivors suffering at least one limb amputation. Victim data collected by MAG shows that in 2021, 84% of victims were male, 51% adults and 33% between the ages of 11-17. Therefore, mine risk education is also critical for prevention, with UNOCHA finding more than 70% of key informants saying that they had not recently participated in mine risk education activities.

5 - Implementation of activities

MAG’s base in Hasakeh will initially serve as the location for the establishment of a training school and mechanical workshop, to serve the recruitment and training of capacity under this project, and to work with local authorities to support the long-term development of locally led management of HMA activities. A total of 20 community liaison teams and 10 clearance teams, comprising approximately 50% men and 50% women, will be engaged to implement activities in Hasakeh and Raqqa governorates. MAG will partner with two local NGOs, Action for Humanity and Bahar, who will enhance the reach and impact of this project. These organisations will supplement MAG’s CL teams by enabling delivery of in-person EORE and associated contamination survey activities in the areas of Aleppo and Deir EzZor governorates that are currently inaccessible to MAG. Further, CL teams will refer PWD surveys to a referral mechanism.To improve access to safe land and infrastructure in rural and urban areas for conflict affected communities and humanitarian actors in targeted areas, clearance activities will be implemented as follows: A2.1 Conduct manual clearance in prioritized areas across target locations; A2.2 Conduct mechanical clearance and processing of damaged and destroyed buildings and infrastructure; A2.3 Dispose all items identified and removed by clearance teams; A2.4 Conduct pre/post clearance assessments at village level; A2.5 Conduct CL activities before/during/after clearance.
