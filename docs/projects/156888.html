Project Summary

Improved food security and livelihoods for conflict-affected HHs and farmers through provision of seeds, tools, livestock, animal fodder, and fertilizer
While the need for ongoing emergency food aid to vulnerable populations in Syria is clear, complementary food security and livelihoods activities that enhance early recovery are also needed to assist these families in building resiliency in the face of ongoing conflict. One essential element of the early recovery effort is a focus on enhancing HH food security in a manner that uses local resources, builds local capacity and avoids dependence on humanitarian aid. This program's goal of increased resiliency for vulnerable, conflict-affected HHs will be achieved through 1) provision of in-kind agricultural inputs for 2,000 HHs (12,000 individuals) in non-camp areas of Northern Syria; 2) livestock and fodder distribution to 1,000 HHs (6,000 individuals); 3) ongoing technical assistance and monitoring for all beneficiaries.

Needs

The proposed program design is based on an analysis of needs from both primary data sources (our extensive on-the-ground knowledge; we are active in 21 districts across Aleppo, Hama and Idleb governorates) and information from our local partners based on their experience and ongoing operations in the target areas; and secondary data sources, including the 2018 HNO, and reports and assessments from United Nations (UN) actors and local and international NGOs. Over the past two years, the amount of food security livelihood assistance has dropped, while the amount of food assistance has increased. This program aims to provide assistance to the most vulnerable small farming families throughout Northwest Syria.

Implementation of activities

From our office in Gaziantep, GC will be responsible for overall program strategy and design, reporting, grant administration and compliance, procurement, vendor and sub-award payments, monitoring and evaluation (M&E) and quality assurance/quality control (QA/QC), and capacity building of staff. Program staff will oversee direct implementation. Our Syrian staff will enter Syria as appropriate to monitor program implementation.

Protection Risk Analysis

Consistent with our standard approach to humanitarian programming, the Program will follow Sphere Guidelines in implementing activities. The Program is based on the "Do No Harm" principle, with activities designed to be culturally appropriate and tailored to the specific context while preventing competition over resources. In settings where humanitarian needs are extreme and resources are scarce, GC recognizes that providing any material assistance has the potential to cause conflict and protection concerns, or to negatively impact local economic conditions. We will make every effort to mitigate the potential for conflict stemming from program resources by maintaining a conflict-sensitive approach to implementation; and promoting open community engagement and transparency in beneficiary selection criteria and processes, and materials distribution and assistance. GC incorporates beneficiary feedback and complaint mechanisms into program design. Our existing mechanism includes providing beneficiaries with feedback forms and contact numbers when assistance is distributed. We publicly post our anonymous feedback hotline number at public and program locations (such as child-friendly spaces and women and girls safe spaces) in target communities and in Atmeh, so that community members may send feedback to us by phone, SMS or WhatsApp. We provide the hotline number to individual HHs during beneficiary verification, assistance distribution, and post-distribution monitoring (PDM) visits to each HH, to ensure that the most vulnerable or disadvantaged have access to the information and the ability to submit his/ her own feedback.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The award will include approvals as requested by GC at the time of proposal submission for restricted items such as long-term vehicle rental and equipment (i.e., generators). • The Bab al Hawa and Bab al Salama border crossings between Turkey and Syria will remain open and accessible for humanitarian goods and staff, without extended closures, and/or alternate border points are opened and accessible; • GC staff continue to have consistent access to target locations; • Active violence does not directly impact GC's offices in Gaziantep, or Qah for a sustained period of time; • Violence in the targeted areas remains at a level which allows GC to implement the program, with both armed and unarmed parties to the conflict allowing access to vulnerable HHs for deliveries of humanitarian aid, and allowing GC to maintain acceptable levels of independence, impartiality, and neutrality; • Camp managers, Local Councils, relief committees, and community stakeholders will collaborate with GC, and facilitate access for delivery of humanitarian assistance; and • GC is able to obtain appropriate export/re-export license renewals in time to allow for program implementation according to the approved work plan.
