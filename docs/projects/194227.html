Project Summary

The Logistics Cluster is a service-based cluster and acts, through WFP, as a provider of last resort for common logistics services. The Logistics Cluster aims to augment the capacities of humanitarian organizations and facilitate the timely delivery of humanitarian assistance to vulnerable populations through logistics coordination, information management, preparedness and facilitation of common services to the entire humanitarian community in South Sudan.

1 - Needs

Poor road infrastructure remains one of the main challenges in South Sudan for people to access essential services and assistance, as well as for humanitarian organizations to reach people in need. Around 350 km of roads outside the urban areas are paved, out of an estimated 17,000 km of roads. During the lengthy rainy season, a large part of the unpaved roads is rendered inaccessible. Due to several consecutive years of above average rains and floods, with heavy downpour beginning as early as April and continuing as late as December, road surfaces have been damaged and impacts are lasting well into the dry season. Many roads, including main supply routes, have become accessible for only a few months a year, while some communities remain cut off completely. The lack of reliable road infrastructure makes the supply chain in South Sudan exceedingly expensive and inefficient. The prepositioning of commodities in strategic locations is an essential strategy for the humanitarian community to reduce costs by making use of the short window of opportunity for road transport and enhance emergency preparedness. Road transport enables the transportation of large quantities of cargo at the lowest cost. To enable prepositioning, humanitarians require access to storage facilities in key locations and the mitigation of prevalent risks such as looting. River transport carries a great potential for South Sudan, with many locations across the country being connected by waterways. In recent years, river transport has become an important transportation mode for humanitarian cargo, with commercial transporters moving large barges on main river routes or smaller boats on streams. However, river operations are generally volatile with many factors such as security challenges, port congestion, and boat availability affecting the reliability of the services. Riverways and docking sites are in urgent need of rehabilitation and expansion works in order to maintain accessibility, improve capacity and reduce delays. Sub-national conflict in areas around the waterways have caused river movements to be put on hold at various times throughout the year, delaying the delivery of essential relief items to affected communities and affecting stock levels in hubs that are served by river.The combination of a general lack of physical infrastructure and the lasting impact of severe flooding puts a heavy reliance on air cargo transport to bring much needed supplies to the most vulnerable people. Air transport is often the only option in South Sudan for humanitarian organizations to reach affected communities not accessible by other means and to respond to acute emergencies requiring a timely intervention, such as disease outbreaks, rapid missions, and response scale-ups. With many organizations facing budget constraints, an increase of people in need, and several compounding emergencies arising each year, the facilitation of common logistics services across South Sudan remains essential to meet the needs of the affected population.

2 - Project activities

The Logistics Cluster addresses the logistical challenges of the humanitarian response in South Sudan by providing logistics coordination, information management, and preparedness services to the humanitarian community. Where gaps are identified, the Logistics Cluster also facilitates access to common services, including cargo transport and common storage space.The Logistics Cluster serves as a coordination body for humanitarian actors to identify logistics gaps, minimize duplication of efforts, and ensure logistics efficiencies. To achieve this, monthly coordination meetings are held in Juba, and on a semi-regular basis at sub-national level at the Logistics Cluster hubs in Bentiu, Bor, Malakal, Rumbek and Wau, while also supporting partners in deep field locations with more localized coordination. The Logistics Cluster provides information management to support operational decision making and logistics planning. This includes the identification, consolidation, and dissemination of essential logistics information, as well as GIS support to partners. Information is shared on the dedicated webpage where all products are publicly accessible and through the mailing list with more than 800 active subscribers.The Logistics Cluster enhances logistics preparedness among its partners through capacity strengthening. In 2023, the cluster will provide capacity building and training services to the entire humanitarian community operating in South Sudan, with a special focus on national actors and female logisticians.The common transport services that the Logistics Cluster facilitates access to include road, river and air transport. To maximize the efficient use of limited resources, the cluster advocates for the prepositioning of cargo during the dry season, making use of the short period each year that roads are accessible. This is enabled by the Logistics Cluster through the coordination of road convoys along all major supply routes in South Sudan. The cluster will increase the frequency of road convoys during the short window of opportunity when key routes are open to allow humanitarian actors to safely transport their cargo with the most cost-efficient transport method. Common storage is offered in locations lacking sufficient capacity to meet storage needs, and a stock of Mobile Storage Units is maintained to loan to organizations in need of storage space in field locations. The Logistics Cluster also works closely together with IOM’s Common Transport Service project to offer road transport to locations reachable by road from Bentiu, Wau and Malakal, further reducing the reliance on air operations. As a last resort only, the Logistics Cluster will facilitate air cargo transport to destinations not reachable by other means or in case of emergencies with a combination of air assets that is adjustable to the needs of the humanitarian community.
