Project Summary

RI aims to provide prevention, empowerment, and response GBV services as well as CP prevention and response services through a standalone and integrated approach with RI health services in the targeted area. RI will target the most vulnerable women, girls, and boys including people with disability, widowed women and children living in widows camps, women and children headed households, adolescent girls, adolescent boys, and older women.RI will utilize a community-based approach to enhance access to the most vulnerable targeted categories through outreach, community committees. RI will support functioning 3 WGSSs in the following subdistricts: Teftanaz in Idleb, Azaz in Aleppo.RI will strengthen its integrated model of health and protection services to prevent and respond to GBV, building the capacity of all staff composed of GBV caseworkers, PSS workers, and outreach teams through training provided by RI technical team utilizing the materials endorsed by the capacity building initiative for GBV and GBV SC including GBV CM training and following supervision mechanisms to ensure service quality including assessing attitudes, knowledge, and skills and providing staff care through in-person and online supervision and follow up. RI will include GBV team in Abin Samaan Medical Complex in Abin Samaan in Atareb /Aleppo to provide integrated GBV prevention and response services with the reproductive health services. RI will use its supported health facility in Teftanaz AlNour Maternity and Children Hospital as an entry point, link survivors to specialized services will be provided in the WGSS there.RI will coordinate with other actors through the referral pathway developed by GBV SC and complementing the localized service mapping will be conducted regularly by the team in the target areas to ensure safe referral and avoid duplication.
In line with the GBV Minimum Standards in Emergencies, the services provided within RI WGSSs are GBV response including case management, PFA, individual and group counseling, and referral to CMR, MH, and non GBV services based on the needs and informed consent of the survivors and women and girls at risk in addition to prevention services, RI will support the establishment of women’s committees to foster the participation of women in decision-making structures as well as build on community protective mechanisms already in place. RI will work on capacity building the humanitarian workers including the health workers to be aware of GBV concepts and deal with GBV survivors upon disclosure.
On the other hand, RI will provide CP services including outreach activities, awareness-raising on CP concepts, structured PSS for boys and girls including children with disabilities, parenting skills, and providing CP case management for children with and at risk of child protection risks. RI will ensure engagement of the community through establishing and training CP committees from the community to be responsible for raising awareness on CP concerns in the targeted areas as well as identification and referral of children at risk to response services provided at the CFSs.
All activities will be implemented while adhering to strict COVID 19 prevention measures. Hotline service will be used additionally to provide online information sharing, Psychological first aid, and referral.
the GBV services will be monitored, measured, and evaluated with recommendations to improve the services delivered in the targeted areas according to the GBV MEAL toolkits developed by the GBV SC with focusing on the quality of service benefiting from the supervision mechanisms and monitoring tools including the survivor satisfaction survey. while CP services will follow a MEAL approach includes monitoring, evaluation, accountability, and learning which is supported by technical expertise in gender, age & disability, child protection and consistent and accurate flow of information and data in relation to gender, age, and disability.

4 - Needs

According to VOICES FROM SYRIA 2022, the most common form of violence mentioned by women and girls in NWS was IPV and family violence, digital/online violence, restrictions of rights and movements, denial of education - linked to early marriage and child labor, deprivation of inheritance especially widows, sexual exploitation and harassment - especially at work and for rent and early marriage continues to be mentioned. women and girls with disability are most commonly subjected to physical, psychological, and social violence: including IPV, psychological violence by the family, public bullying in school, social stigma, and neglect. in addition, widowed women are at increased risk for Forced re-marriage and forced abandonment of children in some cases, movement restrictions by family members,and higher levels of social and community violence (stigma, shaming). According to HNO 2022,Multiple and complex child protection risks will continue to shape the lives of girls and boys.Grave violations against children remain a significant concern. Increased vulnerability will result in further neglect of persons with disabilities,children with disabilities are denied access to basic rights. Girls and boys continue to experience persisting violence including physical,emotional,sexual domestic violence.In addition,Psychosocial distress,family separation, and child labor are among the most CP concerns mentioned.Insecurity,economic hardship,and Covid19 exacerbate both CP and GBV concerns.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will support functioning 3 WGSSs and 1 GBV team in a hospital, in addition to 2 CFSs.GBV response services for survivors and women and girls at risk will include case management , focused PSS, referrals, and PFA while GBV prevention includes outreach, dignity Kits distribution, and PFA especially in the emergency response with coordination with protection cluster,awareness-raising utilizing the GBV SC toolkit, and vocational training after consultation with women and girls.RI will conduct safety audits to services and facilities, risk mapping, contextualize participatory GBV key messages, integrated awareness-raising for men and boys as well as psychoeducation to address behavioral problems to enhance their engagement in the GBV programming. CP services will include CP case management and CP community-based approach will be provided by trained and supervised CP staff. All supplies should be provided to the CFS including the PSS toolkits for children and adolescent boys and girls. Child protection networks CPNs will be established and maintained based on the volunteers from parents shared in the awareness-raising sessions. Supervision will be provided remotely and physically to CP staff to ensure providing quality services and in line with the CPMS. Parenting sessions and structured PSS will be conducted in the CFS and on the community level.Transportation will be provided for women girls and boys to access the services in WGSSs and CFSs.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

First risk: Lack of quality services and referral expose survivors to harm, enhancing the risk of stigmatization, reprisal, and further trauma in addition to the possibility of breaching confidentiality, putting at risk SGBV survivors due to lack of capacity on information management of service providers. Likelihood: High; Impact: High. RI will implement the following mitigation measures: 1. Case management with survivors to be conducted by trained personnel only; 2. Service mapping includes only those that can provide survivor-centered services; 3. Mainstream messaging on the risk of the available legal systems which is likely to cause harm to survivors; 4. Self-care strategies as part of programmers and organization HR policy. Second Risk: Risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls, and boys (and their caregivers) through the provision of specialized CP services if service providers are not adequately trained/services are of low quality. Likelihood: High; Impact: High. RI will mitigate by 1. Ensuring training packages are in line with child protection minimum standards and considering remote management modality; 2. Services should not be provided by any staff who have not received and demonstrated proficiency in training modules; 3. Include a practice element to training modules that can be overseen through remote mentorship arrangements; 4. Service mappings should only include services that is determined to provide safe and quality services.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

the project aims to provide life skills, skills development, and Vocational training for women and adolescent girls in addition to linking them with livelihood opportunities, However, Livelihood opportunities may increase risks of exploitation by service providers or by another actor in the community and RI will work on mitigation measures through the following: 1. Consult women and men, boys and girls in the design of the program. 2. Ensure participants know services were to disclose if violence occurs 3. Raise awareness on PSEA and other types of exploitation and where to report 4. Establish good relations with local authorities, clarify humanitarian principles, and stick to them.5.ensure the compliant and feedback mechanisms are accessible for all women and girls. Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information is linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific person/groups. Likelihood: medium.Impact: High. RI will mitigate the risk by 1. Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and who services are for. 2. Provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA.3. provide awareness-raising sessions on PSEA for boys, girls, and parents 4. Provide child-friendly feedback and complaints mechanisms.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: The CFSs and WGSSs are located in relatively safe areas. Risk: Armed Clashes, arrest and detention, shelling/artillery, VBIEDs/IEDs. Mitigation: staff to contact the security focal point and alert all staff to be prepared for immediate evacuation/hibernation if possible. Activate an emergency plan. Assumption: Communities will engage in CP program activities. The risk is that existing traditional practices and community structures conflict with child protection principles (e.g. child rights messaging is perceived as a threat). Ri will mitigate the risk by: 1. Training some staff as key resource persons to act as trainers/ facilitators; 2. Regular engagement/communication with communities through the CPNs and community leaders. WGSSs will be operated smoothly in the targeted communities especially in the widow's camps. Risk: Resistance or backlash from sections of the community and community leaders on WGSS support in widows’ camps. Mitigation: RI will use their already access through health services in the area to strengthen community engagement from the onset of the project, from the assessment phase, and throughout all project cycles including meetings with religious and community leaders and Engagement with the community and local authorities and clear communication on project objectives to ensure the ability to establish the WGSSs. RI will target all surrounding camps not only the widow’s camps to avoid any stigmatization for the project clients.
