Project Summary

The projects intends to provide a multi-faceted response in an integrated and coherent way. Shafak's proposal is designed to supporting farmers and livestock keepers, Small-medium Enterprises needs of the vulnerable IDPs, returnees and HCs. Over 2023, the project comprises the following key intervention areas:
Activity1: 3500 Targeted winter crops farmers (wheat and barley) are able to resume and restore agricultural activities, to increase household income and improve consumption through provision of agriculture start-up kit that contribute to the cost of : i) agricultural inputs, including improved access to water; pest control, harvesting and V) extension services, VI) purchasing wheat.
Activity2: 2000 Targeted olive trees are able to resume and restore agricultural activities, to increase household income and improve consumption through provision of agriculture start-up kit that contribute to the cost of : i) agricultural inputs, including improved access to water; pest control, harvesting and V) extension services.
Activity3: 2000 Targeted forage crops farmers are able to resume and restore agricultural activities, to increase household income and improve consumption through provision of agriculture start-up kit that contribute to the cost of : i) agricultural inputs, including improved access to water; pest control, harvesting and V) extension services.
Activity 4: 6500 Targeted households are able to increase household income and maintain livestock activities through: i) provision of livestock inputs (500 kg of fodder per beneficiary 1500 beneficiaries) ; ii) associated training, iii) vaccination campaigns per health care calendar (4 campaigns, 200,000 head per campaign owned by 4,000 households).
Activity 5: 4000 Targeted households stabilize, maintain or increase their income through Cash-for-Work activities, which build and strengthen community related value chain infrastructure , disaster risk reduction, mechanical pest and vole control and olive trees harvesting, purification of wheat fields, and afforestation.
Activity 6: Rehabilitation of related value chain infrastructure, irrigation channels, Markets, Mills, bakeries, storage, wells, nurseries , seeds multiplication, fodder mixers, feeder roads and silos.
Activity 7: Graduation Business Grants: 500 Small-Micro Enterprises through provision of business skills training and financial or in-kind support with value 500 USD. Selected beneficiaries will have access to financial services, in addition to improving job skills and linkages to the job market. The identified participants will receive Business Development Skills training (BDS)including operations and financial management(food processing, production, agriculture and livestock enterprises.
Activity 8: 5000 HHs to be supported with regular food basket over 8 rounds.
Activity 9 : 8000 HHs to be supported with Cash/voucher based on populations preference and market dynamics
Activity 10 : 10000 RTEs to be distributed as response to emergencies and shocks
Activity 11: 1800 MT of flour to be distributed to bakeries to subsidize bread prices in the communities .
Activity 12: 2500 MT of flour to be distributed as free bread to the populations in the camps
Activity 13: 1000 smallholders’ vegetable producers increased access to possess productive assets and the means to protect them. The Work with farmers of the selected value chains to increase access to quality inputs to expand their current business and start new crops for their existing business. beginning with climate-smart technical agriculture training to manage their agribusiness, acquire technical farming skills, and gain experience with best practices in agriculture for their crops.
Activity 14: Farmers and livestock keepers capacity building to strengthen their technical capacity and develop their capabilities.
It is worth mentioning that about 20 % of 2022 budget is secured up to date of this submission.

3 - Needs

The food security situation in Syria has drastically deteriorated since last year. In 2023, an estimated15 million people will need some form of food and agriculture assistance, representing 68 per cent ofthe total population. At least 12.1 million people are estimated to be food insecure HNO 23. It is important to note that the sharp decline in crop production and the entrenchment of food insecurity during the 2021-2022 cropping season was not only driven by weather anomalies but also other drivers including issues with water scarcity and availability and disrupted services.Key drivers of food insecurity in the region include low agricultural output and ongoing hostilities. Other contextual drivers have also played a role, including the effects of C-19, the regional economic crises, wildfires and other climate-induced shocks. In addition to the high inflation that has affected NWS for several months, the ongoing war in Ukraine is expected to also lead to an increase in food insecurity.RNA was conducted by Shafak in June 2022 by SO Income generating Barriers: 34% of interviewed people said lack of financial capital is the biggest reason preventing women to start or expand their own businesses, followed by 23% due to baby care, 13% not accepted by the society, 13% lack of training skills and finally 9%lack of family support. As for men 56% and for PWD 41% of interviewed people said lack of financial capital is key hinder for IGAs. followed by lack of training.

Shafak SOPs are in line with FSL, CTWG, WFP. All the HH targeted with regular food assistance will be supported with minimum 8 rounds.
