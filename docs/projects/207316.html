Project Summary

In the context of the Anglophone crisis there has been focus on the safety of IDPs and Returnees. In the same light, this project intends to enhance access to fuel and cooking means while ensuring the safety and sustainability of the environment. About 70% of households in the North West Region of Cameroon depend on burning solid fuel to meet their cooking, heating and other household energy needs. The burning of firewood for cooking is prevalent and in a home, cooking over a 3 stone fire-side is equivalent to burning 400 cigarettes an hour that releases toxic smoke and emissions which mostly affect women and children. Additionally, the source of this firewood results from deforestation which has adverse effects on the environment resulting in climate change. Inefficient cook stoves have sufficed to be one of the major environmental threats considering the emissions and deforestation. This project intends to reduce pollution from domestic smoke and improve the health of beneficiaries through the introduction of an energy efficient-technology for domestic cooking firesides this will reduce the rates of carbon emissions and deforestation in order to enhance the protection of the right to a healthy environment. This assistance targets IDPs in spontaneous informal settlements and host community members living in damaged shelters who are unable to access fuel or markets. This initiative is culturally appropriate. Through this there will be reduced tree destruction for domestic firewood and improved bio-diversity conservation. ASDEPCO will construct and distribute 1000 energy saving stove to 1000 IDP, returnee and host community households. Beneficiaries will equally be trained on how to construct the energy saving stove. Local resources like cement, gravel, sand, aluminum and water will be used in constructing the stove. The initiative will reduce demand for firewood where deforestation rate had been high thereby leading to ecosystem conservation as more forest patches will lay undisturbed. The energy saving stoves not only reduce air pollution but significantly reduces firewood consumption and saves cooking times for women. Charcoal will be bought and distributed after the construction and distribution of the stove.
Community engagement meeting will be hell with various stakeholders to discuss the objectives of the project focus group discussion will also be held with various age groups. Profiling and selection of beneficiaries will be done with community leaders and the training and distribution of the stove and charcoal will be per community, protected of participant will be mainstreamed throughout the project. This project will mitigate gender based violence in that women and person with disability will be including in decision making at all levels. Suggestion box will be installed in the communities and feedback numbers will be distributed for accountability. To achieve this project, a budget of 36,363 USD will be required.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Globally, some 500 million households with more than 70% in Africa depend on burning solid fuel to meet their cooking, heating, lighting, and other household energy needs. The exploitation of wood fuel is having so many negative impacts on many households in Cameroon. It is from this backdrop that ASDEPCO conducted a Needs Assessment in Nkum Sub Division, Oku Sub Division and Jakiri Sub Divisions of Bui Sub Division wherein statistic reveal that more than 70% in Africa depend on burning solid fuel to meet their cooking, heating, lighting, and other household energy needs. It is estimated that 50 000 Envirofit stoves can prevent 570 000 tons of CO2 from being emitted, save 700 000 of trees from being cut, 5.5 million hours of women’s time spent in collected wood, $14 million reduced fuel consummation and 270 000 livelihoods improved. In Kumbo central town in Cameroon, around 90% of people use wood for cooking on traditional 3 stone fires that are both highly inefficient and a cause of indoor air pollution. Most people purchase their wood for cooking, which means that very little of household budget is left over to invest in a cleaner, more efficient cook stove. It is noticeable especially among returnees that a significant proportion of household income goes on rent and housing struggling to afford fuel, with other displaced persons unable to access fuel or markets. Acknowledging these needs, the project intends to distribute less carbon emissive/ less firewood consuming stoves, as well as assisting IDPs, non-displaced and returnees with finances to easily acquire fuel. This will go along way to;Reduce C02 emissions through more efficient firewood use of improved stovesImprove livelihoods through a cheaper, more time efficient and healthier cooking practices (The stoves emit less smoke and also safer to cook on)Make low carbon technologies widely available in the region through subsidized cookstovesReduce Deforestation
