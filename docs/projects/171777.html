Project Summary

Somalia is a complex political and security environment which is beset by poverty, recurring famine, and violent conflict. The country has been in a state of armed conflict and without a functional central government since 1991. One third of the estimated nine million people who inhabit Somalia live in extreme poverty and the average life expectancy is only 47 years. Drought not only costs individuals but undermines the economic growth of the country. The impact of these events is amplified by extreme levels of poverty, which work together to create a latent risk of catastrophe as was evident in 2011 famine. Somalia is heavily reliant on agriculture and livestock as the main sources of livelihoods, which are very vulnerable to climate change. To reduce the impact of climate change, it is important to build the resilience of communities and ensure that production systems are climate resilient. Somalia is experiencing the worst desert locust invasion in 25 years. Currently, the situation is far worse than initially anticipated and has been exacerbated by cyclone Pawan which provided favorable ecological conditions for the locust to breed. Recent government and UN surveys in Somalia reveal that Locusts are at various stages of development with hoppers, immature as well as mature adults. Vast numbers of immature adults migrated south and are reported to have reached, Puntland. Most of these initial swarms have now moved to Puntland and started breeding and laying and have caused massive damage to crop and pasture. When not managed at the place of origin or breeding ground, they can lead to loss of up to 100% of food and fodder crops. An estimated 70 000 hectares of land has been invaded by hoppers and breeding adults with more than 100 000 hectares requiring some form of direct control in the upcoming months. As a result of this, the situation has changed from “Caution” to “Critical’. The infestation spread and threatened agricultural productivity and have directly affected, resulting in major destruction of agricultural production because the locust swarms flew rapidly across them and caused significant crop damage. As a result of the ongoing locust plague, crops and pasture conditions have deteriorated, with adverse consequences on livestock health and productivity. According to 2.1million people across Somalia are expected to face Crisis or worse (IPC Phase 3 or higher) out comes between now and December without sustained humanitarian assistance. An additional 3million people are expected to be Stressed (IPC Phase 2), bringing the total number of people facing acute food insecurity to 5.1million.Humanitarian assistance must be sustained through December 2020 to prevent Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or Emergency (IPC Phase 4)outcomes for 2.1 million people. Livelihoods support is also required for people that are Stressed or worse (IPC Phase 2or higher). In order to address this problem, NCA will undertake a sustained food security and livelihood support intervention in 2021. The geographical coverages of the response include regions of Nugaal and Gedo of Puntland and Jubaland state of Somalia. The specific activities to be undertaken will be largely be cash-based interventions to improve purchasing power and food access for households in emergency situations and Livelihood protection activities covering crop production support and rehabilitation of community infrastructures through CFW activities. In order to ensure that marginalized groups and person with disabilities will participate in the consultation and the decision making process during the project cycle NCA will Ensure communities, beneficiaries are consulted, ensure staff and partners use the targeting criteria developed in consultation with key stakeholders including communities as well as ensuring the inclusion of most vulnerable people through good context assessment and analysis of the affected populations. Project Activities

To achieve the proposed objectives NCA will undertake the following activities in each of its three objective areas a) To improve immediate food security status for households affected by drought and conflict emergencies in Somalia. The activities to be undertaken under this objective will mainly be live saving emergency responses that will exclusively target communities faced with acute food insecurity and classified to be in IPC 3. The target groups include new displaced households, returnees and host communities severely devastated by the droughts who have completely lost their income and food sources opportunities. The focus is to safe the lives of the target households by extending to them opportunities to immediately access food either through improving their immediate income sources and purchasing power to access food from the local markets. A total of 1500 Households (9,000 persons) 700 HHs targeted from Gedo and 800 HHs from Punt-land will be reached through the following activities. 1. Unconditional cash transfer targeting1500 households during the peak period of emergency for three months to meet their basic needs the focus will be displaced households, returnees and host communities severely devastated by the droughts and conflicts. 2. 600 HHs (3,000 persons) will be targeted under the conditional cash transfers through CFW for a period of 3 three months, the HHs will undertake Cash for work activities for rehabilitation and construction of community infrastructures at the same time targeted HHs will have access to the cash needed to purchase food and other basic items and services among the activities undertaken under the CFW include rehabilitating water pans, and other activities which enhance community owned facilities rehabilitation communal (water catchments) b) provision of Agricultural seasonal inputs 500 HHs (3000 persons) provided with varied farm inputs. Provision of Farm inputs/tools assorted certified seeds and fuels for Agro pastoral HHs in Nugaal and Gedo. The project will create diversified income to vulnerable agro-pastoral and realized enhanced food and income sources. c) To build community to withstand the effects of the current and future shocks. The activities under this objective will focus on DRR training on Community managed Disaster Risks Reduction strategies and Disaster early warning systems (10 communities)200 persons. Training of the local administration leaders, community elders to ensure that the communities have skills and knowledge to plan for and cope with future livelihood shocks they will be trained on risk reduction and early warning. The training will use iterative methodologies, with communities encouraged to identify issues, early warning signs that they can monitor and helped to develop tentative response plans

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The key justification for using the cash is give option to beneficiaries how to spend and decide on their households needs and gives them the flexibility to choose according to their own diverse needs and priorities, cash are considered by many as more dignified than receiving in kind. other justification for using cash is stimulating local markets, cash-based interventions may benefit more than the direct recipients of cash. Local traders and service providers, transport workers and suppliers and transporters of goods may all benefit from households having money to spend

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

NCA participated in the Joint market monitoring initiative that has largely informed this response in terms of what activities can be linked to CTP. Internal rapid market surveys have been undertaken to determine commodity prices but this is subject to change with the changing market trends resulting from the impact of COVID 19 and limited access to markets in some area as a result of floods/conflict etc, during the project implementation post-distribution monitoring will be conducted to assess the market functionality, prices of essential commodities in the market as well as the impact of cash transfer on the local market

Mitigation Measures

NCA is committed to gender-sensitive programming which recognizes and addresses the different needs, experiences, capacities and responsibilities of women, girls, boys and men, including female-headed households. Program interventions will be designed to address the varying vulnerabilities, capacities and needs at community and household levels, recognizing that some groups are disproportionally impacted by shocks and stresses. Assessment, implementation, and disaggregated M&E will be informed by gender analysis to ensure that different needs of women, girls, boys and men are correctly identified and addressed. The program will promote empowerment of women by creating greater opportunities for their involvement in key institutions and decision-making processes. It will identify gender roles and responsibilities; access to and control over household productive assets and resources; household decision-making and risk management strategies; access to and control of program benefits; NCA will ensure that both women and men participate equally in community initiatives and influence the design of projects and other decisions taken around CFW activities in recognition of women and men’s different considerations in accessing or using productive assets. NCA will examine the differences between threats identified by women and those identified by men to further refine gender sensitivity in subsequent NCA activities. Mainstreaming of basic protection principles in all projects will be a paramount importance to NCA. The project will be designed based on a clear analysis of the threats, vulnerabilities and capacities of target populations and ensure that the response prevents and responds to the risks identified. Additionally, as stated above, NCA will continue to prioritize the strengthening of safe measures in line with the ‘Do No Harm’ principles to ensure that interventions do not cause harm to the target population. Inclusion and participation of all relevant stakeholders in all processes will be a key priority. The project will also response to meet minimum SPHERE standards, consult IASC Gender Handbook on humanitarian Action, safeguard participatory approaches and address protection issues. The project will make deliberate effort to maintain a protective environment at community level so that girls and women are not exposed to harm such as sexual and gender-based violence while undertaking their daily chores. Further, gender equity will be observed while implementing all project activities. Men, women, Boys and Girls will be treated equally with their different needs being targeted and support provided accordingly. The project will mainstream messages on HIV and Aids into all aspects of the programme and ensure equal participation of all categories of beneficiaries without discrimination. The involvement of religious leaders, community elders and local authorities, will particularly be crucial, as, in the Somalia context, they are well respected and have the moral authority to see to it that potential sources of conflicts are handled before they escalate. NCA will employ both women and men as staff members, so that they can communicate with right-holders of both sexes thus build local capacities for peace by identifying dividers and connectors and develop strategies for addressing them with a view of addressing potential sources of conflicts. . In order to ensure that marginalized groups and person with disabilities will participate in the consultation and the decision making process during the project cycle NCA will Ensure communities, beneficiaries are consulted, ensure staff and partners use the targeting criteria developed in consultation with key stakeholders including communities as well as ensuring the inclusion of most vulnerable people through good context assessment and analysis of the affected populations, NCA Consult with beneficiaries on the appropriate reporting and feedback mechanisms.

Please provide further explanation

The proposed plan will Build the resilience and livelihood of the displaced households this will lead to protection of environment, displaced persons are already engaged in different forms of income generating activities some of which may not be sustainable, for example charcoal burning, firewood collection etc. in order to ensure protection of environment, the displaced households must be supported to develop diversified and sustainable positive livelihoods that will contribute to improved resilience. the project will also construct bunds and terraces to conserve water and soil in the target areas. the project will work closely with the government and community structures. The proposed project will educate the stakeholders and community on DRR and how to integrate disaster risk reduction into livelihoods activities hence a step towards enhancing the capacity of both community and government representative. The training will also strengthen the capacities of the local community to mainstream community managed disaster risk reduction concepts, principles and practices in the planning and implementation of community development efforts The rehabilitated community assets after completion will be managed by the community and supervised/ rehabilitated by the local government. To ensure sustainability, the proposed plan will establish early warning and action centers in the community, the centers will be managed by community DRR committee. the role and responsibility of the community DRR committee will be to sensitize community of possible disaster and help community prepare on how to cope with the disaster. The rehabilitated/constructed communal assets will be handed back to community management committee with supervision of local government to ensure that the assets are accessed by all community groups (women, children and youths).
