Project Summary

Universal Network for Knowledge and Empowerment Agency (UNKEA) will conduct life-saving WASH activities, integrated with nutrition, GBV prevention and Health. The project aims to meet the emergency critical needs of vulnerable people in South Sudan. Particular focus will be in Nasir, Ulang, Pochalla, Yei, Morobo and Tonj North of Upper Nile, Jonglei, Central Equatoria and Warrap States affected by conflict, natural disasters, disease outbreaks, and areas of high malnutrition rates. UNKEA will deliver relevant, effective and needs-based WASH interventions.
The project aims to provide timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected populations, increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices among vulnerable and affected populations to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases targeting the returnees, host community and IDPs. Interventions will include safe water provision through water rehabilitation eg; hand pumps, water trucking, or provision of household water filters and water purification tablets. Others shall include provision or rehabilitation of sanitation infrastructure including latrines, hand washing facilities and solid waste management. Hygiene promotion will be prioritized through hand washing campaigns in communities and institutions like nutrition centers. Proposed interventions shall also include capacity-building of community pump mechanics, water-user committees, community hygiene promoters, and distribution of WASH NFIs soap, hygiene kits, menstrual hygiene kit, water containers, water purification filters, and PUR among others.
Total targeted group is 60,751 beneficiaries that include 16,403 women, 15,795 girls, 14,580 men and 13,973 boys. The project will target 24,301 host community members, 19,440 returnees and 17,010 IDPs. In Ulang county the intervention will benefit 12,000 (host community 7,200, Returnees 2,400, and IPDs 2,400). In Nasir 15,000(host community 9,000, Returnees 3,000, and IPDs 3,000). In Yei 9,000(host community 5,400, Returnees 1,359, IDPs 1,350 1286 and Refuges 900). In Morobo 7,800 (host community 4,680, Returnees 1,560 and IDPs 1,560). In Pochalla 8,000 (host community 4,800, Returnees 1,600 and IDPs 1,600) and Tonj North shall target 8951 (host community 5,371, Returnees 1,790 and IPDs 1,790). The project will target communities with poor access to safe water supply, unsafe sanitation and hygiene practices, poor sanitation coverage and support nutrition, Gender Based Violence (GBV) and health.
The intervention will include detailed multi-sector needs assessment to identify the needs, affected gender, age and diversity groups, the vulnerabilities of the targeted community and coping strategies employed by the affected population. Multi-sector interventions will include joint planning, capacity building, and strategy sessions with focal points of nutrition, NFI, Protection, FSL and Health actors. The project will benefit from humanitarian hub and cluster field coordination to optimise response capacity with other partners operating in the above areas. The main priority is to provide WASH to reduce morbidity and mortality caused by WASH related diseases and limited access to water.
A combined approach of cash-based programing and protection will be utilised to mobilise cash-for-work for public sanitation while ensuring protection threats are minimised. Mainstreaming tools including application of protection checklists during project execution will be undertaken to maximise benefits to persons with disabilities, children, women, elderly-led households. Training and mentorship will target grass roots structures and local authority establishments in order to reinforce coping mechanism to manage current shocks and sustain livelihoods - and to strengthen linkages with development action to support resilience.

1 - Needs

Five years of the most recent conflict has forced nearly 4.2 million people to flee their homes in search of safety, nearly 2 million of them within and 2.2 million outside the country. While the intensity of conflict may have reduced recently, and clashes contained to certain regions, vulnerable people still continue to experience the impacts of the conflict through 2019. UN reports indicate that all parties to the conflict have repeatedly violated international humanitarian law and perpetrated serious human rights abuses, including gang rape, abductions, sexual slavery of women and girls, and recruitment of children, both girls and boys. In Yei County, armed conflicts between government forces and opposition groups accounted for displacement of 26,454 people in 9 locations (DTM/IOM Event Tracking report April, 2019).The conflict and associated economic decline have eroded the government’s ability to provide basic services to its people. Only 40 per cent of nutrition treatment centers have access to safe water, a gap that puts most vulnerable people, particularly women, boys and girls, at risk of further deterioration in health, livelihood, and threat to life. In Tonj North there is repeated evidence of clean water supply breakdown due to unrepaired and un-serviced boreholes/water points (IRNA report March 2019). Most of the boreholes are broken and require maintenance in order to serve the growing need from IDPs and host populations. Increased pressure on the few functional water points has been observed as water stress was reported a major concern during the dry season because people and livestock rely and share the same water points. In Morobo and Tonj North counties, tensions and delays at water points were reported and worsened with reported influx of returnees and IDPs from the cattle raids, armed conflicts, and inter-communal conflicts. Pre-existing latrines have been washed away by floods (Inter-agency flood assessment in Tharwang, Ulang Sept. 2019). In an assessment in Warrap, the main Awan IDP settlement borehole tested positive for contaminants while latrines are filled up and no longer used (Greater Warrap IRNA Assessment-summary of findings March 2019). Relatedly, IOM Displacement Matrix documents insufficient water and sanitation in Yei county from datasets gathered between 23-31, Mar. 2019. During recent assessments and consultations conducted in preparation to this proposal, hygiene practices were observed to be generally very poor in part due to lack of water and soap (even in most markets). Respondents indicated lack of appropriate female dignity kits and sanitation kits. About 95% of households were observed with open defecation being the practice. In 2019, UNKEA identified & trained hygiene promoters and pump mechanics in Ulang but are insufficient to meet the growing humanitarian demands. Where pump mechanics exist, there are shortage of spares and kits to effect repair of water points. WASH NFIs were mostly lacking in most assessed locations. This was attributed to destruction of property and looting during clashes in Yei while Upper Nile is attributed to influx of returnees with very limited household resources. The flood situation recorded in the last quarter of 2019 have caused more massive displacements and threatened hygiene and sanitation even further. It’s highly predicted that the impact of floods will persist through 2020 in absence of appropriate humanitarian response.In all the above counties, humanitarian needs across sectors are likely to remain high among the displaced populations who are either displaced from the usual areas of residence or returning to their original places of residence. The capacity of host households have been over weighed. To ameliorate the deteriorating situations, the project will provide time-sensitive and life-saving assistance to most-in-need beneficiaries in order to save lives and restore normalcy.

2 - Activities

To address the identified needs, the project will deliver an integrated design of activities that address vulnerabilities of households with malnourished children, provides protection mitigation measures to persons or beneficiaries susceptible to violance or abuse and community-wide response to avert threats of health epidemics. The main scope of projects activities will include the following in the counties of Ulang, Nasir, Yei, Morobo, Pochalla and Tonj North;Provision of safe water through water rehabilitation of 50 hand pumps/water points. Where applicable, the project will deliver water trucking to different convergence service points in the most-affected locations. Elevated water points will be established in flood-prone areas. In order to consolidate and maintain the water provisions through established and rehabilitated water points, the project will identify and train water user management committees across respective localities; The intervention will also distribute household water filters and water purifiers to 8,000 households in order to reduce the level of contamination and control potential disease outbreaks. Caretakers of malnourished children will be enlisted beneficiaries in order to maximally support their capacities;Provision or rehabilitation of 80 sanitation infrastructures such as Latrines, hand washing facilities and solid waste management;Hygiene promotion such as hand washing campaigns in communities and institutions including nutrition centers (OTP and Stabilisation Centres). An average of 6 hygiene promoters will be trained and equiped in each payam identified for humanitarian response. A total of 20 Safe water chains and jerrycan cleaning campaigns will be conducted in selected locations within the earmarked counties above; Capacity-building of 480 community pump mechanics, water user committees, community hygiene promoters, Nutrition's hygiene Health centers; Water quality monitoring will be undertaken in a regular basis using standard water tests to ascertain contamination levels and promt redress;Twelve (12) risk assessments and community protection plans will be undertaken and developed respectively for highly sensitive payams in each county for total of six counties.Distribution of WASH NFIs soap, hygiene kits, menstrual hygiene kit, water containers, and water purification filters, PUR or others to 7500 registered vulnerable households. In delivering these activities, UNKEA will ensure that a feedback mechanism that includes women, IDP representatives, youth, and other vulnerable groups are established to oversee quality considerations and stakeholder compliance in the project. The views of persons with disabilities, women, men, local authorities shall be absorbed and addressed through the different cycles of the project execution. Baseline assessments, post distribution monitoring, field visit reports, and endline assessments shall be undertaken to measure the achievements against commitments made in this proposed intervention.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes, this project will ensure that protection of beneficiaries' rights, freedom, and legitimate interests are legally and fully recognized. The national policy encourages establishment of effective interaction among beneficiaries (Internally displaced persons - IDPs), host families and returnees, state authorities and bodies of the local Government) to achieve social cohesion. UNKEA shall periodically profile progress of the project intervention to ensure the right to information for the beneficiary communities is unhindered. This shall include project finance and program reports for the period compiled. The project will be delivered through a rights-based-approach. Beneficiary entitlements shall be communicated at the onset of each intervention. This will accord the local structures of oversight to hold the project management accountable in every project commitment in each county.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

During the design of this project, emphasis were put in place in consideration to involvement of the displaced-affected communities in all stages of project implementation. They will be part of the project steering committees that play a significant role in beneficiary targeting, community mobilization and sensitization, local procurements, distribution of cash and livelihoods inputs, feedback mechanism. In addition, local recruitment of project staffs is done with direct input and supervision of the affected population. Authenticity of documentation for each of the above categories of activities is only considered complete with endorsement of local authorities - as a mandatory internal requirement. In all her interventions, UNKEA will establish a payam level multi-sectoral feedback committee to oversee quality of deliverables, adherance to protection provisions, incorporation of community responses to project at different stages, and evaluating deliberables against beneficiary expectations. These processes have been on-going in 2019 in Nasir, Ulang, and Longechuk. In 2020, we anticipate rolling the same approach in Yei, Morobo, Tonj North, and Pochalla counties in order consolidate gains from the limited resource provisions.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

UNKEA has a strong relationship with other implementing partners in all locations of Ulang, Nasir, Yei, Morobo and Tonj North in the areas of Health, Nutrition and protection. This relations has provided opportunity to engage with government/and or local authority relevant actors and deparments to provide oversight and strengthen institutional coordination for WASH and related sectors of health and nutrition. The project will deliberated provide hands-on technical support to county health department - specifically on the component of public health and community outreach. In other (already) on-going but related initiatives, UNKEA has commenced these engagements in Nasir and Ulang through the health and WASH programing. This proposal will provide a scale up effort in all locations and initiate roll out to other counties proposed for this intervention.The modality of project execution will involve capacity building to water user committees, pump mechanics, protocal for community outreach and sensitisation, and technical support on management of water quality that can be sustained over the long haul. These initiatives have the potential to outlast the project duration and consolidate gains achieved during project execution. The county level coordination for this project will be under the county health department - and direct support of the county public health and WASH officers. Knowledge and experience gained by the officers during the project implementation will provide the much needed impetus to sustain technical support to WASH actors in the county.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is aligned with national objective ( South Sudan National Vision 2040) of provision/access of safe and clean water to all which include of Host community, Internal displaced Person, returnees and Refuges (women, men, girls and boys) and other most venerable communities (Person with disability), protecting the righter of freedom, right of participation during the project implementation stages. UNKEA will work closely with the local authority, community leader, representatives of person with disability, representatives of women and representative of youths as stated in South Sudan National Social Protection Policies. Using the principle of productive support as enshrined in the South Sudan National Social Protection plan, the project blends meeting the needs of vulnerable persons while increasing their productive potentials to promote resilience. UNKEA has factored tailored training to youth as hygiene promoters, water pump mechanics, and grass roots structures. It is anticipated that the training and mentorship support sustains individual and collective productive - an objective espoused in the national social projection policy.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

While male youth and adults face particular protection risks and psychosocial harm related to the armed conflict and insecurity, women, children, people with disabilities and older people bear most of the impacts of the crisis due to their particular vulnerabilities to conflict and other shocks. UNKEA will ensure that safety of its staff and beneficiaries is taken care of during the project implementation period. In scenario where inter-communal violence/ conflict occur UNKEA will work together with local authorities in facilitation of reallocation of project staff and the most vulnerable beneficiaries (PLWD). For beneficiaries to have a better understanding and feel the ownership of the project, all the local staff for this project will be recruited from IDPs, Returnees and Host Communities in the targeted location. This will also ease any misunderstanding which may occurred between the project implementer and the beneficiaries. UNKEA will also work together with all stakeholders; local authorities, Church leaders and women group leaders in order to help in managing any risk which may occur at any level.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

For an effective sustainability, UNKEA will work together with other partners on ground such as Nutrition, Health and local authority/community leaders using an integrated approach. The integrated approach provides more avenues for limited sectoral resources to satisfy the needs of people in need. WASH in health and nutrition provides added opportunities for gains to be sustained through secodary actors supporting nutrition and health service delivery. The local-based trained water management committees, water pump mechanics, WASH volunteers, and hygiene promoters are opportunities to sustain gains in the project since their presence and support extends to cover post project period. The direct involement and and engagement with county health departments to play a key coordination role in each county is one critical step to ensure continuity of oversight and technical backstopping. The project will provide simplified WASH tools that can be intergrated with other CHD monitoring tools for nutrition and WASH in the county. This will strengthen technical capacities to sustain on-ground efforts and community momentum. Where it becomes untennable to sustain operations in a given area, UNKEA will identify local actors that have demonstrated capacity to maintain existing support and continue services that the project delivers.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

UNKEA will strive to engage communities (men, women, boys and girls) and the most venerable like person with disabilities through effective, participation, inclusive and consistent communication by establishing mechanisms and committees to ensure that the affected persons the type, delivery, and quality of services they receive and are fully represented with the committees. The proposed mechanisms will include; conducting project inception meetings to clearly articulate the goals and objectives of the projects, implementing robust feedback mechanisms through monthly coordination meetings, and quarterly dialogue meetings with the communities, establishing information contact persons both within the projects and in the communities. The monthly and quarterly dialogue meetings will provide an opportunity for the beneficiaries to provide feedback on how the activities have been implemented and how well they are addressing their needs including comments, suggestions, and complaints. The project will provide feedback both verbally and in writing. Key information will be posted at the UNKEA offices and where possible local authority offices. However, the communities will propose their preferred medium of communication.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will adopt plural communication channels that will include low technology and non-technology options to cater for both sensitive and non-sensitive feedback. The Feedback and complaints mechanisms will be based on community preferences and cultural norms to ensure that they are accessible, safe and easy to use, and will include paper-based systems (suggestion boxes, notice boards), interpersonal communication through trusted liaison persons, and anonymous calls using the telephone/thuraya and or email where possible. In addition, the project will conduct regular systemic assessments of appropriate channels of feedback as guided by community preferences. Regular feedback/ complaint mechanisms will be incorporated through the project life span; at the Nutrition, health facility, Markets suggestion boxes and client exit interviews will be used; in the community, quarterly dialogue meetings will be conducted targeting various community members. The project in collaboration with the beneficiaries will identify trusted contact persons in the community to act as liaison people. The contact persons will include both men and women and these will be linked with the project contact person both in the field and also at the organization headquarters. All complaints will be investigated in a systematic method ensuring that the complainants are protected and feel safe at all times.
