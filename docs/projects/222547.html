Project Summary

This appeal aims to support 40,032 (6,672 h/h) people in vulnerable communities with humanitarian food assistance, Emergency livelihood support and resilience sustenance in Akobo, Nyirol and Pigi Counties of Jonglei with an objective of preventing famine, improve food consumption, dietary diversity and improve coping strategies for the target beneficiaries in IPC 5, IPC 4 and IPC 3 locations. On the other hand, the project intends to enhance emergency food production while contributing to the reduction and dependency on food and agricultural inputs. CIDO strategies will be married with a combination of interventions that resonates to the following cluster objectives: CO1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 4 and 5), CO2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing, and livestock) CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks & contextual changes
In realization of the freezing funding space vis-à-vis the need for impactful response, CIDO plans a complementarity interventions with its existing projects such as WASH, health, nutrition, shelter & NFI and protection from different partners within the targeted locations, while maintaining coordination and complementing with the efforts of other humanitarian actors in the respective line ministries. To reach the target beneficiaries with the prioritized cluster objectives, CIDO seeks to raise a total of $300,000 for the 2025 appeal
The project seeks to address food insecurity, malnutrition and other basic needs driven by multifaceted factors, conflict, climatic and economic shocks. The project is aligned to the South Sudan Humanitarian Needs and Response Plan (HNRP) objectives and the Food Security and livelihood Cluster Objective 1, 2 and 3. It will reach the most vulnerable particularly the children, pregnant and lactating women, people with disability, elderly, the chronical ill, female headed households drawn from the internally displaced person, returnees, refugees and the host communities. This will target the labor endowed households in both IPC 3 and 4 locations to enable households to meet the food needs and other basic needs whilst creating assets that promote food production, community infrastructure that improve access to basic services, assets that reduce disasters and are climate resilient

1. Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan remains dire, characterized by a protracted humanitarian crisis, widespread displacement, food insecurity, and limited access to basic services, including healthcare (United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs [OCHA], 2023). The country has experienced multiple emergencies, including disease outbreaks, floods, and conflict-related displacement. In 2024, an estimated 9 million people need humanitarian assistance. As per the IPC analysis of October 2023, approximately 7.1 million people of the total population of 12.4 million will face acute food insecurity at crisis levels or worse during the peak of the lean season from April to July 2024, necessitating humanitarian food assistance. The Sudan crisis, an ongoing influx of returnees as a result of suspension of humanitarian aid in Gambella Refugee camps in Ethiopia, intercomunal violence, food insecurity, and the severe impact of climate change present enormous challenges. Akobo, Pigi and Nyirol are among the counities in Jonglei with dire food insecurity situation being driven by the cumulative effects of climate shocks, particularly floods and dry spells, poor agriculture infrastructure, conflict and economic downturns. Their effect in agriculture production and productivity, disruption of livelihoods and hampered humanitarian access already puts the communities in limbo. This is exacerbated by a continued currency depreciation and the price inflation of food and other essential commodities, mainly due to the exacerbation of food prices as a result of COVID-19 and the war experienced in Ukraine and Gaza which has affected markets and caused dwindling the funding scope across the globe. According to the latest Integrated Food Security Phase analysis, severe food insecurity is affecting over half of the population. Food insecurity and malnutrition levels are high, with 6.3million people facing acute hunger and malnutrition-IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse) (SSD IPC report, November 2024). An estimated 2.1 million children aged 6-59 months suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025, including 650,000 cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM).Malnutrition rates are high among children and women due to limited access to healthy diets, poor water and hygiene practices, GBV concerns, and poor child feeding and caring practices. Agricultural production is hindered by flooding, dry spells, poor agricultural practices, lack of improved climate resilient crops or vegetable varieties and mechanized farming and post-harvest technology. Gender disparity/inequality and protection risks: Conflict and climate shocks have exacerbated exploitation, abuse, sexual violence and gender-based violence. The drivers have increased vulnerabilities and reduced access to essential goods and services in the proposed response locations, diminishing its most vulnerable people’s resilience over food or an income to support themselves and their families. Intra-domestic tensions are also increasing, heightening the risk of intimate partner violence. Adolescent girls are facing increased risks of child marriage. To address the above situation, CIDO proposes to intensify emergency food production interventions by distributing emergency livelihood kits, build there salience of at-risk populations through support to animal health, a key component of food security, and establish VSLA groups, establish climate-sensitive agriculture technology to increase food production and incomes at the household level in the proposed project locations, in line with humanitarian needs driven by conflict, violence, climate shocks or food insecurity

2. Project activities

CIDO aligns its implementation to FSL cluster objectives (CO1), CO2 and CO3 to contribute to the HRP strategic objectives and activities as follows:-1. Provide unconditional food assistance to 40,032 population in IPC 4 & 5 in the targeted counties 2. Provide Conditional food assistance/ Asset creation and livelihood to population in IPC 3 and above in the targeted counties 3. Provision of nutrition education and sensitization messages to increase knowledge on balance diet 4. Conduct sensitization session on proper utilization of CASH provided conditionally 5. Mobilization, sensitization and registration of 6,672 households Vulnerable IDPs and Host Community equivalent to 40,032 individuals 6. Distribution of quality main season crop, fishing and vegetable kits to 40,032 people which corresponds to 6,672 households in Akobo, Nyirol and Pigi counties 7. Distribution of quality dry season fishing and vegetable kits to 40,032 people which corresponds to 6,672 households in Akobo, Nyirol and Pigi counties8. Vaccinate/treat 30,000 livestock within the project locations 8. Link up with FAO to provide vegetable kits 9. Support in the establishment of 12 kitchen garden sites at the nutrition centers and schools through the support of MtM group members 10. Capacity building training of Train of Trainers on improved crop vegetable targeting 1061 individuals 60% women and 40% men11. Capacity building of 650 fisheries 60% women and 40% through training to 12. Train of 800 youth and women on Income generating activities (IGA) focusing on beekeeping, Pastry and bead making to improve their livelihood 13. Provide business start-up kits to the trained 800 youth and Women within the response catchment area14. Establish and train 1 VSLA groups in each targeted county 15. Training of 280 people on fuel efficient stove production16. Distribute trees fruit to 1000 individuals to improve climate change
