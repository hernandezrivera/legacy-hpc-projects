Project Summary

The project will build the capacity of community governance structures as WASH committees, train and equip committees/communities to continue to operate and maintain WASH infrastructure, conduct water chlorination, and monitor chlorine levels. Also will conduct training to strengthen multi-sectoral approaches such as WASH in Health Facilities , WASH in schools, and WASH/GBV. NPO will preposition supplies for disasters like floods, epidemics, and other conflict-related displacements. Cash transfer modalities will be explored and piloted to cut logistical costs of transporting supplies in collaboration with other sectors according to agreed point via WASH sector.
The project will intervene with health lifesaving interventions for outbreak control of cholera, typhoid, hepatitis E, COVID19, and other vector control in communities, this is due to more than 35% of the health facilities do not have sanitation facilities in place, so the project will provide technical support and carry out health interventions prioritizing common diseases as cholera treatment centers and COVID-19 isolation centers upon specific demands and investigations.
The project will integrate all three components of WASH to deliver a complete response in IDPs and returnees in Elfashir rural, Tawilla and Melit localities in addition to settlement population and new arrival refugees in Gadaref . WASH interventions will be implemented for those affected by risk of epidemics, and floods. All will contribute to curbing the morbidity and mortality amongst the targeted population.
Project will provide WASH services in the target localities to organize water existing sources management, rehabilitate some water points, constriction for new water yards, provide water containers with avail weekly water by tankers at schools and public places. Also the project will conduct hygiene promotion for communities focusing on women & children and will provide public latrines.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

 The project will put emphasis on local ownership of the planning process, bringing groups of stakeholders together in a process aimed at strengthening mutual trust and respect. Women and men from the area will be closely involved in the design and review of activities, as they have differing needs and preferences that need to be taken into account. Although many children are responsible for fetching water, they will be specifically targeted with activities that promote proper use of the water points - hand pumps - and maintaining cleanliness and hygiene for water points and water containers.  Strengthen of existing contacts with locality administrators, technical experts in line departments and traditional leaders, and also the project will engage the government at state and locality level regarding the development and adoption of relevant policies related to water for sanitation and hygiene. NPO look at supporting the local private sector to make relevant items (soap, brushes, sanitation materials, materials for household water treatment) and skills for installation available as close as possible to where people live.  Important also for sustainability for sanitation promotion, NPO will begin with small steps towards the sanitation ladder to lead to full sanitation, that will make a meaningful difference in people’s lives. The project will promote affordable and desirable latrine designs which are durable and easy to clean and maintain.  To ensure environmental sustainability, the project will pay explicit attention to good practice in design and sitting of latrines relative to groundwater table, flood levels, and water points, and NPO will explore the applicability of Water Safety Plans - WSP.The project considers girls' and women's security and safety. Their engagement will be critical while identifying water points and new water sources to save them travel too far to unsecured places to fetch water. Sector partners will ensure that the communal and institutional latrines are gender-segregated, safe with latches on the doors, and easy to access secured places.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Water collection in North Darfur especially IDPs and returnees communities is women and girls responsibility, reducing time available for other activities. The situation is compounded by the fact that women often have little decision making power and influence in communities. It is critical that the project responds to the differing needs and rights of women and girls. We will do this by promoting their meaningful participation in planning, implementation and monitoring, and also in leadership roles. To ensure women’s involvement in these male dominated spaces, they will receive additional support, as negotiation skills, and public speaking, and men will be sensitized on the value of women’s involvement to act on their concerns. Where hygiene promotion is concerned, the activities will be made gender-specific: we found that in general, women practice better hygiene than men (particularly where defecation is concerned), but also that women and adolescent girls have specific needs regarding menstrual hygiene, hygiene when looking after children, and protection from gender-based violence. The reality is that in some areas, promoting women’s involvement will be difficult, because of strict cultural norms on gender segregation, keeping women out of the public . We will use several strategies to boost women’s involvement as much as possible in the local contexts, such as: additional work with male-dominated water committees to ensure they take the needs and rights of women into account; promoting community dialogue about women’s role. We will carry out a gender analysis for each targeted locality and will engage with communities on issues that emerge from the analysis.For the programme as a whole, a gender equality inclusion strategy will be drawn up, which all partners will agree to, stipulating how the programme will be implemented to promote gender equality. Sex and age disaggregated data will be collected throughout implementation to allow NPO to understand and adapt the programme to ensure best possible impact for women and girls. An analysis of the effect of the project on reducing gender inequality will be included in the project reports. We realize that most traditional leadership and local authorities are male, there is a clear risk of a male bias slipping into the project. Because of this, we will do our best to involve as many female staff as possible, and to have gender actively on the agenda in our internal review processes.
