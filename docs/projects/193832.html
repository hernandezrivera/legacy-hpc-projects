Project Summary

Action Against Hunger in partnership with UAWC (Union of Agricultural Work Committees),
will combine their technical experience and well-established presence in local
communities to provide livelihood support to vulnerable households across 3
governorates of the Gaza Strip (North Gaza, Gaza and Middle Area).
The intervention aims to support rural and vulnerable households, enabling them to cope
with the severe effects of the protracted conflict, including the most recent economic
impact brought by the pandemic and Ukraine crisis, by strengthening and protecting their
livelihoods and improving their food security levels. This intervention was developed to
contribute to the achievement of HRP Strategic Objective 3 (SO3) “The capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with and overcome protracted crisis, including from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued”; and in alignment with two of the HRP 2023 Food Security Sector (FSS) objectives: (O1) Livelihoods of vulnerable households are protected, and their resilience, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced; (O2) Households suffering from limited of access to food and to income opportunities can meet their basic food needs; and is aligned to the strategic priorities of the Ministry of Agriculture.
AAH and UAWC intend to support and restore the rural livelihoods of 952vulnerable
individuals whose income relies on agricultural and breeding activities. These individuals
and their livelihoods are threatened by the rapidly degrading economic context in Gaza
Strip which has compromised their capacity to access, repair and maintain their key
productive assets. Furthermore, the project proposes the rehabilitation of key
infrastructures to guarantee access to water for farmers, such as wells and ponds, and
equip them with solar systems to reduce the costs of energy and avoid disruption in the
irrigation processes. In addition, the action will support 560 individuals, characterized by
high socioeconomic vulnerabilities and lack of access to economic resources, by enrolling
them in a cash for work program that will allow them to meet the basic needs. The target criteria in the different activities will seek to prioritize female headed HH, women involved in the production processes and youths as much as possible to empower them in fulfilling their critical role in rural livelihoods.
The intervention foresees a combined approach of cash and in-kind support, to guarantee
both self-reliance of the recipients and quality of the implementation. In-kind modality will
be adopted for the rehabilitation of assets, such as water wells, ponds, animals’ shelters;
while conditional cash will be provided to access production inputs and equipment.

The proposed intervention ensures protection mainstreaming as it is guided by protection principles and considers safety and dignity; meaningful access; accountability to affected population (AAP) as well as participation and empowerment.

1 - Needs

The living conditions of the population in Gaza) are increasingly deteriorating after 15 years of Israeli blockade, aggravated by one of the world’s highest unemployment and deep poverty rates, with already 53% of people living below the poverty line (HNO 2023). The current crisis is tied to the protracted conflict and political instability at national and international levels. In fact, the population is still suffering from the consequences of the May 2021 and August 2022 escalations and the economic impact of the pandemic and the Ukraine crisis, which has led to a sharp increase in prices of basic commodities, remarkably affecting households’ purchasing power and reduced income (57.3% of households reported a reduction in the past year according to the MSNA). The described situation is ultimately preventing households from meeting basic needs (81% of people reported not to be able to meet their basic needs according to MSNA) and further eroding livelihoods, while 63% of households assessed in Gaza experience moderate to severe food insecurity. Additionally, in Gaza, 76% of households reported being worried about having enough food to eat in the 30 days prior to data collection. This inevitably forces the population to resort to negative coping mechanisms; in fact according to the MSNA, 56% of people in Gaza are adopting negative coping strategy classified as “crisis”, while 12% as “Emergency”, thus consistently raising protection risks. The protracted crisis, aggravated by the recent described events and the negative effects of climate change, have harshly impacted the agricultural sector and generated a decline in the production capacity of farmers and producers, particularly on vulnerable small-scale farmers and breeders with extremely limited coping capacities. According to a damage assessment conducted by the MoA after the recent escalation in August 2022, agricultural lands, greenhouses, livestock, and poultry farms were severely impacted, causing damages and losses estimated at $1m. In terms of livestock and crop production, the report recorded the highest losses in Gaza, Middle area and North governorates. In general, the high production costs and the accumulated debts threaten farmers’ access to agricultural materials, irrigation networks, fertilizers, seedlings, fodder and other inputs. In addition, farmers must deal with water and energy shortages, high water salinity level, severe weather conditions, as well as importing and exporting restrictions imposed by the blockade, further affecting the profitability and sustainability of agricultural activities. An assessment carried out by AAH in April 2022 among greenhouses farmers, revealed that 92% of the respondents have a partially damaged greenhouse, 79% are heavily affected by high production costs and that the lack of cash liquidity prevents their capacity to rehabilitate and cultivate their greenhouses. Moreover, 96% of farmers report electricity shortages as one of the main challenges in crop irrigation and 93% of all farmers confirmed that the water salinity negatively impacts their agriculture production. According to the findings of FGDs conducted by UAWC in July 2022, Majority of the farmers involved in the discussion agreed that the most urgent intervention to meet the described needs and challenges are to improve access to water through wells and ponds rehabilitation, provision of solar energy to guarantee no disruption in irrigation processes, and provision of cash assistance as a preferred modality to access the needed production inputs. Similarly, from a recent assessment carried out by UAWC, sheep breeders reported a limited capacity to access veterinary services and high-quality fodders; this is ultimately compromising the health status of the animals, leading in some cases in malnutrition. Additionally, the quality and materials used by the shelters also affects health, causing diseased and losses. In general, the increased negative trends in rural livelihoods, especially agriculture, are also impacting vulnerable categories involved in agricultural activities. For instance, daily workers have already demonstrated low resilience to shocks. Many of them were deprived of work in 2022 as a coping strategy employed by farmers to mitigate the high cost of production inputs. This is contributing to the unemployment crisis in Gaza, especially among youth. According to PCBS, Gaza unemployment rate in the second quarter of 2022 was 44%, with youth unemployment at 59%. The MSNA recorded 60.3% of the interviewed households having at least on adult unemployed and seeking work. The target criteria in the different activities will seek to prioritize female headed households, women involved in the production processes and youths as much as possible to empower them in fulfilling their critical role in rural livelihoods.

2 - Activities

Building on previous experiences, the proposed intervention answers to the needs identified within the oPT HNO 2023, SO3 The capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with and overcome protracted crisis, including from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued; and aligned with the FSS strategic objectives SO1 and SO2, as well as the damage assessment conducted by MoA. The selection of the target groups will be done through announcements in coordination with the FSS, its partner, MoA and MoSD, to avoid duplication of assistance. Information sessions will be provided in each targeted community to inform about the intervention activities and the available communication channels to reach AAH and UAWC for feedback and complaints. The proposed activities will target vulnerable households who rely on rural livelihood as the main source of income in North Gaza, Gaza and the Middle area governorates, with a special focus on women and youths. AAH and UAWC will ensure adequate gender and youth mainstreaming through women consultation prior to the implementation to ensure gender and youth sensitivity is applied throughout implementation and monitoring.Coordination was conducted with the food security cluster and MoA to identify targets and the priorities. This coordination will be continued throughout the implementation to ensure that there is no duplication with other responses. The different rehabilitation activities will be supervised and closely followed by technical experts in both UAWC and AAH as relevant, namely engineer and agronomist, to ensure appropriateness and quality of the implementation of the activity. Rehabilitation of 5 Agricultural Wells; This activity aims at rehabilitating partially damaged agricultural wells (20-30 m3/hr capacity) in North, Gaza and Middle area governorates and provide them with a solar energy system (10 kw per each well) for a cost of approximately 3,300 USD for the PV system. The target criteria will seek to prioritize female headed households and youths as much as possible. This activity will contribute to recovering water access and improving the productivity of at surrounding agricultural lands, while the solar energy systems will provide farmers with the ability to cope with the electricity shortage crisis, decrease energy costs and maintain the running of the water pump. Rehabilitation of 50 Water Harvesting Ponds; 50 small scale farmers who face difficulties to cultivate their lands and greenhouses, due to lack of access of electricity for operating irrigation pumps and the water salinity, will be supported by rehabilitating their water harvesting ponds (average size: 140 m3) and installing solar energy units (2.5 kw per each pond) to their irrigation pumps in North, Gaza and Middle area governorates. The cost for each PV system allocated to the ponds will be approximately 3,300 USD. The target criteria will seek to prioritize female headed households and youths as much as possible .This activity aims to boost the productivity of the agricultural lands through improving the availability and the quality of water for irrigating a wider variety of crops that cannot be irrigated with highly saline groundwater, address the electricity shortage crisis, and reduce the operational cost though providing substantial increase of water availability with lower energy costs. Rehabilitation of 100 greenhouses and provision of Agricultural inputs in North, Gaza and Middle area governorates. The target criteria will seek to prioritize female headed HH and youths as much as possible .100 vulnerable farming households will receive livelihood support through the provision of Cash assistance for: 1) rehabilitation of their damaged greenhouses (1); 2) purchase of agricultural inputs such as seedlings irrigation networks and fertilizers. The cash transfers will be delivered to the beneficiaries through the Bank of Palestine in two instalments; The first instalment of 50% of the total amount will be delivered to the beneficiaries upon signing the beneficiary agreements and agreeing on the needed inputs. The second instalment is a conditional support, it will be delivered to the beneficiaries upon purchasing the rehabilitation materials and the agricultural inputs. The total transfer value will be of 1,300 USD approximately. The technical teams of AAH will conduct monitoring and follow up field visits to ensure proper use of the cash transfers. Quality of procured inputs and the rehabilitation process will be monitored by AAH’s and UAWC technical staff, along with the timing to respect the agricultural calendar and continuity of productive cycleRehabilitation of 40 Sheep shelters in North, Gaza and Middle area governorates; This activity will target 40 livestock breeders. The target criteria will seek to prioritize female headed households and youths as much as possiblewho are experiencing low productivity and losses due to the current crises, the poor environmental conditions of their farms and their limited financial capacity to access fodder, production inputs and veterinary services. The targeted breeders will be supported through a mixed cash/in-kind modality with a transfer of 800 USD approximately. The cash will be disbursed to purchase production inputs, such as feeders, waterers, water tanks and fodder; while in-kind assistance will be provided to rehabilitate their shelters. The total value of both assistance modalities for each shelter is approximately 1,500 USD, according to the needs. Rehabilitation of 30 Poultry farming units in North, Gaza and Middle area governorates; This activity will target 30 vulnerable poultry breeders facing challenges including high cost of production inputs and shortage of electricity needed for lighting and heating. 3 - Indicators

The MEAL unit will establish the MEAL plan and be responsible for the data quality of MEAL activities throughout the intervention. The M&E framework will measure key indicators defined within the Logical Framework at output and outcome level. The MEAL system will foster learning and the generation of timely and relevant information to adjust project implementation. The MEAL team will conduct frequent monitoring of all activities to ensure the harmonized use of monitoring tools, confirm record keeping and maximize program quality. The Project Tracking Tool that will be developed for the intervention will allow the MEAL team and technical team to conduct regular progress review meetings allowing to capture indicators progress and learnings due to the complexity of social change and the dynamic context in the Gaza Strip. AAH will be responsible for developing and enhancing the capacity of the local partner and AAH staff on evidence-based programming, MEAL and data quality and information management. The MEAL framework will be gender sensitive, as data will provide relevant gender-disaggregated and differentiated information that will capture women and men’s different experiences and perceptions of risks, shocks and stresses, and their different resilience capacities, accounting for other intersecting factors such as age or location. Accountability will be ensured through AAH’s Feedback and Complaint Mechanism, managed by the MEAL department through which formally received feedback and complaints from right holders and other key stakeholders (including partners, donors, etc.) will be registered, investigated and responded to within the identified timeframe. The following will be the key MEAL activities to allow the MEAL team to measure the success of the project: Baseline/endline survey with small scale farmers (either open-land or Green House), the survey will measure the pre-status of the farmers’ income, rCSI, production capacity and access to services. Baseline/endline survey with Livestock breeders, the survey will measure the pre-status of the livestock owners’ income, rCSI, production capacity and access to services. Focus group discussions with community members will be managed and facilitated by the MEAL team jointly with the technical team to allow for monitoring project progress and success through triangulation of data. Onsite monitoring visits will be implemented by both MEAL and project team aiming at monitoring quality of implementation (consists of interviews with BNFs and direct observations), to measure progress of feld work, monitoring feld risks and assumptions, and collect community feedback on risks. The MEAL data from all above activities will be systematically and timely analysed to identify potential improvements, gaps, and ways forward. Through joint follow up meetings and documentation of learning with UWAC, the project management team will be able to put in place mitigation measures and action plans to ensure proper delivery of project activity according to the plan. 9.1c - How will feedback be used?

The Feedback and Complaint Mechanism (FCM) is managed by the AAH’s MEAL unit. Following the FCM standard operating procedures it will be introduced to the targeted communities through information materials like brochures and information leafets inside the communities where FSL interventions are taking place, to provide communities and benefciaries with different channels to access AAH, prior, during and after implementation. The FCM will be active from the beginning of the project through an active hotline number managed by MEAL staff. Complaints, feedback or suggestions are recorded on a protected sheet, and MEAL staff uses the agreed FCM procedures to handle the complaint based on its level and sensitivity. AAH management and the program team jointly discuss the complaint (depending on its nature and sensitivity) and formulate responses on a case-by-case basis. On average, within 2 weeks the response usually is provided to the complainant or community members by contacting him/her back through phone or emails. If the response includes actions these will be considered by the program team, they will fll in the learning log sheet with actions planned to allow the MEAL team to follow up the execution of these actions. On a semi-annual basis, a FCM report will be developed and shared with AAH management and program team to document learning captured from FCM fndings, suggestions and feedback

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

AAH considers safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability as well as participation and empowerment of paramount importance especially in a project that combines cash assistance and case management activities. AAH has in place mechanisms to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse by all staff. All AAH staff have the obligation to adhere to the organisation code of conduct. AAH has in place focal points at base and mission levels, as well as safe and confidential ways to report abuse. Moreover, a majority of AAH staff have received protection cluster facilitated trainings with the aim to know how to detect and refer potential cases of GBV and other protection concerns. As part of AAH accountability to affected populations, a feedback mechanism emphasizing fluid and transparent communication will be in place. More specifically for the project activities, AAH will lead a risk analysis while confirming the mechanism used to transfer cash to ensure safety and do no harm for targeted beneficiaries while commuting to the distribution place and while at it. AAH will also ensure confidentiality of beneficiaries to avoid stigma and possible pressure on the household if the surrounding is aware of a transfer. In its monitoring, AAH will ask about households’ dynamics, and pay specially attention to any tension arising after or because of the transfers to monitor do no harm. Questions about safety and dignity will also be tackled during the PDM and end line surveys. Change to the implementation modality will be made accordingly within available possibilities and current movement restrictions, while applying preventive measures against covid-19. AAH AND UAWC , have core principles to ensure safety and do no harm considering the sensitivity of the work and activities they implement. Experienced case workers will ensure households members are at ease and services at tailored to their specific situation and the risks they might be exposed to. Women field staff and social workers will be communicating with female beneficiaries to ensure a safe and trustful exchange for the beneficiary. Strict confidentiality of all personal information will be ensured to protect beneficiaries through the data protection form explained to each beneficiary ensure safe handling of personal data.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

AAH upholds the humanitarian principles of Humanity, Impartiality, Neutrality, and Independence. During the course of the project, AAH will ensure all projects are easily accessible to those most at need, avoiding risks to the beneficiaries. AAH is committed to operate in an ethical manner and on a programmatic risk mitigation strategy for risk of fraud, waste, and abuse within the proposed activities. At a global level, AAH relies on the organizational Policy on Anti-Bribery, Corruption and Abuse of Power and Code of Conduct. Organizational whistleblowing mechanisms and procedure are in place, `ZERO TOLERANCE towards fraud, bribery or corruption is applied. Reporting mechanisms have also been implemented to report these behaviors to headquarters. Ethical Risks related to any kind of abuse (sexual, power, etc...) are mitigated by providing compulsory briefing to each new staff member on ethical policies. AAH Code of Conduct is aligned with IASC principles. AAH has a Policy for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and in 2019, AAH conducted in its Middle East country offices a Protection Mainstreaming Internal Evaluation for which the consultant spent March in oPt. Particular attention was paid to the way AAH includes vulnerable groups in its work, for instance through inclusive participatory assessments. As one of AAH’s core values, AAH thrives to prevent discrimination and to ensure equal and impartial access though transparency and accountability to affected populations. AAH aims for all community members to be involved and consulted as much as possible in project design and implementation, as well as evaluation, including through household and community consultations and focus group discussions, accessible to all. In order for their participation to be meaningful, AAH focuses on ‘qualitative inclusion’ meaning safe spaces for benefciaries to participate. AAH is reporting under the HRP disaggregated data by sex and age; though pending the reporting format, AAH usually includes further disaggregation on vulnerability such as data on people with disabilities. Random checks will be carried out by the MEAL team members to ensure transparency, neutrality and objectivity and spot potential bias or incongruency.
