Needs

Access to education continues to be inadequate, with only 30 % of children accessing learning opportunities countrywide, and 3 million children still out of school, with the majority of affected children in Southern and Central Somalia. In addition, only 17 % of children living in rural areas or in IDP settlements are enrolled in primary schools while education attainment is higher in urban areas. A number of cluster assessments in 2017 (Education Cluster, Multi-Cluster Assessments and Baseline Survey) have identified many educational gaps and needs. These gaps and needs are due to lack of adequate learning facilities, teachers, basic teaching and learning materials amongst local communities and IDP displaced children as well as increasing returnee children. 90 % of schools do not have access to safe drinking water and 61 % of schools do not have functional latrines across Central and Southern Somalia.Returnee children coming from different neighboring countries with different education curricula, compared to those non-standard curricula used in Somalia (differing both between and within the three “zones”), face challenges in terms of adapting to the system of education. The schools where returnee students enroll, in particular in areas of South Central Somalia, lack the necessary teaching and learning materials.There is uncertainty felt by returnee parents and schools concerning continuity of education after the end of the one-year education grants supported by UNHCR. There are limited intermediate/ secondary education opportunities for IDPs and returnees. Tradition, culture and religion make it difficult to reach the grass-root level, and females are constrained from expressing their ideas and needs. Education providers at schools are also under constant pressure due to limited school infrastructure, inadequate learning equipment, laboratory and other furniture, which creates an additional constraint for returning children to have access to education.

Activities

UNHCR will assist returning refugees mainly from Kenya, Yemen, Eritrea and Djibouti in accessing education. UNHCR will also provide support to schools to increase their absorption capacity including provision of scholastic materials, teaching and learning materials in the areas of return across Somalia. It is key for UNHCR together with the Ministry of Education, to integrate returnee children into the school system in order to prevent the continuation of NGO-managed parallel systems. As part of this, UNHCR will support language classes, where needed, and preparation for curriculum transition to support a gradual transition into national school. This approach will also ensure that the beneficiaries will have access to documentation and certification. UNHCR will continue to provide educational support and scholastic materials to returnees in the first year of return as part of the enhanced return package, to facilitate reintegration into the school system, as well as to IDPs and some local pupils. UNHCR will continue to distribute scholastic materials (books, uniforms, stationery, etc.) materials bi-annually in form of conditional and restricted cash/vouchers of $12 each, per school going child. Teaching aids and equipment will also be provided. UNHCR will work closely with the Ministry of Education and the Education Cluster to ensure adopting a local area based approach, where the emphasis is put on supporting existing systems to increase capacities, with the objective of phasing out separate humanitarian support to returnees and integrating them fully into the national education system on par with other nationals.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Voluntary returns are on-going despite the conflict, violence, insecurity, absence of functioning and effective institutions, impunity, and lack of infrastructure in Somalia. While there is increased effort to promote sustainable return and re-integration, a substantial number of returnees have limited access to basic services and are constantly exposed to protection risks. Lack of proper legal, psychosocial and physical support also remain a challenge in most parts of the favourable return areas. Many returnees choose not to return to their areas of origin, preferring to settle in urban areas. Many of these already face pressures on basic services, further compounded by recurrent influxes of IDPs. Support to enrollment of returnees and IDPs allows them to integrate in the local community and counters exclusion. The support to IDPs and children of the host communities promotes better social cohesion and counters exclusion.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

UNHCR applies vulnerability criteria for the selection of beneficiaries of all its activities, which takes into account specific needs that are related to gender and age. Application of the vulnerability criteria ensures an equitable approach, while also addressing specific risks of exclusion. Recognizing the particular challenges for girl pupils, who have high levels of absenteeism and drop-out rates due to security concerns and menstrual hygiene challenges, UNHCR will provide, sanitary kits to girl pupils to encourage girl-child education and enhancing retention and completion of schooling.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Returnees and IDPs are affected in line with the general population by the ongoing insecurity and fragile political environment in Somalia. The insecurity results in restrictions in humanitarian access and restraints for persons of concern to move freely and in safety, further exacerbated by inter-clan conflicts. Threats targeted at beneficiary communities often force humanitarian organizations to postpone the implementation of affected projects until they receive security assurances for their staff and the beneficiary community. Due to practical considerations related to humanitarian access, concrete reintegration support and assistance can only be provided in areas of the country with relative stability and where UNHCR and partners are currently present and/or can establish operations.
