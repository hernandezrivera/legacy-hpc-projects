Project Summary

Somalia is currently grappling with multiple shocks that exacerbate existing acute food insecurity and acute malnutrition. Desert Locust affected rural livelihoods in Northern and Central regions and presents a risk (serious) to the South. The 2020 Gu seasonal flooding (river flooding) contributed to loss and damage to property, housing, farmland, and infrastructure. The riverine areas of Hiran, Middle Shabelle, Lower Shabelle, Gedo, Middle Juba, and Lower Juba were some of the worst-affected areas. The COVID-19 pandemic and associated socio-economic impacts also contributed to the food insecurity situation in Somalia. Conflict/insecurity led to further population displacement and disruption to 2020 Gu season agriculture in some areas, including Lower Shabelle and Middle Juba.
Humanitarian food assistance needs are high across Somalia, where Stressed (IPC Phase 2) and Crisis (IPC Phase 3) outcomes remain widespread. An estimated 3.5 million people in rural livelihood zones, urban areas, and IDP settlements need food assistance. In July, food assistance reached only 55 percent of the population in need. The below-average 2020 gu cereal harvest, economic impacts of COVID-19, and forecast of below-average deyr rainfall from October to December 2020 drive a worsening food security outlook through at least early 2021. Furthermore, a long-term decline in rainfall performance and preliminary climatological research suggest an elevated likelihood of below-average rainfall in the March to May 2021 season. Two consecutive poor seasons would likely result in rapidly worsening acute food insecurity in 2021.
However, anticipated La Niña conditions are associated with an elevated likelihood of consecutive below-average rainfall seasons, and Somalia faces a high risk that drought conditions will develop by early 2021. Desert Locust could exacerbate crop and pasture losses. Mercy Corps field assessment in target regions reveals the deepening effect of COVID-19, floods and conflict have led to high food consumption gaps and loss of livelihood assets.
Against this background of fragility and vulnerability. This project aims to provide 1,100 most vulnerable HHs (IDPs &urban poor) with urgent life-saving interventions to improve HH's immediate access to food and livelihood support to mitigate risks of adoption of negative coping strategies as well as 80 youth at risk for skills development and job placement. The project is designed to advance towards the food security cluster 2021 response strategy and priorities and proposed interventions are based on the principles of the centrality of protection. The cash response interventions will be based on market assessments and guidance of the CWG and FSC recommendations. Mercy Corps will coordinate with other cash actors to leverage and avoid overlap in cash support.
Some of the most significant economic impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic include lower inflows of external remittances and lower livestock exports, which have implications for household income, business capital to import staple foods and pay employees, and Somali financial system liquidity. According to data collected by the World Bank from money transfer businesses on formal remittance flows, the USD value of remittances to businesses declined by 36 percent in April and 22 percent in May compared to April and May 2019, respectively. At the same time, the USD value of remittances to individuals declined by 15 percent in April 2019 compared to April 2020, though values recovered in May. Meanwhile, port authority data on formal livestock exports is beginning to show the impact of reduced international participation in the annual hajj, which took place in late July and early August. Although cumulative livestock exports from Bossasso and Berbera ports from January to June 2020 surged 50 percent higher than January to June 2019, livestock exports in July 2020 dropped to 60 percent below July 2020.

Project Activities

1.1: Provide unconditional (Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance) cash transfer to 1100 HHs to save lives and protect livelihood assets through Mobile Money Transfer. The use of electronic systems for registration and payment, coupled with rigorous beneficiary validation and monitoring tools to be instituted to minimize risks. Cash transfer value based on FSC recommended % of MEB. 1.2: Conduct Community awareness and engagement through the use of COVID-19 Information Education Materials recommended by WHO and MOH of Federal/State Government.A1.3: Creating job opportunities for 80 youth at risk (both Male and Female) in IDP camps through skills development.

Project Indicators

1.1.1: Total number of people registered and accessed mobile money transfer as a result of the MPCA 1.1.2: Total number of people assisted through multipurpose cash activities.1.2: Number of people directly receiving COVID-19 messaging (excluding mass media campaigns and without double-counting)1.2.1: Percentage of people targeted by the COVID-19 awareness who reported practicing the preventive measures1.3: Percentage increase in income for targeted youth through job placements (disaggregated by gender)

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash assistance enables beneficiaries to have choices for their food consumption as well as transparency in terms of disbursement as the transfer will involve the engagement of Hormuud telecom which verifies the telephone number against the individual beneficiary registered.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

The project supports the injection of cash into the local market as this will increase the local economy as well as the market functionality.
