Project Summary

The project aims at responding to the urgent needs of the communities affected by the anglophone crisis in the Southwest region of Cameroon, through :
Activities or outputs:
Water supply (in communities and health facilities):
Depending on assessments done at community level and following the recommendations of the WaSH Cluster in Buea, reconstruction/rehabilitation will be prioritized over the creation of new water points, as it can be more time consuming and potentially challenging from an administrative and acceptance point of view. This includes rehabilitating water points considered as abandoned, spring catchment construction or protection, or the upgrade of unprotected well or borehole.
WaSH non-food items + menstrual hygiene kit distribution:
MI/SI aims at distributing kits to newly arrived IDPs and returnees in the targeted health areas. The kits have been designed according to the list of minimum items designed at the level of the WaSH Cluster in the NWSW. They include soap, a jerry can, a bucket, a kettle, a plastic jar and a plastic cup. In coordination with other actors and after confirming potential gaps, MI/SI will also include menstrual hygiene items to the kits (underwear and reusable sanitary pads developed by a local company Kmerpad with whom SI is developing a broader partnership at country level).
Hygiene Promotion
The hygiene promotion will be based on the recommendations of WaSH’Em approach. Sensitization on good hygiene practices will be delivered by the 60 community health workers (CHW) supported by the project. Considering the tense humanitarian situation, MI/SI will prioritize household sessions over public ones, expecting to target all beneficiaries from the constructed and rehabilitated water points and from the WaSH kits distribution at least once.
Sanitation (latrines/showers and inclinators construction)
In addition to the water points to be constructed or rehabilitated in health facilities targeted by the project, MI/SI aims at rehabilitating/building segregated latrines/showers in health facilities as well as addressing infection prevention and control through waste management. Facilities will be equipped with a waste management unit, including one incinerator, one waste pit, one bin and latrines will be equipped with a hand-washing device. Hygiene protocols will also be reinforced and hygienist trained and equipped.

Besoins / Needs

The crisis has led to a significant population movements, with 450,268 internally displaced and 203,634 returned according to IOM’s latest estimate (October 2019). On the Kumba-Mbonge axis, that hosts the Kumba Health District targeted by the project, it is estimated that more than 120,000 are IDPs. The movements are in progress, with 5,938 having left their home in July 2019, primarily because of clashes with armed groups and the military, but also because they lack access to basic services. Many have moved to the surrounding bush to their places of origin, making them especially difficult to reach, and most have not yet shown any intention to return. Their needs range from farm tools to protection, with health, education and drinking water prioritized by most when asked about their top-3 priorities (67%, 52% and 49%, respectively). Food comes a close fourth at 47%. Access to safe-drinking water is particularly scarce as well: 32% of those surveyed by IOM drink water from rivers or streams and 24% from unprotected springs. Primary water access challenges include distance (48%), availability (36%), non-functioning water points (25%), poor water quality (20%) and danger (20%). Latrine accessibility remains an issue as well, mostly because available latrines are not segregated by sex, are unhygienic, or too crowded. Low access to drinking water and proper sanitation infrastructure worsens the risks of waterborne diseases that often cannot be treated as populations lack access to health facilities. According to IOM’s latest estimate, only 45% of health facilities are currently functional in the Southwest. The assessment conducted by SI’s partner (MI) has shown most of the functional health facilities lack water and sanitation infrastructures, such latrines for patients and incinerators for a proper medical waste management.
