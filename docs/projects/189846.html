1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

NRC’s FSL HRP project aims to improve FSL situation of conflict affected population by: firstly, supporting the increase in sustainable food production through distribution of inputs and the promotion of activities that reduce losses and maximise resource use efficiency which is instrumental in maximizing availability of food. Secondly, increasing households’ purchasing power and economic inclusion through provision of CVA, provision of cash for work opportunities amongst other activities dependant on needs assessments. NRC’s two-prong LFS strategy will help improve people's access to food through distribution of inputs and the promotion of activities that reduce food losses and maximise resource use efficiency. LFS programming will also aim to increasing households’ purchasing power through, CVA, provision of cash for work opportunities amongst others, with the overall aim of reducing HH use of emergency coping mechanisms. NRC will adjust its targeting strategy to the response phase in which it is intervening. In emergency settings, priority will be given to displaced populations in hard-to-reach areas, while in early recovery settings, priority will be given to families experiencing protracted displacement. In both settings, NRC will also target host communities supporting and sharing resources with IDPs. In return areas, NRC will not only target returnees, but also remainees, as their decision to stay is often associated with extreme vulnerability and lack of resources to flee. NRC will design its intervention paying particular attention to vulnerable and traditionally marginalised groups. Estimated 55% female – 45% male (all ages).

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

As mentioned above the key priority activities are split into two key categories, increase availability of food as well as purchasing power of HH. This will be done through the provision of agricultural (including fisheries) inputs in kind or through CVA, this will be accompanied by agroecological trainings. During harvest season NRC will support the construction of traditional granaries and/or distribution of PICS bags to avoid post-harvest losses. NRC will also support food diversity through nutrition sensive cooking demonstrations. In order to increase purchasing power of the target population, economic inclusion activities such as new livelihood development through cash for work, start up grants for new businesses and/or other CVA depending on needs and areas will be implemented. Assistance will be promoted for people that have been assessed as socio-economically vulnerable through a close linkage with other actors to ensure that scarce resources are directed to those most in need. NRC will be focusing in Cabo Delgado Province. Given the changing environment, NRC will be adjusting its targeting and response plan as needs arise.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

As part of a Do-No-Harm approach, and for a mainstreamed conflict sensitivity approach, NRC will work with both IDPs and host communities not to exacerbate inter-communal tensions. In particular, the provision of agricultural inputs to IDPs will alleviate the burden on local communities and will contribute to develop markets, movement of cash and improvement of availability and accessibility to food to all. When possible, NRC will also support host community with post harvest lossess activities and trainings. This could have a potential positive spill-over effect in the overall community, where improved living conditions in targeted HH could lead to increased acceptance of the continuously growing IDPs population. If required, HLP support will be integrated into the response to solve disputes related to access to land for IDPs.The Building-back-safer approach will benefit the host, as much as the hosted targeted population. NRC will design its intervention paying particular attention to vulnerable and traditionally marginalised groups. Safe programming provisions will ensure inclusion of women, elderly and persons with disability across all sectors. Women and girls in Mozambique are often subjected to gender-based discrimination, excluded from full participation in decision-making at both household and community levels, and more generally denied control over their own resources and opportunities. NRC also monitors the relationship between IDPs and host communities in the locations where it operates. As displacement becomes protracted and depletion of resources more severe, the possibility that in some locations geographical or ethnic origin could become a discrimination/marginalization criterion cannot be ruled out. FSL activities will prioritise the most vulnerable including female headed households and will ensure that women form part of the discussions by advocate for their leadership roles in community structures. Furthermore, NRC is aware on the importance that the natural resources have for livelihood development and the crosscutting protection relevance on potential conflicts that may arise in the community due to competition for natural resources. The response will therefore take into consideration the environmental aspect under the overarching ethos of the “Do-no-harm” approach NRC extends it to the environment.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

NRC takes guidance from the Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS) definition of accountability and understands Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) as a process of using power responsibly, taking account of, and being held accountable by the people we seek to assist. NRC commits to operating in accordance with these principles of accountability by ensuring meaningful participation of affected populations in key decisions and processes as well as regular sharing of clear and accessible information with them during all phases of the programme cycle. NRC Mozambique Office will establish and implement compliant and feedback mechanism to ensure that communities receiving assistance from NRC project have access to a formalized procedure and mechanism that provides a safe, accessible and effective channel. This also provide an opportunity for beneficiaries and project stakeholders to obtain information about the organization and its programmes, offer feedback and complaints, and to be provided response. Although there are various methods of CFM (eg: suggestion boxes, text message/free-phone calls, focus group discussion, community meetings, office visit etc), NRC Mozambique M&E team will do community consultations to identify context-based CFM before the implementation of any CFM method. NRC M&E Manager will lead the establishment of CFM methods and analysis at CO level, while the M&E Officer at area office will be responsible in actual implementation of the CFM including feedback/compliant data collection and registration on the CFM database. The CFM procedure aims to improve the quality of programming by allowing more in-depth accountability to communities and people affected by crisis.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Elderly and persons with disabilities (PWDs) may be more exposed to the risk of marginalization and exclusion. Due to a mix of socio-cultural stigmatization and physical barriers hampering mobility, PWDs are often invisible in the community and relegated to the domestic sphere. In addition of priority and preference during any kind of distributions, People with Special Needs PSN’s committees will be included or ad-hoc focus groups will be consulted for programe design, adapting whenever possible activities to meet their specific needs. This may include, start up businesses dependant on their individual abilities, direct targeting through CVA, referrals to other organizations when possible, to be determined based on PSN’s community consultation. To reach out to a representative sample of PSN unable to attend consultation sessions in the community, NRC team will take care to include households visits at their dwellings during assessment, planning, implementation, and M&E activities.In addition, once funding is secured, programme team will conduct once more FGD including groups specifically for people with disability and the elderly to ensure programming has components tailored to them.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

NRC will lead with NEAT + assessments wherever possible in order to ensure the best possible environmentally friendly solutions and will share all results through coordination with the FSL cluster. All LFS programming will strive to find innovative but replicable local solutions for local problems when it comes to resource use efficiency. The intervention will strive to limit environmental impact through the promotion of agroecological practices through trainigns and information sharing. Whenever possible, re-planting activities and polyculture of species (including trees) will be done. When possible the distribution of kits will be packed with biodegradable materials such a jute bags.
