Project Summary

Save the Children (SC) will deliver comprehensive healthcare services over a 12-month period in 8 camps of Ukhia and Teknaf, Cox’s Bazar to ensure that Rohingya refugees and host communities have access to essential primary healthcare services through health posts (HPs) and primary healthcare center (PHCC), including SRHR, nutrition, MHPSS, and WASH services in line with the Ministry of Health and Health Sector recommended minimum package of essential health services. In responding to COVID-19, SC will also provide quality healthcare to moderate to severe cases of COVID-19 through continuing Severe Acute Respiratory Infection Isolation and Treatment Centre (SARI ITC), and CBS in line with sectoral guidelines for COVID-19 patients in the communities. All health facilities are staffed by doctors, midwives, MHPSS workers, paramedics, clinic aides, maternal child health, IYCF-E counsellors and nutrition promoters and community health workers (CHWs). Clinical services will be complemented with a programme of strengthened health promotion communications and community engagement to encourage timely access to healthcare and address existing fears around access. This will include the promotion of COVID-19 preventive measures and the identification and referral of suspected COVID-19 cases to appropriate health facilities. SC will procure personal protective equipment (PPE), pharmaceuticals, medical supplies, equipment and non-medical supplies. All staff and volunteers will be trained and re-trained on child protection basic concepts, child safeguarding, child rights, and confidentiality and on how to be child/adolescent-friendly providers. SC will strengthen community outreach activities for identification of suspected COVID-19 cases, referrals to the appropriate health facilities, and promotion of COVID-19 preventive measures with key health messages. The project will cover all operational services for 8 health posts, 1 primary healthcare center (PHCC), 1 severe acute respiratory infection isolation and treatment center (SARI ITC) and community-based surveillance (CBS) to ensure uninterrupted healthcare services during multiple outbreaks including COVID-19 and disasters. SC will engage PHD as a local partner to localize the interventions. The core scope of work includes support to two health posts in Ukhia with related healthcare services. PSS services will be provided under the technical guidance and supervision of SC. As a partner, PHD will be responsible for field level operations, financial management and regular reporting. SC will strengthen the Upazila Health Complexes (UHCs) and Community Clinics (CCs) in Teknaf to minimize the adverse impacts of COVID-19. SC will expand IPC measures as stated in National Guideline for Health Care Provider On Infection Prevention and Control of COVID-19 pandemic in Healthcare Setting and support health facility to select one focal point following ‘National Preparedness and Response Plan for COVID-19’ to ensure IPC protocols are in place and implemented according to the set plan.

1 - Identified Needs

Across the 34 camps in Cox’s Bazar, there are currently 99 health posts, 38 primary healthcare centers (open 24 hours), and five hospitals. The key health needs for the Rohingya and host communities are very similar; prevention and treatment of communicable diseases, antenatal and post-natal care services, emergency obstetric care services, reproductive and child health, and SGBV related clinical case management. 9% of individuals from camps were reported as having had an illness serious enough to require medical treatment or to have required a regular medical check-up and 94% of them sought treatment for it (J-MSNA, 2020, Refugees). Due to the low quality or ineffective treatment provided at the health centers, refugees (35% of the respondents) went into debt to pay for health expense privately, and procured medicine from outside the camps. In the host communities, loss of income and increase in transportation costs as well as an increase in prices of medicine/treatment diminished people’s ability to seek good quality treatment. 83% of the respondents from host community paid for availing healthcare services, and 34% of the respondents went into debt to pay for health expenses (J-MSNA, 2020, Host Communities). Common reluctance to seek treatment at health centers due to fear of contracting COVID-19, and lack of health staff and treatment options are the impacts of COVID-19 on health needs. These clearly indicate that though the value placed on healthcare is high and healthcare centers are commonly used, Rohingya refugees and host communities are seeking additional or alterative health care that requires payment, as many perceive the health care provided in the camps inadequate. So, there is need of continuing the improvement of access and utilization of quality healthcare services, and health seeking behavior. Additionally, the need to scale up structured PSS and access to effective MH support remains urgent. The Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) needs for high-risk and special-need populations, including survivors of gender-based violence and adolescents, are yet to be addressed. Married and pregnant adolescent girls still need to be targeted through facility and community support groups to reduce their high risks of maternal mortality and morbidity. There is a need to strengthen SRH care, including access to safe, voluntary family planning and maternal and new-born health services. Gaps still exist in Basic Emergency Maternal, Obstetric and Neonatal Care (BEmONC) services. Unsafe home delivery practices (about 60% of the total deliveries across the camps) and delay in the uptake of Post Natal Care (PNC) follow-up also put newborns and mothers at risk of avoidable complications. Analysis of data from Save the Children’s facilities shows that up to 10% of newborns present with medical complications during PNC follow-up and although no official figures are currently available, Save the Children’s monitoring data shows that there is a high rate of early neonatal mortality. Gender Based Violence (GBV) is under-reported, and existing clinical staff have limited capacity to manage its medical and psychosocial impact. There are currently multiple communicable disease outbreaks such as chickenpox, measles and diphtheria within the Rohingya and the host population, confirming the understanding that the current setting is a “perfect storm” for disease outbreaks. There is an urgent need to ensure the Rohingya refugees and the host communities can access adequate health services for COVID-19 and for non-COVID care and that the current health services are not overwhelmed. Further, healthcare workers will need Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) to protect themselves and their families as they save lives. As evidence shows from Save the Children’s response to COVID-19, health messaging and community engagement can reduce the spread and contain the disease.

2 - Activities or outputs

(a) Health Posts and PHCC Services: Continue 8 health posts and primary healthcare center following minimum package of essential services for primary health care facilities in the camps; provide SRH services; manage post-abortion care (focusing infection prevention and control); continue BEmONC services at PHCC; conduct routine vaccination in fixed HPs and PHCC; provide MHPSS services; follow-up the management of non-communicable diseases (like hypertension, diabetes) and referrals of suspected cases for diagnosis and treatment of complicated cases; procure pharmaceuticals, personal protective equipment (PPE) medical and non-medical supplies; support referral mechanisms for patients to access secondary care facilities; (b) SARI, IPC and Infectious diseases: Run the 30-bed SARI ITC with prepositioning trained and skilled health personnel, supplies (PPE, pharmaceuticals) and equipment; maintain a 4-bed isolation unit for AWD patients in PHCC/Camp 21 and Oral Rehydration Points (ORP) to respond to any cholera outbreak; participate in deworming, Vitamin A campaign and other campaigns (like MR, OCV) as part of minimize risk of disease epidemics; participate in emergency simulation exercises conducted to test readiness of the staff and facilities to respond to a critical event; continue the provision of CBS in the community level to identify the patient with symptoms of COVID-19; conduct training for community health workers on COVID-19 preventive measures along with health and nutrition education; sensitize community leaders on COVID-19 preventive measures (washing hand with soap, social distancing, avoid mass gathering); and strengthen referral systems and ambulance services; (c) Community Health/Outreach: Ensure community engagement aligned with CwC and Risk Management strategy and guidelines; provide training to community health workers; strengthen community based surveillance system under CHW tasks; continue door-to-door visits by; utilize network of trained community health workers for promotion of key MNCH practices, collect socio-demographic information, disease surveillance and vital statistics etc.; procure and distribute contextualized IEC and BCC materials; deliver needs-based health, nutrition, WASH and MHPSS focus campaign activities in camps; (d) Coordination: Strengthen health information management system and regularly feed into Health Sector information database; continue gender–disaggregated data collection from all service points and analyze to inform any potential barriers; introduce individual patient tracking system to ensure continuum of care; start with pregnant women; regularly participate in Health Sector, SAG and working group meetings and share project lessons learned as well as strategies as relevant; (e) Emergency services, contingency plan: Based on the epidemiological evolution and low case detection in Rohingya and host communities, SARI ITC will be phased out to act as multifunctional outbreak center for acute watery diarrhoea (AWD), Diphtheria, etc. or as a secondary level hospital combined with PHCC, complemented with CBS to ensure uninterrupted healthcare services during multiple outbreaks including COVID-19 and disasters; (f) Community Engagement: Save the Children (SC)'s healthcare services will be complemented by risk communication and community engagement, as well as services provided by Community Health Workers and Community Mental Health Workers. (g) Upazila Health Complexes (UHCs) and Community Clinics (CCs) in Teknaf/Host Communities: Support and equip UHCs with medical supplies and human resources for establishing and functioning of “Fever Clinic” and COVID-19 isolation facilities; support UHCs in establishing IPC protocols and guidelines, staff training and capacity strengthening, waste management and disposal; conduct trainings for service providers on COVID-19 national protocols/SOPs and MHPSS; provide PPEs to the health service providers and frontline workers
