Project Summary

The geopolitical and socioeconomic situations in South Sudan have remained dire since 2013 with various shocks that aggravated the plight of the populations. Lack of food, expensive markets, climatic shocks such as flooding, pests and diseases, low coping mechanisms, and limited livelihood opportunities has worsened the nutrition outcomes.
Nasir and Ulang Counties have experienced communal conflicts. The anticipated improvement in security following the September 2018 peace agreement, plus intensifying inter-clan clashes in camps in Ethiopia, has led to a number of people returning to South Sudan.
UNKEA intends to support nutrition interventions in access constrained locations such as Duk, Toaloare, Nyarkueth, and Torkiel in Ulang county; and Wanding, Kier, Dhekdhek, Homkor, Nasir town and Kiechkuon in Nasir county.
In Gogrial East county, insecurity (communal, cattle raids, & civil strives) between Gogrial West, East and Mayom counties yearly which led to massive displacement of communities and destruction of shelters, crops and agricultural inputs. UNKEA is implementing HPF-3 funded primary health care program in Gogrial East, however communities that are in access-constrained locations such as Ameth-nhom in Nyang Payam, Majak nyuom in Toch North Payam and Panacier in Toch West payam are not served with both nutrition and health services. UNKEA intends to establish integrated nutrition/health mobile sites in Ameth-nhom, Majak nyuom and Panacier PHCUs.
This project will ensure the implementation of the following activities which contributes to both the cluster and strategic objectives through this funds;
• Conducting MIYCN individual and group counselling of PLWs and caregivers of children 0 - 23 months using MIYCN counselling cards.
• Training health/nutrition staff on MIYCN guidelines.
• Training MSG & CNVs on MIYCN.
• Establishing baby mother friendly areas/shades for care and key messages education sessions.
• Training health/Nutrition care providers on CMAM guideline.
• Conduct routine screening of children in the fixed OTP site and mobile sites in the community by the community nutrition volunteers.
• Conducting quarterly Mass MUAC screening in the community.
• Training Health/Nutrition workers on SAM with Medical complications (SAM-ITP).
• Providing routine care/management of children with SAM in fixed and mobile OTP site.
• Conducting project initiation workshop for leaders and community members to support the project.
• Conducting community consultation about the quality of nutrition services.
• Training health and nutrition workers on NIS.
• Equipping nutrition sites with data collection tools.
• Ensuring all nutrition sites submit nutrition reports in NIS timely every month.
• Training of health/nutrition workers on Malaria testing for children admitted with SAM.
• Conducting routine screening/test for Malaria in children with SAM at each nutrition site.
• Establishing kitchen gardens for demonstration to PLWs and caregivers of children with SAM.
• Procuring and distributing child-play kits to all nutrition sites for stimulation of children admitted with SAM.
• Training of health/nutrition workers on GBV/CP referral.
• Conducting GBV risk assessment for all nutrition sites.
This project will cost $ 915,000 for 12 months period.

1 - Needs

The geopolitical and socioeconomic situations in South Sudan have remained dire since 2013 with various shocks that aggravated the plight of the population. Acute food insecurity at the household driven by lack of food; high dependency on expensive food commodities in markets; climatic shocks such as flooding, pests and diseases; low household coping mechanisms; limited livelihood opportunities; the prevailing poor macro-economic conditions in the country has worsened the nutrition outcomes. According to the FSNMS round 23 Upper Nile with SAM burden of 16,717 among children 6 – 59 months and GAM rate 14%, however, the SMART survey of March 2018 puts Ulang county at GAM rate of 20.1%; In Warrap state the burden of SAM is 32,924; Wasting among women = 15%; GAM rate = 13.3%. REACH report May 2018 for Gogrial West puts a GAM rate of 26.6% and SAM = 6.9%. These coupled with high morbidity, limited access to basic services, food insecurity as well as poor child care and feeding practices which contribute negatively to the prevalence of acute malnutrition. Therefore continued scale up of treatment of acute malnutrition targeting such populations and expansion of services to previously insecure and inaccessible areas is a priority.Nasir and Ulang Counties have experienced communal conflicts. The anticipated improvement in security following the September 2018 peace agreement, plus intensifying inter-clan clashes in camps in Ethiopia, has led to a number of people returning to South Sudan. The hosting of returnees by the community has led to overstretch of their coping mechanism to shocks. Currently this is aggravated by the massive floods which has caused displacements, asset loss and worse humanitarian situation for people. Nutrition interventions to access-constrained locations is needed since the existing project doesn’t reach those areas. In Gogrial East county, insecurity (communal, cattle raids, & civil strives) between Gogrial West, East and Mayom counties yearly, for instance insecurity in June 2017 led to massive displacement of communities and destruction of shelters, crops and agricultural inputs. Most of the HHs located along the west bank of river Jur were displaced west towards Alek, Gogrial and Kuajok towns and nearby settlements. This is worsened by floods creating inaccessibility to affected populations across the river jur. UNKEA is implementing health in Gogrial East, however the northern part of the county is not supported with the HPF funding. There are 3 PHCUs (viz Amethnhom, Panacier and Majak nyuom) which are not supported with health and nutrition services in Gogrial East. These locations are bordering Unity state and have cases of acute malnutrition. Mothers travel long distances to Gogrial West county to access nutrition services in Alek, Kuajok and other areas or cross the swamp to lietnhom.To combat these challenges, UNKEA proposes to support; In Gogrial East establish 3 integrated mobile nutrition/health sites in 3 PHCUs Amethjak, Panacier and Majak nyuom. These sites are in hard to reach locations and most times accessibility to the nearest OTP/TSFP site is a major challenge to the affected populations.In Nasir the 5 OTP sites that have been established in hard-to-reach locations i.e. Nasir, kier, wanding, Dhekdhek, and Homkor with establishment of 8 outreach OTP sites in Kiechkuon and 2 outreach sites to cover Burebeiy, Makak, and Maker areas which are returnees transit areas. In Ulang the project will support locations (Duk, Nyarkueth, Toaloare, and Torkiel) that are prone to insecurity, flooding and hosting IDPs with nutrition services and additional 4 outreach sites will be established to serve Riang area which have access constraints.

2 - Activities

UNKEA proposes to support the realization of strategic objectives and sector objectives as follows:Strategic Objective 1: Sector Objective 1, 2 &4: Sector responses: Prevention of malnutrition through behaviour change communication; Early detection and treatment of girls and boys under five years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition; Promote community participation for a responsive nutrition service delivery. Improve facility-based data collection and management. Strategic Objective 3; Sector Objective 3; Sector response: Prevention, testing and treating for malaria among children under five years with SAM; Establishing demonstration kitchen garden at nutrition sites for or in the vicinity; Integration of child stimulation and ECD in the provision of care for children with SAM and MAM; Mainstreaming of child protection and GBV risk mitigation at nutrition sites.UNKEA will work with the existing nutrition partners for TSFP program in implementing a comprehensive nutrition intervention in the selected areas for mobile/outreach nutrition services. In Ulang, UNKEA will team with GOAL or a partner that has FLA with WFP for response in mobile/outreach sites (Duk, Nyarkueth, Torkiel, Toaloare, and 3 mobile sites in Riang area). In Nasir, UNKEA will work with Relief International the partner for TSFP to respond in mobile/outreach sites (8 villages in Kiechkuon i.e. Lonylok, Thoar, Dishin, Kiech2, Borkiech, Pierjack, Guenom) by establishing 3 mobile/outreach teams to serve these locations. UNKEA will provide treatment and care for SAM cases while GOAL and RI will provide treatment and care for MAM cases. All the nutrition services will be done within the same premises. In Gogrial East County, UNKEA will develop modalities of nutrition service provision with World Vision South Sudan in ensuring the 3 areas selected for mobile/outreach nutrition activities are supported. UNKEA will explore various possibilities with the partner which may include; getting TSFP supplies from World Vision from the nearest OTP/TSFP site to support the mobile/outreach nutrition services site and submitting the necessary reports to the OTP/TSFP site which will eventually submit the necessary reports in the NIS. This project will support the realization of the Cluster strategic objectives by implementing the following activities;• Conducting MIYCN individual and group counselling of PLWs and caregivers of children 0 - 23 months using MIYCN counselling cards.• Training health/nutrition staff on MIYCN guidelines.• Training MSG & CNVs on MIYCN.• Establishing baby mother friendly areas/shades for care and key messages education sessions.• Training health/Nutrition care providers on CMAM guideline.• Conduct routine screening of children in the OTP site and community by the community mobilizers.• Conducting quarterly Mass MUAC screening in the community.• Training Health/Nutrition workers on SAM with Medical complications (SAM-ITP).• Providing routine care/management of children with SAM in OTP site.• Conducting project initiation workshop for leaders and community members to support the project.• Conducting community consultation about the quality of nutrition services. • Training health and nutrition workers on NIS.• Equipping nutrition sites with data collection tools.• Ensuring all nutrition sites submit nutrition reports in NIS timely every month. • Training of health/nutrition workers on Malaria testing for children admitted with SAM.• Conducting routine screening/test for Malaria in children with SAM at each nutrition site.• Establishing kitchen gardens for demonstration to PLWs and caregivers of children with SAM.• Procuring and distributing child-play kits to all nutrition sites for stimulation of children admitted with SAM.• Training of health/nutrition workers on GBV/CP referral.• Conducting GBV risk assessment for all nutrition sites.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes. This project will on daily basis evaluate the possible protection related risks through the community who will give information about existing situations. 6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes. This project will compliment the efforts of existing nutrition services in all the 3 counties by ensuring services are delivered to populations that are in access-constrained areas, IDPs and returnees who are initially not supported through the existing projects. At the closure of the project, all project activities and sites of operation will be handed over to the existing sister project to continue service delivery. It is anticipated that the various interventions will empower the community to be able to continue whenever the project ends.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

The project through the local structures will ensure all marginalized community are consulted and services delivered to their locations. This project will target all locations that are not accessible most of the time of the year and ensure services are at the door-steps of that community. When making decisions that would affect the project implementation, risks to service delivery and protection related risks, the affected population such as women, girls and men will be consulted prior to any changes thereof. The location of sites that will be identified for service delivery will be based on the community's advice and as such, those location will be evaluated for risks related to GBV, CP and security and safety of supplies and staff. All staff who will be implementing this project will be training on GBV/CP risks and referral mechanisms that exists. UNKEA has zero tolerance to sexual exploitation and abuse, therefore any individual with affiliation to UNKEA signs the policy to ensure measures are in place for abusers.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

UNKEA has incorporated various mechanisms to ensure accountability to targeted communities where UNKEA has been implementing including Ulang and Nasir through integration of feedback and accountability mechanisms into all UNKEA’s operations including the country strategy, proposals, M&E systems, recruitment, staff inductions, training and performance management and reporting. UNKEA will ensure transparency to the community and stakeholders at various levels by providing accessible and timely information on organizational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to ensure that they are aware and able to make informed decisions and choices. Field officers in collaboration with community members will take lead to facilitate a dialogue between UNKEA and targeted community over information provision. UNKEA project areas have an elaborate feedback and complaints mechanism led by the project coordinator by actively seeking the views of affected populations to improve policy and practice in programming, ensuring that feedback and complaints mechanisms are streamlined, appropriate and robust enough to deal with (communicate, receive, process, respond to and learn from) complaints about breaches in policy and stakeholder dissatisfaction. UNKEA encourages community participation by enabling affected populations to play an active role in the decision-making processes that affect them through the establishment of clear guidelines and practices to engage them appropriately and ensuring that the most marginalized and affected are represented. Considerations were made by UNKEA on this project based on feedback obtained from community consultation activities conducted mid-2018.Community feedback was used to modify the project design to ensure joint monitoring and evaluation of the goals and objectives on an on-going basis and reporting on the results of the process.Beneficiaries will be informed by the use of awareness creation activities using portable public address
