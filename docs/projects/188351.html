4 - Needs

WHO recognizes the disability as a global public health issue, a human right issue and a development priority. The disability forms a great challenge, which aggravated in emergency such as in the ongoing Syrian crises. As disability cuts across all sectors and involves diverse actors, the way forward in this issue will need provision of resources by and action from a wide range of international and national partners. WHO in Syria is the leading technical organization in disability and rehabilitation sector. It plays an essential role in coordination and partnership with stakeholders such as MOH, UN agencies and NGOs in developing and implementing specific interventions to improve quality of life for persons with disabilities. Identified needs are as follow:• Poor access to health services due to lack of functioning facilities, • Injuries as a direct result of bombing, fighting and mines/UXO. Millions of people reside in areas contaminated by explosive hazards, • Lack of proper and secured referral system for treatments of injuries and other emergencies and surgeries including NES.• Disruption in medicines and medical equipment supply chains for trauma, Needs for contingency planning and pre-positioning of emergency stocks.• There is still an urgent need to ensure safe and sufficient blood transfusion services at health facilities level in Ar-Raqqa, Al-Hassakeh, and Deir-ez-Zor.• Support for trauma interventions cover the needs for life-saving medicines & supplies.

5 - Implementation of activities

1. Trauma cases supported across the country; Procuring and distributing medicines, trauma and surgical kits, and equipment to main emergency-providing health facilities.2. The capacity of health workers on emergency, burn, chemical exposure and trauma management enhanced.3. Safety of blood transfusion is ensured; procuring and distributing blood safety kits.4. The infrastructure in health facilities/emergency units providing trauma care and burn services is rehabilitated.5. Disabled persons supported across the country; strengthening the provision of physical rehabilitation services through distribution of prosthetic and orthotic supplies. 6. The capacity of health professionals on disability and physical rehabilitation services enhanced.7. The referral system strengthened through procuring ambulances and partnership with related partners8. Improving access to trauma and physical rehabilitation services through supporting health actions conducted by national NGOs.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

1- Attacks on health facilities, ambulances and first responders2- Lack of safe referral pathway for the wounded patients especially in NES to level 1 or 2 hospitals which can provide proper medical interventions. Taking into consideration the volatile and fragile situation and the widespread lands of mines provoking higher vulnerability to trauma injuries in northern eastern part.3- staff burn out. 4--Staff exposure to infection Mitigation measures:1- Reporting on and advocating against attacks on health facilities and personnel.2- Increase the capacity building activities in areas of high severity scale (>3 such as NES and south of Syria) on specialized trauma care management protocols including response to mass casualty, chemical hazards and burn management . Maintain capacity building activities for health staff including training of trainers.3- Expand partnerships with NGOs in order to strengthen referral services and provide surgical and emergency interventions to IDPs and host communities living in high severity scale areas.4- Promoting Work/Life Balance, to Monitor Workloads & Scheduling, to monitor Workloads & Scheduling.5- Ensure that PPEs supplies are provided through the usual supply line within Syria so that HCWs are safe and have access to adequate quality PPEs


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Yes.FPs in WCO are selected and designated to address PSEA activities, guidelines and reporting mechanisms are shared with implanting partners in addition to implementing PSEA awareness sessions to health partners. Mechanisms to facilitate feedback from beneficiaries are in place in WCO, providing opportunities to identify the extent to the delivered services are supporting their health and protection needs, and for people to be able to give feedback to the organization so that they feel confident their concerns will be taken seriously and in confidential way. WHO makes available immediate and confidential mechanisms to report SEA for WHO staff members and WHO collaborators or any other person who may have been a victim of SEA or may have witnessed or otherwise been informed of a case of SEA involving WHO.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1- Lack of reliable data regarding people with physical disabilities including amputees which may affect planning for a proper response according to the real needs.2- Lack of proper and reliable data related to the current health country profile which reflect the real needs and help guiding the level and type of interventions needed (technical support, provision of materials, raising awareness… etc.) especially in the fields of physical rehabilitation, trauma care management and reproductive health. 3- Constant IDPs movements and continuous demographical changes affect the sustainable of a proper response especially for patients who need continuous supervision and non-interrupted treatment.4- Lack of flexibility by the concerned authorities for conducting large-scale assessments and field visits to the newly accessible areas and to the northern part of the country. Mitigation measures:1- Advocating with health authorities the importance of conducting large scale assessment for better planning and response as mobilization of resources.2- Improve the exchange of information and coordination among health sector partners for better responding to the identified needs.3- Support MoH in improving data collection mechanism ad establish national registry for people with disabilities.4- Expand partnerships with NGOs in order to strengthen referral services and provide physical rehabilitation services to IDPs and host communities living in high severity scale areas.
