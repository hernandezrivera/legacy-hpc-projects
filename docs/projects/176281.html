Project Summary

The project will focus on a more targeted emergency response with basic WASH needs for the most vulnerable populations affected by shocks that include IPC famine situation, flooding, disease outbreaks such as the COVID-19 pandemic and inter-communal violence (ICV). This is line with the WASH cluster priority for 2021 which calls for prioritization of needs for the most vulnerable population.
Access to basic WASH facilities and related services both for water and for sanitation is very low in South Sudan. This predisposes the population to numerous environmental risks that include the WASH related disease outbreaks. This project aims at addressing the gaps in most deprived locations with both temporary and (semi-)permanent solutions in locations with severity level 4 & 5 which have high morbidity/mortality related to nutrition and health or where WASH alone is the main driver of the crises. This will ensure that the most vulnerable beneficiaries are reached by both temporary and more durable solutions during reconstruction phase. The project will focus on delivering rapid assistance to affected counties and population affected by a sudden or rapid onset of disasters mainly with temporary services, but also if applicable with semi-permanent solutions. The response will be prioritized in Severity level 4 and 5 Counties that include Pibor, Nyirol and Akobo in Jonglei State and Juba County in Central Equatoria State where Oxfam has physical presence. This project will provide emergency WASH services which include distribution of WASH minimum package for promoting access to safe water, access to basic and dignified gender sensitive sanitation and hygiene promotion.
The WASH activities will focus on providing access to adequate and safe water, sanitation and hygiene services to the most vulnerable communities. To address the lack of access to safe drinking water sources, the project will map boreholes that are broken down/low yielding, and a technical assessment of community water needs based on Sphere standards to provide repairs and maintenance including contamination protection through flood proofing, water treatment and flushing to ensure safe water is available to communities. Water points in communities and institutions will be prioritized to ensure safe water is available to contribute to good public health status among target communities. Trainings for water user committees will be carried out to ensure proper use of water sources and promote a spirit of ownership. Mapping of existing/new community pump mechanics will be carried out and those identified will be trained on maintenance of boreholes. Vulnerable HHs will be provided with water containers and minimum package for water treatment products to enable safe and hygienic water access. Communities will be trained on hygiene and sanitation around water sources, maintenance of water containers and hygiene behaviour and practices to reduce disease, prevent malnutrition and improve general well-being. Hygiene promotion will focus on food hygiene, proper handwashing practices and domestic hygiene. This aims to change knowledge, attitudes, and practices of communities on sanitation and hygiene. WASH NFIs will be distributed to enhance hygiene standards of vulnerable households.
Oxfam will directly implement the project but in close coordination with key Clusters, including WASH and Protection, key TWGs groups such as AAP, other partners operating in the target locations, The State line Ministries of Physical Infrastructure and Health and the local authorities. This will help ensure that there is complementarity among all actors devoid of duplication of services. The project also takes cognizance of the importance of key cross cutting issues that will require to be mainstreamed during the entire project cycle including Protection, Gender, Disability Inclusion, Accountability to Affected Population and Conflict Sensitivity.

1 - Needs

The South Sudan HNO of 2021 shows that the living standards and well-being of an estimated 5.6 million people will be negatively impacted by lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices in 2021. Of this, 50% are female while 53% comprise of children and 15% are persons living with disability. A total of 198,900 and 200,100 people in Akobo and GPAA are in need respectively. Out of this, 43.7% of the population in Akobo and 77.2% in Pibor are in Catastrophic situation. The report also shows that only 36% of households have access to an improved water source in under 30 minutes without facing any protection concerns. The remaining 64% of people across the country either rely on unimproved or surface water sources (41%), take more than 30 minutes to reach an improved water source (19%), or are able to reach an improved source in less than 30 minutes but face protection concerns while accessing the water (4%). The highest proportion of households relying on surface water were found in Upper Nile, with 66%, followed by Jonglei with 43% and Central Equatoria with 41%. The quantity of water available per day is also below standards in many locations, with 17% of people unable to collect enough water for drinking, and 39% of people reporting insufficient access to water for handwashing. Women and girls who are traditionally responsible for water collection face an increased risk of harassment, assault and sexual violence when collecting water far from their homes. In 2020, 16% of the people using water facilities reported safety concerns. The report also shows that access to sanitation remained low, with only 17% of households reporting owning a latrine in their compound, 4% have access to a shared latrine, and 2% have access to a communal/institutional latrine. This means that 77% of people do not have access to a latrine. In 34 counties, zero to 10% of households reported using latrines. At the country level, open defecation is practiced by 73% of the population. The high rate of open defecation also exposes women, children and people living with disabilities at higher risk of violence and discrimination, particularly in IDP sites and other crowded settlements. Regional access to sanitation varied greatly, from 75% in Western Bahr el Ghazal to 8% in Warrap, 9% in Northern Bahr el Ghazal and 10% in Jonglei. Limited access to appropriate and dignified sanitation and hand washing locations, in urban and peri-urban settings, increases the risk of cholera and other diarrheal or vector-borne diseases. An Inter-Agency Rapid Needs Assessment (IRNA) conducted in September – October 2020 in the GPAA Payams of Lekuangole, Gumuruk, Verthet/Doren shows that there exist urgent need for distribution of emergency WASH NFIs (bathing and laundry soap, Water purification products: PUR, Aqua tabs and filter cloth, water storage containers, hygiene awareness raising, distribution of dignity kits to women of child bearing age and adolescent girls. Lekuangole has 12 boreholes with only one operational while 2 had been submerged by flood water. Another 9 borehole are non-functional and require urgent repair and rehabilitation to enhance safe access to clean water for the surrounding community. The community rely on raw surface water for drinking and other household water needs and as result there is reported rise in water borne diseases. Gumuruk has 3 non-functioning boreholes that need to be repaired and rehabilitated including protection from contamination through flood proofing. In Verthet, there are only 2 operational boreholes that required protection from contamination by flood water. The pump mechanics in all there three location need support of hand pump repair toolbox to support in fixing the existing breakdowns. The communities in all these locations lack access to sanitation facilities and as a result open defecation is rampant as observed within market areas.

2 - Project activities

The Project will focus on the delivery of basic WASH needs and services to the most vulnerable disaster affected population. The interventions comprise of the following: Activity 1: Conduct sessions on WASH-GBV Safety Audits, Disability Inclusion dialogue, AAP discussion/dialogue sessions with communities for safe programming. Ten (10) sessions are planned under the projectActivity 2: Train/Refresh 100 community Pump mechanics (with at least 50% female composition) in the Operation & Maintenance of boreholes/ hand pumps Activity 3: Repair/rehabilitate 65 broken boreholes & hand pumps including contamination protection through flood proofing after conducting GBV risk analysis Activity 4: Conduct water treatment, testing and quality monitoring at the source (SWAT), collection and point of use through community-based water quality monitors.Activity 5: Distribution of water treatment products: Aqua Tabs, PUR and filter cloth to promote household level water treatment immediately after a crisis to enhance access to safe water at the household. Activity 6: Establish and train/refresh 65 Water Management Committees (650 members with at least 50% female composition) on the management of existing WASH facilities and ensure that they are self sufficient Activity 7: Construct 370 gender appropriate and elevated emergency sanitation facilities in communal/concentrated sites complete with GBV risk mitigation and disability friendly features Activity 8: Disinfection and decommissioning of full emergency latrines in concentrated/communal sitesActivity 9: Repair and rehabilitate 450 stances of broken down and existing sanitation facilities to guarantee safety and privacy of all users and the ease of access for Persons living with disabilities (PLWDs)Activity 10: Conduct bi-monthly jerrycan cleaning and environmental clean-up campaigns and exercises while targeting concentrated & open defecation sites. Activity 11: COVID-19 awareness and hygiene promotion behavior change campaigns through sensitization of the target communities in strict conformity to the WHO/MOH guidelines. Approaches to include use of public address facilities at public spaces, door-to-door visits, radio messaging and use of IEC materials where appropriate. Activity 12: Training/refresh 350 Community Hygiene Promoters (CHPs), Community Health Volunteers (CHVs) and community leaders on community led emergency and sustainable WASH interventions.Activity 13: Establish 450 contact-free communal handwashing facilities to promote good hand hygiene practices among target communitiesActivity 14: Hygiene promotion through distribution of WASH NFIs and female dignity kit items. (In-kind support from WASH Cluster Core pipeline)Activity 15: Establish/reinforce hygiene clubs in 40 schools to promote WASH In Schools (WINs) Activity 16: Hygiene promotion and messaging through visibility IEC materials. To be used during hygiene promotion campaigns and WASH calendar events (World Water Day, Global Hand Washing Day, World Menstrual Hygiene Day & World Toilet Day)

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Oxfam will adopt conflict sensitive and ‘do no harm’ approaches with the full participation and inclusion of beneficiaries throughout the project implementation period. Should any issues arise related to WASH, Gender and Protection, the CPC/WASH Committee and other stakeholders will be actively involved. Gender and Protection will be mainstreamed in line with the Oxfam commitments to promote gender equality and women’s rights and ensure safe programming. Oxfam has a Minimum Standards for Gender in Emergencies to ensure a consistent approach in all its interventions that will be carefully followed. A rapid gender and protection analysis will be conducted to identify the most vulnerable amongst target communities, as well as the relevant protection threats, they face and gender-specific needs. To promote gender responsive, safe, dignified, and equitable access to assistance, Oxfam will consult the target communities on the appropriate locations/sites and time for distributions. For example, women and girls of different age groups will be consulted to identify and address their reproductive health specifically menstruation-related needs and to ensure support with the distribution of dignity kits. Gender responsive selection criteria will be developed to ensure women’s voices in decision-making platforms. Community Protection Committees and women’s group will be specifically consulted to identify GBV risks and ensure safe access to water points. Oxfam has an existing Complaints Response Mechanism for follow-up action on issues reported and the provision of timely feedback. This will ensure that girls, boys, women and men have access to gender sensitive, safe, timely and appropriate feedback options. Full participation of women, men, boys and girls will be ensured through consultation and community mobilization approach and safe approaches guided by GBV risk analysis will be adopted to promote women’s meaningful participation. Oxfam’s policy on safeguarding prevents, protects, and promotes the rights and dignity of beneficiaries. On recruitment, every member of Oxfam staff is required to sign mandatory policies and the Code of Conduct. This is complemented by mandatory safeguarding, gender and power training for Oxfam staff, partners, consultants, contractors, and volunteers. To ensure a safe, accessible, and dignified response which will create no (further) harm and help reduce existing vulnerabilities and risks, elements of safe programming will be kept at the core of each phase of the response ranging from assessment to evaluation. Community inclusion and feedback will help to design and deliver a safe and accessible response. The WASH and Protection team will ensure that WASH infrastructure is built in an accessible and safe location for the community, to avoid any social or GBV-related risk. WASH facilities (communal latrines) will be clearly marked with the appropriate signage for gender segregation of facilities as well as being placed in the most convenient locations to be accessible to the community, including those living with disabilities. Oxfam considers strong community awareness on Sexual exploitation and abuse and jointly set culturally sensitive and socially acceptable reporting mechanisms in cases of happening. This form part of our mandate in empowering the communities that we serve in all our target locations. As we have safeguarding focal points in each project location and at Juba level we will encourage community members to report on staff behaviour to these focal persons using the most preferred and safe mechanism they think and Oxfam will make sure appropriate action is taken on time after investigation.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Oxfam has an existing Complaint Response Mechanisms (CRM) that provides follow-up action on any issues reported during the project period, to ensure that necessary feedback is completed. These CRM approach will be adopted and used under this project to ensure that all interventions are community centered and encourage community dialogue, consultation, and community participation in delivery of activities. The project will ensure that girls, boys, women, and men (including older people and those with disabilities) have access to timely and appropriate feedback on any complaints related to WASH/GBV affecting the community. This will promote corrective actions towards addressing their specific protection and assistance needs and accurate documentation. Multiple and complementary feedback and complaints collection mechanisms will be considered to avail safe and convenient options to the community. Appropriate mechanisms for sharing feedbacks/complaints will be selected through discussion and agreement with the target community. We keep records of complaints and feedback regularly using the recording data base we have and follow up on the referral and response progress. Oxfam has developed community engagement tools in WASH for use in ensuring that the community is engaged, consulted and actively involved during the entire project cycle. In addition, Oxfam will adopt the guidance on best practices in sanitation 'Sani-tweaks' through which it details on the steps under which WASH practitioners should adopt in ensuring that the community is consulted and engaged in the entire programming. This includes consultation during design and siting of all WASH facilities to ensure that the views of girls, boys, women and men including persons living with disabilities are considered. This will ensure that issues of access and the appropriate design of safe WASH facilities and services are considered so as to reduce associated GBV risks. Oxfam will share timely, relevant and clear information to the target community and other stakeholder through organizing inception workshops, conducting different community level meetings, Meeting with local authorities, etc. Most of the information we share are details of the project including the project activities and budget, beneficiary entitlements, reporting SHEA, etc. Oxfam also ensures that vulnerable community groups are included in information sharing and information shared is in languages, formats and media that can be easily understood by the communities we work with. Participation will be ensured through engaging of women, men, girls, and boys within target communities and their representatives in all the stages of the project to make sure that Programme decisions are based on informed consent and feedback. Oxfam also regularly conducts exit interviews at the spot of distribution of WASH NFIs and setting up compliance desk in all distribution sites to get real time information about the quality of distributed items, the appropriateness of distribution location, etc. Moreover, conducting post distribution monitoring (PDM) is a common practice in all field locations to get views and opinions of targeted people and gage the quality of distributed items, safety and access to distribution location, etc. Periodic review and reflection session that will be organized with local community and stakeholders is also another platform used to share information and get views and opinions from the people we are serving. GBV risk analysis shall be undertaken before the onset of implementation of activities through FGDs and other consultative forums to ensure inclusiveness of women and girls in sitting and design of safe, equitable access and appropriate WASH facilities. The project will ensure that staff from both WASH and GBV partners co-chair and are actively involved in the risk analysis. Community leaders, other WASH and Protection partners and the local authorities will also be consulted and involved.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

Oxfam will ensure adequate access to clean water for drinking, cooking, washing hands, other hygiene needs and domestic use. This will help mitigate the impact of the IPC 3, 4 & 5 situation where access to water is key for human survival. The project will ensure that broken down water facilities are restored through conducting repairs and rehabilitation works to hand pumps and boreholes to ensure that communities have access to clean water for drinking and for food production. Access to water will mitigate the risk factors of water-borne diseases such as cholera and the ongoing COVID-19 disease outbreak. Oxfam will ensure that the project only targets the most affected locations that experience high WASH vulnerability (4 and 5) as per the WASH Cluster and are in IPC 3, 4, and 5 levels. The project will be implemented in close coordination with the key stakeholders such as the WASH Cluster, line government departments, local authorities and other Clusters such as Food and Security Livelihood (FSL), Health and Nutrition and Protection to address the extreme humanitarian needs among the most affected population. The project will also ensure that vulnerable communities in IPC 3, 4 & 5 situation have access to wash NFI items such as water storage containers and soap to promote safe water storage and enhance personal hygiene. Provision of sanitation facilities through repair and rehabilitation of existing latrines will help improve the coverage for latrines and thereby reducing the incidences of open defecation which is a key cause of malnutrition in IPC famine situation.

Other critical areas that require sound coordination with include but not restricted to registration and verification of beneficiaries; and subsequent distribution of WASH NFIs to avoid multiple registration by beneficiaries.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

A lack of access to clean water, sanitation facilities for safe disposal of human excreta and poor hygiene practices impacts on the public health status of communities including children under the age of 5 years. The World Health Organization (WHO) estimates that 50% of malnutrition is associated with repeated diarrhea or intestinal worm infections as a result of unsafe water, inadequate sanitation or poor standards of hygiene among communities. This project will therefore strive to impact on the health of the children under the age of 5 years through provision of protected water sources where communities will access clean water that is necessary to promoting public health. This will help safeguard the health of the caregivers and their children and and eradicate the practice of fetching water from unprotected sources. Studies show that proper handwashing using water and soap can reduce incidences of diarrhea by 42 to 47%. This is a simple, effective and cost-efficient way of reducing diarrhea incidences among the under 5 years. This project will help reduce the incidences of diarrhea through installation of handwashing facilities to be used by the vulnerable communities. This will ensure that children and caregivers maintain high standards of hand hygiene to sustain good health. The project will also promote improvement in sanitation through eliminating open defecation through installation of new sanitation facilities and repair and rehabilitation of existing ones which are not in usable condition. This will help reduce cases of fecal contamination of food and water which is common in open defecation situations.
