Project Summary

Since 2016 the English speaking regions of Cameroon have been embroiled in violent socio-political conflict which led to the forced movement of hundreds of thousands of persons form rural areas.
One of the most affected sectors has been the forced closure of schools depriving thousands of pupils of education. In Lebialem Division schools have remained close for over four years. Recently however, some families have started returning to their villages. In 25 of these villages 45 schools and a community education centre
have partially re-opened with a total of 3046 pupils ( 1 418 boys and 1628girls) attending.
The project will support 2500 most vulnerable pupils ( 1500 girls and 1000 boys) in 20 schools and one community education centre through providing basic learning materials, training on Education in Emergencies to 60 teachers, supporting 25 volunteer teachers with monthly stipends, providing didactic materials and mainstreaming child protection in the curriculum.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Since the end of 2016 schools in Lebialem have been closed due to conflict, and the displacement of families. This September NADEV has carried out a rapid assessment and found that there are 44 schools and a community education centre that are partially functioning as some families have returned to their communities. Although the parents have taken the initiative to re-open the schools they are unable to provide basic school needs and regular incentives for volunteers.The pupils thus lack basic learning materials, teachers are insufficient and do not have appropriate didactic materials, those of them who are volunteers do not have remuneration. Another major challenge is that of learning gaps within the various classes. Many pupils have been out of school for four years and thus lack foundational literacy and numeracy skills. As the teachers do not master the appropriate methodology to address thischallenge, they tend to go along with the more performing pupils with the risk of leaving many of the pupils behind.In this context the quality of education is compromised and the schools may even close again without external support for these parent led initiatives as they themselves are impoverished by their forced displacement.
