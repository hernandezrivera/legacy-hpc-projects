Project Summary

Due to the combined effects of widespread and severe flooding, Desert Locust infestation, socioeconomic impacts of Floods and the cumulative impacts of previous shocks, up to 2.1 million people across Somalia are expected to face food consumption gaps or depletion of assets indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes through December 2020 in the absence of humanitarian assistance. In addition, 849 900 children under the age of five are likely to be acutely malnourished through August 2021. Desert Locust continues to pose a serious risk of damage to both pasture and crops at least until the end of 2020. The 2020 Deyr (October-December) rainfall season is likely to be below average to average across the country, which could lead to drought and trigger a worsening of the humanitarian situation if the 2021 Gu (April-June) season rainfall is also delayed or performs poorly. These findings reflect the outcome of the 2020 post-Gu seasonal food security and nutrition assessment conducted across Somalia during July and August 2020. The assessment and the subsequent analyses were jointly led by the Food Security and Nutrition Analysis Unit (FSNAU, a project managed by the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations) and the Famine Early Warning Systems Network (FEWS NET, a project funded by USAID) with the active participation of Government institutions, UN and NGO partners. 2020 Gu cereal production in southern Somalia is estimated at 74 000 tons. This is 40 percent lower than the long-term average for 1995-2019. The main factors for below-average 2020 Gu cereal production include: recurrent and severe flooding, erratic rainfall and a prolonged dry spell, insecurity and conflict. The Desert Locust infestation has been mostly confined to northern and central Somalia, causing damage to crops, fruit trees and pasture. In the northwest, 2020 Gu/Karan cereal harvest expected in November is estimated at 22 500 tons. this is 45 percent below the 2010-2019 average, mainly due to erratic rainfall and Desert Locust. Internally displaced persons (IDPs), a majority of whom are poor and live in urban areas in desperate conditions, have limited livelihood assets and coping options and rely more heavily on external humanitarian assistance. As a result, a significant proportion of IDPs continue to face moderate to large food consumption gaps. Some of the urban poor across Somalia who struggle to make ends meet also continue to face moderate to large food consumption gaps. For both groups (i.e. IDPs and urban poor), the socioeconomic impact floods was a factor, associated with a decline in remittances, increased food prices, and a decline in employment and other income-earning opportunities in urban areas.
In order to alleviate the situation, STS proposes to provide food and livelihood assistance to the crises affected IDPs and vulnerable host communities in Howlwadaag of Marka District, Afgoye district of Lower Shabelle and Benadir regions.
STS will target 4000 IDPs and Host communities (3000 IDPs and 1000 Host communities) in Marka and Afgoye districts in Lower Shabelle region for cash plus agricultural inputs distribution to farmers who were affected by floods and whose property and farms were destroyed by the Hagaa rains and floods.
Banadir region will benefit from unconditional cash/multipurpose which will be distributed to a total of 3000 HHs comprising of 2000 IDPs and 1000 Host Community Households.
These IDPs were displaced by Al-shabaab from their towns in Lower Shabelle, Marka and Afgoye districts after they were forced to provide child soldiers for Al-shabaab. The project will also target 4000 farmers with cash plus agricultural input distribution in Afgoye and Marka districts of lower Shabelle.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Multipurpose cash is safe and efficient modality for reaching the most vulnerable IDPs and poor host communities as a way of providing them with livelihood assistance. It also provides freedom to priorities household needs and to purchase items and build good credit with local traders.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

The project design considered market functionality in that currently there are private companies that easily provide this service of mobile money transfer that is easily traceable and verifiable.

Mitigation Measures

2a. • Beneficiary selection will be systematic to ensure vulnerable persons, such as marginalized minorities, single/child headed households and Mothers/caregivers/PLWs with disabilities are included. • Different sociodemographic groups will be consulted on their specific food and nutritional requirements and preferences for distribution points.• Outreach messaging will be respectful and inclusive to ensure that beneficiaries from different sociodemographic, economic and cultural groups know about food security and livelihood services, distributions times/sites. 3a. • Safe programming and mainstreaming gender at all stages will be ensured. The integration of STS Protection staff in the project team will help ensure the implementation of key activities beyond basic safe programming and Do No Harm approaches. • STS will engage with local IDP and community leaders and community representatives to play an active role in identifying solutions and the decision-making processes that affect livelihood practices, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build beneficiaries self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response. • STS will train all staff involved in the food security and livelihood sector on psychosocial support (PSS) including identification and support for disabled beneficiaries. 3b. • STS staff and volunteers working with affected populations understand, will sign and adhere to a Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries. •STS staff and volunteers involved in distributions and in direct contact with beneficiaries will receive training in prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). 3c. • STS will locate distribution points in locations that are accessible by vulnerable population groups from the catchment population. 4b. STS will maintain a complaints and feedback phone number that allows anonymous submission of feedback. Timely corrective response or feedback will be maintained.
