Project Summary

It is estimated that about 2.5 million South Sudanese children and their families continue to face multiple child protection threats and vulnerabilities moving into the seventh year of conflict and protracted crisis. Awerial (Lakes state) and Bor South, Twic East and Duk (Jonglei state) are among the priority counties in tier 1 and 2 severity mapping ranking. (South Sudan CPAoR HRP 2020 Strategy). Factors contributing to poor child protection indicators in these counties include prevalence of inter-communal violence, flooding, acute food insecurity and malnutrition (IPC 4), disease burden, large number of IDPs and expected increase in returns, existing huge child protection caseload and breakdown of community protection structures and mechanisms. Crises affected boys and girls are usually exposed to separation from families, psycho social distress, armed recruitment, sexual abuse and violence among other bad cultural practices. Without appropriate care and family-based support, affected boys and girls are forced to process their conflict experience alone thus compromising their emotional development and childhood. This project will target 25000 children and their families (11000 girls, 9000 boys, 3000 women and 2000 men: including 5% as children with disabilities) for integrated child protection in emergency services including: Provision of Comprehensive Case Management including for unaccompanied and separated children with use of CBI for vulnerable children; Service mapping and safe referral pathways; Provision of Psycho social Support including caregivers sessions; Capacity building of front line child protection staff and volunteers on PSS, case management and general CPiE; Child Protection awareness raising and prevention messaging; Strengthening of community structures and mechanisms including community-based child protection committees, child protection desks, etc; and Child protection monitoring, coordination and assessments. To maximize child protection outcomes, CINA will employ a mixed of static and mobile child protection responses, be prepared for larger reunification of UASC and reintegration of CAAFAG due to expected increase in return of IDPs and support in the roll out of inter-agency child protection guidance tools including revised CPMS, CPIMS+, Case Management SOPs, PSS Manual and Practical Guide for working with girls associated with armed forces and groups and support to child survivors’ initiative to improve quality of and access to services for child survivors of sexual abuse in emergencies. Adherence to these inter-agency guidance tools and existing CINA’s child protection policy, Code of Conduct, and Community Complaint and Feedback Mechanism will uphold the Do No Harm, promote centrality of protection principles and enhance accountability to affected populations before, during and after project implementation. CINA will coordinate this project with South Sudan Child Protection Area of Responsibility, protection partners on ground and relevant authorities and will require $400,000 to meet the protection needs of boys and girls in priority counties by 31 December 2020.

1 - Needs

Numerous need assessments highlighted child protection issues in Awerial, Bor South, Duk and Twic East counties in the third quarter of 2019. REACH protection assessment in hard-to-reach locations in September 2019 indicated that Duk is one of the top five counties where children cannot access education and health services due to insecurity. Similarly, INSO security analysis for Lakes (including Awerial) at the same period indicated rampant intercommunal violence (ICV) which is driven by cattle culture, prevalence of weapons, retaliatory violence, seasonality and opportunistic crime committed along identity group lines. OCHA South Sudan Situation Update in September 2019 reported heavy flooding which hit Duk, Bor South and Twic East among other locations in Jonglei, which has resulted to destruction of shelters, crops, drinking water sources; preventing access to child friendly spaces, schools, health facilities and market centers and impeding humanitarian’s ability to move across the affected areas. Moreover, all these four counties are among acutely food insecure (IPC 4) according to September 2019 IPC Updates. In all these contexts, children are the most affected. CINA has active caseload of 805 UASC and other vulnerable children in the inter-agency CPIMS+ database for Awerial, Bor South, Twic East and Duk (as of 18 October 2019). Boys and girls affected by crises suffer loss, fear, panic and anxiety and lack psychosocial support, recreational and learning facilities. Children are exposed to widespread family separations, recruitment by armed forces and groups, sexual abuse and exploitation. Family and community support systems have been devastated and harmful cultural practices including girl marriage and other forms of violence against children have heightened including increased number of boys and girls being exposed to harsh labour, living in child-headed households and working on the streets. Precisely, these needs are urgent and require immediate and unhindered intervention to save the lives and childhood of the affected South Sudanese children.

2 - Activities

In line with SSD CPAoR strategy for HRP 2020, the proposed interventions in this project will include:Provision of Comprehensive case management including for 1000 UASC (540 girls, 460 boys) to be reached with family tracing and reunification services and 1200 other vulnerable children ( 650 girls, 550 boys) for case management including with use of cash based intervention. This will also include service mapping and establishment of safe referral pathways, etc. CINA will use its existing CBI modality for general protection to pilot CBI in child protection case management. This will follow the Case Management SOPs, vulnerability criteria; use of banking agent, risks analysis for cash in child protection and use of other existing guidance tools for cash which will be explored; Provision of Psychosocial Support targeting 25000 children, caregivers and community members (11000 girls, 9000 boys, 3000 women, 2000 men) including provision of caregivers support sessions. This will be provided using community based initiatives, with particular emphasis to PSS in schools, CFS and youth resilience centers and working with youth as members of community-based child protection committees; Capacity building on Child Protection including PSS , Case management and general CPiE will be provided to 100 frontline child protection staff, facilitators and community-based child protection committees (60 men, 40 women) to promote local approaches and project sustainability; Child Protection awareness raising and prevention messaging will be conducted targetting 25,000 children and community members (11000 girls, 9000 boys, 3000 women, 2000 men) in the schools and community using different forums and means, integrated into multi-sectoral outreach activities including WASH, education, nutrition, and health. All beneficiaries (25,000 people in total) will be reached both with PSS and CP risks messaging;Strengthening of community structures and mechanisms including community based child protection committees (CBCPC), Child Protection desks, etc and engaging youth and adolescents in peacebuilding, CP risks mitigation and recreational activities to foster harmony; Timely, Child Protection monitoring , coordination and assessments will be conducted within the deep field locations of the prioritized counties using inter-agency tools to inform child protection response and advocacy as well as providing guidance to appropriate multi-sectoral interventions. CINA will make use of existing operational child protection tools including revised CPMS, CPIMS+, case management SOPs, PSS manual and Practical guidance for working girls associated with armed forces and groups and support to child survivors initiative during project planning, implementation and coordination.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

In most cases, youth are left out in design and implementation of humanitarian child protection activities despite the fact that they are usually the main perpetrators of violence against children and other vulnerable members within their community. This project will target youth as key stakeholders and actors of community-based child protection committees and they will be trained and supported to identify and implement community initiatives to combate violence and ill-cultural practices against children occuring within their community. Due to protracted crises (e.g in Mingkaman, Duk, Bor , Pibor town, etc), beneficiaries are pulled to these locations due to presence of humanitarian assistance, and children mostly find themselves moving across locations, getting exposed to risks of family separation and violence. This project will ensure increased child protection monitoring along transit points (market centers, taxi parks, river docking sites, food distribution sites, etc) to identify children moving alone and increase CP risk awareness at these transit sites. Similarly, girls are exposed to sexual violence in highly populated IDP locations due to insecure shelters, being worsen currently by floods in the priority locations. This project will increase risks prevention with specific messages on prevention of sexual abuse against girls including child-mothers and child headed households and actionable measures will be forged in collaboration with relevant authorities, GBV, and Shelter/NFI partners. Information about services will be disseminated using loud speakers, radio and large community gatherings (e.g schools, worship centers, food distribution points, etc so that no one is left out. CFS will be established in locations that are safe and accessible to children to minimise risks associated with distance to locations of humanitarian services.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

CINA will use its existing Beneficiary Feedback Mechanisms where messages on the banner or compliant box, labeled in local language will be displayed in community centers (school, CFS, church). A hotline number, managed by CBMs focal person will be displayed on all awareness banners for use by beneficiaries to lodge their complaints. Feedback will be delivered immediately through the complaint box for individual cases and in community monthly feedback meetings. Persons with disabilities will particularly be made aware on how to lodge their complaints and will be assigned a Facilitator to support them. Complaints received through these means will be processed by the CBM Focal person (Child Protection Manager), and in consultation with CINA's senior management team, actionable steps will be taken to address the specific issues raised by the beneficiary/community and relayed through the community based child protection committee leadership.
