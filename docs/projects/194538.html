Project Summary

Purpose and overall goal:
To reduce morbidity and mortality by responding to public health emergencies and providing access to quality essential primary healthcare, including reproductive health, services to survivors of SGBV and mental health services, to girls, boys, men and women in emergency situations, with specific focus on accessibility for persons living with disability and the elderly. To build the resilience of communities and the MoH to prevent, detect and respond to disease outbreaks.
Methodology and target:
Medair's project focuses on provision of primary health care in areas of high need and where existing services are inadequate, including Leer and Pibor. Activities include provision of curative consultations in primary health facilities; community-based treatment of common illness in children under five; routine vaccination; reproductive and sexual health including provision of ANC, safe delivery, PNC and clinical management of SGBV; management of at-risk infants through MAMI; and mental health treatment. Medair conducts ongoing training and supportive supervision for capacity building staff of Medair, CHD and other partners in order to improve the quality of service provision. Medair primarily targets children under five, women, and people with disability and mental health conditions, due to their unique vulnerability, though not exclusively.
Through the Emergency Response Team, Medair can respond in any area of South Sudan in response to disease outbreaks, acute displacement, reports of increased morbidity and mortality, nutritional emergencies, flooding and other natural disasters. Medair ERT aims to reduce morbidity and mortality of vulnerable populations through outbreak response such as vaccination campaigns or case management, and provision of primary health care services where these are inadequate. Through training and community engagement, Medair aims to increase the capacity of long-term health partners and the local community to be prepared for future shocks.
All of Medair's health activities are integrated with WASH, Nutrition, Emergency Shelter/NFI and Protection activities to enhance the effectiveness and address underlying causes of morbidity and mortality.

1 - Needs

The Health Cluster in the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022 identified 5.5 million people in need of healthcare, an increase from 5.2 million people in 2021. Of these, 30% are in extreme need and 20% in catastrophic need. “The weakened South Sudan’s health system is still unable to cope and respond to humanitarian emergencies and provide basic essential life-saving health services. Limited health facility functionality and capacity, and poor coverage, have made it difficult to meet additional health needs posed by floods, food insecurity and frequent conflicts.” (HNO, 2022) 80% of the population report barriers in accessing health services, the main barriers include insecurity, distance to health facilities and flooding. Even when able to access health services, only 8% of 1869 facilities are providing the full basic package of health and nutrition services. Malaria continues to be the highest cause of morbidity and mortality, and the number of AWD cases is increasing across the country, though there have not been any cases of cholera reported since 2017, 20 counties are identified as cholera hotspots. South Sudan also continues to experience vaccine preventable disease outbreaks such as Hepatitis E, measles, and COVID-19. Routine immunisation and reproductive health services across the country continue to have very poor coverage, with 49% coverage with Penta 3, and only 39% of women accessing four antenatal care visits during pregnancy, which contribute to high infant, child and maternal mortality. Mental health services in particular were highlighted in the HNO as being inadequate, with an estimated 2.5 million people reported by WHO as being in need of services. Pibor continues in 2022 to be classified as an area with catastrophic health needs. Medair started supporting two Primary Health Care Units (PHCUs) in April and June 2021 in hard-to-access areas which previously had no functioning health facility for over one year due to insecurity and flooding. Ongoing insecurity in the area continues to impact people’s access to health care, due to looting of health facilities and facility closures due to population displacement. Geographical impediments also impact services, with areas cut off for significant periods of time due to seasonal flooding. Reproductive health services remain inadequate due to lack of qualified staff in the area and lack of referral mechanism for complicated cases. Mental health and psychosocial support needs are completely unmet in current health programming. Leer’s classification of health needs in the HNO has declined from severe in 2021 to catastrophic in 2022. This is a result of increasing insecurity and catastrophic flooding, both leading to massive displacement of the population. Insecurity has led to looting and destruction of health and nutrition facilities, worsening in early 2022. Existing health services continue to be inadequate to meet the high needs of the community and there is currently need for emergency response across all sectors. Mental health services continue to be a significant need in Leer, with acute and chronic insecurity increasing the vulnerability of the population to poor mental health and reduced psychosocial wellbeing, impacting families and communities.

2 - Project activities

The Medair project will consist of both acute emergency response capacity, and longer-term primary healthcare interventions. All activities will be conducted through multi-sectoral responses to maximise impact to acute and chronic public health emergencies. The Emergency Response Team will assess and respond to acute public health emergencies in all areas of South Sudan, including disease outbreaks, large-scale population displacement, emergency GAM rates, significant increases in morbidity or mortality, and natural disasters. The health team has the capacity to conduct 1-3 interventions at the same time, in different locations. The emergency response team carries out short-term responses aimed at managing the initial shock of a new emergency, with interventions of up to three months. Efforts are made throughout the response to plan for exit, and handover is done as needed to long-term partners or MoH/CHD. Medair will remain flexible to respond rapidly to emergencies using different modalities including temporary or mobile clinics, iCCM, mass vaccination campaigns, or support to existing health partners or MoH in pre-existing facilities through technical and operational support. In all interventions Medair will focus on capacity building of local health staff and increasing the resilience of community members through training, education and community engagement. In Pibor, Medair will continue to work to improve access to essential primary health care in a community which has experienced multiple compounded shocks in recent years, including ongoing insecurity and geographical access challenges. Medair will continue to support two PHCUs in Gumuruk and Lekuangole with OPD, EPI and maternal health services, with a focus in 2022 on scaling up maternal health care to provide the basic package of maternal and neonatal care. ICCM services will be provided in the two payams, as well as Cascade groups which will aim to improve health, nutrition and WASH knowledge and practices in the community. Medair will provide supportive supervision, technical quality assurance and operational support to all activities and work towards providing all services according to local and international standards. Medair will provide additional support in Pibor PHCC, working alongside the HSS partner to increase quality of RH services. Medair will also introduce MHPSS programming in Pibor in 2023, with community-based psychosocial support and MH treatment following mhGAP-HIG protocols in 3 health facilities. Medair will also provide mobile outreach services to communities with limited access to existing health facilities. In Leer, Medair will continue to implement community-based programming through iCCM, treating common illnesses in children under 5 and working closely with other partners for referral. Medair will also continue to provide mental health services in three primary healthcare facilities following mhGAP-HIG protocols, services for survivors of SGBV, and psychosocial support services through community cascade groups. Medair will support primary health care services through ongoing support to Dindin PHCU, including OPD, EPI and RH services.

3 - Project indicators

Medair will measure the quality of its programme and success of the interventions through the following indicators: Sectoral Objective 1 - Percentage of health facilities assessed using Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) scorecard - Coverage of PENTA3 in <1 year old by administrative unit- Number of community health workers per 1,000 people- Percentage of deliveries assisted by a skilled birth attendant- Number of consultations per person per year disaggregated by sex, age and disability- Number of persons receiving MHPSS services disaggregated by sex, age and disability- Number of SGBV survivors receiving CMR services by age and sex and disability Sectoral Objective 2- Case fatality rate for malaria by age and gender- Percentage of alerts, rumours and outbreaks investigated within 48 hours of notification- Coverage of reactive measles vaccination of boys and girls between 6 months – 15 years- Number of community health events conducted for behavioural change communication- Number of people reached by health education and promotion during disease outbreaks disaggregated by sex, age and disability Sectoral Objective 3- Percentage of facilities with community feedback mechanism- Number of community consultative meetings conducted to identify needs, participate in response, and reduce impact on their lives disaggregated by men, women, boys and girls and disability- Number of functioning health facilities provided with supportive supervision quarterly- Number of meetings conducted with humanitarian, development and peace partners Medair will collect data from each project location on a weekly and monthly basis, data management will be overseen by health managers and health project managers, with technical oversight provided by the monitoring, evaluation and learning (MEAL) team and the health advisor. The MEAL team is responsible for monitoring the success of the projects using standard and custom monitoring tools, as well as ensuring data quality and accuracy, and evaluating the projects against the set indicators. Medair provides weekly reports to MoH through HMIS and monthly reports to Health Cluster through 5Ws. Internal reports are completed monthly by each project in the form of sitreps and quarterly internal reviews are conducted to review progress against indicators. Indicators such as percentage of births attended by skilled attendants and immunisation coverage are measured through KPC surveys, however due to the COVID-19 pandemic these surveys have not been conducted during 2020 and 2021, but resumption of household surveys are planned for 2022 assuming the pandemic remains controlled and no new waves or variants occur in South Sudan.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

During the initial assessment phase, Medair will ensure FGDs and KIIs are held with a variety of community members including women, children, people with disability, individuals who are not literate, and other marginalised community members. Feedback mechanisms and ongoing community engagement will also prioritise the inclusion of these groups in their design and implementation, to ensure their voices are equally heard and their concerns and needs taken into consideration during the project design and implementation. In case feedback is received regarding barriers to services for marginalised groups, Medair will aim to address these to allow for the full participation of all vulnerable community members in Medair’s services.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Before any emergency response or long-term project begins, Medair carries out needs assessments including focus group discussions (FGD) with diverse groups, and key informant interviews (KII), including with vulnerable members of the community, in which information is received directly from the affected communities on their needs, and the modalities they believe assistance can best be delivered. These needs assessments are rapid in the case of emergency response, and more in-depth in the case of a static, longer-term response. All assessments ask the community for their preferences for giving and receiving feedback throughout the project, and airing any grievances or complaints. Medair then sets up accessible, confidential and well-understood feedback mechanisms in order to receive suggestions and complaints. Feedback may be received via a beneficiary telephone hotline, community focal point, information box, regular community meetings, post-distribution monitoring, exit interviews and surveys, and through Medair staff, depending on the context and the preferences of the community. All feedback received is documented in a database which is used to inform future programming, and is responded to by Medair, with responses also recorded on the database. Medair will continuously and proactively seek information from the community about their satisfaction with Medair services in order to improve programming, ensure community engagement and empowerment, and assist in detecting misconduct or inadequate quality or scope of services. Throughout the project, Medair will meet regularly with the affected communities and leaders to ensure open communication and information sharing in both directions.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

All Medair staff, including casual staff in emergency responses, receive training in PSEA and agree to adhere to the Medair Code of Conduct. Medair will provide the affected communities with accessible and timely information about PSEA and about the confidential feedback and complaint mechanisms as mentioned above. Through the Cascade groups in Leer and Pibor , Medair will be able to easily disseminate information through the communities through ‘neighbour women’. Medair will also provide information about PSEA in regular meetings with the community and community leaders, as mentioned above.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiary feedback is collected in order to improve programming, ensure community engagement and empowerment, and assist in detecting misconduct or inadequate quality or scope of services. Medair documents all feedback received, as well as the response given , and goes further to analyse the beneficiary satisfaction with the response given. All feedback is analysed thoroughly by the MEAL team and used to inform future programming, adjusting the design of the project as appropriate to better address the needs of the beneficiaries and community. If Medair is unable to make changes due to seen and unforeseen reasons, the affected beneficiaries will be informed and further discussion will take place to find an alternative approach.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Medair will mitigate the risk of beneficiaries being exposed to violene as a result of the distance to humanitarian services by making services available as close to the communities as possible, for example through iCCM in both Leer and Pibor and Cascade/Care groups in both locations. For fixed site services, Medair will consult with the local community on the most suitable and acceptable locations. Medair will also ensure all activities are delivered within appropriate time windows to prevent beneficiaries from needing to travel in the dark. Medair will mitigate the risk of SEA through the measures mentioned above, but also through targeted training of all Medair staff of PSEA, and enforcing adherence to the Medair code of Ethics. Beneficiaries and communities will be made aware of Medair's policies on SEA, be educated that all services are provided free of charge, and be advised of feedback and complaint mechanisms in place in case of any concerns or misconduct. Medair includes protection mainstreaming in all projects, including emergency response, in order to protect beneficaries from SEA. In many areas where Medair works, especially in emergency response, there is no or very limited protection assistance available. Medair mitigates the risk of limited awareness or referral to protection assistance by training staff in psychological first aid and including clinical management of rape in the priject design of health interventions, when appropriate. Medair also addresses protection issues directly through the project desin, in particular in Leer where MHPSS is included in the project design to support survivors of protection incidents. Medair is involved in national and subnational coordination, including the protection cluster, in order to advocate for prioritisation of protection, and for gaps in coverage to be addressed. Where there are protection partners on the ground, Medair coordinates with them and supports referral of beneficiaries for further assistance. In some areas where Medair operates, marginalised groups such as women are routinely excluded from decision making and consultations by the community. Older people and those with disabilities are even more marginalised, and not seen during community gatherings.To mitigate this, Medair specifically targets marginalised groups during assessments, and during community consultation for intervention planning and project design. Focus group discussions specifically include groups of women, older people, children and people with disabilities as appropriate, and Medair advocates for community leaders to include all community members in interactions with Medair.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

Medair provides medical services for survivors of SGBV in both Leer and Pibor, and in emergency response when implementing primary health care services. Medair's health services target children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women, who are most at risk of malnutrition. Through prevention and treatment of common childhood illnesses in both health facilities and community-based treatment programs, Medair addresses disease as an underlying cause of malnutrition in children under 5. Medair also integrates nutrition screening and referral into health programming to ensure early detection and management of malnutrition in children under 5 and pregnant women. This directly impacts rates of malnutrition in areas where Medair operates.
