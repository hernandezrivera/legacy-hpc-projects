Project Summary

1. Context
Gender-based violence (GBV) affects one in three women globally, with displacement from conflict and natural disasters increasing their vulnerability. In 2022, over 100 million people were forcibly displaced, disproportionately affecting women and girls. Mozambique, particularly the provinces of Cabo Delgado, Sofala, Zambezia, and Tete, faces significant challenges due to conflict and natural disasters. These crises have displaced over 1 million people, heightening the risk of GBV. Displaced women and girls experience increased violence, exploitation, and lack of access to justice, which exacerbates the need for targeted interventions.
Ending GBV by 2030 is a key target of Sustainable Development Goal 5.2, which calls for comprehensive solutions, including crime prevention and effective criminal justice responses. UNODC has been supporting countries since 2010 in addressing GBV, ensuring victim-centered strategies aligned with international standards.
2. Rationale for Intervention
In Mozambique, the displacement of large populations due to conflict and cyclones necessitates innovative and scalable outreach solutions. Digital engagement offers an effective and cost-efficient way to reach vulnerable populations, provide vital information, and raise awareness about GBV, its signs, and available support mechanisms. UNODC's mandate to combat GBV and strengthen rule of law aligns with this need, aiming to enhance both public awareness and the capacity of the legal and criminal justice systems.
3. Methodology
The campaign will be implemented in two phases:
Phase 1 - Encouraging Calls
SMS messages will be sent to mobile phone users (ages 15-55) across key provinces (Cabo Delgado, Niassa, Nampula, Sofala, Maputo). These messages will encourage users to call a designated short code number for more information on GBV. A contracted service provider with experience in digital engagement will manage this phase.
Phase 2 - Providing Information and Action Calls
Pre-recorded messages will be sent through phone calls, using theatrical skits to engage recipients in an entertaining and informative format. The messages will cover essential topics on GBV, including:
Definition of GBV
Signs of violence
Consequences of violence
Where to report and seek help
The calls will be free for recipients, and the content will be available in Portuguese and 10 local languages. The campaign aims to reach at least 25,000 people, fostering awareness about GBV and encouraging reporting and prevention.
4. Post-Campaign Actions
Following the digital campaign, community-based workshops will be conducted for women and men to discuss GBV in intimate relationships. These workshops will build on the messages from Phase 2, using real-life examples of GBV to engage participants in group discussions. Local civil society organizations will facilitate these workshops, ensuring the content is tailored to the local context. Feedback will be collected to evaluate the impact of the messages and improve future campaigns.
5. Specific Objectives
The project aims to:
Increase awareness of GBV: Enhance understanding of GBV risks and consequences among victims, their close contacts, and the general public.
Promote reporting: Encourage reporting of GBV incidents to law enforcement, leading to increased investigations and prosecutions.
Strengthen support services: Inform communities about available support services, such as the Linha Verde helpline.
Improve the criminal justice system’s response: Support the legal system in addressing GBV cases through better reporting and follow-up.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Specific Objectives• Enhance understanding of GBV risks and consequences among victims of GBV, their close contacts, and the general public.• Inform communities about support services and protection mechanisms, such as Linha Verde.• Encourage reporting of GBV incidents to law enforcement and to prosecutorial authorities, leading to an increase in investigations and prosecutions.• Support the criminal justice system in addressing GBV cases through better reporting and follow-up. The campaign will reach at least 25,000 people, with content available in Portuguese and in 10 local languages of target provinces: Cabo Delgado, Niassa, Nampula, Sofala, and Maputo. The target audience of this digital engagement campaign will be individuals aged 15 and above, residing in Mozambique, with the goal of increasing overall awareness about GBV, its signs, and the available support mechanisms, and encouraging a culture of reporting and prevention.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The campaign will be delivered in two phases: Phase 1 - Encouraging calls: SMSs targeting mobile phone users (ages 15-55, both women and men) across key Mozambican provinces (Cabo Delgado, Niassa, Nampula, Sofala, Maputo). These messages will encourage users to call a designated short code number to a dedicated platform for more information. These messages will be sent by a contracted service provider specialized and experienced in digital engagement, including in Mozambique. Phase 2 - Providing information and calling to action: Pre-recorded messages delivered via phone calls, simulating theatrical skits to engage users in a relatable, compelling format. Such messages will be produced by the selected service provider and hosted on its platform. The calls will be free of charge to users. These theatrical skits will provide key information on gender-based violence dynamics, including but not limited to:• What is gender-based violence • Main signs of violence• Consequences of violence• Where to report and seek assistance with reporting 4. Post-campaign actions Community-based workshops for women and men, building on the digital outreach from the previous phases. The goal is to promote equal attitudes, healthy relationships, and deeper discussions about gender-based violence, especially in intimate relationships.During the workshops, participants will first listen to the pre-recorded messages from Phase 2. These messages will show real-life examples of GBV, with a focus on intimate partner violence. Afterward, group discussions will be led by facilitators, allowing participants to share their thoughts, ask questions, and discuss the messages.Local civil society organizations will support the workshops, ensuring the discussions are relevant to the local context. Feedback from participants will be collected to understand their views and how the messages impacted them. A report on what was learned will be made after these activities to evaluate the effectiveness and guide future actions. The lessons learned will help improve future campaigns and tailor the approach to the specific needs of the target communities.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Understanding and Awareness: The project enhances understanding of Gender-Based Violence (GBV) risks and consequences among victims, their close contacts, and the general public. By raising awareness about the impact of GBV, the project helps reduce the stigma surrounding survivors and encourages safer environments. This knowledge helps people make informed decisions, which minimizes the risk of further harm. Access to Support Services: By informing communities about available support services and protection mechanisms, such as the Linha Verde hotline, the project ensures that survivors have access to safe, confidential channels for assistance. This reduces the potential risks of not knowing where to seek help and ensures that support is available when needed. Encouraging Reporting: The project encourages reporting of GBV incidents to law enforcement and prosecutorial authorities, aiming to increase investigations and prosecutions. While this could potentially expose survivors to further risks, the project provides clear information on how to report safely, thus minimizing the chance of retaliation or harm through safe reporting mechanisms. Supporting the Criminal Justice System: By supporting the criminal justice system in handling GBV cases, the project ensures better reporting, follow-up, and accountability. This strengthens the system’s ability to address GBV effectively and safely, thereby reducing the risk of impunity for perpetrators and further harm to victims. Digital Messaging and Workshops: The project integrates pre-recorded messages in local Mozambican languages via a widely used digital platform. These messages provide practical GBV information and have been successful in other areas of awareness-raising. Additionally, the messages are part of group-based workshops, where participants listen and engage in discussions about GBV to challenge harmful social norms. These workshops promote open dialogue, encouraging community-driven change while helping reduce the potential risks of reinforcing harmful behaviors or stigmatizing survivors.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Raising Awareness: The project enhances understanding of GBV risks, empowering women and elderly individuals with the knowledge to recognize and respond to GBV. Informing about Support Services: It provides information about available support mechanisms, like Linha Verde, ensuring women and the elderly can access help when needed. Encouraging Reporting: By promoting safe reporting of GBV incidents, the project empowers women and elderly people to take action and seek justice.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project ensures people with disabilities can access assistance by raising awareness of GBV risks and available support services, including accessible resources like Linha Verde. It provides clear instructions on how to report GBV incidents, ensuring that people with disabilities, including women and the elderly, can safely and easily seek help.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The project is designed to have minimal environmental impact. We will print only a minimal amount of materials to reduce paper waste and ensure sustainability. The primary focus of the campaign is digital, using pre-recorded messages and widely accessible digital platforms. This digital approach minimizes the need for physical resources and reduces the environmental footprint. Additionally, no significant environmental assessments are required, as the project is centered around digital engagement, which has a much lower environmental impact compared to traditional, resource-intensive methods.
