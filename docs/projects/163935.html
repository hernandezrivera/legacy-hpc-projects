Project Summary

This intervention contributes signiﬁcantly to the 2020 HRP sector objectives 1,2,3 and is in line with MoSAL and ER&L sector priorities, guidelines and minimum standards for proposed activities. INTERSOS will provide assistance based on need to approximatelly 13200 persons (IDPs, host communities and returnees) in sub – districts of Suran, Hamra, Karnaz, Oqeirbat, Muradah, As Saan, As – Sukalabaya (Hama Governorate) and Kafr Batna, Harasta, Arbin, Duma (Rural Damascus Governorate). The present intervention will focus on peri - urban or purely agricultural communities to reach the most in need population. Among the targeted subdistricts the 73% has a sector severity scale above 3, being 45% above severity scale 4. The project will promote an AGD approach to respond to the speciﬁc needs of the population, with a speciﬁc focus on youth, women and PwSn. The proposed activities are aimed to re-establish livelihood opportunities for the most affected population such as: youth who have interrupted educational process, women at risk of GBV or survivors and PwSN who are prevented to access to income generating opportunities. The intervention is part of the INTERSOS Syria 2020 Country strategy whose pillars are protection and primary health - ER&L component is hence designed with a strong protection maintreaming with the aim at impacting on the most vulnerable group of population.
Through the rehabilitation of schools, the intervention will enhance the access to basic social services and will provide temporary job opportunities through cash for work modalities. The creation of job opportunities in depressed areas, especially with agricultural traction, will help families and communities to reestablish their income and the creation of positive coping mechanisms and strategies. Skills and vocational trainings will be based on existing labor market opportunities, identifying local sectors of economic growth or gaps in available goods/ services. Those trainings will target youth and women and PwSn to support them in restoration of their livelihood and to support the opportunity of accessing to the job markets. Other vocational training for youth graduates or youth who are not receiving any type of education due to economic lack of opportunities, will be supported to seek employment locally or starting their own small - business activities. Community acceptance for targeted livelihoods activities will be ensured through participatory process in selection of community-based initiatives/community projects as well as in the deﬁnition of the criteria for beneﬁciary selection. INTERSOS will ensure full cooperation with relevant stakeholders, local authorities, SARC and sector members in order to avoid overlapping with other interventions and promoting the knowledge sharing of previous experience among partners.

4 - Needs

The Syrian crisis has affected seriously the socio – economic foundations after nine years of conflict; nowadays the 83% of the population is leaving below the poverty line, in addition to that, the impact of the constant depreciation of the SYP hence the decreased purchasing power is worsening the situation. According to the findings of the ER&L Sector (HNO draft 2020) still there are at least 9.9 million Syrians in need of ER&L support. High severity scale (5 and 6) for income has been found for rural areas like Rural Damascus and newly accessible areas (Hama and Idleb). Around 400 communities have no essential/basic infrastructures due to conflict while local authorities have huge financial constraints in order to activate the reconstruction/rehabilitation process of public and social infrastructures. Moreover, high severity scale is registred for what refers to access to vocational training in Rural Damascus and Hama. From the RNA conducted by INTERSOS in November 2019 and January 2020, KIs have reported the urgent need of social infrastructure rehabilitation/reconstruction mainly schools, PHCs and public bakeries; this could create conducive enviroment for the return of IDPs in their place of origin a part from having a positive impact on social cohesion at community level. Lack of livelihood opportunities, small business projects, vocational training and restoration of agricultural equipment and assets have been reported as priorities. In agricultural areas needs are huge for the provision of livelihood kits inclusive of tools, seasonal crops and rehabilitation of wells, powered by solar panels. Furthermore water, sewage and energy systems in the villages need repair, restoration or upgrade. Local markets for agricultural and livestock products disappeared, local agricultural products are used for self – consumption or the few quantities produced are sold in the markets of the nearest cities.

5 - Implementation of activities

The targeted areas of this intervention have been selected on the basis of ﬁeld visits findings and secondary data analysis such as PIN, sector severity scale, presence of other humanitarian actors, accessibility. For the social and community infrastructure rehabilitation, INTERSOS, in collaboration with SARC and local authorities, will identify three schools in need of partial or more structured rehabilitation in north rural Hama and Rural Damascus. INTERSOS technical staff will supervise the technical assessment in order to have a detailed BoQs and quantify the type of works required (if minor or heavy rehabilitation). Schools’ rehabilitation will be done trhough the creation of temporary job opportunities (CFW modality) - CFW will be prioritized for areas with high level for destruction. Youth unemployed or out from the local labor market will be targeted for CFW activities as well as women (minimum 15%) ensuring an adequate selection of tasks to be assigned (paintings, cleanings), inclusion of PwD will be promoted accordingly with their capacities and skills (minimum 5%). Provision of vocational skills trainings targeting youth and women will be based on labor market and job opportunities for the localities of intervention. Life skills training will be provided for target groups who may be new to the labor market, such as young graduates people, women and PwSN. Those activities will be implemented in vocational schools (MoE) or community vocational centers for a duration period between 3 and 6 months. To support income generating activities in some areas, small livelihood kits and small equipment will be distributed to farmers. Community-based initiatives will be supported upon prioritization of needs in each targeted community.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

At the beginning of the action and before starting any community-based initiative or intervention, INTERSOS will ensure a clear communication with local stakeholders so to reduce the risk that tensions arise in the targeted communities. The selection criteria will be agreed with the local stakeholders with a protection mainstreaming approach. For CFW activities priority will be given to unemployed youth, female head of HHs, HHs with PwSN (disable or elderly), HHs with a high number of members and poor income. Prevention of child labor and SEA will be ensured by abiding to the Do no Harm principle, INTERSOS CP and PSEA Policies, and the National labor framework. Any contracted company or individual will sign the INTERSOS code of conduct. All the INTERSOS staff will receive training in CP and PSEA policies as well as on core humanitarian principles. INTERSOS will train the core project team in basic protection principles as well as on existing SEA complaints mechanism. Local stakeholder and INTERSOS staff will guarantee full transparency on the selection of participants for the vocational and skills trainings, and when applicants are not selected, INTERSOS will provide detailed feedback for the reasons. To avoid inequities on gender participation on access to the activities, special attention will be given to female headed HHs in order to respond to their specific needs and ensure gender equality and avoid any risk of discrimination in the implementation of the project. INTERSOS will monitor constantly the acquisition of new skills by the participants and further evaluations will be conducted in order to eventually proposed adjustments also based on their direct feedback. Monitoring and evaluation will be conducted also on regular basis inclusive PDM after the distribution of the livelihood kits and small assets.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: Security situation remains stable; humanitarian access is granted for INTERSOS in the areas of intervention. Acceptance and participation of authorities, local leaders and communities during intervention. Continued cooperation and coordination between INTERSOS, local authorities and partners. There is no extra-ordinary fluctuation of the SYP and the exchange rate compared to the trend registered in the past 12 months; local market can provide the required good and services. There is no mass movements of populations within the intervention areas. Approval from the relevant authorities for school’s rehabilitation is granted within the established workplan. Risks: local authorities doesn’t accept the type of interventions proposed, access to the identified community is not granted and the level of tension in the target communities is high, exchange rate fluctuations will have significant impact on INTERSOS cash flow and funding, mass displacement of population. Granted funds are not released. Mitigation measures: INTERSOS will ensure full engagement of the community leaders and local stakeholders, community tensions can be mitigated by ensuring participation of the population in program design, clearly communicated beneficiary selection criteria and established complaint mechanisms. INTERSOS will follow up and monitoring on the population movement, high level of information sharing with coordination mechanisms within partners and ER&L sector. INTERSOS will follow-up on the security context and prompt communicate relevant stakeholder to reschedule some of activities proposed. Hight level of coordination among the sector and the relevant national authorities will be promoted to mitigate the impact of bureaucratic impediments.
