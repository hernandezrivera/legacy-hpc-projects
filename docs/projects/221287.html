Project Summary

The socio-political crisis that started back in 2017 in the south west and North west regions of Cameroon has culminated to complex humanitarian emergencies leaving an estimated 2 million people in need in 2024. This conflict has led to one of the most complex in the world with 1.5million needing humanitarian assistance. The conflict has resulted to forced displacements, increased gender based and domestic violence, increased trafficking in persons, rape and family separation, child exploitation, sexual exploitation, forced marriages and other malpractices due to the insecurities in these regions. Pre-existing cultural and traditional discriminatory norms and practices, gender discrimination and socio-economic vulnerability have increased as a result of this crisis. According to the United States Government report, Cameroon stands at Tier 2 in fighting against human trafficking in the country. International organizations reported there were 1,013,568 IDPs in Cameroon as of March 2023, an increase from 933,000 reported in 2022.In addition to internally displaced persons, there were approximately 488,010 refugees and asylum seekers in the country as of April 2023. Traffickers may exploit refugees, asylum seekers because of their economic instability and sometimes limited access to formal justice. Observers also, previously reported government security forces engaged in commercial sex with women in the divisions of Ndian, Fako, Manyu and Meme of the South west region using food insecurity and their authority as leverage. However, vulnerable persons include displaced women/men, young children and women headed households are all at high risks of being exposed to sexual and gender based violence (SGBV) and trafficking in persons (TIP) as they live and depend on host communities the South West region for their most basic needs exposing them to all forms of exploitation and abuse.
Strengthening advocacy and sensitization campaigns, coordinating case management systems that enable survivors get necessary support in these divisions will go a long way in combating violence SGBV and TIP. Not leaving out those of the LGBTQI+ family another set of individuals mostly marginalized in the country and the South West regions in particular.
The benefits of our intervention include an increase in economic independence of women and girls by providing them with vocational training and microfinance opportunities to reduce their vulnerabilities to TIP and SGBV. Beneficiaries will acquire behavioral change from our education and sensitization programs which will also decrease the rate of TIP and SGBV. Our mental health and psychosocial support services will help survivors cope with trauma and improve their mental health. It will also lead to the prevention of future occurrence of TIP and GBV in the above communities. Forming a network of survivors will foster community support and solidarity.
Our intervention is designed to be implemented in four regions and structured to benefit diverse groups, individuals including male, female, LGBTI+ and persons living with disabilities of different age range in three categories including primary beneficiaries will include women and girls at risk of TIP and SGBV (age 7 to 60+), and male at risk of perpetrating TIP and SGBV (age 14 to 60), secondary beneficiaries including community and trade union leaders, family members of survivors, teachers and law enforcement officers, specific groups including children ages from 4 to 17, youth ages 18 to 26 and adults from 27 to 60 years old
Our intervention is designed with end user input through the following approaches to ensure our intervention will be culturally appropriate, relevant and effective. We have been conducting sensitization and data collection, focus group discussions, safety audits in affected communities and there will be regular feedback and complaint mechanisms for continuous end user input all through the implementation.

1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Our intervention is designed to be implemented in four divisions in the South West Region of Cameroon and structured to benefit diverse groups, individuals including male, female, LGBTI+ and persons living with disabilities of different age range in three categories including primary beneficiaries will include female at risk of TIP and SGBV (age 7 to 60), and male at risk of perpetrating TIP and SGBV (age 14 to 60), secondary beneficiaries including community and trade union leaders, family members of survivors, teachers and law enforcement officers, specific groups including children ages from 4 to 17, youth ages 18 to 26 and adults from 27 to 60 years oldOur intervention seeks to increase the economic independence of women and girls by providing them with vocational training and microfinance opportunities to reduce their vulnerabilities to TIP and SGBV. Our mental health and psychosocial support services will help survivors cope with trauma and improve their mental health. It will also lead to the prevention of future occurrence of TIP and GBV in the above communities. Forming a network of survivors will foster community support and solidarity. We will create and strengthen existing partnership with local LGBTI+ organizations and communities to ensure that interventions are tailored to their specific needs and contexts while ensuring their safety and confidentiality. We will also make use of focal points on LGBTI+ human rights while mainstreaming LGBTI+ work within the mandate of a gender focal point, ensuring that gender focal points have high level of expertise on LGBTI+ human rights as well as women rights. Our interventions will make use of inclusive language materials that acknowledge and respect the identities and experiences of LGBTI+ individuals within these communities while fostering a strong sense of cultural sensitivity eliminating discrimination and marginalization.Our intervention was designed with end user input through the following approaches to ensure our intervention will be culturally appropriate, relevant and effective.
