Project Summary

The objective of the project is to empower youth and women through providing technical and financial toolkits to build a sustainable fuel-efficient stove value chain and through providing access for vulnerable household to improved cooking solutions. The expected outcome of the project is access to sustainable energy, protection and resilience among vulnerable households in Borno State through the empowerment of IDPs, returnees and host communities with focus on youth and women.

1 - Needs

Access to sustainable cooking energy solutions is currently almost non-existent in most of communities in Borno State. The National Bureau of Statistics estimates that prior to the insurgency, more that 90% of households in the state depend on solid biomass for cooking. The insurgency and its attendant negative impacts has further exacerbated this problem. Affordability of these modern cooking solutions is another big issue. Today less than 5% of households in Borno State use clean cooking solutions. The use of solid biomass fuels on traditional 3-stone and metal tripod stoves are very prevalent with its attendant health, economic and environmental problems. The World Health Organization estimates that 95,300 Nigerians die annually as a result of smoke from cooking fires. The crowded situation in IDP camps and communities coupled with thousands of cooking fires further exposes them to the heath implications. In addition the forage for firewood is mostly done by women and young girls who are exposed to gender based violence and sexual exploitation. The UNHCR biennial report on sexual and gender based violence has identified the quest for firewood as one of the main ways women and young girls are exposed to GBV and sexual exploitation. The economic implications are quite high. A rapid assessment conducted by ICEED and Mercy Corps in Damboa, Mafa, Jere and other LGAs in 2017 on access to firewood for cooking showed that a household spends on average 300 naira daily on this fuel. This is equivalent to 9,000 per month on firewood. Another assessment conducted by ICEED and FAO in Jere, MMC and Konduga LGAs showed that more than 50% of households surveyed buy firewood and they spend on average 250 naira daily on this fuel. For households that mostly depend on aid for sustenance, the expenditure on cooking fuel is a huge drain on their already lean resources. The environmental impacts of using solid biomass fuel on traditional stoves has reached critical situation in Borno State. A research conducted by ICEED in 2018 revealed that a family uses on average 4kg of firewood for cooking daily. This is equivalent to 1.4 tons of firewood annually. with over 200,000 affected households depending on this resource, this amounts to about 280,000 tons of firewood annually. An average sized tree (30ft high and trunk of 60cm) weighs about 8 tons. So on average 35,000 trees are being cut down annually by households affected by the insurgency for fuel. Another environmental concern is smoke emission from traditional cooking fires. it is estimated that a traditional stove emits about 1.8 tons of CO2 equivalent annually. With the number of crisis-affected households using traditional stoves, the emissions are quite alarming. While this situation persists, there is need to apply energy efficiency and innovation in the use of solid biomass for cooking. Overtime a number of humanitarian actors including FAO, WFP, ICEED among others have implemented projects that aim to expand access to biomass-based clean cooking solutions in Borno State. However the scale of the problem seems to mask the noble interventions by these humanitarian actors. The project will seek to deploy innovative and fuel efficient woodstoves and biomass briquettes in crisis affected IDP camps and communities in Mafa and Gwoza LGAs in the state. The choice of LGAs is as a result of already existing stove interventions not getting to them as much as others. It will target at least 10,000 households in these LGAs for fuel efficient stoves and briquettes distribution and build local capacity on the production of these stoves and fuels. The use of briquettes is especially very important because it will turn waste to fuel. An assessment conducted by ICEED/FAO in 2019 estimates that 22,680 tons of waste are generated annually from sawmills, rice mills and sugarcane processing spots in Maiduguri and Jere LGAs. Utilizing these wastes to produce fuel will aid in waste management.

2 - Activities

The following major activities will be undertaken by the project: 1. Conduct assessment on household energy needs in selected LGAs - to further understand the level and scale of cooking energy poverty in the target LGAs, the project will conduct an assessment. The assessment will aim to identify among other things the economic and health implications of using traditional firewood stoves and options for switching to clean cooking solutions. 2. Establish stoves and briquettes production and training facilities - the project will establish facilities for the production of ceramics/metal based stoves and briquettes in the target LGAs. In siting the facilities, the project will look at access to raw materials, security and availability of trainable women and youths.2b. The production of briquettes depends largely on biomass waste such as sawdust, rice husk, sugarcane peels, sugarcane molasses, coconut shells etc which ordinarily constitutes waste and will greatly aid in waste management. The project will achieve a twin objective of producing sustainable biomass fuels from these waste materials as well as aid in waste management. 3. Capacity development for 200 women and youths on stoves and briquettes production - 200 women and youths will be selected based on set criteria and have their capacity built on the production of ceramics/metal based fuel efficient stoves. In addition they will be trained on how to produce both carbonized and non-carbonized briquettes. 4. Deliver fuel efficient woodstoves to at least 10,000 households - at least 10,000 households will be selected for the intervention based on the vulnerability criteria. The trained beneficiaries will produce and supply stoves to these households. 5. Deliver at least 500 tons of briquettes to stove beneficiary households - the stove beneficiary households will be supplied with at least 50kg of briquettes. The delivery of stoves and briquettes will be done simultaneously. 6. Public awareness programmes on SAFE - While these activities are ongoing, the project will conduct awareness campaigns on SAFE and the co-benefits of using fuel efficient stoves and briquettes. At least 100,000 persons will be reached with these campaigns. In addition to the awareness campaigns on SAFE, the project will sensitize the target population on the co-benefits of using household, agricultural and biomass processing waste materials to produce sustainable fuels.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will set up a down/up feedback mechanism in addition to horizontal communications. Field officers will be engaged by the project who will visit each of the project communities at least once a week. Progress, complaints and feedbacks will be related through them. In addition, the project will conduct periodic village level meetings and FGDs to understand the feelings of the communities on implementation of the project and opportunities for improvement.
