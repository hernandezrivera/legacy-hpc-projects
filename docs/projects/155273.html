Project Summary

Acknowledging the link between inter-generational poverty and malnutrition, WFP plans to implement gender and nutrition-sensitive cash-based activities targeting nutritionally vulnerable households to improve their food security and prevent malnutrition, with a specific focus on the 1,000 days window of opportunity. Cash transfers are combined with Social and Behaviour Change Communication (SBCC) to promote better nutrition, hygiene and child care practices as well as to empower women and girls and engage with men and boys to advance gender equality.

Needs

The last IPC analysis (October 2018) has highlighted that in drought-affected areas 30% of the population is currently in emergency or crisis phase and is thus likely to adopt negative coping strategies potentially affecting the nutritional status of the most vulnerable household members, pregnant and lactating women and girls (PLWGs) and children under 2. According to the same analysis, the global acute malnutrition prevalence is higher than the OMS threshold (5-10%) in all the analysed areas. At the same time, EMMUS IV results have shown that 22% of children are stunted; no progress has been made in terms of chronic malnutrition reduction since 2012.

Activities or outputs

Key activitiesProvision of gender and nutrition-sensitive, targeted seasonal food assistance to families with PLWGs and/or children aged 6-23 months. The provision of the assistance to beneficiaries during the critical months of the year (February to June) will entail a strong collaboration with the Government and in particular the MSPP, MAST, CNSA as well as with UNICEF and local partners with proven experience in nutrition and gender-sensitive programmes. Assessments will be conducted to design social and behavior change activities to promote healthy and age-appropriate eating habits and to address social (gender) norms as the root causes for food insecurity. Expected outputs- Food insecure populations receive cash based transfers to enable them to meet their basic food needs.- Nutritionally vulnerable populations receive social and behavior change communications to encourage consumption of age-appropriate, nutritiously diversified foods and meet their nutrition needs as well as to promote women and girls' empowerment and engage with men and boys to advance gender equality.
