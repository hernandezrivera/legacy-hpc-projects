Project Summary

This program aims at supporting the resilience of the conflict-affected people in northwest Syria, particularly in Idleb and Aleppo governorates. White Hands's proposed is designed to contribute to supporting the transition to the early recovery phase, as it supports the targeted communities with long-term assistance activities, programs that focus on building/restoring the communities' infrastructure, local economic empowerment, and initiatives that ensure engagement and participation of the community in recovering from the protracted crisis.
The program formulation has been based on needs identified by the Early Recovery cluster, supported with rapid needs assessments conducted by White Hands (WH) in January 2021 and firsthand consultations with camps management and local authorities/stakeholders.
WH’s early recovery program recognizes the different needs and vulnerabilities of boys, girls, men, and women of different ages and strives to address the specific needs of each group across the range of ERL interventions.
The plan aims to address multiple Early Recovery sector objectives through the following:
a) Improve access to camps and basic services for IDPs throughout upgrading/rehabilitation of 30 Km of main roads outside the camps which affected by poor weather conditions to alleviate the effects of flooding and prepare for winter and in need of rehabilitation (new asphalt layer) with all the manholes and drainage infrastructure. The project was designed to restore sections of the road network which is in poor condition, and at the same time to strengthen the planning, implementation, and management capacity of the local authorities, moreover, to develop further the emerging local road construction and maintenance industry, using mainly labor-based methods and generating job opportunities to assist with poverty alleviation.
b) Implementing sidewalks through cash for work approach next to the newly rehabilitated roads.
c) Rehabilitation of 20 schools.
d) Establishing or expanding Small-Micro Enterprises through the provision of business skills training and financial or in-kind support to 400 selected beneficiaries from IDPs and Host communities. Grant total will be between 600-1000 USD per beneficiary.
e) Providing vocational and skills training to 600 youth, men, and women applicants and then supporting successful trainees (Expecting 300 trainees) with small-scale cash grants to enable them to restart/initiate their small enterprises, each grant total will be between 600- 1000 USD per beneficiary.
f) Establishing 6 rescue teams and providing them with training and equipment in order to assist in responding to the flood risks.
g) Increase the resilience of IDPs and host community populations by generating new employment for 350 skilled and unskilled workers including women and PWDs (through supervisory roles) through the rehabilitation, maintenance, and cleaning work.
Model inclusion by aiming to have women and PWDs in supervisory roles (Under direct supervision by WH field engineers to ensure high-quality delivery). This could serve as a powerful message and inspiration.
WH will seek to hire PWDs and women mid-skilled workers and assign them lighter tasks or provide them CFW opportunities that enable their participation, such as CFW supervisors (Document and follow up on any discrepancies between the names reported on timesheets and workers on the site, CFW trainers, conduct periodic cleaning campaigns, data collection surveys, or sensitization sessions for the community members, etc.).
The program will seek feedback from both male & female beneficiaries and fully interact with both genders to ensure equitable access & equal opportunities.
The overall target number of beneficiaries is 124,200 individuals (31,990 women, 28,229 men, 32,931 girls, and 31,050 boys).
Note: 51% of this program will be implemented during 2022, while the other 49% are planned for 2023.

4 - Needs

According to ERL HNO 2022, overall severity has increased across Syria, with a corresponding increase in PiN from 2021 by 19% to 13.8M people. Of this, 6.9M (50%) are female & 3.8M (27.7%) are PWDs.One of the emerging vulnerable population groups that are composed of female-headed HHs, families with PWDs & IDPs but also HH seeking better or additional jobs and HH that are unable to cover the cost of minimum expenditure despite in some cases more than one member of the family is earning an income. This group can be defined as “working poor”.Support to livelihood alone has proven insufficient to cover the income-expenditure gap (estimated at 49%), also provision whenever possible of quality services (electricity, water, health & education) alone is unable to substantially reduce that gap therefore only a multi livelihood and services support timely and well-sequenced can provide the needed support.Finally, the level of dissatisfaction with humanitarian assistance among vulnerable Syrians has risen significantly from 2020 to 2021, particularly referring to the “quantity” of assistance received. Overwhelmingly, vulnerable Syrians request assistance that empowers them to meet their basic needs autonomously, thereby respecting their dignity and sense of self-worth. A continued failure to deliver this kind of assistance could significantly damage the credibility of humanitarian actors in Syria, which could ultimately hurt trust in aid actors. This could prejudice access.

5 - Implementation of activities

1. Upgrading/rehabilitation of 30 Km of main roads outside the camps in need of rehabilitation (new asphalt layer) with all the manholes and drainage infrastructure - Implementing sidewalks next to the newly rehabilitated roads - Rehabilitation of 20 schools - Establishing 6 rescue teams. The rehabilitation listed above will target damaged and neglected infrastructure to improve drainage capacity and access to basic services. The activities will include; community engagement, topographic surveys, developing the BoQs and the technical specification, HLP due diligence, procurement process, completing the repair and rehabilitation works via qualified contractors and supervising & ongoing monitoring for the implementation. Additionally, IDPs sites and nearby host communities around project locations will get access to 350 short-term livelihoods in the form of unskilled labor (CFW). This component will be guided by the CFW Recommended Minimum Actions in NWS guidance shared by the ERL cluster/CWG.2. Regarding small business activities, WH aims at supporting 1000 selected applicants with vocational training, receiving their business plans; based on the evaluation, a narrower group (approximately 700 beneficiaries) will receive the small cash grant. Both CFW and small business cash support will be in the voucher-based approach.Market assessment to identify if markets are functioning, what types of products are available, the cost of products and if markets are accessible.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk1: Lack of accepted documentation may unfairly exclude certain populations.Likelihood: M/Impact: H. MS: People are informed about their rights to receive assistance regardless of the documents they have, Training of the staff about the rights of the affected population to receive assistance, Presence, and information of complaints mechanism.Risk2: lack of approval from property owner which may prevent from implementing the activity. Likelihood: H/Impact: H MS: No start of the activity in the camp without obtaining approval from the landowner or the stakeholders, Include HLP documentation into the MoUs or agreements signed. Risk3:Tension between HC, IDPs & returnees. Likelihood: H/Impact: H. MS: Ensure that beneficiaries are selected based on vulnerability and not based on status, Selection criteria are transparent & groups are fully informed, Involve the local stakeholders throughout the process, Establish a feedback mechanism & conduct follow-up visits. Risk4: Exclusion of women, PWDs & other marginalized groups due to cultural/tribal norms. Likelihood: H/Impact: H. MS: Model inclusion by aiming to have women & PWDs in supervisory roles. This could serve as a powerful message & inspiration while engaging LCs and community representatives & considering having specific days/areas/labor schemes for women/PWDs.Risk5:Kidnapping/Abduction –MS: traveling in the daytime, transportation in convoys, high coordination on movement tracking, deterrence through local authorities.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk1: Sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment) of women and girls. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. MS: Establish a confidential complaint mechanism, Train staff on code of conduct, Code of conduct in all staff contracts& Address any SEA complaints.Risk2: The risks of GBV can be compounded by overcrowding and lack of privacy. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. MS: Advocate for appropriate and adequate assistance to be provided. We will include women, girls, and other at-risk groups to determine such things as if systems/criteria are in place to determine how shelters are being allocated; if they are built for safety and privacy, Consultations with women, girls, boys, and men should be organized &community safety audits.Specific attention concerning complaints/feedback mechanisms (CFMs) will be given to preventing sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Following the PSEA Network recommendations, WH's focal points will ensure the availability of information to the beneficiaries concerning CFMs through informative sessions at the beginning of the activities, making sure that beneficiaries are aware and engaged, if necessary in improving the design, monitoring, and review of the CFMs. As from PSEA indications, given the general community members' preference in having key institutions, such as hospitals/schools in running the CFMs, this opportunity will be duly explored and implemented where possible.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption1: WH has full access to the targeted communities & the beneficiaries regularly throughout the life cycle of the project. Risk: Escalation of conflict exacerbates insecurity in the targeted area affecting WH’s ability to operate and access it.Likelihood: M/Impact: H MS: WH will commit to ongoing monitoring to ensure the operational context is secure enough to ensure access to the targeted communities.Assumption2:The activities can be completed without delay. Risk: -Adverse weather conditions will affect the implementation of rehabilitation works. Probability: H/Impact: M MS: A fast track approach shall be followed to maximize the implementation of the rehabilitation works period, so there will be a flexible schedule. The rehabilitation works will be divided into 3 parts to be performed simultaneously by WH, hence ensuring fast delivery before winter arrives around November.Risk3: Spread of COVID-19 pandemic cases in NWS. Probability:M/Impact: M MS3: Follow strict preventive measures, building on the ERL guidelines and recommendations. Those will be reflected in each activity, All project teams and staff will receive sensitization on COVID-19 precautions. Risk4: For cash and vouchers there is the risk of corruption at the stage of beneficiary selection. L: M/I: H. MS: Strengthen over-sight mechanism, develop a range of measures & internal procedures to minimize the risk of corruption.
