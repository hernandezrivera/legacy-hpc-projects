Project Summary

WEFAQ in coordination with partner CBOs in Rafah and Khan-Yonis to implement specialized activities towards realizing the project objective to “enhance protection and respect of rights of most vulnerable women>18-59 and >59 years, girls 14-17 years including GBV women survivors with disabilities, elderly women, women with cancer (WWC) and children 6-12 years and increased their access to comprehensive and responsive multi-sectoral service provision based on IHL and IHRL. The main intervention related to Outcome level Indicator: through provide 500 psychological counseling and 6-8 psychosocial group support sessions to women based on the manual prepared and credited from GBV SC members in a participatory approach based on the severity of the cases via the free-line of WEFAQ beside direct service delivery, virtually according to beneficiaries choice of communication which keep privacy and safety for them or in person; legal consultations to 300 women and girls, and court representation to 75 women and married girls, distribution of 300 dignity kits according to GBV SC standards and based on vulnerability selection criteria, organize 2 community meetings to inaugurate the project, WEFAQ organizes sensitization sessions for 200 men and young men on gender, GBV issues and women rights, non-violent behaviours, including male engagement; training for WEFAQ and partner CBOs staff about PSEA protocols and complaints channels; training of staff to improve case management and referral to respond to highly sensitive cases, including incest, rape and sexual abuse within the family, and GBV perpetrated on women and girls with disabilities; refer (30) traumatized women and girls (3 WWDs)>18-59 and >59 years and 18 girls (1 GWD)<18 years to a psychiatrist and coordination with MHPSS, (10) women with disabilities to receive social services, case management services to 100 women from Rafah and Khan-Yonis; provide psychosocial support sessions for 300 affected children (150 girls /150 boys) of victims and survivors of GBV women who participated in the PSS. Refer (30) children 6-12 years to a speech therapist and (30) children 6-12 years with mild intellectual disabilities receive occupational therapy service from a special education specialist. Provide health care services for (200) women (12 WWDs, 6 WWBC)>18 years and (60) girls (6 GWDs)<18 years, WEFAQ refer to health centre/Awda in Rafah, WEFAQ provide a medical referral for 200 needed children to have medical services; Refer 6 cases of GBV women survivors in need of sheltering to Hayat centre, provide 240 child visitation/family meetings services for (divorced couples) with coordination with the Supreme` council of sharia courts which entails biweekly visits/meetings to WEFAQ; distribution of 100$ as CVA for 3 months to 240 WHHs through GBV case management to increase protection mainstreaming and decrease protection risks and keep in touch with CWG meetings and share it with MOSD and cash to avoid duplication. Organize inclusive recreational activities for 300 women and girls; 300 recreational and school kits will distributed to 300 schoolchildren (150 boys/150 girls). Outcome 2 activities: periodically meeting with the PC, GBV SC, CPC, organize 4 focus groups to measure the quality of services and engage the community; elaboration of a fact sheet documenting learned lesson to deal with GBV survivors in coordination with CC; 4 discussion meetings about received complaints, hard copy template or via hotline. WEFAQ ensures complementary of the project with previous and ongoing projects, advances participatory monitoring and reporting and MEAL plans adopts accountability to Affected Populations, regular monitoring visits by work team, external individual monitoring; realizes 2b gender marker and neutral environmental marker, ensures protection mainstreaming guarantees safe access to the project to cover the needs of the most marginalized localities within Rafah and Khan-Yonis.

1 - Needs

According to MSNA findings 2022, at the governorate-level, the highest proportion of households with at least one member with a physical and/or cognitive difficulty were among households in Rafah (19%). Final reach MSNA factsheet-July 2022 indicates that 16.1% of households in Rafah reporting that women and girls felt unsafe in certain areas in their location. Based on WEFAQ's main findings of the recent study about Rafah protection services reality/May 2022, which indicates: only 6.2% perceive that the Care services for abused women and children are appropriate and meet the needs, only 9.4% confirmed the availability of care services for elderly, and only 4.9% confirmed that Care services for elderly are appropriate and meet the needs, only 6.5% confirmed the availability of care services for people with disabilities, and only 5.2% confirmed that the care services for people with disabilities are appropriate and meet the needs and only 8.4% confirmed the availability of safe play area for children .The Main obstacles families face in obtaining services include: transportation cost (70.5%), cost of services (15.6%) and transportation cost (70.5%), cost of services (15.6%) and transportation difficulties (13.3%). A complex combination of factors consisting of deepening poverty and vulnerability, a lack of livelihood opportunities, the coercive environment created by the longstanding Israeli blockade, and internal political divisions continue to exacerbate humanitarian need in the Gaza Strip (HNO 2022). Owing to these circumstances, the need for basic assistance (cash and in-kind) remains high. With 73.2% of households in Gaza reporting having received any form of humanitarian aid or assistance (of which 92.7% received food assistance) in the 6 months prior to the MSNA data collection, the importance of considering accountability to affected populations is evident. The MSNA survey included a number of indicators specifically designed to assess AAP, and results of the MSNA across all sectoral and inter-sectoral indicators have disaggregated according to aid-recipient status of the household. Women and girls from families affected by casualties due to recent escalation August 2022 were at high risk of GBV particularly in the south of the Gaza Strip. UN Women published a document regarding GBV services in Gaza Gender alert. Their investigation showed clearly that the hardship and poverty exacerbate the GBV in the Gaza Strip. Their study also highlights the contributing factors of GBV in the Gaza Strip including perpetrators rarely facing legal penalties; violence against women being socially acceptable within marriage and a prevailing culture of silence to preserve marriages at the expense of victim's rights. Their examination recognized the need for formal training in GBV sensitization, capacity building and the support of income generation support for GBV survivors. The MSNA survey indicates that the impact of protection related incidents on the well-being of Gaza households is the observation that 40.0% of households reported at least one member of their household experiencing signs of psychosocial distress of trauma in the year prior to data collection. Specific population groups, including women, children, and persons with disability are considered particularly at risk for experiencing protection threats, with 25.3% of households identifying specific safety and security concerns for children, 38.4% of households identifying specific safety and security concerns for children with a disability, and 15.7% of households identifying specific safety and security concerns for women. In July 2020, the (PCBS) released that number of persons with disabilities was 255,228, constitute 5.8% of the total population in Palestine; 127,962 in the Gaza Strip 6.8%. The disability rate among males was 6.3% and 5.4% among females, and mobility are the most a common type of difficulties, which reached 32.2% with a prevalence of 2.9% among the population. In this context, persons with disabilities in Palestine, particularly in the Gaza Strip, face a number of challenges in acquiring basic human rights, obtaining basic services such as health, safe access to services and information, and poor access for persons with disabilities in the public and the governmental services centres. The most demanding issue is that Rafah is the only governorate without qualified hospital with few numbers of GBV actors and specialized organizations. Rafah is one of the governorates with the most refugee camps, where the number of camps exceeds 6 main refugee camps, and the socio-economic context adhering to customs and traditions that violate the rights of women, girls and children, which increases the frequency of violence in the absence of the ratification of the Family Protection Bill/Act, in light of the lack of providing specialized GBV services, the lack of funding to cover the needs of protection and child protection services within these marginalized populations in which women, girls and children suffer from violations of rights and the dominance of masculinity. Women are afraid to report an abuse and do not know where to get protective assistance from. Women who report GBV incidences and may have to leave their family to protect themselves from repeated GBV also suffer from additional risks as the lack of financial resources to cover their basic needs or food insecurity. The project targets women and girls with and without disabilities, who have been victims or are at risk of GBV in order to support them in recovering from GBV acts and targets children of these families in improving their lives and to protect them from (further) GBV. The risk of GBV includes physical, sexual, and psychological/emotional violence by husbands, fathers or other family members, different types of harassment, abuse and exploitation in public areas (e.g. to receive humanitarian aid), social exclusion and discrimination.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

In conflict areas, women and girls are particularly exposed to discrimination and violations of their rights. The project intends to reduce gender issues that are aggravated by the protracted protection crisis in the Gaza strip. It focuses on preventing and responding to GBV committed against women and girls with and without disabilities, strengthening their resilience, facilitating access for them to inclusive GBV-related services, sensitizing the community and building a safe environment for them. To achieve a sustainable impact, the project includes men and boys through sensitization on GBV prevention and protection of women and girls as well as through training in non-violent communication. Gender equality aspects are considered in project design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. The project will cover the most marginalized localities in the southern areas of the Gaza Strip. The project targets women and girls with and without disabilities, who have been victims or are at risk of GBV in order to support them in recovering, improving their lives and to protect them from (further) GBV. The risk of GBV includes physical, sexual, and psychological/emotional violence by husbands, fathers or other family members, different types of harassment, abuse and exploitation in public areas (e.g. to receive humanitarian aid), social exclusion and discrimination. Women are afraid to report an abuse and do not know where to get protective assistance from. Women who report GBV incidences and may have to leave their family to protect themselves from repeated GBV also suffer from additional risks as the lack of financial resources to cover their basic needs or food insecurity, cash has multi-purpose assistance keep privacy keep dignity. Women and girls with disabilities are particularly vulnerable and even more affected and at risk of GBV and the other risks mentioned above but have usually no access to inclusive GBV services.

To illustrate, continuous coordination with beneficiaries in delivering any kind of services.
