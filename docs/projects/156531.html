Project Summary

Throughout 2018, the health situation in Syria continued to deteriorate. People most at risk include people living with chronic diseases; those in need of physical rehabilitation and psychosocial support and; survivors of trauma, including the war wounded. High numbers of war injuries due to hostilities continue to be reported. Some 30 per cent of trauma injuries are reportedly linked with a permanent disability outcomes and the remaining survivors of trauma suffer temporary physical disabilities. This is of particular concern as intensified fighting throughout the crisis in various locations in Syria has led to an increase in the number of trauma cases, and an escalation of hostilities is anticipated across a number of areas throughout 2018. The possible continuation of hostilities in the coming months and resulting shifts in control could lead to further war injuries requiring acute trauma care. One in five Syrians are at risk of developing moderate mental health issues, and one in 30 is at risk of developing severe or acute mental health problems. Health services remain highly fragmented with no clear referral pathways for those who need specialized medical care. Referral systems for trauma are insufficient.
In accordance, Terre Des Hommes Italy is planning to run physical rehabilitation centers (Physiotherapy + PSS) with SARC relying on the experience of both organizations in the country, where SARC is running several rehabilitation programs, and TdH-IT currently has 2 centers running and has already equipped, ran, and conducted proper handover a center to SARC in Homs city.
TdH-IT is looking to target 2700 Persons with Disabilities with Physiotherapy, PSS services, and medical device assistance such as wheelchairs, crutches, mattresses, medical beds, etc. Beneficiaries will be selected through criteria mainly (Severity of Disability, number of family household, number of disabled persons in family, women headed HHs, PwDs headed HHs, in addition to other social criteria). These activities will be conducted through centers/mobile teams with SARC in Hama, Homs, Deir Ez zor Governorates, through specialized personnel of SARC and supported by TdH experts (local, and international) building the capacity of the local partner on Disability inclusion, Physiotherapy, PSS/PFA capacity building training.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: - Gender Based Violence GBVMitigating Measures- Specialized health staff should be trained on providing first-line support, clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals. - Identification of safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases connecting health services and clinical management with social services, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), in a manner guarantees safety, confidentiality, and privacy of the survivor. - Presence of female staff in health facilities increase access of women and girls to health services. Risk 2: Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA- Effective community based complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance.- Effective recruitment and performance management of all humanitarian staff. - Provide information to communities and people affected by crisis about the organization, the principles it adheres to, how it expects its staff to behave, the programmes it is implementing and what they intend to deliver.- Design and implement appropriate programmes based on an impartial assessment of needs and risks, and an understanding of the vulnerabilities and capacities of different groups, clear reporting and feedback mechanisms. Risk 3: Duty of CareMitigation Measures: - Staff Care programs which provide psychosocial support services and resources to staff. - Measures ensuring regular and consistent delivery of salaries, bearing in mind the ethical and logistical imperative to go further than basic labor rights.- Engagement with donors on the financial aspects of “duty of care” in early stages of project design to guarantee payment flexibility, and availability of severance payments in cases of unforeseen termination of contracts or re-location of the programs resulting from attacks on healthcare or change in control scenarios. Risk 4: Lack of Community Engagement and ParticipationMitigation Measures: Establishment of a common feedback mechanism utilizing available communication means, social media, in addition to the face-to-face approach- Establish local partnerships, building the capacity of partners to effectively engage with communities, and strengthen community based health services to facilitate dialogue with communities. - Regular follow-up and reporting on the results of accountability mechanisms in place.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: - Violence against Health Facilities and Healthcare ProvidersMitigating Measures-Continued reporting on attacks against healthcare, and enlisting support of the Strategic Steering Group (SSG), and humanitarian leadership in their efforts to highlight protections challenges facing health workers across Syria. - Reporting from relevant hubs regarding health situation in reconciled areas with continuous monitoring of health status of population in these areas especially in the immediate post-reconciliation phase.
