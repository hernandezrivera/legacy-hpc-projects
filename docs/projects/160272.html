Project Summary

The WASH response project is planned for a period of 12 months targeting vulnerable communities of IDPs, host communities and returnees in Ayod County including Kuacdeng Payam, Kodalong Buma, Wau Payam and Ayod Payam covering Kalibalek, Palith, Pulcholchany, Buol, Canal, Reang, Pilual, and Pankor Boma and villages with WASH, nutrition, food security and livelihood needs benefiting 25,000 people (6,000 men, 9,000 women, 6,000 Girls and 4,000 boys) in Ayod County. The planned cross-sectoral WASH activities will as well support nutrition clusters by creating synergies with nutrition and food security such as to reduce morbidity and mortality through famine prevention minimum package as well as preventing WASH-related disease outbreak in Ayod County, northern part of Jonglei State. This intervention is planned to Reduce Morbidity and Mortality and suffering from protection threats targeting vulnerable families and individuals, malnourished children including their mother/caretaker until the household level aiming to break the vicious circle of diarrheal diseases/nematodes with malnutrition through Rehabilitating/Repairing 20 broken down boreholes benefiting 800 men, 1,700 women, 1,400 Girls and 1200 boys in Kuacdeng Payam, Kodalong Boma, Wau payam and Ayod Payam covering Kalibalek, Palith, Pulcholchany, Buol, Canal, Reang, Pilual, and Pankor Bomas, Constructing 20 Comprehensive water points for cattle and small stock requirements during the dry season at the rehabilitated/Repaired boreholes benefiting 800 men, 1700 women, 1400 girls and 1,200 boys, Constructing 10 flood-proof hand-dug protected wells in 10 different Bomas benefiting 600 men, 850 women, 800 girls and 750 boys, Constructing 4 flood-proof shallow wells with solar-powered pump in Kodalong PHCU, Mogok PHCU, Pagil PHCU and Kuacdeng PHCU benefiting 400 women, 300 men, 200 boys and 300 girls, Rehabilitating the strategic community water point at Ayod PHCC, benefiting 100 men, 150 women, 120 boys and 130 girls, Elevated Water Tank, donkey cart filling and water kiosk provision in flood-prone Ayod Payam, Mogok Payam, Kuacdeng Payam and Wau Payam of Ayod County, Training 175 WASH committees (87 men and 88 women); each water point composes 7 members. The WASH Committees will be trained on better water resource management, HHWT use, maintenance of water facilities, Training 20 wells operators composing (10 men and 10 women). They will be trained on O&M and collection of water revenues for the sustainability of the water source. As a cross-cutting, community members will also be trained on DRR and conflict resolution mechanisms (water resources conflict mostly occurs among the communities due to its scarcity during the dry seasons), Rehabilitating gender sensitive flood proof desludging 4 twin block latrines (2 for male and 2 for female) in 4 PHCUs connected to the water system benefiting (1000 women, 700 men, 800 girls and 700 boys), Conducting CLTS activities with the participation of men, women & children to reduce Open Defecation, and Conducting comprehensive Hygiene promotion and mobilization on AWD/Cholera prevention measures to 14,000 AWD/Cholera risk men, women, boys and girls through the house to house, schools, health and nutrition centres, using trained 50 Community hygiene promoters (20 men and 30 women), Training 50 community Hygiene Promoters (30 women and 20 men) to conduct house to house, schools, health and nutrition centres hygiene promotions, Supporting to promotion of HHWT, safe water collection and storage to AWD risk men, women, boys and girls through the distribution of 1200 Hygiene kits to 800 HHs and 200 caretakers/mother of malnourished child during discharge from PHCCs and PHCUs; each Hygiene kit contains (200 aqua tabs, 1 Jerry can (20L), 1 bucket, 5 bars of soap). . This project comes at the right time when Ayod county is in critical WASH need due to Flooding. PASS will address needs of the most vulnerable people especially women and girls

1 - Needs

The increased IDP population in Ayod County both static and in transit has exerted pressure to already limited resources among the host communities mainly Women and girls and children under 5 years hence limiting the coping abilities to withstand the shocks. The Critical health needs, insufficient food access, high cases of SAM and GAM, and poor WASH services may contribute high levels of morbidity and mortality if not responded to rapidly. The only few functioning boreholes cannot cover the water needs of the community hence there is need for rehabilitation/repair of the broken ones; Open defecation is still a big challenge as well as cattle defecation downstream to house hold level and there is limited knowledge of community members on WHZ, respiratory, AWD, eye infection and cholera transmission. The project underscores the need to close the WASH facilities gap at PHCCs and revitalization of services to the community compounded with nutrition deficiency.From the Needs assessment conducted by PASS in Kalibalek Boma, Palith Boma, Pulcholchany Boma, Buol Boma, Canal Boma, Reang Boma, Pilual Boma, Kuacdeng Boma, Kotdalong Boma and Pankor Boma of Ayod County in June 2019 revealed the lack of safe, equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral basic services; as animals openly defecate into stream water; due to non-functionality of the boreholes and lack of access to water purifying supplies, inadequate excreta disposal and poor hygiene practices had left large portion of displaced populations with high number of women and girls at persistent risk of preventable water-related diseases. Ayod County previously has been prone to Cholera Outbreak during the rain season coupled with the seasonal flood experienced in these locations. Existing WASH infrastructure does not have the capacity to provide adequate WASH services to the conflict affected people in Ayod County. Inadequate WASH services contribute not only to disease outbreak, but also to increased SAM cases. Lack of improved sanitation and limited knowledge of hygiene practices of women and girls contributes to poor feeding habits of children that contribute to malnutrition. Several data from IOM, OCHA and partners revealed that displaced persons are settled in areas without clean water or improved sanitation and the have little opportunity to practice proper hygiene in what turns out to be flooded areas for the bigger part of the year during rainy season. The needs assessment conducted by REACH reported that Hygiene and sanitation practices were generally found to be poor in Ayod County with a range of latrine facilities used, ranging from locally built to permanent facilities. Therefore this project comes at a critical moment to address some of the priority WASH needs in this County.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

The project takes a human rights approach to addressing the risks faced by the targeted conflict affected people. For instance it pays recognition to people with disabilities access to WASH as a human right. On 28 July 2010, through Resolution 64/292, the United Nations General Assembly explicitly recognised the human right to water and sanitation and acknowledged that clean drinking water and sanitation are essential to the realisation of all human rights. Additionally, this resolution calls on international organisations to help provide safe, clean, accessible and affordable drinking water and sanitation for all. The UN Convention on the Rights of people with disabilities also mandates accessibility as a human rights’ issue and changing lives of people with disabilities through several articles that call for states to make public and private infrastructure accessible for people with disabilities. Additionally, the new Sustainable Development Goals (SDG) includes a proposed goal to ‘ensure availability and sustainable management of water and sanitation for all’. (SDG Goal 6: Clean Water and Sanitation) The project is also guided by the Sphere Project Handbook (2011) that sets out minimum standards in disaster response and includes approaches for meeting the needs of people with disabilities. Disability is addressed as an issue cutting across all the main sectors, including water supply, sanitation, nutrition, food aid, shelter, and health services.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

The risk of exposure of displaced women and girls to sexual violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements is to be mitigated by the project team working closely with protection committees when issues of GBV or child abuse are encountered. Secondly, the project will have separate sanitation facilities for female and male users, with visible demarcation erected. These Latrines will be located closer to homesteads at minimum distances, to ensure that women do not have to travel far to access services. Risk of interference of external stakeholders in impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries is to be mitigated through formation of beneficiary elected committees that are to oversee are play an effective role in the project implementation right from beneficiary identification, WASH NFIs distribution management, monitoring and accountability(AAP). The risk of Contamination of targeted project areas with explosive hazards is to be addressed through close collaboration with the protection cluster in creating awareness about safety measures to take regarding to UXOs within the project areas

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

AAP is a key aspect in the proposed WASH intervention in Ayod. The community will effectively be fully engaged right from project inception to trigger community ownership of the planned interventions. A series of well thought through interactions will be initiated to provide platforms for stakeholders to be consulted and project implementation information at both inception and information sharing workshops that will include Jonglei state and Ayod county government authorities to include the County Health Department, INGOs and NNGOs from various sectors/clusters active in Ayod in response to the humanitarian situation. Beyond stakeholder meetings at information sharing workshops, Ayod communities (to include Kuacdeng Payam, Kodalong Buma, Wau Payam and Ayod Payam covering Kalibalek, Palith, Pulcholchany, Buol, Canal, Reang, Pilual, and Pankor Boma) are to be consulted at payam levels where the project implementation is planned to include community outreach information and awareness raising sessions, use of IEC materials (with illustrations), community media live talk shows and Focus Group Discussions. PASS is to ensure active involvement of the floods and conflict affected people (Women, girls, boys, men, and persons with disabilities, IDPs, Returnees and host communities) through the project phase from planning, implementation, monitoring and reporting by introducing community friendly monitoring tools. PASS will leverage regular periodic (monthly and quarterly) community dialogues/consultative meetings to take note of all feedback on key WASH concerns that the affected persons/beneficiaries have raised and apply the acquired information to inform project implementation moving forward. There is to be an open policy on AAP and at any time even outside scheduled feedback and information sessions, PASS is to be receptive to feedbacks and well positioned to account to the affected persons and as well inform project performance reporting to the WASH Cluster.
