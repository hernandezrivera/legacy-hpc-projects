Project Summary

This project will support community-based protection in North Darfur, South Kordofan and Gadaref states. The project will identify through community consultation to support returnees and host community, to avail humanitarian assistance for sustainable life-saving and Strengthen the protection environment for . The project will increased access to basic services to sustain socio-economic (re)integration and local economic development for returnee, IDPs and hosting communities in the project location.
The project will identify critical economic recovery issues and strategies that support outcome-driven and assist people through provision of legal assistance, psychosocial support, emergency cash assistance to mitigate protection risks, and referral to GBV and Child Protection. such as interdependencies to build short-term economic relief and establishing long-term economic resilience for target host communities. In addition, community-based protection will continue to be a key element of the project’s response in the target states - North Darfur , South Kordofan and Gadaref - This will include support to community-based protection networks (CBPNs), community-based dispute resolution mechanisms, youth networks and women’s protection networks, in return villages areas. Community support projects driven, small to medium size infrastructure initiatives- will serve to foster social cohesion between IDPs, returnee and host communities.
The project will support deliver high-quality protection assistance, particularly in the areas of protection monitoring, case management, psycho-social support, legal assistance, GBV, community-based dispute resolution, and advocacy. Protection Information Management (PIM) systems will be reinforced to implement protection strategy.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project will consider the natural disasters and conflicts that had led to mass-movements of people, which has direct and indirect environmental consequences. Communities and their livelihoods are vulnerable to shocks such as drought or conflict when environmental resources are scarce and/or unevenly distributed. Integrating environmental issues into humanitarian programming is essential to improve outcomes and create multiple benefits. Reducing the pressure on natural resources can support livelihoods through job creation opportunities, nutrition and protection (by reducing lengthy walks for firewood collection), safeguard drinking water quality and quantity and provide shelter building materials. Human impact on the environment in Sudan is most visible in the areas of deforestation/ desertification, overcultivation and over-grazing, and over-extraction of water.The project will continue promoting trees planting, replace the use of fired bricks, awareness raising on environmental protection and support Sold Waste Management schemes, and train communities on Biodegradable material, include usage of fuel efficient cooking techniques (e.g. pre-soaking beans, sheltering cooking fires) , enable National authorities to take the lead, and coordinate with WES on Integrated Water Resource Management (IWRM) on monitoring of water levels.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementationThe project will consider : promoting safety and dignity and avoid causing harm such as installing lights near the latrines, especially these are communal or away from homes or alternatives such as providing torches for each household to ensuring meaningful access, accountability and participation and empowerment of vulnerable. And also like not place facilities near possible perpetrators. N.B. The police and armed forces are often seen as perpetrators of violations. Whether they provide a reassuring feeling or instill fear depends on the location. It is important to consult the community and potential beneficiaries about their preferences.- Consider ways to reduce/manage/cope with these threats, including location, accompaniment and arrange appropriate policing if required (as water monitors)- If an individual does not feel safe or dignified in using the latrine, (s)he may go elsewhere, potentially exposing themselves or others to harm.- Physically separate and label the latrines “male” & “female”- Ensure meaning full access such as latrine design accounts for children (size of pits may present a safety risk for children)- It is preferable that latrines and showers can be locked from the inside to ensure privacy. - Consider that women and children are the most likely to be collecting water, and might be waiting in the sun for hours for a turn to fill their jerry can. Establish safe and shaded waiting areas where possible. - Schedule water collection times to limit overcrowding at the water points; collection in shifts.- Children should not carry jerrycans. However, assuming they will be forced to do so, make sure that Jerry cans are not so large as to cause injury. - Ask individuals collecting water when they would prefer taps/pumps to be open. Recognize that different people have different work schedule, women and girls who are typically the water collectors have specific times when they are busy making meals.- Ensure that all are participate in project design and empower to participate in discussions on latrine/shower design with various groups and adapt the design. latrine/shower design with various groups and adapt the design
