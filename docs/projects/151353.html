Project Summary

The Project interventions is targeting 14,970 ( 5660 girls, 5110 boys and 4200 PLW's) in 4 districts in Lower Shabelle region Merka, Qoryoley, Kurtunwarey &Wenlaweyn districts, The populations being targeted are IDP's &Vulnerable hosted communities especially families with malnourished children .
Through this project Nutrition activities will be carried out in the sense of sectoral response of the bellow activities in the program Implementation
-Strengthening 11 OTP's and 4 Mobile Screening, admitting &providing RUFT to 5660 girls, 5110 boys(15%) of severely malnourished <5 children boys,girls, Provide Vitamin A,deworming,multiple micronutrient &iron folic tablets to vulnerable <5 children 5660 girls, 5110 boys and 4200 PLW's &lactating women SAM &MAM cases admitted, Conducts immunization services through static outpatient therapeutic centers as well as the outreach mobile team to ensure under five children were immunized , Conduct community &outreach-based IYCF promotion in target project sites, Conduct 16 promotion sessions on nutrition, health &hygiene practices, Maternal child health &nutrition (MCHN) promotion messages, Provided 5 days training on Nutrition,hygiene &health promotion to 50 technical nutrition staff (24 male &26 female)
AYUUB will coordinate closely with regional sub clusters of Nutrition through monthly sub cluster meeting in Lower Shabelle to enable it share its program progress, lesson learnt and challenges as well as the existing gaps in line with proposed intervention. AYUUB will also coordinate with Federal ministry of health and WHO for technical guidelines and supportive supervision.
AYUUB will demonstrate their commitment to accountability to affected populations by ensuring feedback and accountability mechanisms are integrated into organizations’ strategies, programme proposals, monitoring and evaluations, recruitment, staff inductions, trainings and performance management, partnership agreements, and this will be highlighted in reporting. AYUUB will promote accountability and provide information to the MOH and donors about the progress of the programme in terms of project activities and financial.
AYUUB will collect gender disaggregated data by sex and age of relevant data related to Nutrition activities. AYUUB will ensure that all segments of the affected population including women have equal access to nutrition assistance and that targeted support to advance gender equality is based on a gender analysis.
AYUUB shall apply gender segregated community consultation meetings enabling women and girls to address their specific concerns, prioritize their needs and appropriately inform the development of proposed activities.

Needs

Somalia is one of the top ten countries with the highest prevalence of malnutrition in the world1, and the third highest in the eastern and southern Africa region, 1.4 Million people (49% % male, 51% % female) are in Need Humanitarian assistance. According to the Nutrition Cluster reports 954,007 children are expected to be acutely malnourished, including 173,594 who are expected to be severely malnourished during the next one year period (Sep 2018-Sep 2019). The surveys indicate Critical levels of GAM (15-29.9%) in 12 out of 33 populations surveyed (7 IDP and 5 Rural) while Critical levels of SAM prevalence (≥4-5.6) were only seen among IDPs in Mogadishu and Guban pastoral. Similarly, the Crude Death Rate (CDR) rates are Critical (1 to <2) in 3/33 population groups surveyed while CDR rates are Serious (0.5 to <1) in 11/32 and U5DR rates (1 to 1.9) in 6/32 population groups surveyed, respectively. Furthermore, Morbidity rates are high (>20 %) in many parts of the country but highest among population groups surveyed in northeast and central parts of the country. Lower Shebelle region is among the regions that are experiencing severe drought causing to affect poor urban and IDPs integrated in the host communities putting pressure on limited resources available. Therefore, acute malnutrition rate is high. The majority of affected people are women, children and elderly. Lifesaving humanitarian assistance and livelihood support are vital between now and December 2018 to help meet immediate needs to 14970 (5660 girls, 5110 boys and 4200 PLW's) in Merka, Qoryooleey, Kurtunwarey and Wenlaweyn districts as targeted in this project.In Sep 2018, the communities in Marka district of Lower Shabelle experienced displacement and eviction after AS kicked out by FG aligned with AMISOM Troops, this affected the surrounding villages in Merka district.The need of the IDPs is to access life saving Nutrition services to reduce Malnutrition rate by providing quality OTP Service.

Activities

Activity 1 Regular identification of acutely malnourished children U5 and PLWs including throughMothers MUAC approach in 4 Mobile and 11 OTP's in Merka, Kurtunwarey, Wenlaweyn and Qoryoley districts for 14970 ( 5660 girls, 5110 boys and 4200 PLW's) Activity 2Micronutrient support for vulnerable groups (PLW and U5 children) e.g Vitamin A &MMN in 2 Mobile and 5 OTP's In Merka and Qoryoley district as per rationalized for 14970 ( 5660 girls, 5110 boys and 4200 PLW's) Activity 3Therapeutic feeding support for treatment of acute malnutrition cases for PLW and U5 children 14970 ( 5660 girls, 5110 boys and 4200 PLW's) in Merka, Wenlaweyn,Kurtunwarey and Qoryoley districts Activity 4Integrated multi sectorial Nutrition, Health, Hygiene (NHHP) preventative, Food Securityand promotional support for 14970 ( 5660 girls, 5110 boys and 4200 PLW's) in Merka, Wenlaweyn, Kurtunwarey and Qoryooley districts.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The Project is delivering Nutrition services to all affected population, by giving priority gander and age equality, regardless their affiliation or background, which can significantly contribute in the improvement of beneficiaries’ protection. Beside this, the areas where the project is delivering the services is safe and full control of SNA forces, secondly, AYUUB had a good planning in the project design stage, related with the selection of facilities sites, away from military base of forces checkpoint, stations or governmental premise, this makes the likelihood of protection risks occurring very rare. But there can be small risks theft of RUTF from the beneficiaries during their return from the facilities to their houses, but this will managed by any means. In terms of general security in the area, there can be armed clashes between AS and SNA forces, usually results from AS hit and run attacks to the SNA points, AYUUB by applying its Risk Measurement and Mitigation policy, will mitigate the risks of protection for the beneficiaries and staff through awareness of the area security situation during the IYCF, Health Education and Hygiene promotion sessions conducted in the facilities.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The Project is targeting all different ages and gender of the affected population in the project areas, with health care services, the services will equally reach to all population who access the facilities and need for treatment, there will not be risks of exclusion based in displacement expected in the project services, as AYUUB had a clear policy in the project design and planning phases, by capturing a better estimated population with all respective ages and ganders, in consultation with the community and local authority in the needs assessment.among the additional mechanisms to prevent such risks is the monitoring and evaluation system that will go with the implementation process to disclose all alteration from the planned activities of the project for correction and timely improvement

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Basically, the areas where the project is planned to serve is fully controlled by SNA forces, as part of AYUUB policy which was revised after the AS banned NGOs in their held territories, the organization doesn’t deliver visible services like health and Nutrition in the AS controlled areas to avoid looting and staff detention, therefore, impacts of armed conflicts are rarely likelihood in the services areas, except small things like that one of AS hit and run launch against SNA bases.To mitigate such risks, AYUUB had a good plan in the site selection of the facilities, by having MCHs where the beneficiary’s access in away from SNA bases or governmental facilities, to avoid impacts of armed conflict on the beneficiaries, staff and facilities as well
