Project Summary

The project aims to reduce the threat from Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) to Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and returnees/host communities in Syria through the delivery of Risk Education (RE) and detailed/basic contamination surveys. And to support the protection needs of IDPs and returnees across the whole of Syria by reducing the threat from landmines and ERW through the delivery of RE and gathering basic contamination data.

Needs

• The widespread use of various types of weaponry in Syria has left behind a range of explosive hazards including improvised explosive devices (IEDs), landmines, and explosive remnants of war (ERW) across the country. An estimated 10.2 million civilians live in the 1,980 communities reporting contamination by explosive hazards. Each hazard poses its own risks, contributing to civilian casualties, exacerbating vulnerabilities, hindering safe access to humanitarian support, basic services and livelihoods, and ultimately compounding the humanitarian crisis. • While all population groups are vulnerable to the threat of explosive hazards, certain groups are at a higher impact in correlation with gender and age, as well as social roles and responsibilities. Farming, herding, moving or travelling, tampering or playing with items, and rubble removal are reportedly the activities most exposed to threat. Men and boys are more directly at risk from the immediate threat of explosive hazards, while women and girls are more impacted by indirect threat and long-term consequences of contamination, particularly by exacerbating vulnerabilities. In addition to physical and psychological impact, explosive hazard contamination can have severe socio-economic implications on families and communities, depriving them of livelihoods and access to existing services. • Based on available data, an average of 1.5 people is killed and 2 are injured in each reported explosive accident, with approximately 1 in 3 survivors suffering at least one limb amputation. The growing number of casualties from explosive accidents has steadily increased pressure on the health sector, in particular, on the provision of specialized services and assistance for persons with disabilities, including survivors of explosive hazard accidents. • Given the extensive threat posed by explosive hazards, humanitarian mine action activities can and should be more strongly integrated into other sectors. Key infrastructure such as roads, housing, schools, health centres, and water/sanitation systems have been contaminated as a result of the conflict. Those which have not been destroyed remain unsafe for use because of contamination. Lost access to large quantities of productive land for livelihoods and settlement only serves to reinforce poverty, further destabilise communities and undermine opportunities for recovery.

Implementation of activities

• Our activities will raise the community awareness about the dangers of ERWs and in the same time will provide support to victims through PFA, PSS sessions. • RE facilitators will work on providing lifesaving RE messages and at the same time, the psychologists will provide PFA and PSS to the survivors from ERWs accidents. On the other hand, these activities will achieve at least 90 % of the cluster objective in reducing the impact of ERWs through RE activities. • ERWs victims will be addressed by our psychologists with PFA and PSS sessions. We will select considering “vulnerability criteria” (200) victims of physically disabled victims to be involved in “cash support”. Those will be selected and registered as cases to follow up with them after receiving the support. Our teams will buy the materials for each case that will help to start a small project guarantees an appropriate income to the victim’s family. Our activities will also include physical, psychosocial and socio-economic support for persons with disabilities, including individuals affected by explosive hazard incidents. • Those (200) victims will be included in Cash Assistant program. Our activities will raise the community awareness about the dangers of ERWs and in the same time will provide support to victims through PFA, PSS sessions and assistance by providing some materials (such as tool box, sewing equipment, small project starter kit … etc.) to start a simple career and depend on themselves. • Based on our vision, the main points to be taken into consideration during implementing the activities are: Pay equal remuneration, including benefits, for work of equal value Ensure that workplace policies and practices are free from gender-based discrimination Implement gender-sensitive recruitment and retention practices and proactively recruit and appoint women to managerial and executive positions within the project. Assure sufficient participation of women – 30% or greater – in decision-making and governance at all levels of project life-cycle Offer flexible work options, leave and re-entry opportunities to positions of equal pay and status

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The outputs are based on two primary assumptions. First, that awareness about mines and ERWs continue to be critical. Second, that we will deliver safety messages and instructions to at risk populations throughout the implementation period. Insecurity remains the most significant risk to the project, and we have identified several mitigation strategies to reduce the impact of these risks on program implementation, including: consistent community outreach and engagement, focusing on training for CFPs to ensure continuity of awareness, flexibility of moving our activities to the safe communities.If critical changes occur in the authorities that control our targeted areas, we are commitment to meet employees’ interest of moving towards the north. Our policy allows the employee to receive two salaries in advance if the conditions require such an action. Do No Harm will remain an integral principle. In order to minimize the potential adverse effects to the targeted communities and humanitarian workers while providing our services and interventions, we carried out a thorough forecasting of likely risks that may be caused while delivering its strategy and developed a framework with mitigation measures. These mitigation measures include the development and use of standardized approaches, services packages and tools when delivering services, community engagement and sensitization about targeting, and the adoption of a flexible delivery modality with adjustable distribution schedules.
