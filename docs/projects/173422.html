Project Summary

Save the Children (SC) will operate an integrated child protection programme in Cox’s Bazar and aim at strengthening protective factors and reducing risk factors at every level of the socio-ecological framework. In 2021, through partnership with 3 local NGOs, SC will maintain critical interventions and services including case management, adolescents and PSS programming, with a focus on protecting the most vulnerable children, including UASC, child headed households, married and house bound girls and children with disabilities. Interventions will seek to address ongoing risks posed by COVID-19 and target the most vulnerable boys, girls and their families based on evidence and input from affected communities. Child participation, eliciting the views of boys and girls across age, ability and protection concern will be an integral part of designing, implementing and monitoring of programme components. SC will also continue to enhance capacity of parents and caregivers on positive parenting as well as strengthening the community based child protection mechanism for creating an enabling and protective environment for children in both camps and host communities. Through maintaining and establishing services in both refugee camps and host communities, critical gaps in service delivery will be addressed, and social cohesion strengthened, by targeting inequity of access to services which is a driver of conflict. In 2021, SC will place a greater emphasis on supporting the host communities and especially focus on addressing the worst forms of child labor and supporting indigenous children. Moreover, SC will focus on strengthening the child protection systems that support a child. This will include partnerships in an effort to strengthen technical capacity, and work closely and collectively with duty bearers and service providers in both camps and host communities to see children's rights realized. Multiple accountability mechanisms will be applied to ensure that feedback is actively collected, whilst ensuring the safety and dignity of the beneficiaries.

1 - Identified Needs

More than three years since the influx, the Rohingya refugee crisis remains a protection emergency at its core. The fragile protective environment for boys and girls continues to deteriorate - prior to displacement in 2017, Rohingya children already faced risks and threats to their wellbeing; these have now been systematically exacerbated by the humanitarian crisis and Covid-19. The needs of boys and girls in the surrounding host community in many ways echo the needs of those residing in the camps (SDR, 2020). Both refugee and host community children are struggling to cope in the current climate, facing decreased access to services, stressors in the home caused by loss of income and increased anxiety. With children comprising of 55% of the 860,000 Rohingya Refugees (UNHCR; September 2020) and children making up an estimated 40% of the host community (UNICEF, 2020), it is critical that their needs are understood and met. Since the start of Covid-19 we have seen an increase in child labour, violence against children, dangers and injuries, child marriage, risk of trafficking, exploitation and abuse (CPSS, 2020). Inevitably, the gender implications for these risks vary for girls, boys and children with non-binary gender identities (RGA, 2020). Child labour reportedly increased in April (PWG, April 2020) with boys working in construction, driving tom toms and working at tea stalls and vegetable shops. Girls have increasingly resorted staying at home for fear of sexual harassment (REACH, May 2020). While staying in their shelter is cited as a coping mechanism, 38% of adolescents reported that they were concerned about feeling unsafe in their shelter. The same trends have been reported in the host community with an increase in child labour and child marriage. (SDR, 2020). Interestingly, caregivers and adolescents reported being against child marriage, however an exception to this belief was made if the safety of the girl was believed to be at stake. 17% of adolescents and 25% of caregivers believe that it is then acceptable to arrange a marriage (REACH, May 2020). This is exceptionally worrying as we anticipate that the perceived “safety” of girls to decrease during and as a result of the pandemic (RGA, 2020). The needs of the host community in many ways reflect those of the refugee population with 49% of households reporting an increase in child labour and a 20% increase in child marriage (J-MSNA 2020). Additional protection risks spanning age, gender and ability include trafficking, violence, children in conflict with the law and limited education opportunities especially during Covid-19. Children are at risk of exploitation and can be susceptible to offers of dangerous onward movement to escape harsh conditions. Since the start of the pandemic, children have experienced an increase in physical violence. This is amongst a backdrop of norms that support violence in the home – in a Consultation with Rohingya Children, 90% of caregivers agreed that it was acceptable for parents to use violence to discipline their children. In regards to general safety, both boys and girls cited feeling unsafe in the markets, latrines, and distribution centres (REACH, May 2020). In host communities, violence, petty crime, bullying, harassment, abduction, inadequate lighting in latrines and bathing facilities are among the reasons mentioned by females for feeling unsafe in certain locations in their neighborhoods. Although the long-term impacts of both the disease and implications of Covid-19 are unknown, assessments point to a grim situation for children with significant long-lasting impacts on their development and wellbeing. Now, more than ever there is a critical and urgent need for life-saving child protection services in both host community and refugee populations.

2 - Activities or outputs

(a) Case Management: Activities include support to alternative care arrangements for unaccompanied and separated children (including foster families), basic counselling support to children and their families, provision of family tracing and reunification service, and referrals to specialist referral partners, including health, food, security and livelihoods. (b) Strengthening the protective environment: Targeted parents and caregivers (of children registered for case management services, including UASC) and caregivers of children at risk will be supported to create a safe and violence free home environment and address the unique risks faced during Covid-19. (c) Mental health and psychosocial support interventions: Curriculum based structured and focused psychosocial support for children including children with disabilities will be provided through multi-purpose centers for adolescent and children (MPCAC). (d) Adolescent resilience and empowerment: Targeted adolescent girls and boys will be identified and referred to engage in gender specific life skills curriculums, resilience sessions, and protective behavior sessions. A network of adolescent clubs will also be continued to support peer to peer mobilization, recreational activities for other adolescents, and on safe and supported peace building activities.(e) Community based protection: Save the Children (SC) will continue to support a network of Community Based Child Protection Committees, adolescent boys and girls clubs, and children’s clubs, in and around our protection spaces and targeted host communities. (f) DRR and environment: Child friendly DRR messages and awareness raising will be mainstreamed through all protection spaces, community led responses to family separation will be strengthened and staff/volunteers trained to activate a safe network of ‘Lost Child Caregiver Meeting Points’ and ‘Temporary Family Shelters’, and to follow SOPs for reunification. Accordingly, funds are dedicated in the project for repairs and upgrades of protection spaces.(g) System strengthening: Following the socio-ecological model, SC aims to enhance the capacity of local partners, support to inter-agency systems strengthening initiatives, such as capacity building of law enforcement, CiCs, military and border officials and DSS social workers on best practices in GBV/child protection case management and supportive supervision. (h) Sustainability: By focusing on enabling and empowering community based structures and groups, and their role within referral pathways and the wider system, Save the Children will promote community ownership, capacity and responsibility for child protection in target locations. (i) Accountability: Children are engaged in project design through FGDs and participatory approaches. SC will roll out child-friendly response mechanisms that were piloted in 2019 (in addition to a toll-free hotline, helpdesks, group discussions, and door to door collection of feedback and complaints). (j) PSEA and CSG: Save the Children has prioritized maintaining a robust internal mechanism for PSEA and child safeguarding, consisting of codes of conduct, mandatory induction trainings of staff/volunteers/partners, refresher trainings, and development and monitoring of risk registers, and capacity building of PSEA and CSG focal points. (k) Coordination: In delivering this project, Save the Children (SC) will continue to be an active contributor to the child protection sub sector, anti-trafficking working group, case management-working group, GBV case management-working group, GBV Cluster (national level), and emergency response/DRR core group. (l) Participation and Empowerment: The project is designed to promote equal access to participation opportunities, through taking gender considerations into account for all community outreach and facility-based activities.
