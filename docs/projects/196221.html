Project Summary

Space of Peace Organization (SOP) will deliver multi-sectoral, integrated, and coordinated humanitarian intervention under three sectors: Early Recovery ER, and health. The project will take place in Idleb Governorate, Ariha city.
The project aims to respond to livelihood, health, and protection needs through integrated primary healthcare, ER activities. The project focuses on the most vulnerable groups (women, Under 18 children, IDPs, chronically ill patients, elderly persons, and PWDs). The vulnerability criteria per cluster will be defined according to the specific needs. Mutual vulnerable groups will be identified and defined as the most vulnerable groups. To achieve the project objectives, SOP will have already Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSS) and Child-Friendly Space (CFS) in the same area in Ariha.
Additionally, SOP will support the ongoing health services delivered through its Primary Health Center (PHC) in the exact location of the WGSS and CFS, Ariha. The center will provide essential health services, including medical consultations, referral services, and medicine distribution. The ER services will be delivered to increase the resilience of the target communities by supporting the socioeconomic conditions of the target people.
Coordination:
The modality of service delivery will be according to the clusters' guidelines and SOPs and technical working groups' instructions, and the reporting will be under the reporting mechanism of each cluster.
Project Activities:
ERL: 1- Provide vocational and skills training opportunities. 2- Support business entrepreneurship initiatives through training. 3- Support business entrepreneurship initiatives through small grants. 4- Training on soft skills.
Priority will be placed in the criteria for selecting beneficiaries, so that the vulnerable groups are at the forefront of the people who are selected if they have the required qualifications, especially women and people with disabilities, regardless of the type of disability that exists, but so that it does not prevent the beneficiary from being able to effectively participate in the activities. The SOP center is equipped with access for people with disabilities, and their needs will be studied to provide the center with any additional needs that facilitate access. In addition, women, the elderly, people with disabilities, and other vulnerable groups are an essential part of the needs assessment study.
Health: 1- Deliver medical consultations, including general medicine, internal medicine, and maternity and reproductive health services. 2-Deliver multisectoral referral services (health, protection, ERL). 3-Provide the beneficiaries with basic medicine according to the case diagnosis and medical prescriptions. 4-Conduct light rehabilitation to enhance access of elderly people and PWDs to the center. 5- training the health staff on GBV first-line response, IPC, and cholera CM.
M&E
SOP will monitor the project through its team, in balance in male and female staff, the M&E field team will adhere to SOP PSEA and protection mainstreaming guidelines throughout the delivery of protection, ER, and health services.
The activities will proportionally target the BNFs to ensure that all the groups' needs are reflected and addressed. The M&E team will target women, boys, girls, and men in the PHC centers. Furthermore, the ER beneficiaries will be targeted by a special questionnaire, the M&E team will have direct interviews and hold FGDs with the beneficiaries.
The information system in the protection and health centers will be assessed and enhanced based on any defects to ensure comprehensive data collection.

3 - Needs

This project will contribute to achieving the strategic objectives of the HRP in NWS. Supporting this project is of critical importance for the affected people nowadays in NWS because it will contribute to strengthening their resilience. In addition, supporting the basic and life-saving humanitarian services is crucial at this stage of the crisis in Syria, preparing for the early recovery phase. The recent needs assessment report which was conducted and released by SOP , reveals the gap in humanitarian services in the area of Ariha sub-district, which needs immediate intervention. Moreover, Ariha city is located near the M4 road. Therefore, preparing to respond to such a life-threatening event will be highly critical to maintain the resistance of affected people, especially vulnerable groups. The multi-sectoral approach SOP is proposing through this project will respond to any potential massive displacement rationally by delivering comprehensive assistance to affected people, especially women, girls, and elderly persons.The information revealed by the needs assessment report emphasizes the importance of a comprehensive response in the area witnessing deteriorated socioeconomic conditions for the last few months. Delivering the proposed services will respond to the urgent needs of people and vulnerable groups. The planned services will support and strengthen the system in general, including the health system in the integration with ERL.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Women will be discriminated against or not deal safely with cases of gender-based violence referred to the health center. Mitigation: The capacity of non-protection staff will be built around GBV topics to ensure appropriate handling and ensure confidentiality and safety.Risk: the occurrence of cases of PSEA and Safeguarding. Mitigation: Staff and beneficiaries will be made aware of the reporting mechanism, informed consent will be explained, and the policies and ethical charter of the organization will be explained in this regard.Risk: beneficiaries will not attend the vocational training regularly. mitigation: beneficiaries will be consulted on the topics; the pace of the course and they will be able to give feedback.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information are linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups. SOP will provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA Policy, and will conduct induction sessions for new staff to ensure that they fully understand the content of CoC and PSEA policy. Strict disciplinary measures will be applied on any breach of the CoC. Affected population will be consulted on the convenient reporting channels to ensure effective and timely response for any compliance. SOP is a member of PSEA network (Adopting network's CoC and Policy) and regularly coordinating on the relevant issues and disseminating the central hotline numbers during the provided activities along with the organizational own reporting channels and hotlines. SOP periodically assessing the targeted communities on the suitable reporting channels for them and feed the information into threatening the existing CRM and deploying new channels when needed.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

SoP reviewed couple of Risks/assumption and mitigation methodologies for each assumption as follows: Assumption/1: Targeted communities welcome our activities and don't interfere with our work. Risk: The local authorities decide to prevent us from implementing the project or part of it. Therefore, we will not be able to access high-risk GBV cases and we will not be able to follow up on the GBV survivors’ cases whose ﬁles are open. Mitigation: We will coordinate with the technical local councils and raise their awareness on the importance of the project and the impact of it, and will coordinate also with the Access Group. Assumption (2): The COVID-19 pandemic is stable and no positive cases are recorded in Northwest Syria. Risk: COVID-19 outbreak with positive cases recorded and restriction of movements, and different complicated implications. Mitigation:All Protective measures will be taken by the project team, PPE materials will be secured for all staff members. Activities will be continued as possible considering the remote sessions through WhatsApp and e-platforms until the situation is stable again so we can re-activate the in-person sessions taking the precautionary measures in line with WHO/Guidance and recommendations. Assumption (3): The CP intervention welcomed and accepted by community. Risk: that existing traditional practices and community structures/ Cultural norms conﬂict with child protection principles.Mitigation: Liaise with "neutral" parties a sensitize info
