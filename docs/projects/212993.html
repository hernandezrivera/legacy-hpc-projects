Project Summary

TGH’s intervention is closely in line with the 2024-2025 WASH HRP Objectives 1, 2 and 4, as TGH aims to contribute to life-saving WASH interventions and support and restore existing WASH systems by addressing the needs of people affected by the conflict in Syria, coupled with the deteriorating context due to the hyperinflationary economic crisis.
According to the draft WASH 2024 HNO report, 13.56 million require WASH assistance in Syria with only 54% of the population in Syria having access to an improved water source. At least 70% of sewage is discharged untreated and at least half of the sewerage systems are not functional. WASH in School needs are growing with 56% of school-going children expressed dissatisfaction with the WASH facilities in their schools - with poor hygiene conditions affecting the overall enrollment and especially girls’ attendance.
The project proposed is an integrated intervention combining WASH, Education and Shelter activities with protection, gender, and environmental issues mainstreaming. This intervention will be focused on 6 areas: South Idleb, Hama rural areas, South Idleb, Aleppo rural areas, As-Sweida, Rural Damascus and Dar’a rural areas. The targeted areas were selected based on their accessibility, the severity of their needs varying between 3 and 5 as per the draft WASH 2024 HNO, TGH field assessments during 2023 & 2024 and consultations with Water authorities, SARC, local authorities and municipalities.
The proposed intervention has been designed based on TGH 5-year experience in implementing WASH projects (with a specific emphasis on WASH-in-school interventions) in Syria (GoS). TGH follows a multi-sectorial programming approach that relies on complementary multi-donor funding to ensure comprehensive coverage, addressing abroad range of critical humanitarian needs that exist in Syria, including WASH, Shelter and Education/Protection. The intervention will target 518,151 individuals in 2024 (258,853 women, 259,298 men, 73,811 PwD, 80,774 aged 0-17, 249,847 aged 18-64, 15,608 aged +65). 20% of the budget is already secured for the planned WASH activities in 2024.
In line with Objective 4, activities 1.1, 1.2 and 1.3, TGH will propose an intervention that will include:
• In 2024, TGH will rehabilitate 10 boreholes equipped with solar system, 3 water networks, support Water quality test for targeted water supply systems, capacity building of water authorities technical staff, install ground water level monitoring logger, distribute 600 water tanks (1m3) and rehabilitate 5 sewage networks.
As part of Objective 2, activities 2.2, 2.3, 2.4 and 2.5, the project will include:
• In 2024, TGH will target 6,000 students to distribute of hygiene kits, soap to students and provide them with HP sessions.
• Provision of emergency WASH services, including a potential response to a Cholera outbreak (HDPE water tanks, jerry cans, etc.).
As part of Objective 1, activity 4.1, and in line with the Education Sector objectives, TGH will propose the following:
• In 2024, TGH will rehabilitate WASH facilities in 10 schools, as part of school rehabilitations in order to secure access to water for drinking and hygiene purposes and ensure excreta evacuation to increase measures against infection, prevention and control both at school and at home in Idleb, Aleppo, Hama, Rural Damascus, and Dar’a. The intervention will be part of an integrated approach with the Education HRP 2024-2025.
As part of its activities, TGH specifically abides by protection principles through providing gender and disabled friendly WASH facilities in schools taking into account menstrual needs, including doors lockable from the inside and lighting. TGH will also ensure to provide WASH facilities accessible for children with disabilities.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

M&E will be carried out by the TGH MEAL team through baseline & end line household surveys aiming to evaluate the impact of the activities on living conditions. To support M&E activities and ensure accountability towards affected populations, TGH also set up a strong Complaints & Feedback mechanism through hotline, complaint boxes & regular consultations of the community in order to allow beneficiaries of all age, gender and background to reach the organization. - Content of kits: TGH follows the WaSH Sector guidelines to harmonize the content of the kits. The hygiene kits to be distributed can be used by both genders. - Toilets safety and risk of GBV: TGH ensures that when WASH facilities in schools are gender-separated. TGH will ensure to include locks for doors and lighting in the facilities. -Abuse and exploitation: All of TGH staff receives PSEA training when joining the organization. Additionally, in each of TGH’s base in Syria there is a PSEA focal point in order to follow any potential issue. TGH ensures that they sign an Ethics Clause along with their contract. - Exclusion of beneficiaries: TGH ensures that all the people with disabilities have access to the facilities built. In the schools the rehabilitations, TGH always include accessibility features such as ramps and handles. - Risk of theft of WaSH rehabilitation material: TGH agrees that all the safety of the material while doing the rehabilitation is the responsibility of the contractor. Once the works are finished, the project is handed over to the local authorities. We ensure that the authorities and local population take responsibility and ownership. - Exposure to explosive hazard (IED/UXO): TGH ensures that the area of work is safe and has no explosive devices. TGH coordinates with local population, local authorities and official mine action organisations to assure that the areas of work are safe. - SEA: All of TGH staff receive a PSEA training when joining the organization. In addition to the standard complaint mechanism, one focal point is designated in each operational base to collect the potential PSEA complaints and a dedicated hotline is set up to receive reports. The different channels appear in all visuals that are put in TGH office and areas of intervention (i.e. schools, distribution sites, etc.). All PSEA focal points receive specific trainings on how to deal with SEA complaint and their roles andresponsibilities are defined in ToRs. - Security tensions: TGH presence in the area of intervention since 2018 enable good relationship with local communities. Security and safety protocols: -Access to project locations remains granted but access to beneficiaries is limited by local authorities: Strong coordination with local authorities to get approval on time, TGH hired staff from the area of intervention to avoid travels from Damascus. Currency fluctuations: - Inflation rate increases. Price monitoring and co-financing with other projects from other donors.
