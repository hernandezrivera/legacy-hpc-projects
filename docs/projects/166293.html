Project Summary

IOM will work with the Ministry of Heath and health sector partners to provide a more systematic approach to enhance healthcare provision across three States in North-East Nigeria, namely Borno. Adamawa, Yobe (BAY), whilst always ensuring accountability to the affected populations. IOM’s health teams will work closely alongside MHPSS, Protection, WASH, CCCM and Shelter partners, as well as health sector partners to enhance access to those most in need. IOM will expand its MHPSS programme to a more systematic and comprehensive approach with engagement of community volunteers and health promoters . In the current crisis situation of COVID-19 pandemic, IOM intends to enhance access to comprehensive services through integration with primary health care . A crucial component of the primary health care system is the community-based health workforce ensuring a solid preventative/promotive component whereby CHW’s are tasked with ensuring continuum of care between health facilities and the community (home visits, referral and follow up of beneficiaries) and conducting health/hygiene education on key health topics. IOM will also continue working with the health partners in outbreak prevention and response efforts, on capacity building of health professionals, health information management, and emergency referral and support systems. As part of health promotion and preventive activities, IOM will partner with local NGOs to implement health outreach through Health Promoters and community volunteers observing physical distrancing. Under this action, IOM will also strengthen its collaboration with the State Primary Health Care Development Agency and will continue supporting the State Ministry of Health, with the above ultimately contributing to improving health outcomes for those most-affected by this protected humanitarian crisis.
IOM will expand and strengthen its Health and MHPSS responses during the COVID-19 pandemic, following the response pillars that have been activated through the Health Sector. IOM will contribute to Coordination, planning and monitoring by strengthening, reinforcing and supporting national and local coordination to facilitate access and ensure continuation of services for the affected populations. This includes capacity building, advocacy, developing of SOPs and other operational guidance and assistance; to Surveillance and Points of Entry (POE) by strengthening disease surveillance among border communities and PoEs that includes health screening and referral at PoEs, training of national authorities on infection prevention and control; Infection prevention and control (IPC) by supporting the provision of services in health care facilities and displacement settings that include activities related to the development of protocols, tools and guidance on isolation centres, self-quarantine shelters and contingency spaces; and Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE) by ensuring reliable information is taken into account in public health messaging and that the affected populations have access to timely and correct information. This includes mainstreaming on good practices, provision of technical guidance and tools to national and local stakeholders, development of IEC materials, including on prevention including barrier measures, communication with communities , and other awareness raising activities. The messages are driven by the need to be: empathetic, action oriented (prompting specific preventive behaviours), and focussed on the informational and emotional needs of people and communities. They are designed to help people understand COVID virus disease and make them less likely to become ill, enhance trust throughout the community, promote dialogue and community ownership of the response, and minimise psychosocial distress.

1 - Needs

The protracted crisis in North-East Nigeria has led to a meltdown of socioeconomic and development structures, which has contributed to the destruction of health facilities and an incapacitated healthcare sector. For example, 42 per cent of healthcare facilities in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states are non-functional due to the conflict - a clear indication of the catastrophic impact of the crisis on the health system. Additionally, quality of care is unclear owing to the unstable security situation and lack of standardized health care packages. An increase in quality service provision, enhanced technical capacity of the healthcare providers and the establishment of a robust monitoring mechanism were thus noted in 2019 as critical issues by the agencies responding to the crisis. Furthermore, gaps in referral services have been strongly flagged by Health Sector partners currently providing primary health care (PHC) services in camps and host communities, as a critical need.The situation is expected to worsen due to the COVID-19 pandemic. Partners across the sectors will need to use existing large-scale community engagement networks for social and behavior change approaches to ensure preventive community and individual health and hygiene practices, in line with the national public health containment recommendations. Awareness raising activities also represent an opportunity to include joint messaging and an occasion for MHPSS actors to provide psychological first aid (PFA) adaptation to the COVID-19 to alleviate the stress and anxiety resulting from the situation, mental health and psychosocial consequences of COVID-19 and dissemination of information on positive coping strategies and address social stigma.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Establish three Mobile Health Teams providing an integrated standard primary health care (PHC) service package, including referrals to secondary care. Activity 2: Identify and train local community volunteers on health promotion and community-based health interventions, while establishing clear community support systems for people with vulnerabilities who are unable to provide for themselves or have difficulties to access essential services. Activity 3: Support the national disease surveillance system and contribute to control of disease outbreaks and other public health events. Activity 4: Refurbishment and equipment of 10 selected public health facilities in order to resume their function to provide integrated primary health services. Activity 5: Conduct training for PHC workers with the use of the WHO Mental Health Gap Action Programme Intervention Guide (mhGAP IG), Psychological First Aid (PFA) remote services on e-based PFA or in the PFA adaptation for COVID-19 context and remote counselling services and staff self-care using already trained trainers. Activity 6: Provide remote counselling sessions to those in self-isolation and quarantine, and psychoeducation to the caregivers and relevant community members. Activity 7: Strengthen technical capacity to develop comprehensive treatment plans , with rigorous and systemic assessment of mental health conditions in the provision of specialized Mental Health services at tertiary and secondary health facilities (e.g. Federal Neuro-psychiatric Hospital, State Specialist Hospital). Activity 8. Ensure mainstreaming of protection, gender and MHPSS approach . Activity 9: Conduct an assessment on access of Mental Health services in PHC and conduct assessment on the psychological impact to the community during and after the pandemic, needs and available resources to cope with the situation. Activity 10: Conduct awareness campaigns to promote behavioural change informing the targeted groups of ways to change their behaviours and promote mental health and psychosocial well-being. Through a peer support system, encourage self-care routines among the most vulnerable groups, such as women, adolescents, persons with mental health conditions and elderly . The awareness campaigns during the COVID-19 will be added to inform affected population on how to access services especially for the vulnerable or high-risk groups and to avoid stigmatization. Awareness campaigns will employ culturally-appropriate sensitization to provide reliable information, ensuring IEC materials are translated into the preferred languages spoken by the affected population. Activity 11: Support the coordination of Mental Health and Psychosocial Support Sub Working Group (MHPSS SWG) and coordinate the MHPSS responses on the COVID-19, in close coordination and partnership with the State Ministry of Health (SMoH), health sector and other relevant government institutions and humanitarian actors. The coordination is a cross-sectoral initiatives . Activity 12: Strengthen the national capacity of government staff and humanitarian actors through the development of preparedness and response plan on the COVID-19 , IEC materials on COVID-19, operational guidelines and remote capacity building activities. Supporting the government on training nurses in quarantine facilities to provide a more integral and comprehensive approach to prevent mental health conditions under isolation periods as well as strengthen community and family self-efficacy in coping and preventing COVID.
