Project Summary

UNICEF intends to support the Coordination in the WASH sector, essential to strategize and optimize the intervention of all actors in WASH.
After reviewing different assessments, keeping in view the situations in East, needs, capacity and comparative advantage, UNICEF is planning to respond the needs of 1.6 million individuals in the two conflict affected areas of Donetska and Luhanska oblasts (GCA and NGCA), with provision of safe drinking water, improving access to safe sanitation and hygiene supplies, dissemination of critical water and hygiene related messages on communities located on both side of contact line in Donetsk and Luhansk, in Eastern Ukraine. This will be implemented through the water utility companies (centralized network of primary and secondary water suppliers) and decentralized structures (in NGCA). A particular focus will be made on WASH in social institutions (education/health facilities) on both GCA and NGCA.
The different activities take into account the gender, age and disabilities specificities and promote equality, especially on gender. People reached with more than one activity are only considered once to avoid duplication in counting beneficiaries.

1 - Needs

As the sixth year of Eastern Ukraine’s armed conflict takes its toll, the local population continues to be affected by on-going hostilities and its consequences with issues related to WASH as ones of the most acute. Reports, including the HNO, indicate that 2.8 million people (out of 3.2) whose water supply is affected by the conflict, require direct water supply and sanitation assistance in the conflict affected areas (GCA & NGCA). Conditioned by the on-going conflict, critical public water infrastructure located close to the line of contact (LoC) is put at risk daily of being targeted by: 75 violent incidents (shelling, shooting or other issues) directly affecting the WASH infrastructure were reported over the past 9 months in 2019. Water companies such as VD and PVK continue to struggle financially further requiring support in the procurement of essential chemicals to treat water, and equipment/material to make critical repairs. The stoppage of water would also impact companies in charge of heating networks, with the potential for widespread population suffering as temperatures often reach -20 C in winter.Poor condition of WASH infrastructure both on the institutional and community levels or deterioration of access to water and of water quality in conflict-affected areas could result in applying negative coping strategies among people (7% of HH store water in bathtub or buckets*), with a potential for microbiological contamination and an immediate impact on public health; when water quality near Konstantinovka was compromised in April 2019, there were 99 cases of gastroenteritis reported within days (73 were children <18). Furthermore, 6% of HH with children reported a case of sickness because of water quality in the past 12 months*.According to the recent study on isolated settlements, 35% of HHs have been experiencing water shortages occasionally, while 11% of HHs experience such shortages on a weekly basis (REACH/Protection Cluster 2019). This not only creates additional costs for purchasing and transporting water, but also affects people’s ability to maintain hygiene and livelihoods. As a result, a 31% of HHs on both sides of the ‘contact line’ require help with water supply. This need is even more acute for people living within 20 km of ‘the contact line’, people living in rural areas and HHs with disabled members. Due to the lack of support to the main water suppliers in NGCA, the access to drinking water via piped supply dropped from 64% before the conflict to around 53% at the end of 2017 (REACH). In general, a deterioration of the water situation since the beginning of the conflict is mentioned by the population*.According to the HNO 2020 some 84%, or a total of 771 education facilities in GCA of Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts, required some form of repair, primarily to the roof, windows, WASH facilities and heating systems.Towns and villages near the frontline report problems with garbage village councils can no longer afford to pay for trucks to remove it. The same applies to sewage in remote areas along the contact line, where people also need to empty HH level septic tanks; about 26% of all HHs in GCA and 20% in NGCA need help with sanitation*. Unaddressed water, sanitation and hygiene needs create serious health risks for the population (like diarrhea). Lastly, the access to hygiene materials remain challenging with some being not available in all locations in Donbas. 14% of HHs in GCA and 13% in NGCA have unsatisfactory hygiene practices. The cost of hygiene items remains unaffordable by many people; 94% of households stated that the e-voucher allowed them saving their money and spending it on other needs such as food (43%) and medicine (41%) (PDM UNICEF-TH May 2019). Vulnerable groups are of particular focus (elderly, GBV survivors, single-headed families…): 33% HH with elderly people, 42% HH with people with disabilities, reported needing help with WASH in GCA areas*. * WASH Cluster Assessment 2019

2 - Activities & outcomes

Output 1: Coordination and advocacy WASH coordination mechanism continued for effective, efficient and accountable humanitarian response for affected people (Female, Male and children) in Eastern Ukraine.There is a need for further coordination on activities implemented in the WASH sector, identification of gaps, avoidance of duplication, standard setting, assessments and response strategies, technical guidance. As well as an advocacy work around 1) the targeting of water infrastructure by all parties to the conflict; 2) increased funding for water utility companies (international donors, government); 3) facilitated & safe movement of utility workers and essential materials to complete timely repairs. Output 2: Support to service providers Main Water Service providers supported and strengthened, in coordination with WASH actors, to ensure continued water supply and sanitation services to the affected people in Donetsk and Luhansk, GCA and NGCAUNICEF intends to support VD & PVK directly, for 6 months in 2020, as a pre-identified gap already coordinated with ICRC & SDC with liquefied chlorine gas and chemicals for water treatment (GCA). A support to repairing water companies’ water/wastewater systems, with provision of spare parts, materials and equipment to facilitate them to repair critical water infrastructures in NGCA, focusing on systems run by VD, Karbonite, LV, and smaller vodokanals, is planned. Wastewater systems, pipelines and treatment plants have also been impacted by shelling and blockages due to reduced water flow, will also be supported; Output 3: Support to institutionsBoys and girls and their families in different social institutions including education and health facilities have access to improved and sustainable WASH services Assessment and repairs to damaged water, sanitation and heating systems in health facilities, schools and kindergartens is essential to keep the essential services operational. A particular focus on gender and dignity will be made on toilet related activities.Provision of hygiene kits/voucher-based assistance.Implementation of Hygiene Promotion activities to students, parents and teachers. Partnerships with the following CSOs, at least, will be established: AN, ADRA, PIN, CARITAS, CSD & TGH in GCA, DDC & WRF in NGCA Output 4: Service provision to most affected people, including women and childrenAffected population has access to the efficient WASH services through provision of WASH supplies, emergency repair of WASH communal infrastructure/facilities.Short-term water trucking of potable and technical water (untreated water used for cleaning) will be initiated only when critical needs arise, complemented by bottled water distribution, in respect with SPHERE Stds;In parallel, increasing the resilience of the most vulnerable HH with water storage (water tanks or jerricans) or small-scale water systems (filtering devices);Repair of water structures of decentralized water networks is planned, with empowerment of women in village councils, in NGCA;Desludging of septic tanks and solid waste removal activities are required especially in areas close to the contact line where access has been limited due to the conflict, and where costs of removal are now high. WASH activities supported by integration with livelihoods activities;Given the harsh situation at winter time, some winterization activities will be realized (provision of coal, installation of heaters, boilers…)Improved availability of essential hygiene items, through an e-vouchers programme in GCA, and distribution of hygiene kits in NGCA or in hard to reach areas in GCA. Programmes are designed to answer to the specific needs: women, elderly, people with disabilities... An appropriate hygiene promotion programme will be implemented: hygiene sessions on reproductive health & MHM to youth; for pregnant women; for elderly people.
