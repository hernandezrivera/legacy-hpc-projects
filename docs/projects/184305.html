Project Summary

Given the lack of options for durable solutions and continued conflict in the country which limits the ability to return, it is foreseen that populations will continue to need CCCM services beyond 2022. It is anticipated that increased conflict, frequent climatic shocks and the continuation of the triple threat will lead to further displacements which will strain existing sites and lead to the formation of new sites.
The organization will implement CCCM project in 42 IDP sites in Baidao districts of Sout west region of somalia. Approximately 42,000 IDPs (7000 households) will benefit from this proposed intervention, in addition to the community working in these sites including local authorities, clusters, and other NGOs. These numbers might vary due to external factors, which might attract more IDPs, excessive flooding, or conflict with the landowners or the authorities, which may cause IDPs to flee to other areas.
Gender will be mainstreamed into the project through the specific consideration of the different needs of women, men, boys, and girls during the design of activities. The project will ensure that all participants have equal access to resources and training. Monitoring and evaluation will be based on gender, age, and diversity dis-segregated data. IRS will implement this intervention in Baidao district of south west somalia targeting 7000 HHS. Baidoa District is one of the IDP major hubs in Bay region that hosts the largest number of IDPs. Throughout the project, the needs of women, girls, men, and boys of all ages will be addressed throughout the planning, implementation and monitoring, and evaluation of the activities. Through regular site monitoring, needs and gaps will be reported and shared with partners and all stakeholders. Particular attention regarding needs will be paid to women and girls who also have to contend with GBV and protection concerns within camp settings. Through this project, IRS will also address the current and newly identified needs by setting up camp coordination structures at the national and, where necessary, regional or district levels, facilitating and supporting the establishment of effective coordination and management in IDP sites while respecting national context and authorities. Conflict, drought, COVID-19, and flooding are some of the main shocks that drive displacement and largely effecting the IDPs

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

- Using ‘Do no harm principles’, IR will seek to avoid physical or psychosocial risks that would be detrimental to project beneficiaries, and will serve all people irrespective of age, sex or clan. Given vulnerabilities associated with issues related to clan dynamics, IR has done a thorough analysis of the context and has conducted the Do-No-Harm Assessments for target districts. The assessment reports outlines the clan structure and dynamics, with reports of peaceful coexistence of the clans. Through the recommendations therein, IR will target the hard-to reach and marginalized communities – at the locational level. The project will galvanize the participation of all concerned beneficiaries through identifying and bolstering appropriate checks and balances. IR will allow equal access by involving all stakeholders in planning, improving transparency through informing; access through their representation in the leadership that we shall work with, during selection of committee members, consultation with local leaders and community members on site selection for example on water points, and supporting in clear criteria for beneficiary selection for activities such as cash for work beneficiaries.
