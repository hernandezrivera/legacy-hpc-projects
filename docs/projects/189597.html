Project Summary

Since the conflict crisis in 2013, and the consequent aggression coupled with drought, locusts, floods and the army warm infestation, the factors of food production for families over the years has been greatly affected to the present state. Household livelihood systems and patterns has been on the shift with many households only counting the cost. The cost of foods prices has remained every year high which has also affected the food production capacity of many households. Furthermore, family household incomes and dietary intakes has been greatly affected and to larger extent deteriorated and very wanting. As the hard economic times continue to press and bite the communities, many households have succumbed to survival mode as their livelihoods has been diminished or destroyed. Many household dietary intakes have significantly deteriorated and many can hardly afford two meals per day.
The objective of the project is to provide food and livelihood protection to population affected by conflict and flood related displacements, hunger and diseases in Tonj East, Tonj south and Malakal Counties. In line with FSL Cluster priorities and HRP 2022 strategy, CARO will provide complementary and integrated agricultural crop and vegetable seeds, farming tools, fishing kits and agricultural extension support including training on improved agronomy practices and fish handling/preservation techniques to the communities in in Tonj East, Tonj South and Malakal Counties where indicators show a continuously degrading situation. The project will target 13,000HHs most vulnerable IDP, returnee and host populations in in these counties and provide Agricultural to support and increase their Agricultural production and productivity hence strengthening their capacity to produce enough food for household consumption and help them evade future shocks and builds their resilience.
This project will further ensure protection mainstreaming; acknowledging that women, girls, men and boys with diverse ages, disabilities and backgrounds have very different needs, risk and coping strategies. This will be achieved by paying particular attention to protection and inclusion of vulnerable groups based and on gender and diversity analysis in addition to ensuring prevention of incidences of Gender Based Violence, sexual exploitation and abuse and other protection concerns and ensuring activities adheres to Do No Harm approach, and environmental protection.

1 - Needs

The Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO 2022) estimates 8.3 million people, including refugees, are expected to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the 2022 lean season (May-July). This represents a 7 per cent increase from the 7.7 million in 2021. In addition, an estimated 2 million people, including 1.3 million children under the age of 5, and 676,000 pregnant and lactating women, are expected to be acutely malnourished in 2022. On the backdrop of this, the IPC analysis for second projection period of April to July 2021 estimated 7.24 million people (60% of the population) are likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity with Warrap and Upper Nile states being among the worst affected states in South Sudan.Furthermore, the FSL Cluster analysis identified food security and livelihoods as the most needed forms of assistance for men in 55% of assessed settlements and women in 52% of sample assessed. In the case of children, food and livelihoods were the second most frequently identified pressing need (as reported in 29 per cent of assessed settlements), behind education in 48 per cent.In the Greater Tonj area and Upper Nile state, pockets of conflicts and violence, floods and inflation of food prices have not only destroyed household’s productive assets but also disrupted their livelihoods. These shocks have had a disproportionate effect on children, persons with disability, older persons and women headed households.The current lean season has also been characterized by already depleted food stocks from the last harvest for majority of households, with resultant food consumption gaps. This, coupled with reduced market access as a result of conflicts and fear of conflicts and and due to poor rad conditions caused by flooding The high cost of food and essential commodities further widened the food consumption gap. April-July IPC 2021 indicates a likely deterioration GAM prevalence due to limited food access and availability, especially among children under 5 years and women of reproductive age. Projected high malnutrition levels are driven by poor access to public health, poor access to and availability of food due to dysfunctional markets and reduced farm production, and poor caregiving practices on food utilization. Through this project, CARO targets 9,000 most vulnerable households in Tonj East and South and Malakal Counties with interventions including; distribution of emergency livelihood kits; vegetable, crop, fishing kits and farming tools intended to stimulate food production to save lives while building resilience. Households will be trained on environmental friendly agronomic techniques as well as good fishing practices aimed at maximizing yield for both household consumption and for sale. Demonstration plots will be established 4 in each county to support continuous learning by the beneficiaries. This support will see the communities in Tonj East and South and Malakal get empowered while strengthening their productive capacity and creating opportunities to contribute to their household welfare, while supporting and protecting their assets and livelihoods

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback from the communities and beneficiaries especially through consultations will be an integral part during the project development and implementation as it will provide a platform to accommodate new ideas and opinions. In doing so, CARO will have an opportunity to demonstrate its accountability to the community and ensure that the project reflects local needs, has local support and does not have any adverse unintended consequences.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

During trainings, awareness raising and sensitization, distribution exercises and monitoring sessions, CARO will encourage use of local language so that every person understands and contribute. Equal consideration and participation during all sessions will be a priority so that the minority and marginalized group can feel to be part of the group. Furthermore, grouping of the beneficiaries according to age and sex during monitoring assessment (focus group discussion or safety audit) will be considered so that everyone is comfortable to contribute. Pre and post test questions will be given to participants during trainings to accommodate feedback from every member. At the community level, suggestion box will be used so that people who feel shy to talk can drop their written suggestion into the box or contact the focal point privately to give his/her inputs.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CARO staff will be responsible for championing good practices and maintaining an organizational culture that prioritizes and safeguards all against SEA. Managers at all levels (Both Juba and Field level) have responsibilities to support and develop systems that maintain an environment that facilitates implementation of this Policy and which prevents SEA. During the community mobilization and sensitization meetings, CARO will provide beneficiaries with information on how to safely and confidentially report SGBV/SEA if and when it occurs. CARO will use a community-based complaints and feedback mechanism to provide a platform for beneficiaries to voice any issues of CAROs and feedback on the project activities. In line with CARO's Safeguarding Policy on PSEA, CARO will inform the target communities about CARO's staff responsibilities and the expected code of conduct. At the start of the project, CARO will develop a plan on how information about PSEA will be disseminated. In all its communication activities, CARO will re-emphasize that all the beneficiary entitlements are provided freely and that they should immediately report if and when they’re asked to do or provide anything in exchange of the project’s entitlements. CARO will work closely with community leaders and stakeholders and will sensitize them on how to report any SEA cases that they come across.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The beneficiaries, local leaders and other key stakeholders will be directly involved throughout the entire project cycle. The beneficiaries will be taught on their roles and responsibilities. CARO has been doing this so that the community gets timely information which enhances involvement and sense of ownership of the projects. In addition, CARO will establish a multi-layered feedback mechanism which include a complaint desk, suggestion box and focal person contact number which will give the beneficiaries a multiple and alternative avenue to channel their concerns and complaints
