Project Summary

The project's aim is to provide retention support (RSP) to pupils in public schools. The project will ensure that children would not be left behind academically due to the crisis and ongoing public-school closures. The project will target vulnerable Lebanese and Palestine children ages between 7-14.) Geographic targets include children in Mt. Lebanon and Beirut, Baalbek and El Hermel. The proposed RSP education intervention will take a holistic approach. It will address education gaps by providing and ensuring educational support for Lebanese and Palestinian refugees and encourage them to continue with their learning. Classes will focus on French/English, Math, Arabic, and Science and will be delivered in classrooms following the MEHE curriculum at ADRA’s learning centers as well as in school. Targeted children will receive learning materials to improve the quality of education such as worksheets and school stationery kits. There will not only be activities related to academic education but also a component of activities enhancing mental and emotional well-being through psychosocial sessions. Parents/ caregivers of pupils will also be involved in awareness sessions and critical issues surrounding a child’s education, to ensure protection of children. Healthy snacks will be distributed daily to provide daily nourishment. Transportation will also be provided at the center for students; according to the transportation needs and vulnerability criteria. There will be activities conducted to foster social mobilization campaigns, such as community outreach and dialogue with key actors to raise awareness on education and protection through psychosocial support (PSS). Networking meetings will be held with local communities and social authorities, public school principals and teachers, Mokhtar to inform them of the impact and the objectives of the program. This will ensure support from the local community and advocacy.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

The project activities below will follow the strategic objectives outlined in the ERP SO1: Direct community support to support the most vulnerable children to ensure continued learning and SO2: Improving Formal Education of most vulnerable Lebanese and migrant children through schools. Activity 1.1: Provide non-formal education through a Retention support program to the pupils.Activity 1.2 Conduct Pre-test/Mid/Post-Test for pupils.Activity 1.3: Distribute essential learning supplies and daily healthy snacks to pupils.Activity 1.4: Provide transportation of the pupils from the public school to the center and from the center to their home or to the public school for afternoon and morning shifts according to the transportation needs and vulnerability criteria.Activity 1.5: Distribute eyeglass and/or hearing aid devices to pupils, based on need.Activity 2.1: Conduct social mobilization campaigns including community outreach and dialogue with key actors to raise awareness on education, and protection including psychosocial support sessions (PSS).Activity 3.1: Provide psychosocial support and life-saving messages to crisis-affected children ensuring child protection to direct and indirect beneficiaries (Parents, Caregivers)Activity 4.1: Networking meeting with local communities and social authorities, public school principals and teachers, Mokhtars.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

ADRA’s protection risk mitigation plan is an integral part of the project's design and implementation to ensure that all activities do not harm the affected population and do not create new protection risks. ADRA aims to prevent, mitigate, and respond to protection risks, including PSEA, and promote gender equality, social inclusion, and non-discrimination throughout the project lifecycle. ADRA has a Protection Policy aligned with international principles and guidance on protection and addressing S/GBV, including PSEA. A gender-sensitive strategy will be in place that integrates the gender perspective and regularly. ADRA will train staff on protection principles, including Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) training, localized gender sensitivities, gender equality, and gender-based violence (GBV). The organization also promotes gender mainstreaming throughout the project cycle, ensuring the collection and reporting of sex and age disaggregated data.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

The project aims to have a robust AAP framework in place, with several measures aimed at ensuring accountability to the affected population and maximizing the project's impact. The project was designed to respect the dignity and capacities of the target population, ensuring equal access to assistance and involvement. Beneficiary consultations will take place during the implementation and monitoring phases of the project, using feedback to improve its management and better serve those affected. Beneficiaries have various means to give feedback, including confidential one-on-one conversations with ADRA staff. The project will have an AAP focal point, AAP training for project members, and feedback mechanisms such as complaint boxes, hotlines, and surveys. Community engagement, parent/caregiver meetings, and a do-no-harm approach will also be followed. M&E will regularly share information with communities, donors, partners, and stakeholders.
