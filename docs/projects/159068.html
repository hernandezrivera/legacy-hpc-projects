Project Summary

Context: the occupation and its practices, manifest in the excessive use of force, movement restrictions, prevention of East Jerusalem, demolitions, forced eviction, settlement expansion, settler violence, attacks against schools, killings, injuries and arrests, specifically among minors, combined with ever growing shortfalls in humanitarian funding, an enduring intra-Palestinian political divide, economic hardship, the absence of a political solution to the Israeli-Palestinian conflict on the horizon, anticipated escalation resulting from enforcement of the “Deal of the Century”, restrictions and attacks on humanitarian partners, violations of international humanitarian law, are all factors that pose increased protection threats for the Palestinian population, which necessitate urgent responses to meet emerging or worsening needs and facilitate access to basic services for vulnerable people. The project will target the following locations and groups:
- Targeted Locations: Vulnerable and marginalized communities witnessing increased exposure to Israeli political violence in East Jerusalem (Essawiyyeh, Jabal al-Mukabir, and Shu’fat Refugee Camp).
- Targeted beneficiaries: Children: boys and girls, aged 6-18, Adults: vulnerable men and women (including families) exposed to grave violations, including child detention, demolitions, evictions and settler violence.
- Aim: The project will seek to address protection risks, enhance the psychosocial well-being and mental health of the targeted beneficiaries and communities and enable better access to basic services (education, health) and smooth reintegration into community life and structures. This shall be attained through the provision of a holistic package of services, based on a human rights approach as described below:
(Responsive): The project will seek to address the psychosocial impacts of conflict related violence, through the provision of urgent and timely responses, as well as structured PSS emergency interventions a and MHPSS. This will include conducting emergency visits within the first 48 hours of an emergency, screening resulting needs and providing PFA.
(Remedial): The project foresees tailored case management, MH and PSS plans that will include psychosocial group and individual counseling sessions for children and adults (caretakers and the wider community), peer to peer groups and further screening, detection and treatment of children/adults facing psychosocial challenges, referrals and rehabilitative care when required; as well as remedial education after school hours and for children under home arrest. Summer camps and ventilation will conduct for children and their families (recreational activities included and awareness raising with partners).
(Preventive): The project will seek to prevent future protection risks, through enhancing beneficiary awareness of needs and rights, equipping them with life skills to upgrade their resilience, especially when faced with future emergencies. Capacitation trainings and resilience building activities will be provided to both staff and CBO personnel, to enable them to address basic protection needs and provide sustainable and long-term solutions, and through awareness sessions and summer camps at schools in East Jerusalem.

1 - Needs

MdM-CH and YMCA are currently working in these three proposed locations, operating under OCHA HF of 2018, since November 2018. As a result of this presence and as part of MDM-CH’s standard project cycle management procedures, monitoring, evaluation and SWAP analyses were regularly conducted. Additionally, an emergency needs assessment and risk analysis update was recently conducted, alongside with a lessons-learned, exercise following the latest escalations in Essawiyeh village. The increase in police activity in the area has led to an increased frequency of arrests, and the rapid assessment recommendations have inform the design and strategy of the project submitted. The project targets the cluster’s geographic priority areas after 1. a thorough review of needs assessments conducted by different clusters and/or other partners 2. consulting academic papers, research and studies at national and international level; in order to take informed decisions and propose responses based on MDM-CH’s and partners’ added value. As a result of all the above we would like to emphasize the following key findings: East Jerusalem and specifically Essawiyyeh, Jabl al Mukabir and Shu’fat R.C, have been witnessing an alarming escalation in violence, fueled by Israeli practices over the last 5 months. By the end of July 2019, some 126 structures had been demolished in East Jerusalem, displacing 203 people, (compared to 107 and 93 respectively, for the equivalent period in 2018). In Essawiyeh, an unprecedented number of ISF operations has been recorded, with continuous violent clashes (134 clashes, which represents a 99% increase compared to the same period last year), search operations leading to 350 arrests until now in a period of 5 months time only, 120 people wounded and one young adult killed. moreover 25 arrested on Sep 2019.Is worth mentioning that recent research evidence has estimated that prevalence of of post traumatic stress disorder in Palestinian children and adolescents is 23-70%. Regarding our coordination with the Education Cluster,Throughout the planning phase of the project, feedback from members of the education cluster was received and incorporated in order to inform the proposed strategy. The project will sensitize children on the risks linked to detention, inform them of their rights and provide them with information to increase their overall wellbeing, emotional health and resilience. An awareness booklet will be developed and disseminated in the targeted locations. The awareness sessions will cover the notion of a child’s self-preservation in a conflict environment, the notion of danger/risk/consequences and address the stigma linked to mental health and seeking support. This will allow every child to be better informed about all aspects related to detention and about available services. In order to make this approach available for more children, a comprehensive awareness package will also be designed and shared with other institutions. The present project adopts a holistic approach towards rehabilitation, through three major components:• providing quality psychosocial rehabilitation services to ex-detainee children, through specialized counselling sessions, educational and career guiding sessions, and supporting remedial education classes or vocational trainings.• providing psychosocial support to families of ex-detainee (and detainee) children, through specialized counselling sessions.• liaising with community institutions, organizing awareness raising workshops, in addition to organizing public events on specific national and international days to disseminate information about the programme and its services and to advocate for children's rights.For the remedial education cases, local certified teachers will be contracted, who can provide remedial education in safe places close to the beneficiaries' residential areas. The teachers should be specialized in the topics they will teach.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

MDM-CH and YMCA understand and fully adhere to the core principle of “Do No Harm” as a humanitarian imperative, a cornerstone in all programmes followed by the pyramid of IASC minimum standards in regards to MHPSS. Both organizations remain vigilant throughout project implementation to identify and prevent unintended negative consequences of their actions. In this regard, MDM-CH and YMCA are adopting a number of steps to ensure that its interventions maximize positive impacts for community stability, mainly by strengthening their capacities and resilience, while minimizing harm.AS a result of all the above the do not harm principle appears as a cross-cutting principle at all stages of project cycle management : diagnosis phase of the project, implementation phase, M&E phase and following MDM health project planning tool (https://www.medecinsdumonde.org/en/actualites/publications/2017/04/19/health-project-planning)

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

MDM bases its intervention in ethical and humanitarian principles. This project employed a principalist approach that is widely accepted in the humanitarian community through such vehicles as the SPHERE Humanitarian Charter. Ethical principles in health care include respect for persons to advance human dignity and for individuals’ autonomous choices; beneficence, the promotion of others’ wellbeing, commonly known as “do no harm,” and justice, encompassing both the fair distribution of resources and fair processes for decision-making. Humanitarian principles is the a second source of values and norms that animate all MDM-CH actions. HP include humanity, requiring that suffering must be addressed wherever and for whomever it is found; neutrality, the duty of humanitarian actors not to take sides in a conflict; impartiality, which stipulates that humanitarian actors must not discriminate or give preference to any nationality, race, religious belief, class, political opinion, or similar status; and independence, which demands that humanitarian actors retain their autonomy and remain independent of political or military objectives of other actorsMDM-CH makes sure that representatives of all social, ethnic and religious groups in the area of intervention participate in needs.For example in the current project and following an analysis conducted following focus group discussions and MDM-CH teams observations and reports we are proposing minor rehabilitation works in Essawiyeh Community center in order to improve accessibility and communication of the center adapted for PwD’s. Thus, technical advice was already requested by Humanity Inclusion.
