Project Summary

Under nutrition sector, Dorcas, and based on a previous experience related to IYCF (2019-2021), plans to continue the implementation of the project that started in 2022 that aims to raise awareness about the importance of breastfeeding and its benefits for children who might be suffering from malnutrition and how it can prevent later death. It also aims to support young women who are pregnant or planning to get pregnant or young lactating women in several group sessions related to the importance of the breastfeeding especially for baby aged between 0-2 years and the exclusivity of breastfeeding for babies aged between (0-6 months), the correct breastfeeding technics, infant and young child feeding program and nutritional daily inputs,... Dorcas will reinforce the recommended IYCF practices, based on global guidelines (UNICEF/ WHO) :
 Initiate breastfeeding within 1 hour of birth
 Exclusively breastfeed for the first 6 months
 Introduce age-appropriate, adequate, safe, and properly fed complementary foods starting from 6 months up to 2 years of age
 Continue breastfeeding for up to 2 years of age or beyond.
Moreover, support the beneficiaries with psychosocial and counselling individual sessions and support pregnant women with regular medical checks up and / or referrals.
This project will take place in Hanano and Hellok community centers for 12 months and will target young girls and women who are pregnant or planning to get pregnant in Hanano/ Hellok areas and the surroundings.
Dorcas aims at preventing malnutrition by raising awareness levels on nutritional matters among women & caregivers by providing awareness-raising sessions and IYCF counseling, and at providing supplementary micronutrient supplementation to PLW to accelerate anemia reduction.
This project IYCF main goal is to improve through optimal feeding, the nutritional status,
growth and development, health, and thus the survival of infants and young children.

3 - Needs

More than a decade of the protracted crisis in Syria has led to 5.8 million children and PLW in need of life-saving preventive & curative nutrition interventions in 2022, compared with 4.9 million in 2021.As per the nutrition sector severity report in HNO 2022, Jabal Samaan is classified severity scale 3.In 2020, Save the children reported that 700,000 more children face hunger in Syria owing to the country’s badly-damaged economy, partly due to the impact of the COVID-19 restrictions. In 2021, the U.N reported that «more than half a million children under 5 in Syria suffer from stunting as a result of chronic malnutrition". Moreover a RNA was carried out by Dorcas (Hanano,Hellok,sheikh kheder,sheikh fares,Baaiden,salah din, ) in July 2022 to 391 HHs, the results indicate that 67% of the total HHs have no income & resources to purchase enough food, 55% are buying food by credit or borrowed money, needs medical supplies 45% have health issues , 79% of the total HHs have lack of maternal milk due their nutritional & health status, 62% are not receiving any health care because it’s too expensive for them.Based on Dorcas previous experience in this domain & the long waiting list of PLW requesting to be enrolled in the awareness sessions & counseling, Dorcas will continue to raise awareness about the importance of breastfeeding & its benefits for children who might be suffering from malnutrition & how it can prevent death & will reinforce the recommended IYCF best practice.

4 - Implementation of activities

Dorcas will implement this project in Aleppo Jabal Semaan, it will cover Hanano, Hellok and surroundings (Sakhour, Jabal Badro, Ard Al Hamra...) . Humanitarian needs remain critical in Syria & more specifically in Aleppo where Dorcas is working since 2015.Following MSNA carried out by Dorcas in Oct. 2021 in 10 neighborhoods, Jebel Saman district and sub-districts, it shows that the highest needs in the locations where Dorcas is implementing in Aleppo are basic services, general safety and security issues (theft, etc.) and employment opportunities/Income sources while the other concerns found are; health care (emergency services/chronic disease related services), essential NFIs and shelter/housing options; Dorcas implemented an IYCF prog. in 2019/2020 & 2021 in Aleppo, Rural Damascus and Homs. The program was successful & helped many lactating & pregnant women. Dorcas has always done food distribution according to beneficiaries needs.Act 1: Establish a mother & child Area suitable for young women & girls who can come spend time in it.Act 2: Train IYCF counsellors, Community center manager and Project Officer to provide IYCF & PSS services.Act 3: Conduct IYCF group sessions with pregnant, planning to or women with a child aged from 0 to 2 years Act 4: conduct individual support sessions for young women coming to the IYCF group sessions & in need to more & special follow up.Act 5: Refer pregnant women to the PHC to receive medical consultation and follow up.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Security risk: Physical risk to individuals and assets from acts of war, violence and crime. Aleppo is relatively Modern risk area. No major clashes or security conflict has been reported in the past year. Mitigation: Though personal classes and conflicts can not be eliminated, still Dorcas and their partners implement high level of alert and prevention measures to ensure risk minimization. Dorcas has a staff safety officer that coordinates with INSO and other agencies in order to obtain relevant security information and make informed decisions. The range of options how to assess and deal with security threats is laid down in Dorcas’ Safety and Security Manual. To ensure safeguarding of staff, Dorcas' project manager are trained in personal security and staff safety management. Dorcas security officer will be monitoring the situation regularly and will be collaborating with the partner who will operate there. Fiduciary risk: the risk that money or materials are not used for intended purposes (i.e. fraud, theft, corruption). No fiduciary risk incidents have been reported within Dorcas or partners’ offices. Partners are reporting all projects in timely manner monitored by Dorcas financial department. Mitigation: In case this happens, Dorcas has Multi layer monitoring Segregation of duties Dorcas follows its verified Financial Manual and through this ensures that financial risks are minimized. Selection of suppliers is done in alignment with Dorcas’ Procurement Manual.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

This project will continue the work that Dorcas has been doing through its community centers for several years. Dorcas’ community centers/partners are well-known & trusted; individuals and families understand that Dorcas has the ability to provide a multitude of protection services, as well as many humanitarian services like hygiene items and food parcels. Working in conversation with the community, Dorcas develops many proposals and activities based on information received from its feedback systems and the needs of the community expressed by those coming into the center. Dorcas’ two-way beneficiary feedback system was established in the community centers to ensure that beneficiary populations have a voice in the planning, activity implementation, and evaluation of the project. Feedback and suggestions are listened to as part of the protection risk monitoring and subsequently included in the implementation. This also includes a process to confidentially handle allegations of sexual exploitation and abuse in an effort to ensure that beneficiaries are protected against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Dorcas ensures beneficiaries have multiple ways to provide feedback, including a hotline, text messages, emails, & box established within the centers. Dorcas’ long term presence within the community also ensures that the most vulnerable, such as the elderly, children, PWD, and widows are reached through its services.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: Enabled access to targeted areas is maintained with acceptable security conditions during the intervention. The political and economic situation of the country remains at a level allowing effective humanitarian action. The Cholera and covid-19 pandemic remains contained in Aleppo where the implementation will take place. Risks: Restrictions imposed by the Government of Syria on humanitarian interventions, including humanitarian access. Civil unrest in project locations prevents full geographic coverage as anticipated. Confirmed increasing number of cases and/or potential outbreak of Cholera or covid-19 in project locations. Significant in-flux of conflict affected populations into project locations leads to over stretch of project delivery. Mitigation: Dorcas/the partners will closely monitor any safety and security concerns in targeted areas and act upon its Risk Mitigation Plan. Dorcas/partners will ensure strong participation in field coordination mechanisms (clusters and working groups) and engage with other partners and/or stakeholders in project locations to avoid any duplication of efforts. Dorcas/Partners will ensure most vulnerable women are being selected and targeted through beneficiary selection criteria, including PWSNs and PWDs.
