Project Summary

Kuwaiti Patients Helping Fund (KPHF) proposes to implement integrated and life-saving WASH intervention to assist vulnerable population affected by displacement, conflict, natural emergencies, malnutrition and disease outbreaks in South Darfur State (at the following localities: Al Radoum, Sunta, Beliel, Ed El Fursan, Kubum, Mershing, Nyala Janoub , Nyala Shimal, Sharg Aj Jabal, Shattaya, Tulus and Um Dafoug), North Darfur State (at the following localities: El Fasher , Dar As Salam , Kutum , SarafOmra, Tawila and Um Baru), Kassala State (at the following localities: Reifi Hamashkureib, Reifi Kassala, Reifi Shamal Ad Delta, Reifi Telkok and Reifi Wad Elhilaiw), Red See State (at the following localities: Al Ganab, Dordieb, Haya and Sinkat ), Gedaref State (at the following localities: Al Fashaga, Al Galabat Al Gharbyah – Kassab, Al Mafaza and 2 in Basundah), Sennar State (at the following localities: Abu Hujar, Ad Dinde, As Suki and Sharg Sennar ) and Khartoum State (at the following localities: Jebel Awlia, Karrari and Sharg An Neel). The proposed programming will support 600,000 direct beneficiaries including men (190,000), women (250,000), boys and girls (160,000), comprising of IDPs, returnees, resident and host communities as well as 150,000 indirect beneficiaries. The project aims are to enabling affected population to access to the basic water services and access to adequate, protected, and gender sensitive sanitation services as well as access to hygiene promotion services, water quality, and environmental health interventions. KPHF will involve the direct beneficiaries in the design and implementation of project activities. The project activities will include the following activities: rehabilitation of water yard, rehabilitation and improve hafir, establishment and upgrading of a hand pump to the solar system, establishment and training of water management committees, conduct regular awareness raising session on sanitation & hygiene promotion, conduct sanitation TOT for volunteers, moreover the women, men and children in targeted villages will have improved and equitable access to information on safe hygiene practices whereby the hygiene volunteers conducting hygiene promotion campaign to disseminate hygiene messages, Establishment/ Construction of communal & school latrines to ensuring separate lighted section for women & girls, Establishment of hand washing facilities equipped with soap at latrines and public places, distribution of personal hygiene supplies to HHs to maintain hand washing. improve the sanitation and environment status in the targeted village through a declaration of three communities as open defecation free (OFD) and supporting the trained hygiene volunteers in conducting general clean up campaigns during the project period. Support water chlorination in HH, conduct vector control campaigns.

1 - Needs

Sudan's protracted crisis continues to create water, hygiene, and sanitation needs for vulnerable people. Key drivers are deteriorating economy, conflict, food insecurity, malnutrition, floods, and disease, including COVID-19. About three-quarters of the population have access to basic drinking water, 39 per cent have access to limited sanitation services, and 13 per cent have access to basic hand washing facilities. The people in need of WASH is 9.1 million people across Sudan, including 1.05 million refugees. Diarrhea kills one in ten children, attributed mainly to poor WASH. Two million children suffer from acute malnutrition, 50 per cent of which is associated with repeated diarrhea or worm infections related to poor WASH conditions. Nearly a third of households (over 10 million people) practice open defecation which poses grave public health risks, including the transmission of water-borne diseases such as cholera, diarrhea, dysentery, hepatitis A, typhoid, and polio. There are also high WASH needs related to hygiene and water safety in flood-prone states where thousands of latrines collapse every year and water sources inundate. The 2018-2020 Federal Ministry of Health disease surveillance data shows that the most common vector-borne diseases causing outbreaks in Sudan are Dengue Fever, Rift Valley Fever, and Chikungunya Fever. All of it, including malaria, links to poor environmental health and sanitation, contributing to high mortality. IDPs, refugees, and vulnerable residents will need continued WASH support. In some locations, these population groups walk for over five kilometers to fetch water and can only access around 7- 8 litres per person per day, especially during the dry season. The limited number of latrines leads to open defecation. Specific needs of women and girls are gender-segregated WASH facilities in communal settings, menstrual hygiene management, and engagement in WASH-related governance activities. Access to WASH in public institutions such as schools and primary health centres are limited. Two-thirds of the existing schools do not have improved sanitation facilities, and a quarter of them do not have access to clean water. The situation of WASH in health facilities is even worse. Lack of gender-segregated sanitation facilities is a challenge.Of the four key populations groups identified during the joint inter-sectoral analysis, vulnerable residents account for most people with humanitarian WASH needs. There are five million vulnerable residents throughout the country considered in the worst severity of needs as follows: 5.03 million people needs access to hygiene services, 3.7 million people needs access to sanitation, and 1.4 million needs access to basic water services. About 2.2 million IDPs are in the worst severity of needs. They are in the Blue Nile, Central Darfur, East Darfur, North Darfur, South Darfur, West Darfur, West, and South Kordofan states. Out of these, 2.1 million will need continued and increased support to access hygiene services, 1.4 million will need support accessing sanitation, and 400,000 will need continued support to access basic water services.According to the initial findings of the assessment carried out by partner in North Darfur, 20 % of assessed boreholes and 60% of the hand pumps are not functional due to breakdown or draw down resulted from shortage of rains in past years. People travel long distance to collect water and due to lack of water, 75% of the assessed population are not practicing proper hand washing; more than 80% of the people living in rural areas of the targeted localities do not have access to adequate water, sanitation and hygiene services (WASH).

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

KPHF recognizes the importance of mainstreaming protection in its projects in order to make sure that its efforts would ensure full respect for the rights of individuals and groups. The organization recognizes the importance of the human rights of IDPs, returnees and all groups especially the most vulnerable groups such women, girls and minority groups. While implementing all projects, the organization will make conscious and intentional efforts to avoid all situations which expose beneficiaries to risk, threat, violence and abuse of their rights. Through the Community Committees, partner and staff members, KPHF work to foster trust and mutual understanding between various groups and to minimize conflict and reduce or stop violence. Also PHF focus on building the capacity of the staff to strengthen their understanding on the fundamental human rights and serving beneficiaries with dignity, gender and protection mainstreaming to ensure that vulnerable beneficiaries (women, girls, men and boys and minorities) are respected and feel protected.
