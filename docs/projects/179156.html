Project Summary

This project aims to address the second strategic objective of HRP shared, which aims to scale up the access to basic essential services for children. Bahar will make use of its geographical access to reach the most vulnerable groups in Idleb and Aleppo, as the number of children who were out of school due to protracted crises reached 2.45 million (according to HNO) and 1.6 of their peer are at risk of dropping out. The project is designed to promote education services for 10,000 crisis-affected children through implementing formal and non-formal education programs throughout the academic year. The project is designed to be implemented in two modalities, face-to-face or online, as a response to dynamics changes due to the Covid-19 situation and a number of cases.
1. Face-to-face modality:
Formal education is focusing on provide incentives, stationery, education supplies as well as carrying out rehabilitation for schools including its WASH facilities given gender and children with disabilities needs. Also, a comprehensive package of the capacity-building program will be in place, targeting all education staff working in those schools. 5500 children will be targeted with 10 schools located in Idleb, Atareb.
The non-formal education targets children with low attainment in their formal education with a remedial program extending for a 3-month cycle and dropped-of-school children with ALP program for at least 4 months. The targeted number is 4500 children and will be divided into cycles, 3 cycles over the project's period. Which program should the child attend, ALP or Remedial will be determined according to criteria and diagnostic test—approximately 3000 for remedial and 1500 for ALP. NFE program covers activities such as providing incentives, education supplies, capacity building program for education personnel in addition to rehabilitation of schools. BTL campaigns will be carried out in targeted communities to introduce the project.
In light of the COVID-19 outbreak, precautionary measures will take place in all schools along with conducting frequent awareness-raising sessions and posting posters on walls. In addition to sanitizing and disinfect the schools on a regular basis with the distribution of PPEs for schools' staff and children.
In accordance with COVID-19 task force recommendations that stated the number of children per class should be reduced with considering social distancing. Inclusion of children with disabilities will be through providing transportation from/to schools for both formal and non-formal education.
2. Online modality:
Bahar aims to manage changes caused by the COVID-19 outbreak and deal with partial or complete lockdown in north-west Syria especially in the project’s intervention area through carrying out online-based activities. These activities focus to deliver both formal or non-formal education through an online platform. This distribution of education supplies will be door-to-door through outreach teams to ensure that children are equipped with the necessary tools to keep up with lessons and teachers effectively. Some of the activities’ modalities of implement wouldn’t change such as rehabilitation and incentives..etc. Operational costs will be liquated to cover procuring internet cards to facilitate access of children to internet besides the teachers and education personnel.
In both modalities: 304 teachers and education personnel will be in place to implement project activities following attending a comprehensive package of training.
Regarding textbook, Qatar charity is distributing textbooks for most of the children across the North-west of Syria, and for children who haven't received any copies yet, a communication channel will be opened to attempt to secure and bridge the gap. The inclusion of PWDs will take part in all activities as they're the most vulnerable groups in communities and will be transported from/to schools.
No fund was secured yet for this project.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2021, an estimated 2.45 million children were out of school and 1.6 million children were at risk of dropping out. Due to the cumulative effect of the conflict, the closure of learning facilities, and/or reduced learning hours due to COVID-19, worsening economic conditions, increased population numbers and displacement, the numbers of children out of school, and children who are at risk of dropping out, almost certain to increase.71% of school-aged children are living in areas with severe, extreme, or catastrophic education conditions mainly concentrated in Aleppo, Idleb and Rural Damascus governorates, an increase to last year’s 86%. Due to the compounding impact of the 2020 situation, many students are either behind in their learning, finding it difficult to return to school or at risk of dropping out.With schools and learning facilities in 2020 being intermittently closed between March and December many electronic and paper-based distance education interventions were launched. The reach of this was limited due to multiple challenges including lack of electronic devices and connectivity, movement and other restrictions, and difficulty supporting and monitoring home learningFor returnees, only 19% of children with disabilities 12-23-year-old are attending school while 56% of children without disabilities 12-23-year-old are attending school in North West Syria as HNAP 2020Returnee Disability Overview reported. IDP report series of HNAP 2020 highlighted that 43% of 12–24- year-old with a disability are attending school compared to 56% of 12–24-year-old without a disability are attending school.

5 - Implementation of activities

The activities will be implemented in an efficient way to achieve the objective of the project. Preparatory meetings with local councils and education parties at initiating phase of the project will take place to ensure complementarity in implementation and providing all necessary support. This will be through ensuring the engagement of main stakeholders in decision making to make use of opinions and ideas to implement the activities in more creative ways. Monitoring and evaluation activities will extend over the project phase starting from initiation to closing of the project. Anti-covid precautionary measures-social distancing and mask wearing-will be the main feature during the implementation of activities. CSG training will be held for project staff in the line of other capacity enhancement programs. Complaints number with email will be in presences during any distribution and fixed brochure or roll up will be in schools or will be circulated through online platforms. The project's staff will ensure to targeted all communities component without any discrimination and favorites and allow any children to register in transparency.MEAL team will be recruited to monitor the project activities over the project course. A monitoring plan will be set ahead of implementation along with the implementation of a baseline survey to determine the level of children in addition to conducting FGDs with main stakeholders to introduce the project's objectives. The inclusion of PWDs takes part in almost all project activities to ease access for their to all services such as constructing a special-designed WASH facility, providing transportation..etc,.

Teachers or other education personnel participate in physical punishment, act violently, bully and neglect students. Mitigation: Learning facilities should have an enforced code of conduct. Bahar has a Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA policy that usually is attached to each employee’s job contract clarifying all the related procedures. The MEAL team will monitor if any danger is threatening the safety of the beneficiaries and will report findings in order to take the appropriate measures. Bahar team will also inform the community, particularly men and youth, about the objective of the project and that its aim in order to mitigate exposing women and girls to any potential harm or violence.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks:Risk 1: COVID-19 pandemic may impose lack of beneficiaries’ access and participation in services due to curfew restrictions, home lockdown and isolation, or people’s fear of physical interaction, as well as, the in-place approach might expose the participant to the risk of getting infected. Mitigation Measure 1: Bahar will provide sanitizers and Personal Protection Equipment PPE to beneficiaries accessing the facilities and also during the outreach activities and will ensure applying other prevention measures including social distancing, minimizing the groups sessions participants to 5-7 max. Beneficiaries will be informed also about to measures taken to encourage them to access the services. Risk 2: Breaching Bahar’s CoC, safeguarding, and PSEA policies by the project staff against the beneficiaries in an intended or unintended way. Mitigation Measure 2: Bahar includes these policies in the employees’ contracts and they are being delivered with orientation sessions by the HR department or the FP. In addition, Bahar has assigned 1 FP to conduct training on those policies, receive the allegations and act accordingly, ensure timely response and investigate the allegation in order to take suitable action against the person alleged against. Risk 3: a low turnout of parents to enroll their children in non-formal education.Mitigation: Conducting awareness campaigns in targeted communities Risk 4: Attack on schools or education centers.Mitigation: Release staff and education from school and implement safe evacuation measures. Risk 5: Parents decline to send their children with disabilities to schools.Mitigation: A awareness session about the importance of education for their children.
