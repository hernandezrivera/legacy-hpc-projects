Project Summary

AEID will provide shelter repair kits to the HHs whose houses partially or completely damaged that consider the needs of women, children, PWD and the safety of beneficiaries,
Emergency shelter and NFI kits for saving the life of the displaced persons and returnees. The purpose of the intervention is to contribute to the life-saving activities implemented by the Ethiopia ESNFI Cluster through the provision of ESNFIs, repair kits, and NFIs for IDPs and returnees.
The target for this project will be the conflict affected 29,024 people. AEID will provide repair kits for 100 HHs (450 individuals) returnees of Ataye and NKIs for the rest 2945HHS(13252 individuals) of Ataye Town returnees. Emergency shelter and NFIS kits for IDPs of Habru wereda-Jarra Site 1500HHs (6,750 IDPs), Mersa town 593HHs (2,667), and Bati town 1312HHs (5,905 IDPs) will be delivered. AEID will also construct demonstration shelters for all sites where Emergency shelter will be constructed. Project activities like project launching workshop, committee election, beneficiary registration, selection and verification, NFIs distribution, PDM, Monitoring and evaluation, regular project status reporting, engaging the targets and other stakeholder are the commonly done activities of the project. I n all project activities inclusion of PWD, vulnerable community members and gender will be well considered

1. - Projects activities

AEID will provide ES materials as per the ESNFI Cluster standard will be provided for the 3,405 target HHs. The provision of ES materials will ensure access to approximately 18M2 of safe, dry, private, and habitable covered living space per a household, which is consistent with Sphere Project guidance for the average beneficiary household of five (5) persons ((3.5 M2/person x 5 persons = 17.5M2, rounded to 18M2). Provision of this minimally adequate covered living space is important given public health concerns and specific COVID-related concerns. As well, AEID will distribute ESNFI/NFI Kits for 3,405 households. AEID will provide repair kits for, 100 households as per the ES/NFI cluster standard and sphere standards and NFI kits for 2945HHs of Ataye. Activity 1.1.1: Conduct introductory meetings to share clear communication about the selection criteria and ensure that all stakeholders understand the process. Key points to be addressed in the introductory meeting include:• Explanation of the purpose of the assistance: all stakeholders should be clear on who the target group is and why.• Identify and agree on targeted locations within the Woreda. The meeting should priorities and select areas for the specific registration of beneficiaries. • Suggest and agree a list of beneficiary selection vulnerability criteria • Agree on committee members for the actual registration of beneficiaries and prepare a plan/schedule for registration in in each location. Activity 1.1.2: Beneficiary identification and registration will take place once a committee is established for registration of beneficiaries. AIED will target 29,024 IDPs/returnees living in collective centers and who also live with the host community. The relevant parties from woreda DRMO, kebele officials and committee will work as a team, with AEID, to do the registration.Activity 1.1.3: The final verification will be done independently by AEID. The verification will be carried out at the household level by conducting a damage assessment and HLP due diligence assessment or with the beneficiary community. Groups with specific need that will be prioritized for assistance include: the most vulnerable groups including pregnant women, disadvantage/victim women, children under five, the elderly, marginalized adolescent girls and boys and persons living with a disability. In relation to this activity, complaint and feedback handling committee who will be mandated to manage the community complaint and feedback issues and coordinate with AEID will be established from the local affected community. Any complain or feedback will be recorded and addressed by the committee and AEID. Activity 1.1.4: Procurement and delivery of emergency shelter itemsActivity 1.1.5: Provide training for beneficiaries in constructing emergency shelters with the items in the kit, through constructing six demonstration shelters. The six shelters per location built as demonstration shelters will be given to the most vulnerable households (persons with special needs; disabled persons with mobility challenges and women headed HHs who have the largest under 5 children).Activity 1.1.6: Distribution of ES and Repair Kits for 6,450 IDP/Returnee households. During the distribution of ES kits, AEID will put measures in place to prevent the spread of COVID-19. Activity 1.1.7: Conduct Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM). PDM will be conducted two weeks after the emergency shelter, repair kits and non-food items kit distribution. Enumerators will be trained on how to collect data and conduct assessments using tools adapted to this project, and they will survey a sample of households in the area that received assistance.Besides, AEID follows similar steps and implement the above activities for the ESNFI items delivered in kind by the donor agencies. In addition to the above basic project activities Monitoring and evaluation and reporting shall regularly done.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

AEID establishes Compliance and Feedback Response committee from different stakeholders where the majority are elected by the IDPs/Returnees among themselves. Then the training is given on how they collect data from the beneficiaries or make discussions, collect compliance and give feedback to the complaints in the possible short period of time. The committee transfers the compliances beyond their capacity to the AEID staff or management. Then the AEID staff respond to their compliance properly using various methods. Through phone call, arranging meetings and FGDs, suggestion boxes are used as methods to collect compliances and address the complaints that come from beneficiaries.
