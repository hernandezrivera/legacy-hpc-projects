Project Summary

UNICEF will be working with the Government, local and international organizations, and United Nations agencies, to implement an integrated approach that prioritizes life-saving interventions while contributing to social cohesion, resilience, development and peace in the medium-to-long term.
UNICEF-project will support displaced populations, including in hard-to-reach areas where help is most needed. UNICEF will address their needs through the expansion of basic services to benefit relocation sites, temporary centres and host communities through a gender-sensitive and inclusive approach. For people on the move or in extremely hard-to-reach areas, UNICEF will provide immediate assistance through survival kits.
UNICEF will scale-up proven malnutrition prevention and treatment activities for children and pregnant women, including including simplified treatment approaches using community health workers, training and supply delivery to outpatient and inpatient healthcare centers, pilot implementation of family-led MUAC and expansion of nutrition prevention counseling and support through the Integrated Nutrition Package (PIN).
The prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse will be prioritized across all programmes, including community sensitization and training of government counterparts, partners, and suppliers. UNICEF will also work to strengthen feedback mechanisms and coordination with the wider humanitarian network. UNICEF will further ensure cluster coordination for nutrition.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The humanitarian situation in Mozambique remains concerning, particularly due to insecurity in Cabo Delgado Province, which has left more than 946,000 people displaced, including 517,986 children. The armed conflict has also, heightened food insecurity and malnutrition, with families forced to abandon their homes and fields, and erratic rainfall in some parts of the region has compounded crop losses, according to the latest IPC analysis. These harmful coping mechanisms combined with limited access to quality basic services is resulting in increases in on the undernutrition levels and vulnerability to childhood diseases. A sharp increase in severe acute malnutrition (SAM) admissions was observed in 2022, with 46 per cent increase compared to similar months in 2021. This is probably due to improved access to hard-to-reach areas in northern Cabo Delgado, coupled with a more stable pipeline of nutrition supplies, resulting in better capacity to screen and treat children with malnutrition. further deterioration of nutrition conditions in Northern Mozambique is expected in the first quarter of 2023, with a projected 98,440 children under five years old (40,076 with severe acute malnutrition and 58,364 for moderate acute malnutrition) and 7,884 pregnant and lactating women in need of lifesaving treatment for acute malnutrition in 9 out of 17 districts of Cabo Delgado (IPC AMN, 2022) from April 2022 to March 2023. To respond to the critical nutrition needs, UNICEF will provide life-saving, integrated and gender-responsive assistance to vulnerable populations including girls, adolescents, caregivers and people with disabilities. UNICEF will scale up malnutrition prevention and treatment. This work includes training and supply delivery to health care centres, expansion of nutrition counselling and support for providing simplified treatment using community workers. UNICEF will increase the coverage and quality of community-based integrated health and nutrition services, while supporting mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) integrated in the awareness sessions together with HIV and GBV prevention, and parenting care. Breastfeeding corners will be integrated in existing safe spaces initiative.UNICEF’s proposed project contributes to strengthening humanitarian-development- peace nexus through system strengthening and community mobilization increasing trust in service delivery by affected population; the approach considers three focus areas: service delivery, social behaviour change and creating an enabling environment - which are interconnected and mutually reinforcing and aim to strengthen the transformative and adaptive capacities of individuals and communities, and the institutions and systems that provide services to them.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Objective 1: Lifesaving nutrition humanitarian response to IDPs and host communities affected by conflict1.1. Procure and distribute (to the end user) lifesaving nutrition treatment supplies (RUTF, F-75 and F-100) to treat children with SAM 1.2. Expand implementation of Nutrition Rehabilitation Programme (PRN) at the hospital, health centre and community level through TDC and pilot of simplified approached for SAM treatment.1.3. On-the-job training and mentoring of health staff operating at the facility-based treatment of acute malnutrition 1.4. Enforcement of Breastmilk Substitutes Code through capacity building of humanitarian partners on the implementation of the code and provision of adequate counselling and support to breastfeeding mothers and families.1.5. Expansion of nutrition screenings at community level to reach children aged under five years with services for the early detection and treatment of severe wasting and other forms of life-threatening acute malnutrition in early childhood Objective 2: Lifesustaining nutrition humanitarian response to IDPs and host communities affected by conflict2.1. Integrated Mobile Brigades to deliver key life-saving services including Micronutrient supplementation (Vit A) and deworming, optimal nutrition counseling, infant and young child feeding practices, signs and symptoms of acute malnutrition, WASH, child protection and PSEA. 2.3. Implementation of PIN (Integrated Nutrition Package) in emergency settings.2.4. Conduct nutrition SMART assessments, IPC analysis and assessments in most affected sites to monitor the nutrition situation and inform program implementation. 2.5. Conduct rapid needs assessments following climate-induced shocks and sudden emergencies2.6. Supply chain systems strengthening to ensure real time monitoring of supplies delivered

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Protection issues for nutrition mainly concern the security while accessing and utilizing nutrition services delivered at health facility and mobile brigades which are normally located and built in such a way that limits protection risks. Nutrition humanitarian partners will be trained on specific protection considerations and referrals pathways, in close collaboration with child protection services to support protection mechanisms for vulnerable children.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The project will promote protection, participation, empowerment and solidarity in contributing to leaving no child behind with a focus on stronger nutrition care, by strengthening trusted health, food and community systems (institution building and strengthening of governance) to prevent undernutrition and promote good health while ensuring food/nutrition security, considering a context of public health emergency outbreaks. The project contributes to enhancing meaningful access to universal care through overcoming vulnerabilities of the administrative system in the health sector and promoting positive societal practices to address root causes of inequality in accessing essential and life-saving nutrition services.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Malnutrition and disability are closely interrelated and have a number of points of convergence. Commonly this relationship is framed as a lack of appropriate nutrition in both pregnant and breastfeeding mothers and young children leading to impairment. Therefore, UNICEF efforts focus on improving maternal and child nutrition to prevent or mitigate disability, while strengthening the early identification of developmental delays among children with SAM.The inclusion of children with disabilities will be achieved through a) capacity building of UNICEF’s implementing partner; b) training of health workers on early identification of developmental delays; c) data collection/disaggregation for medical mobile brigades and d) mainstreaming disability and identification of development delays among SAM children.Case management of acute malnutrition services will be adapted to include children with disabilities or caregivers of children with disabilities as 5 per cent of the total target.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Climate change exacerbate undernutrition through three main causal pathways: 1) impacts on household access to sufficient, safe and adequate food; 2) impacts on care and feeding practices; and 3) impacts on environmental health and access to health services.The Global Climate Risk Index scores Mozambique fifth among the countries most affected by natural disasters over the period 2000-2019, and first in 2019, having experienced cyclones of unprecedented severity that affected nearly 2.5 million people, many of them from Northern provinces. Aligned with the UNICEF Nutrition Strategy 2020-2030, UNICEF will: a) mainstream assessments on impact of climate change related effects on maternal and child diets in the planned nutrition assessments, b) increase knowledge and awareness raising for action through SBCC programs for use of locally adapted foods who are more resilient to climate change and c) support implementation of climate resilient emergency programming and adaptations.
