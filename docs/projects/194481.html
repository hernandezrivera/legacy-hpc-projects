Project Summary

Relief Agency (RA) proposed to implement this project targeting IDPs, Returnees and Host communities affected by flood and conflict in Unity and Jonglei states. The project will target 8,000 beneficiaries disaggregated as: 5,000 women, 2,000 girls, 1,500 men and 1,500 boys reached. 15% of persons with disabilities will also benefit through GBV awareness raising, door to door meetings, individual counseling and through other mobile emergency services. Relief Agency (RA) has been operating in Southern Unity in Panyijiar, Leer and Mayendit counties and in Jonglei state of Duk, Twic east and Akobo counties by providing Protection and GBV services such as monitoring and data collection/assessment relating to GBV/Protection risks, returnees needs monitoring, advocacy on gender equality and inclusion through local engagement, awareness raising on protection/GBV concerns, youth and women empowerment activities and WGFS programming and referrals. RA will continue to cement on the existing support in-line with Cluster strategic objective and HRP 2022-2023. RA will further expand its areas of coverage to Mayom, Koch and Pibor counties to increase on the population caseload reached while mitigating GBV risks in the targeting locations.

In order to ensure durable solution is achieved towards combating GBV, Gender and Disability Inclusion gaps in the community, Relief Agency (RA) will work in collaboration with the existing local stakeholders to mainstream local solutions while uplifting centrality of protection to ensure safety and dignity of the affected persons. RA will work in collaboration with WASH to support women and girls with the MH Kits or dignity kits, Education partners or cluster to raise awareness on gender consideration toward equal opportunity to education between girls and boys and health partners to support the survivors of rapes while providing specialized GBV services such as PSS, case management and counseling and referrals for MHPSS or CMR. RA will also work with local structures to strengthen local policies that advocate for Gender equality, Disability inclusion and mitigate GBV in the community. RA will strengthen institutional policies, standards and practices that drive action and accountability to GBV and equality. Relief Agency (RA) will continue to identify and explore existing opportunities within the cultures to mitigate GBV and enhance the enablers that bridge gaps of gender and disability inclusion.
Relief Agency (RA) will establish safe space programming such as women and girls’ friendly spaces to carryout recreational activities, counseling, PSS, case management and facilitate referrals that link beneficiaries to the available services. Local leaders and community stakeholders’ groups will be engaged and trained to become the champion for GBV, PwD and gender inclusion in the community. Other component to mainstream include training of other partners on AAP, PSEA and Do No Harm Principles and protection/GBV mainstreaming approaches.

1 - Needs

According to Humanitarian Needs Overviews (HNO) 2022, the GBV concerns remained a threat to most women and girls in the country and much worse in the targeting locations. An estimated 2.6 million people are at risk to face GBV threats this year. This is 25% increased compare to last year 2021. GBV is reported to be high in some areas including in the counties targeted by this project. The situation is exacerbated by flooding and conflict leaving women and girls more vulnerable. Women feel unsafe to carry-out their routine duties such as collection of firewood and charcoals, access to water points, latrines and to the distribution sites including access to market places has been restricted. While justice and human rights are suppressed in the country and in the targeted areas in particular, many violations are under reported. The vulnerable women and girls, children, older persons and persons with disabilities are more expose to risks of exploitation with no access to justice and rule of law. Continuation of subnational conflict is likely to disrupt the livelihoods and increase humanitarian needs leaving women to pressing economic situation causing physical and mental health deterioration.Early this year according to Inter-Agency Assessment Report 19/2/2022 lead by UNHCR in Leer county, the Southern Unity have been affected by consecutive events of conflicts combined with flooding causing mass displacement with youth clashes of Mayendit and Leer counties following by the change of control between government and the opposition forces. 6,498 HHs displaced to Leer county areas according to RRC data report. RA field reports on GBV and Protection intervention further highlighted a concerning situation as well. The two states in the targeted counties in particular continued to experience cattle raiding and ambushes restricting humanitarian access and intercommunal fighting exposing women and girls to high level of rapes, sexual assaults and domestic violence. The negative cultural practices biased against women and girls, poverty and constraint to access to services pushes women and girls to become more vulnerable. Lack of legal and judicial system limit provision for legal redress in response to gender based violations. The medical, mental health and psychosocial support services have diminished as the country rages with no support mechanisms. Through this lifesaving project, RA will work with women, girls, boys, men, leaders, clusters, existing partners, local actors and persons with disabilities to combat GBV and gender barriers and create safe environment for vulnerable persons among the IDPs, Returnees and Host communities.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

RA has a trained project team and have clear understanding on the PSEA internal policy. The key local authorities and other institutions heads or representatives such as schools, human rights activists and civil societies actors and local or traditional leaders etc are targeted and will be train on the PSEA. The PSEA policy will be integrated in the implementation of the project activities and the beneficiaries will be aware in-order to protect themselves.

The collected information from the beneficiaries will be use to evaluate the project progress and the impact. The information will further be use for wider advocacy and share widely with other stakeholders to further provide support need and ensure the protection of the community in needs. During this process, RA will consider do no harm and ensure integrity on the information provided by the beneficiaries or the community members.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Relief Agency (RA) will integrates protection principles such as Do No Harm while implementing this project. The safety and dignity of the beneficiaries is very important to receive dignified services. During registration of beneficiaries or distribution of food or non food items to the beneficiaries, focus will be place on the safety of vulnerable individuals such as women and disable persons to equally access services and protection or GBV risks will be measure and mitigation measures will be lay out. To avoid exposing vulnerable community to higher risks, services will be provided closer to them for easy and safe access to them. The community will be participating in this regards to decide where local activities can be done. Women and girls will be advise on travels through long distance and in timely manner. Training will be provided to local NGO staff who provide humanitarian services and have interactions with beneficiaries and training them on PSEA, Do No Harm, AAP and other safety and security lessons needed for effective services delivery. Relief Agency will provide an equal participation of the beneficiaries. The persons with disabilities and women and girls will be target considering other persons with specific needs. PSEA policies, Child Safeguard, HR policies and Code of Conduct will be incorporated in the programming to ensure those in power or the staff or humanitarian actors don't exploit the vulnerable groups. Relief Agency will uses a consultative services delivery approach that involve every targeted community members. Their concerns and feedbacks will be collected and uses to reflect the project implementation and measures the impact. Relief Agency staff are trained on confidentiality to avoid doing more harms to the people in needs. During the launching of the project, each targeted stakeholder is provided with information needed and the project target. The project however has limitation in not meeting all the community needs however doing the best to stabilize its target locations and the community members involved. As part of RA values, we practices integrity toward our work. RA will observe the ethical handling of data collected from the beneficiaries and act accordingly base on internal policies.
