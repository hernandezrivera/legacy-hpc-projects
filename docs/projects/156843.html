Project Summary

Solidarités International (SI), will support equitable and safe access to essential basic services including WASH, and Shelter, for the most vulnerable populations affected by conflict and displacement in Northeast Syria (NES.). The program strategy is grounded in needs assessments, contextual analysis and scenario planning. In the areas of intervention, HNO shows 1.2m are facing acute WASH needs, 1.5m acute Health needs, 590,511 acute Shelter needs and 1m acute NFI needs, which negatively impacted their health and well-being. In the past 12 months, of the 1.2m displaced, 241,329 (19.6%) was reported to be from DEZ governorate, due to recent clashes between Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant (ISIL), Syrian Democratic Forces (SDF) and Syrian Arab Army (SAA). Displacement from the Ar Raqqa offensive has exceeded 400,000 people in the last year alone. These trends are likely to continue with IDP flows entering Ar Raqqa, DEZ, Al Hasakah, and Aleppo governorates. Based on acute inter-sectoral needs, the Governorates of DEZ, AR-Raqqa, Al-Hassakeh and Aleppo have been prioritized for the intervention.
LEARN will provide an integrated and harmonized multi-sectoral package of comprehensive essential services for vulnerable conflict –affected populations, drawing on demonstrable technical capacity in Heath, Shelter, WASH and protection. To ensure effective delivery of the program, LEARN will operate under a field-based coordination unit. LEARN will capitalize on its comparative advantage of having efficient, flexible and nimble systems that can adapt to the rapidly changing context and needs. It will draw on widespread and established operational capacity, presence, and community networks. The Consortium will prioritize the specific needs of highly vulnerable households, such as those headed by females as well as elderly, children and people with disabilities. The Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) will provide timely assistance to meet new humanitarian needs across the whole of NES, under an integrated model and joint framework. This mechanism will see LEARN initiate coordinated, rapid response to sudden onset displacement and other humanitarian crises such as health epidemics within 24-48 hours. Rapid Need Assessments (RNAs) will be conducted concurrently with initial life-saving activities, followed by delivery of a comprehensive package of emergency services. The intervention will strengthen the skills and knowledge of communities and aim at building resilience and the ability to cope with, respond to and recover from future shocks and stresses. LEARN will utilize a joint, integrated data management and M&E system, drawing on extensive expertise and strong Complaints Response Mechanisms (CRM) capacity. LEARN Consortium will focus on ensuring monitoring data, learning and beneficiary feedback is integrated into the program cycle. LEARN will continue to proactively take a leading role in relevant cluster and working groups, thus further broadening the coordinated and coherent approach across the consortium partners into the wider humanitarian community. LEARN’s value is significantly greater than its constituent partners, promoting synergies to maximize coherent and holistic responses in areas of greatest need. LEARN management and governance structure is based on robust processes and controls, which will guide operations, as well as swift and effective decision-making, risk management, communication, grant management and compliance. This will ensure accountability and effective management.

Needs

Ar-Raqqa: Displacement from the Ar Raqqa offensive has exceeded 400,000 people in the last year alone. Despite widespread damage, high levels of unexploded ordnance (UXO) contamination and a lack of basic services, residents began returning to their homes immediately after the cessation of conflict in November 2017. REACH reported 25,000 HH had returned to the city in February 2018. DEZ: Settlements in DEZ are distributed on both sides of the Euphrates River, which divides the governorate. Following a two-month offensive, which saw ISIL lose control, the GoS-aligned forces gained control over the west of the Euphrates river including DEZ city, at the beginning of November 2017, and Al-Mayadin city, while the SDF have gained control of the former ISIL-held territory north of the Euphrates following the Khabur river. The renewed conflict since September has led to massive displacements. Menbij (Aleppo Governorate): In 2017, the launch of the Euphrates Shield Operation by the Turkish army, as well as the military advance by the SDF (Syrian Democratic Forces), supported by the international coalition, managed to push the IS back and to access the Eastern part of Aleppo governorate, including Menbij and Abu Qalqal Sub-Districts. The above, in addition to GoS offensives in other parts of the country, has led to large numbers of displaced and returnee populations arriving in the city and basic service provision across critical sectors remains limited. More than 5,000 people were reported to be highly vulnerable (large families, older persons, people with chronic illness, people with low mobility). The municipal council estimated that the percentage of displaced people in Menbij is 37% of the current population, which is estimated at 190,000 people in total. The context in the North East remains highly volatile, with tensions likely to arise between the SARG Forces and the SDF, as well as within areas captured by SDF. Tensions between GoS and SDF along the Euphrates has persisted and competition over the control of natural resources remains a source of potential future conflict. Thus, new shocks and displacements are anticipated to happen in 2018, and will require timely, well-coordinated humanitarian response

Implementation of activities

RRM: The Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) will provide timely assistance to meet new humanitarian needs across the whole of NES, under an integrated model and joint framework. This mechanism will see LEARN initiate coordinated, rapid response to sudden onset displacement and other humanitarian crises such as health epidemics within 24-48 hours. Rapid Need Assessments (RNAs) will be conducted concurrently with initial life-saving activities, followed by delivery of a comprehensive package of emergency services. Emergency Water Supply Systems Based upon initial assessment, emergency water will be delivered through water trucking to targeted populations, if no other option is available. A certification system will allow users to identify trained and reliable water suppliers and direct their choice accordingly. This will encourage water suppliers to comply with best practices. LEARN will continuously monitor the water market (in terms of availability, quality and prices) to ensure that water remains affordable to the target communities, according to baseline level Water supply rehabilitation At the start of the project, as well as on a continuous basis throughout the course of the grant, assessments will be conducted to confirm target sites with the local authorities, as well as commencing rehabilitation in some locations initially targeted. Designs (BoQ and Technical specification) will be finalized in close collaboration with relevant technical departments at project outset following detailed assessments Provision of sanitation facilities: LEARN Consortium will provide 500 sanitation facilities (package will include latrines with hand washing facilities, bathing) in camps and IDP Informal Settlements in the four location (Aleppo, Ar Raqqa, DEZ and Ras Al Ain), in line with SPHERE standards by gender, with a 3:1 ratio of female to male facilities. WASTE management In IDP Informal settlements: In new informal settlements, the project will provide one waste container of 100L per 10 households according to SPHERE Standards. In total, 100 waste containers will be distributed (1 for 200 HH) and 1,000 small ones (1 per HH). Distribution of NFIs LEARN Consortium will follow WASH Cluster recommendation for Hygiene kits and Hygiene Kit Top Ups, provided with the same minimum standards Hygiene Promotion Hygiene promotion will be tailored to each community following the results of a baseline KAP survey as well as on-going community consultation throughout the implementation period Hygiene awareness campaigns: Tailored hygiene awareness campaigns will be conducted. LEARN will design the team to reach a ratio (1: 500 individuals), working with Hygiene Promoters from the community to reinforce the acceptance in the target locations and reinforce local capacity. Campaigns will complement the work of CHWs supported by the health partner in target areas. Based on previous experience, trigger and nudge communications will be used.

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection and gender mainstreaming LEARN has an extensive understanding of the diverse vulnerabilities regarding gender, age and other vulnerable groups, whose needs are taken into account at all stages of program design, implementation and monitoring. Specific and disaggregated data and information regarding gender, age and vulnerable groups will be collected during the needs assessment to support ongoing adaptation of the intervention. When conducting FGDs, partners will ensure that all gender, age and vulnerable groups are consulted and represented and have the best understanding of the situation. Activities are tailored to meet all the beneficiaries' specific needs: the content of hygiene kits, the topics of the hygiene promotion sessions, the design and site location of latrines and showers will be discussed with the different recipients, with a particular focus on women, children, elderly and people with disability; as well as by BO for the provision of health services for women/girls. Partners provide beneficiaries the opportunity to give feedback and raise complaints through different channels accessible to all. PDM surveys and regular monitoring activities will be organized taking into account all gender and age differences in order to obtain reliable and representative data and feedback, disaggregated by gender and age. To ensure that protection mainstreaming requirements are met, LEARN will put in place a series of measures to mitigate against any risks related to its WASH support. The core commitment of LEARN partners will be to reach the following key principles of action: - Ensuring safety and dignity - Ensuring meaningful access - Contributing to Accountability, Participation & Empowerment The initial analysis will aim to understand the prevalence of persons with specific needs, related to physical (age, disability, gender) and social (minorities, isolated persons / mothers etc.) factors. Assessments will therefore pre-identify those people, and a specific focus will be implemented to refine the degree to which their conditions require specific attention. Instruments such as the Washington Group short set of questions will be used to identify physical disability, while social discrimination risks will be identified through ensuring thorough knowledge of communities supported, thanks to presence of LEARN members’ staff in the areas of intervention and the community networks and committees the project will foster. Within implementation, the following measures will be taken • The facilities developed and/or rehabilitated throughout the project, especially in IDP settlements, will be set in locations that allow reducing the threats related to their access. • Community consultations will be held to ensure appropriate choice for these locations. The geographical repartition of facilities will help minimize the distance to/from the facilities to shelters. • Water and Sanitation facilities will be equipped with lightning devices (solar-based solutions), and will be separated for male and female users. • The design of emergency latrines should consider the diversity of population (age, gender, disability). As much as required, accessibility features will be installed in proportion with the population with specific needs related to mobility and/or sensorial impairment – ramps, railing, commode). • Hygiene Promotion messages will be delivered through various media to ensure that they can be accessible to all – posters, oral messages, written and illustrated etc. Technical design are available (IFRC’s All Under One Roof as well as technical designs developed by HI or CBM, for instance, will be made available) and will be enforced by LEARN Protection Specialist. Should the literature not be sufficient, specific advisory will be sought at agencies that have the relevant expertise locally to provide support on detection of persons with specific needs, and to adapt services accordingly.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The program design process and ongoing program cycle management, is based on current needs assessment data and displacement trends, as well as detailed scenario projections. As the situation in NES is constantly developing, and the potential deterioration in the existing situation is significant, this approach enables SI to anticipate emerging crisis, and determine planning assumptions regarding how the situation in NES will develop over the next two years and the potential humanitarian impact. It also provides the basis for contingency planning, which is essential to ensure the Consortium can react in a timely manner, remain agile and adapt to the rapidly changing context. Scenario planning was conducted following ACAPs evidence-based methodology. It provides detailed analysis of the crisis drivers, defines assumptions and provides expected outcomes and projected humanitarian impact. The subsequent contingency plans will ensure that adequate and appropriate measures are taken in advance to ensure rapid response in a timely, effective and appropriate way, which meets the needs of the affected population. This planning process supports advanced decision-making regarding the management of human and financial resources, coordination and communications procedures, and the development of technical and logistical responses. Appropriate preparedness actions including contingency stocks have been included in the program design to ensure SI is adequately prepared and adaptable to the potential contextual developments. The program management approach, scenario planning and contingency planning will be led by the CD and DCD. CD is responsible for the first update to contingency planning within six months, and then monthly risk analysis, and scenario review going forward. The DCD will be responsible for contingency planning, updated on a quarterly basis.
