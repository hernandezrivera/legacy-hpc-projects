Project Summary

Medair handed over in Aweil Centre to ACF by January 2021, and shifted multisectoral programming to the Pibor area given the high needs identified in several assessments and the exit of MSF. The total targets for 2021 based on Medair’s mapped catchment populations and at least 60% coverage in all areas will be 3349 SAM admissions, and 10,243 U5 MAM and 4,791 PLWs. Medair will continue to integrate MIYCN at both community and health and nutrition site levels and will support VAS in Medair’s catchment areas when no other actors are conducting.
In Renk, there has been a consistent nutritional emergency for many years with the last GAM measured in June 2019 alarmingly high at 32.1%. Medair is the only nutritional actor in the county and is doing multisectoral programming—health, nutrition and WASH. NGOs evacuated in October 2020, Medair is currently negotiating re-entry but anticipates the needs to be even higher. Medair is hoping to implement over at least a 9-month period depending upon access and security.
The 2019-SMART survey in Leer County measured a GAM of 14.3% (SAM 3.2%). Due to the COVID-19 pandemic this has not been repeated; Medair is the only nutrition actor covering a catchment population of 40,000 in Padeah, Gandor, Guat, Adok, Dindin and Bow in Leer County and 1 site Luol in Mayendit County. Medair also conducts nutrition outreach services to Meer Islands. Other nutrition actors cover other areas of Mayendit and Leer Counties. Medair is continuing multisectoral programming—community health, nutrition—CMAM and MIYCN via Care Groups (CG) WASH, and MHPSS to address some of the underlying factors.
MSF exited Pibor and requested Medair to take over their nutrition services including a stabilization centre in Pibor. The ERT initially responded and the program is being transitioned to a multisectoral longer term approach. Several Medair and OCHA assessments have been done this past year identifying high needs in all areas. The recent IPC classification predicts extremely critical levels of acute malnutrition that classifies Pibor (Pibor Town, Gumuruk, Lekuangole, and Verteth) as IPC 5, “famine likely” (HNO 2021). Recent NC led mapping with the current nutrition actors Plan and JAM concluded that there were no geographic areas needing coverage of OTP and TSFP services by Medair. Medair continues to run the SC and set up multisectoral programming in Gumuruk and Lekuangole (est population of 55,126 (Feb 2021 OCV Campaign)). As part of Medair’s health and WASH programming Medair will set up a community CG network which will cover MIYCN and other public health topics as well as ensure Mother’s MUAC is established and well linked to Plan and JAM’s services through referrals. Health facilities will also conduct nutritional screening and referral of U5 and PLWs as part of BPHNS. As no actor is addressing at risk infants <6 mo, Medair explores integrating MAMI with the planned health services in 2 PHCUs. To avoid duplication, Medair will continue to coordinate activities with JAM/PLAN to manage all the coexisting illness referrals. Minimum WASH package of safe water and latrine access, including waste management will be supported in all project sites. Where possible, linkage with FSL partners will occur, and child friendly spaces and early child development activities will be promoted.
ERT continues to respond to emergency levels of acute malnutrition by establishing CMAM services to address the most lifesaving needs, in areas with high GAM and low programme coverage. The RRTF in collaboration with the NC give input on priority areas and intervention modalities in scenarios characterised by agreed triggers where other actors are absent or unable to fully intervene. The team will work with the partner on the ground to identify areas for capacity building for quality service delivery. The team will provide nutrition emergency services for up to 3 months and thereafter handover services to an identified partner before exiting

1 - Needs

As noted above there are emergency levels of GAM at all Medair’s projected intervention locations. There have been emergency levels in Renk County since 2014 and with the recent suspension of programming since October 2020 due to insecurities the situation is likely to much worse. The most recent exhaustive mass MUAC screening (March 2020) done by Medair highlighted the need for more decentralised coverage of nutrition services, as the coverage rate ranged between 40% and 60% for acute malnutrition. As soon as Medair can reenter an initial more emergency approach will be needed and then reestablishment of the fixed and outreach locations. Medair will need to conduct rapid MUAC screening (with COVID-19 adaptations for Mother’s MUAC) and rapid food distributions to save lives using a phased approach to open up all sites for continued service delivery. A SMART survey will be planned to measure GAM this year when access permits and when it can be conducted safely with COVID-19 transmission. . Poor dietary diversity, along with the lack of timely initiation of complementary feeding at the age of 6 months, continue to be the most concerning IYCF behaviors which need to be emphasised further through Care groups and at facility level including with cooking demonstrations.In Leer and Mayendit, Medair currently runs nutrition services in 7 sites covering a portion of Leer County and 1 location in Mayendit. Nutrition partner UNIDOR is also present in Leer town and Mayendit running OTP/TFSP sites. Medair covers a catchment population of 40,000 in Meer islands, Padeah, Dindin, Gandor, Guat, Adok, Lual and Bow through a blend of mobile outreaches and static sites that fluctuate based on the security situation. . The population has experienced recurrent displacement and disruption of services due to repeated waves of conflict and because the counties lie on the boundary between the main competing political factions. A mass MUAC screening in February 2020 revealed a ‘proxy GAM’ of 8.4%, corresponding to very critical levels of acute malnutrition (IPC 5). Medair continues to provide nutrition, WASH, community health, and mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS). MIYCN is integrated at nutrition sites and via the Care Group network. Medair led household surveys as in other locations show a need for improved complementary feeding and dietary diversity in IYCF. Medair will continue providing lifesaving services in nutrition, health, WASH and MHPSS in up to 8 sites. Pibor: Pibor has experienced multiple emergency humanitarian situations due to floods and intercommunal violence for the past two years with significant impact on health and nutrition services. Throughout Jonglei State, the insufficient number of health facilities and staff is further aggravated by limited road access during rainy season, recurrent flooding, drought and outbreaks, and continued violence. The HNO 2021 report classified parts of Greater Pibor Administrative Area as IPC 5, “famine likely”. The COVID-19 pandemic, sub-national violence, and floods have led to population displacement coupled with a decrease in nutrition coverage. MSF who had been running the SC and mobile health and nutrition outreaches recently exited and handed over the SC to Medair’s ERT who had been periodically implementing emergency responses in the area over the past year. The community have no safe water resources in their area. The majority boreholes are not functional due to lack of maintenance or broken due to intercommunal violence that occurred in 2020. Communities access water from a seasonal river and open defecation is a common practice. There are high levels of morbidity and mortality in children U5, low vaccination coverage, poor IYCF and health seeking behaviors. 2 - Project activities

Infant Young Child Feeding and Behaviour ChangeIn Renk, Leer, and Pibor, Medair will use the National MIYCN Guidelines to govern the dissemination of IYCF messaging. This will be integrated into each level of contact beneficiaries have with staff throughout the CMAM programmes as well as Care Groups to provide sufficient opportunities for knowledge acquisition and to promote behaviour change. Child friendly spaces, where they are not already established, will be used as an opportunity to support mothers, in particular in the violence-affected areas of Leer, Pibor and Renk. Management of Acute MalnutritionMedair proposes to continue treating children under 5 years for SAM and MAM, through the provision of the CMAM programme (static, mobile and outreach sites) in up to 40 locations including up to 17 in Renk, 1 SC in Pibor, up to 7 in Leer, and 15 in ERT. Medair will use the 2016 National Ministry of Health approved CMAM Guidelines to govern implementation of the Nutrition Programme. As per standard protocol, all children with complicated SAM will be referred to an SC run by Medair (i.e. Renk and Pibor) or another implementing agency. OTP and TSFP will either be integrated into existing Medair emergency health services (as is the case in Wunthou and Abayok in Renk), established alongside health activities being implemented by other actors (as is the case across all sites in Leer and Mayendit) or will consist of screening in supported health facilities and referring children and U5 and PLWs to the nutritional actors in the area (Lekuangole and Gumuruk) whilst receiving malnourished children that require coexisting illness treatment at the PHCUs.. The community mobilisation activities will rely on the development of networks of Community Nutrition Volunteers/Workers (CNVs and CNWs) in Renk, Leer and ERT as well as Care Groups in Pibor, Renk and Leer. Preventative measures to mitigate the risk of developing acute malnutrition will be put in place and will include activities of behaviour change communication targeting maternal, infant and young child feeding practices (noted above), participation in the National Immunization Days (NIDs), as well as periodic campaigns of mass distribution of Vitamin A (children aged 6-59 months) and deworming with albendazole (children aged 12-59 months) when COVID-19 transmission permits and other actors are not conducting. IntegrationAs explained above, the minimal WASH package, health package (RDT and measles vaccination; provision of amoxicillin, and deworming according to the CMAM protocol, management of co-existing illnesses; referral to the stabilisation centre); FSL linkage and ECD activities will be implemented in the patient’s best interest and according to the directives of the South Sudan Nutrition Cluster. The Emergency Response Team (ERT) will aim to provide the full CMAM package in all emergency interventions as needed. The actual response will depend on the needs and gaps identified in the Needs Assessment. Emergency nutrition interventions will be rolled out in the following cluster rapid response phased model depending on security and accessibility of target areas:• Phase 1: High insecurity location with limited accessibility, day trips: MUAC screening at a central location and BP5 distribution • Phase 2: High GAM/immediate need to respond, limited access and absence /lack of capacity of nutrition actor: MUAC screening, provision of 2-week ration for MAM and SAM with deworming, key MIYCN/HHP messaging and follow-up visits as security permits• Phase 3: Secure Location with access and absence /lack of capacity of nutrition actor: establish full CMAM programme, identify or capacity building of long-term partners for handover

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

All Medair staff receive training in Protection against Sexual Exploitation and abuse

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Through a functional beneficiary feedback system beneficiary will be able to provide feedback through direct contact with Medair staff, feedback boxes sited in all clinics and or a dedicated phone line. To ensure the project provides feedback to the most vulnerable members in the community existing communication channels e.g. community leaders, holding community meetings, care group meetings, key informant will be used to deliver messages.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Medair implements mother-to-mother peer-support ‘Care Groups’ in all static locations, in which information is cascaded through communities, and feedback is gathered from the Care Groups. In addition, Medair consults regularly with community leaders and other key stakeholders to update them regarding the initiation of or new developments within interventions.Through a network of community nutrition volunteers/workers who do house-to-house outreach, communities are regularly informed about existing services. When the emergency response team deploys for an assessment, they initially meet with community leaders to inform them about the objectives of the assessment Based on the needs found in the assessment, Medair will decide in collaboration with the cluster whether an intervention is appropriate. Local authorities and community leaders will be informed about the outcome. When an emergency intervention is conducted, continuous messaging through a mixture of channels as mentioned above will ensure the communities are well informed—including through the use of CNVs going door-to-door.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

A database is used to capture beneficiary feedback received through the various channels and response to beneficiary feedback will be processed depending on the nature of the feedback and it was sent back to the beneficiaries either through Medair staff or a community meeting, care group meetings or key informants. Community chiefs and local authorities are regularly updated regarding any anticipated changes in programme activities and the Medair community liaison officers provide an on-going contact point for beneficiaries and other community members to provide feedback. Feedback can be used to improve on the service delivery to the community.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

• Risks that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services Medair coordinates with protection partners to conduct in-depth protection assessments as needed. Mobile nutrition sites reduce the protection risks faced by women and children who otherwise would have to travel to distant facilities. Nutrition facility locations are informed by protection-related considerations. • Risks of women, girls and boys without and with disabilities exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance. All Medair staff receive training in Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, and relocatable staff are trained in protection-sensitive programming as part of Medair’s standard briefing procedures. • Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.) Through Care Group and Cascade Groups (specifically in Leer), women are made aware of existing pathways and service available to timely address protection incidents • Risk that marginalised groups, persons with disabilities and Older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities Barriers to access to services are identified via FGD and mother-to-mother Care/Cascade group meetings. Furthermore, regular mass MUAC screening provides information on the coverage of nutrition services. If coverage is limited, the community outreach component will be strengthened.
