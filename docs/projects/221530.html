Project Summary

The Northwest and Southwest crisis in Cameroon has had a profound impact on the educational system in these regions. The crisis has severely disrupted school operations across the Northwest and Southwest regions. Due to violence, threats, and general insecurity, many schools have been forced to close, and students and educators have been unable to attend classes regularly and an estimated 1,033,000 school-aged children have been affected (Cameroon Education cluster Strategy-2023-2025) . In 2022-2023 academic year a total of 3,502 (54%) of schools were not operational. Recent statistics from the ministries of basic and secondary education shows that there is a steady increase in the number of functional schools in the affected regions (4,195-64% of functional schools). Despite this increase, 2,362(36%) schools remain non-functional. There is a clear indication from the statistics that the crisis still has a direct impact on the continuity of education for children and youth. The conflict has caused significant internal displacement, with families fleeing their homes to seek safety in other areas. This has resulted in significant challenges regarding access to education for displaced children, as many have been unable to attend school due to the breakdown of the educational infrastructure and the need to prioritize safety and survival. The insecurity and violence have created an environment of fear and trauma for students, teachers, and educational staff. Children and youth have been exposed to traumatic experiences, and this has had a detrimental impact on their mental well-being and ability to engage in learning effectively. Addressing the effects of the crisis on education will require comprehensive efforts to rebuild educational infrastructure, support the psychosocial well-being of students and educators, and create opportunities for displaced and affected communities to access quality inclusive education.
In 2021-2022, only 12,321 children with disabilities (including 51% physically impaired, 30% visually and 19% hearing impaired children) attended primary education services . According to education cluster partners, between 3 to 5% of school-age children live with a disability. Addressing the effects of the crisis on education will require comprehensive efforts to rebuild educational infrastructure, support the psychosocial well-being of students and educators, and create opportunities for displaced and affected communities to access quality education.
General Objectives
To Providing quality access to inclusive learning opportunities in a safe and protective learning environment to 5000 school aged children (3-17) yrs.
Specific objectives
1) To provide school fees to 5000 out of school children
2) To integrate the Radio education program in formal learning spaces
3) To train teachers, facilitators and other education personnel on inclusive education in a protective learning environment
