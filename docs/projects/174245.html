Project Summary

The proposed project aims to reduce morbidity and mortality due to severe and moderate acute malnutrition in displaced populations and acutely vulnerable host communities by improving the availability, utilization and quality of essential preventative and curative nutrition services for boys and girls under five and pregnant and lactating women (PLW). Tdh will respond in locations where the IPC classification of nutrition needs is serious or critical (phase 3 or 4). In the GSSS IDP Camp and Ngwom-Host community both in Mafa LGA. Tdh will provide the full integrated CMAM programme including IYCF, micronutrient supplementation, active case finding of malnourished children and defaulter tracking in both locations, and support referral of SAM cases with medical complications from the OTP to a stabilization center.
Tdh has been working in Mafa Community since 2019 as a major actor in the provision of MNCH services, However, a critical gap has been identified in the lack of access to nutrition services within the IDP camp and the HC.
The Logic of intervention is built on three core components:
1. Capacity Building; Health workers, Nutrition assistants, Community health extension workers (CHEWS) will be trained on the national CMAM protocols and guidelines and will be provided with on the job coaching and supports based on the identified needs. Community health volunteers will also be identified and trained on MUAC screening, active case finding and safe referrals to the health facility/ OTP site. In addition, care givers will be mentored on MUAC screening at home and IYCF practices, who will then serve as agent of change within the community.
2. Active case finding and Management of Children with severe acute malnutrition and Moderate acute malnutrition. Health workers and community health volunteers will actively engage the community to identify children with SAM who meets the admission criteria according to the national protocols, after which medical examination, investigations, routine medications and Ready to Use therapeutic food (RUTF) will be provided as required. For children who have medical complications and do not meet the OTP admission criteria, referral to a stabilization center will be facilitated after the provision of first-aid. A mass MUAC screening will be conducted regularly within the community to identify the children in need of nutritional intervention.
3. Infant and Young Child Feeding: Health promotion sessions and counselling will be provided to care givers, Pregnant and lactating women (PLW) and the community on the importance of exclusive breastfeeding, early initiation of breastfeeding and complementary feeding. Given the impact of the social and care environment on nutrition, special attention will continue to be given to behavioral change messaging. Tdh will also Set up IYCF corners at the OTP sites and within the community, implement mother to mother support groups and father to father support group as appropriate to the context and addressing Nutrition, health and hygiene. Cooking demonstrations will be included in the curriculum for the mother to mother/ father to father support groups.
The targeted beneficiaries include Children below the ages of 5 years (Male & Female) who will benefit from lifesaving nutrition services via the CMAM approach. Pregnant and Lactating women (Adult women & Adolescent Girls) who will be reached through IYCF services. In addition, Adult women, Adolescent Girls, Adult Men and Adolescent boys, men will benefit from health promotion sessions relating to Nutrition, Hygiene and COVID 19.
A key component of the project is community outreach which will ensure that vulnerable and marginalised people (especially mothers) are identified, made aware of, and can access the services available. Tdh will use our experience of working with vulnerable groups to ensure particular attention is paid to groups such as persons with disabilities.

1 - Needs

The northeast conflict continues to have a direct impact on affected population’s nutrition status especially the most vulnerable and those in inaccessible locations. Global acute malnutrition (GAM) levels in children aged 6-59 months is on the increase across the BAY states when compared year-on-year, with Borno state rate above the emergency threshold at 10.7 per cent as at September 2020. This includes about 300,000 children currently suffering from Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) requiring urgent treatment and over half a million children moderately malnourished. Without adequate treatment, one in every five of these children could die – According to the Humanitarian needs overview. According to the Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance Round 9 (NFSS IX) and the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification Acute Malnutrition analysis 2020 (IPC AMN), the nutrition situation in Borno is classified as Phase 3 (Serious) level. The nutritional situation is also expected to deteriorate further due to worsening of the immediate and underlying issues including increased displacement and the impact of COVID on the socioeconomic status. Based on the NFSS IX, in Borno Central, the current GAM rate based on the MUAC is at 3.9% and SAM at 0.9%The crisis has also exacerbated pre-existing conditions of malnutrition, driven by a number of inter-related factors, including: weak health infrastructures; poor infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices; limited access to safe water and health and sanitation services; and seasonal food insecurity. In Central Borno, only 55% of Children from 0 to 6months are exclusively breastfed and only 23.1% of children are breastfed up till 2 years which explain the high rate (35.9%) in the level of chronic Malnutrition evident by stunted growth. Women and children are the most affected, these groups continue to bear the brunt of the nutrition crisis due to increased micronutrient deficiencies. If untreated, acute malnutrition could have debilitating consequences on the growth and development of infants and children, further exacerbating the already existing burden of malnutrition in the northeastAccording to the HNO Humanitarian Need Severity Levels and Presence of Humanitarian Actors 2020, Mafa was classified to be severe and thus prioritized than 13 other LGA. Since 2019, TdH has been responding to the health needs of the affected population in the IDP camp by provision of preventive and curative maternal and child health services and thus, is proposing to Scale up its MNCH activities to include nutrition response to the affected population. IDPs (46% of households, Tdh data) outnumber host communities (45% of households) in Mafa. IDPs have often fled AOGs attacks, thus making the Health systems overwhelmed, and health staff overstretched due to the limited Recourses. In September 2020, Tdh conducted a rapid needs assessment through Focused Groups discussions with representatives of the affected population across the various social groups; of which the key findings indicated that; Within GSSS IDP Camp in Mafa, there’s is currently no partner providing Nutrition services which has caused a major gap in the provision of nutrition services, as such standard IYCF practices are not upheld. For example, the adult men group stated that at birth, children are often fed with goatmilk while adolescent girls stated that newborns are given honey and dates. A lack of standard IYCF practice contributes to the increase in Malnutrition, which it is one of the leading causes of Morbidity and Mortality in Children below the age of 5. To reduce morbidity and mortality associated with global acute malnutrition, Tdh will provide lifesaving treatment to SAM cases and contextualized, skilled IYCF activities both at facility and community levels with the aim of improving the exclusive breastfeeding rates

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The project will ensure women's participation while selecting the community health volunteers to ensure it is gender-sensitive. In addition, the timing of health promotion sessions and other committee meetings will be considered to ensure suitability.Close supervision that is also adaptable to the evolving needs of the target beneficiaries will be carried out by Tdh to support the community members to maximize their knowledge and promote their engagement to prevent and address child protection concerns. First, the training modules will be adapted to the needs of the audience and will use interactive activities, visual material, and as far as possible key actors working with children will be invited to stimulate beneficiaries’ understanding, participation, and interests. Before and after each session group play activities will test participants’ understanding of specific issues, if needed, topics will be re-discussed. After the training cycle is completed, Tdh staffers initially will facilitate regular meetings with the community members for the establishment of CPFP and the subsequent design of an active work plan and afterward to follow up on the action plan and its results. The CPFPs will be closely collaborating with Tdh caseworkers at each step of the activity’s implementation. Tdh facilitator will ensure continued assistance and support in the development of community designed and led actions to avoid imposing external model but to build on sustainable solutions Including the prevention and response to GBV in the proposed project, Tdh ensures that the guiding principles are respected in the planification and implementation. Indeed, Tdh will honor safety and security, by ensuring the safety of the survivor, child, and family and will maintain the awareness of safety and security of people who are helping the survivor. Tdh will also respect the confidentiality of the survivor, child, and family at all times. Relevant information on the case will be shared with other humanitarian actors for helping purposes if and only if the survivor agrees to it. Tdh will indeed seek patient consent before engaging in any response or sharing of information. If the survivor is not able to read and write, he/she will provide his/her consent verbally. Tdh will also comply with the respect of the patient: the patient will be provided with information on options available to them and Tdh will maintain a non-judgment behavior. Through awareness-raising, Tdh engages the community fully in understanding and promoting gender equality and gender power relations that protect and respect the rights of women and girls. Tdh will also ensure equal and active participation by women and men, girls, and boys in assessing, planning, implementing, monitoring, and evaluating programs through the systematic use of participatory methods (this will for example be done through the AQIC committee). Besides, Tdh will ensure that the promotion among the staff of Tdh’s safeguarding principles.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Tdh ensured that the accountability to the affected population was engaged at project designed stage. The needs assessment, which was conducted among IDP, host community, and returnees enable to reveal specific challenges faced by the community (as developed in the dedicated section), which enabled Tdh to make the informed programmatic plannification to be accountable to the beneficiaries that are developed in the proposed project. The accountability and quality improvement committee (AQIC) has been set up and reflects the diversity of the community, and through which, Tdh actively seeks the views of affected populations to improve policy and practice in programming. It is composed of community leaders, religious leaders, representatives of women, youth, and other social groups and a representative of all ethnic groups present in the community. Therefore, it contributes to routine transparency and two ways of communication. It also encourages and facilitates a space for communities to provide feedback on their level of satisfaction with the quality and effectiveness of the assistance received, while paying particular attention to the gender, age, and diversity of those giving feedback.The AQIC plans community actions, implements, and acts as a feedback mechanism between Tdh and the community. This committee has had monthly meetings since its inception and had discussed several issues affecting the community. For example, it had been reported that men were not satisfied that the project would only address MNCH and that men were not provided with health kits, Tdh had addressed this issue, emphasizing on the project’s objectives, Tdh technical expertise and had explained the referral pathways.The MEAL department will also ensure a feedback mechanism that can be received using various means, such as FGD, interviews, and feedback boxes.
