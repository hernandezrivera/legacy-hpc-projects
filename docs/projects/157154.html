Project Summary

In 2019, IOM Sudan is planning to continue providing assistance to returnees in North, South and East Darfur to end displacement situations through the provision of sustainable longer-term support addressing protection, stability, social, livelihood and economic concerns. Throughout 2018, the security situation in Darfur continued to improve, prompting families displaced within Sudan and refugees from Chad and the Central African Republic (CAR) to return to their areas of origin. IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) teams, between 2016 and 2018, in coordination with the Return, Recovery and Reintegration (RRR) sector , verified the return of 121,546 individuals in North Darfur and the return of 25,389 individuals in South Darfur. In January 2018, a total of 13,365 returnees were registered in North Darfur across nine localities (Dar el Salam, El Fasher, Tina, Kalamando, Kornoi, Kutum, Malit, Tawilla and Um Baro localities), some returning from Chad, whilst the majority of returnees came from different displacement areas of Darfur, mostly within North Darfur (DTM June 2018 Report ). In South Darfur, during 2018, a total 20,266 permanent returnees were registered in four localities (Awehda, Bielel, Dalami and East Jabel Mara). In East Darfur, even though an increased movement of returnees has been witnessed, no DTM mission has taken place so far to verify and register new returnees.
The returnee movement is currently happening due to an increase in security and stability in certain areas of Darfur. However, the majority of returnees return to areas and villages where access to basic services, water, health and education, is fairly limited and where socio-economic opportunities become more of a coping strategy rather than a source of income and development. Because of their forced displacement, individuals, households and communities had to abandon their lands and lost their livestock, assets, housing and source of income. Those who decided to remain in their villages are also facing constraints in accessing sustainable income generating activities and basic services, as well as coping with other factors such as climate change.
IOM is planning to implement a set of activities that aim to strengthen the capacity of individuals, households and communities to better prevent, absorb and recover positively and effectively to future risks and impacts, contributing towards self-reliance, rapid recovery and reintegration. The aim is to continue supporting communities in achieving safer and more secure living conditions and better access to resources and opportunities.
As part of IOM’s global mandate on mobility and migration, IOM has developed institutional capacity in transition and recovery programming that uses development-principled approaches to comprehensively address root causes driving forced and irregular. This project will reflect IOM’s development-principled programming in line with IOM’s Progressive Resolution of Displacement Situations Framework and the Humanitarian-Development Nexus to maximize opportunities that employ mobility strategies to foster the resilience of displaced persons, migrants and affected communities to support transition towards durable solutions and development. IOM Sudan’s Transition and Recovery Unit will deliver the project drawing on its extensive experience implementing locally-driven and contextualized longer-term development-principled activities that empower affected populations ownership over addressing the conditions of their displacement and restore their ability to make informed mobility decisions and reduce their vulnerability.

Needs

Return and reintegration is not always possible in Sudan but it can be a preferred option especially for IDP and refugee like returnees who may already have access to land or resources or family ties in the return area/community. Most returnees return to their places of origin on a seasonal basis or are prone to secondary displacement to another location because of the lack of basic services, livelihood opportunities and security in their places of origin. IOM plans to create favorable conditions for sustainable reintegration in areas of return through the provision of a comprehensive package that includes immediate and longer term assistance, strengthening humanitarian and develop nexus for returnees and their communities in Darfur in several localities where a total of over 100,000 people have returned (IOM DTM 2016). Following the recommendations received from the recent RRR sector lead Inter Agency mission to Um Dukhun and based on continuing IOM project activity in Central and West Darfur, this project will assess the specific needs of the returned populations in these localities and implement the most suitable interventions including (a) Rehabilitating WASH infrastructure and health services in the most vulnerable locations; (b) and organize Market orientated vocational training to improve food security, livelihood and employment opportunities for vulnerable hot community members and returnees. Throughout the project Early Recovery approach will be followed. This will also include a focus on reducing dependency by increased community and beneficiary involvement and ownership via establishment of committees, and including elements like support of WASH-tariff system via community-led water management committees from the start.This project will also benefit from the results of pilot Durable Solutions profiling project recently under-development in the context of Humanitarian-Development-Peace Nexus, where IOM is working in close collaboration with Resident Coordinator’s Office and other international and national partners including the Government of Sudan. IOM will benefit from and work in collaboration with these partners as achieving Durable Solutions is a shared goal. Under HRP this project fits under the overall Outcomes 2 and 3, whereas it also ensures links between these HRP Outcomes and several other IOM’s activities implemented in the context of UNDAF.Gender Equality and Women’s Empowerment: Special needs of women, men, boys and girls will be incorporated into the comprehensive needs assessment and response plan for return and reintegration. Sustainability and Resilience: this will include community and government engagement in the planning and implementation of this intervention and capacity building training to support local authorities and beneficiaries to have necessary skills to support self reliance.

Activities

1: WASH infrastructures are rehabilitated at the most vulnerable sites of return. 2: Health facilities are rehabilitated and designed to provide Minimum Basic Health Package to returnees and the host community. 3: Facilitating training of water technicians and health workers to support the provision and maintenance of basic services. 4: Vulnerable Returnees and host community members are provided with market oriented vocational and essential livelihood training based on community priorities. 5: Community led workshops and initiatives including water, waste and resource management committees develop community action plans for further initiatives to be launched.

Impact on environment/natural resources

Disasters and environmental degradation threaten to hamper progress towards the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and durable solutions, especially for those countries left furthest behind. Climate change is expected to trigger growing population movements within and across borders. IOM aims to build the resilience of communities to risk factors and expected changes in their natural environment, while contributing to sustainable development of livelihood opportunities; supporting efforts to strengthen the interaction with responsibility holders (national and local authorities) and develop their capacities. IOM is working closely with the Higher Council for Environment and Natural Resources (HCENR), in line the National Adaptation Plan (NAP) to climate change, on establishing integrated water management systems and will also continue working on establishing sources of renewable energy (solar energy) and improving local practices to reduce the environmental footprint and waste of natural resources. Livelihood and agricultural training will continue to focus on improving the targeted beneficiaries' knowledge of agricultural practices so that they are able to better respond to climate shocks. The training will also introduce communities to better coping strategies in order to replace the negative coping strategies currently used and that contribute to ongoing land degradation and desertification (e.g. firewood collection). IOM will consult with UNEP/ISCG on the community environmental action plan tools and conduct area impact assessments before implementation of any supports on rehabilitation of basic service facilities such as water and sanitation facilities.
