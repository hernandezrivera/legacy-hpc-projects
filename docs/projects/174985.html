Project Summary

According to the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) some 12.3 million people will need humanitarian assistance in 2021.
While incidents of fighting have reduced considerably in recent years, the situation of people displaced due to decades of conflict remains unresolved. Some 2.5 million IDPs and 1.1 million refugees and asylum seekers continue to need humanitarian assistance and protection support, both in camps and host communities. Pockets of armed conflict continue in Darfur, and sporadic inter-communal conflicts also continue. In neighbouring Central African Republic (CAR), ongoing intercommunal conflicts have escalated since June 2019, with reports indicating large populations of internally displaced populations along the border with Sudan, near Central and South Darfur, which are anticipated to continue into 2021.
Across Sudan, basic services are lacking, and natural disasters, like floods, affect people each year. But it is a deepening economic crisis, following years of stagnation and little investment in already-weak public services, that is driving worsening food insecurity, deteriorating healthcare, and other needs across Sudan. While the transitional government is committed to stemming the economic crisis, reforms may involve subsidy cuts which could have a further impact on families who are already struggling to cope. Basic service provision, already weak and underdeveloped, has been dangerously degraded by the economic crisis. This has affected people directly, impacting households’ ability to buy food and cover basic needs. It has also deepened already existing needs – including among existing vulnerable groups like displaced people, returnees, and refugees – and may cause new people to fall into humanitarian need

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in Sudan has improved with greater opportunities for internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees to determine their choice of return, resettlement, or reintegration. Despite this improvement, humanitarian needs persist for vulnerable people who have been living in protracted displacement for over a decade; people affected by recent localized clashes in Darfur’s Jebel Marra area; people affected by food insecurity, malnutrition and protection risks; as well as people displaced due to inter-communal violence and conflict. An estimated 12.3 million people in Sudan need some form of humanitarian assistance. Further the situation is exacerbated by the reduced household purchasing power caused by increasing prices of key commodities and medicines. At the same time, the country continues to be a major host of over one million refugees and asylum seekers, the majority of whom are largely dependent on humanitarian assistance. Food insecurity, Malnutrition, Displacement and refugees, and Protection concerns are the key drivers of needs in Sudan. Increasing food prices, and poor WASH systems and health services, continue to drive malnutrition across the country. Sudan remains prone to disease outbreaks including cholera, chikungunya, dengue, malaria, measles and Rift Valley fever. For the 2.5 million internally displaced people (IDPs) in Sudan, durable solutions have not been achieved and the root causes of their displacement remain unaddressed. While at a smaller scale compared to previous years, new displacement continues, new displacement remained in 2020. In addition to living in dire conditions for years with limited basic services, many IDPs - particularly women and girls - still face high protection risks and continue to be harassed, and in some cases killed or raped. Pockets of conflict also remain in Darfur’s Jebel Marra area, with related protection violations. The situation of refugees who have been living in camps for years is also concerning. Protection gaps related to the fulfilment of basic rights and vulnerability to discrimination and threats continue to undermine their physical and mental well-being. Refugees’ lack of access to land, productive assets, financial services and other means of production limits their access to livelihoods, income and education, and undermines their capacity for self-reliance in the long-term

2 - Activities/Outputs

Provide support to the HC and HCT to enable effective leadership in strategic coordination, including the integration of key cross cutting issues such as gender, environment, and accountability to affected people in humanitarian actions 2. Lead the inter sector coordination mechanisms at national and sub national levels in accordance with IASC guidance and support sectors in the preparation of sector response plans and their strategic monitoring indicators 3. Establish and maintain dialogue with affected people, including in IDP settlements in Darfur, to ensure provision of basic services to vulnerable people. This includes evidence based liaison with sector coordinators at state and Khartoum level to address any gaps reported. 4. Support the HCT in developing the Humanitarian Response Plan informed by evidence based analysis of assessed needs with clear measurable objectives 5. Ensure that inter agency risk analysis is undertaken and minimum preparedness actions and advanced prepared actions are routinely carried out in accordance with the Emergency Preparedness Framework 6. Develop and publish on a regular basis communication and information products using a standardized and integrated approach. This includes the coordination of humanitarian public information and advocacy efforts 7. Analyze and provide access reports to guide and inform decision making by the HCT and advocacy, and negotiate for access 8. Promote the adherence to humanitarian principles by humanitarian actors to ensure principled interventions and in line with the HCT Minimum Operating Standards
