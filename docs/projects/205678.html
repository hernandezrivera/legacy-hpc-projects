Project Summary

The proposed intervention seeks to ensure that emergency affected 35,929 children and youth (19,042 girls and 16,887 boys) have access to continuous, quality driven, equitable learning opportunities in safe and inclusive environments. Of these at least 3,594 children (50% girls) will be children with disabilities. Target locations will be those prioritized by the cluster and where NRC has existing physical presence in the CES (Juba, Kajokeji), Unity (Mayom, Panyijiar, Leer and Koch), Jonglei (Akobo, Bor South, Duk, Twic East), WBeG (Wau, Raja), NBeG (Aweil East, Aweil West, Aweil North), Warrap (Gogrial West and Twic) and Upper Nile (Renk) States. In addition, NRC will activate the Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) education response in identified locations as per the education cluster agreed triggers for emergency education response. Both the static and mobile response mechanism will target children and youth (aged 6 – 17) from returnee, IDP and host community families with education services in both formal schools and non-formal learning centres. Proposed education interventions will align with cluster strategic objectives of improving access to quality education and educational systems strengthening. NRC will conduct enrolment social mobilisation through back to learning campaigns, construct/rehabilitate classrooms and provide desks to reduce congestion which has recently been aggravated by the influx of returnees from Sudan because of the conflict that erupted and displaced hundreds of people with 302,838 [at least 50% being school aged children] having crossed to South Sudan at various border crossing. (OCHA, 6 Oct, 2023), For improved physical safety, NRC will construct gender segregated WASH facilities and conduct hygiene promotion activities in school communities, distribute IEC materials with disease prevention messaging, promote child participation through supporting child-led activities in school clubs, distribute recreational kits for structured recreational activities. Activities aimed at improving the quality of education will include teacher training, distribution of teaching and learning materials, provision of textbooks, and supplementary reading materials, supporting catch-up classes for returnee and children previously out of school to facilitate reintegration and transition. The intervention will support continuous teacher professional development through teacher learning cycles and other short trainings. To retain female teachers in hard-to-reach locations, NRC will avail incentives and other non-monetary forms of motivation. In addition to pedagogical training educators will undergo capacity building in conflict sensitive education, PSS, life-skills training, code of conduct, referral pathways, PSEA and safeguarding. The proposed intervention will link up quality issues with education systems strengthening and conduct trainings for PTA/SMCs on school governance, deliver school management training for county education staff on school supervision and teacher mentorship. Trainings will cover cross-cutting issues i.e., gender mainstreaming, peacebuilding, environment, and disaster risk reduction.

1. Needs

Impact of prolonged conflict exacerbated by communal violence, climatic shocks, economic challenges, and the effects COVID-19 continue to affect the humanitarian situation in South Sudan. The capacity of poor and vulnerable households to access essential social services such as education, health, water, and sanitation is weakened. Protection concerns especially for women and girls remain high. In addition to the already dire situation pertaining in the conflict which erupted in Sudan in April 2023 has put additional pressure on already over stretched social services. As of October 2023, OCHA estimates that 302,838 individuals crossed the boarders of South Sudan from the northern neighbour. According to the 2023 HNO, at least 148,000 returnees from abroad remain displaced in country. This could have significantly increased after the Sudan crisis. Above normal rainfall in 2022 led to prolonged flooding damaging infrastructure and negatively affecting agricultural activities and hence food security. OCHA furthers estimates that 76% of South Sudan’s population or 9.4 million people, will need humanitarian assistance. This represents an increase of 500,000 from 2022. More people 6.31 million are expected to face severe food insecurity in 2023, (HNO 2023). This poses a serious threat to education especially as the education clusters notes that hunger is one of the main reasons why children drop out of school. This not only increases associated protection risks but also negatively affects long term physical and cognitive development of affected children. The protracted conflict and climate-related crisis coupled with residual effects of COVID-19 pandemic in 2020/21 which resulted prolonged school closures are still felt in the education sector. There OOSC as of 2022, is estimated to be 2.8 million this represents a reduction of gross enrolment by 7% between 2020 and 2021. Most education challenges identified in 2021 ENA have been highlighted in previous assessments and they persist post COVID-19. County education officials, headteachers, learners, and community members emphasise the lack of finances for both the supply and demand side of education (such as families not being able to afford fees for their children to attend schools, or teachers not receiving regular and sufficient salaries). Economic barriers remain a threat to education as families may resort to pulling children out of school for livelihoods. The risk of forced marriage for girls is further heightened. Other issues include poor infrastructure in schools, lack of washrooms for girls, lack of food, inaccessibility for children with disability and lack of trained and motivated teachers. Communities identify opportunities for better access to quality education include school improvement grants, teacher salaries, rehabilitation of infrastructure, and provision of teaching and learning materials. These priorities inform NRC’s education response for 2024. The project

2. Project activities

The proposed project will ensure emergency (conflict & climate shocks) affected girls, boys, and youth (6 - 17 years) have uninterrupted access to equitable and inclusive quality education in safe and secure environments. The response will target children and youth from IDP, returnees and host communities with learning opportunities that are relevant for psycho-social well-being, physical and cognitive development. Prioritizing out-of-school children (OOSC) and those at risk of dropping out NRC will target 36,262 direct beneficiaries constituted of 35,929 children including 3,594 CWD (16,887b; 19,042g); 333 adults (168m; 165f [235 teachers & 98 parents/caregivers and non-teaching professionals). Among the children targeted 3,594 (at least 50% girls) are children living with disabilities. Rapid education response is planned for 3 – 6 months after which NRC hands over to static partners and/or local education officials to continue providing educational services. The project will through NRC's static operations support formal education and provide ALP classes in prioritized counties. In collaboration with other education partners/local education officials, NRC will conduct back to learning (BTL) campaigns to mobilise enrolment and attendance. BTL will deliberately target OOSC, returnees and children at risk of dropping out of school especially girls. To ensure regular sustained attendance, welcoming and physically safe learning environment, the project will construct and repair semi-permanent classroom blocks, gender segregated school WASH facilities, distribute hygiene materials; provide desks/benches, distribute teaching and learning materials, textbooks, to girls and boys from vulnerable families for inclusivity. Further, the project will deliver activities for quality education which is relevant to the needs of learners through teachers’ training on conflict sensitive education, child centred pedagogy, classroom management, lessons planning/scheming and delivery. Teacher professional development will be conducted jointly with State & County MOGEI for alignment with national standards. NRC will support teachers learning cycles, for peer-to-peer learning. The project will deliver PSS for children affected by conflict to enable them to process mental and psychological stress for better learning outcomes. NRC will train teachers, caregivers on PSS, child protection and referral pathways. NRC will support child-led initiatives, school peace, hygiene, debate, girls’ education clubs. Distribute recreational kits & support structured recreational, life-skills training, disease prevention messaging to promote physical and PSS devpt and impart skills conflict resolution and peaceful co-existence. Menstrual hygiene management training and dignity kits will be distributed to adolescent girls for MHM. Improved MHM contributes to better attendance rates for girls. To encourage retention, NRC will provide cash & vouchers to the most vulnerable for examination registration.Project will contribute to education systems strengthening through training of PTAs/ SMCs on school governance. Community participation will be tailor made for to educational needs of returnees, IDP and girls who face multiple barriers to education including harmful social practices e.g., SGBV, forced early marriages. Boys who are vulnerable to protection risks e.g., child labour, forced recruitment into armed groups will also be targeted. The project will provide technical, and material support to county offices to facilitate school supervision & support. Teachers and officials will also be sensitized on crosscutting issues of DRR, child protection, safeguarding and PSEA. NRC will ensure integration, partnerships, and community participation and coordination with local actors & other clusters to deliver comprehensive package for optimum resource usage.
