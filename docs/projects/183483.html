Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims to contribute to reduction of WASH vulnerability in marginalized communities through improving access to equitable, sustainable, and safely managed WASH services in institutions with a particular focus on Schools and Health clinics in the governorates of Jericho and East Jerusalem.
The intervention components respond to the 2022 HRP SO2, in addition the WASH cluster Objective 2 through its tailored approach to enhancing WASH infrastructure and resources in institutions and target group responsive promotion of hygiene awareness and promotion with particular relevance to the ongoing COVID pandemic. Further, the intervention is also in line with SDG 6 “Ensure access to water and sanitation for all”
The intervention will achieve the aforementioned through two main components that respond directly to the identified WASH Cluster priority ” improve access to WASH Services in institutions” ensuring equitable and dignified access for most vulnerable community groups with a particular focus on PWDs, children and females across 33 vulnerable communities located in the governorates of Jericho ( Jordan Valley) and East Jerusalem
In particular, MA’AN and WeWorld-GVC are proposing to improve WASH facilities and capacities in 62 WASH vulnerable institutions benefiting in total 247,604 direct beneficiaries (40,578 boys, 38,360 girls, 86,068 men, 82,598 women, of which 3,060 PWDs and 6,323 elderly ) in 33 communities the Governorates of Jerusalem and Jericho, mainly in Area C (72%), and Area A/B (28%). through:
i)Rehabilitation of WASH facilities in schools (handwashing, latrines, sewer system) in coordination with and accordance to MoE standards, targeting 31 schools (9 Jericho Governorate, 22 East Jerusalem) located in 16 communities of the West Bank in the Governorates of Jerusalem and Jericho.
ii)Conduct hygiene awareness campaigns in targeted schools in partnership with MoE health units to promote hygiene and sanitation practices with gender and protection mainstreaming.
iii) Tailored Rehabilitation of WASH facilities in health clinics/HCFs (handwashing, latrines, sewer system), targeting 31 highly vulnerable Health care facilities (5 Jericho Governorate, 26 East Jerusalem) located in 29 communities of the West Bank in the Governorates of Jerusalem and Jericho.
iv) The provision of environmental and personal cleaning kits for the 31 targeted HCFs
v)conduct trainings for clinics staff on promotion of hygiene practices to patients and protection guidelines including PSEA.
All proposed activities will ensure protection principles on safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability, participation, empowerment and claiming of rights.
The intervention has been proposed based on coordinated efforts to complement current WASH aid actors proposed and ongoing interventions to ensure comprehensive contribution to reduction in overall WASH vulnerability amongst marginalized and prioritized communities. and complements another initiative submitted by UNICEF for the purposes of the HRP 2022 integrated intervention of WASH in school and WASH in Health . Further, the intervention has been coordinated and endorsed by the MoH, MoE and the WASH Cluster. The project is in line with the MOH Strategic Plan (2021-25), The proposed activities were selected upon the WASH Cluster eligible interventions for HRP 2022, based on WASH 2021 community and institutions vulnerability assessments.
MA’AN and WW-GVC ensure that all the proposed interventions will trigger a positive effect on the reduction of the WASH vulnerability, increasing the protection and the resilience of the communities and institutions under threat of forcible transfer in Area C including PSEA promotion and messaging. Moreover, the intervention would enhance the protection of the communities and institutions, supporting preparedness and response on combating COVID-19 outbreak direct and indirect effects.

1 - Needs

The fragmented Palestinian territorial Sovereignty has many ramifications including inability to enforce the rule of law, undertake physical and socio-economic development planning, construct residential and public service facilities, or provide adequate level of public services and infrastructure including WASH. The pervasive poverty and unemployment is compounded and caused by occupation regimes restrictions further hinders the PAs ability to invest in WASH infrastructures. One of the main factors contributing WASH accessibility vulnerability at the national level ( Areas A,B &C) is the existence of inadequate and deteriorated WASH infrastructures. Improving access to WASH services is an urgent need to ensure protection and dignity of patients, outpatients, health workers and students of the most vulnerable HCs and schools of the West Bank. Improved WASH services in HCs are considered as necessary inputs to be prioritized for achieving all global and national health goals, especially those linked to Universal Health Coverage (SDG 3), and the specific target 3.9 “to reduce the burden of disease from unsafe water, sanitation, and lack of hygiene” and 3.1 and 3.2 “call for reducing maternal mortality and under-five and neonatal mortality”. Effective sanitation, hygiene, and infection prevention and control (IPC) measures are one of the five objectives of the WHO Global Action Plan on antimicrobial resistance.In schools, the need is growing for availability of safe and functional WASH facilities with a minimum level of hygiene standards during operations within the pandemic. The MoE has rolled out safe schools COVID protocols and has prioritized WASH in schools national standards which many schools with high WASH vulnerability are struggling to operationalize in light of limited resources thus further exacerbating their WASH and Health vulnerability during the pandemic. During 2021, the WASH cluster directly and through partners/members , MoE and the MoH staff conducted several WASH vulnerability assessments including the WB&GS Community WASH vulnerability mapping, WASH vulnerability in HCs where 638 clinics in the WB were mapped, and WASH vulnerabilities in schools where 847 schools in the WB including East Jerusalem where mapped. The assessments revealed as presented in the WASH 2022 HNO that with regards to institutions in the West Bank and East Jerusalem, at least 129 schools and 164 HC units suffer from insufficient WASH facilities (primarily toilets, drinking and handwashing stations) to meet the standards set up by the Ministries of Education and Health.In Oct 2021, the WASH cluster team, MA’AN, and Oxfam held a response coordination meeting with MoE where the WASH in schools vulnerability mapping was presented and main indicators for consideration from the mapping to identify prioritized WASH vulnerable schools were determined across 10 main vulnerability indicators. Further, the MoE briefed on proposed WASH in schools national standards (1 latrine for each class, handwashing station for each 2 latrines, 1 drinking tap for each 50 students, at least 1 PWD latrine in each school (AGD segregation if mix), 1 latrine for each 20 staff (AGD segregation if mix). Upon coordination with WASH cluster and others, MA’AN and WW-GVC have conducted elaborated needs assessments Sep-Oct pursuant to aforementioned mappings. The assessment HCs revealed that a significant number of HCs lack adequate gender segregated and/or staff vs patient latrines resulting in overcrowding the existing facilities and undermining hygiene practices, all of which raise particular concern due to the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic. At least 5 identified HCs are impacted annually by mild to medium severity floods mostly located in the Jordan Valley, and 19 have no adaptable toilets for PWDs , 8 are in need of urgent WASH facilities rehabilitations due to substandard unmaintained structure. Majority of assessed HCs have regular access to latrine cleaning materials and tools but often face shortages and could benefit from hygiene kits, particularly HCs with inpatient services whom have increased consumption. Majority of HC’s further reported rarely or never recieving capacity building or training on hygiene awareness and practices. In regards to schools, many schools are in need of replacement of drinking fountains from rustable steel structures to standardized water coolers to encourage students water consumption during hot days. WASH facilities dont meet minimum standards with Lack of appropriate flooring causing sinking of installed toilet or lack there of, Lack of water drainage piping and network causing pooling of water, lack of existing access paths for students, and staff especially PWD, insufficient capacity of sewage Septic tanks. Lack of privacy due to substandard partitions thus detering students in particular females from use, disproportinate number of stalls to students & staff, damaged door frames, need for shading to prevent the student from weather condition (hot temperatures in summer and rain in the winter) and any related fixes of window frames, and water gutters. Existence of oriental toilet seats, rusted sewage pipes causeing backup into water pipes, Based on the above MA’AN and WW-GVC have identified 31/67 schools, and 31/43 health clinics demonistrating high WASH vulnerability in the governorates of Jericho and Jerusalem in the following intervention target localities: Jericho Governorate (Al Auja,Al Jiftlik-al Musaffah,An Nuweima,Az Zubeidat,Ein ad Duyuk al Fauqa,Ein ad Duyuk at Tahta,Fasayil,Jericho,Marj Naja) and Jerusalem Governorate ( Abu Dis,Al Eizariya,Al Jib,Al Judeira,Al Qubeiba,An Nabi Samwil,Anata,Ar Ram & Dahiyat al Bareed,Arab al Jahilin - al Jabal,As Sawahira ash Sharqiya,Ash Sheikh Sad,Beit Anan,Beit Ijza,Beit Iksa,Biddu,Bir Nabala,Hizma,Jaba,Kafr Aqab,Khan al Ahmar - Ab al Helw,Khan al Ahmar - Abu Fellah,Mikhmas Bedouins,Qalandiya,Qatanna).

2 - Activities

The proposed activities have been coordinated with the MoH, MoE Local Authorities, and major stakeholders active in the WASH sector on the target communities to avoid overlapping. At the onset of the project, MA’AN and WW-GVC will lead a coordination meetings to map out implementation plan in target locations and explore special regalotory considerations for program implementation in the 33 target localities in Jericho and East Jerusalem. The proposed intervention will be implemented in mutual partnership between both organizations based complementarity of expertise where MA’AN will lead components related to Schools and coordination with MoE and WW-GVC will lead components related to HCs and coordination with the MoH. Further both organizations have reach across the targeted localities and employ local staff whom will be able to monitor political risks on a daily basis. Before any activity when relevant, a risk analysis, including legal consultation with the lawyer of the community will be conducted. Specific SOPs are followed during implementation to minimize/mitigate risks which will allow reducing the risk of confiscation and other potential obstacles related to the coercive environment of Area C, enhancing the commitment and ownership of the beneficiaries, to guarantee safety, dignity and avoid harming (do not harm) the affected population. Therefore, the intervention will be committed to ensuring gender sensitiveness and inclusiveness on the facilities design for all, including girls and boys with special needs and/or disabilities. In accordance, the design of the sanitation facilities at schools and HFCs level will ensure privacy for women and children with the provision of dignified space with good physical and sound insulation and will be adapted to special needs for PWDs. Proposed Key activities: Activity 1 – Need assessments: at the beginning of the intervention , together with the stakeholders and Local Auhorities, a need assessment will be carried out to verify any change of needs and/or objectives, as well as to assess the security situation to enable the interventions to be conducted according to plan. Activity 2 – Improvement of access to WASH services in institutions: 2,1 The construction/ rehabilitation of WASH facilities in schools(handwashing, latrines, sewer system) in coordination with and accordance to MoE standards, targeting 31 schools (9 Jericho Governorate, 22 East Jerusalem) located in 16 communities of the West Bank in the Governorates of Jerusalem and Jericho.)MA’AN will coordinate with the MoE and its relevant health and infrastructure units at the directorates level to conduct an individual technical infrastructure needs assessment of each of the 31 targeted schools WASH facilities as the MoE lacks several “ As-Built Drawing lists” for materials and networks in established schools. PWDs accessibility and female students privacy will be prioritized for schools lacking there of. As the needs from one school to another differ, some works might entail construction of new bathrooms in order to accommodate standards of 20 students per stall or existing non segregated staff vs students latrines. As per standard MA’AN operation to rehabilitation of schools for the past years, BoQs of materials and works will be detailed in accordance with MoEs standardized design for adequate WASH facilities in schools, and shared with the MoE for approval. The works will be conducted through contractors and supervised by MA’AN civil engineers in cooperation with MoE infrastructure units. 2,2 The construction/ rehabilitation of WASH facilities in HFCs(handwashing, latrines, sewer system), targeting 31 highly vulnerable Health care facilities (5 Jericho Governorate, 26 East Jerusalem) located in 29 communities of the West Bank in the Governorates of Jerusalem and Jericho.WW-GVC will coordinate with MoH and its relevant directorates for the construction and rehabilaition of handwashing, latrines, sewer system serviving in the 31 HCFs based on individual technical assessments. All the works, especially regarding the design of the bathrooms/latrines, will take into consideration the gender needs and separation, equipping of toilets to meet the needs, women, staff men and women, patients, and companions as well as the special needs of PWDs and children, decrease the risk of GBV cases and ensuring safety and dignity to all. Moreover, it will include the Rehabilitation/construction of new hand washing basins and/or alcohol-based hand rub wall-mounted dispensers, supporting on preventing the spread of infectious diseases as COVID 19 and will be implemented in accordance to the National WASH standards for health facilities (UNICEF/WWGVC 2021).Similarly, works will be conducted through service provision modality. 2.3 Conduct hygiene awareness campaigns in targeted 31 schools in partnership with MoE health units to promote hygiene and sanitation practices with gender and protection mainstreaming. Through other programs MA’AN and UNICEF have been partnering with the MoE school health units at the directorates level to promote hygience practices amongst students and in particular relevance to COVID 19 school protocal. 2.4 The provision of environmental and personal cleaning kits for the 31 targeted HCFs . to ensure proper segregation of waste in bins, and availability of cleaning materials and tools for both staff, inpatients and outpatients with particular considerations to COVID protocols for health clinics 2.5 conduct 5 centralized trainings for clinics staff on promotion of hygiene practices to patients and protection guidelines including PSEA. All proposed activities will ensure protection principles on safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability, participation, empowerment and claiming of rights.

3 - Indicators

MA’AN and WWGVC will perform ongoing monitoring, combining both quantitative and qualitative data, then systematized through a series of outputs. Accordingly, the monitoring implies a participatory approach that includes focus group discussions and individual interviews (with men, women, girls, and boys, with and without disability) to assess whether the identified activities take into account the needs and dynamics of the beneficiaries, analyzing the different effect of the initiative on different age and gender groups. After the completion of the intervention, the qualitative impact of the initiative will be addressed through focus groups, interviews, and surveys, collecting the feedback of different vulnerable groups (women, men, boys, girls, elderlies). Overall monitoring will be done by an M&E officer, whom will also lead reporting cases received in the CRM in accordance with MA’ANs complaint systems and procedures. The Field engineers will work and cooperate with the M&E in ensuring that the indicators are being met and ensuring that the best strategies are being used. The MA’AN Project Manager will be responsible for overall monitoring and evaluation and reporting and will lead the reporting to MA’AN and GVC Focal point in the WASH Cluster. The indicators and sources of verification will be the base for collecting data and observation on the ground by the project coordinator. In the event targets are not being met, immediate action will be agreed upon and taken to reach the targets. Monitoring will be shared with relevant stakeholders including project committee to include MoE, MoH and WASH cluster. All information will be shared with donor, especially any lessons learned. The tools that will be used are baseline survey (at the start) end line survey (at projects close) as well as interviews, field visit, general observation and field visit reports. Photos will also be taken to document the project. Monitoring activities will be done throughout the project cycle to ensure proper implementation and targets are being met to achieve results. Special attention will be given for conducting interviews and data collection with women beneficiaries with female team members and. The intervention will be supported by (HAP) compliant CRM accessible to beneficiaries and non beneficiaries through several channels, The CRM will ensure that all complaints are recorded, responded to and handled according to their sensitivity and Beneficiaries will be sensitized on its function, process and usage, and will be encouraged to rely on it without fear of any adverse actions. CRMs when need, are customized under each project for beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback in a confidential and secure way duly reported to MAAN’s management. 9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Based on the classification of feedback or concern, feedback would be used to adapt project implementation taking into consideration needs of target groups.Beneficiaries’ satisfaction will be continuously monitored by MA’AN teams, that regularly visits and interviews beneficiaries regarding ongoing activities. The team will report any issues brought to its attention to project management team. MA’Ans female MEAL officer will also oversee and ensure transparency in the confidential complaint response mechanism, duly reported to MAAN’s management when relevant, and then properly addressed. MA’AN establishes a Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) under each project for beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback in a confidential and secure way. As many communication means as relevant are made available to ensure the mechanism is easily accessible, taking into account the specificities of the local context and culture, as well as project resources and beneficiaries’ vulnerabilities. the staff from schools and HCS will be sensitized by the project team on the mechanism, and explained how to access it, and information is clearly displayed at project sites .Complaints are recorded in an area-level register under restricted access. Under project supervision, committees will then inform and encourage project beneficiaries to share feedback on the provided assistance, potential encountered issues and/or remaining needs to be addressed. Depending on the nature and severity of the cases received under the CRM, a number of different action pathways may be activated. Moreover, MAAN will provide targeted beneficiaries with the PSEA network hotline number and explain that it can be used for complaints involving inappropriate behaviors, sexual harassment, sexual exploitation or abuse, where all correspondences will be addressed with the utmost confidentiality and seriousness.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Addressing the needs of full community clusters and involving community members as leaders throughout the project’s implementation, the proposed intervention seeks to minimize implementation risk and enforce protection standards for the “do no harm” policy. Community stakeholders including governorate offices and local directorates of MoE and MoH will be involved throughout every phase of project implementation. The security situation is monitored closely on an ongoing basis by MAAN to ensure timely advice and relevant security precautions can be implemented. Further MA’ANs local coordinators and continues presence in the communities along with the targeted institutions staff whom will report regularly on any risks to safety and security during implementation to mitigate time and effectiveness impact. . To ensure safety and security and protection of existing services of institutions during implementation and in line with the “Do No Harm” principles, MA’AN ensures that infrastructure work in all its forms in Area C do not affect the arial view of structure dimensions in the community to reduce chances of future demolition orders thus no constructions are done, only rehabilitation works are conducted. Further, in order not to impact the operations and services provided by such institutions, infrastructure works are coordinated to be conducted after school time, and for HCs as much as possible when after hrs of work. MA’AN will ensure that the assistance is provided in times where control is limited (at night, during week-ends) in coordinated times with community stakeholders to assess the security situation. -MA’AN mobilizes/employs local coordinators for its projects in Area C to mitigate access risk in locations where security incidents are likely to occur and be aware of any problem with ICA or Israeli Military Forces in the communities in real time. And when possible in alignment with its procurement policy and guidelines procures from local sources.
