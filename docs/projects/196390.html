Project Summary

Our organization aims to improve access to basic preventive and curative health care services for internally displaced and conflict-affected populations in targeted areas in NWS through the provision of life-saving emergency, primary, and secondary health care services including sexual and reproductive healthcare and cases and those in need of more specialized medical services. The supported PHCs will provide primary health care services including treatment of common acute and chronic illnesses and injuries; the provision of essential medicines including contraceptives and treatment for chronic diseases; and management of mental health services. Programs will be delivered through support to two maternity and child hospitals and twelve static primary healthcare clinics. four ambulances will also provide transportation for emergency communicable and non-communicable diseases and health education. The PHCs will also provide basic reproductive health services including antenatal care, post-natal care, family planning, and syndromic management of STIs. PHCs will operate a referral system for cases to secondary and tertiary service hospitals. Health activities will maintain and ensure sustaining and expanding essential, quality health services within communities through primary and secondary health care to meet the needs of the most vulnerable groups to both IDPs and host communities. In addition, the community health care workers will provide health education and referrals for a wide range of services, support and assist communities, families, and individuals with preventive health measures, and gain access to appropriate curative health and social services. The maternity and new-born care hospitals will provide Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and New-bornCare (CEmONC) and outpatient reproductive services including antenatal, postnatal care, and family planning. The organization will support 7 oral rehydration points ORPs to be able to respond to cholera outbreak and treat the suspected cases properly
Our organization will provide support to twelve static PHCs (Atmeh PHC 1,G0703003), (Atmeh PHC 2, G0703004 ), (Atmeh PHC 3,G0703100 ), (Green Idleb PHC,G0700002 ), (Kafr Yahmol PHC,G0700007 ), (Abi Zar PHC, G0700034),( Souran PHC, B0204038), (Zoghra PHC,B0208011 ),( Bzagha PHC,B0202016 ), (Mhamble PHC, G0705035), Al Zof PHC and (Al Rayan PHC, B0204044) in addition to tow maternity and newborn hospitals, Al Zahrawi MCH (G0700093) and Afrin MCH (B0203079)

3 - Needs

More than 15.3 million of population in Syria, approximately a 3.2m increase from last year, are estimated to be in dire need of life-saving health assistance in 2023. This includes 6.8 displaced persons, 2.2 million children under 5 years – including live births expected, 4.2 million women of reproductive age (15-49 years), and more than 700,000 elderly people who will demand integrated and lifesaving health services in Syria. The increase in PIN is also attributed to the deteriorating socio-economic status of the communities, the depreciation of the Syrian pound, and increases in poverty across the country, which resulted in increased displacement and worsened determinants of health thereby negatively impacting health outcomes, In addition to the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic, a Cholera outbreak was declared by MOH Syria in Sept 2022. Within the health sector PiN, 6.8 million persons are estimated to have a disability which places them at greater risk for exclusion from health services. NGO conducted Multisector Needs Assessment (MSNA), in February 2022, confirmed that the top three challenges in accessing health services included: lack of available medicines (34.7%), lack of transportation and distance to the available services (21.8%), and the unavailability of treatment for required medical diagnoses (10.0%) to include but not limited to dentistry, x-rays, and blood tests.

4 - Implementation of activities

Our organization aims to increase access to life saving and life sustaining health services for those most vulnerable and in need. Our organization will provide support to twelve static PHCs (Atmeh PHC 1, Atmeh PHC 2, Atmeh PHC 3, Green Idleb PHC, Kafr Yahmol PHC, AbiZar PHC, Souran PHC, Zoghra PHC, Bzagha PHC, Mhamble PHC, Al Zof PHC and Al Rayan PHC) which will include: capacity-building through sustained technical support, provision of pharmaceuticals, consumables, and equipment, and disbursal of staff incentives and funds to cover facility running costs. The supported PHCs will serve as a PHC-Unit and center in accordance with WHO PHCs standards/levels. Moreover, the supported maternity and new-born care hospitals in Idleb and Afrin City will provide (CEmONC) services. The medical activities are monitored by experienced. Our organization will implement outreach health activities through 76 community health workers from the targeted communities across the program sites in Atmeh Camp and Idleb City, In response to COVID-19, our organization will continue incorporating strict precaution measures to ensure staff and beneficiaries’ safety and to minimize exposure and disease transmission (i.e. conducting triage and screening at the entrance of each facility) ,moreover the organization will continue operating 7 ORPs for the initial management Cholera patients and referral of the sever cases

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The population, including victims, do not report allegations of SEA which results in the organization being unable to refer the allegation to investigation and hold personnel to account and assist victims.Greater difficulties in accessing health services, as well as increased burdens and separation from caregivers (due to quarantines, or severe illness/death), may lead to SEA against children, in particular girls (including child/forced marriage).• Our organization has prepared a safeguarding action plan to promote and make sure safeguarding measures are in place.•Our Safeguarding Policy has being distributed for all staff to read and acknowledge.•Our organization delivers MAST training which includes SEA training for new hires and an annual refresher for all staff.• Our organization has safeguarding focal points in the field for the staff to revert to in case of any concern, question, or complaint.• Our organization has safeguarding posters placed in all of our facilities along with the several complaint hotlines that staff or beneficiaries can use.• Our Protection Team delivers training sessions for all staff for the safeguarding and the referral pathways.• Our Protection Team conducts safeguarding risk assessment for all the facilities.•Safeguarding measures are implemented in the recruitment processes to make sure suitable candidates are hired.
