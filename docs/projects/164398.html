Project Summary

Violet aims through this project firstly, to provide lifesaving emergency response food assistance for improving food consumption, dietary diversity and avoiding negative coping strategy to the newly IDPs as an emergency response with the first phase of the displacement through implementing the following activities:
1. RTE distribution for the newly IDPs
2. MPGs distribution for the newly IDPs
3. Repetitive MPGs for newly IDPs
3. one-off Food baskets distribution for the newly IDPs
4. Cook meals for the newly IDPs.
secondly, to Support livelihoods on households level through
1. Creating income-generating activities.
2. Building capacities of youth and women through Vocational training and enabling them to start their own businesses.
3. supporting farmers in crop cultivation, irrigation through solar irrigation systems, agricultural awareness sessions, and consultation.
4. support livestock by vaccines, awareness sessions, and consultation

4 - Needs

Since November 2019, the humanitarian situation has reached unprecedented levels of needs, with large-scale displacement from southern Idleb up to safer areas in the north. Between 01.12.2019 and 31.01.2020 approx. 520,000 people have been displaced. The majority of displaced (80%) are women and children, who are facing increased protection risks, combined without the ability to cover basic needs. Multiple displacements increase their vulnerability. It also forced them to leave behind belongings. The Ready to Eat rations has been designed to suit the IDPs who have been newly displaced. These rations will be provided during the first few days of displacement. The humanitarian situation of the newly IDPs is further deteriorating by the displacement during the winter, where the IDPs are in urgent need of humanitarian multisector support, with special focus on lifesaving assistance like shelter, food, health, non-food and winterization. According to Violet reports, the urgent priority needs of the newly displaced respectively, are the Shelter, cash money, food winterization assistance, and NFIs. Insecurity is further challenged through decreased income, overstretched capacities, unemployment or displacement. Limited livelihood opportunities lead to food insecurity due to limited income, higher protection risks especially for women and children as well as increased negative coping mechanisms of the communities such as crime, child labor or early marriage. Due to the crisis, women often have become head of household without work experience or income opportunities. Thus, vocational training can support women, who are in need of sustaining their families but without opportunities/knowledge/skills to create income. There is a need to tailor activities to their needs and providing childcare to enable their participation. Further, PWDs are in need of opportunities that are accessible and create independence.

5 - Implementation of activities

Violet aims to respond to emergency needs by implementing:1. Distribution of 14,273 RTERs to the newly IDPs, covering their basic needs of food for 5 days - upon the family size criteria (1 ration per 5 members) 6,273 RTEs are secured2. Distribution of 20,000 Multipurpose grants for the newly IDPs and vulnerable groups (6100 are secured) 3. distribution (repetitive) 2 rounds MPGs with a value of 120 $ per HH for 1,782 HHs (all are secured)4.Distribution of 11,200 one-off food kit sufficient to cover basic needs of 5 members family for 1 month (3200 secured)5. Distribution of 14,500 cooked meals for the newly IDPs and vulnerable groups (4500 are secured) Also Violet aims to support livelihoods and agriculture activities:1. 1100 HHs have improved access to income-generating activities through CFW activities (at least 50% female) through olive VC.2. 700 individuals (at least 50% female, and PWDs) improved their capacity through relevant vocational training (230 are secured).200 individuals (at least 50% female) have received small business grants to enable them to start their own business (60 are secured). 3. 1,500 HHs (100,000 sheep) have improved livestock production through vaccinating livestock and/or providing consultations.4. 700 HHs have improved their livelihoods by receiving a poultry kit and fodder. 5. 1000 Farmers have improved their agricultural production through receive agricultural commodity restricted vouchers to cultivate their lands and provision of GAP training and consultations (300 are secured).6. installation of 18 solar irrigation systems each one is supporting 10 farmers HHs in Jarablus and Salqin to strengthen the outputs of the agriculture sector (all are secured). Violet is linking between the SO1 and SO2 referring the vulnerable cases who benefited from the activities under SO1 to be included in activities under SO2 in the targeted locations to improve the livelihoods of those families and support their resilience

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. A key risk is gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse through humanitarian workers. As Violet’s staff will interact with the most vulnerable groups of the community, the power dynamics between staff and beneficiary can be exploited. Violet trains all staff on PSEA, raises awareness of beneficiaries and informs on available complaints mechanism. The M&E team will include post-monitoring and PSEA topics in supervision sessions to identify any misconduct. Any complaints, allegations or rumors will be elevated on the country director level and investigated immediately.2. For community activities, there are child protection risks and GBV risks for participants e.g. due to the time of activities or their location. Violet’s team will mitigate those by assessing appropriate times for PSS activities and choosing a location that allows safe travel. Distances will be minimal and locations will provide sufficient shelter. 3. During evacuations, all community members are exposed to protection risks due to their dependency on those conducting the evacuation. Violet will ensure both awareness of staff on code of conduct and PSEA, but also include monitoring questions for PSEA and satisfaction with the evacuation. 4. During the evacuation, people may be exposed to intensive shilling while they are fleeing. Thus, Violet will ensure continuous coordination with OCHA to de-conflict the roads and areas utilized to allocate people to safe areas.
