Project Summary

To ensure continued access to quality and protective education across crisis affected communities of Nagero (Western Equatoria) Uror (Jonglei) and Pochalla (Pibor Administrative area), this project will support 9,200 (5,0016 boys and 4,104 girls) school going age, and 410 (210 female and 200 male) inclusive of teachers and school management committee members. The 12 months’ project will support all the 3 cluster specific objectives. By providing learning spaces and gender segregated latrines, the project will contribute to increase access to education opportunities for out of school and at risk children, support teacher trainings, ensure learning scholastic materials, facilitate recreation activities among other participatory initiatives for children dedicated to relief psychological distress, boost school governance systems as well as county education coordination and information flow. Hold the Child will work in collaboration with other education player as well as other sectors to ensure key message inter-linkages and collectively contribute to making communities safer for children

1 - Needs

In the back drop of poor service infrastructure, high illiteracy rates and exceptional vulnerability to season shock and outbreaks; South Sudan’s protracted violence characterized with localized clashes continue to undermine even the most promising improvements in school enrolments. Between 2018 and 2021 alone, the number of out of school children rose from 2.2 to 2.8 million (UNICEF 2021 reports). IDPs into Nagero from Tumbura crisis, flood displacements in Pochalla, flood and violence displacement in Uror are some of the new displacements have exacerbated the pressure on the already existing below average school facilities. The October 2020 inter-agency assessment conducted in Uror following the flooding session, expressed a deteriorated education state with collapsed schools, latrines with visible open defecation, high school drop-out rates especially for girls due to lack of sanitary pads. Whereas the September 2021 Nagero IRNA report estimated up-to 900 pupils have dropped out of the school due to financial constraints, a lack of lack scholastic materials, lack of trained teachers and no incentives for the supporting volunteer teams, a drop-out risk of over 12,000 registered pupils. The project is therefore designed to address needs including♣ Low school enrolments associated with local walking distances, local ignorance among other social economic barriers♣ Limited or learning spaces and WASH facilities due to collapses under floods and vandalisation during clashes/occupation of IDPs♣ High leaner- teacher ratios, a trend characterized with a lack of trained teachers, constrained remunerations♣ Distress among leaners (girls and boys) due to displacements and associated life experiences including neglect, abuse etc.♣ Weak or no school governance structures such as the SMC to ensure effective handling of existing school resources and directly engagement of communities in school business among measures to ensure higher enrolment and retention rates ♣ Last of scholastic materials And the need for inclusive lifesaving key messages in collaboration with other sectors to ensure protective learning environments for boys and girls; and strengthened education sector information flow from project counties

2 - Project activities

♣ Establish temporary learning spaces/classrooms in areas with high IDP populations♣ Rehabilitate schools (earning spaces and classrooms) formerly destroyed by floods♣ Establish gender segregated latrines and hand washing points in IDP hosting communities, and renovate flood affected units♣ Distribute or provide sanitation and hygiene materials (hand washing soap, sanitizers, masks, etc.) and PPE for students and teachers ♣ Conduct teacher trainings on EiE pedagogical skills and child-centred methodologies to improve teaching and learning process for emergency-affected children.♣ Conduct teacher trainings on crosscutting themes including on PSS (MHPSS), CP, and referral mechanisms including CP/GBV, health and nutrition♣ Conduct stakeholder inclusive ‘back to school campaign’ to ensure that school age going boys and girls are enrolled children in Pre-primary, Primary and Secondary schools/learning centers. ♣ Provide teaching and learning materials for ECD and primary schools♣ Provide recreational materials and promote learner participatory (peer-peer) PSS approaches ♣ Facilitate school based key massage dissemination through clubs and co-curricular activities; thematic message areas to include PSS (MHPSS), CP, and referral mechanisms including CP/GBV, health and nutrition♣ Provide life-skills and basic vocational training to youth and adolescents ♣ Participate in the national education cluster coordination activities, ensure 5Ws reporting, and participate in County/Payam level needs assessments (for education cluster).♣ Provide technical assistance to county education authorities, facilitate local coordination, and improve information flow with state and national stakeholders ♣ Conduct trainings for SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management, governance, psychosocial support (PSS) and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR)/ climate changeSupport prevention and mitigation of communicable diseases through training school communities by providing appropriate Information and Education and Communication (IEC) materials (i.e. posters, leaflets)

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Key beneficiaries’ feedback inputs subject to management review and impact on the overall, cleared inputs will inform: [1] Option delivery approaches especially in the face of access constraints due to crisis, season or outbreaks; and Progressive review of targeting criteria and service packaging to ensure contextual inclusivity during the project cycle/life.[2] Lessons for progressive impact maximization and future implementation in similar contexts.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

To ensure equitable involvement of the most vulnerable members of the project catchment populations, the project will: [1] Information delivery: In coordination with relevant sectors, hold issue based information sessions (e.g. sensitization on local referral mechanisms). Other measures will include IEC materials or existing channels i.e. radio, faith leaders etc. to ensure equitable reach. [2] Feedback collection: Dedicated Focus group discussions (FGDs) for say women, persons with disabilities where project aspects of concern to specific groups will be reflected over and local inputs collected. Dedicated session will deploy among feedback collection technics i.e. consensus, short answer questions, tracking of expressions etc.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

• Carrying out community sensitization on what is meant by the Protection from sexual Exploitation and Abuse, forms and others using protection cluster determined key messages, and in collaboration with protection partners• Where and how report cases of PSEA without putting the life or state of the report in danger (making use of the protection cluster determined local referral pathways• Regular community leader meetings on PSEA in collaboration with protection cluster partners

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will provide beneficiaries information about the project in the following ways(a) The project will commence with an inception session will will include key holders i.e. local authorities, opinion leaders, as well beneficiary representatives. The session will fully explain the project design, beneficiaries and inclusion criteria and entitlements, resources, feedback information flow channels (in simple language). The session will include processes to collect inputs from across the participants, where critical elements could be re-examined by the management to ensure effective delivery and inclusive/equitable beneficiary reach/engagement(b) Hold the Child will continue with its local information flow culture where operational bases are provided with information flow features include suggestion boxes where complaints can be related to the project management, and notice boards where project updates are posted. Project activities will ensure that these channels/features are popularized for optimal local usage. (c) To ensure equitable access and participation of all beneficiary population segments with project information flow processes, Director engagement be the core channel for passing information and drawing information from final beneficiaries including learners for example; (i) the project teams will pass messages during club or regular school activities, and (ii) draw feedback through focus group sessions. 10-20 participants (boy and girls) will compose focus group sessions, some sessions will only include boys or girls; the project work plan will schedule these feedback sessions at given milestones e.g. post recreation material distribution etc. Across the described blend of information flow and feedback measures/features, Hold the Child will uphold the 5 core humanitarian principles, and ensure dignified project delivery. Note: Beneficiary feedback will form a critical element of the overall project evaluation processes, dedicated focus groups sessions for beneficiary categories i.e. PTAs, teachers, local leaders and education officials will be scheduled in at given intervals in the project cycle.
