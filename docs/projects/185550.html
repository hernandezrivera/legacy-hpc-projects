Project Summary

This project will address urgent child protection needs and gaps linked to the protection and displacement crisis in NWSW Cameroon, providing support to 17,510 children and caregivers through a combination of community-based systems strengthening and the provision of specialised support. This project will be implemented within the NWSW regions, focusing on the most vulnerable conflict-affected and displaced children.
Street Child is committed to localisation and capacity building for local organisations with the aim of making interventions more effective, impactful and sustainable. All activities will be implemented hand-in-hand with local partners, and all partners will be provided with child protection technical capacity building as well as organizational strengthening support (targeting, for example, financial management or monitoring & evaluation capacity) depending on need.

1 - Besoins / Needs

As a result of ongoing conflict and displacement, child protection risks in the NWSW regions are severe. Commonly reported risks facing children in NWSW including early pregnancy (reported by 32%), GBV (reported by 30%)< arbitrary detainment (reported by 29%) and family separation (reported by 29%) (MSNA, 2021). Among the most vulnerable are the high numbers of unaccompanied and separated children, separated from caregivers through fleeing violence or being sent to urban centers in search of education or livelihoods opportunities, and at-risk of neglect, exploitation and abuse. Children affected by the NWSW crisis are urgently in need of psychosocial support - the weakening of community support structures and protective environments offered by schools have worsened pyscho-social impacts and distress faced by children, and low levels of mental health literacy and stigma limit community- and family-based support. Street Child will provide age-appropriate mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) to children across all target communities. This will include the establishment of community-based child friendly spaces (CFS) to provide safe spaces for play, PSS and life skills to vulnerable children, particularly out-of-school children. In addition, mental health literacy and peer-to-peer PSS support amongst youth will be strengthened by training youth leaders on PSS and PFA, and developing a culturally-appropriate MHPSS toolbox disseminated through youth-friendly mobile technology. Case management for acutely at-risk children has been identified as gap in the existing response, and there is a need to improve case management in line with global standards. Targeting the acute needs of the most vulnerable children, including unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), and children associated with armed groups (CAAG), the provision of specialised case management support will be strengthened. Community-based case workers will be trained to identify children in need of case management and provide quality case management in line with interagency standards, and including referral to specialised services such as GBV specialist support, mental health support, alternative care and/or family tracing and reunification, or provision of civil documentation. For marginalised adolescents identified through MHPSS and case management work, support is needed to ensure their reintegration and participation in families and communities. Among the most marginalized who will be supported are children associated with armed groups, young mothers pregnant through association with armed groups, and other victims of violence. This support will include enrolment in vocational skills and livelihoods programme, and will be linked to positive parenting training for young parents to promote parental and child wellbeing and early childhood development. Across all targeted communities, there is a need to strengthen the capacity of communities and local actors to provide quality support to the most vulnerable children, to ensure continuity and sustainability of responses. Community capacity to prevent and respond to child protection risks will be strengthened through the establishment and training of community-based child protection committees (CBCPCs) to identify, mitigate, and respond to child protection risks in their communities. Alternative caregivers will be selected and trained to be prepared to provide quality foster care to unaccompanied and separated children identified. In all communities, this project will be implemented in close collaboration with community stakeholders and local/community-based organizations. Where feasible, it will also engage government stakeholders – for example through training of government social workers on case management, working with government to provide civil documentation to vulnerable children, and advocating with government to promote family-based care for UASC rather than institutional care.
