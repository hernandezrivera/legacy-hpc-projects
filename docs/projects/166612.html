Project Summary

The COVID-19 pandemic has worsened the plight of people who were classified into crisis and emergency phases (Food and Nutrition Security Monitoring, April 2020). The country has been placed under lockdown since 30th March and this impacts a further challenge offood security given that 92% of the working population is in the informal sector mainly surviving on resources raised on a day to day basis. ZIMVAC report projects 7.7 million people to be food insecure out of which 2.2 million in urban areas.
The recent Crop and Livestock Report stresses the 2019/2020 season was marked by a delayed onset
and a false start throughout the country. Average dam capacity for the big dams stands at 47.5% (lowest
being 23% capacity). This resulted in reduced area planted and subsequent expected yield. According
to Rapid Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment conducted by Oxfam, the expected yields are very low and
the expected cereal deficit in Bulilima district is 81% and that for Matobo is 85%. El-Nino induced
drought and the destruction caused by Cyclone Idai induced drought resulted in lost casual labour
opportunities for the rural population due to the late start of the 2019/20 rainfall season due to which
Zimbabwe's economy contracted in 2019 by an estimated 6.5%, weighed down by exogenous shocks.
The recent government imposed lockdown to contain the spread of COVID-19 created obstacles for
farmers, farm labourers, farm service providers, extension officers, input suppliers, processors and other
actors in the food system to perform their tasks. A rapid assessment conducted for the markets in
Bulilima and Matobo revealed that most businesses were not fully operational because of COVID-19
measures put in place. Petit traders, especially women groups, who have been affected by the
prolonged lockdown require livelihood stimulus package to boost their small businesses. Cash shortages continue to have negative impact on livelihoods in both rural and urban areas. The use of the
3-tier pricing system (using local currency, USD and other foreign currencies of neighbouring countries)
contributed to distorted prices of goods and services. Poverty continues to be one of the major
underlying causes of vulnerability to food and nutrition insecurity as well as precarious livelihoods in
Zimbabwe. According to the ZIMSTAT Poverty, Income, Consumption and Expenditure Survey 2017
Report, 70.5% of the population were poor whilst 29.3% were deemed extremely poor. Prices of basic
food commodities increased due to prolonged lockdown.
The action will focus on restoring livelihoods and resilience building on the most vulnerable population, and will include: agricultural input distributions, climate smart agriculture trainings, provision of drought tolerant seeds for cereals and legumes, fertilizers, training of extension farmers and workers on climate smart agriculture, crop production training for food and cash crops and post- harvest handling storage and processing and rehabilitation of diptanks, irrigation schemes, and provision of garden kits. The action will ensure that the needs of women and girls that are exposed to COVID-19 related domestic gender based violence will be met by provision of informational campaign on relevant referral pathways and services.
It will target 20.000 people in Masvingo, Mangwe, Bulilima, Chipinge and Chimanimani.

1 - Activities

1.Provide vegetable seed packs and tools for household, community and school nutrition gardens. 2. Provide drought-tolerant seeds and fertilizers. 3. Provide training, extension and advisory services to extension officers and farmers focusing on good agriculture practices, climate-smart agriculture, crop pest management, post-harvest handling, storage and processing. 4. Promote fodder production and supplementary feeding of cattle. 5. Support livestock vaccination, disease surveillance and management through the community paravet model. 6. Rehabilitate dip tanks and provide dipping chemicals. 7.Enhance access to water for productive purposes. 9. Irrigation scheme and community garden rehabilitation. 9. Promote establishment of school nutrition gardens 10. Awareness raising on food preparation through the healthy harvest module Agriculture support is training in climate change adaptation, inputs support and demo plots. - Seed varieties for region 5 will be as follows 5 kgs sorghum, 5 kgs millet, 50 kgs basal fertiliser and 50 kgs ammonium nitrate and 5 kgs cow peas. For regions 1-3 the pulse ration will be as follows 10 kgs groundnuts, 50 kgs gypsum, 50 kgs compound L fertiliser. For thesame region cereal ration will include 10kgs maize seed, 50 kgs basal and 50 kgs ammonium nitrate. - Livestock support is farmer training on good animal husbandry practices as well as practical demos for forage and fodder production.A total of 20.000 farmers will receive input vouchers work $ 80 as per FAO agriculture input guidelines. The beneficiaries twill redeem thr vouchers at selected retail outletsThe beneficiary prioritization will focus on the most vulnerable and food insecure but owning a piece of land, willing to farm and not labor constrained to qualify. A ranking process will be done to identify the people to benefit from the project.People assisted will be mobilised and sensitised on the project. Vulnerability indicators will be agreed upon with beneficiaries and a community based participatory process will be adopted to rank people assisted based on their vulnerability criteria. In addition, resilience building trainings will be provided to target farmers that will present various mechanisms of coping strategies. Local communities will be supported to develop contingency planning that include COVID-19 and other potential pandemics.2. Enhanced access to information on referral pathways for abused women, addressing GBV during COVID-19. Beneficiaries will be targeted by awareness raising on GBV and referral pathways. Oxfam will prioritize vulnerable women, female headed households, disadvantaged minority groups, girls, boys to have have equal andsafe access to essential services including food, water and protection. Oxfam will deliberately target the disabled people to benefit throughout the project and will promote their meaningful participation in project implementation. The project will raise awareness on gender-based violence through IEC material, SMSs blasts, jingles and radio talk shows. Gender champions will be strengthened to facilitate door to door GBV prevention messaging in the target areas. Educative materials on gender and food security, gender and COVID-19, gender-based violence in COVID-19, women’s access to resources as well as other relevant themes will be developed and placed in strategic points in the communities including at the assets distribution points.

Number of women and girls with increased/appropriate awareness on addressing GBV and searching for referral pathways during COVID-19With this project Oxfam Zimbabwe aims to contribute to the government’s efforts through the focus on Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE) of the National Response Plan. As a part of the proposed intervention Oxfam will address child abuse, domestic violence, intimate partner violence and tensions in the household. The project will ensure strengthening off referral pathways, with a focus on prevention of GBV, sexual exploitation and abuse and improved access to GBV, PSEA information as well as timely access to post GBV services the during the COVID-19 epidemic
