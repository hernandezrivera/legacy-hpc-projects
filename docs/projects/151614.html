Project Summary

While food security improved due to average and above average rainfall during the GU’ season coupled with sustained large-scale humanitarian assistance, an estimated 1.5 million people in Somalia are expected to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse through December 2018, according to findings from the post-Gu seasonal assessment conducted in June and July 2018. Pastoralists who lost livestock during the 2016/2017 and those affected by cyclone Sagar along the coast in Somaliland and Puntland, Population affected by flooding in April and May 2018 and populations displaced due to conflict and natural disasters will continue to face food consumption gaps through December 2018. The number of the internally displaced person in Somalia stands at 2.6 million. According to the post-Gu’ assessment by FSNAU and FEWS NET, most of the 13 primary internally displaced person in Somalia are in crisis (IPC 3). In the regions of Sanaag, Bari, Nugaal, Mudug, and Galgadud regions, 495,000 people are in crisis and emergency (IPC 3 & 4) including 1,000 IDPs in IPC 5. It is, therefore, crucial to continue humanitarian assistance to prevent deterioration of the food security and nutrition situation of the IPC3 and IPC 4 caseloads and provide livelihood recovery/support interventions for populations who lost critical assets during the drought and floods to rebuild assets and improve livelihoods.
The IRC, therefore, plans to support 2,500 vulnerable IPC3 & IPC 4 caseloads in Sanaag, Bari, Nugaal, Mudug, and Galgadud regions with scalable safety nets to improve vulnerable households access to food and other basic needs, reduce resorting to negative coping strategies and prevent further deterioration and depletion of assets. Furthermore, the IRC plans to support 8,000 households including pastoralists, fisher folks in Bari and Mudug and poor urban households and IDPs with livelihood recovery interventions ranging from Animal health support services, provision of productive assets and enterprise development and skills enhancement. These support services will ensure and provide space for meaningful participation and accountability for the affected population through proper targeting mechanisms for most vulnerable, marginalized and minority groups.

Needs

According to the Post Gu’ assessment report by FSNAU and FEWS, 1.5 million people in Somalia are in crisis (IPC 3). Over 495,000 people in Sanaag, Bari, Nugaal, Mudug, and Galgadud regions are in IPC 3 and 4 and need continued humanitarian assistance to sustain and prevent further deterioration of the food security and nutrition situation of these vulnerable households. More than 2.6 million people are Internally displaced in Somalia. Pastoralists in the regions of Sanaag, Bari, Nugaal, Mudug, and Galgadud have lost more than 50% of their livestock holding making them destitute. Furthermore, the cyclone Sagar has deeply affected pastoralist and fishing along the Bari coastal band villages leading to loss of key critical livelihood assets. Protracted conflicts and natural hazards had displaced more than 333,000 people in these regions between November 2016 and September 2018. An interagency assessment was done in Hobyo and Adado from 21st to 24th July 2018 to assess people displaced by conflicts between AS and local communities indicated that more than 28,000 people affected and were removed from their homes. The populations affected by drought and conflict in these regions will need lifesaving humanitarian assistance and livelihood recovery interventions to prevent further deterioration of the food security situation and build recovery needs of these populations. Key needs of these communities are summarized below:• Provide immediate relief through multi-purpose cash to enable food and other purchasable needs, pay off debts and mitigate the low purchasing power of households• Nutrition intervention for children, elderly, pregnant and lactating women (PLW) • provision of veterinary health services in areas where livestock diseases and provide demand-driven skills development for pastoral communities such as CAHWs refresher training• Reinvigorate livelihoods through skills development for employability and start-up grants for drought-affected households

Activities

1.1: Provide scalable safety net to 3,000 (at least 50% female) vulnerable households in the target locations, with priority given to female, elderly and child-headed households, as well as IDP households to immediately provide access to food, and reduce negative coping strategy ( Priority: Critical)2.0: Form sustainable fishing associations to protect, manage, conserve and rehabilitate the resources of small-scale fishermen, such as basic fishing gear equipment and cold storage facilities ( Priority: High) 2.1: Provide fishing inputs and seasonal fishing based training and other transferable vocational skills to 200 fisher folks along the coastal bands of Mudug and Bandarbeyla to increase production and sustain their livelihood ( Priority: High) 3.0: Train 40 community animal health workers on participatory disease surveillance, reporting and control modules and provide them with veterinary starter kits to build their capacity to increase the sustainability of the community’s livelihood ( Priority: High) 3.1: Conduct animal health best practice campaigns for 10,000 pastoralist communities through livestock deworming and pastoralist field schools to protect livestock and improve production ( Priority: High) 4.0: Rehabilitate and construct key communal assets and infrastructure through the Cash for Work (CfW), for 1,000 households through Rehabilitation/reconstruction of water catchment,feeder/market access roads,and public facilities- Schools, clinics, markets etc ( Priority: High) 5.0 Provide income-generating activities

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The planned areas of operation are areas, where IRC has an acceptance and experience in working over the past 6-7 years to effectively design and implement programs that lead to durable/resilience outcomes by building on experiences in the context, services, and assistance provided in the past and technical capacities developed over this period of operation. The IRC has an understanding of the populations with protection concern, clear understanding of the marginalized groups, the clans and how resources are shared. This understanding of the protection environment will allow the IRC to design programs that will have durable and resilience focus at its core. For long-term sustainability of programs, the IRC will work to strengthen the capacity of local authorities, community leaders and relevant government stakeholder including ministries and other government agencies through joint implementation and capacity strengthening initiatives to enable the existing community are able to sustainably manage resources created by the programme. The program will ensure meaningful participation of communities and respective government authorities to solicit community support and contribution, outline clear exit strategy and the plans for proper handover to enhance ownership and community accountability.
