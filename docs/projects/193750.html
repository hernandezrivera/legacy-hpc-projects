Project Summary

This project was designed based on a thorough analysis of the needs of the target group which consists of women and girls at risk of GBV including GBV survivors in the Gaza Valley Towers in Juhor Al Deek Area, which is one of the most vulnerable and marginalized areas in Gaza governorate where GBV is prevalent, according to an assessment conducted by PDWSA. This project contributes to Cluster Objective: # 2: "Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of occupation and conflict-related violence". It aims to promote the protection of women and girls in the targeted area by applying an integrated protection approach that focuses on addressing the GBV concerns of women and girls and building a cushion of support around them to reduce their suffering. The community, which consists of 3,315 residents includes people who came from different areas in the Gaza Strip either because of social and cultural barriers or to benefit from the housing assistance provided to them by the Ministry of Social Development based on their poverty classification. This community faces discriminatory actions by the original community as they are seen as intruders, which results in high rate of GBV including domestic violence, sexual abuse and exploitation, and deprivation of economic resources.
Based on the Needs Assessment conducted by PDWSA, women and girls in the Gaza Valley towers are exposed on daily basis to different forms of GBV including sexual harassment and abuse, physical assault, economic and psychological actions, or threats of action. Interviewed women and girls reported that they lack legal protection due to the lack of law enforcement in the area as no police station is located there. In addition, women and girls who face GBV incidents are unable to defend their rights and hold the perpetrators of violence to account because they are unaware of the GBV incident reporting pathways and how to seek legal support and protection. In addition, the needs assessment revealed that the hard-economic situation of the families of women and girls who suffer from GBV is the main driver for the continuity of the problem. women and girls keep silent in some cases because they do not want to lose the sources of family livelihood nor to be exposed to further violence and abuse. Furthermore, women and girls who were previously exposed to GBV reported that if they have a sustainable source of income, they will be able to seek legal support through police and courts, and will be able to improve their shelter conditions, which will reduce their exposure to GBV especially sexual harassment.
PDWSA will implement this project in partnership with CFTA which run a women center in Al Burej. In addition, PDWSA will collaborate with the only local CBO in the area, the local protection committee and other key stakeholders to ensure the effective implementation of the project and the protection of project staff and beneficiaries. The project activities will include psychosocial support services, legal consultation and representation, cash for livelihood assistance, case management and referral services to women and girls in need for specialized protection services, capacity building for PDWSA and Staff project on Prevention and Responding to Sexual Abuse and Exploitation, women’s rights, how to report sexual harassment incidents and wrongdoing, and protection from sexual misconduct, capacity building for PDWSA’s local protection committee, staff of partner CBO, and community leaders on women’s rights, GBV, and PSEA, awareness-raising sessions for community members on local laws and legislations, women’s rights, referral pathways to support GBV survivors, and preventive measures to prevent sexual abuse and exploitation inside the community, and advocacy campaign to influence duty bearers. The project will target a total of 2,117 persons (932 women, 310 men, 725 girls, and 150 boys) including 200 people with disabilities.

1 - Needs

In October 2022, PDWSA conducted a needs assessment to inform the design of this project which included focus groups discussion, and interviews with key stakeholders including the social police section, the municipality of Gaza Valley, the local CBO, and the local protection committee of PDWSA in the targeted area. The needs assessment was intended to analyze the needs of the target group in a participatory manner and to discuss potential activities and work modalities. A total of 10 men and 30 women and girls (20 women and 10 girls) including 2 people with disabilities at risk of GBV including GBV survivors from Gaza Valley towers have participated in the focus group discussions. In addition, 10 persons (5 males and 5 females) constituting the local protection committee and 5 persons from the social police section, local CBO in the area, and the municipality were interviewed. Based on the needs assessment, the following problems and needs were identified: - Gender-based violence is prevalent in the targeted area due to poverty, cultural factors, and weak law enforcement. Among the 30 women and girls who participated in the FGDs, 22 women and girls reported that they were exposed to a form of GBV at least in the past 12 months. This included sexual harassment, verbal or physical assault, and domestic violence.- The new population of the Gaza Valley Towers, especially women and girls, is exposed regularly to different forms if assault and violence as they are seen and treated by the original population of the area which is a tribal community as intruders. The original population believes that this new community competes with them on humanitarian assistance and threatens their livelihoods. In addition, the weak law enforcement which can be seen in the absence of a police station in the area exacerbates the situation. As a result, women and girls are prone to GBV. More than 80% of the assessment population believes that the non-existence of a police station in the area is the main reason for the GBV incidents that happen in the area. Further, almost 100% believe that the intervention of the police cannot be effective without community awareness about women's girls and protection mechanisms. - Poverty is a key driver for GBV in the area. Due to poverty, the population of the targeted community suffers from tough psychological and social conditions that influence their behaviors and attitudes and result in discriminatory and violent actions against the new population. Out of the 40 women, girls, and men who participated in the FGDs, 32 reported that the difficult economic situation for most of the families in the area leads to a higher GBV rate. Women-headed households are the neediest category as they lack safe and sustainable access to economic resources. In addition, the participants have clarified that some women and girls who were exposed to GBV including sexual abuse and exploitation felt reluctant to seek legal justice as the economic situation does not allow them to pay for legal services.- The social stigma and fear of being harmed due to the tribal feature of the community forces women and their husbands not to legally come after the perpetrators of violence. 30% of the interviewed study population believe that women and their families will be exposed to additional violence if they take legal actions against perpetrators of violence. All of them believe that the lack of community awareness about women's rights, GBV, and relevant local laws and legislations is the main reason for that. Therefore, complaining women and their families need legal consultation, support, and protection to encourage them seek legal justice. The aforementioned findings and needs were also confirmed from a broader point of view in the MSNA assessment conducted by OCHA in July 2022 and in the protection analysis update (July 2022) of the protection cluster. Based on the problems and needs identified in the needs assessment, a set of activities were developed to ensure that the needs and expectations of the target group is met. This response includes adopting the cash modality to respond to the protection needs of the target group. A group of women-headed households will be provided with cash for livelihood assistance to establish income-generating projects to promote their protection. The cash for livelihood will strengthen the access of the targeted women-headed households, who are the most vulnerable in target group, to sustainable economic resources, which will promote their protection and safety. They will be able to rely on themselves for living, which will allow them to face perpetrators of violence who are in some cases family members taking care of them financially. In addition, the economic activity of the targeted women will allow them to engage better in the community and establish positive relations with other community members which will reduce the cultural gap and improve the social relations. Based on previous interventions, this will also contribute to reducing women’s exposure to GBV. The value of the cash assistance was decided based on the past expertise of PDWSA in the area of economic empowerment for GBV survivors. PDWSA has also implemented previous projects in the same area during which it collected information about the needs of targeted women in the area of economic empowerment. The defined amount of cash assistance will allow women to establish income-generating projects by supplying all needed assets and inputs. In addition, PDWSA has established discussions with some NGOs with extensive experience in the area of economic empowerment such as MAAN Development Center and United Palestinian Appeal to learn about their past experience and discuss the proposed method and techniques for the implementation of the activity. All results were supportive of the cash modality to strengthen women's access to livelihood.

2 - Activities

AC1: Providing psychosocial support services to GBV survivors: a total of 300 women and girls will be able to receive consultation for one time on how to address a GBV problem/issue. In addition, group and individual counselling sessions will be implemented to help women release their stress, develop skills to face their psychosocial problems, and overcome their mental health difficulties. A total of 700 women and girls will be targeted by the counselling sessions. Each individual will receive an average of 6-8 individual counselling sessions, whereas each group of 10 women/girls will receive 3-5 group counselling sessions. AC2: Case Management and Referral: women and girls in need of specialized protection services (legal, health, mental health, livelihood support, etc..) will be referred to service providers who are members of Wessal Network of CFTA. Case managers from PDWSA and CFTA will be responsible for managing this activity in accordance with the case management toolkit approved by the Ministry of Women Affairs and UNFPA. PDWSA’s case manager in coordination with the case manager of CFTA will be responsible for following up the referred cases to make sure that they are receiving the required services and making progress. Case management process performed by the partner will be supervised by PDWSA’s case manager who will make sure that the case management protocol is in place. AC3: Legal Consultations and Legal Representation Services: PDWSA will provide legal consultation through legal information sessions to women and men in need of this service. The legal consultation sessions will help women and men survivors of GBV obtain legal information to lodge a legal complaint/take legal action following a GBV incident. These sessions will be provided by female lawyers at PDWSA. In addition, GBV survivors will be assisted to seek legal justice through legal representation services in the courts to be provided by lawyers at CFTA under the supervision of PDWSA. PDWSA runs a network of female lawyers in the middle area with long expertise in the area of legal consultation and representation. This network also plays the role of social mediation which will be utilized to address the reported GBV issues before moving them to the next stage of legal representation. AC4: Cash for Livelihood: this activity is intended to support GBV survivors establish income-generating projects by adopting the cash for livelihood modality. This activity will empower the targeted women-headed households to create licit sources of livelihood opportunities in order to achieve economic empowerment and break the cycle of economic dependence upon perpetrators who may otherwise continue to exploit their vulnerabilities. In addition, based on the previous experience of PDWSA, engaging GBV survivors in economic activities will promote their community engagement and participation, which in this context, will help overcome the cultural barriers that lead to GBV in the targeted community. PDWSA will rely on its long experience in this field to ensure the sustainability of the implemented projects. A total of 30 women-headed households including people with disabilities will be selected to receive cash assistance to start income-generating activities (IGAs) based on socio-economic assessment and livelihood mapping that will be conducted by PDWSA. The cash value of each IGA will range between 2500 and 3000 USD and will be paid over instalments based on monitoring reports.AC5: Safety Mapping Assessment: PDWSA and CFTA will implement a safety mapping assessment for women who were selected to receive cash for livelihood to examine the impact of the activity on the protection status of the targeted women and identify and explore any reverse effects that this activity may inflict on the protection of the targeted women. AC6: Training for the project staff: Project Staff of PDWSA and CFTA will be trained on protection topics including PSEA to build their capacity to implement the project effectively and promote the quality of services provided to the target groups. The topics to be addressed will include Prevention and Responding to Sexual Abuse and Exploitation, women’s rights, how to report sexual harassment incidents and wrongdoing, and protection from sexual misconduct. A total of 12 persons from PDWSA and CFTA will receive 12 training hours over 3 training days. Pre and post-training surveys will be used to measure improvement in the knowledge and skills of trainees.AC7: Training for the Local protection committee of PDWSA, staff of partner CBO, and community leaders (Moukhtars) on PSEA, GBV, and women’s rights. A total of 20 persons (both males and females, including PWDs) constituting PDWSA’s protection committee in Juhor Al Deek, community leaders and staff of the partner CBO in Juhor Al Deek will be trained on women’s rights, GBV, and PSEA to promote their role in advancing women’s rights and protection in the targeted community. A total of 20 training hours will be provided to one group of trainees. AC8: Awareness sessions for community members will be implemented to increase community awareness about important protection issues including local laws and legislations, women’s rights, referral pathways to support women at risk of GBV including GBV survivors, and preventive measures to prevent sexual abuse and exploitation inside the community. A total of 700 persons including women, men, and people with disabilities will be targeted by the sessions. AC9: Advocacy Campaign: This will include issuing 2 fact sheets about the GBV situation in the targeted area and the discriminatory actions against the new population of Gaza Valley Towers to bring the attention of the responsible authorities and urge them and NGOs to act. In addition, awareness-raising brochures will be printed out and distributed to community members to mobilize them to support the efforts of key stakeholders in combating GBV.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

In the past years, PDWSA has been working on developing an accountable, accessible, and gender-sensitive complaint system. The system includes particular procedures to address gender-sensitive complaints including SEA complaints. The system is managed by involving different layers of PDWSA’s governance structure. PDWSA adopts simple, safe, and accessible Community Based Complaints Mechanism (CBCM) for SEA. The channels that will be used to report SEA complaints take into consideration specific needs of the target group based on age, gender and diversity, including disability. These channels will include the following: • Promoting outreach to the targeted community by paying visits to informal community gatherings and engaging with them to learn about their concerns and thoughts. • PDWSA will appoint SEA focal point that will be known by both the project staff and targeted community. • Helpdesks will be established during the implementation of the activities and delivery of services. • Secure complaints/suggestions boxes, available for all type of complaints and suggestions (without writing on them a specific designation, for confidentiality reasons).• Training Community members and project staff on SEA.The M&E activities of this project will aim to document the lessons learned which will be done in a participatory manner by involving the project target group and key stakeholders such as the municipality, women police, women organizations, and human rights organizations. The captured lessons learned and other data that can be useful to these stakeholders will be shared with them to support their program advancement in the relevant area. In addition, the received feedback and complaints will be included in the project report and will be shared in a safe way that ensures the confidentiality of sensitive information and the protection of women with relevant stakeholders to enhance the effective provision of protection services by other actors.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

At the beginning of October 2022, PDWSA conducted an assessment to analyze the needs of the project beneficiaries which included identifying the key risks, protection threats, and barriers facing women and girls who live in the Gaza Valley Towers and affecting their protection, wellbeing, and safety. These threats and risks included 1- the discriminatory attitudes and actions of the original population of the area which consider the new population as intruders who are stealing their humanitarian assistance, 2- lack of awareness among the area population about gender, women’s rights, child protection, sexual abuse prevention, GBV, and protection principles which results in poor community support to address the problem, 3- the hard economic situation and poverty among the population of the area which in some cases may expose women to sexual exploitation or to forced child marriage, 4- lack of awareness among women and girls about self-protection, referral pathways, legal rights and services, and 5- lack of access by women and girls who are at risk of GBV or GBV survivors to multi-sectoral protection services including psychosocial support and legal representation. In order to make sure that this intervention will do no harm, the project considers the following: 1- Safety Mapping Assessment for women-headed households who will be supported with cash assistance to establish income-generation projects to achieve economic empowerment. PDWSA will conduct Safety Mapping Assessment to examine any potential negative effects of this support on the protection and well-being of the targeted women and set out mitigation measures. 2- Working with existing influencing community structures and implement advocacy and outreach activities to influence their actions and attitudes. The project activities will be coordinated with a local CBO in the area, community leaders, and the local protection committee of PDWSA to ensure smooth and safe access to the targeted community. 3- Capacity building for the project staff on protection principles, risk mitigation and safety and protection principles and practices in humanitarian response to increase their capacity to address protection and safety risks during work. In addition, coordination and cooperation networks including will be established with key organizations with expertise to ensure the protection of the project staff such as the Social Police Section in Al Nuseirat Policy Center, local CBO in the area, and Moukhtars. 4- Involving the target group and key stakeholders in the design, implementation, and monitoring phases of the project: PDWSA has ensured the effective participation of the target groups including GBV survivors, women and girls at risk of GBV, and men in addition to key stakeholders including the municipality, the social police section, the implementing partner, local CBO, and the protection committee of PDWSA in Juhor Al Deek during the design phase of the project. They have participated in analyzing the needs of the project's main target group and identifying the protection risks associated with the implementation of the project. PDWSA will also ensure the effective participation of these groups in the implementation and monitoring phases.5- Accessible and gender-sensitive complaints and feedback mechanisms: This project will apply a complaints and feedback mechanism that is accessible by all project population which will promote the protection of the project beneficiaries and staff. The mechanism will include hotline, complaint boxes, direct contact with beneficiaries during field visits, and satisfaction surveys. The targeted population will be able to share their concerns and thoughts and share their feedback in a confidential, gender-sensitive, and effective way and by various means which will promote the effective action of PDWSA and its implementing partner to address the received complaints and feedback.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Services that will be provided by PDWSA and CFTA within this project will be accessed by all target groups equitably and in an impartial way. All project beneficiaries will be treated equally and fairly as they will receive the same type and quality of support based on vulnerability type and need, and will have same opportunity to benefit from the project resources and activities. PDWSA applies a code of conduct on which PDWSA’s Staff including the project staff are trained. The core of this code of conduct is the non-discrimination principle which entails empowering all beneficiaries including people with disabilities to access project services equally and to give them the same opportunity to benefit from the available resources. The code of conduct elaborates the actions and procedures that should be followed by staff to ensure non-discrimination and disability inclusion in all work phases and areas. The problems analysis and stakeholder analysis carried out as part of the project design will guide the process of service provision as they show the problem facing each group of beneficiaries, and services they need to overcome barriers undermining their protection. People with disabilities including women and girls will have equal access to the project services. The location and timing of service provision will consider their specific needs as well as they will be provided with extra support by the staff of PDWSA and its implementing partner including information on service provision adapted to diverse disabilities to ensure that they are able to access the project services safely and effectively. PDWSA have included women with disabilities, women-headed households, and widows in the discussion meetings which were implemented to inform the design of this project. Their concerns were reported and the project action plan considered addressing their needs in the most effective way. Some barriers may face the targeted women and girls and may undermine their ability to access the project services such as the cultural norms of the targeted community which does not support women’s participation and leadership. According to the risk assessment, these barriers may include the lack of effective participation of women in the project activities such as the awareness sessions, the inferior look towards the new population who is residing in the Gaza Valley Towers and the discriminatory and aggressive actions by the original population against them, and the difficult economic situation of the target group which may prevent their ability to access the project services. To address these barriers, PDWSA ensured that services provided within this project are accessible to all project beneficiaries including women and girls with disabilities by cooperating with CFTA which runs a women center in Al Burej that is close to the beneficiaries’ location. In addition, PDWSA will work on increasing the awareness of the community members about the rights of women, GBV, and local civil and criminal laws which will reduce violence against women and increase community support. Further, PDWSA will implement the project activities that target women, girls, and other community members, including the awareness-raising sessions and training in a local CBO in the targeted area which is disability-friendly and will ensure that all arrangements in place consider the culture of the community and the specific needs of the people with disabilities. At the level of the project design, specific indicators were developed to measure the accessibility of project services to people with disabilities and their effective participation in the project activities. These indicators include - # of women with disabilities who were provided with cash for livelihood to implement income-generating projects, # of women/girls with disabilities who participated in the awareness-raising sessions on GBV, self-protection, prevention of sexual abuse and harassment, and referral pathways
