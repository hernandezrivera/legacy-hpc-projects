Project Summary

Chad is experiencing a worrying nutritional situation underpinned by food insecurity, a high incidence of childhood diseases, endemic poverty, and a weak health system. Today, 1.7 million people are affected by health emergencies, with children and pregnant and lactating women being particularly vulnerable. Based on criteria of prevalence (GAM >= 10% and/or SAM >= 2%) and/or mortality (overall mortality rate >= 1/10,000/day and/or under-five mortality rate >= 2/10,000/d), 17 provinces out of 23 are considered priority provinces for SAM treatment in 2022 (Ndjamena, Tibesti, Ouaddaï, Sila, Borkou, Chari Baguirmi, Mayo Kebi Est, Hadjer Lamis, Guéra, Salamat, Batha, Wadi fira, Kanem, Barh El-Ghazel, Ennedi Est, Ennedi Ouest and Logone Oriental). The provinces of Lac, Moyen Chari, Mandoul are also considered as priority provinces due to the aggravating factors such as conflict and population displacement. There is a problem with securing the supply of nutritional supplies as well as disruptions in the logistical aspects at the international level, which can increase the risk of disruption in the supply chain, leading to frequent stock-outs. In order to respond to the populations affected by the so-called "Sahel and Health" crisis, UNICEF proposes to strengthen national capacity to improve access to treatment for 262,335severely acutely malnourished children in existing outpatient therapeutic program (OTP) and inpatient facilities. Preventive nutrition services will be strengthened at the community and health facility levels. At the community level, the actions will integrate the early detection of children under 5 years of age suffering from acute malnutrition and their referral through mothers and caregivers, while strengthening the capacity of the latter on essential nutrition actions, including the optimal feeding practices for young children, in order to prevent all forms of malnutrition. Thus, 1,283 community health workers will be trained on the Mother-MUAC approach as well as on interpersonal communication and awareness raising activities and will be responsible for providing coaching to mothers and caregivers on MUAC screening while ensuring their follow-up. The capacity of nutrition service delivery in the targeted health facilities will be strengthened in additional human resources. The Ministry of Health will be supported to strengthen the coordination mechanism at the national and provincial levels as well as the monitoring and evaluation of interventions through joint supervision missions including End User Monitoring. This action will also contribute to the response to measles through vitamin A supplementation for 420,000 children and reduce micronutrient deficiencies.

1 - Besoins, Activités et Resultats Attendus

According to the results of the SMART 2021 survey, the prevalence of global acute malnutrition at the national level is 10.9%, compared to 10.0% in 2020, with great disparity at the provincial level. Seven provinces have very high or critical prevalence (>=15%) according to the WHO classification. Severe acute malnutrition (SAM) hovers at 2.0%, compared to 2.1% in 2020 and 2.9% in 2019. Based on the criteria of prevalence (GAM >= 10% and/or SAM >=2%) and/or mortality (overall mortality rate >=1/10,000/day and/or under-five mortality rate >=2/10,000/d), 17 out of 23 provinces are considered priorities for treating SAM in 2021 (Ndjamena, Borkou, Mayo Kebi East, Chari Baguirmi, Guéra, Salamat, Tibesti, Hadjer Lamis, Batha, Ouaddaï, Sila, Wadi fira, Barh El-Ghazel, Ennedi West, Kanem, Ennedi East, and the Lake (due to the very precarious security situation which may increase the level of vulnerability of the populations)). The highest GAM prevalence is observed in Ennedi West with 18.5 (14.4-23.3).The Nutrition Cluster estimates (HNO/HRP 2022) that in 2022, 275,848 SAM children will need treatment, including 8,438 refugee children and 5,075 repatriated and displaced children. The IPC (Integrated Food Security Phase Classification) analysis of malnutrition in 2021 showed that of the 57 units of analysis (5 provinces and 52 departments), 4 provinces and 27 departments are in a serious to critical nutritional situation with nearly half of the children under 5 years of age (47%) living in these provinces and departments. In addition, the projection shows a continued deterioration of the nutritional situation, particularly during the lean season (June to September 2022) when 5 provinces and 29 departments will be in a serious to critical phase.The corollary of this precarious nutritional situation is the combination of several aggravating factors, such as inadequate food intake, poor infant and young child feeding practices, high prevalence of childhood diseases (malaria, diarrhea, Acute Respiratory Infection) with an increase in the frequency of measles epidemics, low measles vaccination coverage, low vitamin A supplementation coverage, poor hygiene conditions (inaccessibility to adequate sanitation facilities), low coverage of access to drinking water, low coverage of nutritional and health interventions, the harmful effects of farmer-breeder conflicts, the effects of the deleterious security situation with restrictive measures and acute food insecurity in some provinces, the consequences of climate change (flooding, drought, poor rainfall distribution). The management of malnutrition faces many obstacles, such as insufficient domestic investment and weak operational capacity at the health facility and community levels. Nutritional units face a lack of qualified human resources, insufficient materials, and equipment, especially for the management of SAM children with medical complications. In addition, access to nutritional care has been particularly marked by recurrent shortages of nutritional inputs and systematic and specific treatment drugs in nutritional units. These frequent shortages considerably limit access to nutritional care and jeopardize the vital prognosis of malnourished children under 5 years of age. The inefficient use and illicit marketing of these inputs are one of the main causes of wastage and therefore of shortages.Several contributing factors also contribute to the deterioration of the nutritional status of the population in Chad. The most immediate ones are insufficient food intake, diseases, and poor care practices in households. Indeed, nutritional practices remain poorly adapted, negatively impacting the nutritional status of children under 5 years old. According to the results of the SMART 2021 survey, only about 1 in 10 (11.5%) children aged 6 to 23 months have a minimum acceptable feeding practice and only 11.4% of children under 6 months are exclusively breastfed. According to the SMART 2021 survey, 44% of children suffer from acute respiratory infections and/or diarrheal diseases and/or febrile illnesses (predisposing them to acute malnutrition), as well as recurrent epidemics such as measles. Health and community structures, given their limited capacity, also have difficulty offering quality services while respecting COVID's prevention measures.19. Most of the provinces located in the Sahelian strip are particularly affected by this situation, exposing children under the age of 5 and women to high vulnerability.
