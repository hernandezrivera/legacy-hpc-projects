Project Summary

According to the Shelter Sector, the 4 August blast impacted an estimated 72,265 apartments in 9,700 buildings located within three kilometres of the epicenter. It is believed that 29,771 apartments (89,313 individuals) fall within level 1 damages - minor damages, 17,100 apartments (51,300 ind.) fall within level 2 - moderate - and 1,144 apartments (30,660 ind.) fall within level 3 - heavy damage. The explosion also affected communal areas, shared open-spaces and minor infrastructure, further impacting community self-recovery capacity.
With shelter needs at the core of the crisis, UNHCR will support men, women, girls and boys, both Lebanese and other nationalities, who were affected by the blast. UNHCR's response includes immediate weatherproofing temporary solutions, followed by a phased approach to support individuals and families to repair or rehabilitate houses and to sustain rental payments for those displaced. In addition, through community support projects and common area rehabilitation, UNHCR will enhance the safety, security, dignity and resilience of communities.
Speed and appropriateness of interventions, maximizing flexibility through rapid utilization of cash as a key modality for implementation, combined with ongoing response monitoring, will help ensure nimble and flexible interventions that are built on evolving needs. Community engagement will be at the centre of the response, ensuring effective participation. Beneficiary selections will also be based on the LRC emergency needs assessment in addition to UNHCR shelter partner assessments within allocated areas of intervention. According to CAS/ILO (LFHLC Survey), Beirut residents are comprised of about 7% children below 5 years of age, 13% between 5 and 14 years old, 15% between 15 and 24, 43% between 25 and 59, and 22% above 60 years old. In addition, 54% are female and 46% male. Age and gender considerations will thus guide the response, including with special considerations on the selection of beneficiaries and implementation modalities to address specific needs of particular groups. The response adopts a whole-population approach, targeting the most affected and most vulnerable households and individuals – including Lebanese, refugees and migrant workers – as well as their communities in the hardest hit and vulnerable areas affected by the blast. Do no harm, conflict-sensitivity and PSEA will be integrated throughout the response.

1 - Needs

The 4 August blast impacted an estimated 72,265 apartments in 9,700 buildings located within three kilometers of the epicenter. Following the explosion, UNHCR supported and worked closely with the LRC to assess needs and the situation on the ground; determining factors such as age, gender, diversity, and specific needs of affected individuals. UNHCR immediately mobilized its weatherproofing kit stock to support people remaining in level 1 (light) and level 2 (moderate) damaged buildings. Weatherproofing kits are vital in securing temporary protection against weather conditions in addition to providing a level of privacy, safety and security. UNHCR implementing partners in Beirut and Mount Lebanon, as well as partners from other regions, quickly mobilized resources to validate the need for and distribute such items. UNHCR also provided operational partners with kits for further distribution. Within the first two weeks, UNHCR via partners distributed 4,000 kits (assisting approximately 13,600 individuals) to affected Lebanese and other nationals through a vast network of male and female humanitarian workers, volunteers and members of the community. Particular attention is given to age, gender and diversity (AGD). Special arrangements have been put into place, examples of which are tailored installation support for people requiring assistance and gender-diverse teams involved in the assessment and distribution of items. The Shelter Sector also estimates that 29,771 apartments (89,313 individuals) fall within level 1 damages, 17,100 apartments (51,300 ind.) fall within level 2 and 1,144 apartments (30,660 ind.) fall within level 3. The explosion also affected communal areas, shared open space and minor infrastructure, further impacting community self-recovery capacity and requiring action from shelter actors. Shelters impacted by the blast and left without doors, locks, and windows decrease privacy and protection for affected persons, especially women and girls. Initial findings of the multi-sectoral needs assessment highlight the impact of the blast in elderly populations, a population group that could require particular support for repair and rehabilitation actions. The adaptation and accessibility of shelters is also crucial to address the needs of people living with disabilities prior to the explosion or as a result thereof. Among others, inadequate housing can impact individuals’ ability to attain quality health and education outcomes, impedes resumption of supportive family and community life, compromises security and livelihoods, as well as increase the risks of sexual- and gender-based violence.

2 - Activities

1. Emergency Weatherprooﬁng: Emergency weatherprooﬁng is distributed to HHs to respond to light and moderate damage. This assistance is considered short term and ensures temporary protection against weather conditions and a level of privacy, safety and security. Receipt of weatherprooﬁng assistance does not exclude HHs from receipt of other forms of shelter assistance. Target: 22,100 individuals. 2. Minor Shelter Repair & Rehabilitation (Phase 1): Minor Repairs & Rehabilitation (Phase 1) through ‘shelter cash’ provided to HHs to sustain rental payments (where displaced) and to contribute toward housing repairs. This is a non-conditional cash transfer. Receipt of shelter cash does not exclude HHs from receipt of other forms of shelter assistance. This shall be a one-time cash payment of $600 USD per HH/ residence. Target: 39,042. 3. Minor Shelter Repair (Phase 2): Minor Repairs (phase 2) will be identiﬁed through the LRC emergency needs assessment in addition to UNHCR shelter partner assessments within the allocated areas of intervention. Minor repairs will involve technical assessment, BoQ preparation and implementation through homeowners or landlords. Receipt of minor repair (phase 2) will exclude HHs from the receipt of rehabilitation assistance. Target: 14,171. 4. Shelter Rehabilitation (Phase 2): Rehabilitation will again be identiﬁed through the LRC emergency needs assessment in addition to UNHCR shelter partner assessments within their allocated areas of intervention. Rehabilitation will involve the permanent repair of all non-signiﬁcant structural damage and will involve BoQ preparation, HLP considerations, and implementation through contractors, homeowners or landlords. Target: 8,140. 5. Shelter Community support projects and common area rehabilitation: Community support projects and common area rehabilitation will involve the identiﬁcation, assessment and design of multisectoral approaches to improve common and communal areas, including CDCs, affected by the blast with a view to enhancing the safety, security, dignity and resilience of communities. Target: 20,000. Age, gender, diversity, and specific needs considerations will guide UNHCR’s entire programme cycle – including design, community engagement, beneficiary selection, and completion of works. UNHCR also places prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) at the centre of its work, requiring partners, contractors and cash-for-work staff involved in shelter interventions to abide by PSEA protocols. UNHCR will work closely with Protection, Shelter, Wash and other sectors to ensure complementary of the project, both bilaterally and through the coordination structures, such as on Housing, Land and Property. Environmental and other cross cutting issues will be incorporated into both the assessment and implementation phases, including environmentally sustainable practices and materials, and coordinating removal of debris from sites with the appropriate entity.
