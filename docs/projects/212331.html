Project Summary

The public health system in Syria has been degraded by over a decade of conflict and remains extremely vulnerable as a result of the displacement and migration of qualified professionals and the widespread destruction or damage to health facilities. UNRWA’s health services in Syria are facing growingincreasing pressures due to socio-economic crisis, with an increasing number of Palestine refugees now relying heavily on the Agency for health care. It is expected that at least 900,000 patient visits will be made to UNRWA health centres in 2024.
The Agency's 27 health facilities in Syria (24 health centres and health points located in Palestine refugee camps and three mobile clinics) provide a range of primary health care services, including out-patient consultations, immunization services, infant and child growth monitoring, dispensing of medications, gynaecology consultations, laboratory services, non-communicable diseases (NCD) care, MCH care, dental care, and mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services. In addition, the mobile clinics are used to reach areas most affected by the conflict and the February 2023 earthquakes where Agency health facilities have either been destroyed or are too damaged to use.
Access to secondary and tertiary health care for around 40,000 refugees will be provided through a network of partner public and private hospitals. Support for hospitalisation expenditure will cover between 75 and 95 per cent of the cost, depending on the assessment of treatment needs in contracted hospitals. This has become increasingly important for Palestine refugees to avoid them incurring significant debts because of critical health care needs.
Telemedicine was one of the innovations introduced during the COVID-19 pandemic that has grown and developed and was particularly useful in the aftermath of the earthquakes when people were unable to reach UNRWA’s health facilities. Two active phone lines will be supported under this EA for an estimated 9,600 refugees who will access the service in 2024. The phone number has been distributed in the Palestine refugee community for any refugee to use to obtain a remote emergency medical consultation.
Persons with disabilities will be supported through the provision of 4,000 physiotherapy sessions and 500 assistive devices, including hearing aids. while baby kits will be provided to 800 pregnant women for their newborn infants.
Under this appeal, UNRWA will conduct training for health staff to increase their preparedness for emergency situations, such as disease outbreaks, natural disasters and localised conflicts. Furthermore, as part of UNRWA’s commitment to AAP, training on community engagement and accountability will be organised for 150 UNRWA staff (including 50 health staff) and UNRWA volunteers from the refugee community. As part of its commitment to AAP, the Agency will familiarise health centre staff on AAP related principles and actions. This will include supporting patients to provide feedback, and act on same. A standardized CFRM will be strengthened for all SFO programmes, including health.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Protection risk: Exposure to Explosive Ordnance while accessing services. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation: 1. Training and awareness sessions plus distribution of UNMAS EORE materials to staff and community members targeting high-risk areas. 2. Mobile health clinics and health points established near the Palestine refugee communities, minimizing risky movement to access health services. Immunization campaigns limit unnecessary movement. 3. Telemedicine services are being provided which limit exposure. 4. UNRWA delivers regular medication for Non-Communicable Disease (NCD) Patients to their homes and can also provide them by delegation through relatives or friends.Risk: Safety, dignity and privacy are eroded for GBV survivors and vulnerable groups when attending health clinics due to overcrowding. Likelihood: Low. Impact: Medium. Mitigation: 1. UNRWA is using telemedicine to reduce the number of in-person consultations at the health clinics. 2. NCD patients receive long-term supplies of medication to reduce the number of visits to the clinics. 3. Appointment system set up at UNRWA clinics and the most vulnerable are prioritized. 4. Ensure private spaces are made available where possible. 5. Train staff on GBV and confidentiality. 6. E-NCD and E-MCH usage tracked and encouraged.PSEA Strategy: UNRWA’s peculiar modality of work through direct implementation and the consequent large footprint of approximately 4,000 mostly frontline staff in Syria heightens the risk of SEA. UNRWA Syria conducted a PSEA risk assessment in December 2023. SEA risks: 1. Decline in reported cases. 2. limited awareness of official reporting mechanisms by beneficiaries and frontline staff. 3.Limited community outreach. 4. Lapses in confidentiality in handling allegations. 5. Cultural challenges and stigma.Mitigation measures: 1. Country-level SOP on managing SEA allegations developed. 2. PSEA awareness posters (including child-friendly and “reporting misconduct” posters) displayed in all approximately 120 UNRWA facilities (schools, Health clinics, distribution centres, community centres, administrative offices etc) and quarterly checks are done to ensure availability. 3. Train staff on PSEA policy (almost 300 trained in-person in 2023). 4. Ramp up community outreach. 5. All personnel contracts include affirmation on PSEA. 6. UNRWA is part of the UN ClearCheck recruitment screening database. 7. Frontline staff are identifiable with UNRWA vests and IDs. 8. Complaints and feedback mechanisms established and set up at all UNRWA installations.
