Project Summary

In Somalia, Only 52% of the population has access to basic water supply, with limited regulation of private water supplies frequently resulting in high prices, forcing families to fetch water from distant and unsafe open wells. In conflict-affected areas and camps for displaced people, access to safe water is frequently hampered due to damaged water infrastructure, which increases the risk of disease and malnutrition among people living in overcrowded conditions. In March 2021 alone, over 70 boreholes were reported to have broken in Puntland and Galmudug regional states due to over-use, weak management, and high operation and maintenance costs, forcing IDPs and host communities to buy water at prohibitive costs. According to a UNICEF report published in 2020, sanitation and hygiene coverage in Somalia is low, with only 38% of the population having access to basic sanitation facilities and services, posing a high rate of open defecation that leads to contamination of water sources and increases the risk of disease outbreaks. Hand washing with soap at critical moments is practiced by only one-third of the population due to a lack of soap and access to a functional handwashing point Coupled by poor knowledge of good hygiene and sanitation practices. WASH in health care institutions has been a source of concern for many years. A significant proportion of Somalia's health facilities lack on-site water connections and rely on water trucks from long distances. This severely jeopardizes service delivery and undermines safe WASH practices, which are critical in controlling and preventing disease outbreaks. This proposed project will increase access to safe and clean water, promote sanitation and hygiene conditions, and contribute to a reduction in infant and under-5 mortality rates as well as WASH-related diseases in general. The project will address the needs of 28,000 vulnerable population in Somalia's Mudug, Nugaal, Sanaag, Galmudug, and Hiraan regions. This will be done through the rehabilitation of boreholes with solar installations, extension of pipelines and water tank construction for communities and health facilities to improve access and storage, provision of water vouchers, rehabilitation of shallow wells, and training of community structures (committees) to increase their capacity to manage and maintain rehabilitated water systems. In addition, the IRC will construct/rehabilitate sanitation facilities (latrines and solid waste management infrastructure) with a focus on IDPs, as well as ensure access to essential WASH services in health facilities to improve health and nutritional outcomes. In addition, IRC will promote hygiene and sanitation and distribute basic hygiene kits to households, including materials for menstrual management. (Applying market –based programming approach where feasible as a complement to in-kind distribution). The IRC will target communities in hard-to-reach areas with limited access to WASH services, as well as those in cholera/AWD hotspots and crowded IDP settlements, with particular focus on the COVID-19 situation, risk management, and infection prevention and control measures. The proposed response will be delivered through a strong protection lens, which will include efforts to ensure that latrines are gender separated, have lockable doors, adequate lighting, and that the distance to water points is kept within the agreed-upon standards. The IRC will adopt a universal design to ensure water and sanitation facilities are accessible to all, including persons with disabilities. The project will also consider the impact of conflict on women and children in terms of access to WASH services, as well as the unique needs of menstruating women and girls. The proposed actions will address and prevent gender-based violence risks, thereby improving and contributing to greater gender equality.

1. - Projects activities

Rehabilitation and upgrading of five boreholes through solar power installation, pipeline extensions, and construction of water kiosks ( target: 25,000 people ). Water management committees will be trained on the operation and maintenance of water sources and on cost–recovery mechanisms to maximize sustainability. ( target: 37 WMC members) 2.1: Pipeline connections and construction of institutional latrines, installation of potable water tanks and handwashing facilities for health facilities (5) 1.3: Household water treatment (aqua tabs and filters): The provision of HHWT (aqua tabs and filters) will target 3,500 households in target locations. The IRC will provide HHWT training to beneficiaries and WASH management committees. 1.4: Latrine HH Shared NEW: Construction of 150 latrines with hand wash facilities, shared among five most vulnerable IDP households ( target: 3,600 people ). 1.5: Sanitation tools: 160 sanitation kits (comprising of wheel barrow, shovel, and fork) will be distributed to target IDP Households and the rural communities ( target: 3,600 people ) 1.6: Hygiene Promotion: Conduct gender-sensitive hygiene promotion campaigns targeting 3,500 households. IRC will ensure that more than 40% of the community hygiene promoters are women. A pre- and post KAP will be conducted to determine the impact of the WASH interventions. 1.7: Hygiene kit distribution: Distribution of 3,500 hygiene kits (including sanitary materials) where possible, IRC will apply market–based programming (cash & voucher) with a focus on the most vulnerable households (female-headed and physically challenged). .1.8.
