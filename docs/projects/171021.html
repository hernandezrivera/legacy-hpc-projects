Project Summary

This project which is proposed to be implemented in 2021 by Action Against Hunger (AAH) and the Charitable Future Society (CFS) across the Gaza Strip aligns with Strategic Objectives 2 and 3 as well as the WASH Cluster Objectives 2 and 3.
On one hand, the project aims to support vulnerable families (including women, men, girls and boys, elderly and people with disability and specific needs) who depend on cesspits only to discharge their wastewater. Through connecting families of Al Fukhkhari, Maan, Alstar and Jawrat Alluwt communities to an adequate wastewater drainage system, the different interventions aim to protect individuals from being in contact with flooded wastewater during the winter season due to the accumulation of stormwater in the cesspits flooding in the streets and in hospitals and schools. This will further help prevent the risks of infections and diseases from flooded wastewater and other health-related issues. A public awareness campaign targeting newly connected households will spread messages on safe water handling, improving the capacity of families in targeted areas to address hygiene practice issues at household level. This awareness campaign will include information on COVID-19 mitigation measures, to contribute to the efforts to limit the spread of the pandemic in the occupied Palestinian territory (oPt).
On the other hand, the project will provide wastewater collection and discharge systems for targeted quarantine centers (QCs), hosting returnees to the Gaza Strip during their mandatory isolation period to mitigate the spread of COVID-19, avoiding municipalities to evacuate cesspits. Disinfection units will be added to prevent the virus to propagate through wastewater.
In addition, AAH will carry out a technical study to pilot new techniques to improve access to WASH services in the community water facilities, with support from the Water Quality Working Group (WQWG) and key actors in the WASH sector.
The proposed intervention is guided by AAH Gender and Protection policy and AAH staff endeavor to streamline safety and dignity in all humanitarian interventions. Accountability and participation will be ensured through interviews and focus group discussions and through applying AAH’s thorough Feedback and Complaint Mechanism.

1 - Needs

According to the Coastal Municipalities Water Utility (CMWU, WASH service provider), around 27% of the Gaza Strip population are not connected to municipal wastewater networks and discharge their sewage to poorly managed cesspits that have negative impacts on individuals and public health (drownings in cesspits, contamination of drinking water) and the environment (contamination of the aquifer). This figure results from a multi-layer combination of structural challenges born from the blockade imposed as part of the conflict between Israel and the oPt, major funding gaps to the provision of basic services and a low rate of municipal collection services fees as a result of financial restrictions by the Palestinian Authority on the Gaza Strip impeding the ability of service providers to implement new water and sanitation networks to connect households, as well as the lack of suitable material available due to Israeli import restrictions on equipment and materials designated as ‘dual-use items’. COVID-19 exacerbated poor hygiene conditions related health risks and emphasized the need to connect households to adequate sewage systems.In September 2020, AAH supported the WASH cluster in assessments related to WASH service provision in COVID-19 QCs, the impact of COVID-19 on municipalities’ services, and the impact of power shortage on WASH facilities and services. AAH also contributed to the mapping of vulnerable communities by the WASH cluster (September 2020).AAH in coordination with the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD, managing the QCs) highlighted the need to provide QCs with safe wastewater discharging systems. Further, AAH and CMWU identified the most urgent geographical areas in need of wastewater drainage systems. Al Fukhkhari area, adjacent to the European Gaza Hospital (EGH) designated as COVID-19 quarantine and treatment center, and three neighborhoods (Maan, Alsatar and Jawrat Alluwt) in Khan Yunis city were qualified as vulnerable, particularly after the completion of the regional Wastewater Treatment Plant East of Khan Yunis governorate that became operational in April 2020. The findings of these assessments included:• 3 out of the 7 operating quarantine centers (Erez quarantine center-Beit Hanoun, Ehsan Alagh School-Al Fukhari and Tal al Sultan quarantine center-Rafah) are not connected to municipal wastewater networks and discharge the wastewater into cesspits without pre-disinfection, negatively impacting on the environment including the aquifer.• 5,011 households (10,131 women, 12,513 men, 2,710 boys, 2,194 girls including 796 PWDs), living in the said four areas prone to stormwater floods and mix with wastewater from cesspits, depend totally on poorly managed cesspits due to the lack of municipal wastewater networks.• Cesspits are evacuated by the relevant municipalities every two weeks only, costing around 20% of poorest households’ monthly income (amount paid by the households according to the vulnerability mapping and the monthly income of households in deep poverty (<$4.9/HH/day according to OCHA/PCBS).Furthermore, according to AAH’s COVID-19 rapid situation monitoring (September 2020), among 5,011 households monitored:• 54% suffer from water borne diseases.• 61% think that their living conditions is not appropriate to face COVID-19, due to overcrowding, humidity, poor ventilation, absence of tiling and inadequate sanitation facilities.AAH thus intends to support vulnerable communities of the four targeted high priority areas to maintain basic WASH services and protect them from health hazards and wastewater floods by implementing wastewater collection and discharge systems. Around 27,548 individuals will consequently have safe access to sanitation services, releasing the financial burden of evacuating their cesspits, and allowing students (approximately 8,376 boys and 7,563 girls) to reach their schools safely as there will no longer be stormwater mixing with wastewater on the streets.

2 - Activities

The activities are proposed to ensure safe access to sanitation services and protect women, men, girls, boys and people with disability from natural and man-made hazards, prioritizing safety and dignity and taking into account gender, age and diversity:Activity 1: Installation/rehabilitation of WASH facilities in quarantine centers (sewer system)AAH in cooperation with CMWU, related municipalities and MoSD will implement wastewater drainage systems with disinfection units to prevent the spread of virus in the wastewater, in 2 quarantine centers (Erez quarantine center-Beit Hanoun and Tal al Sultan quarantine center-Rafah), to prevent wastewater floods and contamination to the aquifer from the cesspits sewage. This will enable 4,500 people crossing back to Gaza (2,621 men, 1,344 women, 277 boys and 258 girls) hosted in the quarantine centers to use sanitation services safely during their quarantine period, achieving equal and impartial access to those services to all. In addition, it will reduce the burden on the municipalities for the evacuation of cesspits and protect their operators from being at risk of contact with wastewater. Activity 2: Installation/extension of sewer collection network for households in unconnected areasAAH in cooperation with CMWU and related municipalities will construct wastewater networks to drain wastewater collected from the unconnected communities (Al Fukhari area including the connection of Ehsan Alagh School-as QC beside EGH, Maan, Alsatar and Jawrat alluwt in Khan Yunis city) to the Khan Yunis regional Wastewater Treatment Plant. 5,011 households will be connected to the networks to have a more dignified access to sanitation facilities. This will reduce the negative impact that wastewater has on 27,548 individuals (10,131 women, 12,513 men, 2,710 boys, 2,194 girls including 796 PWDs), and reduce the pollution on the environment and aquifer. Activity 3: Provision of awareness sessions and hygiene promotion materials, to enhance safe hygiene and water management practices in line with the promotion of the right to water and the right to healthAAH with support from CFS (a well-known organization based in Khan Yunis governorate) will conduct 135 hygiene awareness participatory sessions (with 20 people in each) with 2,706 households (54% of households who said they suffered from water borne diseases) benefiting from a new connection to wastewater drainage systems (Activity 2), representing 14,876 people (5,471 women, 6,757 men, 1,463 boys and 1,185 girls), taking in consideration the WHO instructions of physical distancing and wearing protective equipment to prevent the spread of COVID-19. The sessions will be developed based on FGDs and interviews with households considering the needs of vulnerable groups and members including PWDs. Household representatives will learn about safe water handling and storage, good hygiene practices including specific COVID-19 prevention measures, and safe drainage of wastewater. These workshops will encourage women, men and children to adopt adequate hygiene behaviours and disinfection practices in order to reduce the transmission of COVID-19 and help control the outbreak. AAH will conduct public media campaigns through developing and delivering Information Education Communication (IEC) materials which will include hygiene promotion messages covered by the awareness sessions, using IEC material produced by the Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE) based on FGDs with men, women, boys and girls in order to promote healthy behavioural changes including basic protective measures against COVID-19. IEC materials will be developed based on FGDs and interviews with households considering the inclusion of vulnerable groups and members like women, girls and PWDs. In addition, the main message of the IEC materials will cover gender differences without any discrimination. IEC material contents will be agreed upon with male and female community representatives during FGDs and will include gender sensitive language considering the differences in gender roles. The materials will be distributed by CFS during the hygiene awareness sessions. The campaign and materials are also going to be used to celebrate World Water Day (22nd March) and any other related events and opportunities that arise along the year. Meetings with relevant stakeholders and development of materials will be organised in order to promote the right for the population in the Gaza Strip to access water and basic services.Activity 4: Piloting new techniques to improve access to WASH servicesAs suggested by the WASH cluster and PWA, AAH with support from the local academic and research institutions will conduct a technical study and provide solutions to improve access to WASH services in the community water facilities, to be piloted and monitored. Topics of focus for the study (addressing issues such as reusing produced brine water from desalination plants and producing chlorine from brine water for water disinfection) will be agreed upon with the main water actors and members of the WASH cluster and WQWG.

3 - Indicators

The following are the expected results and indicators as well as tools for measuring progress and impact:Under Activity 1, 4,500 people will benefit from improved access to WASH services (2,621 men, 1,344 women, 277 boys and 258 girls). The exact number of beneficiaries will be obtained from MoSD records of hosted people in the QCs. On-site monitoring visits and Interviews with quarantine centers’ managers and CMWU staff will be carried out during the implementation and after installation and rehabilitation work by the MEAL team to collect feedback from interviewees about the project progress and achievements as well as monitor project’s assumptions and possible protection threats or unintended negative results on communities and targeted beneficiaries.Under Activity 2, 27,548 people will benefit from improved access to sanitation, solid waste, and hygiene services (10,131 women, 12,513 men, 2,710 boys, 2,194 girls). Exact number of households and individuals will be obtained from field records (list of households connected to the new wastewater network) that will be validated by the CMWU and related municipalities. KAP baseline surveys will target representative samples of the targeted households in Al Fukhari area to measure status of their suffering due to wastewater flooding and waterborne diseases. Following these surveys, an end line survey to measure outcomes and progress towards safe and dignified access to sanitation facilities and improvement in living conditions will be conducted. Additionally, monitoring visits to households will be conducted to further assess impact and obtain beneficiaries feedback including (men, women, and children) on their experience with wastewater flooding and consequences on their living conditions.Under Activity 3, 2,706 representatives of households newly connected to the wastewater networks will participate in hygiene promotion awareness campaigns. More widely, their household will benefit from improved access to sanitation, solid waste, and hygiene services, representing 14,876 people (5471 women, 6757 men, 1463 boys and 1185 girls). The number of attendees to the hygiene promotion sessions will be obtained from attendance sheets. Throughout the implementation of the activities, beneficiaries will be a part of the KAP surveys that will be carried out before and after interventions (baseline/end line). A structured questionnaire will be used to compare their improvements in knowledge, attitudes and practices in hygiene and if possible, to measure our contribution on improvement on children health as well. In addition, onsite monitoring visits will be conducted during hygiene promotion activities, as a quality control mechanism and to obtain beneficiaries’ feedback during the activities for service improvements and to document learning.Under Activity 4, 4,500 people will have improved WASH capacity during emergencies and shocks through piloting new techniques to improve WASH services. The exact number of beneficiaries will be obtained from PWA and CMWU records.To further ensure protection is mainstreamed throughout the project implementation, the indicator “% of project beneficiaries (male/female) reporting that humanitarian assistance is delivered in a safe and dignified manner” will be measured.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

The Feedback and Complaint Mechanism (FCM) is managed by the MEAL unit in AAH. Following the FCM standard operating procedures it will be introduced to the targeted communities through information materials like brochures and information leaflets inside the communities where WASH interventions are taking place, to provide communities and beneficiaries with different channels to access AAH, prior, during and after implementation. The FCM will be active from the beginning of the project through an active hotline number managed by MEAL staff. Complaints, feedback or suggestions are recorded on a protected sheet, and MEAL staff uses the agreed FCM procedures to handle the complaint based on its level and sensitivity. AAH management iand the program team jointly discuss the complaint (depending on its nature and sensitivity) and formulate responses on a case by case basis. On average, within 1-2 weeks the response usually is provided to the complainant or community members. If the response includes actions these will be considered by the program team, they will fill in the learning log sheet with actions planned to allow MEAL team to follow up the execution of these actions. On a quarterly basis, a FCM report will be developed and shared with AAH management and program team to document learning captured from FCM findings, suggestions and feedback

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The proposed interventions prioritise providing safe access for all targeted women, men, boys and girls and ensure their privacy and dignity to use sanitation services.Also, the wastewater networks and disinfection units were proposed to protect users and the environment by preventing wastewater floods from cesspits that have negative impacts on health and the aquifer. These will further prevent drownings of children in cesspits flooding, and water borne diseases that are prominent in the Gaza Strip. These will also improve households’ hygiene conditions and reduce the burden on women particularly, who are mainly responsible for the cleaning tasks of the house. Furthermore, the monitoring and evaluation framework includes specific assumptions’ monitoring through which un-intended negative impact or conflict that might arise as a result of the activities will be communicated on timely basis to AAH management and program team, in order to prevent doing harm, and will be addressed accordingly. According to AAH feedback and complaint mechanism SoPs, feedback and complaints from beneficiaries and targeted communities will be treated confidentially, and feedback to the project to adjust implementation when relevant. Complaints related to sexual abuse and exploitation will also be addressed anonymously, in accordance with AAH code of conducts, and following specific steps in order to take actions immediately and to refer the complainant to relevant services.AAH has gender focal points at capital and base levels and a gender and protection manager providing advice and participating in relevant forums and trainings in the country.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

According to the United Nations General Assembly / Human Rights Council, “The human right to water entitles everyone without discrimination to sufficient, safe, acceptable, physically accessible and affordable water for personal and domestic use. The human right to sanitation entitles everyone without discrimination to physical and affordable access to sanitation, in all spheres of life, which is safe, hygienic, secure, socially and culturally acceptable, which provides for privacy and ensures dignity.” The interventions of this project respond to the human rights to water and to sanitation as they are planned to improve safe and dignified access to WASH services for all targeted women, men, boys and girls. The installation of wastewater drainage systems in the QCs ensures protection against possible public health risks, particularly in light of COVID-19, to all improved facilities’ users without exclusion. Hygiene and awareness sessions will be developed based on Focus Group Discussions and interviews with households, including all household members and particularly vulnerable individuals, taking into account the needs of women, men, girls, boys, elderly and PWDs, making sure their needs are included and their rights are recognised.AAH feedback and complaint mechanism will be in place from the onset of the project to ensure that all targeted individuals can reach different channels to report suggestions and complaints, ensuring that the voice of all is heard to adjust project implementation and tailor it as much as feasible.
