Project Summary

South Sudan’s protracted conflict remains the largest contributing factor to internal displacement and exodus of refugees into neighbouring countries (Regional RRP, Jan 2019 - Dec 2020). 2019 marks the sixth year of the conflict. With 2.2 million people living in poor conditions in exile, South Sudan’s refugee crisis remains the largest in Africa. Approximately 80 per cent of the refugee population are comprised of women and children and more than 50,000 refugee children remain unaccompanied or separated. Within South Sudan, 85 per cent of the 1.97 million internally displaced persons (IDPs) are women and children. Organizational partners are struggling to provide quality and timely assistance to survivors of sexual and gender-based violence
The recently revitalized peace process appears to offer new opportunities in 2020 for South Sudan’s women, men and children.Since the signature of the Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in the Republic of South Sudan on 11 September 2018, the permanent ceasefire is being upheld in most parts of the country and there is a marked reduction in violence. Nevertheless, conflict remains and serious human rights violations continue to be perpetrated by parties to the conflict with near complete impunity.
IPC trends have continually indicated high number of people facing hunger and malnutrition i.e. IPC3,4&5). IPC September – December 2019 harvest period revealed that 4.54 million (39%) people severely food insecure (IPC Phase 3 or worse) nationwide. This figure is projected to increase to 5.5 million (47%) people severely food insecure (IPC Phase 3 or worse) during January – April 2020 post-harvest period. Magwiand Torit Counties are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase3) according to Sept-Dec 2019 projection and IPC phase 4&3 Aug 2019 and Jan-April 2020.
Base Net is a national non-profit humanitarian and development NGO registered on 29th January 2018 in Juba by national RRC with registration number 1287; committed to working with others to finding sustainable solutions to hunger, vulnerability and poverty in South Sudan. Our core mandates include Food and Nutrition Security, Livelihoods Enhancement and Sustainable Environment Management. We also have Education;Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH); Shelters and Resettlements. Weintegrate cross-cutting issues such as Gender Equality, Gender-Based Violence, Protection, and Persons with Disability/Special Needs; upholds accountability to the affected people and promotes social cohesion and diversity.Base Net’s presence, expertisein Magwi Countyenhanced food production and reached at least 546 people, 60% women and girls and 40% returnees. It is well placed in implementing the refugee response with its deep knowledge of local context, field presence and operations in Magwi and Torit counties. Base Net will align its response with the refugee response objectives (CO) 1-3 and linking it to HRP 2019 SO1-SO3. Base Net is an operational FSLC active member at national in Juba, at sub-state in Torit and at sub-county in Nimule. It also participates in Protection, WASH, Nutrition, Shelters/NFIs and Education.
The refugee response had reached almost 100 per cent of the nearly 338,000 refugees residing and targeted in South Sudan in 2019. The 2020 response will continue to support at least 338,000 refugee women, men and children (82% women and children). Base Net’s proposed budget of 80000 US$ will support 5000 people in Magwi and Torit Counties in 2020. Response activities will includes registration, verification and documentation of returns, lifesaving activities and core protection interventions, GBV, cropping, food and nutrition security, shelter improvement and education activities. The requirements of persons of specific needs will be mainstreamed throughout the response.

1 - Needs

Despite the signing of the R-ARCSS, the humanitarian situation in South Sudan remains grave. By the end of 2018, nearly 1.87 million South Sudanese were internally displaced and 5.7 million people were in need of life-saving assistance. Of the spontaneous refugee returnees to South Sudan who have been monitored by humanitarian actors, 85 per cent report to be living in IDP-like situations. They are unable to return to their places of origin or recover the property or land they left behind. The conflict and associated economic decline have eroded the Government’s ability to provide consistent basic services to its people. Currently, one primary health centre serves an average of 50,000 people. Only 40 per cent of nutrition treatment centres have access to safe water, a gap that puts more vulnerable people, particularly women and children, at risk of malnutrition and disease. Only about one in five childbirths involves a skilled health care worker and the maternal mortality ratio is estimated at 789 per 100,000 live births. Every third school has been damaged, destroyed, occupied or closed since 2013, and more than 70 per cent of children who should be attending classes are not receiving an education. Years of conflict, displacement and underdevelopment have limited people’s livelihood opportunities,marginalized women’s formal employment opportunities, and weakened families’ ability to cope with the protracted crisis and sudden shocks, like the death of a wage earner or loss of cattle. Over 80 per cent of the population lives below the absolute poverty line. The food security situation continues to deteriorate due to conflict-driven displacement, low crop production, economic crisis, climatic shocks and humanitarian access challenges. IPC September – December 2019 harvest period revealed that 4.54 million (39%) people severely food insecure and malnourished (IPC Phase 3 or worse) nationwide. This figure is projected to increase to 5.5 million (47%) people severely food insecure (IPC Phase 3 or worse) during January – April 2020 post-harvest period. An estimated 560,000 people (52.6%) in EES were facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity in August 2019. Magwiand Torit Countiesare classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) in Sept-Dec 2019 and Jan-April 2020.The delivery of humanitarian assistance is often impeded due to violence against humanitarian workers, which continues unabated. In 2018, for the third consecutive year, South Sudan remained the most dangerous place in the world in which to provide humanitarian assistance. Despite more than 5000 spontaneous returns in Magwiand Torit Counties(RRC offices 2019), the security, rule of law and human rights situation that prevails today in South Sudan also stands in the way of safe and dignified return for any person originating from South Sudan, whether or not the individual is found to be in need of international protection. Accordingly, while UNHCR recognizes that political changes are underway in South Sudan, the impact of these changes on the ground are not yet such as to make return under conditions of safety and dignity feasible. UNHCR reaffirms its April 2015 position on return to South Sudan – Update I, recommending member States to suspend forcible returns of nationals or habitual residents of South Sudan to the country. Recent community consultative meeting at Nimule county office (Aug 2019) prioritised lifesaving and food security kits, WASH, Prision rehabilitation and women centre construction for all host, return and IDP people. The returnees are in need of lifesaving assistance, access to critical integrated services and recovery & resilience services. 2 - Activities

Base Net is well placed in implementing the refugee response in Magwi and Torit counties given its deep knowledge of local context, field presence and operations.Base Net will align its response with the refugee response objectives (CO) 1-3 and relating it to HRP 2019 SO1-SO3. Refugee Response Plan or Cluster Objectives (CO) are related to HRP Strategic Objectives (SO) as follows:• Objective 1 CO1 - Provide refugees and asylum-seekers and the most vulnerable people among them with adequate access to safety, life-saving assistance and basic services. This objective relates to SO1: Physical and mental wellbeing: reducing morbidity, mortality & suffering• Objective 2 CO2 - Ensure that refugees and asylum-seekers have access to effective protection. This objective relates to SO2: Living standards: access to critical integrated services• Objective 3 CO3 - Enhance and expand extremely vulnerable refugees’ and host communities’ resilience and coping capacities. This objective relates to SO3: Recovery & resilience - recover from crisis & resilience to acute shocks & chronic stress.Currently Base Net organization conducts refugee information sharing and monitoring with chiefs, RRC and county authorities in Nimule and Torit. The organization had also provided cropping kits to beneficiaries in Magwi County in 2018 – 2019 of which 40% have been spontaneous returnees from Uganda. The refugee response will highly be integrated to support refugees, returns, internally displaced and host communities with a diverse set of humanitarian support across food and livelihoods, nutrition, health, shelter, WASH and education sectors. Host communities and refugees share basic services such as education, water and health, and integrated support to host communities remains a key priority in 2020. Promotion of peaceful coexistence will be integrated into various sectorial interventions. The refugee response will conduct activities such as child protection; registration and documentation; prevention and response to sexual and gender-based violence; assistance and protection to other affected populations; and enhance social cohesion. These activities will collectively enhance legal and physical protection: Base Net and partners will continue to improve the quality of services provided to refugees to ensure compliance with international standards, including on shelter, health, food, education and WASH. The protection environment will be enhanced through the engagement of refugee, IDP and host communities with joint activities, such as peace talks, agricultural and forestry activities, sports activities, musical/dancing and cultural events. The refugee response will progressively expand and systematize the use of to provide greater dignity of choice to refugees and IDPs, and improve efficiency and effectiveness in the realization of protection and solutions. The response will adopt a sustainable and environmentally sensitive approach and promote self-reliance and resilience of refugees, IDPs and host communities through livelihood and empowerment programmes. The project will also contribute to Regional Response Strategy and Priorities in:• Anchoring the response within national and regional multi-year protection frameworks, policies, laws, and standards which comprehensively address legal and physical protection needs of South Sudanese refugees with a particular emphasis on children, women and youth;• Promoting social cohesion between refugees and host communities through implementation of targeted resilience programmes. Despite some spontaneous returnees coming in Magwiand Torit Counties (RRC Offices 2019), the security, rule of law and human rights situation that prevails today in South Sudan also stands in the way of safe and dignified return for any person originating from South Sudan, whether or not the individual is found to be in need of international protection.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes, beneficiaries' rights will be legally recognised based on humanitarian principles and humanitarian imperatives. The Humanitarian Charter expresses our shared conviction as humanitarian agency that all people affected by disaster or conflict have a right to receive protection and assistance to ensure the basic conditions for life with dignity. These principles are reflected in international law, but derive their force ultimately from the fundamental moral principle of humanity: that all human beings are born free and equal in dignity and rights. Based on this principle, we affirm the primacy of the humanitarian imperative: that action should be taken to prevent or alleviate human suffering arising out of disaster or conflict, and that nothing should override this principle (Ref: The Sphere Handbook 2018).

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Displacement-affected communities are priority beneficiaries and their access/benefit from the project cannot be overstressed. Usually the organization meet local authority as entry point to be able to discuss issues with communities. Community leaders and groupings such as the women group, youth group, elders, persons with disability / special needs, farmers groups, cooperative association are consulted on issues affecting their communities. In June and July, Base Net did the due diligence of conducting Gender-based food security and livelihood protection situation and community consultative meetings with key stakeholders in Nimule Payam and Torit. The outcome of these assessments and other empirical evidence shape the planning of this project.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The Project is certainly linked to the government and/or community systems. This is because Base Net organization is filling gaps of services for which local government / community leaders are supposed to do for their communities / people. But because authorities are overwhelmed by complex problems they cannot solve the problems alone. They need multi-stakeholders to be able to solve existing problems. Base Net operate in coordination with the local authorities and key stakeholders. Therefore, through these forums, the effectiveness and efficiency of the project is assessed in a transparent manner.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Base Net is aware of the man-made and natural negative impact of climate change on the environment. Over the recent past till to date, there has been indiscriminate poaching of our natural resources especially forest which has led to severe degradation of the ecosystems. The latter have provided communities especially in the rural areas with vital goods and services for enhanced livelihoods. Agricultural activities has also contributed to the environmental degradation. However, by incorporating training aspects on Good Agricultural Practices, farmers will be able to conserve rather than deplete and destroy the environment. Base Net will also link this project to its existing endeavor on agro-forestry which also included the formation of Schools Environment Clubs through whom skills and knowledge are imparted to school children and Youth; considered as change agents.Continued multi-sectoral support to all affected population in the country by all stakeholders including the duty bearer authorities will ensure the environment is sufficiently safe and stable for local people to do their businesses for improved livelihood and Healthy living.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes. The project will include sustainability/handover. Before the project starts, community leaders will be actively informed, involved and engaged in the needs assessments, designs and the way how the project will be implemented. This is done through convening community consultative meetings and inception workshop. Community leaders and/or participants will be able to share their views which eventually can be incorporated into the implementation and monitoring of the project. The organization will organize community consultative meeting and workshop to have their views on how the project has been run - showing progress, success, challenges and lessons learnt.

Furthermore Base Net will uphold the Four Protection Principles that apply to all humanitarian action and all humanitarian actors as follows:1. Enhance the safety, dignity and rights of people, and avoid exposing them to harm.2. Ensure people’s access to assistance according to need and without discrimination.3. Assist people to recover from the physical and psychological effects of threatened or actual violence, coercion or deliberate deprivation. 4. Help people claim their rights.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Accountability to Affected People is defined as “an active commitment by humanitarian actors to use power responsibly by taking account of, giving account to, and being held to account by the people they seek to assist”, FAO 16th Oct 2019 FSLC presentation. Base Net organization project ensures feedback from the most vulnerable community members through initiation and creation of groups of people living with disability or persons with special needs. The group members will have representatives to be able to present their view points to feedback and complaint desks. We do uphold the Seven Accountability Commitments shared by FAO: 1. Leadership and governance and Staff Competencies 2. Transparency, two-way communication, and information provision for affected communities 3. Feedback and Complaints: Offer means for communities to provide feedback 4. Representation and participation 5. Design, monitoring, and evaluation of activities 6. Prevent sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) by Base Net personnel and implementing partners 7. Collaborate with peers and partners to deliver on AAP commitments in a coordinated and coherent way. During implementation of emergency WASH response to affected people / beneficiaries, Base Net will also observe seven values adopted from FAO acronymized PANTHER: Participation, Accountability, Non Discrimination, Transparency, Human dignity, Empowerment and Rule of Law.
