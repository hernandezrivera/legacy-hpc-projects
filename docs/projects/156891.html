Project Summary

The primary aim of this project is to increase access of affected population in Syria to dialysis treatment and medications in Idleb governorate. The project’s objective will be attained through securing both the procuring dialysis kits, medications and consumables and the covering of salaries and operational cost for the Al Ma’ra National Hospital and Taramla Dialysis Unit both in Idleb governorate
The project strongly draws from the key inputs of the planned Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO 2019) since it works within the framework of the Whole of Syria approach to allow access and referral to specialist health services to those affected. This includes dialysis, and cancer and thalassemia treatment, as well as trauma care and rehabilitation. At least 15 percent of the population of Syria is disabled. Forty-five percent of people injured by the conflict in Syria is likely to face a permanent impairment and require specialist support which will far outlast the hostilities

Needs

Non-communicable diseases remain among the most prevalent health issues in Syria. WHO estimates that approximately 12 percent of the population has diabetes and 20 percent have hypertension. Estimates for the prevalence of chronic respiratory diseases and cancers together is around 6% while reliable data on the prevalence of thyroid disease, thalassemia, and other less common NCDs is not available. Untreated non-communicable diseases lead to serious complications such as renal failure and disability due to stroke and amputations. In northwest Syria, a total of 837 renal failure patients are currently receiving dialysis services through 20 facilities (6,575 sessions on a monthly basis). Although the availability of such services is limited, it is essential that this treatment can continue. SAMS IMU team developed a needs assessment tool to collect data from health facilities that SAMS is supporting or planning to support. For the proposed Dialysis Units in Al Ma’ra National Hospital and Taramla Dialysis Unit, SAMS MEAL officers visited both facilities and conducted a series of meetings with the hospitals' focal points to conduct a questionnaire. The questionnaire covered topics included general information about the area/facility, average figures of services provided monthly including the number of dialysis-related cases. The assessment reflected a dire need for dialysis kits and medication in addition to a sustained source of funding that allows such sensitive services to continue without interruptions. Expanding and improving the access to specialty health care services as in kidney care and dialysis is one of the crucial elements of SAMS strategy to provide lifesaving medical care to Syrians. Since the beginning of the conflict, SAMS has focused on supporting medical facilities and physicians in Syria governorates. Initially, the focus was on trauma, field hospitals, and conflict-related injuries. However, as the conflict evolved, SAMS has diversified its programming to address health care concerns indirectly related to the conflict due to the weakened health care infrastructure in Syria. SAMS is currently supporting mobile clinics, primary health care systems such as OBGYN and reproductive health centers, dental clinics, various chronic diseases centers such as dialysis, as well as psycho-social centers in Northwest Syria. Pertaining to the scope of this project, SAMS is contributing to supporting the proposed facilities with staff salaries and operational cost. In addition, reflecting programs complementarity and as mentioned previously, SAMS is integrating nutritional services into all of its RH facilities through different grants. For instance, the following services are being provided in some of the proposed facilities: systematic screening and treatment of malnutrition for both inpatients and outpatients; IYCF counseling and raising awareness; Multiple micronutrient supplements of women and children.

Implementation of activities

Implementing the project activities will take place in Al Ma’ra National Hospital and Taramla Hospital in Rural Idleb and will include:- Procure renal dialysis kits, distribute the renal dialysis kits to renal dialysis department in Tarmala hospital and Marrett Alnoaman National hospital;- Identify and maintain the availability of qualified staff to run the services;- Provide the technical supervision through SAMS Renal Technical Committee from the USA;- Provide training and capacity building to the service providers in Syria;- Provide the medication to renal transplantation patients;- Provide renal dialysis sessions to the patients in the two hospitals - Organizing Staff Care and Self Care Activities

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Sexual Exploitation and abuse by Facilities staff toward beneficiaries. Possibility: high. Effect: high. Mitigation measures: 1. Set PSEA Policy and effectively implement the policy in Hiring the staff and oriented them on the policy, training them, and make sure every staff signs the policy, 2. Effective complaint mechanism in place, 3. Awareness raising for beneficiaries on PSEA, 4. Clear complaints channels for beneficiaries and distribute brochures and leaflets in all places in the facilities (waiting rooms, entrance, Etc.). Risk: Sexual and Gender-Based Violence in the facilities between beneficiaries. Possibility: high. Effect: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Put cameras in the entrance and every floor of the facilities and corridors, 2. Use or establish separated toilets for men and women, 3. Establish spacious waiting rooms to host a huge number of the patient. 4. Put keys for the empty rooms if any. Risk: Facilities are inaccessible for the disabled patient and elderly persons. Possibility: moderate. Effect: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Build a ramp for disabled people at the facility entrance, 2. Make sure of the availability of logistics supplies needed for elderly persons and people with disabilities. Risk: impure or spoiled medications and supplies that could be harmful to beneficiaries. Possibility: moderate. Effect: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Procure medication with longtime validity (taking into consideration the transportation time for these medications and deliver it to the facilities than to beneficiaries to be used), 2. Procure the medications from reliable sources, 3. ensure cold-chain transportation for drugs requiring low temperatures, 4. Regular monitoring of the storage conditions of the medications and the validity date

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Stopping supporting the project and targeted population will still in need for the life-saving and life-sustaining healthcare services.Mitigation: SAMS will discuss with the donor to extend the support and in case of an extension was not reached proposals will be sent to other donors before the end of the support. in the meanwhile, SAMS will support the facility from its private fund. Risk: Logistic difficulties and roads outage which may result in of the schedule of the medication and medical supplies.Mitigation: procurement of medication is carried out as soon as the agreement is reached and purchased medications will be stored in SAMS's affiliated warehouse that can store enough amount till the logistic difficulties are solved. -Security danger which threatens medical and working staff/ Successive targeting of medical facilitiesMitigation: SAMS has an active security team inside Syria and Turkish office who daily monitor the security threats in the area and issues reports to take actions for each incident.
