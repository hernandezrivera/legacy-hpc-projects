Project Summary

After 12 years of conﬂict and persistent economic crisis, people in Syria saw their situation worsen when a series of earthquakes hit northern Syria and Türkiye on 6 and 20 February 2023, which has resulted in severely damaged infrastructures already weakened by the conflict.
The erosion of basic service capacity has continued, with water and sanitation systems, and public health services under immense strain, in a context of barely any development investment. Water supply is a major challenge in Syrian cities due to damaged, broken and underperforming water supply infrastructure, and climate change.
UN-HABITAT has been supporting municipalities and local communities to develop area-based recovery plans through a participatory process. Recovery plans identify the prioritized actions to improve the living condition of vulnerable populations in the target locations. Based on those area-based recovery plans, the proposed project will address the urgent needs of urban communities for WASH services.
As public WASH systems are the most economically viable and equitable way to provide services at scale, UN-Habitat will also support enhanced capacity for WASH related public service delivery including securing necessary energy supply through alternative power system servicing water pumping stations.
The interventions will also contribute to reducing the risk of Leishmaniasis/Cholera outbreak.
The proposed activities of the project for 2024 will serve the Sector Objective 4 "Support and restore water, sanitation and solid waste management systems to ensure continuity of services for most vulnerable affected people in Syria [related to strategic objective 3], and will include:
- Repair and rehabilitation of water networks.
- Restore functioning water supply system by introducing alternative energy sources.
- Repair, rehabilitation of sewage networks.
- Solid waste collection, transport and disposal
In 2023, UN-Habitat has secured 1.2 million USD for similar activities in Rural Damascus Governorate namely in Arbin and Nashabiyeh cities. The activities of sewage rehabilitation, sewage maintenance, water network rehabilitation, water boreholes rehabilitation, solid waste bins, were being implemented in both cities. In addition, UN-Habitat has an ongoing project to implemented WASH activities in Aleppo and Jableh cities, in which rehabilitation of sewage and water networks, support solid waste management, and rehabilitation of water elevated tank in Jableh are planned to be implemented during 2024 and 2025 with a total budget of 3.6 Million USD.
Following the criteria in HRP, the selection of targeted locations will be based on the following criteria:
• Being in line with WASH Sectors and HRP 2024 strategic priorities and objectives, as well as severity of needs
• Being priority interventions and locations identiﬁed by area based recovery plan
• Complementarity with ongoing projects already through UN-HABITAT resources and donor commitments including UN-Habitat projects under HRP Shelter and ERL sectors
• High numbers of people in need, vulnerable and expected returnees in target locations.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

UN-Habitat has already secured fund for similar activities in Aleppo, Homs, and Lattakia (total of 3.6 million, and targeting 430,750 beneficiaries), and this project aims to complement the ongoing investments to improve WASH service in target locations and aim to reach 469,750 beneficiaries distributed as follows:- In Rural Damascus: 20,000 in Nashabyeh, 50,000 in Arbin, and 8,000 in Maliha- In Daraa: 10,000 in Daraa, and 5,000 in Izraa- In Lattakia: 25,000 in Jablah.- in Aleppo: 350,000 in Aleppo city- in Homs: 1,750 in Homs cityThe WASH Sector base target in the above locations is (330,849)Implementation of activities will adopt the following sequence:- Update the local area-based recovery plans identifying the priorities and areas of intervention through a participatory approach aimed to target speciﬁc geographical areas and vulnerable communities, through an integrated and comprehensive response. - Secure the authorities approval - Conduct detailed assessments for physical activities to update the action plan reflecting current and immediate needs. - Tendering process, awarding, and contracting - Implementation - Completion of work and handover to the relevant stakeholders. Through the project implementation, and in coordination with Municipalities and local authorities, the local community will have a signiﬁcant supervisory role in terms of projects’ selection and implementation. In addition to regular reporting to the FTS, all activities and indicators will be reported to the Sector through regular 4W mechanisms. UN-HABITAT has developed a monitoring mechanism including site visits reports, progress reports, inspection tools and ﬁnancial tracking tools. All these tools and templates will be utilized during the implementation to enhance the monitoring process. The Municipalities will have a key role in monitoring the implementation through the technical committees that shall be formed to supervise the work and issue the oﬃcial handover, also the implementing partners are required to provide regular reports showing the progress on the ground.
