Project Summary

The purpose of this response is to improve the nutritional status of the crisis affected population of the Northwest and Southwest regions through an inclusive nutrition response. The project intervention will be carried out using mobile (hard-to-reach areas and populations) and fixed teams approaches. Project will start with training of project team- management staff, field staff, CHWs and community volunteers on the project deliverables, humanitarian principles, PSEA, AAP and disability inclusion. Outreach activities will take place from door-to-door, community halls etc. Each outreach session will begin with hygiene promotion and Social behavior change communication (SBC). Children diagnosed with simple SAM will be managed by CHWs where health facilities are non-functional or not available while those diagnosed in communities with functional facilities will be referred to health facilities and capacity of healthcare workers built to manage SAM. MAM cases will be followed up and IYCF provided to family and monitored while cases of complicated SAM will be referred to stabilization sites and followed up accordingly. The activities will include mass screening of all children 6-59 months, management of SAM, MAM, micronutrient supplementation, capacity building of mothers/caregivers and evidence generation. The interventions will ensure disability inclusion by:
 Carrying out an inclusive risk analysis with both children and caregivers with disabilities to identify the specific factors contributing to malnutrition for persons with disabilities, including barriers to accessing services and use information to provide a stronger basis for planning response activities to directly address the risk factors.
 Allowing for inclusive participatory monitoring of response activities by building a monitoring framework, include specific indicators to monitor specific risks or needs of persons with disabilities, carryout activities to monitor the situation and needs of persons with disabilities, such as accessibility audits and regular focus group discussions or other community engagement activities with men, women, boys and girls with different types of disabilities.
 Including persons with disability in the planning of activities, carryout outreach to persons with disabilities who may have difficulties reaching services, train project staff on inclusion, and provide accessibility of nutrition related information, identify and involve local organizations of persons with disabilities (OPDs) for example Center for the Coordination Unit for Persons with Disabilities for the Northwest and Southwest regions.
 Make complaints and feedback mechanisms accessible to persons with physical, hearing, visual and intellectual disabilities by providing it in accessible formats (braille, sign language videos, etc. ensure the availability of multiple formats for providing feedback and complaints, and to consult persons with disabilities on their preferred formats and channels.
 In the monitoring framework for nutrition, disaggregate relevant indicators by disability to monitor equitable access to assistance.
Targets
- Target mother and caregiver of children 0-23 for IYCF-E and family MUAC
- Target men, women, boys, girls, (including PwDs) for SBC
- Target pregnant and lactating women and children 6-23 for BSFP
- Target adolescent and pregnant women for Micronutrient supplementation
- Target children 6-59 months for MUAC screening and management of malnutrition

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Since 2016, the NWSW regions of Cameroon have been facing a socio-political crisis that has seriously damaged the local economy and deteriorated the humanitarian situation. Beyond human losses, there are vast displacements, with their corollaries on the social system. In 2023 alone, 4.7m people were in need, 2.7m were targeted, and only 758,000 were reached. The conflict characterized by the destruction of the already fragile economy, massive displacements, and exacerbated by the COVID-19 outbreak; has had disastrous consequences on the population of the two embattled regions, with children and women suffering the most. This is because of many people losing their means of livelihood, and reduced farming which constituted the main means of survival for most people thus explaining the many cases of malnutrition in the NWSW regions as reported in the EFSA 2019 report. Given that conflict remains the main driver of food insecurity in 2023 as it disrupts income sources, hinders food access due to market disruptions, leads to price spikes and food shortages, affects the delivery of humanitarian aid, warring parties intentionally denying access to food as a weapon, the conflict in the NWSW is no exception to this phenomenon. In Cameroon, 11% of the population is facing acute food insecurity, representing 3M people with the NWSW being the most affected (Cadre Harmonize, March 2023). Given that food insecurity may increase the risk of malnutrition in all its forms, and given that nutrition and disability are intimately linked, there is, therefore, a need for an inclusive nutrition response. The effects of the crisis on the nutrition aspect of the NWSW regions remain concerning resulting in deficiencies in the nutritional status of many households in these regions and worsening among IDPs, returnees, and other vulnerable host households. According to the CBC Health Services NIP program with support from UNICEF has screened 14899 children in 2023, and diagnosed 951 children with SAM and 344 with MAM during normal routine activities. According to IOM report round 8 of 2023, an estimated 69% of children in these regions have not been screened for malnutrition using the simple technique MUAC. A significant number of people (78%) have not received IYCF-E education. An estimated 87% of children 6-23 months don’t have access to dairy food, 51% have not had access to grains, and 85% have not had access to flesh food. Additionally, about 90% of children do not have access to eggs while 84% of children have no access to Vitamin A-rich food. Furthermore, the protection that is needed for healthy living and growth, especially for child development has been affected with 19000 children separated from family in 2023 alone.
