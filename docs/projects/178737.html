Project Summary

QC will conduct an identification/ assessment of existing water sources through a systematic assessment/ mapping, including the functionality status, major issues (if not functional) and estimated number of populations benefitting from the water source in the targeted areas. Based on the results of the assessment, the project will provide safe water fitted by submersible pumps, for communities that do not have access to water stations. QC will choose to repair/reconstruct water sources over constructing new water facilities.
In order to monitor the effectiveness of this result, reports will be maintained by assistant engineers, tracking the number of IDPs/host communities who have access to safe drinking water, and the percentage of beneficiaries who have access to functional water sources. In addition to this, water quality tests will be conducted and the number of main stations and sub stations which are functional will also be monitored.
Fuel will be required for running the generators, which operate the pumps, powering the movement of water to cities, from the water stations. Engine oil, oil and diesel filters are necessary for the ongoing maintenance of these generators. The electrical panelboards are the electrical circuitry running the water station, which control the operation of the generators.
Water will be treated with chlorine, to ensure the safe consumption by beneficiaries. Additionally, dosing chlorine pumps provide the correct amount of chlorine treatment, just outside of the water well, in order to ensure that water is appropriate for human consumption. Water testing will be completed on a daily, weekly and monthly basis, in order to ensure water quality. Water samples will be taken at random locations throughout the entire water network and tested for Free Residual Chlorine (FRC) and microbiological presence, ensuring that the water is pathogen free.
QC will meet with the local council and local water authority to explain how the repair and rehabilitation of the systems will be conducted, and organize trainings for them in order to continue the water pumping after the project is completed through the collection system, which secures the sustainability of the service.
For those HHs that aren’t able to connect to the city water network, metal tanks and safe and clean water tankers will be distributed to them. In general, the targeted water per capita of this project is 30 liters/person/day.
The sewage systems in the most urban areas/district capitals in northern Syria, discharge to a network of sewage ponds that provide secondary treatment for sewage. In assessments carried out by QC’s team, it was found that some of the sewage systems are provided with pumps and power supply for pumping the wastewater, through the piped network to the ponds. Some of these ponds are situated alongside an existing natural water system. As long as households have access to a sufficient and reliable water supply as well as support to the pumping system, wastewater will move through the network consistently. However, there are blockages and leakages in the sewage system, as with any network. Thus, QC will support the target communities with resources to repair blockages and leaks, so the system is in good working order.
The project team will conduct hygiene promotion sessions through a hygiene promotion team to promote diseases prevention mechanisms, especially for Covid-19, focusing on the most vulnerable groups, such as children < 5 years, lactating mothers and pregnant women, the elderly and households with critically ill family members, and then hygiene kits will be distributed to the targeted groups. The hygiene kits wich will be distributed will ill take into account the needs of the fight against Covid-19.

4 - Needs

According to Syria HNO 2021 and based on WASH assessments, 59% of the Syria population requires WASH assistance , nevertheless, to adequately understand scale and diversity of WASH needs, the overall number of people in need of WASH assistance - 12.2 million, needs to be completed with figures presenting specific WASH needs related to: water quality – 3.3 million and quantity – 1.4 million people, sanitation – 3.2 million people, solid waste management – 4.1 million people, and heavy financial burden linked with purchasing water – 7.5 million or hygiene supplies – 6.4 million people. Further 5.1 million people face barriers to effective handwashing. Over 7 million people is highly dependent on humanitarian WASH assistance, including 1.75 million people living in IDPs last resort sites that require comprehensive and continued WASH assistance to survive. In addition, the entire population of Syria relies on drinking water treatment chemicals provided by humanitarian community. All data from 2020 WASH Sector assessments (winter and summer rounds) was gathered directly from affected population through face-to-face interview. Data collection included enumerator’s observations on the conditions of HH’s WASH facilities and drinking water sampling for free residual chlorine (FRC) testing at the end-user side. To strengthen accountability to affected population the 2020 WASH assessment questionnaires included questions on satisfaction from received humanitarian aid.

5 - Implementation of activities

QC will directly implement project through qualiﬁed staff as the following activities: - Rehabilitation of major water networks, such as water stations and boreholes, and the construction of new water systems for areas without access to established water facilities in communities, camps, hospitals, health centers and schools. - Provision of Fuel for water stations, Lubricants and spare parts stocks such as filters to the local water authorities - Construction/Rehabilitation of the sewage networks for communities, camps, hospitals, health centers and schools. - Capacity building to local council and water authority for operations and maintenance of water system and cost recovery system. - Hygiene promotion campaigns, with more focus on pregnant and breastfeeding women and girls, including the distribution of Hygiene kits. - Distribution of water tanks to the IDPs in camps and communities to increase the HH water storage capacity for adequate and safe water - Distribution of hygiene kits to the IDPs in camps and communities - Organize single sex focus group discussions for women, girls, boys and men to choose the location and design latrines and bathrooms.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project will carry continuous Protection Risk Analysis as part of the implementation plan. The risks might include inequality in accessing services, work safety, gender-based violence and exposure to explosive hazard. The project will mitigate the risk of inequality in accessing services by selecting of the target beneficiaries based on need assessment and that SPHERE/sector agreed standards are strictly followed and adapted to the context for each activity of the project and the project will coordinate with the stakeholders to ensure equitable access to the project activities. The project will address risks related to work safety by adopting the standard safety measures during the operation and maintenance of the WASH facilities. Workers will use the Personal Protective Equipment to minimize risk of injuries or exposure to diseases. The project will take adequate measures to minimize exposure of vulnerable groups (women, adolescent girls, etc.) to GBV. The project team will ensure women’s voices is heard during design of WASH interventions and identify any GBV risk(s). The locations of the WASH facilities will be central, accessible and in well-lit areas with good visibility of the surrounding area. staff will be trained on GBV concepts and referral pathways to properly support survivors. Women, adolescent girls and people with disabilities will be consulted during the design, implementation and monitoring phase of each project, and put the mechanisms in place to facilitate feedback from users.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Outbreak of conflict prevents access to project locations. Assumption: The project locations are safe and the conflicts are limited. Mitigation: Suspend the activities until the conflict is over, in addition to prepare a contingency plan. which includes changing the locations if necessary. QC will train ﬁeld staff to improve their security management on daily basis and improve their knowledge to assist people in risk. Risk: Arrival of new IDPs to the project locations Assumption: The pollical situation is settled after the agreement between Russia and Turkey Mitigation: Advocacy through the WASH cluster for coordination and cooperation to cover the new needs Risk: Current controlling entity or local council no longer supports QC staff and activities Assumption: The local councils are selected by local people and the cooperation with the humanitarian agencies is one of their priorities. Mitigation: Continuous coordination with the local councils to explain them the projects positive targets.Risk: Project workers infected with COVID-19: Assumption: All the staff are aware of the protection procedures against Covid-19. Mitigations: All preventive measures will be taken in terms of wearing masks, using sterilization materials, and following social distancing recommendations.
