Project Summary

The proposed project aims to address the critical needs of vulnerable conflict-affected populations living in sites, settlements, and surrounding areas in Syria of Northern Syria, through camp and coordination support. Specifically, ACTED will implement mobile CCCM responses to coordinate service provision and build local capacity for self-management. ACTED’s proposed assistance, through its CCCM mobile teams, will include key information management activities such as service mapping, risks and demographic assessments, running of coordination structures through regular engagement and advocacy and the strengthening of self-management structures through trainings. ACTED will focus on the reinforcement of accountability systems (e.g. CRMs). In addition to providing CCCM core activities, ACTED CCCM mobile team will conduct rapid needs assessments throughout Northern Syria which will primarily use to inform the programmatic decision to deploy. In NES, ACTED will also continue to provide static CCCM support through maintaining the Camp Management function in Areesheh camp (Al-Hasakeh governorate) and Washokani Camp, as well as Mobile response to sites and settlement across NES. In NWS, ACTED will target 17 formal camps located in the Azaz district (Northern Aleppo governorate) through shelter and settlement assistance. In each of these sites, the implementation of key essential services falls under the responsibility of the one camp manager who is the focal point for all in-camp coordination, as well as the locally recruited Camp Management Unit (CMU) in place

4 - Needs

In NES in-camps, aproximately 551,132 IDPs are living across 150 sites. Including: 249,907 people in Al-Hasakeh, 154,350 in Ar-Raqqa, and 146,875 in Deir-Ez-Zor governorates. In October 2019, as a result of the escalation of the conflict a further 190,000 individuals were displaced throughout the region. As of 18 Janury 2020, 70,590 people, remain displaced in NES. 5,033 of these IDPs were displaced from Mabrooka camp into Areesheh camp (Al-Hasake governorate) causing an additional strain on already limited services and facilities. Significant gaps in Camp Coordination and Camp Management have been identified in NES by the humanitarian community at both site level and more broadly, requiring active support to ensure that the needs of those most vulnerable are identified and responded to. In addition, ACTED have identified the need for more agile information management programming across NES. As a result of the escalation of the conflict in the Southern Idleb governorate, over 312,000 new displacements have been tracked across NWS since 1 December 2019 . Total displacements recorded are over 2.4 million, with 792,790 IDPs in Aleppo governorate . According to the latest estimations, 106,414 IDPs are residing in camps, informal sites and settlements, tents, collective centres and open areas . Despite the existing structure in place, ACTED has identified significant gaps in the delivery of CCCM services and the coordination of humanitarian assistance in camp in A’zaz, observed through its ongoing programming in the camps there since early 2019. Across Northern Syria, a critical lack of information exists regarding population data in camps, compounded by coordination issues both at site level, with the Clusters, and with the local authorities, inhibiting partners to fill in gaps to effectively.

5 - Implementation of activities

Through this intervention, ACTED will provide the following intervention: • Coordination and monitoring services Several coordination mechanisms including weekly coordination meetings and monthly sectoral cluster meetings will be put in place. Coordination will be crucial in ensuring that needs and gaps in these sites are identified, duplication is avoided, participation is enhanced, and humanitarian standards are applied. • Carrying out Rapid multi-sectoral and site assessments to identify the main needs of IDPs and develop coordinated response plans• Conduct demographic assessments and risks assessments in formal and informal sitesAs part of this activity, ACTED will strengthen information management and ensure the regular mapping of services and gaps in the targeted sites. Where ACTED is acting as a Camp Manager, ACTED will lead on and facilitate the capacity building and CCCM trainings for all in-camp actors. Topics will be: humanitarian response standards, code of conduct, conflict mediation as well as principles including mainstreaming of gender and protection.• Establish and/or improve of community governance structuresACTED will first learn about the community governance structures present within new displacement sites. Where community governance structures are already established and functioning, their composition will be assessed and whenever needed, ACTED will provide support to move towards equal representation of women, youth, the elderly and other marginalised and vulnerable groups. Where community structures are not in place, ACTED will support the community in setting up inclusive community governance structures. ACTED will build on its experience to set up such structures composed, making sure that women, elderly, and member of youths are duly represented.• Strengthen and/or establish accountability mechanisms in targeted sitesThis will be achieved through the support to existing accountability mechanisms and the setting up of new ones.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Non-inclusion of highly vulnerable groups and minorities needs during project design/implementationLikelihood: Low – Impact: HighMitigation measure: The proposed intervention will be implemented in accordance with ACTED’s gender approach, which follows the Gender Handbook for Humanitarian Action reviewed in 2017 by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC). ACTED incorporates gender analysis and considerations into the full project management cycle, from project design to implementation and monitoring. During the community sensitisation, ACTED encourages the identification of special needs of most vulnerable groups including women, children, elderly, or people living with disabilities and conducts FGDs and/or community meetings prior to implementation. ACTED addresses where possible gender and protection concerns. In areas out of the scope of programming or expertise, ACTED relies on referral procedures to specialized agencies.Risk 2: Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuseLikelihood: Low – Impact: HighMitigation measure: All ACTED staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct which directly refers to the Protection of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and to Gender Based Violence (GBV). As part of on-going efforts to improve accountability, ACTED is committed to implement a beneficiary Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its projects to allow beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on ACTED work, as well as the work of the contractors, and to relay any arising issue, including potential PSEA or GBV issue.
