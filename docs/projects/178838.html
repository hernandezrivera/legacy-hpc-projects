Project Summary

Building on experience of project interventions in Syria since 2013, Oxfam continues to work with local authorities in responding to the vulnerable populations’ WASH needs in districts/subdistricts of Aleppo, Hama, Idlib, Rural Damascus, Dar’a and Deir Ezzor, based on assessments, severity of needs and in response to further emerging needs in NES and NWS. The subdistricts that will be targeted by this project have all been heavily affected by conflict and require WASH support. OXFAM conducted integrated WASH and EFSVL assessments from January 2021 to date covering over 50 communities through group discussions and key informant in three governorates. This includes consultations with LNGOs, INGOs, secondary data and the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2021. The project is designed to meet specific lifesaving needs around water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) strongly aligned to the WASH Sector. It is gender mainstreamed, with a response strategy overarched by a community engagement approach suitable to access and security challenges; implementation engages trusted community structures, experienced local site engineers, carefully screened private contractors, community-based volunteers and identified local partners, and strong collaboration with other actors.
The total estimated population, as per UN-OCHA datasets in these sub-districts, is over 1.1m people. OXFAM targets the timely response to emerging rapid onset emergency needs in hot spots and potential flashpoints, new returns, new displacements, affected by COVID-19, economic issues, and the current returned areas with persisting WASH needs. The target locations proposed are according to severity of humanitarian needs and number of populations in need which will be adapted to the evolving ground context, access and people's movement. The project aims to support lifesaving WASH interventions that additionally pay attention to ensuring locally sustained maintenance of repaired existing WASH systems: The objectives of the project are; 1): support to water, sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems to ensure regular services for affected people in Syria; 2): deliver humanitarian WASH supplies and services, and improve hygienic behavior and practices of most vulnerable people; 3) WASH coordination structures facilitated and enhanced; and 4) improve WASH facilities and services in institutions to minimize sub-standard WASH conditions of the most vulnerable people in Syria. The type of interventions strongly aligns with: SO1, SO2, SO3 and SO4 which captures the sector efforts to respond and assist local water establishments in restoring WASH services to basic functionality for communities, schools and community health facilities. Oxfam will target all age groups for water and sanitation interventions. Hygiene interventions, will target carers, women, adolescent girls and school children. Accurate disaggregation of population data will be collected from relevant ministries and actors to ensure coordinated and relevant data and appropriate interventions during WASH assessments and interventions. Oxfam has a unique MOUs with several ministries gaining trust, strong relations and approval of interventions. Bi-monthly meetings at the MoWR allow insight and agreement for selection of prioritized possible activities and discussion of operational challenges. This project’s close alignment with the HRP in terms of outcomes, activities and methodology is a reflection of Oxfam’s close engagement with the Sector, particularly through strategic discussions and information sharing throughout 2019 and 2021.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2021 SD Severity reports&OXFAM assessments, water quantity are significantly insufficient in Duma,Nashabiyeh& Haran Al'awameed subdistricts.Quality is affected by the alternative unsafe sources used by the population,& they cope by reducing volume of water used for hygiene purposes.In Kafr Batna&Arbin subdistricts,HH relies on coping strategies by using unsafe drinking water, reducing drinking water consumption& HH relies on economic coping strategies by credit or borrowing, buying less on other things to buy water.Unsafe drinking water at the HH level are reported with highest needs in Arbin,Nashabiyeh,Haran Al'awameed,Banan,Tall Ed-daman,Hadher,Eastern Kwaires As-Safira,Hajeb, Khasham,Sanjar Abul,Thohur Tamanaah& Al-Hasakeh.The availability& affordability of hygiene items has significant needs identified in 15 subdistricts of Kafr Batna,Duma,Ghizlaniyyeh, Arbin,Nashabiyeh,Haran, Al'awameed,Karnaz,Haritan,Tabni,Deir-ez-Zor,Abu Kamal,Al-Khashniyyeh,Khan Arnaba &Al-Hasakeh.The needs on solid waste interventions are identified in 23 subdistricts where garbage are buried/burned,left in the open,collection frequency is less than a week or lacks solid waste management system.The needs on toilets problems are reported in Khan Arnaba,Al Bab,Al-Khashniyyeh while Arbin,Nashabiyeh,Haran Al'awameed are identified having no desludging services, HHs share toilet with 20 or more individuals, &reports sewage overflowing in the neighbourhood.About 17 subdistricts in Aleppo,Quinetra,Idble&RD reported they can not afford the water&sanitation services with some percentage of their income is spent on Water& sanitation/septic tanks desludging.HH having no access to sufficient handwashing facilities reported in 13 sub-d.

5 - Implementation of activities

OXFAM’s emergency response integrated with resilience activities implementation are: SO1.1) repair and light rehabilitation of water systems’ pipeline,tanks,pumping station,boreholes,alternative solar; provision of water tanks; SO1.2) support to water quality assurance and operation and maintenance of water system; SO1.3) Repair, rehabilitation and operational support to sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems; SO2.1) Distribution of essential WASH non-food items (NFI), hygiene promotion and messaging on COVID-19; SO2.2) humanitarian life-saving/emergency WASH facilities and services, including strengthening Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) measures; SO2.3) Market-based assistance for WASH services; SO3.2) Strengthen prevention and mitigation of WASH-related protection risks through; SO4.1) Support to Rehabilitate WASH facilities and services in schools &child friendly spaces in close coordination with Education and Protection sectors.The target communities are selected based on high population and vulnerability,poor coping mechanisms, affected with C-19, returnees , affected residents and IDPs. The criteria for selection of households are those that are female-headed,with disabled member,old age,high number of young children and returnee with lost assets due to conflict.Oxfam will establish baseline,monitoring &end line studies,during &post monitoring, evaluation,community satisfaction,community participation in decision making on selection of most vulnerable population,& ensuring voices of women and those marginalized are heard.Oxfam will aim to target communities with large voluntary returnees, addressing social cohesion.All project data will be desegregated to gender, disability,age& community.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

OXFAM's humanitarian programming has established various tools, policies for conducting risk analysis and safeguarding to prevent abuse and misconduct in the entire programme: Minimum Standards for Gender in Emergencies, Gender & Age Monitoring tool, Humanitarian Gender Action Plan, Code of Conduct, Policy on Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), Child Safeguarding & Support, Security Policy & Local Security Guidelines, and Sexual Diversity & Gender Identity Rights Policy. Activities includes multi-sector & multi-level rapid gender analysis, Gender & GBV/Child Protection training, partnerships/strengthening coordination with the Protection, Child Protection, GBV sub-sectors & engages with specialized agencies (UNHCR, UNICEF, UNFPA).Oxfam has a zero-tolerance policy towards sexual harassment, exploitation, and abuse, hence the PSEA is strongly embedded into OXFAM work along other related policies. The Code of Conduct as an important pillar for safeguarding is executed for all OXFAM employees regardless of location, to carry out their duties and to regulate their conduct. Anyone including Oxfam’s partners and people we work with can raise a concern or complaint to Oxfam about what they experienced or witnessed without fear of retribution. Conflict sensitivity and Safe programming or protection mainstreaming risk analysis are established in selection of activities and locations, updates guidelines, conducts awareness of protection risks & vulnerabilities for staff/volunteers/implementing partners for using these policies including fraud, engages communities & stakeholders safely participates in projects in a conflict sensitive manner; risks prevention, mitigation & management measures established.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1)Risk:Tension develop between recipients & unreached communities for WASH infrastructure&NFIs assistance. Probability: High. Mitigation measures:Community selection consultations with least access to WASH services, set selection criteria, coordination with WASH actors & authorities. 2)Risk: Men may resent women/girls' participation in hygiene & menstrual promotion sessions for going against expectations/norms that women stay at home,taking time away from care work,can lead to domestic violence.Probability:High. Mitigation:Sessions are not timed when women perform their household chores;organise parallel gender discussions with men &develop allies/seek support;separate men&women sessions held in private venues.3)Risk: Increase risks of GBV from unseparated male-female toilets&no locks.Probability: High. Mitigation: Consult women and girls on toilet location/design, ensure door & window locks; separate male and female toilets. 3)Risk: Unstable inflation exchange rates complicates spending &expenditures. 4)Risk: High. Mitigation:Explore options used by other actors, pay vendors in tranches to keep suppliers’ interest, report exchange rate gains to the donor.5) Risk:C-19 infection to staff/construction workers/beneficiaries due to non-compliance of safety measure during activities. Probability: High. Mitigation: Reinforce & monitor workplace C-19 safety SoPs compliance,provide PPEs/awareness,remote management&monitoring plan. 6)Risk:Lack of fuel&increased prices disrupts access to WASH services,risk of diseases as people rely to alternative/unsafe water sources.Probability:High.Mitigation:upgrade to solar system, HH level water treatment,water rationalization ensuring equitable accesss; contingency plan for water needs.
