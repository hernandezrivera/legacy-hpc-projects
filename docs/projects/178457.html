Project Summary

Since the beginning of the Syrian crisis, the wheat farming has been affected severely due to the destruction of infrastructure, degradation of services, in addition to the difficulties faced by farmers in marketing and cultivation of wheat, thus also affecting the milling and baking and bread production as the main food items for people in the targeted location. The project aims at revitalization of wheat farming through purchasing of wheat from 500 vulnerable Small and Mid-Sized wheat farmers (owned between 5-10 dunum) in Azaz and Jarablus sub districts. The procured wheat will be used for purification (sieving) to be distributed for new wheat farmers in the growing season 2021. IRD is planning to procure (750 ton, about 1.5 ton from each farmers), half of procured quantities will be divided equally between Durum wheat soft wheat. After purification (sieving of 750 ton), the expected pure wheat seeds will be about 712.5 ton calculated as the percentage of impurities in the raw wheat will be about 5 %. The purified quantities will be sufficient to support 2850 farmers as each farmer will receive 250 kg to cultivate 10 dunum of wheat. So, it can be summarized that the total HHs who will be benefited from the project are 3350 HHs (500 farmer who will be supported with wheat marketing + 2850 farmers who will supported with wheat seeds for farming). The farmers who will be supported with wheat marketing and wheat cultivation will be selected based on clear and specific eligibility and vulnerability criteria. This project is not secured (Planned project)

4 - Needs

Generally, the planed interventions had been developed based on the key messages of HNO report, 2021, which clearly mentioned that agricultural value chains had been significantly affected by the ongoing crisis. In 2021, the economic, political and security landscape is likely to remain complex and challenging. The widespread acute food insecurity situation will most likely persist in many parts of Syria and will have severe consequences for the well-being and the living standards of the majority of Syrian families. Farmers also suffer from limited access to markets due to physical and economic constraints, limited access to finance, lack of water for irrigation due to damage of the needed infrastructure such as irrigation canals, limited access to relevant early warning information and the impact of COVID-19 on agriculture value chains. Although the quantity of wheat produced in 2020 is relatively good compared to previous years, amounting to about 2.8 million tons, there is still a significant gap between available wheat and the national consumption needs. This is resulting in the Government of Syria facing difficulties in filling the huge deficit in wheat and flour, in light of the difficulty to import due to measures against Syria, and the decline of the Syrian currency. With no signs in place regarding an improvement in the macroeconomic conditions, it is likely that high food (and agricultural input) prices will persist, and this will drive more people into food insecurity as 2021 progresses.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Examples of protection risks: Protection risk (1): Child labor (involving children in wheat value chain. Mitigating measures: Ihsan RD will be sure that the staff working in this project are trained and fully aware of child safeguarding, Child protection and GBV principles. Protection risk (2): Lack/difficulty of accessibility for PWDs in the proposed activities. Mitigating measures: 1- Availability of wheelchair for PWD at the reception point (IRD contribution), a trained staff to deal with PWDs, Priority for the PWDs, included in the criteria, House to house distributions that will ensure that those with special needs that can’t reach the distribution points can reach the assistance. Protection Risk (3): Sometimes, crowding during distribution of bread, may not provide the services at the desire quality, which might affect the BNFs dignity, especially women. Mitigating measures: :Set up separate waiting and entry lines at registrations Raise the awareness of community leaders on GBV and child protection principles and make sure that all BNFs can voice their concerns through the designated channels. Protection Risk (4): Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation for women during implementation of the project. Mitigating measures: Choosing appropriate time for distributions of bread and receiving of wheat crop, voucher exchanging in Hawala agent will taking place during daylight hours to allow women to return home safely, ensuring regular security and incidence reporting, conducting awareness sessions for the women about the issues surrounding with GBV to minimize and avoid such damage as possible. ensuring that the filed staff include female members to coordinate with female BNFs.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Base on Ihsan RD PSEA policy and before starting of project interventions, Ihsan RD will conduct SEA risk assessment of to identify all potential SEA risks such as :1- Risk of SEA act by non-PSEA trained staff while provide the support to BNFs, 2- BNFs do not report SEA cases for Ihsan RD staff, which results in the Ihsan RD being unable to investigate these cases and provide the needed assistance to them. Mitigation: After in-depth assessment for these risks, Ihsan RD through collaboration with PSEA focal point will designed the mitigation plan all these risks as follows Mitigation measures: 1- Ihsan RD will be ensure that all its staff had signed code of conduct to prevent acts of SEA by its staff and all recruited staff should attend PSEA training before any contact with BNFs. 2- Beneficiary awareness-raising (Beneficiaries must be aware on: what SEA is, what their rights are in regard to SEA, what they can do if SEA occur3- Establish safe and accessible community reporting channels to report SEA complaints tool will be also available (e.g. complaints box / telephone hotline to deal with these cases)

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:1- All protective tools materials needed to avoid COVID-19 infections are available during project cycle2- Timely disbursement of funds3- Market assessment for what that was conducted during project designing remains applicable during wheat purchasing from farmers 4- Stable security situationRisks and mitigation: 1- Corvid -19 Outbreak in the target location Mitigation: Ensuring that all protective measures had been carefully considered and applied to avoid COVID-19 infections all stage of project such as, social distancing at least two meters, providing of masks and gloves for all persons involved (field staff, vendors, drivers and ensuring the availability of sterilization materials at distribution points). Applying all updated preventive measures for COVID-19 shared by WHO, ERL and FSL cluster. 2- Delay in fundsMitigation: Senior level of coordination with SCHF team and responding to requirements to push them to release the requested payments at the optimal dates3- Fluctuation in pricesMitigation: IRD will conduct an in-depth market assessment to be shared with SCHF team, suggest the available and suitable options and the best solutions to address this issue after coordination with SCHF team. 4- Changing the security situation Mitigation: Monitoring and analysis security situation, temporary suspension of field works. When it is impossible to implement the project due to the bad security condition, IRD will develop a plan B for project implementation to change the project location to safer areas after advanced collaboration and coordination with SCHF team.
