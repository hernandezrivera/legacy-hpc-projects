Project Summary

Building off the achievements of ongoing MC food assistance program, which together have reached over 165,000 vulnerable people to date, Mercy Corps will continue using a food voucher approach, along with complementary nutrition sensitization to enable vulnerable, conflict affected populations to meet their basic food needs and dietary requirements. This will help in addressing immediate food security needs by providing 10,000 HH (estimated 70,000 individuals) with food support through e-vouchers in Dikwa and Bama LGAs. The 10,000 HH households will be newly registered and selected according to specified criteria.

Needs

Food insecurity remains an urgent concern across Northeast Nigeria as the violence of the ongoing Boko Haram insurgency continues to displace the population and restrict sustainable agricultural activities. This has, in turn, caused market disruptions that have led to increases in the prices of staple foods and severely limited the coping options for households on the move. Displacement appears to be on the rise; as of April 2018, an estimated 1.88 million people were displaced in the northeast states of Borno, Yobe, Adamawa, Bauchi, Gombe, and Taraba, an increase of nearly 100,000 individuals from earlier estimates in February.3 More than 75% of these internally displaced persons (IDPs) are in Borno State, and have largely gravitated (or been instructed to move by the security forces) towards urban areas. In an effort to protect the host and growing IDP populations of these urban centers, including the Borno State capital of Maiduguri and other Local Government Area (LGA) headquarters, the Nigerian government is implementing a strategy of “encamped garrison towns,” establishing a secure perimeter to defend these locations from Boko Haram insurgents. In April 2018, the Nigerian army launched operation "Last Hold," an offensive against Boko Haram, resulting in additional displacements into overcrowded garrison towns and exacerbating the humanitarian situation. Although these enclosed areas are defended, there is little to no presence of formal security actors outside the towns, posing significant risks to those who venture beyond the urban perimeters and greatly limiting the available area for farming. Food Insecurity Indicators - Northeast Nigeria: The perimeter radius of five to 10 kilometers typically set aside for farming activities to sustain the population inside these garrison towns has proven insufficient to date, as reflected in key food security indicators. According to FEWS NET, most households in central and northern Borno, southern Yobe, and northern Adamawa States are facing immediate crisis (Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) Phase 3), with predictions that in the coming lean season (July-November 2018), with higher food prices, assistance-dependent households could face IPC Phase 4. According to an April 2018 update from the U.N. Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA), current funding to address food insecurity in the Northeast only covers 34.1% of identified needs, leaving a significant gap. This has also left over 3.5 million people suffering from malnutrition, with funding levels only addressing 59.2% of identified needs.Based on MC baseline survey and need assessment, it has shown that a relatively high number of displaced persons and returnees in Dikwa and Bama don’t have access to farmlands to farm which is their predominant source of livelihood, thereby causing food shortages and malnutrition in a wide scale. Mercy Corps has been supporting about 15,000 HH with food assistance in form of voucher and a complementary nutrition intervention to promote good dieting and intend to continue as we hope the security situation in these communities improves for the better and people start accessing farmlands. Although other partner agencies have their foot over there, organizations like Oxfam has pulled out and thereby creating a gap, and Mercy Corps has done well in covering the gap by initiating more life sustaining emergency assistance to the community. There is need for more inclusive and robust food intervention in these locations so that people will can have good food to live their lives before linking them to early recovery to rebuild their resilience and livelihood. Mercy Corps is also training community beneficiaries in agricultural activities which include Permagarden, Crop rotation and Composting to improve their skills to do more with less farmland available to them.

Activities

Building on the achievements of ongoing MC food assistance program, which together have reached over 165,000 vulnerable people to date, Mercy Corps will continue using a food voucher approach, by providing 9 rounds of food voucher top-up with a transfer value of 25,000 NGN worth of food items, along with complementary nutrition sensitizations and to enable vulnerable, conflict affected populations to meet their basic food needs in Dikwa and Bama. The transfer value is based on the need assessment which will and has been covering the MEB of households because monthly price survey of food items usually fluctuates higher with the security threats on roads leading to these locations. Mercy Corps will reach and sensitize people on Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) and Community-based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) across Dikwa and Bama LGAs.Mercy Corps will continue coordinating with other sectors and partners in conducting market assessments, price survey of food items, and having an inclusive and robust stakeholder engagement to help implement its projects. Mercy Corps will ensure that activities are effectively and efficiently monitored in these locations. With the identified needs, MC will register beneficiaries based on specified vulnerability criteria, select, and verify these beneficiaries to benefit from the food assistance. After every food voucher top-up, M&E will conduct post-distribution monitoring to assess the impact of the assistance to these beneficiaries. After the 9 rounds of food assistance, M&E will conduct end-line survey and contribute to the final report of the project.Protection MainstreamingPrioritizing safety and dignity and avoid causing harm , complains and feedback mechanism by ensuring that we have a complains mechanism to receive feedback on concerns of communityCreating awareness of and rights of the people and their entitlementEmpowerment of beneficiaries – This will be a key focus for assistance.25% of the total budget will be used for protection mainstreaming. Accountability to Affected Populations Plan (AAP)Program design: To include beneficiary input in the design of the program, a range of sessions with Community Relief Committees (CRCs) in each LGA were conducted during February-March 2018 to define the prioritization criteria for ongoing programs. In addition, Mercy Corps consulted key stakeholders to determine priority needs, including target communities, local government officials and other humanitarian actors present. Consideration of gender and inclusion of people with disabilities helped inform the design of the program. In addition to CRCs and master trainers, Mercy Corps establishes Community Accountability Reporting Mechanisms (CARMs) which are designed to empower individuals to report concerns from program efficacy to staff misconduct and to feel confident that their complaint will be reviewed and addressed. CARMs embody the following principles: They are SAFE, CONFIDENTIAL, TRANSPARENT, ACCESSIBLEPSEA- Mercy Corps condemns all actions of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) in any nature to affected populations committed by staff, contractors, volunteers, partners and other actors who provide aid in communities. Mercy Corps will consider this the most serious breach of accountability by the humanitarian sector. Consequently, Mercy Corps has put protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) as a distinct subset under accountability to affected populations (AAP), with a high profile and a strong set of specific commitments. To accomplish this Mercy Corps will provide clear communication on prevention of SEA using, flyers and posters at distribution points as possible modes of dissemination. Mercy Corps Nigeria will also ensure that they: Communicate to beneficiaries: Use the key message that humanitarian aid is free; beneficiaries should never need to exchange sexual favors to obtain assistance. Beneficiaries have the right to complain.
