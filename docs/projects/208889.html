Project Summary

IRW contributing to provide integrated health, nutrition and protection services to affected IDPs and host communities in WK, those impacted by current war between Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and Rapid Support Forces (RSF). The intervention targets El Nuhud and El Khoway Localities. Delivered services are align with HRP 2024, objectives 1 & 2,1- Provide safe, timely, principled and gender-responsive life-saving humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable groups to reduce mortality and morbidity in areas with high severity of need or at risk of a further deterioration of critical humanitarian needs, 2- Directly respond to the conflict-driven protection needs of crisis-affected people through principled and conflict-sensitive assistance and advocacy as well as prevent and mitigate emerging protection risks, with regard to international standards. The intervention designed to address basic needs for 23,520 individuals, (9,408 women, 8,232 men, 3,528 girls and 2,352 boys), through health, nutrition and protection services.
In Health, project target 15,309 individuals (6,123 women, 5,350 men, 2,296 girls and 1,530 boys) to increase access to improved healthcare services, by rehabilitating HFs, strengthening capacity of health staff and community workers to provide essential health services including antenatal services.
In nutrition, project target 2,088 girls, boys and PLW, to be serviced with nutrition package in 4 existing facilities and 4 new facilities opened in El Nuhud and 43 IDPs sites. Focus include; Caregivers of infants and children aged 0-23 months, reached with MYCN counselling, care takers of children with SAM with medical complication, supported with food costs at SC, two SC nutrition sites supported with running costs, children >fives screened and referred to treatment/counselling, through mass MUAC screening, children 0-23month reached with growth monitoring, children aged 6-59 months supplemented with (e-BSFP), PLW supplemented with (e-BSFP), PLW at risk of acute malnutrition reached with FBPM, children >two provided with nutritious food, PLW with global acute malnutrition newly admitted targeted for treatment and supplemented with folic acid. Children >five years with moderate acute malnutrition newly admitted, targeted for treatment in targeted (TSFP). Children 6-23 months receive Micronutrient Powder (MNP), Children under five years with severe acute malnutrition without complication newly admitted targeted for treatment in OTPs. Children under five years with severe acute malnutrition with complication newly admitted for treatment in SC. SAM children with medical complications supported with transport costs. Children 6-59 months receive Vitamin A supplementation. IYCF/E messages disseminated. Mother to mother and father to father support groups established and trained. IYCF breast feeding corners established. Four additional OTP, TSFP, TFC sites opened. Existing four OTP/TSFP sites rehabilitated to meet minimum service requirement. Existing nutrition warehouses facilities improved as per supplies' minimum requirement. Twenty nutrition staff receive training on CMAM (OTP/TSFP), 20 staff trained on micronutrient supplementation, and target nutrition staff trained on IYCF/E. Two monitoring visits conducted by national teams in the field, four joint monitoring visits organized with MOH. IRW ensure sites submitting regular reports monthly. Interventions will coordinated with relevant stakeholders. Accessibility and risks issues will coordinated with HAC & OCHA. IRS will raise funds appeals to complement intervention.
In protection, project targets 6,123 beneficiaries to improve protection conditions a, provide support to access basic services with dignity and advocate for their rights to receive quality protection services. Protection issues considered, particularly unaccompanied children fleeing war areas and deliver psychological support to displaced families and focus on issues affecting women & girls.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection will be a key feature within planned interventions. In all planned activities, woman and girls will be sensitized about the protection issues to reduce the risk of abuse, interaction and violence. Water facilities and water points will be constructed in safe locations that minimize risks of various types of GBV. Pitching water in most part of remote areas in Sudan is the responsibility of women and young girls. However, this project will ensure sparing women an ample time and give them the opportunity to carry out other activities such as food security and livelihood related activities and participate in the development process of their communities, state and country. Women and children will be the project focus to support through provision of direct support especially children and women of special needs. The project will ensure protecting communities particularly women while delivering FSL related activities and at the same time ensure the delivered activities will cause no harm to affected population or to the environment around them..

4 - AAP & PSEA
4.1 - How will the project share information and collect community feedback? Please select as many as relevant.

Face to face with individuals (in homes) through aid workers ,Face to face with group consultations with aid workers,Face to face with community leaders with aid workers,Phone calls,Email,Letter,Social media,Complaints / suggestions box

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

IRW has in place a robust complaint response mechanism (CRM) strategy that is fully implemented during project life-cycle and are culturally appropriate, accessible and effective in line with CHS 5. These allows for timely response and feedback to the affected population. Three levels of monitoring beneficiaries feedbacks will followed for the purpose of quality delivery and continuous improvement of services. These are the establishment of complaint boxes, to enable affected populations to report their complaints and provide feedback, The CRM number and email address is printed on each activity implementation banners in local language to allow beneficiaries to report and CRM mobile number is also provided which can be used by majority of the affected populations. These three levels of obtaining community feedbacks is crucial aspect ensured by IRW staff to manage and respond to feedbacks for improvement purposes.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Complaints management is a critical and pivotal aspect of program accountability and continuous improvement of any service delivery program. Islamic Relief worldwide recognizes the need for a standardized approach to establish and strengthen complaints handling mechanism and related processes to be able to demonstrate effectiveness and impact. Special CRSM- Complaint Response Mechanism System for all planned activities will be developed to ensure quality services are delivered to communities in positive manner and feedbacks were collected for improvement purposes on programme delivery. Global guiding document has been developed through a consultative process at local and global levels. Islamic Relief continues to stand firm on zero tolerance for (sexual) misconduct. Therefore, through all delivered interventions IRW, will make sure to create and maintain an environment that prevents sexual exploitation and sexual abuse. IRW staff at all levels have a particular responsibility to support and develop systems that maintain this environment which includes placing and having direct contacts with the target population to manage and deal with such cases if happened. IRW used to share mobile number where beneficiaries can provide feedback, suggestions or complaints on SEA activities or miss appropriateness.
