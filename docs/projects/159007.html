Project Summary

Poverty, the consequences of past hostilities that continue to be felt, displacement, political instability, weak protection systems, sub-standard housing and overcrowding continue to increase vulnerabilities, especially for women, children and persons with disabilities. In addition, needs arising from GMR continued to raise with more than 33,690 people injured - including at least 8,003 children (OCHA figures, 14 October 2019), posing serious concerns in terms of civilian protection and access to emergency health.
Through its programmes and services, UNRWA will mitigate the impact of the erosion of coping mechanisms and increasing poverty, which heightens protection risks for the most vulnerable.
Thematically, specific Agency efforts will focus on Gender Based Violence (GBV), child protection including child labour, children out of school, children with disabilities, child marriage and the aftermaths of the ongoing Great March' mass demonstrations.
Operationally, UNRWA will seek to strengthen inter-programme coordination and cooperation, referral systems, protection mainstreaming, and internal capacity. The training of front-line staff will continue, focusing on protection principles, referral systems for individual protection cases such as children and victims of GBV (response, mitigation and prevention) in emergency situations. UNRWA will also raise awareness on the protracted and current humanitarian crisis in Gaza and its protection implications for Palestine refugees through inter-agency coordination and direct contact with external interlocutors. In particular, the Agency will take advantage of visits by foreign delegations to Gaza, providing up-to-date information on the humanitarian and protection environment and advocating for refugee rights.

1 - Needs

In Gaza, 33,690 people (85% male, 15 % female) have been injured and 201 killed (198 male, 3 female) (including 44 children (43 boys, 1 girl)) during the Great March of Return (OCHA, 14 October 2019). In the same period, UNRWAs funding crisis started in 2018 continue to impact its operational and program capacity. Unprecedented level of unemployment (52%), violence alarming levels of poverty (more than half the population depend on emergency food rations), more than 12 years blockade, repeated hostilities, political and geographical fragmentation, and issues of over-crowding and sub-standard housing continue to increase protection threats and eroding capacities and positive coping mechanisms. This situation has left the majority of the population dependent on assistance from UNRWA, especially the most vulnerable women, children, and persons with disabilities. In such a context, the Protection & neutrality office responds to urgent protection needs across the massive scale of UNRWA’s operations, which provide essential services in food, education, health, including MHPSS, basic WASH to almost 1.4 million refugees (49% women and 51% men; 29% children under 18, gender proportions are the same as for adults). With poverty being the single most significant driver of child labour, it is not surprising that anecdotal evidence and research show that numbers have continued to increase since 2012. UNRWA area protection committees all agree that a worrying phenomenon is represented by a spike in children, both boys and girls, begging in Gaza streets. In 2017-18, the UNRWA Protection and Neutrality Office in Gaza ran a project addressing child labor. It successfully reintegrated 126 (of 138) children into the educational system. The Child Protection Working Group has prioritized child labour and called for increased effort to prevent and respond to children working. Child labour has a detrimental effect on children, compromising their physical, mental or educational development. Analyzing UNRWA data on refugee children out of school, disabilities still represent an obstacle to the right to Education, as well as learning difficulties, insufficient MHPSS to children, conflict-related injuries and chronic diseases. UNRWA statistics show that 375 students have been injured in the GMR. Interviews conducted with injured students, teachers and school counsellors showed that students were heavily affected by their injuries and often put them at risk of drop-out. Interestingly, household vulnerability is a constant factor for children out of school, drop-out, early marriage as well as present in 87% of GMR injured UNRWA students (97% boys, 3 % girls).According to UNFPA, 51% of women in Gaza are survivors of gender based violence (GBV). Specialized services for survivors are limited, and access is further compounded by existing barriers due to social stigma and fear that intervention will increase harm to the survivor, to the children in the household, such as through separation or isolation. GBV victims are identified across UNRWA services by counselors in health, education and social services. They are targeted by professional health and psychosocial counselling services, as well as legal orientation. Since 2014 and until today, the Agency has worked actively to build staff capacity on identification and management of GBV cases. In life threatening cases, UNRWA directly activates governmental resources. Nevertheless, the lack of additional financial capacities limits UNRWA possibility to materially support GBV victims moving ahead to abandon their violent environment and support them maintaining custody of children. Such precarious humanitarian conditions reinforce the Agency’s commitment to respond to material and protection needs of the refugees, including continuously advocating for the rights of Palestine refugees and especially for those who are most vulnerable, in light of a continued lack of accountability and persisting violations.

2 - Activities

Boys and girls are removed from situations of child labour or isolation, receive adequate and targeted support and possible reintegrated into education system- Identify boys and girls under 16 years old for intervention. Obtain consent for age appropriate interventions from families and design interventions based on a participatory approach, in order to ensure that the safety and dignity of beneficiaries are respected - Coordinate with UNRWA services such as Relief & Social Services (RSS), Education or Health Program to identify actions to protect these children- Follow-up with UNRWA services on implementation of appropriate services and progress of children and their families- Coordinate with Ministry of Education on those who need to attend literacy classes- Medical examination to assess assistive devices, learning disabilities, didactical materials and specific needs Particularly vulnerable families are supported to reintegrate children into school and no longer rely on children’s income- Social workers identify families reliant on working child's income without other options- Provide small cash injection to households to relieve hard economical situations and allows enrollment into school- Social worker support family, and check on progress and provide ongoing guidance on managing expenditure and building economic resilience- Organize guidance sessions with parents/caregivers in order to increase understanding of impact of early marriage, the value of education, especially for children with disabilities- Vocational training opportunities are explored with children over-aged The family, as the key protective environment, is strengthened and well informed about their children’s needs- Conduct awareness raising sessions with staff and communities to promote responsive behavior and active participation in monitoring out of school children, children working, married and with disabilities- Liaise with other organizations (especially the Child Protection Working Group) to share information and to make referrals for additional assistance. Publish advocacy materials in collaboration with other humanitarian cluster members- Document the risk factors of exclusion and neglect and present learning at relevant forums such as CPWG, to contribute towards building knowledge and strengthening responses Acute protection situations of women, in particular GBV survivors, are responded to or mitigated- Identification of GBV cases in health clinics, schools and RSS to case managers- Design of individual response plans by case managers, including internal and external referral (following UNRWA SOPs). High risk cases are referred to other established GBV and child protection referral networks or the Governmental authority for additional support (e.g. police protection)- Provision of targeted support and follow up, incl. when relevant cash based assistance to protect the victim and restore dignity- Capacity building of front staff on safe identification and SOPs for referral (will be liaised with GBV sub-cluster) Provision of relevant, timely and accurate information to raise awareness on the direct and indirect impact of the GMR on Palestine refugees and UNRWA services, support advocacy interventions and inform programmatic responses - Systematic monitoring, documenting and analysis of the impact of the GMR on the safety and wellbeing of Palestine refugees and on UNRWA service provision- Collection and analysis of disaggregated data to identify vulnerable groups facing specific protection risks- Active engagement in and proactive provision of information to humanitarian coordination and protection mechanisms and relevant international human rights bodies- Collaboration with, and strengthening the capacity and effectiveness of Area Protection Committees through identifying and addressing information gaps and needs Reporting on indicators- Reporting on set indicators to HRP periodic reporting and Financial Tracking System

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Safety and dignity concerns are fully embodied in the action. For what refers to Child Protection, the proposal considers the promotion of integrity and safety of children as the first priority, especially when targeting children out of school (children engaged in hazardous forms of work, children with disabilities excluded from their right to education, early marriage and the reintegration of children traumatized and injured during the GMR). Interventions targeting those children allows for a dignified response to their multiple needs and protection of their rights, stemming from multi-disciplinary approach connecting education, social services, health, case management and psycho-social support. With reference to protecting and supporting survivors of GBV, the action place their safety in the first place with a path to gradually recover their dignity and integrity. To do so, it also fully includes an understanding and analysis of the “Do No Harm principle” in the peculiar Gaza context where a set of discriminatory and patriarchal laws, cultural norms and praxis tend to inflict further harm to women and their children seeking remedies to escape violent situation. Safety is guarantee by the inclusion of emergency health care, to individual case management and legal counseling until initiatives to restore dignity of the GBV survivors, including through direct cash assistance.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNRWA services are open to all refugees and assistance can be provided either as UNRWA has a specific obligation to mainstream protection across its services and operations, respect and promote humanitarian principles and made a clear commitment to protect and uphold Palestinian refugees’ rights. UNRWA since 2016 has a dedicated Protection and Neutrality Office and human resources dedicated to guarantee the provision of its services is done in an impartial and non-discriminatory way. In fact, UNRWA regularly assess the level of compliance with Protection standards of the services provided to refugees through a Protection Audit. Entitlement to UNRWA services are provided on the base of the family registration as refugee (for instance for basic education or primary health care) or on the basis of needs (a poverty assessment is regularly conducted such as for food assistance). Further, families can directly request assistance to UNRWA social services as well as protection cases are identified across UNRWA services. The project, targeting children and GBV survivors, aims to provide assistance to a number of child and GBV survivors facing neglect, exploitation and deprivation of their rights. Identification of children and GBV survivors will happen inside schools, through home visit done by social workers, and health centres. Possible beneficiaries seeking remedies will be informed and supported, without any discrimination or barrier and through the project will receive targeted and evidence-based assistance. An individual and family approach is conducted to guarantee violations are halt and possibly a safe environment is restore for victims. Professional case managers follow up the cases until the pattern of abuse is stopped or relieved.
