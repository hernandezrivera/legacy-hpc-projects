Project Summary

South Sudan Development Agency (SSUDA) is a national NGO established in 2006 and has long experience in implementing food security and livelihood activities in Upper Nile. SSUDA is currently implementing FAO supported Agriculture and Livestock intervention in Fashoda and Manyo and has just completed a 3 year EU funded Food security and Disaster Resilience project in Western Bank. Through this project SSUDA is planning to implement an emergency livelihood support to improve food security and resilience of 6,500 vulnerable HHs (40,000 men, women, Children and PWD) in Manyo, Fashoda Melut, and Baliet (Upper Nile State) classified in IPC Phase 4 and Phase 5. The project is in line with FSL Cluster Objective 2 and 3 and contribute to 3 HRP 2021 Strategic Objectives with specific aim to save lives, protect livelihoods and enhance food production while strengthening resilience against the 3 drivers of food insecurity name; conflict, floods and COVID-19 pandemic. Specifically, the project will involve distribution of vegetable kits consists of an assortment of vegetable seeds; okra, amaranth, tomatoes, onions, watermelon, kale, and eggplant, the crop kit comprises of maize, cowpea and sorghum seeds while the fishing kit has different types of fishing equipment. SSUDA is planning to procure and distribute a few small treadle operated pumps to farmer groups to increase vegetable and fruits production along the river banks during dry season. In addition, beneficiaries of agricultural inputs will receive training on best agronomic practices - postharvest handling to minimize loss and value addition such as the extraction of fish oil. SSUDA will work with women groups and schools in the target locations to promote kitchen and school gardens respectively. The livestock component will deliver emergency livestock vaccination and treatment, training and support of animal health workers and awareness on animal disease control and prevention among the livestock-keeping Households. In addition, SSUDA is also planning to provide ToT training for community health workers and lead-mothers on basic skills in nutrition and hygienic preparation of food for service providers toward improving caregivers’ feeding practices for children aged six months to five years, elderly and the sick including PLWHIV. Further training for selected led-women in making energy saving stove and briquettes will be considered to reduce time for cooking and GBV risk of women and girls going to the forest to look from firewood. The project will build on or be integrated into other planned and ongoing SSUDA projects such as the conclude PRO-ACT funded by EU and Emergency Livelihood Response Programme (ELRP) funded by FAO. In order to make impact, SSUDA will apply a multi-sectoral approach which include collaboration with other cluster partners such as Health, Nutrition, WASH and Protection. SSUDA will ensure livelihood supplies are effectively and sufficiently distributed to the most people in need and that feedback mechanism is well in place including carrying out age and gender-sensitive Post Distribution Monitoring Assessment involving female, child and PWD hHs. While the project responds to immediate needs of the affected population, SSUDA will ensure that the local capacities and resilience of the affected population (women groups, Water Committee, youth and children in and out-of- schools, PWD, local authority) are strengthened to enable them bounce back from the crisis and further withstand future shocks through formation, training and awareness raising of local committees on HLP, natural resource management, Disaster Resilience, Peace building, Climate Change Adaptation and prevention and response to Gender-based violence COVID-19 pandemic. SSUDA will participate in FSL Cluster meetings to receive and share relevant information for better coordination to build synergies and avoid duplication of resources.

1 - Needs

According to IPC report, IPC Acute Food Insecurity and Acute Malnutrition Analysis, October 2020 to July 2021 Issued: 23rd December 2020, by April 2021, by April 2021, an estimated 7.24 million people (60% of the population) will be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, among whom an estimated 2.5 million people are facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and 31,000 people are in Catastrophe (IPC phase 5). Furthermore, the report indicated that around 1.4 million children aged between 6 months and 5 years are expected to be acutely malnourished in 2021 and will need life-saving treatment. This includes 313,000 children suffering from severe acute malnutrition and more than a million children suffering from moderate acute malnutrition. 483,000 pregnant or breastfeeding women are acutely malnourished and in need of treatment. WFP has expressed concern about the rapid rising numbers of people suffering because of the lack of sufficient food and nutrition, intensified conflict, unprecedented flooding and high food prices in the country. While livestock plays a critical role in the food security and overall well-being of pastoralist and agro-pastoralist communities by providing milk, meat, income, and as drought power in farms, FAO reported an increase in outbreak of animal diseases include Zoonotic diseases due to flood. Upper Nile worst affected by main drivers of food insecurity especially the among women, children and people with disabilities. REACH Report (2020) confirmed widespread destruction of food stocks, decimated livestock and destruction livelihood assets, disrupted access to market and increase in market food prices is affecting food availability and accessibility. Movement of good and fishing activities along river Nile has been interrupted leading to reduction in trade and market functionality in the major urban areas. The IDPs and returnees hosting area are equally vulnerable communities. The presence of new and protracted IDPs and returnees further undermines the copying abilities of host communities by increasing demand and competition for natural resources, food and basic services such as health and education and often lead to tension and flare up of related HLP issues. The biting hunger has made families to turn to other negative coping mechanisms such as one meal a day, wild food, burning of charcoal and brewing of alcohol. These negative coping mechanisms are detrimental not only to health of women and children but also pose risk of sexual and gender based violence as families resort to marry of their young girls in exchange of dowry to cushion family social safety net. Health partners in Upper Nile especially in areas where SSUDA is operating have been reporting increased rate of malnutrition among the children under 5 and lactating and pregnant women. The signing of Peace Agreement and establishment of Revitalized Government at National and State levels have created opportunity for displaced populations to resettle back and reintegrate. Despite the difficult environment, the communities are doing their best to produce their food though lack of access to agricultural input, vaccines and drugs have been the main challenge (SSUDA - ERLP Report, 2020). If provide with timely support of agricultural inputs, skills and fishing equipment, the communities will be able to produce sufficient food especially during wet season. River Nile provides opportunity for communities especially women groups to practice vegetable and fruit farming and increase household income and nutrition in the dry season. During dry season most communities reside along the river Nile. However, they lack the necessary small-scale irrigation tools. SSUDA is planning to support farmer groups with small treadle operated pumps to support them irrigate their farms along the river banks. SSUDA will support agroforestry by encouraging tree planting along the river and swampy places as mitigating measure for floods.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

SSUDA operates under zero-tolerance on Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) and all staff members, Board of Directors and venders are expected to sign a commitment with the organization to avoid, prevent and report any SEA case at all times. SSUDA will be vigilant among its staff to detect any issue regarding SGBV such as Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Further, while developing the beneficiary targeting list and the registration system, SSUDA will allow female and child-headed households priority access during distribution times and have all safety arrangement and equitable access for these vulnerable groups including people living with disabilities. In addition, SSUDA will hire and train volunteers and temporary workers (men and women) who will work daily in collaboration with AAP committee members to help with security, data collection and distribution of livelihood supplies.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

SSUDA recognizes that women, girls, boys and men all have very different FSL needs, interests and vulnerabilities. Vulnerable groups such as women, girls, people with disabilities and elderly can be taken advantage of and stigmatized. SSUDA will target and ensure that perspectives from these vulnerable groups are included in the consultation and decision making process as well as implementing a system for people to submit complaints and issues that arise. This will allow the participation of women and other vulnerable groups to get prioritized and ensure their access to food security and livelihood as well as other humanitarian assistance. Through needs assessment, registration, monitoring and evaluation and Post distribution monitoring, SSUDA will be able to collect sex and age disaggregated data and monitor whether these vulnerable groups are receiving adequate assistance.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

SSUDA will communicate and provide information for all stakeholders including cluster and the beneficiaries about the interventions through various means and channels. The organization will first conduct initial community consultative meetings with the community leaders and representative of the beneficiaries to solicit their opinions and manage expectation. The meetings will also provide opportunity to identify AAP committees and to review the project plans. AAP committee will be the connection between SSUDA and community and a channel for communicating information and feedbacks from the community. Other means include complaint feedback during distribution and Post-distribution monitoring.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

As part of the Food Security and livelihoods Cluster, SSUDA will have designated officers to attend national and State Level Cluster and Inter-Cluster meetings regularly to obtain and share important information about the work, progress of our intervention as well as the general humanitarian situation in the country. This will enable better coordination with the UN agencies and other NGOs to prevent duplication of efforts and address gaps in relief response and unmet needs by using Standard reporting formats.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

SSUDA has experience in implementing FS activities in Upper Nile. According to SSUDA’s experience and observation, the population groups at great risk of acute food insecurity are rural poor, people living with disabilities, women and children, IDPs, as well as destitute urban households. Food insecurity in urban areas such as in Kodok, Wedakona, and other County headquarters is emerging as a particular concern, as migration to urban increases in the face of displacement caused by conflict and floods. Of particular concern are those households with multiple/overlapping risk characteristics – for example female or widow headed IDP households. SSUDA will integrate and work with protection partners in respective areas, protect the most vulnerable groups in the target communities.

12.1. Does your project contribute to any of the two Collective Outcomes, i.e. Food insecurity and Gender-Based Violence?

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

Food insecurity during emergencies is an issue that affects entire communities, but affects women and girls in particular ways. Women tend to have limited control over and access to resources and finances during an emergency, and their resulting dependency can put them at increased risk of sexual assault and exploitation. Through this project SSUDA aims to reduce pressure on families to marry daughters at young age for lessening food needs with in the family and community. SSUDA is planning to adopt integrated approach and work with other clusters to ensure that condition that put women at risk of sexual assault are reduced such as collection of firewood, water as well provision food /NFIs for cooking, shelter. During distribution, SSUDA will ensure that factors that pose risk for sexual assault and exploitation of women beneficiaries are prevented ie unsafe locations of distribution sites and long distances

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

SSUDA will Provide access to food to those in greatest need in an impartial manner, without bias or prejudice; and enable communities to find durable means to meet their own food security needs. With Disaster Risk Reduction mainstreaming SSUDA seeks to save lives of the affected population but also protect livelihoods from future shock, to make food production system more resilient and more capable of absorbing the impact of, and recovering from, disruptive driving factors of food insecurity and malnutrition including conflict, flood and COVID-19 pandemic.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

The timely provision of livelihoods is life-saving assistance to people affected by conflicts and natural disasters. SSUDA will coordinate with other Cluster partners (NFIs, WASH, Protection, Health etc) to ensure the most vulnerable IDPs, returnees, include people with disabilities, female-headed households and unaccompanied minors, on arriving at their place of displacement or return, have the same access to livelihoods and other basic services including COVID-19.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

A key component of food insecurity is lack of access to a sufficient quantity of nutritious food, which is a potential risk factor for malnutrition in children, PLW, sick and PLWHIV. By supporting the project that intends to increase food production, diversification, accessibility and intensify consumer knowledge for mothers and caregivers about and hygienic preparation of nutritious food, SSUDA will be working towards delivering healthy, nutritious and safe foods which is a requisite for reduction of malnutrition among the vulnerable under IPC Phase Crisis – 3 and IPC Phase Emergency – 5.
