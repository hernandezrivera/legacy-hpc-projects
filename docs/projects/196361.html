Project Summary

1. Community based health activities
-Health awareness activities for vulnerable individuals (pregnant, disability, elderly): Vulnerable people are more likely to face challenges in access to health care as well as to timely and correct health information. Therefore, awareness will be conducted focusing on the vulnerable individuals and their family. The topics of the awareness will be selected based on disease trend at the camp and the health/ hygiene topics that are specific to the vulnerable individuals . Awareness materials such as flipcharts will be developed for each topic. Awareness will be provided in forms of group sessions and individual counselling. Group sessions are conducted by the CHWs/ CVs who are familiar with the community and aims to facilitate interaction among participants by sharing challenges and success stories and discuss solutions on how they can maintain their health and well being. Not only the vulnerable individuals and their families, but their neighbors will also be invited to create an opportunity for the community to develop a better understanding of the vulnerable individuals and think how they can be supported within their community. Individual counselling will be conducted through home visits by the CHWs/CVs, who will provide specific awareness and tailored advice to the individual needs. Depending on the topic, hygiene items such as soap and cloth masks will be distributed along with the awareness.
-Training for community Volunteers (CVs) : In order to promote health at the community level, it is essential to develop the capacity of the community people and engage them as actors in the awareness activities for a sustainable and realistic intervention. The selected CVs will receive comprehensive training to obtain the knowledge of health and hygiene that is necessary to conduct the health awareness activities and home visit as outlined above. In addition to knowledge, participants will be trained in practical skills such as communication skills of how to facilitate discussion so that the awareness messages are effectively delivered to the participants. CVs will be remunerated according to the progress and achievements.
2. Providing essential primary health care services at the Primary Health Care center
-Provision of healthcare services:Providing counselling and treatment for maternal and child health, SRH, NCDs, EPI, communicable diseases, general OPD and 24/7 emergency care services including delivery for both the FDMNs and HCs. Consultations rooms are separated by gender to ensure privacy and female patients will be attended by a female doctor. Triage system is in place to provide immediate care for those with emergent needs. Patients requiring further care will be referred according to the referral pathways. Patients with suspected infections will be isolated from other patients in the isolation room.
-Ensure adherence to the minimum package of essential health services for primary healthcare facilities in the camps where feasible. Health sector, government authorities, other health sector partners will be consulted to achieve this when needed.
-Facility based health awareness sessions: Health care workers will organize awareness sessions at the PHC to the patients based on the patient trend and/or the seasonal trend in the camps to enable timely health education and promote behavior change as well as foster enhanced communications among patients and clinic staff.
-Data analysis and monitoring: Patient data is routinely collected through the patient records, and analyzed /monitored to capture the disease trend of the communities as well as timely reported to the health sector to contribute to surveillance. Patient feedback is collected through suggestion boxes and interviews. All data gathered will be disaggregated by sex and age for analysis and monitoring.
-Our PHC will contribute to the health sector campaigns when requested(e.g. COVID-19 vaccination etc)

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Although 5 years have passed since the influx, the circumstances surrounding the refugees are yet challenging. The total population of FDMNs and Host Communities (HC) in need of assistance is projected to be more than 1.5 million (BGD Rohingya Response Host Community Population 2023-25). The camp continues to observe spikes of communicable diseases such as skin diseases, AWD, which are caused by the congested and unhygienic living conditions in the camp. The uptake of SRH services has improved with increased facility delivery rates and utilization of modern family planning compared to the initial stages of the displacement. However, variations are observed among each camp due to the availability of services and geographical access. For instance, the facility delivery rate in Camp 14 is 63% (HS Coordination meeting presentation slide of Aug 17, 2022) which is lower than the overall rate of the camps. In addition to the health issues that existed from the beginning of the crisis, the increased needs of NCDs and mental health has been recognized. Similar trends are observed in the HCs, where ongoing negative effects caused by the influx is recognized and service accessibility is challenging. In order to cater the health needs of the refugees and HCs, partners have been providing services under the leadership and coordination of the health sector. Given the protracted nature and financial constraints, service delivery must seek for medium and longer term solutions considering efficiency and sustainability. The Health Sector Strategic Plan 2023-2024 strives for strengthening health system capacity to enable efficient, effective and sustainable delivery of quality health care and ensuring equitable access to essential health care at all levels including the community level. Among the refugees and HC, there is a tendency of recognizing that medicine is the sole solution for health, rather than understanding the importance of practicing healthy behaviors and preventive measures. Health education is essential at the facility level as well as at the community level so that beneficiaries understand that behavior change is the key along with timely health seeking behavior to promote their health and well-being. Despite efforts of health sector partners, not all the beneficiaries have equal access to health care. Among the refugees living in the camps, 4% are elderly persons, 3.87% have at least one specialized needs, and 1% have disabilities (UNHCR Population Fact sheet as of September 30). Other reports indicate a higher prevalence of disabilities, with 12% individuals having a disability and 35% of the households have at least one member with a disability (Age and Disability Inclusion Needs Assessment, REACH Initiative, 2021). Despite that vulnerable individuals such as persons with disabilities, elderlies and pregnant women have higher health related needs, there are more likely to face barriers in accessing health due to the distance and lack of transportation options in the camp, which may disturb their access to health care when needed. Vulnerable individuals are also reported to have less opportunity to attend community meetings and are less likely to be visited by NGO workers implying that they do not receive necessary information in a timely way. Access to basic water, sanitation and hygiene is also challenging, which hampers their practice to maintain hygiene. A community based approach through CHWs/ community volunteers to deliver correct and timely health awareness to promote health and behavior change and enable timely health seeking behavior is needed. In line with the Health Sector Strategic Plan, our project will provide awareness for health and hygiene with special focus on vulnerable individuals and provide essential primary health care services to ensure that beneficiaries including those with specialized needs are able to receive health care when needed, where not only the FDMNs but also the HCs will be beneficiaries.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Our PHC has been operating in Camp 14 in coordination with the Health Sector ever since the Rohingya influx. We also participate in the camp level coordination with the CiC and Camp Health Focal Point and have coordinated well with them. Since we have been providing essential health services through our PHC and conducting awareness activities and home visit in the community for a long time, our PHC is well recognized and accepted in Camp 14 and the surrounding host community. Our PHC is especially known as the only facility that provides delivery service in Camp 14. Despite the challenging geographical features of Camp 14 where there are many uphill and downhill, pregnant women have been coming to our PHC to deliver their child as a result of our extensive community activities to aware them about the importance and safety of facility delivery. New mothers who delivered at our PHC are satisfied with the care they received and have recommended their neighbors and families to deliver at the PHC, and have also come back to deliver their next child. In this way, we have built good relationships with our patients and the community, and have contributed to promoting safe facility deliveries, which is encouraged by the sector Although our PHC was not recommended as the result of the rationalization exercise, the ranking was “green” indicating that we have been providing quality health care services. The recommended PHC in Camp 14 is not providing facility delivery and is not planning to start providing this service. The sudden discontinuation of our service will mean that there will be no facilities available in Camp 14 that can provide facility delivery, which will not only confuse the beneficiaries, but may lead them to give up the thought to deliver their child at the facility, which is a concern that must be avoided. Our PHC will provide delivery service in Camp 14 to fill the gap of the lacking function of the recommended PHC, until an alternative solution is ready.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

This project focus on vulnerable people in the community such as pregnant women, elderlies, people with disabilities who have difficulties in physical access to health facilities and limited access to correct health information. This project will support vulnerable people to obtain the correct knowledge, educate surroundings not only vulnerable people to understand each other’s, and support physical supporting devices. This will reduce the difficulties they have and protect their rights to health-seeking behavior regardless of their vulnerability. In addition, to ensure that sexual and reproductive health and rights are protected, including unwanted pregnancy, unsafe abortion, gender-based violence (GBV), and decision-making in the use of maternal and child health services, through awareness activities for women who are particularly vulnerable, such as pregnant women and those with disabilities, the target population will be given access to necessary information and services.

3.2 - Gender

Consideration will be given to the appointment of women as CVs so that female beneficiaries can be approached. Since the understanding of male, such as husbands not only approaching to female, is essential for female's decision-making to be respected. This project will also support the target population in accessing necessary information and services, as well as conduct educational activities on the same topic targeting male. By gaining male's understanding, we can lay the groundwork for female to express their opinions and contribute to their participation in decision-making within the household. Our PHC provide separate consultation spaces for male and female patients, female doctors, midwives, and other female medical staff, as well as TBA who are trusted by refugees, to make it easier for female patients to visit PHC. Care is taken to create an environment in which female feel comfortable, such as by ensuring privacy by prohibiting male from entering the delivery room and echo room

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

The list of vulnerable people created by the project includes orphans. Based on that list, CVs check the health and living conditions of the child through home visits, and confirms whether the child has an appropriate guardian and whether the child is receiving appropriate support. If a situation is identified that requires some support, we will provide necessary information and assistance to access appropriate support authorities and facilities. The vulnerable list also includes children as well as the elderly. CVs visit their home and they check the presence of the caregiver and the living environment in the same way as above. Through these activities, the project will contribute children and the elderly to live safely.

3.4 - Diversity

The target beneficially for this project's awareness is the vulnerable people like pregnant, disability, elderly. Participants will include, of course, pregnant female and persons with disabilities, but will also include husbands of pregnant female and their families, as well as family members and neighbors of persons with disabilities. While it is important for vulnerable people themselves to gain knowledge to cope with each difficulty, the understanding and cooperation of those around them is essential for a solution. By learning about the difficulties of people in different situations and how to support them, it is possible to build a diverse society in which it is easier for diverse people to live.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

During the awareness session, encourage participants to speak up and encourage discussion among themselves. During these discussions, they are asked to discover problems in their community and discuss ways to solve them on their own. Based on the content of the discussion, the project will consider the content of material support and decide on the topic of the next awareness session. We will try to determine the projects and priorities based on their input. Bi-monthly reports on the status of awareness activities, the PHC's consultation status, and the disease trends of patients are also posted at the clinic in English and Bengali to show our activity to patients and visitors.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Due to difficulties in physical access to health facilities and limited access to correct health information, vulnerable people in the community such as pregnant women, elderlies, people with disabilities who have higher health risks are more likely to be left out in case of emergencies. CVs will identify the vulnerable individuals and provide them with the necessary information to promote health and well-being, and about available health services accessible from their community, how they can get there, whom to contact, so that they can be supported in case of emergencies. CVs will serve as the key person in their community that the community people can rely on when they need health related information. By identifying vulnerable, this will contribute to confirming the people’s safety in a disaster and providing emergency information without any missing. In addition, support to discuss on transportation methods like man power with neighbors on a daily basis will facilitate evacuation.
