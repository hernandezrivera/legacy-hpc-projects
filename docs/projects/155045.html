Project Summary

The goal of this project is to provide integrated healthcare services through 9 health posts, a comprehensive maternity center (CMC) and a birthing center to the Rohingya refugees and members of the host community throughout 2019, with technical assistance from Norwegian Church Aid (NCA).
The facilities will ensure availability of minimum service package (as agreed by Health Sector) through medical doctors and other service providers, including SRHR. The CMC will be operational 24/7, with provision of Normal Vaginal Delivery, C-Section and other maternal and neonatal services. Other services such as EPI and an external referral linkage with secondary service facilities will also be ensured.

Objectives

Sector Objective 01: Improve equitable access to and utilization of quality lifesaving and comprehensive primary and secondary health services for crisis-affected populations with special focus on sexual reproductive, maternal, neonatal, child and adolescent health aimed at reducing avoidable morbidity and mortality. Project Objective: Provide reliable healthcare service to the Rohingya communities through 9 health posts, one comprehensive maternity center and one birthing center in Ukhia, Cox's Bazar throughout 2019.

Needs

Already before the recent influx, more than 212,000 Rohingya were living in Ukhia and Teknaf sub-districts in Cox’s Bazar. The recent huge increase in number (more than 688,000 Rohingya) means that there are even fewer resources available per person. Plus 58% of new arrivals are children and 60% are girls and women including a high number of pregnant (3%) and lactating women (7%), with high risks of miscarriage and other complications. The new arrivals are building makeshift settlements in hill areas near established camps where hundreds of thousands of Rohingya already live. The Bangladesh Army has taken charge of the coordination of the relief and other intervention efforts of all actors and is arranging space for the afflicted population in temporary settlements. But the resources currently available to help the community are inadequate compared to the demands. According to the United Nations, the humanitarian crisis is “one of the fastest growing refugee crises of recent years”. A visit by UNHCR (United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees) in the area found “people suffering real hardship and some of the most difficult conditions seen in any current refugee situation”. Children, including newly-borns, are dying every day of pneumonia and water-borne diseases. More than 700 cases of measles among children have been identified. In many cases they have not received any treatment or medication at all. In particular, pregnant women, nursing mothers and children are suffering the most. There's also limited availability of female staff members and required medicines.

Activities or outputs

(1) Provide integrated healthcare services (including essential medicines, equipment and supplies) through operations and maintenance of 9 health posts, one comprehensive maternity center (CMC) and one birthing center in the settlement camps for refugees and members of the host community. Minimum service package, as agreed by Health Sector, will be ensured in all healthcare facilities through well-trained and experienced service providers, including physicians, nurses, paramedics, psychosocial counselors and Community- based Skilled Birth Attendants. Medical doctors will be available during all hours of operation in the healthcare facilities. The CMC will be operational 24/7, with provision of Normal Vaginal Delivery, C-Section and other maternal and neonatal services. (2) Ensure reliable Sexual and Reproductive Healthcare and Rights (SRHR) service delivery including health response to GBV and with emphasis on Maternal Health and Family Planning. (3) Support expansion of routine EPI services among Rohingya community. (4) Ensure a proper external referral linkage and network with secondary service facilities, including public facilities. (5) Ensure timely reporting and knowledge sharing with all relevant sectors and sub-sectors (i.e. SRH, GBV) involved in humanitarian response for the Rohingya.
