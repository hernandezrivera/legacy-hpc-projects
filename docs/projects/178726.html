Project Summary

The project seeks to provide services in the Child protection sub-cluster to achieve the HRP objectives (SO 5) " Increased and more equitable access for boys and girls to quality child protection interventions in targeted locations in line with the Child Protection Minimum Standards in Humanitarian Action "in Dana, Harim in Idleb
governorate. All project activities will in line with the Child Protection Minimum Standards
in Humanitarian Action(CPMS) and other standards and SOPs such as UNICEF alternative care SOP, CP Case management SOP, UNICEF Guidance for Inclusion of Children with Disabilities in Child Protection Interventions (UNICEF 2018) " to physical/psychological injuries, severe neglect, and lack of appropriate care, family separation and immediate risks of worst forms of child labor, child recruitment and child marriage.
MASRRAT insure (300) vocational training (female) during MASRRAT WGSS centers.
Finally, the project team will take into consideration the data protection and confidentiality at all stages, information sharing protocols, and acquiring informed consent from beneficiaries.
This intervention will support 7795 beneficiaries affected by the Syrian crisis (345 men,550 women, 3100 boys and 3700 girls, 100 PWD) in Idleb /Harim / Dana /Tal Elkaramej .
Also, the project team will ensure data protection, confidentiality at all stages, protocols of information sharing and acquiring informed consent from beneficiaries. The project activities will be in line with Protection Cluster Recommendations, SOPs, Inclusive COVID-19 response - NWS Disability - TWG on Inclusion Guidance Note April 2020 and GBV SC guidance note on GBV service provision in the time of COVID-19 such as Hand sanitizing when conducting the sessions/ Gathering of five beneficiaries per session and social distance/Measure the temperature for each beneficiary as a precaution/ Plan has been put for online sessions/ Put awareness posters about Corona disease during sessions.
MASRRAT has been in the protection sector for seven years. MASRRAT is a focal point to provide GBV services in Jebel Seman district.
This project will complement the ongoing UNFPA project through covering of more area of intervention in Jebel Samaan ,where the two teams will work together under the same management and technical support, also the project will support the centers by vocational activity (sewing).
Each outreach team will be accompanied by a case manager who identifies the cases and coordinates with the GBV technical supervisor on UNFPA project to receive the cases in WGSS centers.

4 - Needs

HNO 2021 identified that 42 per cent of the assessed communities reported presence of at least one of the assessed eight protection issues as very commonly occurring and in 53 per cent of the communities respondents have reported presence of five or more out of the assessed eight protection issues . At least five of the eight key protection issues were reported to be occurring in all 14 governorates.Children of all ages face violence and protection concerns in many life situations.During the month of May and April 2020, ’MASRRAT conducted a multi-sectoral assessment in Aleppo and Idlib, (Boys and girls of different ages and abilities consulted in the need assessment equally and appropriately about the project) and the following results were: Insecurity in the region and lack of education are among the most problems facing boys and girls in Idlib and Aleppo, in addition to the presence of separation from home and parents. 60% of children suffer from health and psychological problems as a result of dropping out of education, and early working conditions. 72% of children do not receive any services related to protection or psychological support. 31% of families without civil documentation face challenges getting help. The information providers also mentioned that there are many children separated from their families, estimated at 250 children in rural Aleppo, and no official statistics have been indicated about their numbers, which increases protection risks, as most of them suffer from shock, stress and hunger, In addition, there are no child safety spaces in the village of Tal al-Karama, which was included in the needs survey, indicating that there is a gap around protection support in that village.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will include main integrated activities of humanitarian intervention which are:- Establish child-friendly space.- Provision of psychosocial support for children which is accessible for all children. - Referral of children to relevant services (Health, Nutrition, mental support, etc.). - Child Protection Case Management addressing children’s and caregivers’ protection concerns (including child labour, unaccompanied and separated children including children with disabilities, Children Associated with Armed Forces and Groups amongst others) in an effective multisectoral (including livelihoods, education, health, nutrition) and child-friendly response from relevant providers working in a coordinated and accountable manner.-The project will provide accessibility to children with disabilities (CWD), 220, 110 boys and 110 girls. the project will provide the center with CwD-friendly equipment such as ramps,walking bars, and ADA yellow tiles and transportation means .-Behavioral Change Communication. Awareness raising. Child Protection prevention focusing on implications of negative coping mechanisms inclusive of child labour, child marriage and family separation. M&E :All the activities will be monitored through field visits and monthly reviews. The reviews will assess the actual outcomes and actual inputs of the team, as compared with the expected outcomes and expected inputs defined by the project plan and logical framework, considered any unforeseen circumstances. The M&E system will include providing a response to concerns and complaints (mobile boxes, hotline phone number, and directly by the Masrrat team) to reach feedback from the children and local communities and sharing the information.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk: Girls, boys, women and men's physical security is put at risk as a result of the violence associated with civilian populations/spaces being targeted by armed parties to the conflict; a lack of consideration for the safety and security to access/receive services; lack of consideration of contextual information (e.g. aerial bombardment) and targeting of civilian infrastructure enroute to, during and directly after service provision.Likelihood: High. Impact: High Mitigating measures: Ensure a comprehensive service mapping is undertaken so that risk mitigation and communication with communities can be proactive and informed. Ensure consultation with communities and neighboring communities related to location of services to understand perspectives of communities are all understood and addressed. Including access barriers for those with injuries/disabilities and communication mechanisms for persons with visual/hearing impairments. Liaise with "neutral" parties as much as possible and triangulate all information from all sources prior to decision making. Utilize sector agreed key messages to ensure consistency and inform sector of discrepancies or if new messaging is required. Ensure safety audits for access are conducted for each fixed site or mobile service location. Consider conflict patterns and contextual analysis as part of decisions for establishing fixed site or mobile service provision. Ensure safety audit is conducted to ensure safe access (route and location) by boys, girls, women and men, elderly and populations with decreased mobility (due to physical, visual/hearing deficits, psychological issues). Engage with local authorities on safety and security in the community.
