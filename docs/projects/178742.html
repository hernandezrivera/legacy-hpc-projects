Project Summary

The scale, severity and complexity of the humanitarian needs of people in Syria remain extensive. This is the result of continued hostilities in localized areas, displacement, increased self-organized returns and the sustained erosion of communities’ resilience during close to 10 years of crisis. Across Syria, 12.4 M people in Syria are food insecure and 1.9 M people are at risk of food insecurity, with certain population groups facing particularly high levels of vulnerability.
The project aims to increase the resilience of IDP Households who had to leave their houses due to the deterioration of the security status, as well as targeting a percentage of the host communities who match IYD’s selection criteria. The project will provide food rations on monthly basis for 12 months to 61,500 families in camps of Dana Sub-district in Idleb province, and multi-round cash distributions of $60 for 12,000 HHs in Aleppo province divided as follow: 2,500 vulnerable families in Al-Bab sub-district, 2,500 vulnerable families in Jarablus sub-district, 4,500 Jebel Saman sub-district, and 2500 in vulnerable families in Afrin sub-district, 50% are from the host communities. In addition to that, the project aims to improve food security for the newly displaced people households and most vulnerable families by providing emergency cash for food of $100 value for a one-off to 5,000 vulnerable households within the project targeted sub-districts (1,000 HHs in Al-Bab, 1,000 HHs in Jarablus, 1,000 HHs in Harim, 1,000 HHs in Jebel Saman, and 1,000 HHs in Idleb). In addition, the project will distribute bread to beneficiaries in camps every other day as a part of the general food assistance, which covers the minimum need.
The IDPs are wholly dependent on humanitarian assistance for their daily needs, and the host communities in both provinces Aleppo and Idleb, are agricultural communities with very limited financial and livelihood resources, and the IDPs became a liability on the host communities because of this lack of livelihood, this issue is clearer in the areas where a limited number of NGOs provide humanitarian support.
IYD's MEAL plan will be done by monitoring the implemented activities and evaluating the objective achievements, to ensure that the project meets the required quality and time frame, to tackle challenges faced and summarize lessons learned. The IYD’s MEAL team will prepare a post-distribution monitoring report, and the data analysis and issue the report with key findings.
IYD will share the 5Ws report to appropriate clusters in order to find the gap if any and coordinate more on the cluster level. Also, IYD will prepare a final narrative and financial report.
IYD will coordinate with the camps' management committee in Idleb only, and with local councils in Aleppo. This coordination will facilitate IYD’s work especially in terms of data collection and verification, assist in solving any disputes that might happen during the distribution.
The fund for this project has not been secured yet.

4 - Needs

Syria remains one of the world’s most complex humanitarian emergencies characterized by ongoing hostilities which have killed hundreds of thousands of people, triggered one of the worst displacement crises of our time, and led to the widespread destruction of civilian and agricultural infrastructure, including homes, schools, health facilities, water supply, and irrigation systems. Today, 13.4 million people in Syria are in need of humanitarian assistance – 21 % increase compared to 2020 – with needs increasingly being exacerbated by an economic decline (HNO 2021). IYD has conducted a needs assessment covering all the project areas. The first three needs; 32% is cash, 26% is food, and 18% livelihood. Most of the respondents stated that the main method of coping is mainly purchasing food on credit and relying on less preferred and less expensive foods. 56% of respondents stated that no cash assistance has been provided in their communities during the last month. While 34% said that there has been cash assistance provided. 92% of respondents stated that the most urgent food intervention required is cash assistance, while 89% stated food baskets. According to the needs assessment, the studied HHs has reported that 86% of the families have no food stock for more than one month. The coping strategies of the studied families vary between relying on less preferred and less expensive food items, reducing the number of daily meals, buying food items on credit, sending the family members to beg, borrowing food, keeping the whole day with no food, and sending the children to eat with neighbors.

5 - Implementation of activities

IYD field team will distribute General Food Assistant GFA on a monthly basis to 61,500 HHs for 12 months in Dana Sub-district camps in Idleb province and multi-round cash distributions of $60 for 12,000 HHs in Aleppo province. In addition to emergency cash for food of $100 for a one-off to 5,000 new IDPs •Registration & Verification: IYD team will visit the potential beneficiaries and collect their data, and according to criteria, IYD team will select the project beneficiaries•Contracting the money transfer agent and arranging beneficiary payments, the procurement process to select the supplier must be carried out according to IYD policies•A Market Assessment will be conducted by IYD in the suggested area to check the Market’s functionality. •Distribution:oFood Rations: IYD team will coordinate with local authorities to determine a food distribution point “FDP”. The beneficiaries must provide an ID to prove their identity. After that, IYD team will deliver the ration. Elderly and pregnant women will be given priority. oVouchers: Vouchers will be distributed by the IYD field staff to selected beneficiaries in pre-determined distribution site at the community level. Vouchers will be redeemed to verify the receipts against the distribution records using the beneficiary’s name and voucher serial numbers. •Post Distribution monitoring: IYD MEAL team will conduct PDM with beneficiaries to verify that the activities are done according to IYD and donor policies. •IYD will contract bakeries to bake and deliver 61,500 KG of bread every other day in camps.•Beneficiary Feedback Mechanisms: all IYD channels for receiving feedback are available depending on the most suitable communication options for the relevant locations

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1-GBV risks can be exacerbated by overcrowding and mixing during the distribution of cash vouchers and food baskets in distribution sites that include women and girls, the lack of separate lines for males and females can increase vulnerability to sexual harassment and assaultProbability: Medium. Impact: HighMitigation measures:• A queue for females and another for males should be formed in the distribution site to prevent any harassment cases which might happen if only one queue is available 2- Protection risk: There is a risk of discrimination or a feeling of discrimination if a service is provided to certain groups or individuals and others are excluded.Probability: Medium. Impact: HighMitigation:• The distribution should be done on an equitable basis on vulnerabilities criteria with no discrimination irrespective of gender, age, skin color, religion, and others. • The relations between the beneficiaries and IYD’s teams will be based on mutual confidence, consent, and respect, while IYD’s team will be highly aware of the behaviors or acts which might cause resentment among the beneficiaries• Establishing a complaint mechanism in order to respond to the complaints and feedback submitted by the target community. 3- The risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment) women and girls. 8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: Full support from the local authorities and host communities to implement the project’s activities smoothly.Risk: Disputes between the host communities and the IDPs might take place in terms of benefiting from the project’s activities. Steal or loss of food rations. The fuel price is a risk for bakeries which is changeable according to the exchange rate, Attempts of interference by military groups might take place. Mitigation: IYD will conduct FGDs on the selection criteria, as well as distributing leaflets give more details on the selection criteria. The food commodities will be stored in well-placed, safe and guard warehouses. The warehouses will be locked and monitored on 7/24. IYD will sign a fixed-price contract with the bakeries to have a stable cost during the implementation.Risk: Lack of liquid cash in project locations.Mitigation: IYD will allow more time for transactions/distributions to take place. Risk: Money transfer issues. Mitigation: IYD is using PTT (Government Turkish Money Transfer Agency) to transfer money from Turkey to Syria, a list of vetted and trusted Hawala office is used to transfer money inside Syria.Risk: Forged vouchers. Mitigation: Non-disclosure agreement with the print house. Technical specification for printing. Communication with the print house via authorised methods only. Capacity building of all staff in the field level.Risk: COVID 19 spread among the BNFs and staff Mitigation: IYD will apply social distancing in the distribution location, using hand sanitisers, and all measures recommended by health cluster will be applied.
