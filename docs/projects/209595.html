Project Summary

This project builds on ongoing initiatives being implemented by the organization in the emergency, food security, livelihoods, WASH and protection sectors in Northeast Syria. The aim is to continue, expand and scale-up the current programs in order to cover the increasing needs and gaps, and reach more people-in-need, particularly in the host communities and informal settlements. This integrated, multi-sectoral assistance targets vulnerable and conflict-affected people in the governorates of Areesha, Tel Tamer, and Al-Hasakeh subdistricts (Al-Hassakeh governorate); Kisreh, Sur, Thiban sub-districts (DRZ governorate), and Raqqa sub-district (Ar-Raqqa).
The organization's ‘Impact Hypothesis’ for this project is in line with the HRP Strategic Objectives SO1, SO2, and So3. “If the targeted households have immediate access to emergency NFI kits [emergency/NFI]; If they have improved access to cash assistance, livelihood inputs and capacity strengthening activities [FSL]; If they have increased access to safe and adequate water, sanitation, and hygiene services and improved hygiene practices [WASH]; and If protection risks to affected persons is mitigated and women and girls’ vulnerability to gender-based violence is reduced [Protection]; THEN vulnerable households and communities in NES can be able to increase their long-term resilience, reduce their vulnerability to future shocks and disasters, and improve their overall wellbeing.”
The project is based on a primary MSNA for East line Deir Ez Zor (Jan 2024), WASH MSNA, Sitrep#3 for air strike affected areas in northern Hassakeh, Protection safety audits in HSK and DZ, ongoing project data, and rapid gender analysis, as well as secondary data and updates from the working groups.
This project integrates the provision of humanitarian aid, early recovery approaches and resilience building, with the ultimate goal of seeking sustainable and longer-term solutions to Syria’s most immediate and pressing needs. It incorporates:
- Building on a nexus approach to incorporate life-saving assistance while working towards early recovery and resilience: All four sectors are focused on providing lifesaving assistance in line with HRP’s priorities, but also preparing for early recovery and resilience through: income-generating activities; skills development; agricultural/livestock inputs and trainings; market linkages; upgrades to critical livelihoods and more reliable sources of water supply; and, enhanced skills for women and girls.
- This project will serve both communities in an effort to reduce tensions between the two groups and recognize the host communities’ vulnerabilities so they remain resilient and able to continue to host IDPs.
- An integration approach across all its programs means activities in the sectors are mutually reinforcing and have an amplified effect on the outcomes. Eg., FSL and protection are linked whereby women who seek livelihoods assistance but also require GBV response will be supported through survivor-centered approaches.
For WASH – support to airstrike-affected areas includes solarization, maintenance supplies, and back-up generators as far as donors allow. In DZ work on water rehabilitation, safe water supply, water safety planning, and case investigation will take place.
For Core NFI and winterization, 30% is estimated as CVA. Winterisation and core NFI activities include children’s winter clothing for camps, informal settlements, and informal settlements; and also core NFI for winter (mattresses, blankets, plastic sheeting) as directed by SNFI assessment in July/August.
For FSL/early recovery: short term food assistance will be provided to existing population, with some crisis modification to respond to newly displaced peoples as needed.
For Protection: the project will deliver risk mitigation, prevention, and some response through mobile teams, WGSS, and participatory mechanisms, as well as training and support to providers.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

In terms of protection risk analysis, the organization’s safety audits in project locations revealed that GBV is a dominant feature of the Syrian humanitarian crisis. Women and girls, especially the displaced, are subjected to increased denial of economic resources and education, movement restrictions, exploitation, forced and child marriage, intimate partner and family violence, and various forms of violence. The organization’s programming ensures the integration of gender, protection & inclusion across projects guided by the ‘Gender in Emergencies’ framework. The different needs and impacts for women, men, boys and girls are continuously assessed and interventions are tailored accordingly. Gender disaggregated data is collected measure impacts and changes. The organization also implements protection-focused activities that are designed to directly address gender & protection concerns through comprehensive GBV and general protection services.In terms of PSEAH strategy, the organization has a zero-tolerance approach toward sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse. The organization carefully examines allegations and investigates, and takes appropriate disciplinary action where needed, taking into consideration the rights and interests of the survivor, consistent with a ‘survivor-centered approach’. The organization has a robust Community-based Feedback and Response Mechanism to solicit feedback & receive complaints. Its CFRM procedures provide guidance on: (1) effective community consultation; (2) detailed steps on managing feedback and complaints; (3) Clear informed consent; and (4) Routing and management of sensitive complaints, including SEAH and safeguarding, as well as protection measures and investigation pathways. The mechanisms include both proactive and reactive channels. Both staff & BNFs are sensitized on the available channels.In terms of risk analysis and mitigation measures, the organization is well-positioned to undertake this project due to its strong presence in the target communities, and local and global expertise in the core program areas. This relies on several critical assumptions: • Security situation allows access to beneficiary populations in the targeted areas • HAO, local councils and community leaders provide approvals in a timely manner • Local markets/prices remain stable enough for procurement and delivery of project supplies/materials • Local community continue to accept the organization’s work in the targeted geographic areas • The organization maintains strong relationships and trust with host and IDP communities • The organization is able to develop and deliver programming in such a way that attention to women and girls does not result in backlash among men • Adequate funding is secured • The organization maintains a risk register for all its programs to identify and mitigate risks from the project’s onset. The risk register is reviewed and updated throughout the project implementation.
