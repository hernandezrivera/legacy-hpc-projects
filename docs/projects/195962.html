Project Summary

"The project seeks to maintain access to essential healthcare that addresses the main causes of mortality and morbidity amongst Rohingya and host community beneficiaries, as aligned to the Health Sector Strategic Objectives and articulated in the strategic plan 2023-2024. Health facilities supported will comprise of 39 facilities (14 PHCCs, 19 Health Post, 3 hospitals and 2 SARI ITC/IDTC), the range of services provided at this facilities will be standardized and aligned to the Minimum Package of Essential Health Services for primary health care including outpatient curative care, routine immunization, essential non-communicable disease interventions, comprehensive integrated sexual and reproductive health services, GBV prevention and response services (CMR).
The project will support emergency referral system in line with health sector referral SOP, 14 ambulances will support referrals to secondary hospitals and tertiary hospitals. This referral system will also extend to other health sector partners that may not have adequate referral capacity. Additionally, the project will support 3 Secondary hospitals (2 Health complexes and Friendship maternity center) to ensure referred patients’ access 24/7 CEMONC services, surgical care for trauma, imaging and diagnostic services and psychiatric services.
All health facilities will be staffed with appropriate human resources. Essential medicines, medical equipment and supplies will be made available. Emergency preparedness and response will be put in place; Surveillance tools including (EWARS) will be adopted in order to detect and investigate diseases with outbreak potential and trigger the necessary response actions for the comprehensive management of communicable diseases (such as AWD, Diphtheria, COVID-19 and Dengue). Additionally, Mobile medical teams will be on standby to support emergency response during cyclone, monsoon and fires.
The project will have special focus on quality integrated comprehensive Sexual and reproductive health services including ANC, PNC, Facility Based Deliveries, Menstrual Regulation and Post Abortion Care, Syndromic Management of STIs, treatment and referral of Obstetric and Gynecological complications across supported facilities. Key emphasis will be placed on Adolescent group with integration of Adolescent Friendly Sexual and Reproductive Health (ASRH) services across supported facilities. In line with the Family Planning (FP) Strategy 2021-2023, the project will provide modern FP Counselling and Services with emphasis on long acting and reversible methods. GBV prevention and response services will be integrated in supported health facilities including Clinical Management of Rape (CMR), Intimate Partner Violence (IPV) and other forms of GBV, referral for psychosocial support and services within established referral pathways.
IOM will continue to work with existing network of 400 community health workers (CHWs) to improve general health status, health seeking behavior and linking community members to health facilities through promotive preventive outreach health activities. Community engagement sessions will be conducted with key stakeholders including CIC, Madjis, and actively engage men and boys in promoting SRH rights for women and girls.
Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) will be integrated into essential health services including assessment, counselling, support to persons with moderate to severe mental health conditions and referrals to specialized services provided by psychiatrists. At the community level, MHPSS services will be through evidence-based interventions including support groups, socio-relational activities, creative and art activities, ritual and celebration activities, sport and play activities and self-awareness sessions as per the IOM community based MHPSS approach. In mainstreaming MHPSS considerations into humanitarian responses, the team will be supporting MHPSS WG coordination and capacity building of partners

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"In 2022, As estimated 909,282 refugees are registered in Bangladesh and reside in 33 extremely congested camps in Cox’s Bazaar and on Basan char Island (UNHCR, 2022). The refugees have increased health-related vulnerabilities due to the limited access to comprehensive health care services, poor living conditions and increased risk of communicable diseases. According to (J-MSNA 2021), 55% of Refugee households reported needing to access health care, of this, 9% did not have access to health care when they needed to. As of 31st August 2022, the monthly average of outpatient consultations in 49 Primary, secondary and COVID-19 health facilities is 956,211, an increase of 34% compared to the same period in 2021 indicating higher health facility utilization. Immunization coverage is low with Penta 3 (68%) and MR 2 (37%), this is attributed to sub-optimal community engagement, mobilization and risk communication. Timely access to secondary and tertiary care is vital in saving lives, IOM has been a lead agency for emergency medical referral with 14 ambulances and extending referral support to other health sector partners. In the Rohingya refugee camps, over (52%) of the population are women and girls with (25%) being women of reproductive age (UNHCR, March 2021). As per 2021 SRHWG annual bulletin: there was (47.5%) increase in expectant mothers attending four or more ANC visits compared to 2020, facility based deliveries increased from (58%) in 2020 to (70%) in 2021 (CHW working group), However, Rohingya refugee girls (5.4%) are still more likely to give birth before the age of 18yrs compared to (2.8%) within host communities. Facility maternal deaths reported increased from 16 in 2020 to 23 in 2021 (MPMSR report). COVID 19 exacerbated existing conditions for intimate partner violence (IPV) in the Rohingya refugee camp contexts among other factors. Vulnerable groups including adolescents and survivors of SGBV and IPV faced barriers to accessing adequate care and referrals. Special populations groups including persons with diverse gender and sexual orientation face a variety of different risk factors to sexual violence and additional barriers to accessing essential health services. A recent study titled ‘MaBoinor Rosom’ highlighted cultural and religious beliefs, practices and male dominance that hinders access to basic SRH services for women and girls of reproductive age.COVID-19 is still a concern with the threat of new variants and unpredictable trends. As of 17 July 2022, there were 30,093 confirmed cases in Cox’s Bazar District, of which 6,067 were amongst the Rohingya refugees and 24,026 were amongst host communities (WHO, 2022). Additionally, an upsurge of dengue, diphtheria and acute watery diarrhea in the camps and adjacent host communities were reported in 2021 and 2022. Out of the total 6,375 dengue cases reported through the WHO Early Warning Alert and Response System (EWARS) in Cox’s Bazar, 5,180 cases were reported from IOM facilities (WHO, 2022; IOM 2022).IOM‘s comprehensive 2021 Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) needs assessment in Cox’s Bazar revealed that the three main MHPSS difficulties experienced by Rohingya community are “feeling tension or nervousness” (37.86 per cent), “feeling tired or having little energy” (34.58 per cent), and “little interest or pleasure in doing things” (31.51 per cent). The community faces adversities, such as fire incidents, heavy monsoon rains, cyclone, and the COVID-19 pandemic that compound their vulnerabilities. Other stressors contributing to mental health difficulties including difficult living conditions in the camp and insufficient access to basic needs. These conditions underscore the need for multi-layered MHPSS activities to ensure the accessibility of services to everyone, promote mental health and psychosocial wellbeing, and strengthen the resilience of communities."

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project adopted health sector August 2022 health facility rationalization as a primary consideration to prioritized availability of essential healthcare services for Rohingya refugees in the camps and host community. All services in the Essential Service Package of the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoHFW), Bangladesh and in reference to the Minimum Package of Essential Health Services for Primary Health care (PHC) Facilities in the Refugee Camps will be made available. An integrated model of health services including nutrition (Systematic detection of acute malnutrition among children 6 to 59 months and PLWs and referral, IYCF, growth monitoring, deworming and Vitamin A supplementation) will be adopted. PHC facilities will provide promotive, preventive and curative services as well as access to secondary and tertiary referrals through ambulance referral. Community level health promotion and prevention services will be implemented through community health workers linked to primary health care facilities. Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) will be integrated into essential health services including referrals for moderate to severe mental health conditions to psychiatrists at health complexes. At the community level, MHPSS services will be implemented through support groups, socio-relational activities, creative and art activities, ritual and celebration activities, sport and play activities and self-awareness sessions. The project will strengthen preparedness and response capacities for health emergencies/disasters (such as cyclones, monsoons and fires) and communicable disease with outbreak potential (such as acute watery diarrhea- AWD, and COVID-19). Currently due to the ongoing upsurge of diphtheria & dengue,part of SARI ITC are temporarily repurposed to support case management of dengue & diphtheria & establish the SARI ITCs into multi-purpose IDTC to respond to outbreaks/communicable diseases & ensure continuity & sustainability.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Project will incorporate protection principles & promote meaningful access,safety & dignity in health response.This will include adaptation of health facilities infrastructure such as gender & disability friendly latrine,use of ramps & railings,handle grip for older people & persons with disabilities so all individuals can access and use health facilities in safety and dignity. In line with Integrated triage assessment and isolation guideline, IOM will ensure that waiting areas are organized with appropriate space, and ventilation, and provide clear guidelines on how the queues form (give priority to vulnerable individuals including persons with emergency health conditions,older persons, PWD.To avoid causing harm,Standard health care waste management measures will be adopted including colour coded bin and incineration to reduces incidences of health care associated infections in patients,training on PSEA rules & code of Conduct will be provided to all IOM staff & those working with IOM

3.2 - Gender

"The project will deploy qualified medical and support staff including female health staff members with skills and experience working with women, train them on guiding principles of GBV prevention, response and referral pathways. Where possible IOM health facilities will ensure male and female disaggregated waiting area, consultation rooms and that refugees can make their preferences known about the sex of the staff assisting them. If requests are not able to be met, this is explained properly and, if possible, make alternate arrangements to meet the request. All health facilities are well equipped with the necessary job aides/ IEC materials and updated camp specific GBV referral pathways, medical commodities include supplies for clinical management for survivors of sexual assault. The project will strengthen referral linkages between GBV-SRH-MHPSS service providers. The project ensure gender disaggregated data is collected, analyzed and interpreted routinely to understand the coverage

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

Data from IOM health Programme will be disaggregated by age,analyzed & interpreted routinely to understand coverage,outcome & impact of health response on different age groups for purpose of programming.Ensure children can access health facilities safely with or without caregiver & that health facilities have space for children to wait in child-friendly environment.Linkages with Camp Child protection FP will be made to support engagement with children when without caregiver.Project will ensure supported health facilities are accessible to elderly & PWD.Promote initiatives to transport elderly & PWD to health facilities,widen entrances,improve signage,& facilitate movement. Physical barriers that inhibit ability of elderly & persons using an assistive device for mobility will be removed in all health facilities.Key emphasis will be placed on Adolescent group with integration of Adolescent Friendly Sexual and Reproductive Health (ASRH) services across supported facilities.

3.4 - Diversity

Data collected by IOM health Programme will consider gender diverse population for the purpose of programming. The project will ensure that other vulnerable individuals including Gender Diverse Population are able to access facilities safely with confidentiality and build the capacity of health staff members to enhance their skills in working with vulnerable individuals including Gender Diverse Population. Establish and promote inclusive and safe complain, feedback and response system with due consideration for additional confidentiality needs for gender diverse population.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Health program works through network of community health workers,volunteers & FPs.These networks will act as primary communication channels between healthcare facilities and beneficiaries.Monthly community health facility group (CHFG) meeting is conducted to provide for beneficiaries’ involvement in health facilities & increase health facilities responsiveness to local needs & improve beneficiaries’ awareness of available services.Feedback & complaints mechanism,through IOM CwC unit,actively seeks views of beneficiaries from commuunity(e.g. men, women, girls, boys, older people and persons with disabilities) and share their feedback with health program team. IOM health teams receive, process, respond to all the complain, provide feedback to the communities and learn from beneficiaries complains about the services. The health program conducts facility-based patient satisfaction survey to improve understanding of patient satisfaction and improve service delivery.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, These projects will continue to support environmental friendly health system through electrification of health care facilities with solar energy to ensure access to quality essential services for women and children, promote the uptake of health care services, cut health care costs, all the while protecting our climate. Solar power to provide enough electricity to power entire health facilities – from medical laboratory and refrigerators for vaccines, to incubators, lighting and autoclaving.
