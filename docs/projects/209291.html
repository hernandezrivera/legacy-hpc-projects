Project Summary

The project aims to facilitate access to coordinated and responsive services to displacement affected communities, where internally displaced persons (IDPs) will inclusively participate in the decision making, implementation, and monitoring processes for planned multi-sectoral assistance within displacement sites. The project will target approximately 49,121 individuals (9,905 Households (HHs)) in IDP sites and site- like settings out of approximately 953,546 individuals (190,709 HHs) in Gedaref and Kassala states.
The project will establish, or actively contribute to site-level coordination mechanisms to support humanitarian response, with strong emphasis on using this forum to raise joint advocacy on protection risk and concerns of the community, such as forced evictions, support access to services for the displaced, reduce duplication of services, and improve coverage and efficiency of the response. Site-level coordination will also ensure mainstreaming of protection by strengthening community representation mechanisms through increased awareness raising, participation, and empowerment of the affected population; as well as the establishment of more robust community feedback mechanisms that foster accountability to the affected population. IOM will use DTM data from IDP gathering sites and profiling as well as the Cluster prioritization for an area-based approach and work closely with the Cluster leads, partners, and with the community and local authorities to ensure that overall support for the emergency response is available for the target locations. The project will include site management and mapping using an area-based approach to identify the needs and gaps and support with improving cluster coordination and referrals to other clusters such as WASH, Health, Protection, and specialized service providers for additional support. The project will also include trainings on site management for local authorities and site management committees (SMC), dissemination of key messaging on service provision, and support to committees living in gathering sites to improve services. In consultation with SMCs and community leaders, IOM will support mapping site management improvement activities through safety audits, complaint and feedback mechanisms and observations/recommendations to improve the environment and increase the quality of living conditions for site residents and ensure that basic standards are met. IOM will support the SMC to develop site improvement recommendations and planning focused on preparedness activities such as digging of drainage systems, sandbag filling, risk mapping, building or reinforcement of communal infrastructures, and support to site safety committees. The project will also focus on mainstreaming protection in care and maintenance activities recommending that the site management and other clusters provide additional support such as proper lighting, fencing, and demarcation when required. The site management project will assess the specific needs and ensure support for all groups of IDPs including them in the planning and implementation of the relevant site management activities (i.e., composition of the SMCs will include men, women, youth and members from underrepresented groups).

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

In the current context, IOM will be prioritizing the use of Communicating with Communities (CwC) to ensure that there is a continuous process of listening, responding, and adapting programs based on the needs of the community and factor in conflict sensitivity. The site management programming will have diverse community committees inclusive of men, women, and persons with special needs, youth, elderly, underrepresented groups at the forefront actively planning, designing, and monitoring the site improvement works in coordination with assigned community focal points and humanitarian actors. The project will receive and utilize community feedback through established communication channels including site level coordination meetings with humanitarian actors, meetings with site management committees, community engagement forums, and focus group discussions, individual and household surveys at critical times (especially the design stage, early on in implementation, and after action) to have a full spectrum of feedback. There will be a complaints desk set up for continuous communication with the community to ensure feedback is collected. The complaint desks will also support follow-up with communities to ensure at the very least that referrals are made to relevant partners and where possible concerns are addressed directly, to close the feedback loop. Representative structures are critical to understanding the perspectives and needs of different groups in the communities. The project will strengthen these structures through capacity building and other support to enhance two-way communication and empower the selected and or appointed committees to be more self-sufficient and seek synergies with each other to strengthen the area-based approach. Community feedback will be documented in results monitoring frameworks, activity and field visit reports, surveys analyses and actively referred to throughout the project. The feedback received including best practices and lessons learnt will be incorporated during regular project review exercises, to improve the quality of the ongoing work, adapt to emerging needs and changes, or improve planning of future programming.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

IOM will continue strengthening existing referral pathways to facilitate multi-partner and multisector referral at the site level, leveraging on relevant inter-agency coordination forums and community complaint feedback mechanisms to facilitate two-way communications with partners and project participants. Through these coordination and community mechanisms, the project will support protection partners in ensuring safe and accessible communication channels are available to the project participants, stakeholders, and partners to facilitate complaints, including on SEA. Where there are no active protection partners at the site level, the project will facilitate joint advocacy through the Protection Cluster to establish linkages with protection services and referral pathways. Beneficiary feedback will be collected periodically at three critical stages of the provision of assistance: (1) During assessments information will be collected about the selection processes and additional beneficiary needs. (2) During main activities such as awareness sessions where key informant interviews and complaint desks will be set up for beneficiaries to make inquiries and provide feedback. (3) During monitoring and evaluation missions, where key members of the community committees and 30-50% sample size of the participating population will be selected for questionnaires about the support provided. There will be follow-ups at each of these stages to ascertain if beneficiary complaints were addressed either by IOM or by sector partners. IOM has a zero-tolerance policy on SEA, which is in line with the UN policy, and has institutional guidelines on PSEA and reporting mechanism in case of suspected SEA as well as guidelines from IOM's Institutional Framework for Addressing GBV in crises. IOM staff will receive refresher training on how to report SEA and ethical and appropriate ways to handle a GBV disclosure and make safe referrals through existing referral pathways. These guidelines will also be included and highlighted to any implementing partners to ensure compliance.
