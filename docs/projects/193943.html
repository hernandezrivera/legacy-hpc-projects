Project Summary

Driven and facilitated by community-based, women-led teams in Hebron and Bethlehem governates in previous project by DanChurchAid/Norwegian Church Aid’s (DCA/NCA) and the local organization, the East Jerusalem Young Men’s Christian Association (EJ-YMCA.) The overall project objective is to enhance vulnerable women´s economic coping capacity to overcome the impacts of occupation and socio-economic situation. The implementing partner EJ-YMCA will work closely with established Protection Groups to select and support 96 vulnerable women and their families (451 individual beneficiaries) through unrestricted, multipurpose cash grants that can help beneficiaries in any way they need. While the entry point for identifying target women is their socioeconomic situation, the project’s impact encompasses women with different sensitive and vulnerable living circumstances, of different societal groups and different age groups, and their families including, but not limited to, women headed households, widows, women with disabilities or have disabilities within the family and chronic disease, and elderly women.
The EJ-YMCA will work through a highly participatory approach centered around collaboration with established Protection Groups (PGs) in 14 communities. The project relies on a participatory approach in its design and implementation. Key elements of our project approach are:
A Survivor Community-Led Response (SCLR) method
The SCLR approach puts communities in the driver’s seat when identifying their own needs, deciding on priorities, planning, and implementing actions to respond to needs identified.
Results from previous projects showed that the SCLR approach is effective in enabling communities to identify and address protection and development needs; it contributes to resilience, strengthens cooperation between local community members, and enhances the ownership.
The proposed project will be work with the already established Protection Groups in the two governorates of Hebron and Bethlehem.
A community Protection Group (PG) is an informal structure that consists of a group of women, young women, men, and young men from the community. PGs were formed through previous projects supported by DCA/NCA. The SCLR approach has equipped the PGs with the necessary skills and preparedness to facilitate, organize, and coordinate any efforts within the community. The proposed project will further empower the PGs as they will play a key role the implementation of the proposed project as they are the connection between communities, DCA/NCA and its partner EJ-YMCA.
The Protection Groups (PGs)
In this project each PG will nominate 3 women and they will act with the EJ-YMCA field teams to reach out to the vulnerable women within their communities. The PG will follow the principles of neutrality and impartiality will nominate three representative women members to map women’s needs in their communities for the vulnerable targeted women, based on their first-hand knowledge of the socioeconomic situation and vulnerability of women in their communities, develop the localized selection criteria, assist the EJ-YMCA project field team collect data, and in documenting the action/process.
Community sensitization With the project relying on nominated PG women, it seeks to promote women as active, capable, resilient participants in their communities. An important element of enhancing community sensitization to the important role of women, is to support the women themselves in making the choice to undertake and commit to responsibilities that go beyond the family sphere and are at community level. The project seeks to enhance their confidence in decision-making and highlight this within their communities. These discussions seek as well to add transparency, accountability, and responsiveness to the PG women’s role and enhance visibility in the communities.

1 - Needs

The project targets 14 communities in the Hebron and Bethlehem governorates of the West Bank that DCA/NCA´s partner EJ-YMCA WDP already operates in. These communities are primarily located in Area C, where Israel retains nearly exclusive control, including over law enforcement, planning, and construction. Most of Area C has been allocated for the benefit of Israeli settlements or the Israeli military, at the expense of the wellbeing of the Palestinian communities. This impedes the development of adequate housing, infrastructure, and livelihoods in Palestinian communities. Structures built without permits are regularly served with demolition orders, creating chronic uncertainty and threat, and encouraging people to leave. Where the orders were implemented, they resulted in displacement and disruption of livelihoods, the entrenchment of poverty, and increased aid dependency. Israel further imposes restrictions on movement, which affect community members’ access to education, healthcare, financial institutions, and job opportunities. The 14 communities that are targeted are deemed vulnerable as these communities experience a lot of the everyday difficulties. Based on DCA/NCA and YMCA experience in MPCA project in 2022 the benefits of MPCA were greatly seen all livelihood and coping strategies index have decreased. As an example, Families selling some assets such as furniture, gold or livestock in order to cope with financial stress has decreased by 70.1%, spending savings has decreased by 31.2%, purchasing food needs with debt has also decreased by 31.2%, selling a house or a plot of landdecreasedby81.8%, withdrawing children from school in order to work decreased by 40.9% and marrying girls off to ease the economic burden has decreased in 6.5%. An example of these communities is Bet Mirsim, the village is subject to flying checkpoints. There is also a bypass road within the village. The Israeli Forces has confiscated 220 dunums of land. As a result of these activates in the village and surrounding it, there has been a great amount of pollution in water supply. The people have also experienced difficulties in accessing health centers or hospitals. Sometimes, farmers find it difficult to pick their fruit trees as the Israeli Forces have cut and destroyed the olive and almonds trees, and closed the agricultural roads in the village. Approximately eight families in the village have had their agricultural product damaged. The educational sector also suffers from the Israeli activities, students from the neighboring villages must walk about two kilometers to reach the school in the village. 11 harvesting wells have also been destroyed by the Israeli Forces. The Segregation Wall surrounds the village from three sides, the north, west and south. About 400 olive, almonds and forest trees were destroyed while constructing the Wall. Five houses and five establishments were also demolished to construct the wall. As a result of the impact of the occupation on the access of Palestinians to their land/livelihood and basic needs a survey conducted by Birzeit University in 2019 report that the main sources of income are unstable and insecure, with less than one-quarter of households relying on the stability of a regular salary for their primary income. Episodes of physical and verbal assault, stone-throwing, damage to Palestinian property, and vandalizing cars, houses, and agricultural property added to routinely heavy-handed operations by the Israeli military and to the mental stress and anxiety of thousands of people and communities in Area C. In addition, according to the humanitarian needs overview (HNO) for 2022, The restrictive and discriminatory planning regime applied by Israel in Area C and in East Jerusalem, which continues to prevent Palestinians from addressing basic housing, livelihood and basic service needs. In the Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) that was carried out in July 2022, in the West Bank, “households reported difficulties meeting basic needs or accessing essential services (including healthcare or education services), financial barriers (related to high costs and being unable to afford expenses) were among the most frequently provided reasons. Among the 81.4% of households with a member with a disability reporting a healthcare need in the 3 months prior to data collection, 95.6% reported having experienced a barrier to care, with the most frequently reported barrier being that cost of services were too high (71.2%). 6.8% of households with a member with a disability had taken on debt for healthcare related reasons” In addition, according to the assessment, assistants from others were the primary income source in certain communities and is seen more at a female-headed households, as its 34.5% of female headed households compared to 6.6% of male-headed households.

2 - Activities

The project will target 96 vulnerable women and their families from the West Bank in Hebron and Bethlehem governates. While the entry point for identifying target women is their socioeconomic situation, the project’s impact encompasses women with different sensitive and vulnerable living circumstances. The targeted families will be selected based on clear criteria and scoring system, including their socioeconomic vulnerabilities and of a Vulnerability Assessment Tool that DCA/NCA has. YMCA’s field staff will conduct home visits to complete the needed information before analyzing the data, scoring, and selecting beneficiaries. DCA/NCA staff and YMCA will conduct verifications for a random sample of the beneficiaries to ensure they are selected based on the agreed upon vulnerability criteria. Furthermore, the PG will be consulted to endorse the qualified list of beneficiaries. DCA/NCA and YMCA will ensure that the qualified beneficiaries have not received other assistance from other organizations and/or the governmental social protection schemesThe targeted women and their families will be selected based on their socioeconomic vulnerabilities. Criteria will include vulnerable women, of different societal groups and different age groups, and their families including, but not limited to, women headed households, GBV survivors, women who lost their source of income due to deteriorating economic situation, widows, women with disabilities or family member with disability and chronic disease, and elderly women. The PG women will contextualize and expand upon the selection criteria in each community. Each case will be looked into to see what cases need to be prioritized depending on the socio economic situation of the women, and the vulnerability. As an example, if there are two women with disability then the socio economic situation of the Household will be looked at as well to see who needs to prioritized. The targeted women live in an estimated number of 14 rural communities in the Hebron and Bethlehem governorates (Area C, including community members in Area B whose livelihoods are in Area C). The MPCA modality will allow beneficiaries to address their basic needs and priorities as they deem relevant. Time and locations of cash distribution will be identified based on consultation with target group, women and people with disabilities in particular. Cash distribution facilities (banks) will be ensured to be easy to access for people with disabilities. Cash distribution modality through banks, in general, implies less direct contact between humanitarian staff who provide the service and beneficiaries which is considered as a risk mitigation measure for SEA. The proposed project will include the following activities: 1. Unconditional Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance: 96 women and their families (451 individuals in totals) over four months (one payment per month). Each Household will receive 1190 based on the MEB identified by the West Bank Cash Working Group. In case of women who are not be able to receive MPCA (i.e., due to accessibility issue is relation to people with disabilities, women do not have control over the use of cash/can be subjected to violence due to receiving cash), the project will consider other modality for cash disbursement or by referring the beneficiaries to other modalities to address the mentioned groups’ needs 2. Each woman nominated by the PG will be given a one-time payment of approximately 325 Shekel to cover basic costs like transportation and communication. The PG women will enhance the accountability of the project by posting general information about the activities on their already established Facebook group pages, while ensuring do no harm principles and reserving women dignity and privacy. 3. The project will include a complementary component to promote the capacity of women in the Protection groups. Community sensitization with the project relying on nominated PG women, it seeks to promote women as active, capable, resilient participants in their communities. An important element of enhancing community sensitization to the important role of women, is to support the women themselves in making the choice to undertake and commit to responsibilities that go beyond the family sphere and are at community level. By providing this opportunity with this project, where PG women will lead consultations on needs with the community stakeholders and make decisions based on this for the implementation of the multi-purpose cash component. In this way, the project seeks to enhance their confidence in decision-making and highlight this within their communities. These discussions seek as well to add transparency, accountability, and responsiveness to the PG women’s role and enhance visibility in the communities. 4.In addition, DCA/NCA and YMCA-EJ field team will carry out the monitoring of the project, monthly post distribution monitoring survey and data collection by PG women and EJ-YMCA staff. In addition, a Baseline/ end line study will be carried out as well. DCA/NCA and EJ-YMCA will ensure that the qualified beneficiaries have not received other assistance from other organizations and/or the governmental social protection schemes. Further coordination with CWG, and other relevant actors for beneficiary selection will be ensured during the implementation to avoid duplication.DCA/NCA will conduct a PSEA session with its partner’s staff at the beginning of the project and will ensure that a plan is put in place on how the project will raise awareness about PSEA among their targeted communities and considering safe and accessible complaint systems. DCA/NCA and its partner will develop and disseminate awareness raising materials including messages about PSEA and the available complaints mechanisms and their entry points.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

Multipurpose Cash Assistance (MPCA): will address the multisectoral needs of the women and their families, bolster women’s social protection and provide a pathway for independence for women. The MPCA transfer value was developed by the West Bank Cash Working Group. The cash will be given through bank transfers, each woman goes and collects the money from the bank. This method was used for security and safety measures including safety for SEA as it will remove direct interaction between the field team and the beneficiaries. The women will go to the bank provide their name and ID and the teller at the bank will provide the money to them and they sign a document that they have received it. Each month post distribution monitoring survey will be carried out to assess how beneficiaries use the cash and address their satisfaction, where corrective measures can be taken accordingly. More than one modality can be considered regarding accessibility and safety of targeted women, i.e restriction of cash, or paper vouchers. For these cases, a steering committee consisting of DCA/NCA, and EJ-YMCA Cash Focal Point, will discuss case by case to decide on the best modality to provide cash to enable targeted women to utilize the cash assistance in the best way for them. With regards to people with disability, transportation will be provided to them to pick up that cash from the bank and to assist them in purchasing. PSEA messaging will be shared with beneficiaries during post distribution monitoring surveys throughme.complaints@dca.dk

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

DCA/NCA will support partner to increase the capability of the complaints and feedback mechanism to inform decision-making, enhance accountability and transparency. This will be ensured through engaging beneficiaries in addressing the expected risks and challenges and empowering them to participate in the monitoring and evaluation process of the project. Beneficiaries will have access to both DCA/NCA and its partner complaint mechanism. DCA/NCA and its partner will consider the beneficiaries feedback and recommendation through direct contact and consultation with them. The complaint mechanism will provide the chance for all beneficiaries especially women and people with disabilities to express their concerns freely and provide their feedback on the delivery of project activities through web site, hotline, complaints boxes, directly to the fi eld monitors, and directly to partner or DCA/NCA offices. The applied complaint mechanism considers specific standard operational procedures in case of gender sensitive complaints. The received complaints and feedback will be documented and analyzed by the responsible and relevant focal point at either YMCA or DCA/NCA to feed into the decision- making process and inform possible corrections during implementation that ensures a better response to the needs of the vulnerable groups. Community engagement committees will be formed to build community confidence and networks and ensure efficient and continuous participation of the community in the implementation and monitoring processes and to inform decision making. The project will organize regular focus group discussions with the community members to collect feedback on the provided services. The collected information will be used to inform decision making by searching for possible areas of improvement and revisiting the implementation approaches and tools.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

DCA/NCA have strong experience in responding to humanitarian crises, with proven capacities to provide responsive, efficient, and accountable services to the affected population. This has included testing of the SCLR approach (survivor and community led response), resilience approaches and promoting the know how in programming multi-sectoral interventions, together with local partners. This is to materialize localization, the grand bargain commitments and to promote successful practices of nexus. The fundamental premise of the approach is to respect and reserve the dignity of people in conflict and crisis situations. Since 2015, the EJ-YMCA WDP has been working in community led approaches and providing community cash grant schemes to help communities design and implement appropriate responses to the risks they face in their communities, including previous work with PG´s on individual. The project design recognizes the rights and needs of beneficiaries in terms of safety, security, and dignity and proposes activities that foster the realization of these needs and rights.DCA/NCA and its partner use culturally responsive approach which involves respect for the cultural backgrounds of all right holders, particularly women and people with disability. DCA/NCA and partners apply Do No Harm (DNH) approach that is integrated in the project at all levels. Both partners are trained and aware of the approach which is part of the human rights-based approach adopted by DCA/NCA and YMCA-EJ WDP. Confidentially of the most vulnerable is a key element and carried out across the project period. As an example, the cash will be given through bank transfers, where each woman goes and collects the money from the bank. In cases where there is a risk that women will not have the authority to use the cash as they anticipate, a steering committee consisting of DCA/NCA, EJ-YMCA Cash Focal Point, will discuss case by case to decide on the best modality to provide cash to enable targeted women to utilize the cash assistance in the best way for them. The monitoring process will include PDM that will be carried out by the PG and YMCA, regular field visits by YMCA and field visits and random calls to check if beneficiaries are satisfied with the services provided to them and that they are treated with dignity and respect by DCA/NCA. The women in the PG will also follow up with the women regularly if they are satisfied, if they have any issues or concern and will inform YMCA if any issues arising. DCA/NCA implements a code of conduct that is signed by all its staff that reflects among others the values and standards regarding PSEA. DCA/NCA ensures that the cooperation agreement with implementing partners consists of an article that requires its partner to have an internal staff code of conduct in place that reflects as a minimum the values and standards of partners regarding PSEA. • DCA/NCA will ensure that the target groups and communities have an accessible, a confidential and a safe complaint handling mechanisms during the implementation of the project, where the sensitive complaints are dealt with and acted upon with confidentiality (either by using DCA’/NCA’s already functioning complaints mechanism: me.complaints@dca.dkand/ or using the partner complaints mechanism channels mobile number +970562610061, or complaints@ej-ymca.org– • DCA/NCA will conduct a PSEA session to its and partner staff at the beginning of the project and will ensure that a plan is put in place on how the partners will raise awareness about PSEA among their targeted communities •DCA/NCA staff will monitor closely the practices of partners to reinforce the PSEA and ensure that the targeted communities are aware of PSEA and the available functional complaints mechanism. • DCA/NCA’s is an active member in the oPt-HCT-PSEA Network and will continuously update its partner with relevant and needed PSEA information especially on available referral pathways for sensitive complaints.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The response will be directly implemented by DCA/NCA and its local partner the YMCA-EJ WDP in the West Bank. DCA/NCA will conduct regular visits to the response areas, following up on the indicators and response plan, and assessing response implementation via discussions with the response team and rights holders and by visiting the response sites. DCA\NCA with its partner will ensure equal and impartial access to services as follow:- DCA\NCA access approach is consistent with the humanitarian core principles (impartiality, neutrality, independence, and humanity) in accordance with IHL. Partners apply: 1) Humanity in addressing the human suffering of most vulnerable women; 2) Neutrality so that humanitarian aid must not favor someone over the other; 3) Impartiality in providing services to vulnerable women solely based on need and without discrimination; and 4) Independence in advancing the autonomy of humanitarian project objectives from political, economic, or other objectives. The project provides multipurpose cash to vulnerable women and their family for them to utilize the cash assistance in the best way for them. While the entry point for identifying target women is their socioeconomic situation, the project’s impact encompasses women with different sensitive and vulnerable living circumstances. The planned intervention will focus on 96 vulnerable women, of different societal groups and different age groups, and their families including, but not limited to, women headed households, GBV survivors, widows, women with disabilities and chronic disease, and elderly women. As these communities have already an established Protection Groups these Protection Groups are aware of who in these communities are in need and if there is People with disabilities in the communities.The Women of the PG will create a list of the vulnerable women, this list is then sharedwith the field team of YMCA-EJ who will validate the information as they have prior knowledge of the communities since they work with them as well in previous projects. In addition, the YMCA field team will meet with the village council to receive their list as well and to make sure that the information they have is accurate and that they are not missing anybody in the community those needs fits the criteria.
