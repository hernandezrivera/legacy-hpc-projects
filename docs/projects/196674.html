Project Summary

Management, coordination and monitoring of activities, and deployment of professional staff are essential for UNRWA to deliver timely and effective humanitarian assistance, to ensure efficient coordination with relevant stakeholders and to enhance advocacy on behalf of Palestine refugees.
While the vast majority of posts needed to deliver the mandate of the Agency in Syria will continue to be filled by Palestine refugees and Syrian nationals, UNRWA will maintain some international capacity, particularly specialist staff in the fields of humanitarian response, monitoring and evaluation, and grants management.
UNRWA will continue to conduct safety and security trainings for its staff across Syria, UNRWA has made significant investments in the safety and security of its staff, assets and facilities despite financial challenges.
Finally, under this project, UNRWA will also sustain the operational needs of support departments, including HR, Administration, Procurement and Logistics. UNRWA has also included a budget for the minor repair and maintenance of UNRWA facilities to ensure safe and functional delivery of services for both beneficiaries and staff.

3 - Needs

Twelve years into the protracted Syrian crisis, its population continues to endure the cumulative effects of years of instability and a far-reaching economic meltdown. In 2022, according to the UN Special Envoy of the Secretary-General for Syria, intensified hostilities were witnessed in the northwest, northeast and south of the country with an increase in strikes and shelling causing civilian death and injury.The estimated 438,000 Palestine refugees remaining in Syria, who were already among the most vulnerable groups in the country, are continuously pushed further into vulnerability and poverty and will remain highly dependent on the assistance provided by UNRWA.It is imperative for UNRWA to continue provide essential service to the Palestine refugee community in Syria and to ensure operations are adequately supported. Support for UNRWA operations continues to be key. Staffing, emergency repair and maintenance of UNRWA’s facilities, as well as investments in the safety of personnel and assets are a prerequisite for UNRWA to continue operating in Syria, and to deliver vital assistance to Palestinian refugees affected by the crisis. UNRWA has been assisting Palestine refugees within a volatile security environment over the past decade. Investment in experts, security and safety of staff, assets and facilities, as well as in its security procedures, is critical for reducing the risk faced by staff and beneficiaries while maintaining operational integrity and neutrality.

4 - Implementation of activities

The project activities include 1. Recruitment of local as well as international staff; 2.Procurement of security and safety equipment items; 3. Security enhancement of UNRWA’s facilities; 4. Building the capacity of staff on safety and security; 5. Maintenance and repair of UNRWA programme support and operational premises; 6. Covering the operational costs of support departments.The project aims at ensuring that UNRWA support departments are well equipped to provide support to the UNRWA implementing programmes, to ensure the efficient provision of service to PR and a secure and safe environment for UNRWA staff and beneficiaries. Maintained UNRWA installations will ensure the Agency’s ability to continue operate and provide assistance to Palestine Refugees. Management, coordination and technical staff are essential for UNRWA to deliver humanitarian assistance, to ensure efficient coordination and to enhance advocacy on behalf of Palestine refugees. Monitoring will be guaranteed through regular field visits and feedback from implementing staff based in the field and their respective programmes. UNRWA will continue to build staff capacity and to participate in humanitarian coordination mechanisms at country level, including the humanitarian sectors and the HCT.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Discrimination of vulnerable groups and risk of SEA for beneficiaries ofUNRWA services and assistance • Likelihood: medium • Impact: high • MitigationMeasures: 1. UNRWA staff prioritize vulnerable groups such as women, olderpersons and persons with disabilities in order to reduce their waiting time. 2.UNRWA provides training on protection mainstreaming and disability inclusion. 3.UNRWA is conducting protection audits on a biennial basis to get feedback fromthe staff and the community on how to improve its services and how to optimizeits compliance to the main protection principles. 4. All UNRWA staff sign the codeof conduct on commencement of their contracts. 5. Training to staff on the code ofconduct and PSEA and regular reinforcement of the zero-tolerance policy as well asUNRWA staff standards of conduct, reporting obligations and channels for SEA. 6.Reinforced messaging to community members on the importance of reporting SEAand ensure mechanisms are in place to ensure conﬁdentiality and set up accessibleand efficient complaint mechanisms. 7. UNRWA is enhancing focus on communityengagement and participation, and makes sure feedback is responded to.

Security situation andactive violence do not deteriorate any further. 4. COVID-19 pandemic challenges remainwithin the scope of the Agency's capacity to respond financially and operationally. RiskUNRWA is unable to continue providing services and respond to PR needs because of lackof funding or due to increased humanitarian needs. Last year, UNRWA received onlyaround 30% of its requirements for Syria. Mitigation: 1. continue to monitor the situationand to adjust plans prioritizing most pressing needs 2. Regularly engage with donors andpartners to leverage funding opportunities 3. Ensure robust financial, planning andmanagement processes 4. Maintain communication with stakeholders on status offunding. 5. Continue to monitor interventions through results-based monitoringframework. Risk UNRWA staff and PR are exposed to violence and security threats.Mitigation: 1. Monitor security updates and conduct security assessments for facilitiesand missions 2. Train staff and beneficiaries on ERW, and personal safety. Risk: COVID-19pandemic situation persists/worsens, leading to further operational challenges.Mitigation: 1. UNRWA will adapt its modality to ensure sustained delivery ofservices while prioritizing the safety of staff and beneficiaries 2. PPEs and sanitization mate will be provided to frontline staff.
