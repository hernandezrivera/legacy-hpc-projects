Project Summary

The purpose of the project is to improve food security, economic and social well-being of vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host families in the target area.
NRC Cameroon’s Livelihood and Food Security (LFS) programme will target displacement-affected people based on vulnerability criteria and not their displacement status only (IDPs, returnees (former IDPs) and host communities) will be targeted. NRC will apply social and economic vulnerability criteria to identify vulnerable households most at risk to food insecurity. Priority will be given to female-headed households, child-headed households; pregnant and breastfeeding women; elderly people (60 and above); households with a person with physical or mental disability or debilitating chronic disease. For the economic vulnerability criteria, NRC will use as a reference: poor or limited food consumption score; no food stock, number of meals per day, no productive assets, and loss of income. For agricultural restoration activities, NRC will also include the following criteria: having at least one able-bodied person in the household who can farm land. NRC’s LFS programmes will also target as far as possible vulnerable youth and women for vocational training and economic empowerment.
To address the intermediate needs of affected populations NRC will use cash, e-vouchers or in-kind assistance based on the results of market analysis and cash feasibility studies. To maximise the chances of success in this activity, NRC will as far as possible couple livelihoods interventions with food assistance to meet basic food needs. Moreover, NRC will implement complementary activities such as training and awareness raising sessions to support target households. Programme activities will gradually scale down and phase out so that by the end of the projects, communities have the skills and resources needed to be more resilient to shocks and crises in the medium and long term. NRC will work in coordination with local partners such as local technical and administrative structures and will collaborate closely with them so they can provide technical support during programme implementation and continued follow-up after the programme ends.
The LFS programme will include Cash/voucher assistance, livelihoods promotion and enhancement applying a market-based approach (MBA) that refers to local traders so to improve access to income and revive/strengthen the local economy. The intervention will be geared towards supporting the most vulnerable displacement-affected households and at the same time building their capacity in food production, Livestock production and Income generating activities.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Violence continues in the North-West and South-West regions, leading to protests and social unrest. In the crisis-affected regions, which have experienced six consecutive years of reduced agricultural production compared to the five-year average, household food stocks are below pre-crisis levels. Most poor households that still have stocks of staple foods have enough to support only minimally adequate food consumption for up to one month. The stocks will last three to five months less than in pre-conflict years. In the Momo, Lebialem, Meme, and Menchum divisions, which are most affected by food insecurity and where production was lower than in other divisions and compared to the five-year average, poor households exhausted their stocks shortly after harvest in October, four to five months earlier than usual, and are therefore entirely dependent on food from the market. In most reference markets in the North-West and South-West regions, staple food prices remain above seasonal averages, and are rising as households deplete their stocks of produce and become increasingly dependent on the marketAccording to the analysis of Cadre Harmonisé, most of the divisions of the North-West and SouthWest remain in the food insecurity phase crisis. Short-term, pendular movement continues within the two regions. Often people move back and forth between their home and their place of refuge. As they hide in the bush, people build huts and cultivate plots of land. In 44 per cent of cases, it is the entire household that undergoes such displacement. In the rest of the cases, men hide in the bushes alone, while their families remain in the village or move to safer urban areas. Pendular displacement is observed in 61 per cent of surveyed villages of the North-West and in 44 per cent of localities in the South-West. 33 per cent of these displacements are a result of military operations while 30 per cent result from a fear of attacks. Displaced families regularly go back to their village to check on their houses or to see if the area has become safe enough to move back. Women often visit their homes first to check if the area is safe before assessing if male family members can return. Return to the North-West and South-West were observed in 2021, inter alia because of a lack of service and assistance in areas of displacement.2Access to the population in need in the North-West and South-West regions remains difficult and volatile, largely due to underfunding, insecurity, physical access challenges, and administrative impediments. However, access in the North-West and South-West is possible if resources are available. The need for additional resources for access negotiations increases the operational costs in a humanitarian crisis which is already hugely underfunded, further limiting the reach of humanitarian organizations to assist people in needIn the socio-political context of the North-West and South-West, the Food Security Cluster will consider the most vulnerable people identified by the October 2023 Cadre Harmonisé analysis, and focus on IDPs, the host population, and those left behind with no access to livelihood activities due to insecurity. The Sector will support immediate access to food to improve the food security status through the provision of emergency and time-critical agriculture assistance and increase access to income-generating opportunities through the rehabilitation, creation and/ or maintenance of sustainable livelihood assets. In line with the “do no harm” approach, the Sector will assess the market and security situation to determine the feasibility of CVA, which would be the preferred response modality.
