Project Summary

In a context- like Gaza's one- characterized by prolonged strain and economic isolation, vulnerable families have resorted to different negative coping mechanisms to deal with the high level of poverty and vulnerabilities. Child labour, withdrawal from schools, and engaging children in risky occupations were amongst the commonly used distress mechanisms to alleviate poverty (Overseas Development Institute, 2014; Tdh, 2018). Recently OCHA (Oct 2018), has identified providing mental health and psychosocial support to children who could resort to negative coping mechanism or could be the victims of harmful practices, including children drooping out of schools and children involved in child labour as a key humanitarian response. This project is a response to such needs. The project aims to eliminate child labour in the Gaza Strip, particularly in Gaza North and Khanyounis governorates through provision of child protection services, psychosocial services, educational reintegration, family support and increased community mobilization toward the issue of child Labour and dropout of schools. This project focuses on the right of the child to be protected from economic exploitation and from performing any work that is likely to be hazardous or to interfere with his or her education, or to be harmful to health or physical, mental, spiritual, moral or social development. The project will target 8584 beneficiaries, including 400 working and or dropped out of school children (300 boys and 100 girls, as child labour is more common among boys). The proposed activities will include responsive, remedial, and environment-building child protection activities. The responsive activities will include (1). Provision of psychological first aid to the identified cases of working and or dropped out of school children, with particular focus on children victims of worst forms of child labour, (2). Provision of child protection services to the identified cases of working and or dropped out of school children, including individual counseling sessions.
Focusing on providing activities that aim to prevent further harm, remedial activities will include (1) provision of child protection case management, including provision of remedial classes, provision of individual and group counseling sessions, joint child-parent interaction activities, life skills, integration into schools and vocational training centers, referral to specialized mental health services. (2) Provision of economic support to 40 families of working children. Finally, environment- Building activities will include conducting training sessions to 8000 community members, all are caregivers, on the importance of eliminating child labour (4000 men and 4000 women), a total of 250 sessions will be conducted. The project team will provide services to the identified cases through the two child protection centers, in Beit Lahyia and Khanyounis. Under this Action, Tdh and its partner organizations will utilize the Child Protection Web-based Case Management System and the referral pathways that have been developed jointly by Tdh and UNICEF in 2017, in partnership with the Ministry of Social Development. A high level of coordination with relevant ministries and relevant clusters, including protection and education. Finally, Tdh will report its intervention to Protection cluster, Child Protection Working Group, and FSS.

Needs

Gaza children were born into one of the most complicated and protracted conflicts in the world, involving 70 years of war and 51 years of occupation. Gaza has been under Israeli blockade since 2007. Consequently, the unemployment rate has soared to 49.1%, already the world’s highest (PCBS, 2018). Furthermore, 53 % of Palestinian families live under the poverty line (PCBS, 2017, 2018). In a context characterized by prolonged strain and economic isolation, vulnerable families have resorted to different negative coping mechanisms to deal with the high level of poverty and vulnerabilities. Child labour, withdrawal from schools, and engaging children in risky occupations were amongst the commonly used distress mechanisms to alleviate poverty (Overseas Development Institute, 2014; Tdh, 2018). Compared to 2013, child labour has risen in the past six years as economic conditions have deteriorated in the Gaza Strip. Qualitative data collected by Tdh in 2018 have revealed that child labour is common among children, mainly boys, of poor families, as families with working children live below poverty line, have a higher than average number of children and unemployment rates. With the ongoing energy crisis in the Gaza Strip, preliminary findings of a study conducted by UNICEF (2017) have revealed significant increase in the number of working children. Finally, Child Protection Rapid Needs Assessment has been carried out by Tdh in partnership with the Child Protection Working Group in Gaza in 2018. The study findings have shown that child labour is common in the Gaza Strip, more among boys and that child labour appears to be a frequent coping mechanism with household's loss of income, livelihood, and poverty, mainly among boys (Tdh, 2018). Finally, recently OCHA (Oct 2018), has identified providing mental health and psychosocial support to children who could resort to negative coping mechanism or could be the victims of harmful practices, including children drooping out of schools and children involved in child labour as a key humanitarian response. Against this backdrop, Tdh and Beit Lahiya Development Association (BLDA) have been offering child protection services for working and/or dropped out of schools in Gaza North governorate since May 2012. Currently, Tdh in partnership with BLDA operates a Child Protection Center in Gaza North. The Child Protection Center is located in vulnerable area in Beit Lahyia. Most of the inhabitants of Beit Lahyia are poor families, have high fertility rates, and many of them have working or out of school children. Up-to-date, Tdh jointly with BLDA have managed to identify more than 3050 dropped out and/or working children (2536 boys VS 514 girls). Reintegration of working and/or recently dropped out of school children into schools and vocational training centers is a core activity of Tdh child protection services. So far, a total of 951 children (213 girls VS 738 boys) were reintegrated into either vocational training centers or schools. Tdh has SPSS dataset that includes detailed information on all identified cases of working and/or dropped out of school children; currently, there are tens of children who have not yet received any services, due to financial limitations. From Tdh experience in dealing with working children, it is noticeable that child labour negatively impacts the physical, emotional, and cognitive development of children, as well as working children were exposed to physical and sexual exploitation from their employers, peers, and community members (Tdh, 2017). This project focuses on the right of the child to be protected from economic exploitation and from performing any work that is likely to be harmful to health or physical, mental, spiritual, moral or social development. Tdh will expanded its child protection services targeting working children to Khanyounis, as both Gaza North and Khanyounis governorates have the highest child labourers as reported by key-informants (Tdh, 2018).

Activities

Identification of vulnerable working and/or dropped out children will be a priority for the project team, the aim is to identify at least 400 children. The process of identifying children will involve conducting field visits to working areas and to the most deprived areas in project localities. It will also involve visiting governmental schools and obtaining a list of dropped out of school children. With regards to working girls, special attention will be dedicated to identify them through home visits. The project team will provide services to the identified cases through the two child protection centers in Beit Lahyia and Khanyounis. Under this Action, both boys and girls will receive protection services according to their assessed needs. The responsive activities will include (1). Provision of psychological first aid to the identified cases of working and or dropped out of school children, with particular focus on children victims of worst forms of child labour. New identified cases of working and/ or dropped out of school children will be provided with psychological first aid in order to increase their feeling of safety, connectedness, and to be emotionally assured. Psychological first aid will also be used to assess the needs and concerns of newly identified cases. (2). Provision of child protection services, including individual counseling sessions. The remedial activities will focus on providing activities that aim to prevent further harm. Activities will include (1) provision of child protection case management, including provision of remedial classes, provision of individual and group counseling sessions, joint child-parent interaction activities, life skills, integration into schools and vocational training centers, referral to specialized mental health services. (2) Provision of economic support to 40 families of working children (224 members). The economic support would motivate families to reintegrate their children into schools or vocational training centers. Within the scope of this project, Tdh will utilize the in-kind modality, each family will receive in-kind goods that equal of a value of $ 2000 to establish their own micro-project. The selection will be based on vulnerability gird with a scoring system that will be developed. Tdh has previously supported tens of families of working children with micro-projects. Although, beneficiaries prefer a cash transfer as compared with an equal-valued in-kind transfer, Tdh will utilize the in-kind modality as this option is economically viable, especially for the most deprived people as it reduces the risk of spending the assistance to cover daily living expenses. Beneficiaries will be encourage to select projects that have short-life cycle such as mini-markets in order to quickly generate income. Families with highest scores will receive the in-kind assistance. Tdh will make sure that families will identify their projects based on the experience they have, families will have unrestricted flexibility for purchases as they will identify the items they need. Finally, Tdh will respect the confidentiality of beneficiaries' data. Children coming from families that will not receive cash assistance will be provided by school stationary and uniform. Tdh will refer cases that need economic support to the MoSD's. Working children coming from female-headed households and families with disabled children will be considered as a priority. Environment-building activities will include training 8000 caregivers on the importance of eliminating child labour, a total of 250 training sessions will be conducted. A high level of coordination with relevant ministries and clusters, including Protection and Education Clusters, FSS, MoA, MoSD, and other INGOs providing assistance in the targeted areas will also be enhanced as they are already established, to ensure a highly efficient cross checked system of case identification and referral and to avoid duplication of receiving services.
