Project Summary

The objective of the project is to a) To improve vulnerable women's and girls' access to
income generation opportunities and quality multi sectoral services in Gaza b) To improve
the quality of GBV services provided to vulnerable women and girls.
UN Women will capitalize on the achievements and lessons learnt of the last year interventions in addressing GBV needs, supporting partners to improve quality, accessibility and responsiveness of the
services to the needs of those who are most vulnerable. UN Women proposes to
implement this 12-month project to improve displaced and vulnerable women’s
accessibility to multi-sectoral services, protection and economic support in the West Bank
and Gaza. UN Women’s interventions will be carefully tailored to address women’s and
girls’ needs following holistic and woman-centred approaches, where tailored
interventions are comprehensively designed to address the needs of the most vulnerable
groups of women i.e. women with disabilities, women at risk of forcible transfer, women
heads of households, women survivors of violence etc. Through the project, UN Women
will ensure improving the quality of services provided to women and that their voices
inform and shape those services. Through the project interventions, UN Women will also
work on identifying women champions of change who have the ability and capacity to
support other women in their local communities overcome challenges and difficulties. The
project responds to HRP Strategic Objective 2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians
living under occupation are met through the provision of essential services, in accordance
with the rights of protected persons under IHL.

1 - Needs

In the oPt, the fluid political situation, the shifting vulnerabilities, the protracted occupationwith its limitations on development and humanitarian action, all create additional layersof potential discrimination at all levels, in addition to social, cultural and economicfactors. The Gaza Strip remains in a protracted and deep humanitarian crisis inflicted bythe Israeli blockade and successive rounds of destructive conflict. By 2020, the UNprojects that Gaza will be “unlivable.” Of a total population of 1.9 million people, 1.6million or 64 per cent are estimated to be in need. The territory is facing a crisis of hyperunemployment, food insecurity, electricity blackouts, sanitation disasters, and a failingmedical sector. Cuts to the budget of the United Nations Relief and Works Agency(UNRWA), which serves 1.36 million refugees in Gaza, by the American administration andthe cuts in the salaries of civil servants by the Palestinian Authority (PA) have furtherdeepened the vulnerability of households. A total of 39 per cent of Palestinians in Gazaare living below the poverty line, more than double those in the West Bank (18 per cent).Only 10 per cent of households have “direct access to safe drinking water” and inDecember 2017, received only an average of 4-5 hours of electricity per day. Thesestresses have been linked to increased incidence of gender-based violence (GBV),including early marriage, while shelters and other service providers struggle to meet theneeds with increasingly limited resources. From the start of the Gaza Great March ofReturn demonstrations on 30 March 2018 and up to 31 May 2019, two women and onegirl were killed by Israeli forces, and over 2,434 women and girls were injured . The highnumber of injuries has also had negative repercussions for women’s lives, often resultingin emotional stress at the household level, including higher exposure to GBV . Somewomen have reported psychological and physical abuse by their husbands, who heldthem responsible for their participation in the demonstrations and the injury or loss oftheir children. Women who have been injured and might end up with a form of disabilityface social discrimination and limited access to services. Those who have lost a spouseand become “new widows” are exposed to financial need and legal problems as death ofa primary breadwinner has a direct and immediate impact on the living conditions of thewidow and children . Moreover, young girls in families who lost a breadwinner face ahigher risk of child marriage. All in all, key GBV service providers have received double thenumber of GBV cases that they received in 2017 because of the mass demonstrations .

2 - Activities

In the framework of IASC Guidelines for GBV Interventions and building on UN Women’sexperience in providing multisectoral and gender responsive services to vulnerable groupsof women, UN Women proposes to implement a one-year project in cooperation withthe GBV sub cluster partners that have experience and comparative advantage inimplementing projects focused on GBV prevention and response in Gaza (The partners will be selected through a competitive process and in consultation with GBV SC). The project aims to achieve the following results: Output 1.1. 200 vulnerable women and girls including victims and survivors of GBV have new sources of income and economic opportunities through a Cash-for-Work (CFW) modality. According to a new UN Women assessment (2019) of the impact of cash for work on women beneficiaries, the percentage of increase in income of household as a result of cash for work activities was measured to be around %345.2 increase over household income. • Activity 1.1.1: Based on market needs, develop and provide skill matchingcash for work programme targeting 200 vulnerable women including womenwith disabilities and women survivors of violence (age between 20 -50). • Activity 1.1.2 Support, through coaching and training, each beneficiary over the course of CfWimplementation, build a profile that can support them compete in the local market. Output1.2. 4250 vulnerable and marginalized adult and young women, girls and men haveincreased access to multi-sectorial services, including prevention and response to SGBVthrough specialized service providers from protection cluster partners. • Activity 1.2.1: Provide GBV case management, psychosocial & legal assistance, and referral services to vulnerable women and girls and their families. This will include comprehensiveassessment and participatory development of a reintegration plan that meets their needs.These will include (500 women receiving GBV case management, 100 women receiving legalcounseling and court representation, 300 women receiving health and psycho social support, 250 men receiving MHPSS services, family counselling, and couple therapy; and 100 women receiving referral services) • Activity 1.2.2: Provide child visitation services for at least 20 families (divorced couples) and their children.• Activity 1.2.3: Provide reproductive and health services to 300 vulnerable women including survivors of violence and breast cancer survivors among adults and young females. • Activity 1.2.4: Provide physical rehabilitation services and assistive devices for (100) elderly women and women and girls with disabilities on operational service providers including those identified by CP-GBV SOPs and referral pathways. • Activity 1.2.5: Awareness raising dialogues with 450 men (HeFor She dialogues) to promote positive masculinity and positive attitudes in relation to GBV and GBV services. • Activity 1.2.6: Provide training and support to 50 GBV actors to act as experts and demand better quality multi-sector services. This will build on GBV SC experience in building capacities of non GBV specialists across the West Bank and Gaza. The non GBV specialist training will be implemented in full coordination with the GBV sub cluster.• Activity 1.2.7: Organize awareness raising sessions targeting 2000 women, men boysand girls on GBV and GBV and disability services.Output 1.3: Capacity of service providers (50) to offer support services to marginalized women and girls in Gaza strengthened. • Activity 1.3.1: Co-design and implement tailored capacity building ofservice providers (GBV, psychosocial, legal and health) on quality

UN Women will work closely with GBV Sub-cluster members and partners to make sure the project is implemented according to project workplan, logframe and set indicators and targets. The Project will establish a Project Management Committee (PMC) which will consist of UN Women and all three partners. The PMC will meet on a quarterly basis and is responsible for the overall monitoring and quality control of the project.
