Project Summary

The project will target vulnerable populations in the regions of Yambio in Western Equatoria most affected by the armed conflict where MaCDA has already built the key elements of a response mechanism as part of its humanitarian interventions. Differentiated approaches to programming will be applied considering the prevailing humanitarian situation and its evolution: a)The primary focus for humanitarian response will remain to be on the communities along the contact line b) Critical needs of the most vulnerable IDPs and host communities members will be prioritized with a more gradual transition toward longer-term solutions for response to GBV c) As humanitarian access to the regions for protection activities remains open, the project will seek opportunities for partnerships with local authorities; will reach out to those in need through Survivors Support Networks, Referral Pathways, Community Based Protection Committees; and will use existing access points such as healthcare facilities for referrals. Needs

GBV remains a significant protection concern and imposes life-threatening risks to the most vulnerable among IDPs and host communities members in the conflict affected areas of Yambio, particularly in the areas close to the contact line in the current conflict. An estimated 139,091 persons remain displaced in Western Equatorial (South Sudan OCHA Humanitarian Bulletin February 2018).Of those displaced; women constitute the largest population registering 45% of the displaced in the state (Assessment of hard to reach areas in South Sudan, REACH report, March 2017). In the latest displacement, more than 18,500 IDPs, mainly women and children have been displaced following fighting in Namutina and Nagero in April and May 2018.The IDPs are currently residing in Tambura and surrounding areas with significant protection concerns reported (Humanitarian Bulletin, South Sudan: Issue 6/16th July 2018).The high concentration of military and locally armed groups, coupled with a proliferation of weapons, weak law enforcement and impunity for perpetrators has increased the risk of human rights violations, including sexual and gender based violence (GBV) prevalence. Women, girls and boys experiencing domestic violence and other forms of violence are often forced to remain in unsafe living environments because there are no alternatives. Displaced women and adolescent girls are particularly at risk. The South Sudan’s Humanitarian Needs Overview 2018 stipulates an urgent need to strengthen the capacity to respond sustainably to cases of GBV and prevent future acts of violence by using a survivor-centered approach. Of special importance is a need to scale up psychological support services for GBV survivors. Furthermore, service delivery needs to be coordinated and integrated in order to ensure the client gets comprehensive treatment including physical protection and shelter; medical care, including HIV/STI treatment (e.g. post exposure prophylaxis); psychological counseling; social support; and referrals. Survivors of violence and those at risk of violence need to have access to Women Girl Friendly spaces.

Activities

1. Essential GBV services (PSS mobile teams and referral to health services providers, protection, and livelihood) are available and accessible for the most vulnerable women and girls living in the areas close to the contact line (also along the contact line).2. Referral pathway established and has the capacity to lead response to GBV.3. Essential GBV service packages provided to survivors.4. PSS services provided to GBV survivors.5 Local healthcare providers capacitated on clinical management of rape6. Community led information and awareness raising campaigns on GBV response and prevention conducted in 5 locations.7. Community Based Protection Committees equipped with information materials on GBV and incapacitated to implement community level GBV prevention, advocacy and communication actions.8. Train police officers in prevention and response using the GBV Police training manuals 9) Coordinate with the local authority in establishing community policing networks 10) Train case workers, 1 in each Payam including on how to identify survivors of physical and sexual violence and refer them to service providers.11) Disseminate behavior change messages on sexual violence and forced/early marriages, including via workshops and trainings. Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

MaCDA is well versed with barriers that hinder access to assistance for various groups .For example, gaps in service accessibility for those living with disabilities is wider compared to those with no impairments. High illiteracy rate in rural South Sudan may bar individuals deserving assistance from accessing information on available services. Other factors may include distance covered to access services, protection concerns, and possibility of stigmatization in case of GBV survivors. Aware of these barriers, MaCDA will seek to minimize the impact of these barriers by implementing activities closer to settlement areas, awareness raising using local languages on services available, ensuring all individuals living with disabilities are registered among IDPs and Host communities, preserving the privacy of GBV survivor’s e.t.c.

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Local and MaCDA project staff will regularly meet with beneficiary’s representatives in order to feed their comments, security incidents, and assessment into the wider security assessment.

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

The project will promote the dignity of beneficiaries by enabling them to participate and influence decision making during project implementation and monitoring. Project staffs will accept responsibility for their actions and will be called upon to account for their actions.
