Project Summary

"Guided by UNFPA’s global strategy on adolescents & youth: My Body, My Life, My World, the project recognizes the strengths and aspirations of Rohingya & host community youth aged 10-24, who face challenges due to their developmental stage and risks posed by the current and volatile security context.
In line with the Peace & Security (P&S) Campaign, the project will build young people's capacity, focusing on young women & girls, to be agents of change through Positive Youth Development programs, providing them with opportunities to showcase leadership skills through Youth-Led social cohesion & peacebuilding initiatives. The project ensures engagement of both girls & boys by addressing the distinct barriers faced by each group, with attention to the needs and empowerment of girls & young women. The interventions shall highlight the role of youth for peace, and the creation of a conducive environment for young people to fulfill their potential in their communities. This approach aligns with UNFPA's broader mandate to ensure every young person's potential is fulfilled, supporting equal participation & leadership of young women & men.
Intervention 1: Youth for Peace
Through 7 dedicated youth centers, community households and schools, UNFPA will enhance young people’s capacity to adopt positive coping mechanisms & engage in community development for sustaining peace. Referral mechanisms will address protection concerns or violations, ensuring safety and well-being. Gender-sensitive programs will consider specific needs of girls & boys to ensure that they have equal access to these spaces, and that girls can engage in activities without fear of violence or exploitation. Needs based access to specialized and non-specialized psychosocial counseling to adolescents & their caregivers shall continue through the Alapon helpline. The initiative will develop, pilot and document youth-led revitalization projects in the communities surrounding the A&Y Centers and Host Community Schools. Communities will benefit from programs aimed at fostering peace, i.e. Youth for Peace, and Sports, Theater & Arts for Protection.
Intervention 2: Community Engagement
Through 9 Women Led Community Centers & 1 Multi-Purpose Center, young women & men will access integrated P&S messages in GBV prevention, PSS, social mobilization and SBCC activities. Caregivers & community leaders will be engaged through awareness raising to promote gender equality and positive social norms. Interventions will contribute to building the leadership of community-based groups of women & youth affiliated to the centers. UNFPA will collaborate with other agencies to print and distribute IEC materials and build capacities of relevant actors for reinforcing joint messages on women/youth P&S.
Both interventions will strengthen the capacity of local youth-led, community-based organizations in refugee camps, providing contextualized capacity building on communication, negotiation and leadership for peace & security. A critical mass of young men & women peer leaders can be trained on arbitration and mediation of conflict, including by adopting the common ground approach.
This program's Theory of Change states that If adolescents & youth, particularly young women & girls have access to capacity building on life skills, and adults in their communities are sensitized to recognize the potential of young people to contribute to the economic & social decision-making opportunities within their families and communities, then positive coping mechanisms become more pervasive, creating a virtuous cycle, where improved well-being and empowerment of the women and youth leads to greater resilience, social cohesion, gender equality, and ultimately a harmonious society. and development in Rohingya refugee camps and host communities."

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The escalation of conflict in Rakhine State, Myanmar and its spillover effects in Rohingya refugee camps have far-reaching consequences for adolescents & youth, and women & girls, affecting their protection, mental health and access to vital services & opportunities. An alarming trend has emerged of adults, adolescents & youth, and even children forced, intimidated, manipulated, or enticed to join organized groups, including for military training and deployment in Rakhine State. Incidents of abduction, kidnapping and violent crimes in the camps, targeted young people for ransom related abduction. ISNA reported, 22.26% of youth felt unsafe in their households, indicating significant concerns about youth safety & protection across the camps. Increased reports of human trafficking involve both young men & boys but also women & girls. Organized gangs reportedly threatened or perpetrated GBV against the female family members as a pressure tactic to force the men and boys to join their groups. The security situation significantly impacts young people's access to health, protection, education, and livelihood services as they feel unsafe to leave their homes. The lack of safe spaces was evidenced by the 24.12% of youth reporting limited access to places for socializing. As boys and young men face fears about forced recruitment, girls are at a greater risk of GBV, domestic violence, child marriage, sexual assault and exploitation. Child marriage is often justified as a means to ensure their protection and purity in an increasingly insecure environment. These exacerbate the adverse social norms, in both Rohingya camps and neighboring host communities and further limit access to basic needs and opportunities for meaningful engagement in political and other decision-making processes from the family to the community levels. The majority of youth (72.46%) feel they lack opportunities to contribute to their community or take on leadership roles, with 35% of respondents reporting a lack of skills necessary for participation. Furthermore, 84.37% youth have not been involved in the planning, implementation, or monitoring of humanitarian activities, highlighting a missed opportunity to leverage their potential and ensure their voices in interventions designed for them. As part of the ISNA, more than 80% of surveyed youth reported not receiving life skills training, including communication, problem-solving or decision-making. The increased violence in the camps creates a climate of fear and uncertainty, limiting access to health, education, and livelihood services as well as opportunities for recreation and socialization, thereby increasing the stress and mental health distress of a population that has already suffered significant trauma. A survey conducted by UNFPA and SANEM with SDC support found that 73% of Rohingya adolescents and youth and 61% of young people from host communities suffer from mild to severe childhood post-traumatic stress disorder (CPTSD).These compounding factors further limit their prospects for personal and social development, perpetuating a cycle of vulnerability and marginalization, that can propel young people to negative coping mechanisms including child marriage, early pregnancy, human trafficking, grooming and recruitment into organized groups. The lack of opportunities and meaningful participation in decision-making further exacerbates their marginalization, perpetuating a cycle of unmet needs and unrealized potential that fractures social trust. Rectifying it would require joint action and a fundamental shift in perspective recognizing young people not merely as vulnerable victims or potential perpetrators, but as powerful agents of change.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

This initiative aligns with Pillar Two of the Youth Peace and Security Entry Points on protection, justice, and human rights. It aims to facilitate an inclusive, safe, enabling, and gender-responsive environment where the leadership potential of youths from various backgrounds are recognized and adequate support and protection is provided to implement social cohesion and violence prevention activities. This ensures they can carry out their work independently, without undue interference, including in situations of armed conflict, and that threats, harassment, and violence against them are investigated thoroughly and impartially. The project promotes the protection of affected populations and prioritizes the safety, dignity, and rights of young people, maximizing the protective impact of humanitarian programming. The principle of ""do no harm"" is incorporated throughout the project cycle and applied to youth centers, services, and outreach activities. Through the incorporation of protection principles, including meaningful access, participation, and empowerment, the project will promote and protect the human rights of beneficiaries without contributing to or perpetuating discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect, and exploitation. UNFPA will ensure that protection mechanisms and principles are in place by applying protection mainstreaming assessment toolkits, conducting safety audits, and adhering to IASC protection standards across supported facilities. This will be monitored through regular needs and vulnerability assessments and participatory discussions with target populations. The results of these assessments will inform further support for vulnerable groups, such as children, adolescents, persons with disabilities, and members of female-headed and youth-headed households. Finally, UNFPA will continue to promote engagement with affected populations through community engagement and awareness-raising efforts on protection topics and referral mechanisms.

2.3 - Age & Disability

UNFPA both adheres to and supports the capacity development of partners on the IASC Guidelines on Working with and for Young People in Humanitarian and Protracted Crises to provide age-responsive and adolescent-friendly services. UNFPA has a repertoire of resources, including toolkits and checklists, on disability inclusion that it implements in its planning, implementation and monitoring mechanisms. When possible, the project will hire refugee teachers and prioritize the recruitment of staff with appropriate language, gender, age and cultural competencies, including staff with disabilities, to work with Rohingya populations. Data collected will also be disaggregated by sex, age and disability to better understand the service uptake among different groups.In Cox’s Bazar, UNFPA plans to further improve the accessibility of its supported facilities in 2025 through various measures. Needs based adaptation to improve the accessibility of services to people with various disabilities shall be prioritized, based on the feedback received from them.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

UNFPA has zero tolerance for sexual exploitation & abuse (SEA) and sexual harassment (SH).UNFPA has been a member of the ISCG PSEA Network in Cox's Bazar since 2017, and has appointed PSEA Focal Points for the organization. UNFPA provides training on PSEA, including obligations to report wrongdoing, for staff and non-staff personnel,IPs, vendors, and service providers. UNFPA's Office of Audit & Investigation Services (OAIS) ensures that staff can easily report misconduct through an online platform/hotline or through PSEA focal points. The organization also has a policy to prevent retaliation to protect survivors, victims, and whistleblowers.UNFPA conducts awareness-raising sessions among communities and visibly posts IEC materials in all its supported facilities to ensure that communities are aware of SEA & reporting mechanisms. As needed, UNFPA will conduct capacity assessments and support the implementation of capacity strengthening plans to ensure IPs are maintaining the required PSEA and safeguarding mechanism.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

UNFPA will ensure full adherence to DRR/DRM standards, including the Government of Bangladesh’s National Plan for Disaster Management (2021-2025) & Action for Disaster Risk Management Towards Resilient Nation. UNFPA Community Mobilizers are trained to support the response to disaster-related emergencies in the acute and post-disaster phases. UNFPA recognizes the critical role of youth, particularly in the context of climate change adaptation and disaster resilience. Youth are not only one of the most affected by climate-related disasters, but they also possess valuable knowledge, energy, and innovation to lead efforts in mitigating risks and building resilience within their communities. In line with this, UNFPA will integrate youth-focused initiatives into its climate action strategies, emphasizing the importance of youth as climate leaders. Basic DRR information, environmental sustainability and climate action. is also shared with the beneficiaries of UNFPA’s interventions such as its Life Skills Education program, while UNFPA-supported facilities, including the Adolescent & Youth Centers, are used as temporary shelter during emergencies and is equipped with solar energy the construction of these youth centers took into consideration the availability of natural light, utilizing transparent roofs wherever applicable to maximize daylight use, in addition to planting three trees within the area of the facility. UNFPA also prepositions and stores customized Dignity Kits and emergency Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) Kits that are quickly distributed to its project beneficiaries during emergencies to reduce the vulnerability of affected women and girls. Finally, UNFPA will continue to explore and adopt implementation modalities based on contemporary understanding and best practices in DRR and climate change adaptation.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Within the framework of the climate change adaptation and mitigation plan, emergency preparedness and response (EPR) measures are incorporated as integral components of the project. Firstly, Community Mobilizers supported by UNFPA are equipped to respond to disaster-related emergencies during the preparedness, acute, and post-disaster phases. Additionally, UNFPA's interventions, such as its Life Skills Education program, integrate basic disaster risk reduction (DRR) information. UNFPA-supported facilities, including Adolescent and Youth Centers, serve as temporary shelters during emergencies, offering access to adolescent-friendly and gender-sensitive DRR preparedness and response information. Secondly, UNFPA proactively stockpiles tailored Dignity Kits and emergency Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) Kits to ensure prompt distribution to project beneficiaries during emergencies. This action mitigates the vulnerability of affected women and girls. Thirdly, it prioritizes the development of community recovery and resilience plans (incorporating preparedness) following hazardous events, integrating DRR and climate change adaptation (CCA) in a manner that leverages the skills and recommendations of young people. Engaging the youth in the labor intensive post disaster reconstruction ensures positive channelization of their energies, providing impetus to youth positive development and peace. Lastly, to uphold best practices in DRR, climate change adaptation, and mitigation, UNFPA adheres to Social & Environmental Safeguarding principles.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

The project follows UNFPA’s global strategy for adolescents and youth, placing young people at the heart of its programs and embracing their diversity. It is also aligned with United Nations Security Council Resolution 2250, which acknowledges the contribution of young people to peace and security, as the project calls for their meaningful participation and leadership across different spheres that impact their lives. Designed in accordance with the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) guidelines on working with and for young people in humanitarian and protracted crises, the project involves youth throughout the humanitarian response process—from the initial design stage to implementation, monitoring, and evaluation. This engagement includes: Inclusive and multiple Consultations – grouped by age and sex of participants – with a diverse cross-section of young people to identify needs and challenges related to peacebuilding.Gather information on which youth organizations, networks and associations are already engaged in peacebuilding and social cohesion-related initiatives.Create safe spaces and promote the voices of young people.Create opportunities for young people to share their peace and security challenges through intergenerational dialogue and consultative forums, and to take part in decision-making processes to ensure that their needs are addressed.As co-lead of the Youth Working Group (YWG) within the inter-agency coordination mechanism, UNFPA ensures that the project aligns with best practices for coordinated programming aimed at Rohingya and Bangladeshi host community youth. The YWG is committed to advocating for the youth agenda and integrating the needs and capacities of young people at both strategic and operational levels across various sectors of the humanitarian response. It promotes the implementation of IASC Guidelines in relevant sectors and prioritizes meaningful youth participation in its activities and decision-making processes.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

The project promotes resilience through the following activities: Training for 156 youth volunteers across 7 camps to enhance their skills in delivering peace-related awareness sessions and increasing their engagement in the management of adolescent and youth-responsive facilities. Linking with UNFPA’s existing life skills programs (such as Girl Shine and Champions of Change) as well as functional literacy programs, these initiatives aim to improve refugees' ability to access services, manage livelihoods, and reduce aid dependency. Implementation of Sports4Peace, theater, and arts activities to foster resilience, build social cohesion, improve mental well-being, and develop leadership skills among refugees. Development, piloting, and documentation of youth-led initiatives to revitalize the surrounding communities of Adolescent & Youth Centers and Host Community Schools, empowering young people to lead positive change in their local environment.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

UNFPA project aligns with Cox’s Bazar District Development and Growth Plan, which highlights a need for holistic and inclusive development targeting both the Rohingya refugee and host communities. The document stresses that the district is affected by both humanitarian and development challenges and prioritizes: i) strengthening the economic resilience, ii) improving human capital and productivity, iii) promote social cohesion and emphasized that all the initiatives will be gender inclusive, addressing the needs of children, youth and elderly, and gender based violence concerns, which are fully in alignment with what have been proposed in the proposal.
