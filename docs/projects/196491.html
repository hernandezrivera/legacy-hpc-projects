Project Summary

The project aims To provide life-saving humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable people to alleviate suffering.
through this project, the bread will be offered to targeted families to reduce burden of living, improve their life condition, and support their resilience. The beneciaries will be chosen as per most vulnerable families with the help of hosting camp in the region. Through these procedures, the foundation ensures most benet for vulnerable families as that will be measured by consult those people about the feasibility of the project and its outputs. This project is designed to respond to the needs of the new inux of IDPs from southern Idleb and Aleppo, This project will provide cooked meals, RTERs and food parcels. As per the FSL Cluster recommendations, the cooked meals are going to be implemented for the rst 3 days of the emergency distributions, which will be followed by RTERs for a period of 4 weeks, and nally, we will distribute cash/voucher for food for 8 months to the same IDP households With a monthly value of $ 60 per family. The total number of beneciaries targeted are 5,500 individuals (1,100 households). This project will also help in decreasing severe food insecurities and that may result in malnutrition and other health related problems.
Al-Sham Humanitarian Foundation has a good experience in Humanitarian Action especially bakeries. The foundation owns 4 bakeries one of them is currently working and 7 our mills offering bread since 2016 for 1.8 million citizens.The project aims to Distributing bread to 2,100 families on a daily basis for a year for1 kg per family. ncluding cash-for-work to 26 individuals for 312 days to support the Bread distribution .
Last year, funding for the implementation of bread distribution and cash-for-work activities was fully secured.

3 - Needs

Based on the HNO The food security situation in Syria has drastically deteriorated since last year. In 2023, an estimated 15 million people will need some form of food and agriculture assistance, representing 68 per cent of the total population. At least 12.1 million people are estimated to be food insecure, including all 2 million people living in camps who are deemed to be 100 per cent food insecure, and 2.9 million people areestimated to be at risk of food insecurity, an alarming increase of people at risk who could shift to become food insecure during 2023 if the crisis continues at same worsening levels.145The impact of Syria’s crisis continues to hinder people’s ability to meet their immediate food needs and livelihoods, in a context marked by very complex drivers and contributing factors. This includes economic worsening and weakened local currency, soaring food and non-food prices, as well as drought- like conditions and severe agro-climatic fluctuations, water scarcity, and limited energy supply. In addition, food and agriculture commodities prices increase, inflation, lack of purchasing power due to limited livelihood and lack of income sources, population movements that shows no sign of abating, and the Ukraine crisis have had an important economic impact on markets and commodities cost. The needs require the sector and partners to go beyond short- term responses, and to also focus on scaling up early recovery, restoration, protection, and promotion
