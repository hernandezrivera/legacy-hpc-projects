Project Summary

In total PUI is planning to support 121,020 people as follows:
- Enable vulnerable conflict affected populations to become self sufficient by developing and enhancing income generating capacities through small business projects (SBPs), professional tool kits (PTKs) and vocational Training (VT).
- Ensure the conflict-affected population have better to basic services, through the rehabilitation of public bakeries.
PUI will ensue the full cooperation with relevant stakeholders, SARC, and Early recovery & Livelihood sector, to facilitate needed approvals, avoid overlapping between INGOs and share previous experiences and knowledge

Needs

The prolonged conflict severely affected socio-economic foundations’ of the Syrians, pushing about 83% of the population to below poverty line. Depreciation of Syrian Pound rate, and high inflation rate all negatively affected Syrians incomes and eroded their purchasing power. Additionally, the destruction reached many economic activities, leaving many Syrians out of jobs, which weakened their resilience capacity to cope with shocks, and left heavy financial burdens on Syrian Families’ shoulders and forced them to lower their standards of living. There are at least 8.6 million Syrians in acute need of Early Recovery and Livelihoods support. (HNO 2019- Draft) Many essential infrastructures are out of service due to conflict, with huge financial constraints on local municipalities preventing them from launching the rehabilitation process ,leaving the population with limited or no access to clean drinking water, essential Health and Educational services . Additionally, the damages in public schools, bakeries, and PHCs is mounting and deepening the poverty trap, in which the affected population finds itself. It is also hindering the return trend of IDPS to their places of origin, pushing them towards undesirable coping mechanisms, and deepening dependency on the scarce humanitarian supplies. (HNO 2019- Draft) However, the negative effect of the crisis was different on age and gender groups; adolescents and youth were the most affected, as exposure to/ or witnessing violence negatively affected their psychological health, interrupted their educational process & prevented them from developing their mental health, which altogether will prevent them from building their capacities and consequently will reduces their chances in accessing the labour market and prevent them from being active citizens in the future . Additionally, social norms and customs have the largest influence on females’ lives in the Syrian society. Although social norms vary according to religion, ethnicity, and between rural and urban areas, yet the general nature of these norms often put females in second place and give most of the privileges to men. With the arise of the Syrian crises, Women were forced to play multiple roles - as their male guardians either died or get involved in the fighting- which placed further pressure on them and forced them to cover for the men’s absence as their houses are now headed by themselves, in addition to their traditional role in the domestic work. Yet, the unfair social norms are preventing equal access for women to livelihood opportunities, which is making them the most affected group and in deep need for assistance. PUI has gone further in their identification of problems, needs and risks aiming for a deeper understanding than the HNO, in the select areas for actions programming, through information gathering both desktop and in the field. More specifically, through obtaining information from various media sources, context analysis, think tanks and meetings with key informants, and combining them with PUI staff’s observations on the ground. Accordingly, PUI in targeted locations - during their field visits- identified huge destruction of essential infrastructures, mainly PHCs, Bakeries and schools. Some WASH infrastructure are also out of services due to destruction and lack of local municipalities to support and secure spare parts which is negatively impacting public health with the spread of insects and disease around accumulated water. Additionally as the return trend is accelerating many returnees need to be supported with Livelihood opportunities to support their resilience and reduce their dependence on aid .

Implementation of activities

PUI is planning to conduct the following activities targeting the most vulnerable conflict affected people : - Provision of Vocational Training Courses (VT) targeting IDPs, host communities and returnee populations to enable the establishment of new livelihoods activities for the targeted populations, through the building of new technical skills; The VT specialities will be selected based on a labour market assessments and the needs of the targeted communities. The activities will be implemented mainly in MoE vocational schools, and the VT courses will be provided for the duration 3 to 6 months. - Provision of professional toolkits (PTKs) targeting IDPs, host communities and returnee populations to enable the restoration of livelihood activities for the targeted populations, and contribute to the rebuilding of markets and communities; PUI will identify the professional specialties targeted for the PTKs based on a rapid market assessment, and the types of professions of the selected applicants. Additionally, PTK beneficiaries will receive a 5 days business skills training to insure their integration to the market. - Supporting Small Business Projects (SBPs) targeting IDPs, host communities and returnee populations to enable the restoration of livelihood activities for the targeted populations, and contribute to the rebuilding of markets and communities; The selected SBPs applicants will receive a 10 days business Management Training, where they will be able to develop a business plans for their proposed business projects. By the end of the training, applicants will present their plans to a selection panel, whom will select the business to be supported based on their feasibility. - Provision productive assets to rural enterprises targeting IDPs, host communities and returnee populations to enable the restoration of livelihood activities for the targeted populations; PUI will provide the agricultural productive assets based on the assessment for the needs of the targeted population. For the previous activities, the selection of beneficiaries will be based on PUI predefined selection and eligibility criteria and the PUI will prioritise the inclusion of females, youths and PWD to the activities. And finally PUI will rehabilitate the basic social infrastructure through the rehabilitation of bakeries. PUI will conduct the needed assessment and will provided the rehabilitation of bakeries’ structures including the provision of production lines with all needed equipment for the proper functioning of the bakery. PUI is planning to support the conflict-affected population in Aleppo, Rural Damascus, Dara, Homs and Lattakia, with better access to basic services, and livelihood opportunities. All selected location have high numbers of people in needs, with severity scale ranging between 4-5. However, Lattakia despite its lower severity scale, compared to others, PUI will continue to support its conflict affected population with access to Livelihood opportunities, as PUI is the only INGO working there, and needs is still high .

Protection Risk Analysis

During implementation of ERL activities, PUI identifies the key protection risks prior to the launch of the intervention to ensure mitigation measures are in place to reduce the likelihood and impact on the population.To reduce the risk or tensions arising amongst the targeted communities, during the initial application and selection stages, PUI communicates with all stakeholders to ensure wide understanding of the proposed intervention, including the selection criteria in place to identify the beneficiaries. For the SBPs and PTKs specifically, PUI establishes a selection Committee composed of various entities to ensure full transparency of the beneficiary selection, and when applicants are not awarded with the support, PUI provides detailed feedback on the reasons. During the selection of grantees, PUI will ensure the legality and ‘morality’ of the selected business to avoid potential exploitation and/or child labor and to ensure adherence to Do-no-Harm principle.cial attention will be given to the female headed household in order to respond to their specific needs and ensure gender equality and avoid any risk of discrimination in the implementation of the project. Additionally, attention will be given to PWD to ensure their integration. PUI will monitor and evaluate the progress of beneficiaries in acquiring the needed skills on a daily basis during the implementation of capacity building and VT programmes. In addition, an impact study will be conducted within six months from the end of the program to evaluate the impact on their self-reliance. M&E system will also be put in place for the Livelihood support activities through the provision of grants and productive assets. To assess the success of the businesses supported and the impact of the activity on the self-reliance of the beneficiaries, PUI will conduct monitoring visits on weekly basis for the first three months after distribution of grants

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The following are seen as key assumptions: - Security situation remains stable, and humanitarian access consistent; accordingly PUI ensure through the intervention that all security procedures are in place and followed . - No major fluctuation of Syrian Pound; - No mass movements of populations into/out of intervention areas;- Financial transactions are still possible to Syria . Below are the main risks to the action and the measures PUI will take in response: - Local population does not accepts PUI´s intervention; to mitigate this PUI will ensure the full engagement of the community leaders and local stakeholders in project design and well introduce the benefits to the targeted community that will occur . - Lagging Return movements; it is not fully under PUI control, however PUI follows a multi sector integrated approach in programming to support the community resilience in a multi dimensions which might encourage more people to return to their original houses, if the lack of access to basic services is their main hinder to return . - Delay in receiving authorizations and clearances to quickly intervene. As mitigation to this PUI is fully coordinating with the relevant line ministries to facilitate the process of getting needed approvals
