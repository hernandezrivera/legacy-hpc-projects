Project Summary

UNHCR delivers comprehensive protection services throughout Cox’s Bazar and ensures the centrality of protection through protection and gender mainstreaming, inclusive coordination and leadership of the humanitarian response. In 2023, UNHCR will continue to work with the GoB to secure identity and documentation for Rohingya refugees through continuous registration, which will serve as the keystone for protection interventions, assistance delivery and solutions planning. UNHCR will also strengthen its community-based protection approach, which provides the foundation for age, gender and diversity responsive services. UNHCR’s response will deliver integrated protection services including registration and documentation, legal protection, and community-based protection.
UNHCR will conduct protection monitoring in all 33 camps to identify protection incidents/trends to support advocacy and address protection gaps, and identify refugees facing urgent and serious protection concerns, and facilitate multisectoral referrals. UNHCR, in coordination with Government and UN partners, will also promote community safety inititiatives to improve the security environment in the camps, involving refugees and the host community, addressing the broader challenges that undermine protection space while promoting peaceful coexistence and social cohesion. UNHCR will strengthen access to justice through capacity building of law enforcement/justice sector actors, strengthening of legal assistance activities (through its partners) in all 33 camps, including outreach and legal awareness to eliminate barriers for all persons of different ages, genders and specific needs, legal counselling, legal assistance/representation, and mediation support.
UNHCR will empower Rohingya refugees by building community capacities for leadership, self-management and engagement with the government and the humanitarian community in 33 camps, including through the elected refugee representatives in four camps. UNHCR will support refugee volunteer networks such as Community Outreach Members (COMs), community groups, and religious networks (including imams and female religious teachers). COMs will identify and support persons with specific needs, and will mobilise communities for protection. Male and female community groups will be supported to deliver community initiatives that foster social change. Communication with communities will be mainstreamed. Community and information centres will also act as essential hubs to disseminate timely, accurate and life-saving information. UNHCR will strengthen disability inclusion in all its protection interventions as well as provide targeted services for persons with disabilities.
Within the current government policy framework, UNHCR will advocate for greater access to age, gender and diversity responsive education and economic inclusion, so that young refugees retain hope and become agents for positive change in the community. UNHCR’s solutions strategy takes into account the government’s policy that voluntary repatriation is the only feasible solution for Rohingya refugees in Bangladesh, and promotes the conditions needed for voluntary, safe, dignified and sustainable return in Myanmar, while recognising large scale returns are not likely to take place in 2023. UNHCR will work to improve protection conditions and quality of life for refugees in Bangladesh pending solutions, building upon the framework for responsibility sharing provided by the GCR. Reinforcing accountable government leadership of the response will build a foundation for the inclusion of refugees in the national systems in the future, and UNHCR will continue to support capacity development of both government and humanitarian actors across the operation.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The effects of the COVID-19 pandemic will continue to reverberate in 2023, with the exacerbation of pre-existing protection risks, including specific risks for refugee women and girls and persons with specific needs, deteriorating security, stressed community protection structures and deepened inter/intra-communal tensions all continued to be highlighted by the community in regularly held consultations. In 2022, serious protection incidents within the camps continued to be reported, heightening fear and insecurity amongst refugees. Difficult terrain, a lack of socio-economic opportunities, unequal gender norms and a culture of impunity foster pervasive violence in refugee households and communities. Women also participated less in income-generating activities, which is significantly correlated with higher levels of vulnerability (WFP 04/2020; ACAPS 04/10/2020).Joint GoB-UNHCR Rohingya registration was completed in 2019, but the COVID-19 lockdown has created a backlog of cases to update. Continuous registration must remain an accessible process to ensure refugees can record and document their status and have access to assistance and services. Major gaps remain in the civil registration and documentation of births and deaths in the camps and remain a top concern for refugees according to Centre for Peace Justice report( CPJ, 02/2021). Refugees’ unrecognized legal status hinders access to justice and effective protection, putting them at risk of arbitrary arrest, unlawful detention and leaving them vulnerable to exploitation, abuse and human trafficking. UNHCR provides legal assistance to refugees, who are often reluctant to report (and lodge formal complaints of) crimes in fear of retaliation by criminal groups and due to lengthy formal processes and lack of trust towards service providers, law enforcement institutions and administration of justice. Lack of legal recognition and documentation of marriages among refugees also limits their access to the Family Court, and thus women’s right to divorce, maintenance and guardianship in the formal justice system. Despite a Women’s and Children’s Tribunal in Cox’s Bazar, limited awareness of recourse mechanisms, and cultural norms, disproportionally impact women’s and girls’ access to justice, including survivors of violence. Women and girls, persons with disabilities (PWD), religious minority groups and gender diverse populations continue to face heightened barriers in accessing services and information where 73% of women surveyed did not know how make a complaint or give feedback Añárar Báfana (ACAPS 04/2021). Meaningful participation of women and female youth remain challenging given restrictions on movement and access to communications due to cultural norms (ACAPS, IOM, UN Women, Comprehensive Gender Analysis 10-12/2021). Persons with disabilities also face barriers related to mobility in shelters (over 52%) and around camps (over 75%). Reach, Age and Disability Inclusion Needs Assessment (May 2021). This is particularly true among older and female PWD who have the least access to services. In total, 67% of female older persons with disabilities had reportedly not received any assistive devices despite needing them, compared to a maximum of 58% of male or female persons with disabilities across all age groups, and 55% of female older persons without disabilities. Reach, (May 2021). Strategic intervention is therefore necessary to strengthen inclusive age, gender and diversity sensitive responses which support refugees to become drivers of change in their community. The protracted nature of the Rohingya refugee crisis has resulted in a shrinking protection space and tensions with host communities, who report safety and security within the top two protection concerns across all unions, according to the 2021 JMSNA. Decreased livelihood opportunities for both refugee and host communities has resulted in an increase in theft and violence, fuelling inter/intra-communal tensions.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

UNHCR has worked with Rohingya refugees in Bangladesh for over 30 years, providing registration and documentation, legal and community-based protection support and services for all refugee men, women, girls and boys. UNHCR has provided continuous support for registration, documentation and legal protection in all 33 camps from the outset of their establishment. UNHCR’s international protection mandate underpins our continuous commitment as both a first responder and provider of last resort, alongside the Government of Bangladesh, to ensure refugees’ access to protection. UNHCR continues to provide a comprehensive community-based protection programme in 31 camps, and aims to expand this programme to all 33 camps, taking into account existing services and capacities and adapting the programme to fill gaps and avoid duplication. In each camp, UNHCR prioritises assessing existing community capacities as well as humanitarian actors and capacities in order to ensure complementary and effective interventions. UNHCR leads the Protection Sector and participates as an active member in order to ensure coordination with other protection stakeholders, and also participates in all camp-level protection coordination structures. UNHCR already provides integrated protection services at community centres, comprising community engagement and empowerment activities, information service centres, GBV and psychosocial support, and legal assistance in many camps. In 2023, UNHCR aims to increase the integration of protection services in order to ensure a seamless and accessible provision of information and support for all refugees.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

UNHCR’s protection programmes are built on an analysis of community power structures and cultural norms, and aim to strengthen existing community capacities whilst ensuring access for and inclusion of the most vulnerable and marginalized groups. UNHCR and partners provide access to services through community centres that provide integrated protection interventions in a non-stigmatising manner, and also ensure outreach activities reach those who cannot or do not feel comfortable attending centres. UNHCR conducts regular community consultations, risk assessments and safety audits which engage men, women, boys and girls, including persons with disabilities, persons of diverse sexual orientations and gender identities and persons with specific needs in order to identify and mitigate risks, and to optimize safety, dignity and equity. UNHCR continuously builds capacity internally and for partners on PSEA, the Code of Conduct and other essential protection standards.

3.2 - Gender

UNHCR’s protection work begins with an analysis of gender norms. In Cox’s Bazar, gender inequality underpins the majority of protection issues, not only for women and girls, but also men, boys and persons with specific needs. UNHCR and partners design programmes to be accessible to women, and create female-only spaces to ensure women’s safety and opportunities for empowerment, as well as promoting women’s leadership in all protection fora. UNHCR’s registration, legal and community-based programmes tackle protection issues relating to gender, including intimate partner violence, child marriage, sexual harassment and other forms of sexual violence, and gender discrimination. UNHCR programmes promote equal participation of women, for example in working with female religious teachers on an equal basis to imams, and in including discussion of harmful gender norms. UNHCR promotes gender parity and equality amongst its own and partner staff as well as in community-based protection work.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

UNHCR’s protection programs are designed with AGD as a key component. All UNHCR partners are trained on child safeguarding, PSEA and the Code of Conduct. FGDs are regularly conducted with boys and girls to identify priority activities, risks and challenges in accessing programs. UNHCR and its partners engage outreach volunteers in reaching out to refugees of all ages, with a focus on adolescents, youth, adults and older persons and work with male/female youth groups, providing training and resources for youth to identify priorities and providing recreational activities, awareness and opportunities for engagement to children aged 8+. Its outreach activities ensure that all refugees are provided with information about protection services and the opportunity to access services and its outreach volunteers are trained to identify and support older persons, persons with young children, and children with specific needs and to provide support to ensure their access to necessary services.

3.4 - Diversity

UNHCR’s protection programmes specifically consider the needs of PWD, persons of diverse sexual orientations and gender identities, and persons from minority ethnic and religious groups, and UNHCR and its partners provide a tailored and responsive approach for PSN, recognising that a person’s different identities and vulnerabilities intersect. UNHCR provides targeted protection interventions for PWD and persons of diverse sexual orientations and gender identities, and works with partners to mainstream diversity considerations across all aspects of its response. UNHCR will conduct disability accessibility audits for its premises and capacity-building for staff and partners on disability inclusion to reduce barriers for inclusion in CBP activities and accessing protection services, and incorporate the Washington Group set of questions on disability to ensure that PWD be appropriately identified during registration, outreach and case management activities.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

AAP is a core responsibility and central to UNHCR’s mandate. Refugees participate in deciding priorities for UNHCR activities and lead their own initiatives through community-based mechanisms regularly during the operational management cycle. UNHCR conducts regular community consultations in all camps to identify priority issues and obtain feedback in order to adapt protection programming. UNHCR operates community feedback and response centres in order to elicit feedback and complaints from refugees. UNHCR prioritises two-way communication through a variety of channels including mail, phone and in person mechanisms. A fundamental part of UNHCR’s work on AAP includes its commitment to the Code of Conduct and a Zero-Tolerance Policy for Sexual Exploitation, Abuse, and Harassment for staff. All staff are trained on CoC and PSEA annually and internal focal points are part of the PSEA Network. Training and awareness is cascaded to community members on identifying and safe reporting of SEA.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

UNHCR will remain a central player within interagency disaster preparedness efforts for the monsoon and cyclone responses and in addressing the challenges brought on by COVID-19 and epidemics. UNHCR includes monsoon, cyclone, landslide, fire and other disaster preparedness aspects in all of its communication with communities activities, and also in community initiatives. When disasters occur, UNHCR targets protection services to respond to the needs of those affected, with a specific focus on women, children and persons with specific needs. UNHCR built facilities include consideration of environmental and climate concerns, and DRR.
