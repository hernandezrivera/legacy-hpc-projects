Project Summary

Syria Relief SR aims to continue providing General Protection, GBV, Child Protection services in NW Syria in Dana, Maaret Tamsrin, Salqin, Armanaz, Kafr Takharim, Afrin, Jandairis, Aghtrin Subdistrict. All those locations according to Protection Cluster Turkey hub ranked as having a severity scale of 4, which is mean there is 80% PiN. project aims to deliver planned services to the most vulnerable Girls, Boys, Women, and Men GBWM in Harim, Afrin, Idleb, A'zaz district through 2 static community centers CCs including 2 CFSs in Dana and Salqin Sub Districts, and 1 specific CFS in Kafr Takharim Sub District. In addition to 4 WGSS in Jandairis, Aghtrin, Salqin, and Kafr Takharim Sub Districts.
Also, 12 mobile units will be attached to reach PiN in IDP locations within the same sub-districts. SR will ensure the services are accessible by PwDs through minor rehabilitation for static facilities in coordination with WASH team in SR and ensure inclusion of PwD in all planned activities. SR will consider as well all possible precautious measures (social distancing and using the PPE) Cluster guidelines related to adapting Protection services delivery modalities in light of COVID-19, to ensure the safety of staff and beneficiaries avoid causing harm. services will be delivered for 24 months starting from 1st of Jan-22, addressing identified Protection, CP needs of targeted individuals. Overall Project Objective is designed to provide integrated protection, GBV, CP services and coordinate closely with local service providers from other sectoral services (FSL & Early Recovery, WASH, and Health-Nutrition actors) to facilitate access of PiN to those services. The outcome of the proposed intervention will be conflict-affected and most vulnerable GBWM, including PwD, have a better understanding of their rights and better resilience through benefitting from legal Protection, Protection, GBV, and Child Protection Services.
SR aims at the continuation of protection activities within areas of operation. Besides, the protection teams will extend their reach into new subdistricts in Idleb and Aleppo governorates. Protection services are intended to provide a package of essential preventive and responsive services to affected Host communities, IDPs, and Returnees through expanding the capacity of mobile outreach teams, launching new Protection activities in parallel with the existing education, health, and FSL activities that SR implements in Dana, Salqin, and Maaret Tamsrin. In addition to delivering an integrated project with the WASH sector within Dana and Kafr Takharim sub-District. project is designed to respond to most affected populations through 2 CCs, 4 WGSS, and 3 CFSs static facilities and 12 mobile units will be attached to those centres to reach out PwDs and IDPs locations. gender-mixed mobile units will also deliver the project services within 12 neighbouring communities according to needs in community ( HNO 2021). proposed CCs, WGSS, CFSs, and mobile units will provide much general protection, GBV, and child protection services including Providing Case Management support to GBV survivors and women at risk, Support groups for women and girls at risk (skill building, PSS, life skills), Community outreach about GBV issues targeting all community members, including men and boys, establish and support GBV Community committees, provide a capacity-building about GBV issues for internal and external service providers, community members and stakeholders. Also, awareness-raising about legal protection, general protection. In addition to Child Protection awareness-raising sessions about CP concerns. SR Teams will also provide case management and IPA to identified most vulnerable GWBM. PSS will be delivered to girls and boys. Besides, parents will have the opportunity to join positive parenting skills sessions. Training about HLP/CSD will be delivered to concerned humanitarian workers to ensure they are well capacitated.

4 - Needs

Syria remains a protection crisis and violations of IHL and IHRL continue to drive humanitarian needs ( HNO 2022). protracted nature of the conflict, compounded by the impact of the economic downturn, food crisis, and pandemic has severely depleted resilience of affected populations to protection threats. (HNAP Assessment 2021). Civil documentation, Housing Land and Property issues, and freedom of movement remain major countrywide protection issues. Lack/loss of civil documentation was reported in 76% of assessed communities, and 61% of assessed communities reported HLP concerns (KI MSNA 2021). Women and girls are subjected to increased denial of economic resources and education, movement restrictions, exploitation, forced and child marriage, intimate partner and family violence, and physical, psychological, emotional, sexual, and social violence. 19% of HH mentioned that women and girls feel unsafe in certain areas (2021 Household MSNA).71% of communities mention that child marriage is an issue for adolescent girls (2021 Community Level MSNA). Economic hardship and Covid-19 exacerbate child protection concerns. These fueled harmful coping mechanisms and increased child labour and child marriage. Child labour is reported in 84% of communities; child marriage is an issue for adolescent girls in 71% of communities; family separation in 36% of communities (2021 Community level MSNA). Psychosocial distress among children is reported in 26% of surveyed households (2021 HH MSNA)

5 - Implementation of activities

there will be community centres within relatively densely populated locations in 8 sub-district in Aleppo, Idleb governates .12 Mobile units will be attached to those static facilities to reach out to random IDP camps. interventions will focus on improving the protection of affected population through community-based, individually targeted interventions, increased and equitable access for MWGB to protection intervention. SR lawyers will continue to provide legal counselling, legal representation on civil status documentation/registration, as well as on housing, land, and property, prioritizing IDP and returnees. Project will strengthen integrated approaches to GBV response with FSL, SRH and MHPSS through referred cases to each team. A survivor-centred approach will guide all service especially specialized GBV case management and PSS services. Also, establishing and maintaining women and girls safe spaces (WGSS), building women , girls resilience and vocational skills, linking these to livelihood opportunities. While the CP intervention will focus on enhancing child protective environment , response to CP concerns according to CPCM SOP through Structured and sustained PSS activities, case management, parenting program, life skills, awareness-raising, capacity development for CP actors. Also, continue supporting established protection committees, be influential in providing community-based solutions to GBV, CP concerns’ through base and end line, satisfaction survey.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

General Protection Risk1: For community centers: physical security risk due to conflict activity; barriers for certain groups in access centers (limitations on movement); services provided in centers not adequate to address scale/scope of protection issues; sustainability of centers given current funding environment Mitigation risk 1: A. Collaborate with key members on accessibility mainstreaming of centers; B. assess the impact of community centers through baseline and end-line surveys; C. advocate with donors for direct funding to Syrian organizations to ensure continuity of centers; D. ensure center staff is trained on PFA and first aid. GBV Risk 1: Lack of quality services and referral expose survivors to harm, enhancing the risk of stigmatization reprisal and further trauma. Lack of quality services also leads to fewer disclosures and a lack of trust in services. Mitigation risk 1: Service mapping includes only those that can provide survivor-centered services. Promotion of SOPs rolls out for all staff of organizations involved in the referral.GBV case management service providers ensure confidentiality principles are in line with global standards and the GBV SOPs. CP risk 1: Risk of increasing violence in the home and community for girls and boys receiving specialized services. Mitigation for risk: A. ensure training packages are in line with CPMS. B. Services should not be provided by any staff who have not received and demonstrated proficiency in training modules.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: A member of the SR team uses their position of authority as a method of exploiting vulnerable populations. Their actions cause significant long-term damage to this individual or group. All trust between SR and the affected community is damaged. SR faces severe reputational damage and loss of funding.Mitigation: All staff who join SR Project are going through a thorough and transparent recruitment process. References are obtained for all staff during the recruitment process. The Project Team will sign SR PSEA and CSG Policies SR has developed PSEA, Code of Conduct, and Child Safeguarding policies and mechanisms in place to ensure every effort is made to all SR staff and Protection benefciaries.SR PSEA Mechanism is designed around four core components: Awareness Raising (for staff regarding power dynamics, risk, and vulnerabilities, etc.), Prevention (awareness regarding the existing controls which are in place and how PSEA is prevented), Reporting (how participants can report and what are the specific steps which need to be taken regarding SEA cases) and Response (how and what SR will do when cases of SEA are reported). A similar mechanism is available for CSG where the staff are familiar with how to approach and deal with CSG cases of child abuse, exploitation, or violence. All community centers and mobile units’ staff will sign SR CSG and PSEA Policies to ensure a safe environment for all Projectbeneficiaries


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: • All Syrian lands are exposed to the possibility of receiving a ground military offensive initiated by a group against another, aiming to take capture them. This is subjugated to international changes and agreements most of the time.• GoS constant willingness to capture new areas in NWS with GoS support, especially M4 road and the areas surrounding it. Risk: • Staff casualties • Impact or loss of access to targeted areas. • Damage to assets and premises in case of direct targeting. Mitigation: • •Follow up on the recent military and political development in NWS and Syria in general. • Areas assessed are far relatively far from M4, the main axis of a possible coming GoS offensive. • Current Turkish forces presence NWS.• Most NWS areas, including the ones in hand, are full of IDP camps, and attacking such areas would be preceded by several military and political indicators. • Contingency plan to be in place taking into consideration all scenarios including the worst. Assumptions: Weather conditions do not restrict access to beneficiaries Risk: SR will not be able to access affected communities due to extreme or unforeseen weather conditions Mitigation: Activate the SR Contingency Plan and explore alternative routes. Some activities might be suspended during extreme hot or cold days.
