Project Summary

This project aims is to contribute to education support for emergency-aWected school-age children and adolescents, particularly girls, in Adwa and Adigrat woredas of the Tigray region in Ethiopia. Education in Emergency (EiE) support will be provided by ensuring safe, protective and equitable access to formal learning opportunities and improving the quality of education. Out-of-school IDP and host community children, aged 7-18, will be especially targeted through this action. Adolescent girls have been identified as a vulnerable group amongst the beneficiaries given the nature of the conflict which places girls and adolescents at great risk of child protection, GBV and psycho-social related problems. To this end the project seeks to mitigate, prevent and respond to SGBV and child protection issues in formal learning environments. While CP-GBV will be cross cutting across project activities, attention is also paid to SEA in schools for which CFFM will be established, monitored and strengthened throughout the intervention to ensure ownership and continuity. This project is designed to provide a holistic EiE support by also focusing on empowering and better integrating school staff, PTSAs, SMCs and Mother groups in protective, inclusive and quality education. Thus, specific activities will attend to these beneficiary groups. The project will also encompass the EiE coordination network in the Tigray region to reinforce a harmonized approach and improved joint monitoring of and response to the critical needs arising from the conflict.

1. - Projects activities

1. Organize Back to school Campaign to ensure the school age children back to school and attend their learning. Strengthening and establishing parent teacher student associations and Mother in school groups.2. Every child accessing learning opportunities enhance the resumption and continuity of inclusive and quality education and learning for children affected by emergencies through rehabilitation.3. Construct 5 blocks of gender-segregated latrine with 6 holes (1 pit hole for 40 girls nd - 1 pit hole for 50 boys) per school or IDP location4. Procure and provide reusable sanitary pads for adolescent girls with appropriate orientation of Mensural Health Management(MHM) and provide minimal dignity kits.5. Create safe learning environment for adolescent girls.Girls clubs established in schools with support of volunteer especially female teachers and active engagement of adolescent girls.6. Strengthen PTSA and school mothers through providing trainings on the safe school operation and management and on childr protection principles.7.Providing tutor classes to cover missed contents for primary school children.8. Organize three days training to school directors and teachers on adaptive pedagogy , community awareness approaches, safe school operation, transferable life-skills, PSEA- or psycho- emotional Learning (SEL) and Psycho social First Aid (PFA) These activities have been implementing by Plan international Ethiopia capacity.Plan International Ethiopia is the local partner of Plan International Spain, and implementing the project, facilitating humanitarian access to the area of project implementation. PI Ethiopia has a main country office in the capital and an operation office in Tigray. Plan International Spain office technically support and funding the humanitarian acts has been implementing in Ethiopia.A total of 6000 children and adolescents (50% girls and 20% IDPs), aged 7-18 years, will be reached through this action. The wider school community and relevant education and child protection stakeholders at local and regional level will also be targeted through specific activities and thus also considered as direct beneficiaries. The breakdown of direct beneficiaries is as follows: 1. Holders of rights: - 6000 children and adolescent girls and boys in targeted schools in the districts of Adwa and Adigrat of the Tigray region. Plan aims to target at least 50% girls and 20% IDP children and adolescents. Of this group of right holders, 300 adolescent girls will be targeted directly through adolescent girl empowerment activities in schools, 2200 children (50% girls and 20% IDPs) will receive learning materials, at least 2250 children (50% girls and 20% IDPs) will receive tutored catch-up classes and 50 girl students will benefit from cash grants for their enrolment in schools. 2. Holders of responsibilities: - 213 teachers from the 6 targeted schools will benefit from this action. More specifically, 60 teachers and school directors will be trained on EiE and 75 will receive incentives to support tutored catch-up classes. - 30 members of Parent Teacher Student Associations (PTSA), Mother groups and School Management Committees (SMC) will be targeted through CPiE, prevention of SGBV, safeguarding, PSEA and safe school management amongst other awareness raising sessions.3. Holders of obligations: - 40 representatives of education stakeholders, including district education officers, NGO cluster members and school staff representatives of PTSAs and SMCs will be involved in workshops on EiE approaches and strategies to ensure an effective and efficient response.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

At the design stage the stake holders and relevant secretarial actors were consulted. Similarly, the rapid assessment reports done by the region and UN agencies had reviewed. During the project implementation period,the project has a joint monitoring sessions, quarterly review meetings and stakeholder and other CSO actors knowledge creation sessions.Plan International Ethiopia is an active member of the Education in Emergency (EiE) cluster and the Child Protection in Emergency (CPiE) cluster at national level and the respective sub clusters at regional level. The clusters ensure a coordinated response to the conflict and avoid duplicating efforts and resources. Furthermore, Plan has designed an activity to support and ensure the EiE coordination network in the targeted areas is effectively operationalising a harmonised strategy. Plan will provide a two-day training to ensure the effectiveness and efficiency of the EiE response and that adequate coordination increases the level of operational capacity and overall readiness for resilience during the emergency response. The coordination in the project intervention areas encompasses both government and non-government structures. This segment of the community should have common understanding on the EiE implementation strategies and the overall protection issues through capacity building sessions. Thus, this training will be provided not only to the relevant humanitarian agencies and NGOs in the targeted areas but also district education officers from the government. Plan International has a results-based monitoring system, with the aim of using the information generated to optimize execution, enable accountability and learning. The monitoring system includes: 1. The project-specific monitoring system allows for regular data collection to compare actual and expected achievements. The monitoring system will be managed and supervised by the project coordination team, with the support of the Monitoring and Evaluation specialist from the national office, as well as supported by Plan International Spain 2. Outcome and impact indicators set achievements throughout the project. 3. The reporting and review of results by specialized technical staff (EiE, gender, compliance) allows for verification of the fulfillment of goals and the correct execution of activities according to the logical framework and timeline, and for adjustments to be made. 4. Final external evaluation: it provides complementary and independent information on the results and impact achieved, planned and unplanned, and provides lessons learned and good practices, aimed at internal learning. 5. Plan has a global strategy of accountability as well as ensures the participation of the target group in all phases of the project, including specific measures to ensure equal participation of men and women, boys and girls as well as promoting girls and women's leadership and participation in decision making.
