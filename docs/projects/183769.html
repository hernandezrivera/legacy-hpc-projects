Project Summary

The project aims to provide multi-sectorial emergency assistance in Eastern Sudan (Kassala/Gedaref/Red Sea) and North Darfur, through provision of WASH, FSL, Protection and Shelter/NFIs services. This project
also mainstreaming Covid-19 in all locations and protection activities focus on sensitization and awareness raising of key protection issues. This project mainly targets IDPs and vulnerable host communities members. In North Darfur and in Kassala the project interlinks with a BMZ project to operationalize the Triple NEXUS, while in Red Sea the project interlinks with SIDA resilience project. For FSL component targets group focused on Host Communities and IDPs in the Eastern Sudan and North Darfur. Small holder pastroralist and agropastoralist farmers will be targeted, using different criteria including vulnerabilities level. Women and youth will be prioritized for activities such as skills development training.

1 - Needs

With this project,WHH will respond to the needs of IDPs and vulnerable HCs in different sectors across 4 states of Sudan.North Darfur:Localities Al Lait, Mellit, Kutum, Al Kuma, Seref Omra,El Fasher Rural and Kebkabiya. In comparison to other localities within ND, both Al Lait and Kebkabiya localities are remotely located.Some of those localities such as Al Lait, Kebkabiya, Kutum, Mellit are hosting high number of IDPs and humanitarian needs are uncovered due to limited funding opportunities. According to the HNO 2021 there are 1.44M people in need in the state, 0.78M are in extreme need. The mentioned above localities are also experiencing high level of food insecurity.According to the last IPC report those localities are categorized as in crisis (Phase 3), the main drivers of food insecurity are economic crisis, inflation together with floods and conflicts.There is still a huge gap in accessing to drinking water and improved sanitation, shelter and NFI as well as agricultural inputs that can contribute to saving lives of the IDPs and vulnerable host community members. In Al Lait existence of both the South Sudanese refugees and the IDPs within their host communities is exerting pressure on the existing limited resources such as water points due to competition between the displaced population and the host community members over access to drinking water. Protection risks are still high in the state and there is little knowledge of protection and GBV among communities. Kassala: Localities Wad El Helew. Kassala state has a total of 0.67M people in need, including 0.41 people in extreme need. The project will target vulnerable host communities that are living around the refugees camps (Shagarab and Abuda).Living conditions of host communities are similar as the refugees’ camps. Lack of access to drinking water, improved sanitation and inadequate hygiene practices is affecting vulnerable host communities around the refugees’ camp. In terms of food security Kassala,according to IPC projection for Oct. 2021 to Feb. 2022 the locality is categorized as stressed (Phase 2). Protection risks are still high in the state and there is little knowledge of protection and GBV among communities.Gedaref:Localities El Fashaga, Galabat Ash-Shargiah, Ar Rahad, Al Mafaza. The project will target most vulnerable HCs in the above mentioned localities. Host communities living around refugees camps in Gedaref are experiencing the same needs as the camp population in terms of water quality, quantity and access to improved sanitation. Furthermore, those localities population are in an acute food insecurity situation, with the level of IPC is categorized as phase 3, in crisis. In addition, there is a need for support for short-term food production and food use and processing to help meet frequent supply shortages. Red Sea:Localities Tokar, Agig, Sinkat,Dordieb, Haya. WHH is one of the few humanitarian agencies present in Red Sea State, providing assistance to vulnerable host community in the state. The state has 0.55M of people in need and 100,769 people are in need of water, 218,180 in need of sanitation and 223,337 people in need of hygiene services, with Haya,Tokar and Sinkat being the locality with the highest number of people in need.Red Sea is also classified IPC Phase 3 (crisis).

2 - Activities/Outputs

The project will address the needs of IDPs and vulnerable host communities through a multi-sector response. In the area of WASH the project aims to improve the access to safe and sufficient water supply, through rehabilitation of water sources, such as Hafir, Water yard (including solarization), etc. The project also aims at improving access to sanitation facilities, through CLTS approach at HHs level and construction of sanitation facilities at community spaces level. Hygiene and COVID-19 awareness-raising is part of this component, but it will be mainstreamed in the project. Wash activities are implemented in North Darfur, Red Sea and Gedaref states. In the area of FSL, the project aims to provide timely food support and enhance the self-reliance of the target group by providing training in agriculture and livelihood activities, seeds and tools distribution and livestock support. The states targeted with FSL assistance are ND, KAS,Ged,RS. In the area of SNFI, the project aims to provide vulnerable host communities and IDPs with essential SNFI support. The response also includes prepositioning of key SNFI items, market assessments as well as sensitization and training for the target group on mitigating the impact of natural disaster to improve preparedness. For this component, ND is targeted. IDPs and host communities alike are targeted for protection sensitization which is very relevant to the context since their localities host refugee populations who feature increased protection vulnerability. Community based protection network will be established in Kassala and North Darfur and supported with capacity building (protection, SGBV case identification and referral, complaints mechanisms), linkages to line Ministries and relevant camp actors, and in-kind support (stationaries) for their daily management. For Protection ND, Ged and Kassala are the targeted states. The project will be carried out in line with WHH MEAL standards and respective activities such as base/end-line, continuous monitoring and a final evaluation. Specific selection criteria will make sure that most vulnerable groups are addressed taking vulnerability criteria such as age, gender and disability into account.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The intervention area is characterized by resource pressure and degradation of natural resources. Water is a key natural resource which is scarce in all the targeted areas. The project aims to enlarge access to save drinking water through rehabilitation/construction of water sources and reduce the potential for deforestation through promotion of fuel-efficient/solar stoves among the target groups, as mitigation measures. Through other more resilience interventions, WHH will also aim at increasing locally available seeds through the establishment of communal storages and seed banks as well as communal forests. Those measures/activities applied in other interventions will also benefit the present project.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

WHH mainstreams protection into all programs so that activities (i) prioritize safety, dignity and access, in addition to avoiding causing harm, (ii) ensure equality and meaningful access, (iii) include mechanisms for beneficiary accountability, and (iv) emphasize participation and empowerment to meet the project minimum requirements. WHH prioritizes the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and avoids causing harm by preventing and minimizing as much as possible any unintended negative effects of its interventions that can increase vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. The project will ensure that targeted beneficiaries have safe access to assistance and services based on need and without discrimination. WHH considers people with specific needs, ensuring access to assistance and services. Through community-based monitoring, target populations will be able to measure the adequacy of interventions and address concerns and complaints, thereby supporting the development of self-protection capacities and assisting people to claim their rights, including – not exclusively – the right to shelter, food, water, sanitation, etc.WHH will adhere to Sphere Protection Principles to implement activities under this project according the following: (1) Enhance people’s safety, dignity and rights and avoid exposing them to further harm; (2) Ensure people’s access to impartial assistance, according to need and without discrimination; (3) Assist people to recover from the physical and psychological effects of threatened or actual violence, coercion or deliberate deprivation; and (4) Help people claim their rights. These three principles are interdependent and can be carried out at the same time. The principles promote the right to dignity in three ways: • mainstreaming protection risks, activities and related information across all humanitarian programs.• integrating specific protection objectives into assistance projects; and• promoting specific and/or specialized protection activities that address specific protection risks and violations.In addition, WHH takes care to design program activities that consider findings from a risk analysis. The agency also actively looks for ways to minimize threats and vulnerabilities and work with different groups of the affected population, specific at-risk groups, and the organizations they trust. Such approaches help WHH understand how best to address needs and any positive or negative consequences.
