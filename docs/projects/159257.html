Project Summary

The overall objective of the project over 12 months is to respond to the immediate shelter needs of vulnerable populations in Area C communities surrounding areas A and B of the West Bank. This response will be through self-help approach for the most vulnerable families to provide shelter repairs and transitional shelter solutions close to existing homes, in order to: 1) Promote self-reliance, maintain, and support most vulnerable families who live with the effect of violence, coercion, and deprivation with shelter related solutions. 2) Enhancing the protection and reducing the likelihood of displacement or forced eviction. 3) Contributing in solving the problem of overcrowding houses by creating new spaces through rearrangement to ensure privacy and dignity 4) promoting more environmental friendly housing, cost and energy efficient building practices and reducing the cost of living by adopting green approaches. 5) Extend the life span of existing shelters; insight of the nullity of the issuance of building permits in Area C. 6) supporting access to job opportunities in Area C and creating short-term employment opportunities. 7) Better understanding of the scope of the need for urgent housing rehabilitation in Area C and the way to address them, advocacy and awareness raising trainings. Thus, complying with the second HRP strategic objectives “SO2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of essential services in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL”. And complying with the shelter cluster strategic objectives “Vulnerable households exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns are supported to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacity” and “Capacity to respond to natural or manmade hazards in a timely, efficient and effective manner is increased”.
This project complies with PHC’s Rehabilitation Program objectives, aiming at contributing of elimination some poverty effects on vulnerable women HH’s, improving the quality of the proper housing stock, reducing land designation for housing and infrastructure by restoring the existing housing stock and improving the social, economic, and health conditions for the beneficiaries. The project will provide urgent housing rehabilitation to 50 FHH or vulnerable families in Area C of the West Bank whose shelter is unfit for weather conditions, overcrowded, and/or unsafe as described hereafter.

1 - Needs

The Israeli occupation and the separation wall in the West Bank have negatively affected Palestinian communities especially women’s and children’s lives and rights. These policies significantly increase the humanitarian vulnerability of Palestinians and affect their residency status, economic situation, access to education, health services, and their ability to plan and develop their communities. The lack of planning and the nullity issuance of building permits increases the likelihood of displacement and forced eviction of Palestinians. as a result, displaced women and girls lose the space where they enjoy privacy, autonomy, social networks and the center of their lives and are negatively affected by occupation-related political, social and economic oppression. Safety, security risks and threats, including the violence perpetrated against women, the restrictions imposed on their freedom of movement, and the family forced displacement, are direct consequences of the Israeli occupation policies and measures, as perpetrated by the Israeli military, settlers, and systematic discrimination. These threats are reflected as domestic and social violence for females, and military violence for males. In addition to the declining economic conditions across Palestine led to a situation of a chronic shortfall of housing, and an inability of vulnerable families to maintain their current housing units.According to shelter NFIS 2020 HNO depending on vulnerability survey updated 2019: 3,450 shelters are in poor condition and in need of urgent interventions The most vulnerable are currently denied or restricted in their access to essential services. Elderly women are more vulnerable to poverty, disease, and have limited access to services. There is no social security system in place to ensure a decent life for the elderly and to provide different life requirements, such as housing, healthcare, income, disability needs. Palestinians are expected to continue to depend on their children in their old age instead of social security, and the burden of the career role is often placed on the shoulders of women. Disability remains a critical source of vulnerability, that can lead to gender-based discrimination. Women and girls with disabilities suffer from amplified forms of GBV and have limited access to services.In September 2019, PHC updated the assessment done in 2018 as a joint work between PHC and Ministry of Social Development MoSD and approved a list of 50 vulnerable families in urgent need for assistance to rehabilitate their houses in Ramallah, Salfit, Jericho, and EJ (outside the wall) in Area C surrounding area A and B in the West Bank. Through the selection criteria, PHC Grading System identifies the neediest beneficiaries, this system assigns that divorced and widowed women or FHH have a high weight that leads to increase their benefit chances in 2020. Furthermore, this system designed to respond to different segments of the beneficiaries (women, men, boys, girls & elderly) and their different needs. Findings of the assessment in Area C mainly were:1. 50 HH who live in Area C of the following governorates (Ramallah 20 HH, Jerusalem 15 HH, Salfit 5 HH, Jericho 10 HH ) in emergency need. 2. Inadequate housing conditions increase the likelihood of displacement from area C to areas A and B, in particular for FHH families.3. Weak capacities for self-protection and limitation of the Palestinian Authorities.4. Nullity of issuance of building permits in Area C made keeping and maintaining the existing shelters in that area very significant especially for the vulnerable families.5. FHH often have less capacity to do the basic repair work themselves.6. Some HH's have family members with disabilities reducing the chances for the active members to seek employment and limiting their income mostly due to social welfare related to the disability.

2 - Activities

PHC’s methodology ensures the participation of different gender/age targeted groups in the project cycle; starting with the needs assessments, implementation, monitoring & evaluation. PHC will develop a proactive approach to emergency preparedness, in order to ensure early action is taken when required. This approach includes risk monitoring, the establishment of coordination and management arrangements and preparing for joint needs assessments along with the Palestinian official bodies especially MoSD, CBO’s. The official bodies will provide and support services inside the community using available resources, voluntary and skills, and will manage adverse conditions that potentially could lead to an emergency, this will lead to increase beneficiaries coping capacity and contributing to building resilience. PHC aims to avoid, minimize and mitigate the risks associated the delivery of this project, by identifying risks of the targeted beneficiaries in terms of safety, dignity, do no harm by mitigating physical & sociological threats through prioritizing intervention to provide the most appropriate assistance to beneficiaries in a without discrimination or exposing them to further risk. PHC approach is also guided by Meaningful access related to the provision of its assistance (i.e. handicapped, elderly). Accountability: maximizing transparency and setup inclusive and accessible feedback & complaint mechanism. Beneficiaries’ participation and empowerment: ensuring that the beneficiaries are actively involved & participated in all the process; decision-making, implementation, monitoring, evaluation & management. They are the key element to analysis their needs and the means of solving their problems. Since the building is prohibited in area C. The project will contribute to reducing the likelihood of displacement of vulnerable families to areas A or B through upgrading existing unsuitable shelters to avoid displacement from area C to area A or B.Output 1: 50 shelters of FHH’s and vulnerable families in area C are made adequate for living. 1. Define the outstanding beneficiaries: re-visit the vulnerable families according to the above-mentioned assessment, technical & social information gathering, and discussions with the suggested beneficiaries to gather their needs & concerns at all levels with special attention to people with disabilities, children, elderly & FHH i.e. widowed or divorced. 2. Specify the Rehabilitation budget for each beneficiary: Preparation of preliminary social & technical reports, & preliminary bill of quantities and cost estimate for each case. The project Committees will then list the eligible cases for 2020. 3. Preparation of technical documents: preparation of the necessary work documents and BoQ’s. 4. Sign agreement with beneficiaries & determine the amount of each grant according to activity 2 with an average amount of $5100. 5. Determine the number of payments for each beneficiary according to the beneficiaries' grant. The payments will be paid according to each subproject progress. 6. Work in each shelter will be monitored & supervised on a daily basis; the final payment will be conditional by the completion of all works. 7. Community capacity building & awareness-raising: kick-off workshops will be conducted with beneficiaries to clarify the project methodology, guidelines, complaint mechanism, rights and obligations, and will train them on technical practices for basic repairs, and encourage them to get voluntary work from the local community. PHC will respond to their concerns and inquires & will encourage them to share feedback on the provided assistance, potential encountered issues and/or remaining needs to be addressed. The workshops will enrich their knowledge through discussions concerning their living standards and a better life for the elderly. The workshops will contain a part of how the beneficiaries will negotiate with workers and contractors 8. M&E: Please see the indicators section.

3 - Indicators

The indicators of the project will be 1) Shelter rehabilitation and repair through providing cash or materials assistance and technical training: 50 vulnerable families are supported with rehabilitation of substandard shelter in Area C in 2020. 2) Provision of winterization assistance or household NFIs to vulnerable households at risk of exposure or affected by natural or manmade hazards: 28 HH's exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns are supported with shelter assistance PHC aims to achieve the optimum results regarding beneficiaries by defining the aptness support for emergency vulnerable families at a reasonable cost. Previous phases of the program showed that small grants make a significant difference in the lives of vulnerable families at several levels: At the community level, PHC formed the Advisory Committee, which consists of community female and male representatives who acquaint with the areas of the target group and familiar with their needs and challenges; to support the program implementation and participate in the selection of the beneficiaries according to housing conditions and socio-economic situation of families. PHC involves beneficiaries in the different stages of the project cycle. One of the assessment criteria is to involve the families in determining their needs according to their gender and age (e.g. vulnerable FHH’s, the elderly,…etc) or individuals with special needs. At the monitoring and evaluation level, PHC will monitor the progress of works on a daily basis. The monitoring process for this project will be based on regular field visits (PHC, Shelter cluster, and OCHA), and periodic reporting. As a result of the field visits reports the Project Committee will share feedback and information concerning challenges and issues raised on-site to partners. PHC always aims to provide assistance in ways that are supportive of recovery and long term development, and do its best to “do no harm” by concealing them from the additional risk Evaluation process will be based on a questionnaire, financial audit, meetings, and quality assurance process. It is worth to note that the questionnaire is a tool designed and used in the recent phases of the program to indicate the impact on the living conditions of the most vulnerable families, the degree of safety and adequacy, quality of work, and the progress achieved. And in order to assess the changes that were attributed to the project intervention, as well as to examine whether targets have been achieved. Knowing that one of the Project committee mandates is to implement and monitor the project to be gender-balanced. Through the evaluation process, the changes occurred in terms of safety, dignity, and integrity will be measured separately for women, men, girls, and boys where according to this project 225 are direct beneficiaries; 121 Females, 104 males, 81 Children, 34 elderly. PHC pays special attention to the environmental issues; neutral impact on the environment with mitigation or enhancement, to achieve this PHC improved technical practices for basic repairs, resulting in more energy-efficient and environmentally friendly living environments. PHC will take into consideration during the implementation of the project measures to promote more environmentally friendly housing, cost, and energy-efficient building practices.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

PHC in coordination with the community will prioritize intervention to provide the most appropriate assistance to beneficiaries without discrimination or exposing them to additional risk, in addition, the project team has sufficient experience to be aware of protection concerns identified during rehabilitation activities in a safe and confidential manner. Thus, PHC will pay attention to the following issues:In terms of safety, dignity, and do-no-harm:1) Through regular site visits and the committees' meetings; PHC will ensure that beneficiaries are providing efficient lighting throughout the site, and are respecting the minimum space standards in shelters to minimize risks of exploitation and abuse.3) Through preparing appropriate BOQ's and discussions with beneficiaries; PHC will ensure accessibility to disables and elderly, and will ensure including partitions and door locks to better protect women and girls, particularly FHH’s.In terms of meaningful access:1) Through applying the PHC accountability framework; PHC will prioritize people and groups based on their needs.2) Through preparing appropriate BOQ's and discussions with beneficiaries; PHC will ensure that the housing unit is accessible and appropriate to all groups and individuals, in particular, people with physical or mental disabilities, elderly and women, and will ensure that bathrooms are accessible to persons with disabilities and elderly, and safe for small children.In terms of accountability, participation, and empowerment:1) Through applying the PHC accountability framework; PHC will maximize transparency by its accessible feedback and complaint mechanism and will conduct regular discussions with individuals of different ages, gender, particularly those with specific needs on housing unit issues, to ensure that any protection concerns highlighted are discussed and resolved.2) Through the awareness-raising workshops; PHC will ensure that the local community is involved in project planning for convenient implementation.3) Through the workshops with beneficiaries and the regular site visits and the M&E procedures; PHC will ensure providing the appropriate technical and construction assistance, and will reinforce the community’s self-help capacity by encouraging traditional construction methods, particularly in areas where transportation of materials is restricted. PHC will also ensure that women will have the opportunity to equally participate in the process; equip beneficiaries with knowledge of safe construction practices. in addition to involving persons with disabilities and the elderly in all project cycle in order to have accurate information about their specific needs and constraints.5) Through regular site visits; PHC will monitor the implementation process to avoid exploitative and fraudulent practices, and will ensure avoiding and monitoring any exploitative labor especially child labor on construction sites.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

PHC has several practices and fundamentals for earning trust and gaining access to vulnerable people, as transparency, confidentiality, consistency, proximity with vulnerable people, and timely response to the needs. The qualifications of PHC staff are also critical to equal and impartial access to assistance. PHC Communicating transparently with all influential actors for explaining the projects' goals and operational alternatives and constraints. In addition, to explaining policies or practices sometimes in case of confidentiality PHC conduct a bilateral dialogue with actors to maintain the degree of acceptance necessary to gain the safe access needed to deliver impartial assistance. The PHC Consistency springs mainly from its mission that aims at contributing to solving the housing crisis by contributing to providing low and middle-income Palestinian families with the opportunity for adequate housing through soft loans or grants. To ensure equal and impartial access to aid: 1) PHC in this project has updated the above-mentioned assessment as a joint work with the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) and approved a list of 50 HH of the most vulnerable who live in Area C of the following governorates (Ramallah 20 HH, Jerusalem 15 HH, Salfit 5 HH, Jericho 10 HH ) who are in emergency need 2) PHC will promote the project in local newspapers, PHC Web Page and social media, and disseminate the promotion to the related actors to make sure that it reached the maximum number of the targeted population in need, knowing that receiving applications from vulnerable families is open all over the year for rehabilitation works in the WB and EJ. 3) After revisiting the outstanding beneficiaries; PHC through its Committees (Project and Advisory) will study, review and list the eligible beneficiaries according to PHC’s Grading System. 4) PHC will support the beneficiaries to access to legal aid through establishing a proper linkage between them and the SC legal entity (Information Counselling Legal Assistance program ICLA) that works in Area C, this entity will advocate beneficiaries before the commencement of the rehabilitation process and during/after if needed. The service of this entity is gratis. Also, PHC legal advisor is available to guide the beneficiaries and provide them as well as the project team with necessary legal support to ensure the accessibility of the assistance to all selected beneficiaries.All the above-mentioned processes will be guided with PHC accountability framework and PHC M&E procedures, taking into account the cross-cutting issues (protection, environment and gender/age) mainstreaming.
