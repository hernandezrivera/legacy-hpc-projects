Project Summary

Mercy Corps proposes to implement a 12-months project to carry on and build up current lifesaving water, sanitation and hygiene services for returnees, host and IDPs communities in Unity State and Western Equatoria. The program aims to reach a total of 120,829 individuals in Unity (70,829) and Mundri East (50,000).
The goal of the program will be to ensure access to clean water, access to sanitation facilities, hygiene promotion (including NFI distribution) and enhancements to environmental health conditions. The intensified package will implement comprehensive hygiene behavior-change components, robust integration with health and nutrition interventions, and fully streamlined gender and protection needs as identified via implementation of current OFDA and UNICEF funding and protection partners.
The main components of the program will be:
Provision of adequate safe clean water supply to households and community structures through ensuring access to water infrastructures that include rehabilitation of broken/damaged boreholes and drilling of new boreholes with rigorous water quality testing.
Provision of dignified sanitation infrastructure using community participation strategy to host communities and returnees and construction of semi-permanent structures for IDPs will be implored with tailored designs for people with disabilities/older adults and children.
Extended, all-inclusive hygiene promotion with a focus on behaviour change interventions and menstrual hygiene and NFI distributions that address acute basic needs and facilitate individual ownership of personal and community health and well-being.
Health, nutrition, food security, and WASH integration to mitigate WASH-related illness and morbidity and malnutrition rates via referral tracking systems and integrated outbreak response planning. Coordination with Health, Nutrition and Protection clusters to ensure an integrated timely response to any need. Mainstreaming of Menstruation Hygiene Management among school going children with an aim to increase school enrolment among girls.

Needs

UNITY STATEMercy Corps has been and continues to provide water, sanitation and hygiene services in different locations of South Sudan including Rubkona and Koch since 2014. As a result of continued conflict and cattle raids, multiple displacements were done compromising the already crippled WASH services. Many populations remain in remote rural locations which has limited to or no basic WASH services. Existing WASH structures in these locations is unable to provide adequate WASH services due to population increase and movement. Communities are in dire need of water, sanitation and hygiene services in Unity state. The widespread lack of safe drinking water, improved sanitation facilities and poor hygiene practices has left large proportion of communities at risk of preventable WASH-related diseases like cholera and Hepatitis E. All efforts towards improving access to safe drinking water, hygiene, and sanitation should be enhanced to halt the spread of cholera. The WASH needs for the communities of Unity state are but not limited to:There are in need of adequate safe water supply infrastructures in the state most of it was damaged and vandalised during the conflict and cattle raids. The existing water infrastructures need monitoring to ensure the quality of water is still meeting the minimum standards.Communities lack access to dignified sanitary facilities. There is insufficient basic WASH services and facilities at schools, nutrition and health centres which put population lives at risk missing school days, malnutrition and WASH related diseases. People with disabilities do not have access and dignified WASH services because of limited WASH services in Unity stateDue to the multi displacements adolescent girls are missing schools because of lack of dignified menstrual management resources.Insecurity makes most people not to access items to enhance hygiene practices like water containers, hand washing facilities.As malnutrition and food insecurity increases, communities become more prone to WASH-related diseases. WESTERN EQUATORIAMundri East is one of the areas that have suffered serious security episodes in Western Equatoria since the conflict broke out in 2016 . Insecurity in Mundri East has severely hampered humanitarian efforts particularly in Kediba, Witto, Karikyere and Lakamadi. This turbulent context provides the perfect conditions for outbreaks of disease. About 900 HH of IDPs from Mundri East are currently hosted in Mundri West (Mundri Town).In the most recent rapid assessments conducted by Mercy Corps, communities were most concerned about security, as they could not access water points early in the morning or late in the evening, as most of the armed groups usually access the water points at those times. IDPs reported access to water within the camps, but not outside the camps. Poor hygiene practices such as open defection, use of dirty clothes or cotton instead of sanitary pads, hand washing without soap or ash are common practices in Mundri East. It is estimated that an average of 66% of households practice open defecation in Mundri East, which has the potential to contaminate other water sources. Low latrine coverage is attributed to lack of digging materials. In Mundri East, only 82 boreholes were functional out of 156. Diarrhea is among the 3 most prevalent diseases in the area.Mercy Corps is currently implementing a WASH program in Mundri East. However, the program targets only 10,000 individuals leaving out close to 120,000 people who are in dire need of WASH services.
