Project Summary

The project aims to contribute to the overall efforts in Idleb and Aleppo governorates to reduce mortality
and morbidity rates and increase the resilience of the target communities by maintaining the ongoing health services within the targeted locations and facilities in order not to overburden the existing health system in the area in case of closing any of these facilities.This project aims to continue supporting three secondary health care facilities and specialized PT center in addition to 2 thalassemia clinics and related blood banks as well as 21 PHCs (level 2) and 2 MCs, 3 cholera central treatment units for 12 months by assuming the running costs, staff salaries and providing medicines and consumables. 3 - Needs

According to OCHA, situation in NWS remains grim, with 3.4 million persons in need of humanitarian aid. Out of the total 4.4 million persons in NWS, over 70% are considered people in acute need of life-saving assistance. With health facilities already stretched thin given the high level of needs in NWS, the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic has added further strain to an already fragile healthcare system. By the end of 2021, over 4 million people were in need of basic and life-saving medical services. Lack of fuel safe drinking water low level of public health awareness, and suspension of many health facilities led to increased morbidity and mortality rates, consequently further jeopardizing the resilience of affected people in NWS, especially vulnerable people. Provision of basic health services with focus on vulnerability of host and IDP populations, is high importance in the winter season, particularly the most vulnerable people such as children, elderly and people suffering from chronic diseases, including asthma and other upper respiratory infections to avoid the harsh winter weather has a serious negative impact on the health of over 4 million vulnerable people. Equitable access to health care services, including medical consultations, medicine, referral services, and reproductive and child health services is essential to avoid an increase in morbidity rates, especially during winter months.there is a significant gap in secondary healthcare services in northern Aleppo.

Also, service providers will be periodically trained on such topics.Risk 2: Risk: safe closure of facilities and protecting data. (likelihood: low, impact: high) Mitigation: Electronic data and information are properly protected and backed up according to IDA SOPs - IDA has develope its own exit strategy guidance to exit in emergencies.Risk 3: Spread of COVID-19 in the project facilities.(Likelihood: high, impact: high)Mitigation: COVID-19 mitigation procedure will be in place during the project and will include social distance between beneficiaries and the regulation of the patients flow in a way that keeps all of them safe where the crowding will not be allowed at all at the entry of the facility or within the waitin hall or at the clinics. 8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Maintaining a gender balance in the organization staff to ensure better inclusion and mitigate the optional risk of PSEA and in order to ensure a comfortable atmosphere for the beneficiaries. The organization has an established community-based feedback and response mechanism to include a PSEA hotline to report suspected cases and no tolerance towards SEA. The organization's staff, partners, and sub-partners are required to sign a code of conduct and receive training in child safeguarding and SEA protection with clear protocols on reporting potential SEA of beneficiaries, staff and children staff, and children.1- Active PSEA reporting system available in all the community centers and have clearly visible signs with guidance on how to register complaints and feedback.2- BNFs has access to reporting phone number and feedback boxes3- Training the staff and providing awareness to the communities 4- Mixed-gender follow-up committee 5- Coordination and communication with PSEA working group6- IDA PSEA and safeguarding policy is applied.7-accessible environment and CBFMs for PWDs including CWDs.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: The ever-changing security situation may badly affect the response and result in a mass influx of new IDPs including children and children with disabilities. (likelihood: weak, impact:high) Mitigation: Safety and security measures and emergency plans will be set and considered before and during the implementation period. Project activities will be designed flexibly to be modified appropriately with the changeable security situation.Implementation sites could be located in safer places as needed in coordination with the Health Cluster. Risk 2: the UN cross-border resolution is not extended (Likelihood: mid, impact: high) Mitigation: Intensive advocacy, in addition to coordination with the international NGOs to secure medicine and medical consumables. Risk 3: The local authorities decide to prevent IDA from implementing the project or part of it. (likelihood:weak, impact: mid)Mitigation: Intensive advocacy, negotiation, and coordination with local councils, stakeholders. Risk 4: The lack of well-qualified specialties in the targeted location (likelihood: mid, impact: high) Mitigation: IDA will communicate with the health directorates to ensure that the required specialties are recruited. Risk 5: Corruption and abuse use of cash (Likelihood: mid, impact: high) Mitigation: IDA will assure that all accounting and financial management practices are in line with IDA FinancialInstructions.
