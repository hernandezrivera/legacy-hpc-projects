Project Summary

SO1. In a response to the continued deterioration of the food and nutrition situation and the increased reliance and adoption of negative coping strategies that impacted the resilience of households as many as 77 per cent of households reported buying less expensive and less preferred foods resulting in poorer diets, this project aims to contribute to increased food security for vulnerable displaced and host community households in Idleb and Aleppo Governorates. It has 2 main components Household Food Security and Bakery Support. Within the Household Food Security component, NGO X targets an average of 65,000 HH/ 325,000 food-insecure individuals per month with food kits, cash for food, or value vouchers. The regular food assistance is to the value of 70% of the food SMEB (Survival minimum expenditure basket) or 70% of the Sphere minimum standards of 2100 kcal/p/day.
The Bakery Support component provides subsidized bread to individuals living in supported bakeries ’catchment areas with bread at a reduced and stabilized price. This is achieved through subsidizing up to 40% input (flour and yeast) of bread production thus supporting local bakeries to produce a stable quantity of bread that meets local needs. This support is conditional upon the selling of this bread in the local markets at a reduced and stabilized price agreed with and monitored by NGO X’s Cash and Markets, and Monitoring Teams. Approximately 33 bakeries are targeted to benefit from this assistance.
SO3. Also, NGO X will be conducting a rehabilitation activity for 5 bakeries in both of Idleb and Aleppo, the component will be used as an emergency response to the contracted bakeries under the clustering objective of SO3.
NGOX will implement the proposed project in partnership with 3 NGOs. Please note that these 3 NGOs will input into the OCHA HPC separately under this project. This decision was agreed between all parties and submissions have been coordinated to ensure there is no duplication with regards to either budget or beneficiary targets.
No funding is yet secured for 2023, but with high likelihood of doing so. NGO X has permission to operate in all proposed locations.

3 - Needs

In 2023, an estimated 15 million people will need some form of food and agriculture assistance, representing 68% of the total population, the result shows a 7% increase in the PiN from last year. At least 12.1 million people are estimated to be food insecure, including 2 million people living in camps, and 2.9 million people are estimated to be at risk of sliding into food insecurity. The food security crisis in Syria is” a protracted crisis” and remains characterized by very complex drivers, from economic worsening and weakened local currency, to drought-like conditions and severe agro-climatic fluctuations, water scarcity and limited energy supply and population movements. In NWS, an estimated 4.1 million people (out of 4.5 million) in need of assistance at the end of 2022. 2.8 million people in NWS are IDPs, many of them still in tents and makeshift shelters. Energy and food prices have risen considerably as the crisis drags on Syria has not been spared from this economic tumor as of September 2022, the price of a WFP standard reference food basket reached up 40% since the onset of the crisis in Ukraine and nearly increased four-fold in two years. Cholera continues to rapidly spread in NWS. Until 26 November, there were 16,389 suspected cholera cases in NWS, of which 408 were confirmed positive. 12 people died during the same time because of cholera. The water crisis affects access to sufficient and safe drinking water, irrigation, food production and power generation.

4 - Implementation of activities

SO1. The HH food security component will support food security for HHs through CVA or food kit distribution. Each HH will receive 8 rounds of assistance. NGO X plans to continue to transition HHs from kits to vouchers and cash as conditions allow, , using the selection vulnerability criteria, CVA will be provided through both modalities; cash transfers and, vouchers for food, having this applied to the same HH, NGO X will maintain following a best practice so the HH will get the big portion of the total transfer value as cash in hand, and the leftover amount as voucher top-up to be redeemed from the contracted shops. NGO X will provide blanket distributions in IDP camps due to high vulnerability of the target populations. The second component is bakery support, which will advance the HHs access to diverse foods in adequate quantities, and communities access to bread at affordable prices. NGO X will support 33 bakeries, providing required flour and yeast inputs to ensure high-quality bread which will be made available to the bakery catchment population at an agreed affordable weight and price (up to 38%reduction of the current market bread price). Routine monitoring including DM and PDM will be conducted for all distribution-based activities. For the bakery component, bakery feedback surveys, shopkeeper satisfaction surveys and bread beneficiaries survey will be conducted, in addition to the rehabilitation activity under bakeries business support component for 5 bakeries.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Tension between host communities, IDPs and returnees Mitigation: a) Ensure that the response is designed based on needs among b) Consult both IDPs and members of the host community when assessing needs and designing activities. Promote the active participation of women and other at-risk groups in all consultations c) Inform people in targeted locations about selection criteria to ensure transparency d) Target vulnerable host community in every intervention that aims to support the IDPs e) Raise awareness of NGO X’s feedback mechanism f) Involve the local stakeholders throughout the process 2. Method and location of distribution of cash or vouchers can put vulnerable beneficiaries at risk of robbery, exploitation, or extortion. Risk of cash and or voucher value diversion to non-humanitarian purposes. Mitigation: a) Distribution methodology with clear protection risks analyzed and mitigated b) Select safe and accessible distribution points in consultation with the beneficiaries and local authorities c) Organise separate queues for men and women at the distribution points d) Raise awareness about NGO X’s compliant mechanism and reporting channels e) Arrange home deliveries for the most vulnerable beneficiaries f) Verify beneficiary list to prevent cash or voucher value being misused for other non-humanitarian purposes.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Child-headed/Orphans: Abuse from the children guardians / authorized people for the assistance Mitigation: • Commitment from the authorized people on the PSEA policy• The protection team contact the children regularly (after obtaining the consent from the delegates) to check their status• Awareness sessions for the child-headed HH on how to prevent/ report such cases R: Sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) by staff, vendors, and contractors M: NGOX to consult women, men, girls and boys and persons with disabilities and older people about their access to delivery mechanisms and identify any additional safeguarding measures - Conduct PSEA trainings with staff and inform of sanctions if PSEA policies are violated- Ensure Code of Conduct and PSEA protocols are shared and signed by vendors and contractors- Monitor the safety of delivery mechanisms R: Sexual exploitation/abuse for beneficiaries at or travelling to and from distribution pointsM: Distribution site considerations include proximity to active hostilities, presence of armed actors, timing of distributions and other risks. Distributions are close to beneficiary villages so they do not have fear to travel, to minimize the risk of GBV and theft. NGOX ensures they are accessible and safe to travel to in daylight and all distributions are undertaken in daylight. If beneficiaries can’t attend distribution points they can authorize someone to collect their assistance for them, or home delivery can be organized


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1:Deteriorated security situation making access more difficult Mitigation: NGO X has established a remote monitoring mechanism, with multiple WhatsApp rooms, including bakery owners, bread distributors, local councils etc. This mechanism provides NGO X with sufficient monitoring data to ensure bakeries can continue to produce subsidized bread. Assumption 1: NGO X & partners can maintain long term access and if accesses restricted that it is temporary with the above mitigation measures are employed during a short-term access issue. Risk 2:Syrian pound depreciation against USDMitigation: Vouchers and cash distributions are provided in USD, which is the most stable currency in circulation which protects the transfer value provided to the beneficiaries. FGD are carried out with community representatives regularly to understand the impact of currency fluctuations. Assumption 2: That sufficient USD notes are in circulation in Northwest Syria to distribute at the scale NGO X & partners intend to. USD is allowed to be distributed by local authorities in NWS. Risk 3:Supply issues as Vendors are unable to provide needed goodsMitigation: The availability of goods in the markets are monitored through monthly market price monitoring with REACH and PDMs. Contracted vendors are given notice of voucher distributions so they can re-stock accordingly. NGO X has a Feedback system so beneficiaries can report lack of goods. NGO X set-up a programme to support vendors to increase access.
