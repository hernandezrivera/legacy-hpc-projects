Project Summary

The Humanitarian Response Plan identified WASH red spot in areas that are annually affected by natural disasters. WV Mozambique will position stocks in 4 provinces for response after a disaster strike. These are places where WV had been working; Gaza, Tete, Nampula and Zambezia provinces. The major disaster in Mozambique with recurrent flooding. When the places flood access is limited and the affected communities are not attended on time. WV is mindful of local custom and culture. It is also committed to participatory and sustainable development and to providing assistance impartially. WV acknowledge their accountabilities to those whom they assist and those who provide support to their work and also committed to ensuring that it does not expose intended beneficiaries, including children, to any form of discrimination, abuse or exploitation. To ensure sustainable delivery, WV seeks to encourage participation in decision making processes and skills training so as to ensure that the beneficiaries will act as an active component in the implementation of the program.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Mozambique is a country prone to disasters. The country suffers, on average, 1.17 disasters of great magnitude per year and is seen as one of the three regions most exposed to natural disasters in Africa (INGD et al., 2003). Its vulnerability to disasters results from:-its location at the mouth of nine international rivers; -the long extension of the national territory located in the zone of intertropical convergence subject to excessive moisture losses and gains;-the extensive coastal zone that suffers from tropical cyclones and -the existence of zones of active seismic activity. Climatic-the targeted areas are prone to 1-4 cyclones a year. The cyclones result in flooding for communities close to big rivers and the devastation of existing handpumps and water intrusion. Also, the winds destroy the latrines, and households revert to open defecation and because the soils are Kalahari-sandy, building strong and sustainable latrines remains a priority. The districts are also drought-prone. As a result, the rivers are intermittent in flow, given the dry spells of up to 6 months a year. The dry river beds limit water available for drinking and agricultural purposes. A decline in agricultural production from kitchen gardens adversely affects nutritional value. Reduced rainfall in an already semi-arid region reduces water aquifer recharge and possibly increases water salinity.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Financial-Proposed Mitigation Measures: World Vision has audited and sound financial systems that ensure checks and balances to avoid financial misappropriations. WVI and UNICEF finance policies will guide the project to ensure that financial resources are utilised responsibly and appropriately. Planning will involve the donor, World Vision and disaster Committees, use of detailed action plans and all financial management systems. Collaborative decision-making regarding actions to prioritise and use the available resources to meet the project's objective.Operational-Project participants will be made aware of the details relating to the project, including objectives, activities, project participants selection criteria and process, project deliverables and the channels for raising complaints and or providing feedback-Safety and security- Security risk assessment will be ongoing and incorporate a review of political risks and World Vision staff safety plans with the guidance of internal staff safety policies. World Vision is recognised as a non-political and well registered organisation. This will help in overcoming any political challenges that may emerge. The focus will remain guided by the humanitarian imperative, i.e., to save people's lives.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The goal of all gender equity and inclusion efforts is to eliminate health disparities and ensure equal access to disaster response, services for vulnerable groups, including persons with disabilities, minority ethnic groups, women and children in vulnerable situations. The project, among other strategic interventions, seeks the adaptation of key messaging to ensure a communication package that addresses the different population segments. Project staff will gather insights from women, minority ethnic groups and people with disabilities. WW Mozambique staff are trained on gender equity and social inclusion, and all subscribe to a safeguarding policy that protects project beneficiaries against sexual exploitation and harm. Deliberate efforts will be made to include the mainstreaming of gender in emergency response in the capacity-building processes for response committees.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

In keeping with the principles of equity, it ensures resources are deployed to the most vulnerable, i.e., most at-risk segments of the population. WV Mozambique affected will support districts in developing work plans specific to their context. This participatory approach to program design ensures that representatives of affected people, who know the affected context's peculiarities, can design strategies encompassing the various segments of the population they represent. The project will provide equal opportunities (through preferred feedback mechanisms) to all affected participants to contribute and share ideas that will help achieve the project's objective, especially the actions that will eliminate further harm and save the lives of the affected populations.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

World bank social and environmental safeguards will be adopted. The project's strategic approach of ensuring that the Ministry of Health and other government line ministries are capacitated to take the lead in risk communication and community engagement is calculated to ensure that beyond the initial round of funding, government departments can continue deploying RCCE initiatives with minimum to no external support. The project will strengthen existing platforms through capacity building and joint planning. One of the key expected outcomes is the existence of active RCCE committees with clear terms of reference at the district level, which are ready to respond to any outbreak of public health concern. Through a grounded collaboration of all key stakeholders and the use of evidence-based experiences and existing structures, the project is premised on using sustainable processes replicable in future responses. The actions that will be implemented will result from robust discussion and consideration of maximum benefits to the affected communities.
