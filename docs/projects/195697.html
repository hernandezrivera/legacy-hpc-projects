Project Summary

The HNO of February 2022 estimates that 3.6 million, comprising of 97% children below 17 years of age will need education services in 2022. The education sector has been severely affected by a number of factors that have negatively impacted leaning. Insecurity led to school closures and limited access to learning facilities. Flooding has affected the school infrastructure destroying learning materials and the TLS’ previously established. Over 190 schools have been destroyed by the flooding water. Integrated Rapid Need Assessment (IRNA) reports from both Fangak and Koch Counties conducted in August and December 2021 respectively indicate massive flooding and destruction of school infrastructure across the counties. COVID-19 pandemic led to closure of schools in many parts of the country and efforts to provide education through radio could only reach a half of the leaners targeted. Poor renumeration, delayed salaries and incentives has led to high teacher absenteeism and turn over. Inadequate learning spaces, inappropriate facilities for children with disabilities, untrained teachers and lack of learning materials also hamper learning. Similarly, flooding and violence has negatively affected the food security situation. With crops destroyed by the flooding and in other instances limited access to farms as a result of violence reduces crop production with negative impacts on food security. There is a direct linkage between food security and access to education. Without food at home, formal education becomes secondary need as community adopts negative coping strategies including hunting wild food which limits school time or relocation which limits access to education.
Based on these needs, World Relief seeks to intervene in seven counties , with the aim of mitigating these challenges and building resilience within the Education sector. This project will target and support 13,651 learners in Early Childhood Development (ECD), and Primary and Accelerated Learning Program (ALP) Centers. The intervention will ensure children out of school, internally displaced persons (IDP) and host community children, adolescents/youth aged 3-17 years have equitable access to safe and protective learning opportunities. This will be done through establishment and rehabilitation of temporary learning spaces, latrines and handwashing facilities in the project sites. The project will aim to establish/rehabilitate four temporary learning spaces (TLS) per county, 2 8 TLS’ in total. The project will also conduct back-to-learning campaigns, train and provide incentives to teachers, and integrate nutrition in education through school feeding programme to encourage learning.
Coordination with County Education departments and local authorities will be a key implementation strategy. WRSS will also involve stakeholders through the Parents Teaches Association (PTA) and directly with community to receive feedback and improve programming. This intervention will work with protection partners to develop community complaints and feedback mechanism as well as a referral pathway. This will improve access to education by the most vulnerable segment of leaners including Children Living with Disabilities (CLwD), girls, adolescents and survivors of gender-based violence (GBV). The project targets mainly leaners aged 3-17 years.

1 - Needs

The HNO of February 2022 estimates that 3.6 million, comprising of 97% children below 17 years of age will need education services in 2022. Education Needs Assessment (ENA) conducted in 2021 notes that the education sector has been severely affected by a number of catastrophic factors that have negatively impacted learning. Insecurity manifested as recurring communal conflicts and violence, destruction and occupation of schools led to school closures and limited access to learning facilities. Flooding which has persisted in many parts of South Sudan for three consecutive years has also continued to have a negative impact on Education. Flooding has affected the school infrastructure destroying learning materials and the TLS’ which had been previously established. The HNO reports of February 2022 notes that over 190 schools have been destroyed by the flooding water. Integrated Rapid Need Assessment (IRNA) reports from both Fangak and Koch Counties conducted in August and December 2021 respectively indicate massive flooding and destruction of school infrastructure across the counties. COVID-19 which was first reported in South Sudan in 2020 has seen schools closed for a better part of 2021 limiting access to education. UNICEF and its partners have been able to provide distance learning through radios. However, this has had some challenges that includes the cost implications of radios, allowing only a half of the leaners being able to access teaching through radios. Another factor noted in ENA report is the issue of poor and or delayed salaries and incentives which led to high teacher absenteeism and turn over. Inadequate learning spaces, inappropriate facilities for children with disabilities, untrained teachers and lack of learning materials also hamper learning. Similarly, flooding and violence has negatively affected the food security situation. With crops destroyed by the flooding and in other instances limited access to farms as a result of violence reduces crop production with negative impacts on food security. All the target counties for this project are classified as IPC 3+ with Fangak County classified as IPC 4 with pockets of IPC 5 (catastrophic level). There is a direct linkage between food security and access to education. When children are not able to have sufficient food at home, education becomes a secondary need. School enrolment and attendance drops drastically as children are also involved in searching for food and other negative coping strategies. WRSS has been involved in Education supporting learners in ECD, Primary and ALPs. Until 2020, WRSS was running 21 schools in Rubkona County with an enrolment of 16,658. In Koch County, Unity State, there were 18 schools with a total enrolment of 2,582. In Fangak County, Jonglei State, WRSS had 21 schools with an enrolment of 5,758. In Guit County Unity State, there were 11 schools with enrolment of 2,352. In Ibba and Mvolo counties of Western Equatoria State, there were 23 and 15 schools respectively with enrolment numbers of 6,655 for Ibba and 2,818 for Mvolo. Most of these children are currently out of school and vulnerable for recruitment into armed forces for boys and early marriage for girls. WRSS has maintained contact with County authorities including County Director of Education, RRC and County Commissioners. County authorities have continually raised above needs and requested WRSS to intervene in Education sector. Without this intervention, the future of these children will remain bleak.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The most vulnerable members of community will be included in every aspect of project implementation. The project will target to include 60% of beneficiaries as women and girls. Women and girls will be consulted to ensure that activities planned are in line with the socio-cultural needs of women and avoid over-burdening them through this intervention. Women and girls have been structurally denied a chance to be in school through the cultural norms prevalent in the communities. Providing women and girls with an opportunity to give feedback will ensure their views are taken into consideration in order to transform the gender roles and relations within the community and improve the health, nutrition and well-being of not only the individual but household at large. Teachers recruited will be from the local community, with great command of the native language of the county. This will make communication much easier with the learners as explanations can be done in the local language to ensure proper understanding. Curriculum delivery, especially at ECD will be conducted in the local language. This will ensure learners can understand the content being delivered and provide feedback in a language they understand better. Such feedback will ensure training schedule and timing will consider the needs of such groups. Efficient communication in the local language will ensure that learners have an effective mechanism to provide feedback on the training content. Similarly, training will also take into consideration needs of the illiterate population. Most of the project beneficiaries are actually illiterate, especially for those participating in SMC/PTA. Training will involve mostly demonstrations and practical sessions which emphasize more on learning by doing than theory. Any IEC material developed will be in pictorial form to make knowledge transfer learner centered and easy. IEC Materials developed will also be translated into the local language to make reading much easier. These will provide effective communication avenues through which the beneficiaries can also provide feedback on the training content and delivery method. Training and provision of dignity kits will be done by women teachers and girls of similar age. This is to allow free interaction with the recipients for them to seek any clarifications that may be necessary considering the fact that cultural norms and taboos may hinder uptake of dignity kits among women of child bearing age. Girls will also be provided with girl friendly spaces where they can feel safe to change their dignity kits when need arises. Each school will have a gender focal person for both boys and girls. Learners will be sensitized and encouraged to channel any issues of concern and provide feedback on services offered through the gender focal points. Issues raised will be channelled to the relevant gender focal teacher and PTA member to be addressed. People living with disabilities (PLWDs) will also be accorded sufficient attention and avenues for feedback. PLWD will be documented per school with the nature of disability. This will help to ensure that special provisions are made for them to fully participate in the learning process and also give feedback to on project relevance and effectiveness.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will engage with stakeholders in the education sector to provide them information about the project activities. The County Director of Education (CDE) will be the direct point of contact for the project. Right from inception, project objectives and activities will be discussed with the CDE and other local leaders including the Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC) and County Commissioner. The project will also engage directly with the community as well as community representatives through the Parents Teachers Association (PTA). The PTA will play a pivotal role in relaying information to the community and provide feedback to the project right from project design, implementation, Monitoring and Evaluation. The PTA will comprise a broad-based representation including children with special needs and disabled. Even before implementation starts, project activities will be discussed with the PTA to include their inputs. Inputs provided will be used to modify or redesign the project activities. PTA will be in charge of back to learning campaigns to ensure that children are mobilized back to learning. It is expected that the PTA will have direct engagement with the community to sensitize the community against some of the cultural barriers that limit opportunities for both girls and boys to have access to education. The PTA will also be in-charge of mobilising volunteer teachers for the program. Teachers mobilised will be incentivized by the project. However, PTA will be in charge of teacher incentives. Any construction work will be conducted after consultation with the PTA. Technical review by project team will be to ensure all needs are adequately provided for including safe spaces for girls, separate latrines for the boys and girls as well as needs of the disabled. Through these engagements, the PTA will provide a very effective mechanism to relay project information to the community and receive feedback which will be relayed back to the project. The project team will also engage directly with the leaners to provide them information about the project and receive feedback from these leaners. Leaners will provide regular feedback on curriculum delivery so that their feedback is discussed with teachers and PTA and taken into consideration.The PTA will also be very instrumental in the management of the school feeding programme. Community participation will be required in contributing firewood that will be used to prepare the food. Through the PTA, parents will be mobilized to play this role. The PTA will be in-charge of meal ratios provided and collect and relay any feedback concerning school feeding program to the project. Security for the school property and learning materials will also be a responsibility of the PTA. Through the PTA, project information will be relayed to the community for them to be involved in school security and management. The PTA will also work as the community complain and feedback mechanism. Through the PTA, communities around the school will be sensitized to register their complains on education services being provided. The PTA will channel the complain to the project team and local authorities for resolution. Feedback on these complaints will also be provided through the PTAs and channeled back to the community. However, WRSS will also conduct meetings with parents and stakeholders on termly basis. During these meetings, community will be encouraged to register any complaints and provide feedback on project progress.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

World Relief South Sudan (WRSS) has in place a PSEA policy. All staffs are trained on PSEA and are required to sign on it as a prove of having gone through and understood the policy. During project implementation, all staffs are not only expected to adhere to PSEA policy but also to sensitize beneficiaries on this policy. The protection team within WRSS is charged with responsibility to ensure continuous sensitization about PSEA. Sensitization will be provided at all stages of project implementation and any public gathering. Such public events will include back to learning campaigns, during distribution of scholastic materials and during training. The protection officer will be provided time to explain to beneficiaries concerning PSEA as well as the role of community in ensuring PSEA. The PTA will also be trained on PSEA and instructed to disseminate similar information informally during communal interactions and formally during organised events such as trainings and distributions. WRSS will identify and train gender focal points in every school. leaners will be informed about the presence and roles of the gender focal point. The gender focal point will relay information to the rest of the leaners about PSEA. WRSS will also provide a PSEA reporting and feedback mechanism. working with the gender focal points, leaners will be encouraged to report any suspected cases of SEA. All beneficiaries will be sensitized on this reporting mechanism for their use. Confidentiality on any information provided through the feedback mechanism will be ensured to build the confidence of the leaners to use this reporting mechanism. complains registered will include Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) or any attempt of such. These complains will be handled with utmost respect and confidentiality and will be handled firmly. Any reported or suspected cases on SEA will be investigated by a committee of minimum five persons including both men and women and lead by WRSS gender focal point. These investigations will be handled with uttermost confidentiality and respect for cultural norms of both the suspect and victim. Upon completion of the investigation, a report will be developed and shared with the senior management for decision making. Depending on the outcome of the investigation, firm action will be taken against the offender including dismissal from duty and sharing findings of such an investigation with other partners for any future reference. Where outcome of the investigation turns negative, the outcome will be communicated to the suspected staff and any negative effects arising from the investigation will be appropriately ameliorated.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback received from beneficiaries will be pivotal in designing and or re-designing project interventions. Given that WRSS has been operational in these project locations for a long time, WRSS has had a chance to engage the beneficiaries at various stages and times. In the past, WRSS has been able to conduct Knowledge, Attitude and Practice (KAP) surveys as well as integrated Rapid Needs Assessments (IRNA). The baselines being used to develop this plan are from these past surveys. WRSS will continue to collect feedback from beneficiaries through such engagements and use the feedback to redesign project objectives and activities to reflect any changes in context and needs of the target beneficiaries. Beneficiaries will be engaged during mobilization and sensitization. Project objectives and key activities will be discussed. Feedback provided from these sensitization meetings will be used to tailor project interventions to meet the needs of the beneficiaries. The School Management Committee (SMC) and PTA will also be very instrumental in providing feedback on the project. Feedback from PTA/SMC will be crucial in identifying locations for the TLS and schools to ensure ease of accessibility and are safety for all leaners. The PTA will also be involved in monitoring project progress. During these monitoring sessions, views from the PTA as well as views of the direct beneficiaries will be used to adjust subsequent schedule and type of activities so as to address the needs expressed by the beneficiaries. Leaners will provide regular feedback on curriculum delivery so that their feedback is discussed with teachers and PTA and taken into consideration. This will done through structured surveys with the leaners on termly basis.The county government leadership, including County Director of Education (CDE), Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC), County Commissioner, Payam Administrators and Chiefs will also be conducted to give feedback on project activities. This will be done through regular meetings with these leaders. Feedback provided will be used to adapt education sector interventions.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Owing to the low literacy levels and cultural taboos that emphasize male dominance, women and girls tend to take a back sit even during community meetings. Boys are accorded more chances of accessing education which makes literacy level within women and girls even higher. This exposes women and girls to more exploitations as they may not be aware of their rights and entitlements from project interventions. Women and girls will be accorded 60% of opportunity to be in school and learn. Women and girls and elderly people may not even be aware of the available complaint mechanisms especially where such mechanisms involve reading materials that may be written in English or other unfamiliar languages. This project will therefore ensure that people in vulnerable groups are specifically targeted and accorded an opportunity to have their views heard and included in the project's decision-making process. Available complaints registration and resolution mechanisms will be communicated verbally and explained in the local language. The project will also ensure that schools and TLAs are centrally placed and accessible to all learners in their community. Before establishment of schools/TLAs, there will be consensus on location and management so that all learners have unhindered access when they want to access for training and other uses. Schools/TLAs will also be sited in locations that have no dispute on land. The tenure of the school/TLA has to be ensured for the whole length of the project and even after the project life to ensure that beneficiaries can still continue to learn from it.Girls in school could be exposed to SEA by male teachers for better grades. Given that target areas have highly patriarchal gender relations, sexual exploitation and abuse can be perpetuated sometimes unconsciously even with the women and girls. It is generally acceptable to marry underage girls or for girls to be married even against their preference and wishes. SEA from a male teacher of PTA member may be perceived as a normal practice especially where the male is willing to pay dowry for the girl. WRSS has in place a PSEA policy which guides both staff and partners to ensure no sexual misconducts are perpetuated against girls and women. WRSS PSEA policy will be trained for all staffs and signed on it to guide interactions with beneficiaries. Local government authorities, particularly from Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC), Payam Administrators and Chiefs attempt to influence registration of beneficiaries to suit their own needs. Whereas local leaders are much more familiar with context and targeted beneficiaries, when such a process is left entirely left to the local authority it is prone to abuse. In most cases selected beneficiaries will be relatives of the local authority staffs recruiting them, while the most vulnerable are not accorded priority. When communities feel disfranchised by their leaders, conflicts are bound to arise. This can mainly be a risk for the school feeding programme. To mitigate this risk, all partners and the PTA will be sensitized on the PSEA policy and encouraged to sensitize the community on the sameWRSS has an integrated approach in service delivery. This includes provision of protection and Health services. WRSS provides primary health care services in both Koch County in Unity State and Fangak County in Jonglei state. In these two counties, WRSS runs both PHCU and PHCC services. WRSS also ensures protection to children, women at risk and GBV survivors. WRSS will also work with protection partners to establish referral pathways. Through these referral pathways, children, women at risk and GBV survivors will be able to access information on prevention and management of GBV. WRSS is also able to handle clinical management of rape within the PHCC and where such cases would be beyond WRSS capacity, WRSS is able to refer such cases to other facilities with better capacity.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

The three target locations have been ravaged by floods for three consecutive years. with most of the locations in these three counties still flooded in the first quarter of 2022, likelihood of flooding is very high. WRSS will work with communities to institute disaster risk mitigation measures across the target locations. Working through the PTA, the project will build the capacity of the community to institute early warning signs that will trigger response before the flooding occurs. Through this early warning signs, the community can respond with appropriate measures. The project will work together with community will be through flood control measures, mainly berms and dykes. these activities will be conducted through Cash for assets where possible. The community will be capacity build to identify and prioritize areas prone to flooding. The community will then be facilitated to establish dykes or berms that will be used to control the flooding water. This will help to reduce flood related displacement in the target locations.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

WRSS has an integrated approach in service delivery. This includes provision of protection and Health services. WRSS provides primary health care services in both Koch County in Unity State and Fangak County in Jonglei state. In these two counties, WRSS runs both PHCU and PHCC services. WRSS intends to expand same health services in Canal/Pigi due to unmet needs in this county. WRSS also ensures protection to children, women at risk and GBV survivors. WRSS will also able to work with protection partners to provide minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV. 1) These services will include; counseling-empathetic listening, inquiring about needs and concerns, validating, and ensuring safety of the victims.2) Treatment of injuries associated with GBV3) STI screening and treatments4) Rapid HIV testing and counseling services and referral to care and treatment as needed5) Post exposure prophylaxis (PEP) within 72 hours for sexual exposure and to be taken for 28 days6) Emergency contraception within 120 hours7) Referral and linkage to other services:-child protection, legal counsel, police, psychosocial support, shelter) . Through these referral pathways, children, women at risk and GBV survivors will be able to access information on prevention and management of GBV. WRSS is also able to handle clinical management of rape within the PHCC and where such cases would be beyond WRSS capacity, WRSS is able to refer such cases to other facilities with better capacity.
