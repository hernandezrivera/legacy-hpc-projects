Project Summary

This project will continue seeking to improve the protective environment for those affected
by conflict, improve access to and quality of protective services and advocate for the
sustainable changes in the legislation and policy nationwide. R2P interventions will:
- Monitor the situation of internally displaced persons and other affected populations
along the line of contact and other areas of intervention, collecting, verifying, and
analyzing information in order to identify human rights violations and protection risks
encountered by IDPs and other affected populations. Entrance-exit check points (EECP)
monitoring will continue at all operating EECPs (in 2021 4 EECPs are expected to be
working). The protection response will be delivered to people of concern. This will include
both individual and community level monitoring;
- Improve access to Legal Assistance and Legal Remedies, by providing high-quality
counseling and legal assistance to the internally displaced and conflict affected
population;
- Through community-based protection activities build capacity of representatives of local
authorities and people residing in local communities to search for alternative housing
options and durable solutions;
- Promote National Law and Policy which is consistent with international standards on
internal displacement. This will be completed through advocacy, assessment and
analysis, commentary on legal acts and drafts, and strategic litigation.
The project will primarily serve internally displaced persons living in the most densely
populated areas in Donetsk, Luhansk, Kharkiv, Dnipro, and Zaporizhia regions, with a
specific focus on the territories along the line of contact and on the people crossing
EECPs.
Covid-related restrictions will be considered in the activities planning and implementation.

1 - Needs

The situation of IDP and conflict affected persons in 2020 got critically worse due toCovid-related restrictions and limitations. Only 2 EECPs (Stanitsa Luhanska andNovotroitske) ensure crossing of persons, though with considerable limitations. Thisresulted in 80 thousand crossings in September 2020, which is much less than inSeptember 2019 (1,184 mln). Some technical difficulties with Diy Vdoma app and therequirement of self-isolation\observation result in overnight stays at EECPs for somepeople, and even in monthly stays for others. The EECPs and border lockdown togetherwith other Covid-related restrictions (incl. limited public transportation movement)considerably deteriorate access to social, administrative and judiciary services for personsliving along the contact line and in the NGCA. The number of requests for legalconsultations related to the freedom of movement steadily grows since the summer 2020,compared to requests on access to pensions. R2P relates these changes to theimpossibility of entering the GCA for the majority of NGCA residents. Access tobeneficiaries has become limited. To respond to the challenge, R2P changed the modalityof work: the majority of beneficiaries is being reached out via phone. This ensuresavailability of legal and protection consultations to persons of concern. Access to servicesfor NGCA residents has remained restricted as per October 2020. People living close to thecontact line remain the most vulnerable because of the security situation and lack ofaccess to admin and basic social services. Local, national and international NGOs remainthe key service providers, ensuring their access to protection measures including legal aid.They also have poor or no access to public transport. This restricts freedom of movementand does not allow for travel to rayon centres and obtain their social and medicalservices. Mines and unmarked mine polluted areas are another obstacle for access toservices by local population. Many people, especially those living in locations of highconcern and in the NGCA, have considerable legal aid needs. Many lack information aboutlegal options to protect their rights. Lengthy litigation procedures often discourage peoplefrom applying to courts. The organization continues to address a wide range of issues requiring legal consultation or litigation. Although the large portion of legal requests stillrelate to life-saving issues of pensions and social benefits, the percentage of morecomplicated cases managed by the organization is steadily growing. These issues includespecialized pensions, compensation for damaged or destroyed property, compensation forcasualties and physical damages, etc. With the relaunch of administrative procedure ofcompensation for destroyed housing, R2P will make its implementation a priority. In orderto identify beneficiaries willing to take an advantage of the procedure, the caseload offormer R2P clients will be analyzed and relevant persons outreached as potentiallyinterested in legal assistance on this issue. R2P will raise awareness of the procedure,document its gaps and communicate them to the responsible authorities. With theincrease of more complicated caseload, R2P continues to refer the straightforward cases(e.g. facts of birth and death, family issues, pensions for non-vulnerable persons) to FreeLegal Aid Centers (FLAC). However, due to Covid-related limitations a number of referralshas critically dropped down. The monitors\community workers remain the major entrypoint for people asking for legal assistance – some 56% of cases come to our legal teamfrom the monitors and from the helpline operators. In the course of the housing mappingcarried out in 2020, R2P teams in Kharkiv, Dnipro and Zaporizhia have identified somehousing opportunities in the communities across the mentioned oblasts. Although themajority of the objects belong to temporary housing and cannot be treated as durablesolutions.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

- A1. Protection monitoring. Outcomes. EECP crossing conditions improved as a result ofEECP protection monitoring and strategic litigations; PoCs ensured a possibility to crossthe contact line with respected dignity; - A2. Legal assistance. Outcomes. Ensured accessto quality legal aid and protection counselling with special focus at compensation forhousing and other damages for civilians; freedom of movement; access to pension; anddurable solutions; - A3. Capacity building. Outcomes. Representatives of local authoritiesand people residing in local communities will be empowered to search for alternativehousing options through community-based protection activities; - A4. Advocacy.Outcomes. Improved policy and legislation on pension; housing, land and property (HLP)rights (administrative and court compensation for damaged or destroyed properties andsearching for housing alternatives), and the right to life (compensation for death andinjury of a person), freedom of movement, etc.
