Project Summary

The proposed project will be implemented in 4 districts in Bakool (Xudur,wajid,rabdhuure and Elbarde), 3 in Banadir (Kahda,hodan,abdiaziz) ,1 in Lower Shabelle region (Afgooye) , 2 districts in Nugaal region(Garowe and Eyl) and 1 district in Bay region (Baidoa) to cover Health needs of internally displaced people and host community women, men, boys and girls.
The project seeks to reduce Reduce of excess morbidity and mortality due disease outbreak driven by climatic shocks and conflict among 1.6 million IDPs and non-displaced by 5% by end of 2020 emergency levels of acute malnutrition through integrated multisector response in line with 2019 strategic objective 2 for Somalia. The primary targets of the project are U5 boys and girls and pregnant and lactating women in 11 districts in Somalia. In line with the Essential Package of Health services. Action Against Hunger will through support to Ministry of Health and direct delivery of services provide the following core services through 3 levels of service delivery i.e Health centre, Primary Health unit (PHU) and Community outreach.
A. Maternal, reproductive and neonatal health
Action Against Hunger will provide basic obstetric and neonatal care in supported Health centres. The supported facilities will be equipped with maternity beds for 24 hour observation of post delivery cases. Other services which will be provided through the health centre would include; family planning counseling and service delivery, antenatal care, post natal care, safe delivery care, immunization, nutrition (Micronutrient supplementation and education) etc.
B. Child health
ACF will train the medical staff of the health center so as to diagnose and treat childhood illnesses based on the IMCI guidelin Health centres., The following services will be provided to children: treatment of acute respiratory illness (ARI), Diarheal diseases and Management of Anemia. ICCM package will be provided at the community level by CHWs.
C. EPI
Routine immunization for both underfive children and women of reproductive age will be provided. Mid- year micro planning involving the local community through the Health Committee and the MOH would be held.
The project will integrate with other sectors such as WASH, food security and protection. The program’s approach involves both men and women while acknowledging their respective roles and needs, and fostering mutual awareness and partnership. Recognizing that child care in Somalia is mainly considered a female role, the project will take into account the socially constructed roles for each gender ensuring that women do not spend so much time in the project at the expense of their other children who are not malnourished, while also seeking to influence them. The project will also target men to encourage them to take an active role in better child care practices, while also improving women’s knowledge on infant feeding and care. The project seeks to enhance the capacity of community and Ministry of health staff to provide life saving services and build community resilience . The activities will be implemented in line with Ministry of health/WHO/UNICEF guidelines. A total of 1,492,108 persons including 298422 women of child bearing age ,151195 boys and 152195 girls, , 60 staff (20 men and 40 women) will be targeted for a period of 12 months.

Mitigation Measures

Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacementThe main target population for the project are IDPs and the surrounding host communities. Displaced populations, youth, women and the elderly, most notable those in camps lack basic services. In addition, poor access and long distance covered in accessing health services expose them, especially women and young girls to sex and gender based violence. Moreover, access to basic services and opportunities is further compounded by the pervasive marginalization and exclusion of IDPs and certain sections of the host communities mostly attributed to clan dynamics. IDP and host community families from weaker clans or minorities face extra vulnerabilities and protection threats including economic exploitation, trafficking and discrimination. To mitigate against the risks identified, ACF will ensure protection sensitive data collection and analysis to identify the specific protection challenges of the target population at project implementation stage. Engagement at community level will emphasize representation of all groups/clans in the target locations. ACF staff are already trained and sensitized on PSEA and have signed a code of conduct that commits them to strictly adhere to humanitarian principles when providing services. This is expected to curtail any cases of humanitarian staff exploiting the beneficiaries. Health program will employ approaches that reduce distance to treatment/consultation sites. This includes use of mobile teams that visits villages where the most vulnerable are located. This will minimize exposure to potential distance related risks. Proper consultations will be done at the inception of the project with all the key stakeholders in the camp to ensure that those who benefit from these interventions are not put at a risk. Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and ageThe health projects will provide primary health care services mainly target children under 5 years of age and women actively in the reproductive stage regardless of their age. Communities in hard to reach areas due to insecurity and poor infrastructure suffer from lack access to health services. Due to prevailing social norms, male caregivers of sick children may be excluded from targeting. Moreover, male and female caregivers from marginalized communities may miss opportunities to access health services due to lack of information or reluctance due to their social status. The roll out of mobile outreach services will ensure that remote but accessible villages are reached with health services. This will include engagement of community health workers who represent the various clan profiles, who can increase reach to all groups in the target communities. Moreover, the project will engage with all major groups to ensure that they are aware of the availability of services and their entitlements. The Project will also engage Traditional Birth attendants and improve their capacity to refer cases for delivery by skilled birth attendants. The project will also pro-actively engage male caregivers so that they don’t miss on services. Data collection and analysis will be designed in a way that allows ACF to better understand age and gender needs to ensure inclusivity. Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violenceACF works in areas where there is active conflict between state and non-state armed actors or between clans. The key risks include Service delivery being appropriated by different sides to the conflict against the others, aid services being diverted to the most powerful groups, retaliatory attacks or victimization of those who seek services by hostile groups, attack on beneficiary on their way their way to service delivery points

Please provide further explanation

The proposed intervention is aligned to the Health,WASH ,Food security and Nutrition cluster HRP priorities to focus on basic life-saving activities and community resilience-building in all high GAM/SAM and IDP areas. The regions where the interventions will be implemented are characterized by enormous environmental challenges related to poor waste disposal, deforestation, land degradation, increasing aridity and water scarcity . By increasing access to nutrition services through use of mobile teams and community focused approaches,the project reduces abnormal migration of human to other regions where they inflict significant environmental damages by exerting pressure on exisiting services . In the absence of latrines, in the new areas of displacement, open defecation would increase posing serious risks to the environmental wellbeing. Portions of land are usually left open to be used by the displaced population for human waste disposal. This poses serious health risks due to the spread of faecal matter, especially during the rainy season when there is seepage into water wells used for drinking water. This has been partly responsible for the periodic outbreaks of Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) in IDPs and villages in Somalia which increases the risk of high morbidity and mortality rates among vulnerable groups. The project will use an integrated approach with WASH to Provide latrines with hand washing facilities that will ease the level of public health risk posed by providing safe places and consequently check the spread of human waste. Whose location will be far from the water wells to check the risks of contamination. Public health will be ensured through proper disposal and management of medical equipment, supplies and waste, including the use of incinerators at the health facility. A component of nutrition training will also include lessons on environmental awareness with the objective of providing skills to the beneficiaries on better waste management, which doesn’t endanger their immediate environment. The project will jointly with line ministry representatives support the establishment and capacity building of community groups ( Community Health Committees, camp management committees, Mother support groups) to ensure resilient community systems are in place. The immediate/emergency components of the project will be linked to ACF’s resilience program in the country and handovers to the capacity built community resource committees will ensure that the resources established are maintained and contribute to building resilient communities. The services and commodities planned for transfer to beneficiaries under this project adhere to government policies and have been designed jointly with community user groups. The transfer will be witnessed by local authorities to ensure it fits within the existing legal framework at district level.
