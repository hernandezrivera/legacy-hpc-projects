Project Summary

Amidst ongoing crisis and the destruction or inaccessibility of schools, available educational institutions lack the capacity to accommodate students adequately. This project aims to support HRP's strategic goal of enhancing resilience and service accessibility, particularly in education, reaching a total of 13,239 beneficiaries (12,920 children and 319 teachers and educational personnel). Concentrating on Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) in camps in Idleb and Aleppo governorates, the project will prioritize children facing severe adversities, offering both formal and non-formal education programs and inclusive support for eight schools and five non-formal education centers. Its ultimate aim is to foster a safer environment for children, especially those aged 5-7, by addressing their specific needs and vulnerabilities while emphasizing protection measures.
The main objectives MF seeks to accomplish are as follows:
1. Facilitating access to education services for 12,920 conflict-affected children aged 5-17 (6300 girls,6620 boys) in Northwest Syria, including 300 children with disabilities (CwD) . This will be achieved through the provision of formal and non-formal education programs, with 10,600 children (5000 girls, 5600 boys including 250 CWD) attending formal education classes in eight schools using GIS curriculum and 2,320 children (1300 girls and 1020 boys including 50 CWD) participating in non-formal education (ALp & remedial program) for two cycles in five non-formal education centers using curriculum B. Operational costs, school bags distribution, and other necessary expenses will also be covered. Additionally, MF plans to conduct 13 BTL campaigns to encourage students to return to school.
2. Enhancing the learning environment for 12,920 children by ensuring cleanliness, sterility, and safety in 13 schools and education centers in Northwest Syria ( 8 schools operating formal education and 5 education centers operating non-formal education). This will involve conducting minor rehabilitation works in 104 classes, such as installing windows, doors, and desks, to address wear and tear from the previous academic year. Accessibility for children with disabilities will be ensured, and measures like providing heating materials.
3. Improving the quality of educational services within a secure and nurturing learning environment by training 319 teachers (159 female, 160 male) on Psychological Support (PSS) and Social-emotional Learning (SEL), Education in Emergencies (EIE), INEE (Inter-Agency Network for Education in Emergencies) standards, educational subjects, child-centered and protective teaching methods, and Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). Trained teachers will be encouraged to reflect on their teaching attitudes and enhance their relationships and communication with students. Furthermore, incentives will be provided for teachers and educational personnel.
Throughout the project, efforts will be made to ensure the inclusion of children with disabilities in various activities, with rehabilitation works and staff training tailored to meet their needs. MF is committed to involving all relevant stakeholders at every stage of the project management cycle, taking responsibility for design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation (M&E), and closure phases. Coordination among clusters will be facilitated, and the project will be overseen by competent programmatic specialized staff to ensure high-quality results and efficient progress.(the same 319 teachers will participate in formal and non-formal activities).

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The project will launch upon signing a MoU with the ED.10600 children (5000 girls,5600 boys) will be targeted by FE, The face to-face modality will be adopted in this project. 1160 children who are out of school (720 girls& 440 boys) will attend the ALp program and 1160 children who are at risk of dropping out (580 girls& 580 boys) will attend remedial classes through two cycles the NFE will work to refer children to FE after completion of the cycle. At the beginning of the project a training program will be conducted for 319 teachers (150 females, 160 males) for three days as well as 104 classrooms will be rehabilitated. MF has already designed monitoring and evaluation tools that ensure accountability of the affected populations, and it has also provided beneficiaries with these tools. Monitoring and evaluation will be in line with all project phases, starting from initiation until the close-out phase. The monitoring tools that will be used: are surveys, on-site observation reports, meetings with community key informants, interviews, and pictures. Besides, M&E staff has established many channels for complaints mechanism. MF will directly implement the activities since MF has full access to the targeted location.Regarding the protection risk analysis, Maram will take into consideration the risks and mitigation measures mentioned in the Education Protection Risk Analysis (PRA) Matrix, including the following risks:Protection Risk/ThreatLearning facilities can be attacked by parties to the conflict or by communities. Attacks may include: airstrikes, bombs/explosions/shelling, fighting/gunshots, arson/fire, looting/theft; kidnapping/arrest of teachers or students, direct attacks on teachers or students, intimidation of school staff/students or military use of the school, arson, theft or looting.Mitigating MeasuresLearning facilities should have safety protocols in place. These protocols should be adequately resourced and regularly practiced. Supply-related resources may include walkie-talkies, basic firefighting equipment, basic first aid supplies, flashlights, exit signs, etc. Parents and caregivers should be informed of the learning facility’s safety protocols and what will happen in case of an attack. Protection Risk/Threat:Aid workers or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they should be serving.Mitigating MeasuresProtection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policies should be in place, practiced and monitored. Policies should cover standards of behavior for the organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and include accountability measures. Policies should address risks at the organizational level and the program level.
