Project Summary

This project aims to inform the revised 2023 HRP and focus on addressing life-saving multispectral assistance and protection services in line with HRP Strategic Objectives (SO) 1 and 2. through timely provision of lifesaving and protection interventions from Shelter/NFIs, GBV and WASH sectors for the newly internally displaced person and the most affected vulnerable populations in BN, ND and Kassala States. Sudan has faced crisis and inter-communal violence in Khartoum, Blue Nile, ND and many other parts, resulting in a deteriorated situation for many people who have lost their livelihoods and urgently need lifesaving protection, essential services and support to restore their lives. This project will provide an integrated multi-sectoral lifesaving response, including protection assistance (GBV) to 28,200 PoC NFI kits to 18,000 PoC and WASH services to 30,000 PoC. The project targets 76,200 vulnerable IDPs in 6 localities in the three targeted states.
GBV Protection interventions will cover persons with specific needs to help them access proper and safe assistance. Throughout the project, protection monitoring will be conducted in each target locality to identify and track protection concerns and to inform a consolidated protection cluster monitoring at the state and national levels. 3000 Adults will receive group-based mental health and psychosocial support services so parents and adults can have improved psychological well-being by reducing levels of psychological distress, improving daily functioning and ensuring effective coping strategies. Community-based structures will be supported or established to strengthen the community resilience mechanisms. Large-scale public community awareness-raising campaigns on GBV will be conducted through 12 campaigns targeting 6000 people. Providing 12,000 dignity kits to women and girls of reproductive age will help them during their menstrual and improve their hygiene.
Shelter/NFI interventions will be through providing timely NFI assistance to the most affected people; 300 tents and 3000 nonfood items kits, targeting 18,000 individuals.
WASH interventions include the provision of 3000 multipurpose bar soap to households, construction of 60 hand washing facilities, provision of 3000 hygiene kits for HHs, conducting 6 hygiene campaigns targeting 3000 individuals and conducting 6 vector control campaigns targeting 6000 people.
All these multi-sectoral activities will be implemented in close consultation with target communities and with due consideration to the local capacities to ensure equal and representative participation and information sharing with all stakeholders. Engagement of local communities, civil societies, line ministries, sector/cluster focal points and humanitarian actors at the field level will be prioritized.

1 - Needs

Clashes between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) that erupted on 15 April 2023 are devastating for civilians; over 936,000 people have been newly displaced, including about 736,200 people displaced internally since the conflict began. Due to the escalating violence across the country, which has caused tremendous human suffering, the Sudan HRP 2023 has been revised and updated the response strategy of the initial 2023 HRP regarding the scale-up of lifesaving multisectoral assistance and protection services in line with HRP Strategic Objectives (SO) 1 and 2, including related implementation approaches and costs. The proposed project is an integrated project that covers the Shelter/NFI, GBV & WASH interventions offering lifesaving and protection services, which will be critical to ensure privacy and safety, mitigate GBV risks, and safeguard displaced populations from harsh environmental conditions to preserve their health amid the upcoming rainy season. The revised 2023 HRP indicates that the Shelter/NFI cluster’s PIN has experienced a 90% increase, from 3M to 5.7M, which is in line with the IOM DTM Scenario 2 or 1.8 newly displaced people by the end of the year, while the target increased from 1.5 million to 1.9 million. The Water, Sanitation and Hygiene Cluster in the revised HRP shows that the PIN rose from 10.9m to 14.1m people affected by WASH constraints. Many of these affected groups either do not have access to WASH or cannot afford basic WASH services. Internal displacement, food security and natural disasters are the key drivers of humanitarian needs in the three targeted states. Economic crises and inadequate basic services such as water, sanitation and hygiene, health, and education compound these. Since 15 April, the number of people in need of GBV services in Sudan has increased by over 1 million to 4.2 million people. The number of individuals targeted for GBV services has increased to 1.3 million, with an increase of over 900% of targeted individuals in states that are heavily impacted but still accessible. Internal displacement, food security and natural disasters are the key drivers of humanitarian needs in the three targeted states. Economic crises and inadequate essential services such as water, sanitation and hygiene, health, and education compound these. Some 442,000 internally displaced persons (IDPs), vulnerable residents, refugees and asylum seekers in BN, ND and Kassala need humanitarian assistance. Aid organizations report a deteriorating humanitarian situation. Conflicts have impacted livelihoods and the availability of basic services, so providing essential household items will be crucial for restoring basic daily activities. Urgent and timely interventions are needed to prevent these populations from slipping into worse phases. However, by the date of writing this proposal, humanitarian partners continue extending life-saving assistance to affected people they can reach in the targeted states and those who fled to other localities and states. The grant includes food, non-food items, health, water and sanitation services.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The project interventions are designed in a multi-sectoral approach to address the urgent needs of the new IDPs in BN, ND & Kassala states. The Proposed interventions will cover water, sanitation, and hygiene activities to contribute to WASH HRP 2023. Objective 1: 4.5m crisis-affected populations can access gender and climate change-sensitive essential water services. Shelter/NFI activities contribute directly to sector strategic objectives 1 &2: to Provide timely NFI assistance to crisis-affected populations and Enable crisis-affected populations to access adequate18 shelter solutions that provide protection, safety, security, and space to live in a dignified manner. Protection activities will enhance people's safety, dignity and rights and prevent further harm. The proposed multi-sectoral response will cover the following activities: S/NFI interventions will provide shelters and nonfood items (300 tents and 3000 NFI kits) to the most vulnerable IDPs. WASH interventions will cover; the provision of multipurpose bar soap to 3000 households, construction of 60 hand washing facilities, provision of hygiene kits for 3000 HHs, conducting of 6 hygiene campaigns targeting 3000 people and conducting six vector control campaigns targeting 6000 people. GBV interventions will cover; Provision of group-based mental health and psychosocial support services to 3,000 adults; large-scale public community awareness-raising campaigns on GBV will be conducted through 12 campaigns targeting 6000 people, Provision of dignity kits for 12,000 women and girls at reproductive age, All activities will be implemented with due consideration to the local capacities. It will ensure equal and representative participation and information sharing with all stakeholders.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Practical Action recognize that the environment in which people live and work is essential for their health, well-being and recovery from crisis. It also acknowledges that humanitarian operations affect the environment both directly and indirectly. Therefore, this response is designed in a way to minimize the environment impact by considering how procurement, transport, choice of materials, or land and natural resources use may protect or degrade the environment further. With due consideration, this project will ensure a proper measure take place to contribute to environmental sustainability. Furthermore, PA has a long experience in adopting Climate Resilient approach that ensures equal access to water for different users while maintaining ecosystem balances and enhance environmental restoration. PA has a track record of implementing environment projects focusing of forestation and environment conservation. Interventions such as applying IWRM, replacing diesel operated water yards into solar-operated systems to reduce hazardous gases emission from burning fuel for water pumping. Interventions of this project will ensure and promote positive impact on environment and natural resources, by reducing the deterioration of forests, through the provision of clean cooking stoves, afforestation through tree plantation, and building the capacity of target communities on the use of clean energy sources.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Practical Action acknowledge, adopts, and adhere to the protection principles in humanitarian context. PA recognizes that enhancing people's safety, dignity, rights, and avoid exposing them to further harm is a fundamental protection principle and integral part of our programming, design, and delivery. PA also will ensure people's access to impartial assistance according to the need and without discrimination. Furthermore, PA will assist people to recover from the physical and phycological effects of threatened or actual violence, and help people claim their rights. Practical Action ensures understanding and commitment to the safeguarding policies package by all employees, partners and contractors, which states Zero tolerance to all types of sexual exploitation and abuse especially against children, women and vulnerable adults. Complaints system will be put in place to ensure safe and effective communication channel is active for all beneficiaries to raise their concerns. The system includes different tools including hotlines, emails, complaints boxes and others. As communities will be well-oriented and engaged in initial consultations and need assessments, Practical Action will ensure inclusion of the needs of different community groups including women and disabled people to assure that no one is left behind! Doing No harm principle will also be maintained to ensure no negative implications will arise from the project interventions that violate people safety, security, dignity or create damage to the resources.
