Project Summary

South Sudan's public healthcare system is in crisis due to severe flooding and violence in Koch, Rubkona, Leer, and Fangak, causing significant population displacement and healthcare disruptions. A flood warning issued on May 6, 2024, threatens over 1 million people across 42 counties, exacerbating urgent health issues. The ongoing economic crisis has also driven up prices for essential goods and hampered local government operations, particularly the State Ministry of Health.
Unity and Jonglei States are facing a severe humanitarian crisis, with some counties in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) for acute food insecurity from September 2024 to July 2025. Unity State is the second most food-insecure region in South Sudan, with 58% of its population, including 14,000 returnees, in IPC Phase 4. The FSN Report shows that 54% of households in Unity face moderate to severe food insecurity, and child wasting rates have reached 15.5%. Severe flooding in Fangak has worsened the situation.
Fangak County in Jonglei is also in crisis due to ongoing flooding, displacing 5,406 households and affecting 26,610 people. This has limited healthcare access, especially for pregnant women and individuals with disabilities, while increasing the risk of waterborne diseases like Hepatitis E and cholera, underscoring the need for disaster response and improved WASH services.
Koch County in Unity, home to about 98,851 residents, has seen severe flooding affecting over 36,000 people, worsening living conditions and hindering humanitarian aid access, leading to increased public health risks, including malaria and diarrheal diseases.
In Leer County, flooding has impacted 10,071 of 77,000 residents, causing significant livestock and property losses, with 60.8% of those affected being children and 15.7% elderly, further intensifying food insecurity. WRSS and DRI are collaborating with NRC and Coalition for Humanity to provide food assistance through RSRTF initiatives.
Ongoing financial support is essential for lifesaving services in Rubkona, Koch, and Fangak, highlighting the urgent need for integrated WASH and health support to enhance Medair's nutrition program.
Premised on the low understanding between diseases and WASH, WRSS will collaborate with donors, communities, and governments to address essential needs by promoting awareness of hygiene, sanitation, and safe water services, which influence health behaviors. By strengthening systems that support these practices, communities will actively manage these services for healthier families.
In partnership with others, WRSS aims to improve hygiene practices through targeted messaging and campaigns focused on safe water, handwashing, latrine use, and waste management. This initiative will align with education, health, and nutrition efforts to ensure change agents are knowledgeable about hygiene. Increased awareness will drive demand for WASH infrastructure and empower communities to manage and maintain these facilities. WRSS will establish a community-led supply chain for spare parts and work with local governments for quality control.
WRSS is committed to protecting the dignity of all individuals, implementing the Child Protection Policy to enhance protection mechanisms and build staff capacity to prevent child abuse. Community Feedback Mechanisms (CFM) will be used to address related risks.
Gender considerations will be integrated into all WASH initiatives, focusing on issues like sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) and developing co-empowerment strategies for both men and women.

1. Needs

The FEWSNET analysis reveals severe food insecurity (IPC 4+) in Greater Upper Nile counties, especially Koch, Leer, Rubkona, and Fangak, worsened by social factors, climate change, violence, and high disease prevalence. Malaria accounts for over 45% of health issues, with acute respiratory infections and diarrheal diseases at 19% and 10%, respectively. The November 2024 IPC report identifies Rubkona, Leer, and Fangak as critical areas needing urgent intervention. These counties have faced four years of significant flooding, severely impacting agriculture and livestock. Farmers struggle with poor seed quality, inadequate tools, limited irrigation, insufficient veterinary services, and lack of agricultural knowledge, hindering recovery and food security stabilization. Rubkona County is particularly affected, with 26.4% of its population experiencing severe food insecurity, including about 15,000 individuals in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophic) conditions. From September to November 2024, it is the only area classified as Emergency (IPC Phase 4) due to flooding's impact on agriculture and livestock. This has led to decreased income, disrupted supply chains from ongoing conflicts in Sudan, rising food prices, an influx of returnees, and disease outbreaks. Rubkona is expected to significantly contribute to the estimated 255,000 individuals facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity in the state. An April 2024 assessment found a Global Acute Malnutrition rate of 22.87% among children aged 6 to 59 months, linked to flooding, displacement, food shortages, and disease outbreaks like measles, hepatitis E, cholera, and malaria. Approximately 48,000 returnees in Rotriak are further straining local resources.Koch County, classified as IPC-3 for acute food insecurity, has been severely affected by flooding, impacting over 36,285 people, nearly half of whom are children. Limited food access due to inadequate fishing gear has increased reliance on wild food and high market prices, worsened by a lack of arable land during harvest. Further flooding is expected, raising concerns about displacement and waterborne illnesses. Approximately 13,743 children under five are at risk of acute malnutrition and need nutritional support. While community awareness of diarrheal disease prevention is high, improved sanitation facilities are urgently needed. Fangak, an IPC-4 county in Jonglei, urgently requires intervention due to severe flooding, local conflicts, and heavy rainfall, affecting 5,406 households and 26,610 individuals. There is a critical need for WASH services to combat waterborne diseases like Hepatitis E and cholera. A March 2023 survey showed that only 59.2% of participants knew diarrhea prevention methods, with just 44.7% able to identify three strategies. While 60% recognized critical handwashing moments, only 54.4% practiced it. Soap availability was at 43%, and the number of handwashing stations with water and soap had decreased by 25.8%. Alarmingly, 78% relied on surface water for drinking, with only 8.3% using boreholes. Additionally, only 51.4% used separate containers for water, and 50.2% used lidded jerry cans, although 80% reported treating water by boiling. In September 2024, significant flooding in Leer County affected 6,611 residents, primarily children (60.8%). Médecins Sans Frontières reported a surge in malaria cases, with 500 new patients in one week. The flooding contaminated local water supplies, forcing the community to seek alternatives. From September to November 2024, Leer County faced IPC-3 acute food insecurity, as many households struggled to meet their food needs due to poor agricultural production, lack of fishing gear, market access issues, and rising prices, increasing reliance on foraged food.

2. Project activities

Provide WASH assistance to 122,445 individuals (5,9997M:62,448F) by ensuring access to clean and safe drinking water, improving hygiene practices, and promoting basic sanitation at household, community, and institutional levels. Key activities include:2.3.1 Strengthening hygiene promotion by engaging caregivers and community hygiene promoters, connecting them with local nutrition and health services. This involves: Distributing WaSH non-food items such as buckets, jerrycans, filter cloths, and PUR water treatment sachets, along with boiling pots, while training 6,000 households (at least 30,000 individuals) on water disinfection; with 2,000 in Koch, 2,000 in Fangak, and 2,000 in Leer. Conduct post-distribution monitoring and surveys. Enhancing hygiene practices through 120 community hygiene promoters and improving hygiene and sanitation conditions in vulnerable communities. Educating women and schoolgirls of reproductive age on menstrual hygiene management (MHM) and providing MHM kits through core pipeline and market procurement. Launch public awareness campaigns to promote hygiene practices, including hand hygiene and waste disposal. Implementing the Child-to-Child hygiene promotion strategy, supporting community initiatives, and addressing under-5 nutrition cases. Creating and distributing IEC materials and signage as job aids for essential hygiene actions. Celebrate global events such as World Water Week and Global Handwashing Day. 2.3.2 Ensure the restoration of access to clean and safe drinking water for households, communities, and health and nutrition facilities by: Repairing, upgrading, and securing damaged boreholes in Koch (14 boreholes), Leer (10 boreholes), and Fangak (7 boreholes) counties. Providing training and support to pump mechanics and water user committees on the operation and maintenance of handpumps. Conducting water quality monitoring using H2S test kits or similar methods (presence/absence tests) for both pre and post-rehabilitation assessments where applicable. 2.3.3 Enhance and establish access to safe disposal of faecal waste in communities and health and nutrition facilities by: Improving latrines and handwashing stations in selected sixteen health and nutrition facilities and densely populated areas (IDP gathering sites) to promote latrine usage. Promoting the construction of household-level latrines to improve sanitation practices. 2.3.4 Gender-Based Violence and Protection Integration: World Relief will incorporate protection and gender-based violence (GBV) considerations across all sectors, adhering to established humanitarian standards and Protection/APP guidelines. Key activities will include Organizing PSEA training sessions for midwives and other ANC/PNC care providers on post-rape treatment in line with cluster and national guidelines. Increasing awareness of APP, protection, and referral guidelines among program participants. Creating and distributing IEC materials for educational outreach. Offering psycho-social support and referrals within Nutrition and Health services. Conducting safety audits and monitoring with a focus on protection issues. Observing global events such as the 16 Days of Activism and International Women’s Day.
