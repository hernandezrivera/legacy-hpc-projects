Needs

The presence of Ukrainian armed forces and armed groups in populated areas increases the risk of sexual violence against civilians. The deterioration of the economic situation, particularly in conflict-affected regions, combined with the destruction of community ties caused by the conflict and displacement, have compelled some people to use harmful survival strategies and coping mechanisms that may increase the risk of sexual violence and trafficking;National legislation and legal practice regarding the prosecution of sexual violence is limited and is not fully in line with international standards and practice. Due to a number of gaps in legislation and a lack of capacity, acts of sexual violence are often recorded by law enforcement as other crimes such as bodily injury. Lawyers, police officers, prosecutors and judges lack knowledge of how to document, investigate and consider cases of conflict-related sexual violence, as well as with regards to allegations of ill-treatment and torture. Consequently, victims of sexual violence are often confronted with inaction from state authorities or armed groups who exercise control over certain areas;Another problematic aspect is the lack of support available for victims. Overall, professionals in medical and social state institutions lack the specific knowledge and skills required to deal with survivors of torture and conflict-related sexual violence. Consequently, services for these individuals are provided by civil society organizations through donor-funded programmes. In addition, while high quality services are provided in urban centres, mostly in Kyiv. There is little or no assistance available in smaller towns and rural areas. According to OHCHR data, along the contact line, due to lack of income, women and some girls, were resorting to harmful survival practices, for instance engaging in sexual intercourse with members of armed actors in exchange for money or food;According to the data within the ‘Emergency protection-based support to conflict affected older women and men in the GCAs locations of Donetsk and Luhansk Oblasts’ presented by HelpAge on 19 of September 2018, 20,5% of older people aged over 60 were subjected to at least one type of violence and abuse. Of them 96,9% reported emotional and psychological abuse, 0,9% reported gender-based violence, 0,4% - physical violence and 0.2% reported sexual type of violence. Among the elderly who subjected to at least one type of violence, share of women were 75,9%;According to the findings of the by members of the Coalition “Justice for Peace in Donbas” and presented in 2017, the GBV against men is almost as regular and widespread as against women - 92 men and 114 women were victims of GBV. Among the reasons for the use of GBV were support of Ukrainian army, military service in Ukrainian army or volunteer battalions, political and religion views, race, ethnicity, sexual orientation or in number of cases GBV were used without any particular reasons.

Activities & outcomes

Outcome: Conflict-affected community members in the Donbas increased their capacity to prevent and respond to Gender Based Violence. Output 1: Conflict-affected community members in the Donbas are better informed on GBV issues, including the importance of gender sensitivity in family and community activities, through community-led activities - Activities for output 1: 1) Conduct training on GBV related issues for the community members, including boys, girls, women and men; 2) Provide consultations with the community members for the development of community led initiatives to share the information about GBV; 3) Open a call among the targeted communities to apply with community-led development initiatives to share the information about gender and GBV issues; 4) Select community level development initiatives through the competitive selection process; 5) Conduct procurement of assets and other needed materials and services to be needed for the community members to implement approved activities; 6) Monitor the implementation of community led-initiatives. Output 2: Local officials from police social services and other relevant stakeholders in targeted communities are better prepared to provide relevant services regarding the GBV issues - Activities for output 2: 1) Select local officials (male and female) to participate in trainings on gender based violence; 2) Select expert trainers and prepare training materials on gender-based, domestic and conflict-related sexual violence for local officials; 3) Carry out trainings for selected of local officials on gender based violence issues; Output 3: IDPs and other vulnerable persons are better informed on the GBV though the awareness raising campaigns and events - Activities for output 3: 1) Carry out round tables involving women and men from the targeted communities, police workers and social service workers with a focus on complaints procedures and to build trust; 2) Carry out workshops with women and men leaders in targeted communities to train them as peer to peer multipliers to empower women and man to speak out and raise awareness of gender based violence; 3) Carry out an awareness raising campaign to inform on the right to a life free of violence and the about the existing help lines and complaint procedures. IOM is also an active member of the Humanitarian Country Team (HCT), Inter Cluster (ICWG), as well as an active member of the Early Recovery, Shelter/NFI, Wash, Protection Cluster and the Cash Working Group, among other technical coordination working groups. Under the current project strong cooperation with the GBV Sub cluster and actors providing assistance to GBV survivors is foreseen. Activities will seek to take advantage of a gender expert with experience working on GBV issues that will be attracted by IOM. IOM is committed to incorporate recommendation for a collective, system-wide approach on AAP in Ukraine. The project’s approach to gender is underpinned by the recognition that the phenomenon of human trafficking is an abuse of human rights, rather than a criminal activity suffered by women or men, girls or boys. Equal access to all project activities is provided to all beneficiaries independently of their sex. At the same time, a gender sensitive approach is applied in assessing the needs of beneficiaries and the provision of rehabilitation and reintegration services to them. Sex disaggregated data is collected for all activities. Project monitoring and evaluation tools will be designed and focus on the relevance, efficiency, effectiveness, impact and sustainability of project activities and results. IOM has acting Hotline that is widely used as feedback mechanism by the project beneficiaries.

Outcome: Conflict-affected community members in the Donbas increased their capacity to prevent and respond to Gender Based Violence: 1) % of local officials confirming overall improvement in their capacities to provide services to the GBV victims and identify GBV victims (Target: 75%); 2) % of individuals who reported that they improve their knowledge on GBV related issues (Target: 75%); Output 1: Conflict-affected community members in the Donbas are better informed on GBV issues, including the importance of gender sensitivity in family and community activities, through community-led activities- 1) # of trainings on gender-based, domestic and conflict-related sexual violence for representatives of targeted communities conducted (Target: 15 trainings); 2) # of training participants (Target: 375 participants, including 225 female and 150 male, out of whom 50 children (teenagers), 275 adults, 50 elderly); 3) # of the community led-initiatives on GBV issues conducted (Target: 100); 4) # of community members participated in the community led-activities (Target: 10,000, including 6,000 female and 4,000 male, out of whom 1,000 children, 7,000 adults, 2,000 elderly). Output 2: Local officials from police social services and other relevant stakeholders in targeted communities are better prepared to provide relevant services regarding the GBV issues: - 1) # of trainings on gender-based, domestic and conflict-related sexual violence for local officials organized (Target: 30 trainings); 2) # of training participants (Target: 750 participants, including 450 female and 300 male) Output 3: IDPs and other vulnerable persons are better informed on the GBV though the awareness raising campaigns and events - 1) # of round tables conducted involving police workers, social workers and representatives of target communities (Target: 15 round tables); 2) # of individuals that actively participates in the round tables (Target: 375 individuals, including 225 female and 150 male); 3) # of workshops conducted (Target: 15 workshops); 4) # of awareness raising campaigns on GBV related issues (Target: 15 campaigns); 5) # of leaflets with the information about GBV related issues printed out and distributed (Target: 30,000 leaflets); 6) # of persons covered through awareness raising campaigns (Target: 40,000 individuals, including 24,000 female and 16,000 male, out of whom 8,000 children, 24,000 adults, 8,000 elderly).
