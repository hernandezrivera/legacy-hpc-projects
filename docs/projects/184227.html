Project Summary

According to the JMCNA 2021, shelter is one of the top three priority need of more than half of the entire population and of 58 percent of IDPs406. An estimated 3.7 million people need shelter and NFI assistance mainly due to poor shelter conditions, overcrowding and lack of space, poverty, and lack of security of tenure. Those in need of shelter and NFI assistance include displaced and non-displaced populations. However, IDPs, refugee returnees, refugees and asylum seekers are particularly affected as they do not have financial capacity to build adequate shelters. Conflict, drought, flooding, evictions and cyclones are the key drivers of displacement, which in turn exacerbates humanitarian shelter and NFI needs. Natural disasters are one of the main causes of displacement and therefore accelerate migration from rural to urban and peri-urban centers that started decades ago. Lower Shabelle and Middle Shabelle regions are among the most vulnerable parts of Somalia that could face the natural disasters mainly with floods at the districts along the river passing areas, like Marka, Qoryooley, Afgooye, Kurtunwaarey ( Lower Shabelle) and Jowhar and Balcad ( Middle Shabelle) of Somalia. On the droughts and locusts impacts, Baraawe and Wanla Weyn, Marka, Qoryoley ( Lower Shabelle) and Jowhar and Balcad ( Middle Shabelle). According to the CCCM Cluster, the eviction and Armed conflicts are another factors affecting the Lower & Middle Shabelle communities and therefore an estimated of 1.8 million of IDPs (62 per cent of IDPs) are at high risk of eviction. According to AYUUB assessment on this regard, Wanla Weyn, Marka and Jowhar are the vulnerable locations compared to other parts. However, according to the Detailed Site Assessment 2021, a total of 593,058 IDPs are estimated to reside in high-risk IDP sites due to both conflict and climate related forcing factors. These informal and largely unplanned IDP settlements tend to involve particularly hazardous living conditions. A lack of tenure agreements and housing options for IDPs from minority and marginalized groups leaves them without alternative residence options. Among these population, 42 per cent of the main income-earner of the household are women. Finally, women and girls living in sub-standard and overcrowded shelters such in Km50 IDPs sites in Afgooye and Jiliyaale, Airportka, Sheikh Omar and Tawakal IDPs sites in Jowhar are particularly vulnerable. They tend not to feel safe in makeshift shelters which do not offer privacy and sufficient protection from weather elements and increase the risk of gender based violence. Over a quarter, 27 per cent, of IDPs reported that their bathing facilities are not safe as their main shelter concern. Through this HRP 2022, AYUUB will respond to the conflict and climate impacted 45,000 people with gender disaggregation of (20,000 female, 10,000 male, 7500 Girls and 7500 Boys) in Marka, Afgooye and Baraawe ( Lower Shabelle) and Jowhar and Balcad ( Middle Shabelle)with Shelter and NFIs distribution service. The mode of the distribution will be decided upon the circumstance. AYUUB will further strengthen the community feedback mechanisms through following a proper community complaint mechanisms data collection and enable the IDPs for easily access to AYUUB Hotline to deliver their complaints and their complaints will addressed timely by the respective office.

1. - Projects activities

Though proposed intervention, AYUUB will support the Conflicts and Climate Shocks affected populations estimated 45,000 people with gender disaggregation of (20,000 female, 10,000 male, 7500 Girls and 7500 Boys) in Marka, Afgooye and Baraawe ( Lower Shabelle) and Jowhar and Balcad ( Middle Shabelle)regions, Somalia with provision of ESKs and NFIs (3750 HHs will receive NFIs and the other 3750 HHs will receive ESKs) the project will specifically targets the populations affected by the conflicts, droughts, locusts, Covid19 and floods affected peoples. The mode of the distribution will be decided upon the circumstance. AYUUB will further strengthen the community feedback mechanisms through following a proper community complaint mechanisms data collection and enable the IDPs for easily access to AYUUB Hotline to deliver their complaints and their complaints will addressed timely by the respective office. AYUUB will distribute NFIs and ESKs through in Kind distribution to 45,000 people with gender disaggregation of (20,000 female, 10,000 male, 7500 Girls and 7500 Boys) in Marka, Afgooye and Baraawe ( Lower Shabelle) and Jowhar and Balcad ( Middle Shabelle). Based on AYUUB Market Assessment in the markets at Lower and Middle Shabelle regions, AYUUB noted that Markets are poorly functional and cannot supply the quantity and quality ESKs and NFIs to the targeted number of beneficieries, therefore, AYUUB saw Cash Use is Impossible alternatively, hence, AYUUB will precure basic Non Food Items through Tender process from Bakara and Suuq Ba’ad Markets in Mogadishu. The age and gender disaggregation is based on the demographic profile in Somalia and prioritization of women and girls who are the most affected by displacements. The actual gender and age breakdown will be reported once beneficiary registration is completed. AYUUB will promote the dignity of the beneficiaries by placing the decision of the content of priority NFIs in their best interests. The approach will empower displaced people in prioritizing needs and give them the power of making decisions for their needs. AYUUB will publicly mobilize to the Targeted community through microphones and meetings and placing the AYUUB Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms Hotline number / Free charge (2288) in the public gather areas for feedback and suggestions. Beneficiaries will be targeted based on vulnerability criteria that will prioritize women headed households, the elderly and children headed households especially girls. The beneficiaries will be distributed equally with each beneficiary getting the Standard NFI and Shelter kit approved by Cluster. AYUUB will closely coordinate with Other Agencies distributing NFI's in the areas and start the project implementation with community consultations and coordination with Local Authority to identify vulnerable people displaced by the drought.
