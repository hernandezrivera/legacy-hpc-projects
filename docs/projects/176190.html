Project Summary

In South Sudan, WASH needs remain as one of the highest priorities for a sustainable response to the humanitarian crisis. Nowadays, only 36% of households reports having access to an improved water source in under 30 mins without facing any protection or security constraints concerns. This number is even more dramatic considering the percentage has actually decreased when compared to the 38% reported at the end of 2019. Safe access is even less for vulnerable groups constantly being disproportionally affected by the crisis as women and girls suffering significant risks of GBV at WASH services and people with disabilities, often unable to physical use them. The main drivers of WASH needs include limited physical infrastructure, inter-community violence and cyclical flooding in many parts of the country, particularly in Jonglei, Upper Nile and Unity state. WASH needs are particularly high among IDPs and returnees in non-camp setting and among already stretched host communities. Feuds for resources and “control” of WASH services, often exacerbate inter-community violence in already volatile areas suffering from constant revenge feuds like Akobo, Pibor, and Mayendit. To this already dire situation, the general food insecurity in the areas reaching already an emergency phase (IPC 4) in all of the counties targeted with expected pockets of famine through the lean season of 2021 in Pibor, Akobo, Nyriol and Ayod, increases the need of ensuring an increased access to WASH services.
To ensure the most severe needs are met in priority, INTERSOS proposes to tailor the response for each county targeted, addressing the most urgent WASH needs. The overall response will seek to integrate as much as possible the provision of WASH services with the prevention of GBV, especially in the counties where INTERSOS has already ongoing protection interventions including Akobo, Pibor, Ayod, Nyriol and Mayendit. The proposed intervention will reach around 60,000 people in the counties with the highest IPC and GAM levels and will consist of two packages of assistance. A first one targeting Akobo, Pibor and Mayendit, counties with the highest overall WASH needs aimed at sustainably increasing access to safe water and sanitation services through the construction of additional water point and communal latrines, the provision of HHWTS kits, provision of MHM kits and strong hygiene awareness. The second package targeting Ayod, Canal/Pigi and Nyriol will focus only on the services with poorest coverage including access to safe water in Canal/Pigi, NFI Wash distribution in Nyirol and Distribution of MHM kits in Ayod. The second package will also be completed with a strong hygiene promotion component. Both packages will provide GBV safety audits across locations with an envelope of the budget reserved to enhance protection mitigation measures at the assessed WASH facilities including solar streetlamps and lockable door, among others. Finally, gender equality will be promoted in a sustainable way through the participation of women at WASH committees (minimum of 50%) and the recruitment of female community mobilizers.

1 - Needs

In 2021, 5.6 million people in South Sudan are in need of WASH services. This number is higher than the one of 2020, showing the last 12 months have had a nefast impact of the overall humanitarian situation of the country, especially in the North- east part of the countries, in the states of Jonglei, Unity and Upper Nile. The situation rapidly deteriorated by the multiple simultaneous shock’s populations faced, particularly the increased inter-community violence, the unprecedented floods, and the negative impacts of COVID19. Said shocks carried continued displacement of people, restricting their access to already limited WASH infrastructure. The situation is particularly dire in Jonglei and Upper Nile states where food insecurity has reached the phase of emergency with pockets of famines in counties like Pibor and Akobo. The lack of WASH services coupled with the high food insecurity poses a significant threat to the health of children through the corelated increase of malnutrition. INTERSOS presence through protection projects in some of the most vulnerable counties including Akobo, Mayendit, Pibor, Ayod and Nyirol has allowed the analysis of a direct link between poor WASH services and protection risks, mainly related to GBV. In fact, only 36% of households across the country report having access to an improved source in under 30 minutes without facing any protection concerns. According to INTERSOS needs assessment of January 2021, this number can be even lower in places being severely hit by community violence, where respondents reported an overall lack of safety in the water points where due to clan feuds harassment and abuse from rival groups is constant. Additionally, across locations a lack of preventive GBV measures at sanitation facilities has been reported, with counties such as Akobo, Ayod and Pibor reporting a higher degree of concerns. In said locations, distribution of dignity kits aiming at reducing vulnerabilities of women of menstrual age has been lacking and increases their overall protection risks.In Canal/Pigi, there are low reports of protection concerns in WASH facilities general. However, according to the need’s assessment performed in January 2021, this mainly due the overall lack of WASH services rather than a protective environment. According to WASH severity index, Canal/Pigi 0% of the population have access to safe water, being the counties in more dire need. The situation is exacerbated by the fact until now, no WASH partners are available in the area. The severity of the situation remains the same when looking at access to sanitation services or handwashing services. In Mayendit the situation is also quite severe, with the county being in WASH severity 5. The main reason for this is the floods that left the county under water throughout the second semester 2, hampering the access to humanitarian assistance. The access situation remains challenging in January 2021, with humanitarian staff having to land in Leer and do the rest of the trip by canoe to Mayendit. The overall situation has remained critical in spite of INTERSOS GBV intervention restarted in September 2020, with high prevalence of SGBV cases in WASH facilities partially of completely destroyed by the floods. The floods have also ravaged Ayod county, that until January 2021 remains largely under water with high influx of IDPs leaving flooded payams to reach Ayod town. According to protection monitoring reports of January 2021, over 13,000 people would have arrived to Ayod town in the last 2 months, posing an extra burden on already limited and functional WASH infrastructure in the area.

2 - Project activities

INTERSOS project plans to implement activities in 6 locations: Akobo, Pibor, Canal/Pigi, Ayod and Nyirol in Jonglei State and Mayendit in Unity State. Given the need of prioritization among locations and targets, the project proposes two different packages aiming at addressing the most immediate needs in each of the locations. The first package consists in a robust and holistic response for the counties in dire need of WASH services. This package will be implemented in Akobo, Pibor and Mayendit and will include the following activities: 1.1 Installation of additional water points, targeting the Payams with lowest coverage, 1.2. Rehabilitation of water point including hand pumps repairs. 1.3. Establishment and training of Water management committee (WMC). The training will include specific modules on operation and maintenance of water sources and material support the WMC with pump maintenance tool kits. It will be ensured that at least 50% of committee members will be women. 1.4 Construction of communal latrines gender and disability sensitive, using iron sheets and plastic slabs clearly marked, separate, lockable, well-lit latrines for women & men, boys & girls fitted with handwashing point. 1.5 Establish Solid Waste Management Committees (SWMCs) in the target villages supported with cleaning tools and training with active participation of both men and women. 1.6 Treatment of water sources at HH level through chlorination and distribution of household water treatment and storage (HHWTS) kit (including 1 bucket with lid with tap, 1 jerrycan, soap for 3 months, 1 filter cloth). 1.7 Provision of safety audits before and after any new construction and at every facility rehabilitated facility aimed at addressing protection and specifically GBV concerns in WASH services. The safety audits will also include the establishment/update of referral pathways in every location. 1.8 Implementation of protection mitigation measures including, stand-alone solar streetlights and other security measures (reinforcement of overall structure, lockable doors, etc.) in the facilities considered most dangerous by the results of the safety audit. 1.9 Provision of MHM kits for menstrual age women, including solar lamps and prioritizing GBV survivors or particularly vulnerable women and girls at risk (widows, child headed households, etc.) 1.10 Hygiene promotion at community level with emphasis in the following topics: diseases prevention (Water-borne diseases and COVID-19), handwashing, safe excreta management, safe methods to keep villages clean and free of waste materials, GBV messages and promotion of referral pathways. Training of health/hygiene promoters who will be 70% female will conduct hygiene promotion at household and community level, early detect and manage AWD/cholera cases in the areas targeted by INTERSOS and also identify and refer GBV cases to INTERSOS protection team for subsequent actions and guidance.The second package will consist of a lighter response aimed at providing the most urgent WASH services in counties with little to no humanitarian presence and where only one or two sectoral objectives are considered priority. The target locations for this package include: Ayod, Nyirol and Canal/Pigi. The package will be tailored to each location to ensure maximum efficient of resources. The main activities of this package include: 2.1. Installation of additional water points and establishment of WMC (Canal/Pigi) as per package 1. 2.2 Installation of handwashing facilities in crowded public places including communal latrines, and markets (3 locations) 2.3 Distribution of HHWTS kit (3 locations) 2.4 Safety audits in existent and to be constructed WASH facilities (3 locations). 2.5 Implementation of protection mitigation measures as package 1 (Ayod). 2.6 Provision of MHM kits for menstrual age women (Ayod). 2.7 Hygiene promotion at community level as per package 1 (3 locations).

3 - Project indicators

Measuring the impact of the WASH intervention will be done by a robust MEAL plan developed to measure both outcome and outputs of the project in a quantitative and qualitative way. The measure of outcome results will include the collection of a baseline, to have a base against which to measure the success of the intervention. Additionally, indicators measuring overall satisfaction of beneficiary will be measured on the regular basis through PDM exercise and quality surveys performed quarterly in all locations. Outcome 3 % of respondents to community survey who know at least 3 key moments for hand washing# of people reached with hygiene promotion activities and COVID-19 messages.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

For complaints related to PSEA and CP policy issues, two members of each CRM committee (one men and one women) will be selected by the community as PSEA / CP focal points. They will receive specific sensitivity training and direct communication line (phone and airtime) with the PSEA focal point in mission in Juba. The focal point will then follow INTERSOS PSEA protocols and liaise directly with the internal audit.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Each CRM committee will have one representative of the most vulnerable groups of the community including among others PWD, elderly, youth, and women. The CRM committee mechanisms was put in place to ensure most vulnerable members had access to said mechanisms after other feedback mechanisms including complaint boxes showed their inadaptation to the context precisely by the important number of illiterate individuals. It is expected that through this inclusive mechanism feedback of the most vulnerable will be collected on a regular basis.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will use several channels to ensure proper communication with beneficiaries. First, the project will include a strong awareness and mobilization component where community mobilizers will be in charge of reaching all the community using door to door methodologies to comply with COVID19 regulations and FGD in small groups to present the project, the selection criteria and the activities in general. Additionally, the project will work closely with local authorities to ensure they also promote the project within the community. To ensure beneficiary perception of the project is gathered on a regular basis, satisfaction quality survey questions will be included in the PDM exercises done after every distribution . The project will also include a final satisfaction survey at the end of the project, aimed at determining the impact of the project in the overall WASH access of beneficiaries. INTERSOS will ensure that complaint and feedback mechanisms are in place throughout the implementation period, aiming at safeguarding proper accountability and program quality, For complaint and feedback on the implementation of activities (including for example delays of implementation, complaints about selection of beneficiaries, etc.), a CRM committee in each location will be established by ensuring all groups of the society - and particularly women, children and people with disabilities - have a representative. Each CRM committee will be trained about standard procedure for treating complaints and feedbacks. Additionally, complaint box will be established at every distribution point and training venue, and the CRM will also be in charge of collecting feedback provided by this mean.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

On a monthly basis, program staff on the ground will meet the CRM committee and add to a pre-established matrix all feedback received by the beneficiaries will the measures to be taken to address said feedback. This matrix will be shared with INTERSOS coordination team in Juba that will do a strict follow up of the measures taken during field visits. For the PSEA and CP complaints, a separated matrix will be kept by the focal point where all feedback receive will be stored as well as the measures taken. This matrix will be shared on a regular basis with the internal audit.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

• Risks that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services.- The risk is medium: INTERSOS will involve the camp leaders and local authorities in identifying the most vulnerable persons based on the criteria set and registration done by INTERSOS local staffs (Hygiene promotors). Distribution of Hygiene kits, dignity kits, solar laps and other supplies to the registered beneficiaries will be done in an orderly manner, during the day and in a safe place proximity to the beneficiaries’ residences. Door to door distribution will be considered to the most vulnerable e.g disables and persons stigmatized due to GBV incidences• Risks of women, girls and boys without and with disabilities exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance.• staff will have to sign the code of conduct when signing the contract and before activities start all teams will receive PSEA and Child protection training. Additionally, specific PSEA complaint mechanisms will be put in place through the PSEA focal points within the CRM committee to ensure complaints reach Juba and the internal audit as early as possible.• Risks that the project does not have sufficient internal mechanisms to combat and prevent child safeguarding and PSEA. - Anyone involved in this project in one way or other is required to sign INTEROS’s Code of Conduct. Violations observed during the implementation will be reported to the donor and local authorities. Further steps will be taken in mobilizing the community to combat early child marriages and observe the rights and dignity of women, children and persons with disabilities. All staff, implementing partners and volunteers involved in distributions and in direct contact with beneficiaries have received training in prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA).• Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.) • Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and Older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities.High: in each CRM it will be ensured at least on representative of each marginalized group is represented in the committee to guarantee feedback from these vulnerable groups is received. Additionally, during PDM exercises, a percentage of the sample interviewed will have to come from every vulnerable group. • Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources.- Medium: INTERSOS will carry out conflict analysis in all intervention areas and work with conflict sensitivity as an integral part of all programme implementation (through community sensitization, transparency on selection criteria, complaints and feedback mechanisms) to ensure that the intervention do not exacerbate existing conflict dynamics. The selection of beneficiaries will be done as per vulnerability criteria developed at the beginning of the intervention with the participation of the communities including local authorities and community leaders. The selection process will include the creation of a selection committee composed of INTERSOS staff, local authorities and representative of the different vulnerable groups including women, youth and people with disability Selection beneficiaries through this mechanism will ensure the transparency of the project and increase the participation of communities inside the project.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

The project is designed in such a way that it addresses WASH needs in the target areas through integrating protection services predominantly GBV and complementing the ongoing existing protection services provided by INTERSOS in those areas with the support of UNHCR, UNICEF and other partners. Additionnaly the project directly aims at integrating WASH and protection needs through the establishment/ update of referral patwhays in the locations targeted and promote the services available through the awarness component. The awarness component built around hygiene and protection services promotion will allow large parts of the population to have access to the information. Additionnally, EIC material adapted to the context will be published in every WASH facility targeted aimed at increasing accesss to information, including access to complain mechanisms where suggestions boxes will also be made available.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

The package 1 tackles the counties with most severe food insecurity (Akobo and Pibor) with a holistic response to increase overall access to WASH services, and indirectly improving the overall food insecurity.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

A particular focus will be put in ensuring all services are accessible to most vulnerable population in a safe way. This includes mainly women and girls at higher risk of GBV and people with disability and older persons. The provision of safety audits as a crosscutting activity will guaranty the equitable access to all services provided.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

Given the direct correlation between poor WASH services and malnutrition, it is expected that by increasing access to clean and safe water in areas with severe GAM through the rehabilitation and construction of water point and the distribution of HHWRS kits, the prevalence of water borne diseases will be reduced, improving indirectly overall nutritional status. To ensure the distribution of HHWRS has an influence on the overall nutritional status, households with children under five, particularly children in their first 1000 days of life and/or pregnant and lactating women, will be prioritized for the distribution. The selection will also be coordinated with nutritional partners on the ground aiming at providing priority to families where cases of MAM and MAS have already been identified and where the lack of access to clean water could jeopardize the improvement of the cases.
