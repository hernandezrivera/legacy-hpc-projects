Project Name

Project Summary

The objective of the project is to increase resilience and reduce vulnerability of the conflict-affected boys, girls and their families, caregivers, males and females, through life-saving direct support/assistance, MHPSS activities, informal education and recreational activities by strengthening protective environment and improving access to child protection services.

1 - Needs

The 2019 HNO indicates that humanitarian needs are deteriorating, especially in NGCA, due to the protracted and forgotten nature of the conflict. There are 700,000 children in need (322,000 boys, 378,000 girls) in eastern Ukraine, including 120,000 living along the contact line of GCA, 285,000 living along the contact line of NGCA and 100,000 living in NGCA outside of contact line areas.Child protectionAccording to the 2019 HNO, there are 3.1M people in need of protection, including 465,000 children (209,250 boys; 255,750 girls). Children are facing protection risks such as shelling, mines/ERW, all forms of violence (including physical, emotional, sexual), neglect, psychological distress, lack of documentation. Children are particularly vulnerable due to their weakened protective environment: family separation, displacement, and constant shelling result in extreme stress, sleep and concentration problems, bed-wetting, hair loss, avoidance, intrusive memories, hyperactivity, and social isolation. SCI's experience shows that psychosocial support (PSS) is of utmost importance for affected children. Many children experience violence and neglect in HH where parents rely on negative coping mechanisms, and CWD are at higher risk of violence, GBV, abuse and exploitation. In MSNA2, children are identified as one of the most vulnerable groups in terms of protection in NGCA, and their psychosocial status is a significant concern. Many respondents identified an increase in Gender-Based Violence (GBV), and many survivors do not report it. GBV also affects girls, including resorting to survival sex to support their families. The Protection Cluster 2019 HNO confirms that children in conflict-affected areas are at higher risk of domestic violence as well as being victims and/or witnesses of SGBV. The gender and disability disaggregated impact of the conflict on children is only anecdotal, since there is no comprehensive assessment focused on this subject. In terms of psychological distress, the research by UNICEF (unpublished) shows that conflict-affected adolescent girls are more likely to internalize problems related to the conflict (anxiety, depression, self-harm, suicidality) than adolescent boys who will externalize problems (delinquency, substance abuse, bullying, risky sexual behaviors, aggression). These findings reflect the reinforcement of traditional gender social norms as a result of the conflict. Child protection systems, including referral mechanisms and case management, need further strengthening, as very limited numbers of social workers are available to manage child protection cases. Information gaps between referral services hamper adequate response; caregivers, education and health actors have limited knowledge of existing referrals to support children.

2 - Activities & outcomes

Outcome 1. Delivering immediate, life-saving humanitarian assistance along the contact line of Donetsk nd Luhansk GCA and Donetsk NGCA (through partners). Boys and girls of different age groups, are provided with psychosocial services through structured activities at community centers and through mobile units in education institutions. 1) Strengthen 3 existing community centers with 12 mobile units along the contact line. Establish 1 new community center. 2) Strengthen 2 existing Case Management unit of oblast level and identify locations and establish 2 new CMUs of regional level to provide individual support for the most vulnerable categories of children and 5) establish an emergency fund to provide support for the most vulnerable children and meet their individual needs.3) Continue with a comprehensive program of psychosocial support including SCI signature programs HEART, Child to child, Life Skills and Resiliency for children aged 6-18 y.o. Conduct child protection and gender sensitivity public awareness campaign. 4) Reinforcing the existing referral and case management systems at the local level through capacity building of duty bearers and protection actors with strong linkages to social services in GCA and NGCA. Most staff of the case management unit are also engaged with the state social services in Druzhkivka. Their capacity is built by SCI in line with international best practice on case management and therefore this knowledge will remain in the state social service. The unit in Druzhkivka is the municipal centre of social services. Save the Children also makes effort to involve state social workers in all relevant trainings and working closely with the CM unit.5) Provide mine risk education sessions in schools and kindergartens along the contact line. Outcome 2. Adults, parents and caregivers, elderly males and females, of conflict affected vulnerable boys and girls, are better equipped to care for, protect children in distress, need of psychosocial support. 1) Continue with positive parenting program and positive discipline sessions to increase their knowledge, form practical skills how to support children experienced crisis situations and traumatic events. 2) Continue with SCI signature programs such as Life Skills and Resilience and HEART programs for children, parents and caregivers. The activities are planned to meet the urgent need of psycho-social support for youth and their families. 3) Hold community meetings and events to mobilize local communities and resources, make families more united and supportive; inform about existing local mechanisms, available services or referral pathways. Outcome 3. Local professionals, males and females, staff of centers of social services, school/after school institutions, public libraries, culture institutions, volunteers, local authorities representatives, local NGOs are trained on how to provide better support to children including identifying signs of distress and providing support personally or through referrals, case management approach, strengthening referral system and coordination at all levels, regular updating of service mapping. 1) Hold 10 learning events on Humanitarian principles, CPMS and CPiE, MHPSS recommendations, PFA, Case Management approach, Child Participation, Parenting without violence, etc. 2) Conduct professionls on Life skills and Resiliency for children and youth trainings; HEART direct trainings with further mentorship and supervision sessions. 3) Ensure the mentioned programs are further implemented through community centers and child spaces to meet PSS needs of children and their parents, caregivers. 4) Formalize referral system at local levels and ensure proper referral mechanisms in place. Outcomet 4. Identify key implementing partners from local organizations and increase their and other local NGOs capacities through tailor made and general capacity building program
