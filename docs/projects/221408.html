Project Summary


Context and Justification
Children continue to bear the brunt of the ongoing humanitarian crisis in the North-West and South-West regions. The primary child protection concerns are psychosocial distress, mental disorders, and family separation. According to the 2024 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), many children have been exposed to or have become victims of extreme violence, witnessing the killings of their parents, siblings, or other relatives. Their young minds struggle to process these traumatic experiences. Additionally, many children have been separated from their families due to insecurity, the death of parents, destruction of homes, poverty, or voluntary separation to attend school. Boys are at a higher risk of recruitment and use by armed groups, which also increases their risk of arbitrary arrest on suspicion of being members or potential members of these groups.
The lack or loss of birth certificates continues to exacerbate other child protection risks by hindering access to basic services and exposing children to further risks of exploitation, arbitrary arrest, and detention. With many schools closed or under attack due to the crisis, numerous children lack access to protection services. These circumstances increase protection risks for vulnerable groups, compounding the challenges faced by children, women, and crisis-affected families. In the Southwest Region of Cameroon, particularly due to the ongoing conflict and instability, children face a range of protection risks, including:
1. Recruitment by Armed Groups – Children are at risk of being forcibly recruited as child soldiers or used in support roles by armed groups.
2. Separation from Families – Conflict and displacement often lead to family separation, increasing vulnerability to exploitation and abuse.
3. Sexual and Gender-Based Violence (SGBV) – Girls are especially vulnerable to sexual violence, exploitation, and early marriage as a means of coping with insecurity.
4. Child Labor – Economic hardship and displacement drive children into exploitative labor conditions, including domestic work and hazardous environments.
5. Lack of Access to Education – Many children are denied education due to school closures, insecurity, and attacks on educational facilities as well as threads within the community for anyone who will send His or her child(ren) to school.
6. Psychosocial Distress – Prolonged exposure to violence, displacement, and loss can cause severe emotional and psychological trauma in children.
7. Neglect and Lack of Basic Needs – Displacement often leads to inadequate access to food, shelter, healthcare, and basic services, increasing neglect and malnutrition risks.
8. Physical Abuse and Domestic Violence – The stress of conflict often results in increased domestic violence, affecting children within their homes.
9. Child Trafficking – Vulnerable children, especially those separated from families, are at higher risk of being trafficked for exploitation, including sexual exploitation.
10. Early and Forced Marriages – Families may marry off young girls as a coping mechanism for insecurity or economic hardship, depriving them of their childhood and education.
These risks highlight the urgent need for comprehensive child protection interventions in the region.
This project seeks to alleviate the impact of the ongoing crisis on children in the South-West Region of Cameroon by providing specialized psychosocial support and family tracing and reunification services. It aims to rebuild the resilience of children and their caregivers, enabling them to reconnect and cope with the traumatic effects of the conflict. Comprehensive case management services will be offered to children with protection concerns, including unaccompanied and separated children (UASC). Family tracing and reunification (FTR) services, as well as alternative care arrangements, will be established for children in need of care and protection.
