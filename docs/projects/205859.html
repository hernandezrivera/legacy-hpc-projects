Project Summary

In line with the South Sudan 2024-25 HRP objective of Reducing morbidity and mortality of Vulnerable crises-affected people through equitable, safe, and dignified access to life-saving assistance to meet their needs and the FSL Cluster priorities and cluster objectives, in 2024-25, the FSL Cluster aims (1) Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 4 and 5); (2) Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock and; (3) to reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability both to absorb shocks and adapt to a changing context, especially in relation to climatic, economic and conflict related risks. Agency for Child Relief Aid (ACRA’s) project is aimed at providing food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations in IPC 5, 4 and some in 1 target County of Akobo of Jonglei State, Baliet and Longuchuk Counties of Upper Nile State. The project aims to improve the food security and livelihoods of IDPs, returnees and host communities through provision of timely and appropriate main season agricultural kits (crop and vegetable seeds, and fishing kits), (linked to cluster objective 2), capacity building & training, asset building and market support and training in improved agronomic and fishing practices, (linked to cluster objective 3). ACRA will also provide emergency assistance and livelihoods support to save lives and avert total collapse of livelihoods in the target Counties. The provision of agricultural & fishing in kits, training in agricultural production and fishing is expected to improve the food production and in turn the food security of the target households (HHs) thus supporting them to recover from crisis & placing them on an accelerated path to early recovery and resilience to acute climate shocks.
ACRA seeks to support 3,725 HHs (22,350 individuals) in the targeted Counties with agricultural and livelihood kits. The kits will be procured and distributed directly by ACRA and some will be secured from FAO pipeline. Training on improved agronomic & fishing practices, marketing and group dynamics will be done through model farmers & fisher folk who will undergo a Training of Trainers (ToT) and subsequently train the farmers and fisher folk in their communities; knowledge gained from these trainings will improve the agricultural and fishing practices of the target HHs thus improving food production, addressing their immediate food security needs and building their resilience to future shocks. The provision of agricultural and fishing kits and subsequent enhancement of agricultural production and fishing is expected to contribute to improvement of the Household Diversity Score (HDDS) and Food Consumption Score (FCS) of the target HHs
ACRA will employ a community-based beneficiary selection approach which will entail establishing PMC’s to lead the selection of eligible beneficiaries based on the beneficiary selection criteria. Vulnerable HHs with severe food insecurity including female, child, disabled/chronically ill headed HHs; persons living with or at risk of HIV/AIDS and survivors of Gender-Based Violence (GBV) will be targeted in this project.
ACRA will hold regular meetings with the PMC’s and community members and conduct PDMs to get their feedback on the project implementation. ACRA will ensure that their feedback is incorporated in the implementation of this project and design of future projects. Further, to promote accountability to the affected populations, ACRA will use the community-based complaints and feedback mechanism to provide a platform for beneficiaries to voice any issues or concerns. ACRA has ongoing WASH and FSL programs in Upper Nile and in Jonglei which will be compliment these interventions

1. Needs

The high levels of acute food insecurity in South Sudan remain worrying, with the most severely acute food insecure populations significantly affected by frequent climate-related shocks (flooding and dry spells), the economic crisis (currency depreciation and high food prices), conflict and insecurity– including the spillover effects of the conflict in Sudan– causing forced displacement, low agricultural production and a reduction in humanitarian assistance. In the current analysis period of Sept to Nov 2023, an estimated 5.83 million people (46%) are facing Crisis (IPC 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 1.64 million people are facing Emergency (IPC 4) acute food insecurity. According to the IPC AMN (Oct 2023-March 2024), a total 45 counties are classified in IPC AMN Phase 4 (Critical), 16 counties are in IPC AMN Phase 3 (Serious), 10 counties are in IPC AMN Phase 2 (Alert) and 9 counties are in IPC AMN Phase 1 (Acceptable). 31 of the 45 counties classified in IPC AMN Phase 4 (Critical) are from the Greater Upper Nile region in Jonglei and Akobo and Uror are part of them. In the harvest-post-harvest projection period of Dec 2023- March 2024, an estimated 607,000 people (59%) are likely to face Crisis (IPC 3) or worse acute food insecurity in Jonglei State, of which 11,000 people are likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity, 326,000 people are likely to be in Crisis (IPC 3) acute food insecurity, and 270,000 people are likely to be in Emergency (IPC 4) acute food insecurity. Akobo and Uror is among 8 counties that will be classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. In upper Nile during the same period 3 counties will be classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Baliet and Longochuk is among 10 counties that will be classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. Food insecurity is driven by the influxes of returnees from Sudan and EthiopiaIn the lean season projection period between April - July 2024, an estimated 696,000 people (68%) are likely to be in Crisis (IPC 3) or worse acute food insecurity in Jonglei State, 300,000 people will likely be in Emergency (IPC 4) acute food insecurity, and 385,000 people will likely be in Crisis (IPC 3) acute food insecurity. All the counties in the state will be classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. In Upper Nile the food security situation will deteriorate further in Upper Nile with an estimated 541,000 people (66%) likely to be in Crisis (IPC 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 145,000 people will likely be in Emergency (IPC 4) acute food insecurity, and 396,000 people will likely be in Crisis (IPC 3) acute food insecurity Baliet and Longochuk are among 6 counties that will be classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. Food insecurity is driven by multiple shocks including the continuation of flooding in western Jonglei as well as sub-national and localized insecurity and violence, including incidents of raiding and looting, leading to significant internal displacement, restrictions on market access, and reduced access to livelihoods activities. Recent influxes of returnees from Sudan and Ethiopia have additionally stressed local resources with many households hosting the newly arrived. In addition, major underlying vulnerabilities continue to exacerbate the impacts of acute shocks including poorly maintained or inadequate road networks, and poor wash facilities, educational facilities, and health facilities. The Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JMMI) conducted by REACH on behalf of the South Sudan Inter-Agency Cash Working Group (IACWG), highlighted that Akobo, Uror, Baliet and Longuchuk are one of the assessed marketplaces experiencing upward trends in food prices, high Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) costs, and reduced market functionality throughout the year.

2. Project activities

1.1. Community-Based Participation and Planning (CBPP): The CBPP exercise will focus on the full participation of selected community planning teams, who in participatory ways, will identify community needs, suggest some recommendations and draw the final community’s plan. A range of participatory methods will be used to guide the planning process. This exercise will be conducted at the Boma level. ACRA will then establish beneficiary’s selection committees (BSC) and project steering committees (PSC) 1.2. Formation and training of BSCs and PMCs/PSCs: ACRA will facilitate the formation of the Boma-level PMC’s. Each PMC will comprise of 10 members (5 women, 5 men) who will be trained in beneficiary selection criteria and monitoring of the project implementation. The PMCs will also be trained in protection and mainstreaming cross-cutting issues across all activities with a particular focus on gender mainstreaming, GBV, putting additional effort to ensure accountability to the affected population. 1.3. Beneficiary selection: ACRA adopts participatory, vulnerability and rights-based approaches in identification, verification and registration of target beneficiaries. ACRA will furthermore strengthen community communication to ensure beneficiary and non-beneficiary communities are well informed about the project, beneficiary criteria, and how to provide feedback or complains to ACRA. The PSCs with guidance form ACRA PMC’s will identify and register the beneficiaries according to ACRA’s vulnerability criteria for targeting food-insecure women/child/chronically ill-headed HHs, People with disabilities, widows, elderly, pregnant/lactating women, orphaned, Survivors of GBV. 1.4. Distribution of complementary cropping and livelihoods inputs: ACRA will Protect and stabilize livelihoods for 8,370 vulnerable HHs (female/male 51/49%) (46,800 individuals) through provision of Livelihoods inputs (seeds and tools plus fishing gears and distribute Crop kits (Maize, Sorghum and Cowpea), vegetables (Amaranth, Collard, Okra, Tomato, Onion, Watermelon, Pumpkin and Maloda) and Fishing kits (hooks, monofilament and spools of twine). This will also improve food availability and income from sale of surplus vegetables 1.5. Formation and training of farmers and fisher folk groups: ACRA will facilitate the formation of farmer and fisher folk groups. ToT training on improved agronomic practices, fish preservation, marketing and group dynamics will be provided. The trained farmers and fisher folk will then sensitize and build the capacity of the other farmers and fisher folk in their communities. Training on crop disease control including training of farmers and surveillance on Fall army worm (FAW) and Desert Locusts control through Community based surveillance and Early Warning. Training farmers on grain/seed harvesting, storage and support through traditional granaries and hermitic bags. Training on post-harvest management and market linkages. Fisher folk training in good fishing techniques, preservation, processing and marketing and on the use of fishing kits received. 1.6. Support provision of veterinary services through community based initiatives to 20,000 livestock. 1.7. Provision of livelihoods skills training: Female-headed HHs and fisher folk supported through training on IGAs. IGA’s will be linked to the other two activities of fishing and vegetable production on market linkages. Some markets in Jonglei, Upper Nile and EES are functional and ACRA will target this market-based activity in these areas 1.8. Facilitate formation of VSLA groups and supporting with start-up kits and supporting small scale farmers on group formation, value addition to their produce and linking them to markets and agro-input dealers and other value chain actors 1.9. Monitoring and reporting: ACRA will also conduct post distribution monitoring after every distribution to ascertain the appropriateness of the response.
