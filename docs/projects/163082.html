Project Summary

Communities across Zimbabwe are affected by severe water and food insecurity and loss of livestock and livelihoods due to abnormal drought conditions. In addition to lack of water resources, the open defecation rate is still very high (around 50% according to CRS ground surveys in project areas) in vulnerable and remote parts of the country, increasing chances of water-borne diseases in these communities. Rural WASH Information Management System (RWIMS) of July 2019 put 29% of all water points across the country only provide water seasonally, only 13% have good yields year-round. This shortage especially affects women and children as they typically are the ones to have to travel further to find water. According to the June 2019 ZIMVAC Assessment report, showed that 16% of households were over 1km from the nearest main water source and 32% over 500m away which does not fit into Sphere Standards.
CRS has extensive experience and strong reputation for constructing new and rehabilitating boreholes across Zimbabwe, in addition to responding to cholera outbreaks and providing hygiene promotion during emergencies. In response to the multi-hazard crisis currently facing Zimbabwe, CRS proposes providing access to water through borehole rehabilitation and new construction in the most affected and remote of these areas where government and local authorities are unable to assist due to the financial situation of the country. This project also will deliver hygiene promotion using the Wash’em approach for key positive behavior change in emergencies and critical inputs in these communities to target open defecation and handwashing with soap. CRS has already established necessary relationships with key stakeholders at national, provincial, district, and ward level as they already are working in the proposed areas in WASH, shelter, and livelihoods. The project will target the most affected areas of drought, water insecurity, and open defecation. Of these affected population, the vulnerable groups targeted by the project would be female or child-headed households, households with multiple children under five, elderly, chronically ill, and physically or mentally challenged persons. Participants, especially female participants, and local leadership would be consulted in project design and implementation. CRS would work closely with other partners in the area in order to establish positive collaboration in targeting, project management, and implementation.
