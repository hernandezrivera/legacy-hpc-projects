Project Summary

After 12 years of hostilities and persistent economic crisis, people in
Syria saw their situation worsen when a series of earthquakes hit
northern Syria and Türkiye on 6 and 20 February 2023 uprooted hundreds of thousands of families, resulting in almost 5,900 deaths and more than 12,800 people injured in Syria, and severely damaged infrastructures already weakened by the conflict.
Many families lost their main breadwinner due to death or injury, at a time when the economic situation was already dire, increasing the
vulnerability of millions of people previously unable to meet their basic
needs.HNO-2024
Lack of jobs, services, and assistance driving needs: For 29% of households in NES and 49% of
households in NWS, loss of job or unemployment was reported as a barrier to meeting basic needs.
In both NES and NWS, high costs of services (including health, education, and electricity), loss of
humanitarian assistance, unavailability of key goods were also significant barriers in both areas.HNO-2024.
the proposed action comprises main activities on the underserved areas in Idlib & Armanaz SDs
1. Support camps with free bread due to the bad food conditions.
2. Support small farmers olive with olive harvesting service.
3. Support IDPs with daily work through CFW scheme because of lack of income.
4. Enhance the income-generating activities for women to empowerment their livelihoods in the camps.
5. Supporting the wheat value chain through marketing wheat from local farmers.
6. Forming an agro-food processing workshops (groups) for female-headed households in the camps, and equipped them with the necessary trainings, tools, equipment, and raw materials.
7. 7. Rehabilitation and repair of two schools in the villages of Armanaz and Al-Ghafar to reduce the phenomenon of school dropouts.
Over 3350 HHs are to be reached throughout the locations targeted by this project—at least 5% PwDs. Participation will be open to residents and IDPs.
Under ERL, project targets (55 M, 150 W, 355 B, 872 G) to reinforce and enhance better access to income-generating opportunities and to have community resilience strengthened. 150 female-headed HHs (included external referrals) in the targeted camps will provide with labor market-informed skills training, then delivering cash grants for 50 successful applicants, On the other hand, rehabilitation of 2 public schools through 45 CFW injection scheme.
Under FSL, project will support ( 3487 M, 4334 W, 4175 B, 4077 G) to increase access to bread and improve economic conditions and labor market opportunities. WSA will establish 50 CFW workgroup from camps. Each group will perform CFW activities in olive picking, this will contribute to the injections of money into the local market. Moreover, 10 workers in CFW activities will be at the camps to distribute bread. In addition, WSA will offer small grants to support food processing with a focus on female-headed households, 50 female trainees from the residents of camps will target and offer 50 joint grants within the framework of home food processing and market their products within the camps themselves.
