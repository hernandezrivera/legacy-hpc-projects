Project Summary

Through this project, YOPCODI seeks to improve the resilience of Children affected by emergencies through providing continuous access to accelerated basic education Program. The project intends to provide opportunities for children affected by conflict by providing safe and quality basic education, Mental Health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) and as well portable water, sanitation hygiene services available simultaneously to enhance their physical and psychological well-being and improve the chances of their recovery from shocks and build their hope for prosperity. The project will provide both access to quality and provide gender sensitive WASH facilities MHPSS for children affected by emergencies.
The project aims to complement and upscale ongoing Education responses and interventions in Bade, Gujba, Geidam, Jakusko, Yusufari and Yunusari LGAs in Yobe state. This is building on the findings of the 2021 Joint Education Needs Assessment (JENA) as well as project evaluation conducted by YOPCODI at the end of education in Emergencies interventions in these locations, which revealed a significant number of the people in need, especially out of school children and Children with protection risks require not only access to basic education but integrated Mental Health and Psychosocial Support, and WASH services to reduce excessive threats to their life, health and well-being, especially for girls
YOPCODI will collaborate with communities, relevant government agencies at state and LGA levels across Education sector to implement this proposed project in locations within Yobe State where major collective gaps exist in access to safe and quality education, MHPSS as well as water sanitation and hygiene promotion service and are underfunded. The specific locations proposed for this project are Bade, Gujba, Geidam and Yunusari LGAs identified as areas with high severity of needs among Girls and Boys affected by emergencies in these three -pronged areas. The intendment of this project is to catalyze humanitarian protection impact that will enable boys and girls affected by crisis as well as their teachers, parents and caregivers recover sustainably to pursue their aspirations.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian context in northeast Nigeria remains complex. Continued insecurity, in conjunction with government mandated camp closures, has created further displacement challenges. Many people are unable to return sustainably to their places of origin or have difficulties becoming self-reliant and require assistance to meet their most critical need sand enjoy their fundamental rights. Consequently, about 70% of the affected persons are women and children who whose hope for accessing or continuing their educational pursuits were dashed by life-threatening circumstances such as displacement, food insecurity, high cost of living, and psychosocial instabilities necessitated by shocks coupled with impacts of climate change that resulted overflooding during the rainy season. The outbreak of cholera has also affected many schools aged children's due poor sanitary conditions in communities and schools. Similarly, at mid-year (2022) only 25 percent of the Nigeria Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) was funded, compared to the global average of 33 percent. Funding forecasts for the rest of the year remain uncertain and many NHF and CERF projects are coming to a close in the next few months.As a result, a lot of Children have been out of school for a long time and have lost some productive period. Despite the effort of Government and humanitarian organizations in creating opportunities for learning for children, conflict affected boys and girl in hard-to reach communities still not reached to gaps in funding. The situation has impacted Girls impacted more severely due to historical and deeply rooted socio-cultural norms and the absence of facilities in schools that promotes and uphold their dignity while in school, such as gender segregated latrines and hygiene kits which reduces the rate of girl child enrolment and retention at schools. Many adolescent girls have been victims of exploitation, sexual harassment, teenage pregnancy and early marriage. Despites the Child Protection interventions of actors and stakeholder's majority of these children remain unreached by child protection actors, and there is currently a dire need to step up community-based care for children at protection risks spread across communities in the target locations of Gujba, Damaturu and Geidam LGAs. Even families that are initially taking care of neglected are no longer able to do so without adequate and regular support due to worsening poverty. Children who are unaccompanied and or separated from their families are at risk of abuse, neglect, violence, exploitation, discrimination, and marginalization. In general, these children have limited access to food, water, healthcare services, adequate shelter and supervision, clothing, psychosocial support and education. Without appropriate care or the financial need to meet their basic necessities, conflict affected children a face immense obstacle to their survival and development.Drawing from the Joint Education Needs Assessments as well as the emergency response strategy of the WASH sector and the Child Protection AoR, reveal that poverty has been a major hindrance to access to care and services. The JENA also reveals that dilapidated classrooms and high teacher to pupil’s ratio, inadequate or complete absence of WASH facilities has hindered access to basic education by boys and girls affected by crisis and conflict. This is in addition to, lack of sufficient learning materials and inadequacy of teachers with PSS skills which has adversely affected the quality of learning at school/ learning centers.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

This project will enhance mainstreaming protection of children through gender sensitive programming and also take in to account training of parents, caregivers and teachers as well as the SMBC and community members on MHPSS inculcating in them positive discipline, positive parenting and guidance and counselling. Life skills training will include topics such as mine action, identification of unexploded ordinance and what steps to follow. Using the safe school initiatives, the project proposes engaging with the School-Based Management Committees, for provision of security to the learners while in school. The project will further establish an identity protected feedback mechanism through establishment of feedback mechanisms to ensure that beneficiaries are able to freely provide feedback without exposing them to further risk. YOPCODI will ensure strict compliance with its internal child safe guarding policy on which staff are consistently trained and committed to upholding its provisions to ensure that project staff and contractors do not cause any harm to the beneficiaries. In addition, the project will ensure that all facilities constructed or provided are gender friendly. YOPCODI also proposes to train teachers and SBMCs on, MHPSS disaster risk reduction and management in order to ensure that children are safe at schools and in the event of any attack organize an evacuation of the school children.The distribution of Affected persons are distributed equitably targeting men women Boys and Girls are targeted based on the sector PIN. YOPCODI will also engage with conflict affected children and their caregivers through Health/Hygiene Talks to create awareness and prevention of Diseases outbreak, exploitation and abuse.
