Project Summary

Hope Revival Organization (HRO) aims to continue providing GP, MA services for IDPs and Host community in our already existed community centres in Idleb (Jisr Ash Shugur, Darkosh) and in Aleppo (Afrin) and through outreach visits to the widows’ camps (1 in Janudiyeh and 2 in Darkosh) to target the female widows and children with different GP, MA activities. HRO will also provide integrated protection services through community centers and mobile teams, to ensure access to all age groups, including vulnerable groups such as widows, divorced women, elderly, and people with disabilities, including the following topics: Risk education awareness sessions, protection monitoring, legal services, awareness-raising sessions (integrated services) other than legal awareness, initiatives and community-based protection structures, recreational activities, training the humanitarian workers and local authorities on protection principles.
HRO will build the capacity of employees on (GP, MA, Inclusion of PWD), and will provide services to IDPs, HC and the most vulnerable groups who are at risk of protection. Awareness-raising activities for community and caregivers on GP will include:
- Community-based protection services to ensure IDPs and host community have a greater role in day to day running of centers, fostering social cohesion and a strong sense of ownership over the activities.
- Legal services (HLP and CSD) to increase legal awareness among affected population on how to access civil and HLP documentation including provision of individual legal assistance in line with the Guiding Framework on Advisory Protection Services on CSD and HLP.
- Protection monitoring service through conducting service mapping and establishing referral pathways and reporting on protection concerns and issues, in addition to monitoring the application of protection principles during mass IDPs influxes and IDPs relocation.
- Risk education awareness sessions to prevent and reduce existing risk of war remnants.
- PM awareness sessions: PM Team members will conduct awareness sessions to beneficiaries on general protection subjects, dealing with the psychological stress, and non-specialized first-line responders on core protection principles and protection mainstreaming.
M&E team will be responsible for ensuring confidentiality of beneficiaries’ data, through ensuring files are stored in a safe place within the facilities, communication about cases is being made through coded names, and monthly reports include key findings attached with MoVs and monitoring findings will be shared with Donor and with cluster as requested.
To ensure that IDPs and host community are effectively involved in the project lifecycle from design to implementation to feedback through the already established protected feedback channels to help them report suggestions, complaints, and PSEA concerns. 22,750 beneficiaries including (15,925 IDPs and 6,825 HC) will be targeted with the project’s activities including 13,650 Females and 9,100 Males (6,825 Young Children, 5,232Children, 9,100 Adults, 1,593 Elderly) among them are 910 PwD, through 3 community centers including 12 protection staff (6 outreach teams), in addition, M&E teams will ensure the quality of the activities implemented and the application of the accountability mechanisms, considering the gender equality. Also, HRO will ensure smooth coordination with the protection NGO partners local councils, stakeholders, and camp mangers throughout the project period, in addition to the provision of MHPSS services through our ongoing projects to ensure the integration between our protection and MHPSS services considering the WHO standard COVID-19 preventive measures throughout the implementation period.

4 - Needs

As of January 2021, around 13.4 million people are estimated to require some form of humanitarian and protection assistance. Inadequate shelter conditions as well as the immediate and longer-term effects of the COVID-19 pandemic as indicated in HNO (March 2021), where 25% of them have disabilities and 75% of them are women and children, where children with and without disabilities comprise 44% of the people in need across Syria. As of February 2021, approximately 1.7 million IDPs were living in 1,385 camps or informal sites (including 24,800 people displaced in January and February 2021 alone), Women and children represent 80% of this caseload.According to HRO Protection NA conducted during Feb. and April 2021 in Jisr Ash Shugur, Kafr Jales, Darkosh, Gandoura, and Afrin, it was reported by 40% in Jisr Ash Shugur and Kafr Jales that they lack housing, land, and properties documents (HLP), while it is 65% in Darkosh and 67% in Afrin. Also, it was reported in 2021 Mine Action Needs that 10.3 million people live in areas affected by explosive ordnance.As for MSNA Key Informant Protection Needs Assessment level for HNO 2021, as in Aleppo 45.1% of the KIS reported Lack/Loss of civil documentation, and 81.0% reported that Explosive Hazards are identified in community since beginning of crisis, while 73.4% in Idleb reported Lack/Loss of civil documentation.

5 - Implementation of activities

After ensuring the preparedness of the project logistics and recruiting procedures, staff will be trained on GP, MA basics, inclusion of PwDs. Afterwards, teams will proceed with conducting the following activities:Legal awareness raising: The sessions will focus on sensitization of CSD & HLP rights, the importance of documentation, steps, procedures of obtaining & registering the legal occurrences, in addition to the risks associated with obtaining and holding these documents. In addition to legal counselling.Risk Education awareness sessions: HRO Outreach team will raise the beneficiaries’ awareness on risk education using MA Sub-working group IEC materials.Communities reached with protection monitoring: HRO teams will conduct protection monitoring assessments to identify protection needs/risks in the target communities. Key informant interviews (community level assessments) will be conducted to help the PM team identify cases in need of referrals.Referrals made for protection & non-protection: Referral pathway is in place and fully functional inside and outside the community centers. cases identified and referred to protection and non-protection services including HRO MHPSS services. Referrals will be done according to the most updated service mapping.Complaints’ mechanisms are in place to ensure quality, transparency, and access of vulnerable groups to all available services. Key messages and IEC materials on COVID-19 will be disseminated at the end of each session.
