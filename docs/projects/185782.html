Project Summary

AMAN (Association for Mass Advancement Network) has developed the project with objectives to Obj_01: Ensure access to quality, gender-responsive and inclusive learning opportunities in a safe and protective environment for Rohingya refugees and host community children, adolescents and youth (aged 3 to 24 years) (Linked with SO1, SO2, SO4 & S05); Obj_02: Provide equitable, inclusive and quality education opportunities, including through the rollout of the Myanmar Curriculum Pilot and scale up, and alternative learning pathways for the Rohingya refugee and host community children, adolescents and youth (aged 3 to 24 years) (Linked with SO1, SO2 & SO4) and Obj_03: Support the community engagement and capacity strengthening of Education Sector partners and affected communities to enhance their participation in the education of children, adolescents and youth. (Linked with SO1, SO2, SO4 & S05).
The project proposed location is 8E & 15. Total 4 Learning Centers will be operated where 240 learners will have enrolled. Total budget for the project is US$ 91,547 for one year of the project period. The proposed project will be implementing following education sectors policy and guidance.
Major activities include establishment of Education Learning Centre at Rohingya Camps 8E & 15 including WASH facilities, construction and furniture; Providing education among 3-24 Rohingya children, adolescents and youth for their grade level competencies for MCP and other alternative learning. (disaggregated by gender and age); Providing training among teachers and facilitators on thematic education principles and pedagogy in line with the Teacher Professional Development Framework, including the Myanmar Curriculum; Capacity building for Community stakeholders (SMC, LCMC, and other education committees) actively engaged in capacity building and mobilization activities in education response supporting inclusive education initiatives and promoting return to school/learning centers (post COVID-19); Committee meeting quarterly for 4 LCs; Supported learning facilities and schools that have a functional DRR plan.
AMAN will make coordination and collaboration with CiCs, CMO, RRRC, education department and others stakeholders for effective implementation of the project. Majhi and camps leaders’ involvement and help from SMC groups will strengthen the program also.
AMAN has skilled manpower to run the assigned project. AMAN has necessary policy and guidelines specially DRR plan that will be followed for the implementation of the project.

Key findings in Education, ref. to Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (J-MSNA), Preliminary findings, 20 September 2021, that , Enrolled in learning facilities before learning facilities closed in March 2020 (pre-COVID), having regularly accessed home-based learning since the start of the 2021 school year until support for home-based learning stopped at the end of March 2021, and that reportedly sent back once learning facilities will re-open which result are for Girls, 3-5: Enrolment 40%, home based learning 44%, sending back 72%; boys 3-5: Enrolment 41%, home based learning 42%, sending back 69%; girls 6-14: Enrolment 81%, home based learning 73%, sending back 78%; Boys, 6-14, Enrolment 88%, home based learning 82%, sending back 89%; girls 15-18; Enrolment 18%, home based learning 15%, sending back 12%; boys 15-18; Enrolment 46%, home based learning 43%, sending back 45%; girls 19-24; Enrolment 3%, home based learning 2%, sending back 2%; Boys 19-24; Enrolment11%, home based learning 11%, sending back 11%. 47% of households with school-aged (6-18) children (girls/boys) reported at least one school-aged child as not having been enrolled in learning facilities pre-COVID. Households with girls/boys aged 3-24 reporting challenges that girls/boys aged 3-24 faced towards benefitting from or reasons they could not do any home-based learning; Top 5 reported challenges are Not enrolled pre-COVID 17%, Not enrolled pre-COVID 14%; Marriage and/or pregnancy 16%, Home-based learning is not effective 8%;Home-based learning is not effective 7%, Marriage 8%,Lack of guidance from learning facilitators 6%,Lack of guidance from learning facilitators %;Children too old to participate 6%, Children too old to participate 5%.Camps unmet needs & coping; of households per education LSG severity score None/minimal 18%, Stress 30, Severe 43%, Extreme 4% and Not classified 5%. 12% of households without unmet needs (LSG severity score < 3) reported having used negative coping strategies to meet their needs Across most of the older age and gender groups, roughly the same proportions of individuals reportedly having been enrolled in learning facilities pre-COVID, and having regularly accessed home-based learning, will reportedly be sent back to learning facilities once they will re-open. However, among girls aged 15-18, lower proportions were reported as having accessed home-based learning than were previously enrolled, and even lower proportions will reportedly be sent back, indicating that girls aged 15-18 may have been most likely to have dropped out of their education as a result of learning facility closures. Likely related, for girls, marriage and/or pregnancy was the second most commonly reported barrier towards home-based learning, while it was the most commonly reported reason for not sending them back. Unmet education needs may not only be driven by barriers related to access and availability, but also sometimes by household perceptions on who should be in education. Needs may also differ between different types of households. Home-based learning not being effective was among the most commonly reported challenges related to home-based learning, while a lack of qualified teaching staff, as well as children having fallen behind as a result of learning facility closures, were among the most commonly reported expected challenges when sending children back to learning facilities. As such, the most commonly reported support needs to facilitate home-based learning were cash to pay for private tutors, as well as school supplies, and more guidance from learning facilitators. However, risk of infection with COVID-19 also remains a concern, being was the most commonly reported expected challenge when sending children back to learning facilities.
