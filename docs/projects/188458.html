Project Summary

COVID-19 pandemic is a global public health emergency and specially to Syria, which is facing this emergency in addition to the complex emergency with a significant impact on health and economic development. In 2020; Syria declared its first COVID-19 case on 22 March 2020. The transmission of COVID-19 is classified as community transmission. As of 15 January 2022, the number of confirmed COVID-19 cases reported by MoH was 50,674, only 145,491 NAATs (PCR tests) were performed at public health laboratories, the average weekly testing rate was 1,580 for total population, far below WHO recommendation for the testing to be at the level of 1 per 1000 people in the context of comprehensive surveillance (21,000 tests per week) . Moreover, the positivity rate across the country was 34%, higher in some governorates, eg; Swieda, Deir Ez-zor, and Homs above 70%. Considering the limited number of tests being performed the actual number of cases and deaths likely far exceed official figures. Official statistics show an increase in cases, but the high positivity rate and death reports suggest that the true scale of the epidemic is largely underestimated.
In NWS the current health system is fragmented and has very limited capacity in terms of COVID-19 surveillance, laboratory testing, contact tracing, human resources for health, case management and measures at points of entry (PoEs). In addition, implementing partners have insufficient capacity to detect, test, isolate, quarantine and treat COVID-19 confirmed and suspected cases. Access to primary health care and hospital care is very limited. Intensive care unit capacity is inadequate. Infection prevention and control (IPC) capacity is very low, due to lack of adequate material and supplies. Access to medicines and medical supplies is also very limited. The COVID-19 Task Force has been formed in early March 2020 to carry out activities under identified pillars in collaboration with implementing partners. The COVID-19 Preparedness and Response Plan for Northwest Syria (CPRP2020) has been published in 2020
The overall aim of the project is to reduce morbidity and mortality resulting from COVID-19 among the population of Syria by scaling up the country’s preparedness and response operations. It also aims to stop transmission of COVID-19 in Syria and protect all persons within Syria from the disease, and to strengthen health systems to attain resilience and respond to emerging and re-emerging public health threats. The response strategy aligns with that of the Health sector strategy as well as the WHO global strategy. It thus adopted the 11 pillars approach. The main objectives are as follows:
• Strengthen existing national multisectoral coordination mechanism to deliver timely and integrated response and to contain the pandemic, by actively engaging Directorates of Health, line-ministries, UN agencies, INGOs and NNGOs.
• To ensure people’s understanding of the situation and their compliance to social and public health measures
• Strengthen surveillance for early detection and response of COVID-19 infections to reduce person-to-person transmission.
• Early detection and timely isolation of suspected COVID-19 cases among travellers at PoE
• Strengthen central and directorate-level public health laboratories for virologic testing of COVID-19 samples
• Strengthen the public health laboratory network central and directorate-level public health laboratories for virologic testing of COVID-19 samples and sequencing of SARS-CoV-2
• Strengthen and sustain infection prevention and control (IPC) measures to ensure safety of patients, healthcare workers and the caregiving environment.
• Establish and maintain designated isolation facilities across the country.
• Maintain stockpile of supplies required for the response,
• The continuity of essential health services as to avoid substantial disruptions is secured.
• Update and maintain vaccination strategy and microplans.

4 - Needs

The social and economic impact of the crisis and the COVID pandemic is large and growing. A lack of sustained access to health care has exacerbated the effects of the conflict and pushed millions of people into unemployment and poverty. With a severely degraded healthcare system, Syrians remain extremely vulnerable to additional shocks, such as the COVID19 outbreak still unfolding. In addition, the introduction of sanctions has further restrained Syria’s external economic ties, leading to difficulties in procurement of lab supplies and medicines, fuel shortages for the operation of RRTs, and a rapid depreciation in local currency.MoH struggles to keep up with the spread of the epidemic and to significantly scale up the surveillance and testing capacity. Protection of health care workers and frontlines is still crucial. In NW Syria there is a high risk of transmission. In a scenario in which sustained community transmission has been detected, surveillance system will need to be expanded to include monitoring the geographical spread of the virus, transmission intensity, disease trends, characterization of virologic features, and the assessment of impacts on healthcare services. COVID-19 community transmission will continue in 2022, driven, in part, by low vaccination coverage, low adherence to public health and social measures, and testing capacity which remains inadequate. The COVID-19 pandemic has been accompanied by widely circulating misinformation and rumors.

5 - Implementation of activities

While strengthening the response activities carried out in the acute phase of the pandemic, it is important to meet longer-term needs. In parallel with this, more permanent, medium- and long-term solutions should be produced on top of the successes in initial capacity building process in Syria in humanitarian crisis. In Country preparedness and response plan for 2022, main activities that will support COVID-19 preparedness and response under the following domains: •Leadership, coordination, planning, and monitoring through facilitating COVID 19 taskforce committees at different levels. •Risk communication and community engagement•Surveillance: support operation cost of RRT for sample collect and field visits, support MoH/NGOs and community to establish points of care/tests through the provision of RDTs, case investigation and contact tracing•Travel, trade, points of entry (PoE), and referral system•Diagnostics and testing: Procurement of COVID19 lab supplies, testing kits, PCR machines, GeneX machine, capacity building on sequencing, and rehabilitation of 3 labs in rural areas. •Infection prevention and control•Case management and therapeutics: provision of life-saving medicines, enhance oxygen supplies, ICU equipment. •Operational support and logistics•Essential health systems and services•Research, Innovation, and evidence

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Hostilities in Syria continue to take a heavy toll on the lives and livelihoods of civilians who have already endured a decade of violence, multiple displacements, and a major economic downturn. The COVID-19 pandemic has only worsened the situation, adding new layers of complexity and hardship. Therefore, the availability, access and sustainability of essential health services and medical supplies was and continues to be always in risk due to the security situation, internal displacement and donors’ support.According to the HNO 2020, Attacks on health care have left 14.5 million people in 42 districts below emergency standards for hospital beds. Additionally, up to 70% of health workers has left Syria and many others have been injured or killed. Lack of health personnel is particularly acute in areas affected by recent hostilities and displacement, with 64% (13.2 M) of the population living below minimum standards for healthcare workers. Overcrowding and long waiting times are top access barriers reported by patients.Regarding the health system risks the recent assessments indicate the need for referral capacity, including ambulances to overcome gaps in secondary care and connect patients to life-saving care, with this regard WHO regularly conducts Prioritization Exercise in collaboration with health cluster partners aiming to identify needs, target population, accessibility, availability of services, monthly case load and overlapping with other facilities.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Cross-border operations between the Gaziantep Hub and northwest Syria depend heavily on the relevant UN Security Council Resolutions (UNSCR). The current UNSCR 2504 authorizes cross-border access to northwest Syria through Turkey only until July 2022. Non-renewal of this resolution may therefore affect cross-border delivery of medical supplies and equipment. WHO is in continuous discussions across all three levels of the organization to ensure its mandate as a guardian of public health in the area is maintained, along with its ability to sustain partner coordination and technical support, support in procurement and preposition supplies, administer ongoing and future contracts with implementing partners and manage funding mechanisms.WHO’s functions in the areas of health coordination, HIS, technical support and capacity building are very important and don’t involve cross-border movement and not impacted by UNSC Resolution. But what is more important since WHO has been instrumental in providing support for cross-border operations, including the delivery of over 30% of all essential medicines, supplies, and equipment for over 4 million civilians (2.7 million of which are internally displaced) dependent on humanitarian assistance supplied almost entirely through cross-border operations from southern Turkey.
