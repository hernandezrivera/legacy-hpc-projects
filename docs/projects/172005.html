Project Summary

This project aims to support health and social institutions, as well ask key community sites in the field of WASH in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts through most needed rehabilitations of WASH-related infrastructure. This will allow targeted institutions to conduct their lifesaving, life-preserving and regular work more safely and effectively.
After six years of simmering conflict in Easter Ukraine, WASH infrastructure in areas close to the contact line is severely impacted by the shelling, as well as from the protracted lack of maintenance resulting from unavailability of public resources, reprioritization of expenditure and difficulties in accessing affected areas. As a result, many settlements on both government and non-government-controlled areas have remained cut out from water supply or must deal with the consequences of frequently disrupted supply. This has a great impact on the communities’ capacity to access running water and means to maintain proper hygiene behaviour. The most affected are vulnerable community members who reside or seek services in medical and social institutions, with precarious structures, inadequate water and sanitation facilities, as well as affected by chronic underfunding to address maintenance needs and replace obsolete or non-functioning WASH equipment.
In response to the critical need for improved access to water and sanitation facilities in social and medical institutions of Donetsk and Lugansk oblasts (both GCA and NGCA), IOM proposes to capitalize on its extensive experience to continue conducting WASH-related structural rehabilitations in 80 facilities serving the most vulnerable community members affected by protracted armed conflict and including COVID-19 cases. Targeted facilities are most likely to include, amongst others, hospitals, outpatient clinics, IDP centres and institutions for the elderly and people with disabilities. The envisaged medium-size interventions will consist of, but are not limited to, the replacement and/or repair of water supply elements, sewerage and wastewater management systems, rehabilitation of sanitary structures with installation of appropriate plumbing equipment, as well as repair or substitution of water containers and boilers.
Given the frequent damages to the water supply systems at the settlement level, which cause disruption of water supply for individual households and social and medical institutions, IOM also intends to conduct WASH-related structural interventions of relatively high complexity in 19 community sites. These activities, which will focus exclusively in the NGCA of Luhansk and Donetsk oblasts, might include cleaning, repair, drilling of wells for water supply, installation of water towers, replacement and/or repairing of water supply, sewerage and heating systems at community level, rehabilitation of water reservoirs, installation of septic tank disposals, replacement and start-up of pumps, pumping stations and frequency converters to ensure efficient power consumption by water supplying equipment. IOM has a proven track record in rehabilitations in NGCA and GCA and is scaling up GCA WASH rehabilitations in response to the increased need for functioning WASH facilities in social/medical facilities in light of COVID-19.

1 - Needs

Due to the conflict-related damages and severe financial cutbacks the access to essential services such as water supply, adequate heating, waste water disposal, sewage in key health and social institutions (SIs) is very limited. In addition, the structural damage to critical heating infrastructure of areas and communities outside of urban centers or close to the contact line also mostly results from the armed clashes or from the lack of proper maintenance and funding for rehabilitation since the beginning of the conflict. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (2020), the availability and safety of water and electric infrastructure remains a concern in the entire Eastern Conflict Area. Based on WASH Cluster assessments, 88 security incidents have affected civilian water facilities in 2019. Damage to one water facility on either side of the LoC could mean loss of access to water for the populations on both sides of the LoC. First Lift pumping station of the South Donbas Water Way, which is located on the LoC and provides water to 1.1 million people on both sides, have been affected more than 40 times in 2019 as stated in the Humanitarian Needs Overview (2020). The high density of landmines in the area, the ongoing and active conflict prevents maintenance workers from accessing damaged infrastructure to conduct needed repairs safely. The deterioration in the work of the centralized water system since the beginning of the conflict was mentioned by 37% respondents in the REACH Multi-Sector Needs Assessment. The WASH cluster reported that 51% of assessed households have access to only one source of water, which leaves them particularly vulnerable to shocks or disruptions (such as conflict related damage) to their water supply. In light of the COVID-19, access to only one source of water and the frequent water stoppages reduce the ability of social/medical institutions and the broader communities ability to maintain adequate hygiene/sanitary standards to mitigate against the spread of COVID, also highlighted by 29% of institutions surveyed by REACH. This places vulnerable groups such as the elderly, people with disabilities and chronic health conditions in communal care centres more at risk as inability to clean communal facilities as frequently and thoroughly as recommended by WHO and Ukrainian government standards increases the risk of surfaces acting as vectors. Moreover, water is in fact critical for staying warm as the WASH cluster assessment states in so far that 38% of individuals reliance on the central heating. Individuals residing in the institutions/homes where temperature below 18C are more vulnerable to diseases such as flu and tuberculosis and water based heating systems are essential in the reduction of morbidity and mortality. Further, lack of hot water has been sighted by the WASH cluster as a key humanitarian need and a contributing factor to the possible reduction in hand washing over winter, an essential behaviour to mitigate against the spread of COVID-19. As such , the lack of heated water and the cessation or temporary discontinuation of water supply, lack of water heating capacity at social facilities along with the chronic lack of resources to pay for water trucking, repairs and regular maintenance, remain the key concerns in NGCA and GCA of Donetsk and Lugansk Oblasts. Field monitoring visits conducted by IOM, complemented by regular communication and assessments together with local counterparts, as well as by frequent field reports prepared by long-time implementing partners, identified multiple additional sites needing WASH rehabilitation works, close to the contact line, in the areas of early hostilities and in very poor/marginalised locations that have not been frequently accessed. Based on pre-assessment of 17 SIs in Donetsk NGCA conducted in March-April 2020 by IOM engineers, it was identified, that 100% of SIs are in critical need of WASH-rehabilitations.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

Outcome 1: The most vulnerable social groups in Luhansk and Donetsk oblasts have improved access to water, hygiene and sanitation. Output 1.1: Key social and medical institutions in GCA and NGCA of Luhansk and Donetsk oblasts have improved WASH infrastructure Activities: 1.1 Identification of target institutions and assessment of rehabilitation needs1.2 Performing of rehabilitation works* and testing of improved systems1.3 Monitoring of adequate functioning and maintenance *Rehabilitation works at social and medical institutions might include rehabilitation of WASH facilities, heating systems, water storage and distribution systems. IOM aims to mainstream both IASC protection and disability standards across relevant rehabilitation works to ensure equitable access. This will aid accessibility of facilities for individuals with restricted mobility (broader doors and access ramps for wheelchairs or by allowing extra space for carers) and ensure that protection considerations for users are implemented in line with cultural and gender requirements. Further, IOM interventions will take into consideration SPHERE standards to ensure minimum requirements are met. Rehabilitation works are identified and discussed with local line ministry representatives and authorities and the final scope of work is determined through assessments by IOM engineers. Where direct implementation is possible, IOM engineers will lead in the implantation of the works and closely monitor IP implemented projects. Social institutions providing support to the elderly, and people with chronical and mental health illnesses will be prioritised as will medical facilities that are front line COVID 19 responders and medical facilities in underserved areas. Output 1.2: Key community WASH infrastructure in Luhansk and Donetsk oblasts NGCA is improved and functioning. 2.1 Identification of target key community sites and assessment of rehabilitation needs2.2 Performing of rehabilitation works** and testing of improved systems2.3 Monitoring of adequate functioning and maintenance Rehabilitation works might include cleaning, repair, drilling of wells for water supply, installation of water towers, replacement and/or repairs of water supply, sewerage and heating systems at community level, rehabilitation of water reservoirs, installation of septic tank disposals, replacement and launching of pumps, pumping stations, frequency converters to ensure efficient power consumption by water supplying equipment Key community WASH infrastructure close to the contact line, rural areas, sites heavily damaged as a result of the ongoing conflict, and underserved by other actors and with a large catchment area will be prioritised. During the selection process IOM take into consideration the share of the elderly people, large families with three and more children, people with disabilities, people with chronical diseases and other vulnerable groups residing in the community. ***IOM will compliment interventions with provision of WHO materials on key hygiene messages to be disseminated among the key social/medical facilities to support community awareness raising on good COVID practice. Dissemination of IEC materials in NGCA should be approved by de-facto authorities. To ensure accountability to the population, IOM operates a number of feedback mechanisms which allows beneficiaries to contact IOM by phone, email and through a global accountability platform. Information on the mechanisms are distributed to beneficiaries of IOM projects and a dedicated team are responsible for investigating claims and providing feedback to complainants. Post distribution monitoring by IOM M&E teams are further mechanisms through which beneficiaries can provide feedback. Feedback is incorporated through lessons learnt processes to ensure that programmes going forward are better able to meet the needs of the populations.
