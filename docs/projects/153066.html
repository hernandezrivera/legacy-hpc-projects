Project Summary

This project aims at providing 1.8 million IDPs and other emergency affected populations, with core emergency WASH supplies through planning, procurement, prepositioning, distribution, and management of the supplies. This project fits within the overall WASH Cluster objectives for 2019 i.e. WASH for IDPs/host communities and returnees, mitigate WASH related GBV, WASH in Nutrition for famine prevention, WASH in Health for outbreak control for the targeted affected population in all the states. Throughout the project, UNICEF will procure a total of 28 Emergency WASH supply items, preposition in strategic locations throughout South Sudan, support distribution, and strengthen management, monitoring and reporting. The supplies will be used by WASH partners currently operational in South Sudan in coordination with WASH Cluster at national and state level. UNICEF will work with local authorities and NGOs at various levels to streamline access to, and utilization of, these supplies. UNICEF in coordination with beneficiary partners will conduct end user monitoring of the supplies to ensure that supplies procured address the real needs of the vulnerable populations.

Needs

The continuing internal conflict in South Sudan has caused over 1.9 million internally displaced people and another 2 million refugees in neighbouring countries. Over 250,000 people remain in the POCs and as per the WASH Cluster HNO an estimated over 5.1 vulnerable people have been classified as those most in urgent need of WASH services. Even after the signing of Comprehensive Peace agreement on 31st October 2018, critics are not very optimistic of this deal. South Sudan also faces recurrent cholera outbreaks closely associated with the deteriorating WASH services among the IDPs and host communities as well as movement of pastoralist communities, the last outbreak ended in February 2018, a cumulative of 20,438 cases and 437 deaths (CFR 2.13%) were reported from 26 counties countrywide. Over 47% of the affected population are children. Diarrheal diseases are also closely linked to malnutrition. In addition to conflict and recurrent disease outbreaks, a range of other risks hamper access to WASH services in South Sudan. Lack of clean water is one of the factors that force women and girls to venture outside POCs, either to bath or to collect water for household use, which exposes them to the risk of becoming victim to sexual violence. Flooding and drought have been reported in various locations and have a major impact on the WASH sector capacity. In 2018, flooding in Jonglei ( Bor) and part s of Upper Nile, Western Bharghazal, Lakes and Unity triggered the displacement of over thousands of people. A country wide economic crisis is deepening in South Sudan. With an inflation rate of over 600 percent, most families cannot purchase their basic WASH supplies like safe water, soap and hygiene items. As a result, the population has even less access to safe water and sanitation services than before the conflict. The continued insecurity, disease outbreaks, nutrition crises and other natural emergencies combined with economic crises and poor infrastructure throughout the country highlight the need for UNICEF and humanitarian partners to continue providing emergency services to vulnerable communities. UNICEF is the Cluster lead for WASH and manages 60-70 per cent of the core pipeline supplies on behalf of the cluster partners for 2018, and will continue to manage the same percentage in 2019
