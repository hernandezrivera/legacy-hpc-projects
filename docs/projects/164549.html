Project Summary

Protection risks have increased with the ongoing displacement, lack of shelter options and sub-standard living conditions in areas of displacement, specifically related to gender-based violence (GBV), child protection (CP), and exposure to explosive hazards. Overcrowding in IDP sites and makeshift shelters and a lack of WASH facilities places women and girls at particular risk of GBV.
According to 2019 Voices report, various forms of GBV are experienced primarily by women and girls including restriction of freedoms, denial of services, domestic violence, early marriage. Men are generally perceived in Syria as the head of the family with great power over the freedoms and the movement of the female members of their families.
In this project SOP will target the sub-district AL BAB center, with a total population of167366 people with 54% of the population IDPs and the sub-district Qabasin with a total population of 62829 with 70% of the population being IDPs.
Therefore a new WGSS in this sub-district will be established.
GBV prevention activities and outreach activities will be conducted in two WGSS located in ALbab and Qabasin sub-district. The activities will include recreational activities such as development of peer-support-networks, development of communication skills, positive thinking, time management, self-confidence, self-esteem and future-planning. The life skills activities will entail literarcy course, computer skills, English language course, Psychological First Aid (PFA) and non-specialized individual as well as group counselling. For vocational education sewing courses will be offered. Recreational activities such as sport will be offered for boys where they will also receive messages on gender equality and GBV-sessions. Besides that, for adolescent girls a Young Mother’s Club and for adolescent boys a Young Father’s Club will be formed. Throughout all these activities GBV survivors will be identified.
The two static WGSS facilities will also include response to life threatening forms of GBV through the provision of specialized GBV services that will be accessible to IDPs and host communities. SOP has will establish and train teams that would be able to provide case management, psychosocial support, individual and group counselling and referral to health and livelihood services. The two mobile teams will be also able to reach out to communities and provide access to specialized services for boys and general PFA. The referral pathways that will be used during the response will follow the GBV SC. Besides that, GBV training for health service providers and education providers, and other front-line workers will be conducted in GBV basics, referral pathways and protection principles. Further, SOP will train their newly recruited staff in Al Bab and Qabasin sub-district during a period of 15 days on CMR, MISP, GBV SOPs, GBV risk mitigation, referral pathways, case management and child protection principles. In addition, during outreach mobile team’s will be distrubting dignity kits for the newly arrived IDPs as well as when entering the new communities.

4 - Needs

According to 2020 Voices report, various forms of GBV are experienced primarily by women and girls including restriction of freedoms, denial of services, domestic violence, early marriage, and GBV. Men are generally perceived as the head of the family in those areas and they have great power and influence over the freedoms and the movement of the female members of their families. Women and girls work in agriculture but they usually do not have control over their income and early marriage is common in those areas. SOP conducted a needs assessment in the proposed locations, which details cases of early marriage and adolescent widowed women who are in need of GBV services and for economic empowerment. According to the needs assessment, it has been confirmed the needs to provide life-saving specialized quality GBV services, including case management, psychosocial support and establish referral pathways. 6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Enhance strategies to empower women and girls and prevent GBVThe security situation remains stable and the center remains accessible.The acceptance of SOP and its center by the local community and the local authorities is sustained.SOP will leverage the presence of its staff in the proposed locations.The need for GBV program in the area persists. Risk 1: the confidentiality of the GBV is breached.mitigation 1: we will put in place strict disciplinary measures and train the staff on the code of conduct. Our staff has been working in this center for several years and they are aware of the sensitivity of GBV cases.Risk 2: beneficiaries will not attend the vocational training regularly.mitigation 2: beneficiaries will be consulted on the topics, the pace of the course and they will be able to give feedback. the reasons for their lack of commitment will be analyzed and addressed accordingly.Provide life-saving specialized quality GBV services, including case management, psychosocial support and establish referral pathways.Assumption: the security situation remains stable and the area is accessible.Risk 1: the local community refuses to participate in the assessments or share information.mitigation: they will be briefed about the aim of this activity and the expected outcomes, they will be assured of the confidentiality of the information collected.Assumption: the host community acknowledges the critical needs of IDPsRisk 2: the tension between IDPs and the local communities due to the emergency assistance that targets IDPs.Mitigation: awareness-raising sessions will include both IDPs and host communities to alleviate the tension and enhance cohesion.Assumption: staff is capacitated to conduct referrals.Risk: beneficiaries have high expectations of case management that cannot be fulfilled.Mitigation: The survivor-centered approach will be followed and staff will be transparent and realistic about the available assistance.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The risks associated:• Limited access to target locations because of insecurity and escalation of fighting or extreme weather conditions.• Changes in the security situation or authorities/groups in control of project areas. Changes could increase the risks faced, displace or restrict movements of the population, local partners and others relevant to the project.Planned risk response:• Ensure relevant authorities/groups are aware of, and technical authorities engaged with, the project and commit to ensuring basic safety/access for its implementation.• Ensure projects in insecure locations have security plans for their staff and the projects beneficiaries and enough resources to enact those plans. Commit partners to ensure a minimum duty of care to their staff including a right of refusal.• Promote the establishment/support of early warning systems, building reinforcement, safety/security training and equipment and other measures that can mitigate the impact of attacks.SOP will seek OCHA and other donors’ funds to continue the implementation of theand other donors’ funds to continue the implementation of the activities after the completion of this project, however, if no fund is secured, then we will hand over the center and the activities to the other implementers and in full coordination with OCHA and protection cluster.Ethical closure guidelines put in place by the GBV sub-cluster will be used to develop a long-term exit strategy and an emergency exit strategy. SOP will include in its mitigation plan efforts to:1- ensure protection of beneficiaries’ information.2- Identifying the location of staff and maintain contact with them in case of sudden displacement.3- Ensuring all GBV survivors receiving specialized services have a contact number from SOP so they can communicate with the team if they are displaced to a new location and the case will be followed up or referred to another actor with the consent of the survivor.
