Project Summary

The humanitarian crisis in the Northeastern Nigerian States of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) continue as hostilities between government security forces and non-state armed groups enter their tenth year. Civilians still bear the brunt of the conflict that has resulted in widespread displacement, destruction of infrastructure and collapse of basic services including nutrition. With an estimated total population of 13.04 million, in the three NE states – the states hardest-hit by the conflict - remain the epicentre of the humanitarian crisis. Conflict and the consequent displacement results in disrupted livelihoods, food insecurity and population overcrowding, which in turn increase the risk of malnutrition and mortality in affected populations.
The Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance (NFSS) conducted in the Northeast Nigeria in October 2019 showed that while the overall nutrition situation in the BAY states is gradually improving, the situation is still very volatile and highly responsive to seasonality. The survey findings showed that the prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe was 7.2 per cent, 8.1 per cent and 11.5 percent respectively. This is further compounded by the presence of sub-optimal child care and feeding practices which was found to be below 60% in the three states.
Based on analysis from the 2020 Enhanced Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) of the conflict affected population, it’s projected that 289,197 boys and girls below 5 years will suffer from Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM), out of this total UNICEF’s Nutrition Sector is targeting 255,619 SAM cases for treatment.
To overcome the challenge of malnutrition in the North-eastern states of Nigeria, UNICEF nutrition interventions will be focused on the below interventions;
 Procuring, managing and prepositioning nutrition supplies for effective management of SAM. A total of 306,743 cartons (including a 20% contingency) of RUTF will be procured to cover the sector target of 255,619 boys and girls aged 6-59 months and any unforeseen upsurge in needs. This will cover the entire 2020 sector needs as UNICEF is the sole pipeline agency for the procurement of RUTF.
 Timely detection, referral and treatment of children with severe acute malnutrition in the conflict-affected states of Borno (51,172), Adamawa (19,200) and Yobe (31,876). This is 40% of overall sector target for SAM treatment. The remaining 60% will be undertaken by other sector partners with UNICEF providing the required RUTF.
 Promoting uptake of recommended child feeding and care practices among 107,797 pregnant and lactating women in the BAY states (25,770 in Adamawa, 64,266 in Borno and 17,764 in Yobe.
 Strengthening the capacity of government, partners and the community to identify and treat malnutrition in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states
 Conducting nutrition programme assessments to identify barriers and boosters in access and developing suitable measures to ensure maximum programme coverage in all prioritized locations.
 Strengthening Nutrition Surveillance to determine the evolving nutrition situation that will further inform the response implementation strategy and location prioritization
 Coordinating and information management of the collective in the nutrition response in the three states.
UNICEF has planned to mobilize and generate $28.7 million funds to accomplish the planned activities for children and women in North-east Nigeria

1 - Needs

With an estimated total population of 13.04 million, conflict and the consequent displacement in North-east Nigeria results in disrupted livelihoods, food insecurity, and population overcrowding, which may in turn increase the risk of malnutrition and mortality in conflict affected populations. The BAY states currently register 2,018,513 IDPs with Borno state recording the highest number of IDPs at 1,483,566 (73.5%) with majority of this number (55%) living in camps and camp-like settings. The majority (79%) of the IDPs are women and children, and 26 per cent of the children are under five years. Food has been expressed as the most pressing need by 73 percent of IDPs . The Cadre Harmonisé report of October 2019 accesses that there is a degradation in food consumption and livelihood opportunities in BAY state, with about 2.7 million people in the states being in the critical phases (3 to 5) of food insecurity between October – December 2019. These numbers are projected to increase to 3.6 million in the period January – March 2020 if the ongoing humanitarian assistance is not sustained. Malnourished children, particularly those with severe acute malnutrition (SAM), are 11 times more likely to die than their healthy counterparts as SAM has been shown to increase the risk of death from common childhood illness such as diarrhoea, pneumonia, measles and malaria. It also increases the risk of stunted growth, impaired cognitive development and non-communicable diseases in adulthood. Poor nutrition in the first two years of a child’s life can lead to stunted growth, an irreversible condition that is associated with impaired cognitive ability and reduced school and work performance and productivity in later life. The nutrition surveillance conducted in October 2019 by National Bureau of Statistics in collaboration with UNICEF and Centre for Disease Control in the Northeast showed that while the overall nutrition situation in BAY states is gradually improving, the situation is very volatile and highly responsive to seasonality. The prevalence of global acute malnutrition (GAM) in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe was 7.2 per cent, 8.1 per cent and 11.5 percent respectively. Domain - level analysis of the NFSS revealed that no domain has a GAM rate of less than 5 per cent – the acceptable level of severity. Four of the surveyed ten domains had GAM rates greater than or equal to 10 percent. The SAM rates at domain level were estimated to be between 0.5 percent (East Borno and Northern Adamawa) to 2.3 per cent (Central Yobe). The under-five mortality rate also varied widely within the state; from as low as 0.10 deaths/10,000 under-fives/day in East Borno to as high as 1.02 deaths/10,000 under-fives/day in Northern Borno and Northern Yobe domains. The NFSS findings showed that exclusive breast-feeding rates were below 60 per cent in all 3 states while continued breastfeeding up to 2 years was 29.2% in Adamawa, 21.3% in Borno and 35.1% in Yobe state. Vitamin A supplementation coverage was below 31% in all 3 states while Micronutrient Powders (MNP) coverage was highest in Borno (6.9%) and below 6% in both Adamawa and Yobe states . Domain - level analysis of the NFSS report indicates that no domain has a GAM rate of less than 5 per cent – the acceptable level of severity. Four of the ten domains had GAM rates greater than or equal to 10 percent which implies that the nutrition situation is of serious concern and actions needs to be taken; the remaining six domains had GAM rates within the medium severity range (5 – 9.9%).While the GAM and mortality rates are below the emergency threshold of 15 percent and 2 deaths/10,000 under-fives/day respectively, the rates are still higher than the recommended acceptable threshold in some locations. It is also important to note that results of the assessment only represent accessible areas, GAM and mortality rates in inaccessible areas most likely may be worse

2 - Activities

 Procurement of Nutrition Supplies: UNICEF is the sole procurement agency for Ready to Use Therapeutic Food (RUTF) used by the government health facilities and those managed by NGO partners. The nutrition sector project has targeted a total of 187,863 SAM children to be treated in the North eastern states of Nigeria. To meet this target, UNICEF will support in the procurement of the required 187,863 Cartons of RUTF.UNICEF will also procure essential routine medication, anthropometric equipment and micro-nutrient supplements.  Provision of integrated preventative and curative nutrition services: To break the cycle of malnutrition, UNICEF will support the delivery of an integrated and comprehensive package of preventive and curative nutrition services. UNICEF will support timely detection of SAM among boys and girls age 6-59 months at the community level and refer them for treatment through a strengthened community mobilization component. To reduce the incidence of malnutrition and to sustain positive discharge outcomes, UNICEF will work closely with Government and other Civil Society Organizations (CSO) to promote optimal infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices through community-based support groups as well as through facility-based approaches. Additionally, UNICEF will provide micro-nutrient supplements to all children age 6-23 months except those who are receiving any fortified foods. To increase programme coverage, UNICEF will support implementation of the bi-annual Maternal, Neo-natal and Child Health (MNCH) week and will commemorate the world breastfeeding week in August 2020.  Humanitarian-Development Nexus (HDN): Even before the conflict malnutrition was widespread in the 3 states in North-east Nigeria. The insecurity related crisis through the disruption of agricultural activities, and the devastation of an already fragile public system has just exacerbated the nutrition situation and let to the current humanitarian respond. While the insecurity situation remains of concern in many parts of Borno state, there are places like south Borno and several LGAs in Yobe and Adamawa where the context is favourable to a progressive transition from humanitarian to development type of response which is more likely to address the structural cause of malnutrition. UNICEF in conjunction with the Nutrition Sector has developed the HDN strategy which outlines that UNICEF: (1) is the provider of last resort for the collective nutrition response in areas under the emergency phase; (2) provide complementary services as per existing gaps in nutrition services implemented by other CSOs aimed at ensuring provision of comprehensive and integrated nutrition services; and (3) undertakes system strengthening. UNICEF is currently undertaking the revitalization/ re-activation and operationalization of the State Committee on Food and Nutrition (SCFN) as a strategy for linking both humanitarian and developmental actors.  Nutrition Response Coordination: UNICEF, as the Nutrition Sector co-lead, will work closely with the state primary Health development agency (SPHCDA), INGO and NGO nutrition partners to conduct joint assessment, joint planning and support multisectoral response to the humanitarian needs of the affected population.  Nutrition Assessment: To identify barriers and develop effective programme coverage, UNICEF in partnership with State Primary Health Care and Development Agency will undertake a coverage investigation using the Semi-Quantitative Evaluation of Access and Coverage (SQUEAC)/Simplified Lot Quality Assurance Sampling Evaluation of Access and Coverage (SLEAC) methodology will be conducted in 2020 by UNICEF in collaboration with the government. This programme will further inform the on-going Out-Patient Therapeutic Care Programme rationalization exercise to prevent duplication/ overlap in service.  Nutrition Surveillance: To assess the evolving nutrition situation and inform programming
