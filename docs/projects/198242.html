Project Summary

This project targets 10,000 vulnerable households in need of shelter and domestic household assistance. CARE proposes to distribute comprehensive family kits to 6,200 households, conduct shelter training targeting 1,500 shelter volunteers and provide construction support to 1,500 households to construct emergency type B and C shelter structures (1000 type B and 500 type C). CARE will distribute survival kits to 3,800 conflict affected HHs in hard-to-reach areas of Quissanga and Macomia districts. Activities will be closely linked with PSEA trainings/awareness-raising sessions and accountability awareness training sessions to engage beneficiaries on available feedback mechanisms.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

CARE proposes to target 10,000 vulnerable IDPs HHs (50,000 people) who require basic domestic items and shelter assistance, using CAREs vulnerability criteria which targets:• Female-headed HHs• Child-headed HHs• HHs with the chronically ill, PWDs or elderly persons• HHs with more than 5 members• Newly arrival IDP households• IDPs who have been excluded from previous assistanceCARE proposes to distribute comprehensive family kits to a total of 6,200 HHs in Metuge and Ancuabe districts 60% of which will be prioritized for assistance targeting female-headed households. This includes 3,700HHs in Ancuabe and 2,500HHs in Metuge, reaching an estimated total of 31,000 people. Additionally, CARE will provide shelter training in each district and support with the construction of 1,500 type B and type C shelters for the most vulnerable families. This includes 1,000 Type B and 500 Type C shelters in each district, in line with shelter cluster guidelines, which will be supported by trained community construction volunteers. CARE will target an additional 3,800 HHs (19,000 people) in the hard-to-reach areas of Quissanga (1,800 HHs) and Macomia (2,000 HHs) districts with survival kits. CARE will also conduct PSEA training in each district to strengthen the capacity of staff and key community stakeholders to prevent and respond to protection risks.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The proposed project will adhere to the Do No Harm principle. This begins at the beginning of the project cycle, with project design being informed by various analyses conducted by CARE including a Conflict-Sensitive Gender Analysis and the needs assessment to be conducted at the beginning of the project to ensure a relevant and appropriate response and identify potential risks. CARE will regularly collect data through established Feedback and Accountability Mechanisms (FAMs), as well as complement them with secondary data, to best examine how our intervention may interact with the conflict and social context. CAREs 4 FAM entry points include Linha Verde, a toll-free hotline that serves as a feedback mechanism for humanitarian assistance recipients, Complaint Boxes managed by an accountability officer, Beneficiary Registration/Post-Distribution Monitoring (PDM) and directly with project focal point during activities. The results will inform any adaptation of activities to prevent negative impacts. Finally, CARE has robust safeguarding policy in place, with accountability holders throughout the organizations, ensuring safety and dignity are prioritized. In the proposed project, CARE plans to conduct a training on PSEA to all staff and key community stakeholders so they can identify, respond to and prevent protection risks.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

All CARE projects are held to standard by the CARE Gender Marker, an accountability and learning tool that monitors how well gender has been integrated into the approach of humanitarian projects. This ensures all projects are, at the very least, gender sensitive. Through the needs assessment, PDMs and routine project monitoring, CARE considers gender and different age groups by monitoring and analyzing sex and age disaggregated data and changing protection risks and needs. Based off these findings, the project design and activity implementation can be informed to adapt to the distinct needs of women, men, boys and girls of different ages. The assessment to be conducted is a crucial tool to identify the different needs, roles, relationships and dynamics between different population groups. CARE will adapt the NFI distribution and shelter assistance accordingly to findings and routinely follow-up on any feedback given by beneficiaries through Feedback and Accountability Mechanisms (FAM) managed by CARE’s accountability officer – see below. The planned PSEA training planned for each district will also address the different protection needs of different sexes and ages.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

During the needs assessment planned at the beginning of the project, CARE will be able to identify and assess the needs of People with Disabilities (PwDs). This will allow CARE to consult PwDs to determine how assistance can be delivered in an appropriate and accessible manner. During distributions, CARE will accommodate PwDs to remove barriers to assistance. This includes a bypass system for the queue, registering an “alternative collector” to pick-up items and adapting the distribution site where possible to ensure they’re safe (e.g. removing tripping hazards, etc). For the Shelter Type A & B assistance, CARE will use Community Construction Volunteers to support PWDs in the reconstruction of their shelters. By conducting PSEA training, CARE will highlight the specific protection risks PwDs face and how they can be responded to and prevented. Finally, CARE having Feedback and Accountability Mechanisms (FAMs), information dissemination and awareness raising will come in a variety of formats, to account for different disabilities (radio, use of images, speakerphones, etc.).

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Overall, the project will have a minimal impact on the environment. As this project focuses on material support through survivial kits, aid packaging waste will not have a negative impact as nearly all materials/packaging will be re-used by beneficiaries in some way. For example, at the CARE warehouse, pallets will be reused (all pallets made of wood which upon end of lifespan can be utilized by community members); items such as sacs distributed alongside family kits are used by community members to sell charcoal and other goods. For aid packaging, plastics straps can be used ot secure tarpaulin and as part of CARE’s pilot on re-use of single use plastic these straps will be provided to local lartesans who can produce baskets and related materials and sell for income, shelter kit plastic bags have been used to transport goods and plant seedlings and wrap food in, such as cereals and beans. Bales can be used to demarcate spaces in agricultural plots and home gardens (as a type of fencing) so that chickens and other animals do not invade the area. This will prevent beneficiaries from using mosquito nets for this purpose, which has been seen in the past. Resourcefulness of the recipients of aid, and CAREs own reuse of material/packaging in its own warehouse, ensures resource efficiency. During project activities, CARE will monitor the use of material and packaging, to ensure minimal to no environmental damage occurs.
