3 - Needs

The war, which lasted for more than ten years, caused widespread destruction of civilian infrastructure, economic collapse, explosive ordnance contamination, and the largest number of internally displaced persons in the world.Access to basic services, including water, electricity and health care is also becoming increasingly limited, as well as the widespread destruction of civilian and agricultural infrastructure, including homes, schools, health facilities, water supply and irrigation systems, reducing the resilience of the population.Many cases of school dropouts were recorded due to the frequent displacement of the population, or due to the significant decrease in the number of teachers.Therefore, through our project, we seek to:Ensure that children and young people have access to safe and protective learning environments.Increase the access of boys and girls, including adolescents who face life-threatening risks, to child protection services, protecting them from abuse, neglect, violence, exploitation and extreme distress at home and in the community.

4 - Implementation of activities

The main objective of the project is to develop the education sector in the most vulnerable communities through:1 . Provide accelerated learning programs in the form of remedial classes, and catch-up lessons to support the integration of drop-out children and enhance school retention for those enrolled, and provide emergency incentives for teachers.2 . Distributing health supplies to teenage girls at school.3 . Provide targeted activities for children under the age of 18 with the aim of raising awareness about protection risks and harmful practices resulting from them. All interventions are monitored and evaluated by a specialized team that prepares periodic monthly reports to Management of project and UNHCR through which it measures quantitative and qualitative output indicators such as (measuring the number of school dropouts before and after the intervention). The work team are keen to be careful in dealing with the children of the aid provided in a way that guarantees their dignity and respect, by paying attention to the following points:- Patience: recipients often feel stressed and frustrated.- Attention and listening: to the observations and complaints submitted by the children.- Using positive language: while dealing with the children and focusing on the humanitarian goals, while being careful not to discriminate on the basis of gender, gender and social classes, achieving equality between boys and girls and respecting privacy.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

We have a code of conduct as well as a protection policy that all employees and volunteers in the association adhere to.The administration of the association and the work teams in charge of implementing the project activities are also keen on being careful and cautious in dealing with children in a way that guarantees their dignity and rights, using positive language while being careful not to discriminate on the basis of gender and social classes, respecting privacy and promoting a safe space for each child, in addition to respecting the evolving capabilities of children. People with disabilities and respect their right to preserve their identities Al Birr Association takes all allegations of harassment, exploitation and discrimination very seriously, and does not tolerate the perpetrators of harassment and exploitation at all. It has a complaints office that allows feedback and complaints. During our project, we will provide awareness campaigns for children on how to file a complaint in the event of harassment or exploitation.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk / Threat: Lack of educational staff from the residents of the project area.Effect: mediumMitigating measure: temporary assistance from the educational association’s staff.Monitoring: field reports from education centers that include (attendance schedule for students and teachers, follow-up and evaluation forms for students...).Resources: Searching for specialized educational cadres from outside the region.Acceptable level required: Yes Protection risk/threat: The risk of children, especially females, being sexually harassed or exploited.Effect: mediumMitigating measures: 1. Informing children, especially females, of the mechanisms for filing complaints. 2. Training employees on the rules of conduct and including them in their contracts.Monitoring: 1. Subjecting staff and teachers to regular awareness sessions. 2. Inclusion of the issue of exploitation and harassment within the psychological support program provided within the project.Resources: Monitor all staff and teachers.Acceptable level required: Yes
