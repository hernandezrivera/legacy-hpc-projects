Project Summary


Action Against Hunger strategy is designed to address the main causes of malnutrition through an integrated approach linking nutrition interventions with health, WASH and FSL interventions to foster a meaningful impact upon communities' resilience and prevent childhood morbidity and mortality. Under this project, the proposed activities include prevention, treatment of severe and moderate acute malnutrition in children U5 and PLW using the national guidelines through static and outreach sites; provision of basic health services at our Stabilization Centers (SC) and community-based healthcare using ICCM approach through the outreach clinics; provision of minimum WASH services to beneficiaries at nutrition sites and at community level in Pibor and Fangak (Jonglei), Tonj East and Gograil West (Warrap) and Aweil South and Aweil Centre (NBeG). Additionally, Action Against Hunger will implement preventive services through early screening at the community level using the Family MUAC approach, timely referrals, increasing access to MIYCN services, deworming and Vitamin A supplementation as well as improving the access to integrated nutrition, health, WASH and FSL services. Disaster Risk Reduction is a key element of the proposed project given the locations and both natural and man-made disasters experienced frequently in these areas. South Sudan continues to face extremely high prevalence of Gender-Based Violence (GBV). Hence, it is imperative that humanitarian interventions are gender sensitive. Action Against Hunger proposes to: a) continue its efforts to mainstream GBV and gender considerations in its Nutrition, WASH and health programs, and b) support the humanitarian community in South Sudan to remain accountable for GBV and gender-sensitivity. The project will target a total of 115,287 beneficiaries. Action Against Hunger will strengthen nutrition surveillance mechanisms through accurate and timely reporting, conducting SMART surveys and monitoring and analysis of data for early and objective decision-making.
The nutrition interventions are in line with HRP strategy and all the four cluster objectives for the 2022 HRP.
Primary/Direct beneficiaries: will include children under 5 with SAM and MAM, PLW with MAM, children under 5 and PLW screened, caregivers of children under 5
Secondary/Indirect beneficiaries: will include family members of beneficiaries and other community members. In Duk Pagaak, the project will be implemented at the community level using revised guidance on COVID 19 at 3 outreach locations: Chol Tin, Pajimar and Polgour. Chol Tin is located in Tindir Boma in Pagaak payam. Pajimar is located in Uror payam while Polgour is in Ayod payam. However, since both these locations are over 10 hours walking distance from their payam headquarters and closer to Pagaak payam (about 2-3 hours on foot), the community prefers to go to Pagaak to access the services. As per information on ground, there is an internal arrangement between the payam administrators so that these communities can avail the services in Pagaak. In Pibor, ACF will be operating in Kiziongora Payam and there will be no overlap.
Integrated nutrition, health, and WASH outreach clinics will be organized twice a month at the identified worst affected and hard to reach villages. CU5 both malnourished and non-malnourished, children at risk, PLWs, and other community members suffering from common illnesses will be treated/referred to the nearest health facility.

1 - Needs

According to the HNO 2022, an estimated 8.3 million people, including refugees, are expected to experience severe food insecurity in 2022. This represents a 7 per cent increase from the 7.7 million in 2021. Counties of most concern are in the states of Jonglei, Lakes, Unity and Warrap (specifically Greater Tonj), with the most extreme cases being in Fangak County in Jonglei State, where the situation is comparable to Pibor County in 2021. This increase is largely driven by compounded shocks triggered by continued conflict, widespread flooding, deepening food insecurity, inflation, high food prices, and lack of access to basic services. Food consumption gaps are extreme, and people have largely exhausted their emergency coping strategies. In 2022, the precarious situation is expected to be further exacerbated by the country’s increasing humanitarian caseload, the expected increase in the duration of needs in the most affected areas and the projected further reduction in humanitarian assistance across the country. Food assistance is expected to decrease by 10 to 20 per cent from the already reduced levels in 2021. The current economic crisis is affecting people’s ability to access markets, while conflict; flooding and seasonal effects are limiting the flow of both commercial and humanitarian goods and services. According to the inter-sectoral severity of needs analysis, humanitarian needs are most concerning in Fangak, Duk and Pibor counties in Jonglei and Cueibet and Rumbek East counties in Lakes, which were classified in catastrophic need. The SMART survey conducted in the counties of Fangak, Pibor, Aweil South, and Tonj East by our Surveillance team indicates a GAM of 16.2% and SAM 3.3% (2019), GAM 21.6% and SAM 4.9% (March 2021), GAM 23.1% and SAM 3.9% (June 2021) and GAM 9.3 and SAM 2% (Nov 2019) respectively in the locations mentioned above. These are above the WHO threshold of 15%.

2 - Project activities

Action Against Hunger will continue its high-quality nutrition services in the catchment areas of Old Fangak, Pibor, Aweil South, Tonj East and Gogrial East Counties. These services focus on both treatment and prevention of malnutrition in compliance with national guidelines. Key activities include: 1) Conduct passive and active screening through the Family MUAC approach and referral of SAM/MAM cases to nutrition sites. At the community level screening will be done by Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs) and the Mothers of the children U5. 2) Demonstration of developing kitchen gardens and cooking demonstrations at the nutrition sites. 3) Therapeutic treatment of children (0-59m) with SAM and with medical complications at Stabilization Centre (SC). Child-friendly spaces and early childhood development stimulation are offered at the SC. 4) Therapeutic treatment of children (6-59m) with SAM without medical complications at OTP. 5) Targeted supplementary feeding for children (6-59m) and pregnant and lactating women with MAM through TSFP. 6) Therapeutic treatment of children (6-59m) with SAM without medical complications and Targeted Supplementary Food for children (6-59m) and PLW with MAM through outreach clinics. 7) Sensitization on nutrition, WASH, health, and protection for caregivers of CU5 and PLW. 8) Train staff and community volunteers on CMAM, MIYCN, GBV, Covid-19 SOPs and key messages on nutrition, WASH, Covid-19, protection and health. 9) Engage community members to prevent undernutrition through mother-to-mother support groups (MtMSGs) and male group discussions (MGDs). 10) Train lead mothers of the MtMSGs on facilitation techniques and key MICYN concepts in line with South Sudan’s MIYCN Guidelines. 11) Encourage male participation as change agents through Male Group Discussions to initiate a dialogue around and sensitize men on MIYCN and women’s issues (e.g., SRHR, GBV, etc.) and promote gender equality. This increases male awareness on nutrition interventions and thereby enables women’s participation, as women often need men’s consent to participate in activities outside the home. 12) Provide nutrition and basic health services through ICCM+Nutrition approach in the hard-to-reach areas through outreach clinics. This will incorporate mobile delivery of OTP/TSFP to increase coverage for communities facing seasonal stress migration. 13) Conduct regular monitoring of the nutrition situation in the project area through SMART surveys to take appropriate actions at the project level. 14) Conduct MIYCN barrier analysis to understand the feeding and care practices at household level and design the project activities.Action Against Hunger will collaborate with other partners in the location to avoid duplication of services and rather complement the same.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Action Against Hunger considers the feedback from beneficiaries as an instrument to improve the organization’s acceptance, and improvement of our interventions in the community. Hence, all feedback from beneficiaries are analyzed and used to improve the relevant intervention/project as we place emphasis on the organization being a learning entity.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Safety Audits will serve as an additional Feedback and Complaints Mechanism (FCM) modality in our nutrition program. Safety Audits will be conducted using the Safety Audit tool provided by the nutrition cluster. All focus group discussions occurring as part of Safety Audits will ensure provision of an enumerator who is the same sex as the participants. As part of the MEAL system surrounding Men Group Discussions, participants will be specially asked to share their concerns with the program and suggest improvements. In each of our project sites, we avail five FCM modalities as “minimum standards”: 1. Monthly staff meetings with first-line implementers, 2. Focus Group Discussions with Direct beneficiaries, 3. Individual interview form, 4. Comment Box at Field Office Gate and nutrition facilities, 5. Anonymous Hotline Number. The identities of all feedback and complaints mechanism (FCM) complainants will be kept strictly confidential by program management staff when provided. We have standard, context appropriate FCM IEC materials (posters, stickers, flyers), which are displayed at all project sites and on all project structures (e.g. equipment used by beneficiaries). IEC materials include images and text in both English and the local dialect to ensure comprehension by a maximum number of individuals. During all community meetings, trainings, and sensitization sessions, staff to inform beneficiaries on available FCM modalities will use a standard script.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

At Action Against Hunger the PSEA policy is considered while developing Organization-specific strategies, policies, procedures and action plans to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse. To mitigate risk of SEA and GBV committed by and/or against staff and our beneficiaries, Action Against Hunger staff are bound to our Policy on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), which prohibits sexual exploitation and abuse by employees and its representatives and affirms its zero tolerance approach to such cases. All staff sign and agree to Action Against Hunger Code of Conduct, which establishes the organization’s zero tolerance to harm, discrimination, and/or stigmatization of other staff, partners and beneficiaries.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Action Against Hunger will hold regular community meetings at project site including local leaders to discuss about the types of services available; feedback mechanism in place and how they can approach our teams on ground to provide their feedback on the services on regular basis. All the modalities of the feedback mechanism implemented will be introduced to the community members to enable them to choose the modality.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Given the context of South Sudan, the risk of beneficiaries being exposed to insecurity while accessing humanitarian services remain high due to several reasons like inter-communal violence, cattle raiding, revenge killing etc., including long distance of service sites from their homes. Action Against Hunger teams on ground have built strong relationship with local community leaders and authorities to ensure safety and security of our operations, as well as, beneficiaries. The Field Coordinators at each of our bases are the security focal points and they are in close collaboration with local authorities and other relevant stakeholders to exchange information.COVID-19 has affected the entire population in South Sudan. In South Sudan, as of 10 March 2022 there have been 17,044 confirmed cases of COVID-19 with 138 deaths, reported to WHO. Action Against Hunger is ensuring enough PPE supplies to all our staff, awareness sessions on prevention of COVID-19 at community level on regular basis and supporting the government on vaccine enrollment campaigns. All our program activities at field level are implemented in line with the revised COVID-19 guidelines shared by the MOH and nutrition cluster.The flooding in South Sudan has affected 854,000 people and displaced over 365,000, adding to the already high levels of internal displacement. Malnutrition is at record levels, with several areas reporting levels above the emergency thresholds (15% GAM). Women and children are feeling the greatest impact of widespread flooding, sub-national violence, fragile health systems and food insecurity, exposing them to protection risks, malnutrition and life-threatening diseases. Action Against Hunger mitigation measures involve the management and control of flood water movement, such as redirecting flood run-off through the use of floodwalls and flood gates (Dykes), rather than trying to prevent floods altogether. In far-off areas where beneficiaries are not able to access services at out nutrition sites, our teams provide outreach services in the community using local canoes.To prevent instigation of SEA and other forms of GBV by leading gender-targeted interventions, Action Against Hunger will aim to sensitize all gender and age groups on project activities before their implementation. All our project activities are designed to safely challenge harmful gender dynamics in a context-appropriate manner. Results of gender analyses will directly inform project design and implementation strategy. All staff implementing GBV referral pathway awareness, Men Group Discussion facilitation, Psychological First Aid, and any other sensitive discussion with target community members will be instructed on our mandatory policy for maintaining the identity of project participants strictly confidential. Data related to GBV and other sensitive topics will be anonymized in organizational databases to preserve the privacy of survivors. Moreover, survivors or reporters will not be solicited for further participation/discussion. Any GBV case concerning staff or beneficiaries which proceed to investigation will only involve the Country Program Ethics Committee, consisting of the Country Director, Head of Logistics and Head of Operations. All information will be confidential with access restricted to those personnel. We will train key stakeholders on the use of the feedback mechanism at each project location. Additionally, the organization will print complaint procedures in the local language and in simple English. The project team will regularly review the use of the feedback mechanism and information gathered from the review process will be used to make improvements. A diverse set of FCM modalities ensures that differing gender, age and ability groups have access.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

Action Against Hunger has a policy on fair provision of cross sectoral/ integrated services to all sections of the affected population including women, children, the elderly and persons with disabilities and special needs. The program will follow this policy and all sections of the affected population will be served according to their needs. The provision of services will be based on the organization’s needs/vulnerability analysis. Key protection services are incorporated within our programs which include GBV focal persons identified at all facilities; training of staffs on GBV risk mitigation and available referral pathways and services; provision of Psychological First Aid (PFA) to GBV survivors approaching the project teams; hiring more female staffs in the project team and committees formed at community/village level; conducting safety audits at our project sites etc.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

Action Against Hunger mitigation measures involve the management and control of flood water movement, such as redirecting flood run-off through the use of floodwalls and flood gates (Dykes), rather than trying to prevent floods altogether. Action Against Hunger will also manage the people, through measures such as awareness creation on where to settle to avoid such incidences and if it comes to worst then evacuation of the people to dry lands.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

This project will contribute through our food security program which is aimed at providing sustainable sources of foods, which will positively impact a reduction in the level of malnutrition in our intervention areas. Our nutrition sensitive agriculture activities are specifically intended to reduce the GAM rate among the U5 children. This is complemented by our intervention in the nutrition programs being implemented by Action Against Hunger at static and outreach nutrition sites. The production of more food, promotion of resilient activities will go a long way in supporting populations in IPC 3, 4 and 5. Enhancing the productivity of people in need of humanitarian aid will reduce the number of people in IPC 3, 4 and 5 as they become self-reliant and continue to fend for themselves. This will also include sensitization of the community on Kitchen gardening to improve variety of food production and cooking demonstrations to enable them to prepare nutritious foods at home using available materials.
