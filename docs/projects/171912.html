Project Summary

The needs for sexual and reproductive health (SRH) remains high considering the protracted crises and the lock down due to COVID-19 outbreak, associated with redirection of resources (human, medical and logistics) from the provision of SRH services The project aims to fill critical gaps in sexual reproductive health services delivery for conflict affected population and enhance provision of essential quality SRH services according to the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) for RH standards. The project complement previous and ongoing UNFPA projects, CERF-2019, will be implemented in cooperation with local health administrations and local government and will continue implementing key activities targeted on promotion of emergency preparedness and response, and provision of outreach SRH service to the vulnerable population in condition of COVID-19 pandemic:
1.To provide technical assistance and advocacy to local health authorities and local governments in emergency preparedness and provision of SRH service according to The Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) tool.
2.To provide technical guidance on reinforcing infection control measures within maternity hospital, including triage flow and segregation of neonatal and maternal health units.
3.Essential supply of drugs, commodities and supplies including PPE to increase capacity of local health facilities on provision of maternal and new-born life-saving service. Provision of Female Health Workers Sanitary kits for woman health workers working in COVID-19 frontline facilities
4. Provision of outreach minimum essential SRH, GBV and internal medicine services in rayons and cities along the contact line Donetsk and Lugansk oblast (GCA) by three mobile SRH teams in cooperation with governmental COVID-19 mobile teams.
5.Increase capacity of health providers and managers in planning and provision of emergency SRH service including STI, GBV/SGBV and addressing SRH needs of woman with disabilities, improvement of IPC control.
6.Inform the population (community level) and pregnant women on sexual and reproductive health, nutrition, sexual and gender based violence prevention, and referral pathways and COVID-19 risks, symptoms and transmission and prevent discrimination of people that present with COVID-19 like symptoms.
Since the onset of the crisis, UNFPA Ukraine is leading the humanitarian response to reproductive health needs of most vulnerable women and adolescent girls, providing life saving supplies and ensuring accessibility of medical services to those in need, in particular in remote areas and near the contact line.
7. Provision of qualified OBGYN surgeon to perform OBGYN surgeries 210 OBGYN surgeries on severe cases uterine prolapse and fibromioma to 6 rayon hospitals and Avdyivka city hospital (GCA)

1 - Needs

1.3 million people in eastern Ukraine are in need of essential healthcare and disease prevention, out of them 0.6 mln “along the Contact Line”. Access to the essential healthcare, including reproductive health services is limited by the compromised travelling ability of the majority of the affected population in the areas close to the contact line due to the security reasons, unavailability of the public transportation and very often unaffordability, and the breaking down of referral systems ultimately adds to the decreasing quality of care and patient’s safety. Women and children (more than 60%) are disproportionately affected by a severe reduction in health services. Pregnant and lactating women continue to face compromised access to reproductive health and referral services, to antenatal care and post natal care and safe birthing practices. This exposes the pregnant women to a high risk of pregnancy related morbidity and mortality. Maternal health and psychological support network care is largely not available due to the lack of trained care providers and non functioning referral system; health services for GBV survivors, including post rape care, enhanced referral pathways, is either not available or not user friendly.There is a strong evidence of a marked uptick in sex work, including among adolescent girls, and an increase in the number of unwanted pregnancies as well as abortions among underage girls in Luhansk GCA (up to 10 cases monthly compared to 1-2 cases before the crisis for the entire oblast). Without access to quality reproductive health services, they face an increased risk of life threatening complications, including pregnancy related morbidity and mortality. The high risk of increased sexual transmission of HIV and STIs in the affected regions adds to the needs in the area of reproductive health. Estimated 32,800 adults in GCA, NGCA and areas of high concern in need of STI treatment. Around 18,000 potentially unrevealed HIV infected in NGCA: every 2nd potential HIV case is not revealed. At the same time, local healthcare authorities report the lack of diagnostic equipment and treatment supplies. UNFPA SRH mobile teams outreach work along the contact line in 253 settlements show very poor woman health conditions. Out of – 8226 woman examination during June-December 2019, 1070 have Pelvic Inflammatory Diseases , 2110 -Urology System Diseases, 953 - uterine prolapse, 800 - Uterine fibrosis. Around 450 woman has disability due to last 2 mentioned pathologies and requires surgical treatment that significantly improve quality of life. During last 2 years from state and donor moneys was procured laparoscopic equipment for rayon hospitals along the contact line, but unfortunately there is a big gap in number of medical staff, especially OBGYNs (before conflict was 10-12, now only 1-3) and their qualification is very low to use the equipment. One surgery in referral hospitals cost for patient with all legal and under the table fees around 400-500 USD plus travel and accommodation cost. UNFPA supported visit of qualified OBGYN surgeon to Popasna rayon hospital, he performed during 2 weeks 27 surgeries, that is more cost effectiveness solution and do not require long travels for patients .More than 90% in SRH service facilities are female health workers. Women in the health sector are more likely to be exposed to the virus and dealing with enormous stress balancing paid and unpaid work roles. Long wearing of PPE, washing, disinfecting hands and massive use of disinfectants affect the skin barrier as well as the skin micro flora, lead to skin maceration, local allergic reaction, acne, ulcers.. etc. Respondents from COVID-19 frontline health facilities besides PPE, emphasize on importance of proper care for skin, availability of antiperspirants and other hygiene items. The protection of health workers , must be prioritized as critical and lifesaving.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

1. Essential supply of drugs, commodities and supplies including PPE to increase capacity of local health facilities on provision of maternal and new-born life-saving service and treatment of complications of abortion and miscarriage. UNFPA will supply UNFPA emergency SRH kits to maternity and rayon/city hospitals, to assist normal and complicated deliveries and complication of abortion and miscarriage to 8,650 woman of reproductive age (WRA).2. Provision of outreach minimum essential SRH and GBV services in rayons and cities along the contact line Donetsk and Lugansk oblast by three mobile SRH teams during 6 month. To address the problem of medical human resources shortage in the areas most affected by the conflict 3 mobile teams consisting of OB-GYN, general practitioner, nurse/midwife would be providing outreach services visits during 6 month May-November 2021. The mobile teams will be managed by IPs NGO, "Ukrainian Medical Mission". Targeted screening visits to rural settlements, local educational institutions (colleges and schools) and workplaces would be scheduled with local health authorities. The scope of free services addressing acute SRH needs of women and girls and GBV/SGBV will include:-Provision of Maternal health services are mainly prenatal and postnatal care, assessment of high risk pregnancy and identify the referral cases to the secondary and tertiary level maternities. -Provision of Family Planning Counselling and Commodities including counseling and distribution of commodities; oral contraceptives, condoms, IUDs and injectable.-Provision of general and individual counseling on women's health issues including SRH, pre and postnatal care, maternal heath, ultra-sound examination by portative machine, breast-feeding, nutrition, sanitation and child care, cervical cancer screening.-Provision of outreach STI/HIV prevention: distribution of male condoms among sex workers, HIV positive (PLHIV), other group risks and uniform; provision of STI syndromic diagnostic and treatment. Express testing of pregnant woman, sex workers and population at risk on HIV, syphilis, hepatitis B and C – 5000 tests.-Addressing SRH needs of woman with disabilities. Trained providers will provide SRH service to 400 woman with disability.-Provision of Informational and educational materials (IEC): brochures, leaflets and other IEC materials about sexual and reproductive health, sexual and gender based violence prevention, and referral pathways, COVID-19 risks, symptoms and transmission and prevent discrimination of people that present with COVID-19 like symptoms to 50 thousands of woman and youth.-Provision of medical, psychosocial, legal support and referral of GBV/SGBV survivors. 3. Provision of qualified OBGYN surgeon to perform OBGYN surgeries to 6 rayon hospitals and Avdyivka city hospital. Besides direct service provision it will be also in-service training for local OBGYN surgeons. Will be provided 7 two weeks visits or 14 one week visits. Around 210 OBGYN surgeries on severe cases uterine prolapse and fibromioma will be performed.4. Increase capacity of health providers and managers in planning and provision of emergency SRH service including STI, GBV/SGBV and addressing SRH needs of woman with disabilities. conducting trainings for 180 health providers and managers: "The Minimum Initial SRH Service Package (MISP) in crisis" , "Comprehensive management of GBV survivals, including cases of rape", "Addressing SRH needs of woman with disabilities", “Infection Prevention Control5. Provide technical guidance on reinforcing infection control measures within facilities, including triage flow and segregation of neonatal and maternal health units in exceptional cases and according to the decision of governments- IPC assessment and recommendations will be provided.6. Provision of PPE and Female Health Workers Sanitary (FHWS) kits for woman health workers working in COVID-19 frontline facilities (24000 FHWS kits)
