The project will be implemented under the Food Security Sector in liaison and coordination with the protection sector, especially when identifying the most vulnerable households for proper targeting to avoid doing harm to beneficiaries (such as Gender Based Violence). It will also be implemented in close collaboration with the coordination and common services sector for proper targeting, coverage of needs and transportation of agricultural inputs to target locations.

Needs

The conflict in Libya has led to the deterioration of the food security situation in the country with 24 percent of IDPs categorised as food insecure and up to 62 percent at risk of sliding into food insecurity. The conflict has put a lot of pressure on the already dire economic situation which continues to deteriorate, deepening vulnerability of the affected population due to the depreciation of the Libyan currency, lack of liquidity in banks and proliferation of the shadow economy, supply chain disruption, rise in food and commodity prices. An assessment by FAO in 2018 indicated that agricultural production has declined due to insecurity, high costs and limited access to or lack of agricultural inputs. The conflict has negatively impacted the income of IDPs, returnees and host communities, with most of them reporting that their income had decreased by up to 50 per cent since the escalation of the conflict. Their main income source is now the state salary, which accounts for about 40 percent of their income. Most of the IDPs and returnees need food, health services, shelter and education. A rapid food security assessment of IDPs indicated that 24 percent were food insecure with poor or borderline consumption and were not able to meet their essential food needs without engaging in severe or irreversible coping strategies (selling belongings especially gold and silver items). Women headed households are the most affected as they tend to, in addition to selling their valuable belongings, rely more on less expensive food, reduce the amount and frequency of food consumption. The youths require safe and appropriate livelihoods opprotunities as they are at great risk of early mariages and engagement in detrimental activities as coping mechnisms for themsleves and their families. If urgent support is not provided to immediately address the food needs and capacitate the IDPs and especially returnees to produce their own food or increase their purchasing power to access food through the market, the 62 percent of IDPs who are at the risk of slipping into food insecure will do so in the coming months thereby worsening the current situation and making the country slip into catastrophe or famine. It is against the above background that FAO plans to support 2,500 most vulnerable households (returnees and non-displaced) in 4 Mantiqua with essential agricultural inputs and services (staple crop seeds, vegetable seeds and kits tools and training) for them to be able to produce their own food and sell the surplus (especially vegetables) to generate some income. Mobilization and identification of beneficiaries, targeting and implementation of interventions will take into consideration needs of the different vulnerable groups, including women and youths.

Activities

a. Rapid needs assessmentsb. Distribution of cereals (wheat and sorghum) kits (improved seeds, farming tools and training on Good Agriculture Practices “GAP”)c. Distribution of vegetables kits (improved seeds, tools and training on GAP) - Prioritization was based on a composite score that assigned 50% weight to rates of food insecurity based on the Food Security Index (FScI/CARI), which accounts for food consumption scores, food expenditure share, and livelihoods coping mechanisms, and 50% to an agricultural stress index composite indicator used in the HNO, consisting of rates of abandoning agricultural production and reports of challenges in maintaining production due to the crisis. - Determine which type of agricultural production should be prioritized: In addition to prioritizing locations based on rates of engagement in agriculture FAO calculated the ratio of livestock producers to crop producers and vice versa in each mantika. In Sebha, households were twice as likely to be livestock producers as crop producers, so livestock is a priority. In Aljufra, households were four times more likely to be engaged in crop production as livestock, so crop producers were prioritized. In mantikas like Wadi Ashshati and Alkufra rates of engagement in crop production and livestock are both high, so they could prioritized for both livestock and crop interventions.
