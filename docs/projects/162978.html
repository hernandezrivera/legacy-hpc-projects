Project Summary

The proposed action will be implemented in Central Darfur and South Darfur ( Bilel and Al Radom camps) - It is expected that target beneficiaries will amount to an estimated 47,500 people - Sudanese host communities, CAR Refugees and South Sudanese refugees. Norwegian Church Aid's NCA's objective is to consolidate the achievements from previous projects focusing on WASH specific programming while simultaneously strengthening local capacities in H&N support services and ESNFI distribution processes. Furthermore, WASH governance structures will be improved upon in an effort to build upon community-based WASH services and support cost recovery modalities, among host communities and IDP camp settings. For SS refugee communities and camps, the provision of water (15 lppd, as per Sphere Standards) and sanitation infrastructure (increased latrines per person in schools) will be dramatically improved. As for H&N, NCA is proposing the continued support to Bilel and Al Radom clinic in an effort to address malnutrition through OTP and reduce MAM and SAM cases among children <5. It is expected that through implementing this proposed action, NCA will be able to build not only the capacity of those included in technical aspects of this programme but the community and its relevant duty bearers.

1 - Needs

Access to Emergency Shelter & Non Food Items (ESNFI)NCA conducted rapid needs assessments in Rokero in September and November 2018, where the main findings were that most recently displaced families are living in poorly constructed and poorly located shelters. Reasons for this relate to difficulty in securing land, a lack of materials with which to rebuild temporary or more permanent shelter, and the very high cost of building materials. Markets are largely unable to operate with any degree of reliability or accessibility by any but the wealthiest in the community.The assessments show a pressing need to continue to provide basic services in South and Central Darfur, and at the same time to look to ensuring sustained service provision through local authorities and other service providers this cannot stay an “emergency” forever. For 2019, NCA proposes an integrated response targeting the most pressing needs. WASH, H&N and ESNFI services and support will be the crux of this action. We envision reaching a total of 191 121 people including Sudanese IDPs, and host communities with integrated multi sector approach. We also envision a clear pathway to transition of emergency response to a more durable and sustained service.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

All activities are designed with the environment in mind. There will be drainage ditches to support with water overflow into backyard gardens which will inevitable enhance vegetable production in homesteads and also help to avoid stagnant water; breeding grounds for mosquitos (malaria risk).In WASH, communities and sanitarians actively participate in the latrine site selection to make sure there is a reasonable distance between water wells to avoid contamination of water sources from the latrine pits. To this, dry waste collection and disposal has been designed in accordance to the SPHERE Standards. Regular cleaning and awareness campaign are organized to sensitize communities and improve environmental hygiene.The FSEE team will encourage people to prepare farmyard manure or compost as a means of fertilizer. This practice will improve upon the soil fertility as well as enhance the physical and microbial environment of the soil. Moreover, Natural Resource management is central to the FSEE sector; whereby activities such as Hazard Vulnerability Capacity Assessments will be conducted to identify main Vulnerability of communities and plan for activities in Natural resources management to improve resilience. Community Action Groups will be strengthened and capacitated to undertake activities aiming at improving the natural resources. Sudan is facing a wide range of environmental, social and natural resource challenges such as deforestation, land degradation, loss of biodiversity and habitat, pollution of air, land, and water, conflicts over diminishing natural resources, food insecurity and poor waste and sanitation services in urban areas. All of these issues are aggravated by climate change and are hindering Sudan’s sustainable development- all DP projects will have a through consideration of their consideration to the climate before and during designing and implementation. The health clinics and nutrition centres will properly collect packing materials, used syringes, dressing materials, gloves, etc. and dispose of it with incinerators to ensure that the environment remains clean. Damaged and expired drugs will be incinerated to avoid contamination of the environment. The EPRU encourages people to use mud bricks for construction of their residences in order to minimize tree and bush cutting – the leading cause of deforestation. Likewise, EPRU and ERRADA will continue to facilitate and provide seedlings to plant trees in schools, IDP camps and in rural areas. This is to provide shade for the children on one hand, protect the soil from erosion and set model for students and communities to practice environmental protection.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Given that NCA is not only a certified member of the CHS but also a signatory to the Protection Call to Action, a Do No Harm (DNH) approach is taken throughout the project cycle. Gender-sensitive responses have been included into the proposed action and are based on the varied needs of men, women, boys and girls. Thus, gender and age disaggregated data has been provided in this proposed action and will continue to be shared through all future reporting. Given the Darfur context, protection needs specific to women and children have been considered and included in this project. This has been done to ensure their safety, security and dignity are maintained and can be exemplified by GBV responses included in the proposal. GBV is a real problem, often very inadequately addressed within these communities, where traditional norms may prevent appropriate support and counselling being sought.Plans have been made to incorporate sensitization messages on the importance of women’s inclusion in community decision making processes and awareness sessions on SGBV. To improve awareness on SGBV, trainings are planned for community leaders and local officials. To ensure gender and cultural sensitivities are secure, NCA targets women and men separately especially in the SGBV response. Women are involved in leading women specific FGDs and KIIs so that the voices of all vulnerable populations are heard and included in appropriate programming. One of the outcomes of the women specific FGDs was, lack of services to treat victims of GBV, lack of confidentiality and inadequate awareness of the problem when they go for treatment at the clinic. The project will support in establishing a quality SGBV response, which will include equipping the Al Radom PHC with proper kits such as a full Clinical Management of Rape(CMR) kits, trained nurses and a referral linkage with higher hospitals. In the target areas, roles and preferences in producing, providing, preparing, and consuming food differ depending on gender and age groups. Food insecurity poses different risks for different groups. Inability to secure employment and food cause men and boys to migrate in search of income, while the same situation may force girls and women to engage in negative coping mechanisms such as prostitution and expose them to SGBV. When targeting for agricultural inputs, appropriateness of the planned inputs for men/women headed households as well as the youth is considered. Income generating activities and vocational trainings will focus more on the unemployed youth who are prone to negative coping mechanisms, while women headed households are considered for support in provision of agricultural inputs.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

NCA is a Core Humanitarian Standard CHS certified organisation, and NCA staff and partners were trained in accountability, specifically the feedback/complaints receiving mechanism, as well as other CHS core components . The Darfur program will continue to involve communities throughout the entirety of its programming. The DP will share projects plans with affected communities and stakeholders and involve them to the extent possible in the whole project implementation cycle. The NCA complaints response mechanism (CRM) will be strengthened- and to this the telephone hotline will be introduced to new program areas in 2020. This will be in addition to community-based feedback mechanisms will continue to be implemented. The program will work with WASH committees, SILC groups, Women’s groups and parents and teachers’ associations- to inform the program and facilitate better understanding of the context and enable people and communities to express their feedback and complaints to management. The DP is similarly committed to mainstream safety, dignity and access across all interventions. Interventions can safeguard wellbeing and dignity, but they can also put people at increased risk. It relates to the approach the DP takes in all programmes. It does not mean changing what is done but means one should think about how assistance is provided. This is essentially safe, good quality programming: it focuses on effective participation of targeted communities while considering gender, age, vulnerability and diversity (economic, social, cultural etc.). NCA respects and works with the principles of humanity, impartiality, neutrality and independence. Much effort is being made to ensure that all vulnerable men, women and children are eligible to receive humanitarian assistance from the integrated sectors (WASH, FSEE, health, nutrition, education and ESNFIs), based on their prioritized needs and without discrimination.
