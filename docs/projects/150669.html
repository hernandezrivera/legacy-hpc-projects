Project Summary

The project will ensure protective environment for children and develop positive coping strategies in Gaza through provision of structured, inclusive and integrated child protection and psycho-social support (PSS) as well as counselling, case management and resilience-building activities through community based Child Protection (CP) mechanisms for the most vulnerable boys, girls, and adolescents. This will build on ongoing Child Protection in Emergencies intervention and the capacitated Governorate Protection Focal Points, delivering emergency CP and Education support through PSS and recreational activities, as well as on national and international advocacy efforts.

Needs

In Gaza, the on-going occupation is most visibly expressed in the 11-year long Israeli land, air and sea blockade and three devastating offensives. This has resulted in the current humanitarian crisis, extreme distress of boys and girls and their families, a toxic and contaminated environment, difficulties accessing quality education and child protection services, and a massive lack of employment opportunities affecting families’ ability to provide children with their basic needs, limited service provision, unemployment, siege, lack of electricity are the main source of stress for adolescents. The ongoing deterioration of security and humanitarian situation has left children with a deep sense of insecurity for their future, hopelessness and despair. This deterioration is driving the on-going mass demonstrations at the Israeli-Gaza perimeter fence. Since 30 March up to the end of September, 180 Palestinians, including 29 children, have been killed and over 20,000 have been injured, including 1,642 children and over 5000 demonstrators hit by live ammunition. This escalation of violence has generated widespread immediate and long-term mental health and psycho-social consequences with approximately 52,098 people, including 26,049 children, in need of MHPSS responses. This is in addition to the estimated 21,000 already acutely vulnerable children suffering from severe or moderate mental health disorders (HNO 2019). According to CP/MHPSS WG in 2017, one in four children living in Gaza are in need of psychological support. The CPRA assessment carried out by CPWG led the Terre des Hommes in Gaza in 2018 found that across governorates of Gaza an increase in manifestations of psychosocial distress among boys and girls including: aggressiveness, bedwetting, unusual crying and screaming, violence against younger children, unwillingness to go to school, disrespectful behavior in the family, nightmares and sleeping inability, sadness and avoiding others. According to an assessment on children’s mental health and psycho-social wellbeing carried out by SCI in Gaza in 2018 – a month before the latest escalation in hostilities – it is clear that Gaza’s children and young people continue to suffer. The assessment report revealed that despite children and young people showing high levels of resilience and reporting that they feel supported by their families, they are suffering from high levels of psychological distress and are on the brink of a mental health crisis. Feelings of depression, hyperactivity, a preference for being alone, and aggression were reported by 95% of surveyed children. Findings also revealed that children of Gaza were showing worrying signs of distress including nightmares, which were experienced by 63 percent, as well as difficulties sleeping, reported by 68 percent. For children, the threat of conflict, the fear of bombs, and the constant insecurity caused by the unstable political situation were the biggest source of stress, with 60 percent of caregivers saying it was taking a toll on their children`s lives and well-being. Additionally, aircraft sounds were cited as the single biggest source of fear by 78 percent of the interviewed children. Community resilience and family copying mechanisms have also been weakened as a result of the conflict, economic deprivation and unemployment and the regime of restrictions. Protection concerns, including the risk of domestic violence and resort to negative coping mechanisms have consequently increased (e.g. school drop-out, child labour, adolescent girl child marriage). The Governorate Protection Focal Points reported that adolescent boys between 14-17 are particularly exposed to the risk of violence and worst / harmful forms of child labour, while girls face heightened risks of child marriage.

Activities

The project will directly support the Protection cluster Strategic objective 2 of putting in place protection mechanisms to prevent and mitigate the effects of occupation and conflict-related violence through ensuring safe and improved access to quality child protection and MHPSS services for boys and girls being most vulnerable and affected by the humanitarian crisis. The project will target a total of 7,000 boys and girls from all Gaza governorates requiring CP and MHPSS support through a range of activities. Effective case management and psychological first aid (PFA) will be provided through emergency home visits by the existent Governorate Protection Focal Points, group and individual counselling. The project will prioritize children who are most vulnerable and exposed to conflict-related violence in the context of Great March of Return and children at risk of resorting to negative coping mechanisms and harmful practices. Provision of emergency child protection responses, structured and recreational stress relief activities will be conducted around designated shelters and family centres. 20 CFSs each receiving 500 children will be rehabilitated and equipped to ensure accessible, inclusive and protective environments where children can play, socialize and overcome traumatic events. PSS activities, gender and age sensitive child and youth resilience workshops and workshops for parents/caregivers. Child-friendly Spaces (CFSs) will serve as hubs for CP and community based psychosocial support interventions and identification and referrals of children with critical child protection risks and concerns. The most at risk boys and girls of child and adolescents age with protection violation cases and concerns (including, violence, exploitation, sexual abuse, GBV, non-accidental physical harm, neglect, attempted suicide, school drop-out and child labor) will be referred through case management and referral pathways to specialized service providers. In addition to children, the project will also target 3,000 parents of affected children will benefit from structured PSS services using field-tested and contextualized modalities. . CBOs/CPCs and GPFP (Governorate Protection Focal Point) will also engage in monitoring and reporting of grave child rights violations through conducting regular situation monitoring of most at risk communities. The above activities will result in three concrete outputs:1) Girls and boys affected by the humanitarian crisis (including CwDs) receive timely and quality response and have access to structured and recreational stress relife PSS services, group and individual counselling, and case management.2) Girls and boys and their families affected by the humanitarian crisis have access to age, gender and culturally appropriate information on key child protection risks, injuries and Explosive Remnants of War.3) CBOs and CPCs members (male/female) are capacitated to support CP/PSS interventions and referral, monitor and report grave violations of child rights. SC has been delivering Child Protection programming in the target locations and has secured access for the implementation of the project.
