Project Summary

In line with the Sudan Multi-Year Humanitarian Strategy 2017-2019, and the associated annual Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) , UNICEF continue to support children affected by conflict in a context of chronic underdevelopment, particularly in hard-to-reach and new accessible areas where children’s needs are most acute. Sudan’s ongoing violence in the Darfurs, the Kordofans, Blue Nile and Abyei has forced more than three million people to flee their homes, including some 1.9 million children. Displacement has resulted in high levels of family separation, leading to over 2300 active cases of unaccompanied, separated, and missing children during 2019 among IDPs population. Recruitment, mobilization of children to take part in armed conflict continues to be a high risk in the targeted states including in tribal conflicts. More than 400 children released from armed forces/groups will need to be enrolled in a reintegration program to enable them to return to their normal life in addition to many other vulnerable children. While UNICEF continues working to ensure that all children are released and reintegrated, many children are still recruited or associated with armed forces and groups. A communication strategy to end the recruitment and association of children with armed forces and groups. Sexual and Gender based Violence specially against girls, is another serious violation that needs to be addressed specially in the newly accessible areas in Darfur. Displacement and family separation during flighting further exacerbates the risk of SGBV. Other grave violations such as abductions, killing and maiming are another major risk facing children in the conflict affected states. Children in the newly accessible areas in south Darfur, Central Darfur, north Darfur, south Kordofan and Blue Nile are in urgent need of lifesaving child protection emergency services such as psychosocial support (PSS), family reunification, as well as protection from grave child rights violations including sexual violence, abductions and recruitments.

1 - Needs

Although Sudan remains a large protracted humanitarian crisis, there are still some areas in Darfur, Blue Nile and south Kordofan where armed conflict errupts from time to time including local communal conflicts over resources. So, both protracted and new displacement continue to be a challenge. Last year around 27,000 people were displaced in areas experiencing localised armed conflict in Jebel Marra, Blue Nile and South Kordofan. Localized violence continues to take place due to fighting between sedentary farmersand nomadic-pastoral communities, and between nomadic communities. Protection risks and threatscontinue; IDPs particularly women and girls still face high protection risks and continue to be harassed,and occasionally killed or raped; between December 2017 and January 2019, UNAMID documented 192 cases victims of sextual violations and GBV. Children affected by armed conflict countinue to face seriuos pschosocial problems due to disruption of their noraml life, the seperation from their families or primray care givers which results in the loss of family and community protection where many of them become vulunerable to abduction, recruitment and abuse by different partners to the conflict.
