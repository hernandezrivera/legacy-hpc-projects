Project Summary

The intervention for 2020 targets a total of 45733 persons for the 12-month duration. The program will be implemented Maiduguri, Jere, Askira Uba, Chibok, Gwoza and Hawul of Borno and Michika, Madagali, Mubi North and Mubi South of Adamawa states.
The intervention will target conflict-affected people, i.e., Internally Displaced Persons, Returnees and Host community members. The program interventions include Food Security, Livelihood & Early Recovery through cash transfers for food, income generating activities (3000), agricultural activities covering crop production (including home gardening; rainy season farming) and livestock farming, training on best agronomic practices and organic farming; dry season farming, vocational training and access to employment opportunities through entrepreneurial trainings; distribution of cookstoves, social cohesion and social investment opportunities through formation of producers groups and SILC groups with linkages to markets; Protection and gender mainstreaming. Through unconditional and unrestricted CBTs 24,400 will receive funds (Caritas Nigeria will adopt post paid eWallets for all financial disbursements) to address their most urgent needs; 1500, 4500 and 3833 persons will receive agricultural inputs for livestock, dry season farming, and rainy season farming respectively and extension services. Under Livelihood & Early Recovery, small cash grants for IGA will be given to 6500 persons. Selection criteria includes the following: Separated Children, child-headed HH, HH headed by an older person, female-headed with many dependents (especially under 5) especially widows, breasting feeding and heavily pregnant women, men, women, girls and boys with disabilities, chronically ill individuals, households with malnourished children, Survivors (adult and children) of Gender-based violence (GBV), minority groups (including language and ethnic), ex-combatants or individuals identified with perceived affiliation. Through monthly coordination meetings at the LGA, State and federal levels, Caritas Nigeria will ensure it updates her intervention strategy, while coordinating activities with other humanitarian actors in the locations and beneficiary synchronization to avoid double-dipping. Activities are designed such that it includes training sessions on PSEA as well as continuous awareness creation on its dangers. This will be followed by signing of Codes of conduct. For protection, funds collection points will not be more than 30 minutes away from points of origin; those who live far away will be supported with logistics. Sensitizations on best times in the day to collect funds will be ongoing. Home gardens will be prioritized for the women and will be trained to speak out when necessary.
In a word, Caritas Nigeria’s integrated approach to humanitarian action aims at improving resilience, protection and provide assistance to targeted Internally Displaced and Returnees' households in the Adamawa and Borno state, restore human dignity and promote avenues for an end to the plight of forcibly uprooted people, through the provision of an improved access to means of livelihoods. The project outcome is to foster self-reliance through a multi-pronged approach with intra and extra project linkages created where relevant. The intra project linkages will link those identified with Severe Acute malnutrition to interventions on Agriculture and livelihoods so as to address the root cause of malnutrition. The extra project linkage will focus on linking producer groups on the value chain with off-takers; and linking Savings and internal lending Communities (SILC) with reputable microfinance institutions. The agriculture and farming initiatives will include livestock production, enhance self-employment through grants for income generating activities, vocational training and start-up kits for employed youths will improve the general resilience of the vulnerable population and reduce the negative coping mechanisms.

1 - Needs

In addition to the above, 2019 was marked by the General Elections which saw a significant change in administration in Nigeria. Naturally, the election hustings came with scares, attacks and risks. INSO reported that between January and April alone there were about 896 incidents. In Michika, Madagali, Askira Uba, Chibok, Damboa, Gwoza and Hawul LGAs in Northern Adamawa and Southern Borno respectively dozens of Non-State Armed Group (NSAG) incidents were recorded. They ranged from suicide bombings to armed attacks against military and civilian infrastructure. The spate of attacks escalated in February and again in May 2019, leading to a halt (suspension) of project activities. The attacks in May had in its view mostly communities in Michika, and this led to a temporary displacement of about 5,350 HHs from their homes, rubbing off even on Askira Uba and Chibok, the neighbouring LGAs. The latter insurgent action led to the displacement of some community mobilizers and even the death of a Community Leader (also a member of our Community Project Implementation Committee) in Kubula community, Michika. Even though the military had made further efforts in the government-led counter-insurgency measures, indiscriminate attacks resulted in further population displacements, death and destruction of property and counter military measures were often delayed. The displaced families are returning to find most of their basic services and productive infrastructure destroyed by the insurgency; and the remaining resources under extreme pressure by increased IDP populations. Even in the relatively secure Local Government Areas (LGAs); large parts of the suburban areas that lack military protection are susceptible to Boko Haram attacks and are therefore only partially accessible. This is the case in Michika and Madagali.Almost all the LGAs in Borno acutally experience 'Crisis' to ‘Emergency’ (Phase 3-4) food insecurity in late 2019 according to the HNO. Food related malnutrition particularly in children peaks during the hunger period/lean season of July – October particularly in Hawul and Michika. Rapid Assessments conducted by Caritas Nigeria in August 2019 show that one in every five households have increased negative coping mechanisms; due to the increase of food prices as a result of insurgent attacks, more than 49% of the total population are food insecure. Most of the productive assets and infrastructure have been destroyed by the insurgency. The related violence continues to prevent most families from accessing their lands and thus unable to cultivate. Over the 10 years of instability, the crisis has metamorphosed into a protracted humanitarian emergency characterised by increased urban population; transformed livelihoods symptomized by over 60% of youth in the NE abandoning agricultural activities due to poor returns and lack of access to farmlands; increased migration to urban centres; greater reliance on cash for food and basic services; and, an increasing proportion of livelihoods drawn from labour markets. In beleaguered safe havens such as Mubi North, Mubi South, Askira Uba, Hawul, Chibok, Gwoza, a significant proportion of the population in have relied on humanitarian aid since the outbreak of the crisis. The reduced presence of humanitarian actors in the areas mentioned above has made building resilience even more difficult, leading to increased vulnerability especially for the IDPsKey Problems • Extreme food insecurity that require lifesaving food aid particularly during the hunger period;• Continued displacement and the associated emergency shelter needs;• Emergency level malnutrition rates especially among children under 5 years and pregnant and lactating mothers;• Lack of investment to enable livelihoods diversification especially for families that are unable to access their farmlands due to insecurity

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

A feedback mechanism shall be instituted through complaints/suggestion boxes, toll free lines as well as stakeholders email address at designated locations within the communities which will be accessible to all, with dedicated contacts details for complaints/feedback will also be made clearly visible at these points for those who may wish to call or email their complaints and or feedback. The Community Implementation Committees and the various support groups shall designate one or two members with a clear mandate to animate the complaints/feedback of affected individuals to Caritas Nigeria.We will also institute a quarterly beneficiary satisfactory survey to get feedback from the beneficiaries with regards to the program, as well as take their inputs to ensure a better programming subsequentlyA monitoring, evaluation, accountability and learning framework will be established to ensure that the beneficiaries participate fully in the monitoring the implementation of the project. This plan will keep track of activities through regular communication with field staff, weekly management meetings and monthly project review meetings. Appropriate tools for gathering information at community level will be designed with input from the CPICs and beneficiaries. Gathering of monitoring data for the project will be done by diocesan Caritas staff and partners. Information on project progress will be shared, adjustments made as required and lessons learnt quickly put into use. In addition to the above, Caritas Nigeria will establish Help desks during all interventions as a channel for the collection of feedback and resolution of complaints and issues.
