Project Summary

The proposed project is designed by SECD as a response to the urgent community’s needs to improve their housing and shelter’s conditions and contributing to the Winter NFIs emergency response, in the north of Syria ,The proposed project could be considered as one of the urgent relief projects that could make a positive impact on the host people, IDPs, and the community, The project is designed by SECD as a response to the community’s needs to improve their housing and shelter’s conditions and provide emergency shelter rehabilitation support to response to overcrowded conditions. Through this project: at least 725 HHs will be rehabilitated and 725 NFI kits should be disturbed. Also, SECD will conduct cash for work activity which considers the action as a short-term intervention to provide temporary employment in this project and empowerment of women by involving them in this activity.
While selecting the targeted households, SECD will give priority to the most vulnerable host communities households to receive assistance, The project will target at least 20% female/child-headed household (female-headed HHs mean the absence of a male supporter/child-headed HH refers to the loss of supporting adults classifying both cases as vulnerable.

Needs

In September 2018, over 84,000 people were newly displaced in Syria. Most recently, an estimated 53,000 people were newly displaced in Idleb resulting from renewed conflict in the first half of September. As of October 2018, out of the total number of people in need, 1.9 million are displaced people (IDPs). The impact of the winter season compounds humanitarian needs, especially for displaced people residing in camps and similar sites, in addition to other needs, such as food, NFIs, and basic services. As of July 2018. According to the most recent assessments, 4.2 million people remain in need of shelter support in Syria, as shelter and infrastructure damage has taken place on a massive scale as a result of the conflict. According to the Sector Assessment conducted by REACH during July 2018, 11 percent of assessed HHs in Idlib and Aleppo were reporting house land and properties (HLP) issues. This increases to 15 percent for IDP HHS. 10 percent of assessed HHs have experienced threats or eviction or harassment from landlords or others. 19 percent of spontaneous returns in Idleb have reported the theft of private property, it decreases to 10 percent in Aleppo. More than half of Syria’s population (over 10.5 million) has been forced to leave their homes, and it is estimated that 6.5 million people are now internally displaced within Syria. The majority of the displaced population fled after their homes were destroyed and/ or due to the direct threat of violence IDPs in urban areas often live in substandard, overcrowded conditions. The targeted locations are located Aleppo and Idleb governorates.SECD conducted need assessments in Nov/2018 in the targeted locations: the key finds were : - There were severe gaps in shelter response. - A lot of shelters do not agree with the Sphere project standard.- The people in these communities are so poor.-There is an urgent need for NFI.- A lot of IDPs stay in unfinished buildings, separate shelters at host families, in or informal settlements.- Most of HHs of IDPs and hosted family need urgent maintenance and rehabilitation.-There are about 725 of HHs at the targeted locations, which could be classified in (Grade (Moderate damage): 2, it can be rehabilitated averagely cost (830) USD/shelter.The main prevent cause from rehabilitation the houses for people is: 90% of the targeted beneficiaries do not have the financial fund to rehabilitate their houses and they have not materials.-The shelter owners do not have any problem related to rehabilitation shelter which will be conducted by SECD so there are not any big issues related to HLP.SECD during this project hopes to address the need of the people at the targeted locations.

Implementation of activities

SECD team have good experience in rehabilitation shelters they work in this sector from 2013 until now and rehabilitated 600 shelters in Aleppo city 2016. Also, SECD contributed in preparing “shelter repair and rehabilitation guidelines/2018.SECD is active in Northern Syria and was a member of SAG and CRC of SNFI Cluster Turkey /hub.All components of this project will be implemented SECD staff with coordination with shelter/NFI meetings in Turkey, submit 4W's to the cluster along with regular information and updates about the region. Efforts will be made to coordinate with any humanitarian actors on the ground to ensure duplication is avoided. SECD in this project cycle takes into account the lesson learned and recommendation (Guidance note on Shelter and NFIs kits response for Syria) SECD will use the HLP/2017 during all stage of rehabilitation shelter.The key activities are:-Sign MoUs between SECD and related local Councils of targeted communities.- Training of stakeholders on resilience and quality-oriented shelter/NFI skills and capacities, about HLP to respond to any immediate or future HLP issues and Provision of HLP related information, advice or referral support related to shelter interventions-Upgrading of unfinished buildings.- Advertisement and application for the targeted beneficiaries for the targeted Shelters should be 100 percent compliant with the Shelter and NFI Cluster Housing, Land and Property Rights Due Diligence Guidelines.- Verification that targeted Shelters and Beneficiaries are eligible: 100 percent compliant with the Shelter and NFI Cluster Housing, Land and Property Rights Due Diligence Guidelines and Carryout HLP Ownership Community verification and resolve/mitigate any issues related to HLP.- Beneficiaries will be selected through a scores vulnerability and eligibility markers. 100% of the beneficiaries will be verified, produce beneficiaries list of 725 eligible shelters and HHS and 725 HHs which will be received NFI, and Sign agreements between SECD and the beneficiaries. FGD with women and the elderly to address their basic needs through rehabilitation.• Application of registration will be distributed to the council in each community and they will be instructed on the filling process in order to clarify it to the applicants while applying will be under the supervision of M&E team. For applicants who do not own the residence it is essential to provide permission from the property owner; and on the other hand, the and council must assure that the property owner will not exploit the HH after the rehabilitation work is done.• During the registration and verification stages, SECD field team will identify the rehabilitation service and materials providers in the communities and qualify their ability to work. Within this process, After preparing the final list of the beneficiaries in coordination with the local council, and starting the rehabilitation works on the targeted HHs.All the works will be supervised by the SECD Staff along with the M&E team in order to assure the QA and QC of the provided works, through all the project’s aspects, SECD will work closely with the local council and communities in the targeted area in order to identify and prioritize beneficiaries in addition to delivering effective assistance to those most in need. The local council can facilitate obtaining permissions and needed papers from property owners for host communities to live on the property, as well as supporting the monitoring of the project, verifying services’ delivery to beneficiaries, and ensuring that rehabilitation works meet the Sphere minimum standards. Also, SECD will conduct Cash for work activity which considers the action as a short-term intervention to provide temporary employment in this project and empowerment of women and men by involving them in this activity.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risks and mitigation (Mitigating Measure) :Risk (1) : A. Increased risk of inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages across members.Likelihood: medium, Impact: medium.Mitigating Measure : Use the Cluster guidance on minimum standards and recommended content, Use recommended minimum standards in quality and specification of items, If different to recommended standard, identify and exclude from variance any items particularly prone to create tensions.Risk (2) Overcrowding living conditions and absence of adequate services might expose to risk vulnerable beneficiaries.Likelihood: high, Impact: high.Mitigating Measure: Ensure that adequate space is provided by family, Monitor the Shelter living conditions periodically after the completion of the rehabilitation, Raise awareness among the beneficiaries of the dangers of overcrowding, Ensure that a management system is in place for big collective shelters rehabilitation, consider sphere standards when planning the response, and ensure that management is coordinating services with other actors to cover needs and gaps.Risk(3) : Eviction from the shelter due to lack of approval from the property owner and/or exposing beneficiaries at risk due to lack of HLP documentation for the shelter rehabilitated, Likelihood: high, Impact: high.Mitigating Measure: Abide by the HLP guidance document that was developed by the SNFI Cluster, Do not proceed with the Shelter rehabilitation unless with the consensus of the owner, proof of registered HLP ownership, verified ownership claims. If this won’t be available, feasible, refer to the HLP DD, Include HLP caveat into the MoUs or agreements signed , and Have an HLP section in the M&E assessment form.Risk (4): Outbreak of conflict prevents access to project locations.Likelihood: low, Impact: Very High.Mitigating Measure: Lesson learned from 2013 operationsfound that while conflict occurred, access could be restored within a few days. A cautious approach that plans for thesetype of delays is expressed in the project work plan.Risk (5): Airplane shelling.Likelihood: medium.Impact: Very HighMitigating Measure: The risk of Security situation (only heavy shelling) and The risk of the local council buildings being targeted by airstrikes are a very low probability according to The historical information for 7 years ago.
