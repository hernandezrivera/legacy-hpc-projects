Project Summary

UNICEF will implement the HRP 2024 through the Health Cluster approach, through integrated and multisectoral response to address the needs of most vulnerable children, children with disabilities, adolescents, and pregnant and lactating women (PLW). UNICEF will work in close coordination with Health and Nutrition partners to deliver essential health services, supporting lifesaving for populations affected by the conflict, providing essential commodities, strengthening systems and building technical capacity of (CHWs, APEs, ACSs, Activists).

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Mozambique has been cyclically plagued by catastrophes related to climate change and high vulnerability to disasters. The conflict in the North region, cholera, cyclones, and floods, have been the main causes of public health emergencies, weakening the already fragile National Health System. The health situation in the country, affected by the conflict in the North with 1.5 million displaced people, was worsened by cholera epidemics in all provinces of the country and 67 districts, where from September 2022 until September, 34.698 cases of cholera were cumulatively reported, with 144 deaths Case Fatality Rate (CFR: 0.4%) and cyclones Freddy stuck Mozambique twice on 22nd February and 12th March affecting around 1.2 million people in the provinces of Zambézia, Sofala, Tete, Manica, Niassa, Gaza, Inhambane and Maputo. Mozambique is one of the countries with high prevalence of HIV at 12.5% and vertical transmission rate at 10%, People living with HIV are also very vulnerable during emergency. There is need to improve retention to care and guarantee continuity of ARV treatment. UNICEF's response to health needs of people affected by emergencies and people living with HIV manly on prevention of HIV from mother to child transmission by focused on screening, prevention, referral services and treatment in pregnant and breastfeeding women including their exposed children. Supporting emergency repairs, temporary facilities and rehabilitation to ensure essential services for children and underserved communities. For cholera, UNICEF is aligned to the national response prioritizing containment and prevention through treatment, awareness, vaccination, critical supplies and access to safe water.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The priority activities of the HRP 2024 will be to.Objective 1: To provide accessible essential health services to targeted populations (children, adolescents, pregnant women), focusing on main causes of morbidity and sexual and reproductive health.Support integrated mobile brigades (IMBs) and community health workers (CHWs) to carry out community outreach on health and nutrition including community awareness sessions of positive health and hygiene behaviors through our implementation partners ((AVSI (20%), CUAMM (20%), CARITAS (20%) and ADEL 20%), HELPO (50%)); Procure health supplies (including kits to support CHWs and drugs for the health facility to support the health facilities and outreach services) implemented by UNICEF; Expand implementation of integrated community case management (iCCM), and HIV care through the Community health sub-system and Re-establishment of services and/or equipment of health facilities in Cabo Delgado emergency related districts, 15 Health Facilities to equip and 2 do re-establishing to ensure that women, adolescent girls and newborns safely and equitably access quality life-saving and high-impact, maternal and neonatal health services implemented by UNICEF. Objective 2: To provide quality care for people with physical injuries, disabilities and mental health needs. Train community health workers (APEs, ACSs, Activists) on adherence to ART, PSEA, Disability, GBV and Gender equity ensure to reach at least 2,859 LTFU pregnant and breastfeeding women and their children living with HIV and referrals to HF through our implementation partners, MENTOR initiative (50%) and CUAMM (50%). Objective 3: To prepare for, detect and respond to epidemic prone disease outbreaks and respond to any hazard event. Prevent measles outbreaks in IPDs children and host families by supporting vaccination campaigns to ensure reaching at least 142,512 targeted children 9 months to 14 years in humanitarian situations who are vaccinated against measles through UNICEF and Ministry of Health (DPS/SPS from Cabo Delgado); Procure and distribution of Emergency kits to insure that Individuals receiving treatment at least 7,600 cases of cholera in supported facilities implemented by UNICEF; Training of Community health workers (APES), community volunteers in community surveillance, Emergency preparedness, to prepare for, detect and respond to epidemic prone disease outbreaks and respond to any hazard event implemented Mentor INICCIATIVE e CUAMM; Printing and distribution of IEC materials, with key messages to prevent and respond outbreak reaching out at list 374,000 community members through our implementation partners, MENTOR initiative (70%) and UNICEF (30%);

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Protection issues for nutrition mainly concern the security while accessing and utilizing nutrition services delivered at health facilities and mobile brigades which are normally located and built in such a way that limits protection risks. Nutrition humanitarian partners will be trained on specific protection considerations and referrals pathways, in close collaboration with child protection services to support protection mechanisms for vulnerable children.UNICEF will also build the capacity of frontline humanitarian actors on inclusive humanitarian action and will empower children and adolescents with disabilities to participate in humanitarian processes and decision-making. Emphasis will be put on identifying and addressing barriers to accessing services, information and humanitarian assistance by children and adolescents with disabilities.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

All UNICEF partners are required to identify accountability mechanisms including complaint mechanisms in their programme documents which are reviewed by a committee in Maputo before selection.Communities, both host communities and resettlement sites for displaced persons, will be engaged and mobilized to participate in the identification of key preventive and protective practices to improve the well-being of children zero through five and their caregivers. Communication and active community engagement platforms will stimulate demand for services and enhance beneficiary’s knowledge to practice home-based and community behaviours that positively impact children’s survival, growth, and early childhood development. Key behaviours that will be promoted will include breastfeeding, young child feeding, early childhood development, handwashing and hygiene, training caregivers on positive caregiving practices, signs and how to address malnutrition, and how to recognize disabilities in young children

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Malnutrition and disability are closely interrelated and have a number of points of convergence. Commonly this relationship is framed as a lack of appropriate nutrition in both pregnant and breastfeeding mothers and young children leading to impairment. Therefore, UNICEF efforts focus on improving maternal and child nutrition to prevent or mitigate disability, while strengthening the early identification of developmental delays among children with SAM.The inclusion of children with disabilities will be achieved through a) capacity building of UNICEF’s implementing partner; b) training of health workers on early identification of developmental delays; c) data collection/disaggregation for medical mobile brigades and d) mainstreaming disability and identification of development delays among SAM children.Case management of acute malnutrition services will be adapted to include children with disabilities or caregivers of children with disabilities as 5 per cent of the total target.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Climate change exacerbates undernutrition through three main causal pathways: 1) impacts on household access to sufficient, safe and adequate food; 2) impacts on care and feeding practices; and 3) impacts on environmental health and access to health services.The Global Climate Risk Index scores Mozambique fifth among the countries most affected by natural disasters over the period 2000-2019, and first in 2019, having experienced cyclones of unprecedented severity that affected nearly 2.5 million people, many of them from Northern provinces. Aligned with the UNICEF Nutrition Strategy 2020-2030, UNICEF will: a) mainstream assessments on the impact of climate change-related effects on maternal and child diets in the planned nutrition assessments, b) increase knowledge and awareness raising for action through SBCC programs for use of locally adapted foods who are more resilient to climate change and c) support implementation of climate resilient emergency programming and adaptations.
