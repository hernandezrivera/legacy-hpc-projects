Project Summary

As one of the active sector partners, LESGO in collaboration with Health sector in an attempt to support the humanitarian responses in the northeast following the UN-led coordinated response plan in BAY States, LESGO Is proposing an 9 months project to address life-threatening gaps and needs of the conflict-affected population and underserved communities in primary healthcare services in some selected Local Government Areas (LGAs), Adamawa state, Nigeria These LGAs include Hong, Girei, Ganye, Fofure, Song, Lamurde and Demsa. The design of this project is to strengthen the provision of health care services that is currently available in the selected locations, provide mobile healthcare services via teams to locations without services but accessible and making sure underserved are reached with adequate integrated PHC services and reproductive health services. The project will also empower local communities and service providers by working through existing structures where available, adhering to Nigerian guidelines and protocols, and empowering community stakeholders and other project’s beneficiaries in direct service delivery. Women and children will be focused on because they contributed to huge percentage of the People in Need from the HNO/HRP 2022. LESGO will target communities where Primary Health Care services are hampered and not fully running and with a focus on inpatient, outpatient, reproductive health and community public health. Location with providers of health services not currently available in terms of numbers to cater for the overwhelming needs of target beneficiaries.
In locations with functional health facilities but operating at limited capacity, LESGO will train staff, equip and supervise them to provide quality lifesaving integrated PHC and reproductive health care services. LESGO will work through mobile teams by targeting areas where there is no - or limited - access to health facilities or areas hosting new arrivals. The mobile outreach team will comprise of nurses and CHEW supervised by a clinical supervisor, auxiliary staffs and CHVs for community mobilization, hygiene and health promotion, providing care for active patient findings and referrals to outreach sites for SRH cases (ANC and PNC clients).
Activities will focus on both the IDP camp, camp like settlements and host communities, temporary health facilities and mobile teams serving available sites.
As part of a community-based and integrated approach, LESGO through community Health committee will train community health volunteers to strengthen referral networks, monitor health and nutrition, and conduct awareness raising on available services. Before actual implementation, key and critical stakeholders would be advocated to and these will include relevant government ministries and agencies, target LGAs as well as the traditional/community leaders of the concern communities to ensure ownership and buy in.
Also, separate training/orientation packages for different health workers of different categories (Nurse, CHEWs, JCHEWs, and CHVs) on surveillance and outbreak response, minimum service packages for mobile outreach, inpatient and outpatient services in their various post of responses. The training will also include BEmONC, which ensure quality delivery of integrated management of childhood illness, antenatal and post-natal care. Community sensitization on prevention of communicable diseases (e.g. diarrhea, measles, cholera and Malaria) and GBV. These teams will work on weekly basis with an option for follow-ups on patient with acute cases. These services will encompasses first line care and referrals. Complaints will be discussed during monthly meetings and reports shared with partners for effective coordination and feedbacks by the MEAL Officer in conjunction with the project Manager. The total budget for the project activities stood at $900,000.

1 - Needs

Since the last quarter of 2022, the worst floods in a decade have affected 3.2 million people across Nigeria, of which an estimated of over 60 per cent are children. Heavy rainfall and overflow from rivers have damaged public health facilities, water systems, and sanitation facilities, increasing the risk of waterborne diseases, such as cholera, diarrhea, and malaria, heightening the chances of an epidemic and increasing childhood illnesses. Children living in makeshift displacement sites lack basic facilities and are exposed to additional risks, such as separation from their families and gender-based violence. Across BAY states, 210,800 people affected with flooding and 87,700 displaced across 43 of the 65 LGAs (UNICEF). Adamawa state is the worst hit among the three states by the ongoing flood situation and urgently needs attention. According to the assessment conducted in Adamawa state by REACH in collaboration with partners and SEMA, an estimated 260,000 people potentially exposed to floods and around a 1,000 km2 of land submerged across the state. The worst affected LGAs are Madagali, Fufore, Yola South, Yola North, Girei, Demsa, Numan, Lamurde, Guyuk and Shelleng local government areas. Also, looking at the over 13 years of insurgency in the BAY state, which has affected the transitioning of the BAY states into fully recovery phase from an emergency, an estimated 8.4 million people out of which 2.2 million people are displaced in IDP camps across the BAY states according to HNO/HRP 2022. Taking a critical look at the available information, 21% of the people in need are women while 58% are children and 4% are people with one disability or the other. Health sector specifically stated that 5.0 million people are still in dire need of humanitarian health assistance and according to the sector bulletin published in October 2022, only 2.21 million people has been reached. This is showing that there are still huge gaps to fill with the humanitarian assistance. These assistance in collaboration with the NHF 2nd Allocation Reserve 2022 are not limited to the following:i. Reduce morbidity and mortality of malnourished women and children through prevention and treatment of malaria, cholera and other waterborne diseases.ii. Improving access to reproductive health services especially safe deliveries in displace sites and locations. Currently, the affected people across northeast Nigeria remains at significant risk of epidemic-prone diseases like cholera, measles, meningitis, and viral hemorrhagic fevers. Malaria continues to be endemic and is reported to be the main cause for the highest percent of the total consultations in the EWARS system. In addition to malaria, acute respiratory infections and watery diarrhea are the other two leading causes of illness among prone communities in the selected and targeted LGAs. There is also the issues of border crossing which easily spread the outbreaks of these diseases if rapid response were not engage on time. Malaria remains the major cause of morbidity and the leading cause of death among children under the age of 5 as well as pregnant women.
