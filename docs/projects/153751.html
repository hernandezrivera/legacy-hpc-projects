Project Summary

The project will focus on hygiene education on household level in rural and semi-rural communities as well as implementing child-friendly hygiene trainings in schools. Both will be combined with rehabilitation of existing WASH infrastructures at individual level as well as in schools. Welthungerhilfe plans to implement simultanously a FSL programme to ensure improved WASH facilities and hygiene behavior changes are strongly interlinked with agriculture activities as improved, diversified crop production and improved food use to contribute to strengthening nutrition security in a wholistic approach. Welthungerhilfe also plans to pilot menstrual hygiene promotion activities in its areas of intervention to contribute to women's and girl's equality and not longer being stigmatized during their menstruation cycle. In total up to 20 schools will be targeted in Torit and Yambio for improved WASH facilities and child-friendly hygiene trainings. In NBeG 12 primary schools will be targeted for rehabilitation of latrines in schools, serving approx. 12,000 school children and provide trainings on improved hygiene practices to 1,200 children and teacher. In NBeG in total 600 rural households, i.e. 4,200 individuals will be trained on improved hygiene, practices and will benefit from 12 rehabilitated drinking water boreholes. In Torit another 800 households will be targeted for hygiene trainings through a network of volunteer hygiene promoters. In each target school a WASH club will be strengthened or newly established where school children will be trained using the CHAST-approach on improved hygiene practices, they will be supported in organizing particular WASH-days (handwashing day, etc.) where they will be supported to do mass-campaigns and hygiene messaging for other school children.

Needs

Due to the conflict and the lack of government capacities, many basic social services are massively supported or entirely provided by humanitarian organisations. In NBeG access to safe drinking water and sanitation facilities is also insufficient. Although 51-75% of the settlements in NBeG report that functional boreholes are in place, only 20% of the settlements reported that their main source of drinking water is an improved source such as a borehole or tap stand which is reachable in less than 30 minutes, according to REACH. Moreover, in 50-75% of the assessed settlements in Aweil West and in over 87% of the settlements in Aweil North, water sources are used by both humans and livestock at the same time. According to REACH, 87.5% of the assessed settlements reported to not have access to or not use latrines. Of the 195 schools in the project area, only 77 have functional latrines, according to the local education authorities. Poor sanitation leads to infectious diseases which have a negative impact on the children's nutritional status increase absence in schools. Children prone to malnutrition are also most likely to have a reduced learning performance in school. Schools are seen as valuable environment where children can learn about adequate behavior, consequences of insufficient hygiene behavior and practice improved hygiene behaviors. School children and young adolescents who adopt new behaviors can then also function as voices to improve hygiene practices on community level. Insufficient hygiene behavior is widely common in rural South Sudan and besides lacking infrastructure often linked to insufficient knowledge among communities and hence insufficient adoption of new behaviors. Women and girls are often strongly stigmatized during their menstruation cycle and left out from social life which also impacts their standing in the communities and hence hinders gender equality. Lacking menstrual hygiene management is one of the root causes for young girls to abandon school education when they reach a certain age and influences therefore their possibilities to generate income in the future. An additional in-depth needs' assessment will be conducted at project inception.
