Project Summary

The proposed project aims to continue the provision of life-saving health care services for IDPs in the camps, through sustaining the operations of two mobile clinics with following HeRAMS codes: (G0700149 & G0700150. These mobile clinics will cover 85 camps (including 15074 HHs with 76795 individuals) in Maaret Tamasrin sub-district in the communities: Kelly, Kafr – Kafrehmul, Maaret Elekhwan , Kaftin , Haranbush , Sheikh Baher , Kafr Jales , Kafr Nabi , and Ma'arrat Tamasrin.
The project will provide comprehensive package of services including health promotion and prevention services, care and treatment, and First Aid care to the most vulnerable groups who due to age or poverty cannot approach regular doctors in underserved areas where no health services are available in order to meet their urgent health needs.
36000 individuals are expected to directly benefit from this intervention (direct beneficiaries).
Qualified and trained health staff consists of GP, midwife, lab technician, nurse, and CHW will provide the Primary Health Care services including, basic reproductive health, child health services, management of communicable and non-communicable diseases, referral services, and lab services.
The mobile clinics are well equipped, each mobile clinic has general medicine clinic, gynaecology clinic, nutrition clinic and a lab room that includes the following equipment: (Hematology analyzer, CBC (Complete blood counter), Hematology Centrifuge Tabletop centrifuge, Microscope, Autoclave, and Refrigerator &Freezer) which will provide blood, urine and stool analyses. In addition to that Ultra sound device is available for pregnancy monitoring.
All the Mobile clinics’ data will be submitted to prime via 4W reports, DHIS2, HeRAMS. The mobile clinics will refer the cases in coordination with the referral network to secondary or tertiary health facilities.
Health Awareness sessions about preventive health care issues including family planning, IYCF, communicable and other diseases will be conducted in the mobile clinics, schools, and mosques by community health workers, midwives and doctors.
Training on topics of (NCD/PEN, IPC/Covid19 screening AND Cholera enhance IPC prcedures protocols, basic Nutrition skills) will be conducted to expand the quality and skills of health staff.
The mobile clinics will move twice monthly between the camps to provide medical aid to IDPs in order to save them the trouble of making long, expensive journeys to health facilities in towns or cities . in addition to that the staff of mobile clinics will conduct home visits to the people of disability who need health care but their disability hinders them from arriving to clinics.
The mobile clinics will Coordinate with surveillance system to monitor diseases outbreak and to refer cases of epidemic diseases like COVID-19 , Cholera ...etc.
All services provided including lab , medicine and medical supplies will provided for free for beneficiaries.
INSAN will provide a simple and accessible mechanism that allows beneficiaries to provide feedback, suggestions, or complaints. A hotline or WhatsApp and email address for complaints will be advertised clearly on project banners. in addition to that, the M&E team will monitor the project by conducting spot check visits to mobile clinics to measure and verify the beneficiaries' satisfaction with the services provided by the project.
INSAN will coordinate regularly with the IHD, Health cluster, nutrition cluster local councils, and NGOs operating in the area to avoid any duplication. And will coordinate to respond to any gap in any area based on WHO and IHD recommendations.

3 - Needs

The project was accepted in 2022-2023 HRP process. 38803 consultations provided in 2022 and 2023 to 35098 beneficiaries. 24428 beneficiaries received free medicine and 6317 lab tests conducted, 251 health awareness sessions conducted.More than 15.3 million of population in Syria, approximately a 3.2m increase from last year, are estimated to be in dire need of life-saving health assistance in 2023. This includes 6.8 displaced persons, 2.2 million children under 5 years – including live births expected, 4.2 million women of reproductive age (15-49 ), and more than 700,000 elderly people who will demand integrated and lifesaving health services in Syria. In addition to the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic, a Cholera outbreak was declared. The outbreak is mainly attributed to the deteriorating socio-economic conditions and the water crisis in the northern part of Syria, which triggered a significant increase in waterborne diseases across the country. The outbreak is spreading fast and as of the 4th of October, 13,059 suspected and 900 confirmed cholera cases including 60 associated deaths, have been reported from 13 of 14 governorates in Syria. These additional crises continue to overstretch the already weak health system and have direct consequences on vulnerable communities and patients, increasing morbidities and mortalities. According to the 2023 health sector severity scale, areas of the highest severity continue to be in 5 governorates in northwest and northeast Syria.

Risk: Gender-Based Violence (GBV):mitigation measures:1-Enabling factors such as the presence of female staff in health facilities, separate waiting areas, and women-only hours can increase access of women and girls to health services and offers a safe environment for receiving health services.2-Analyze and disaggregate all data by sex, age and disability. Analyze the needs of vulnerable and minority groups (such as adults and children with disabilities), throughout the program cycle (assessment, analysis, design, implementation, and monitoring) with identification of risk factors and rights violations impacting service provision for beneficiaries. 3-conducting orientation and refresher training for staff on humanitarian principles and how to interact, communicate and engage with vulnerable groups. 4- All staff should receive training on communicating with persons with diverse disabilities to enable women and girls with disabilities to be identified and receive support on an equal basis.work with GBV counterparts to identify safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases – connecting health services and clinical management with social services, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS),

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Risk of COVID -19 & Cholear outbreaks. mitigation measures:- INSAN will enhance IPC procedures protocol to avoid any spread of COVID-19 and cholera to the clinics, and this will include:-provide PPE and IPC supplies. -follow up triage protocol at the entry of the facility. -refer any suspected cases according to the health cluster guidelines.Risk of access to mountainy areas.mitigation measures:-The mobile clinics will be located at the nearest point to these areas.Risk of security :All the targeted places are considered safe and in case of any risk the mobile clinics will be moved to safer places.Risk: Inability of elderly / people with health conditions / persons with disability to access to mobile clinics and to its services.mitigation measures: The staff will conduct home visits to health cases which can't arrive to the clinics. Risk: insufficiency of medicine provided to clinics by donor.Mitigation measures :The budget will include budget line for procurement of medicine to avoid any shortage may affect the work of the mobile clinics.
