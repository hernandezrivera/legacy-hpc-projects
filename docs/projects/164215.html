Project Summary

In order to continue to address the burden of the leishmaniasis epidemic across northern Syria, The MENTOR Initiative will continue to partner with stakeholders and communities to ensure that the most vulnerable receive:
1. Prevention of leishmaniasis transmission within their homes. MENTOR will provide protection to 3,728,917 people through an indoor residual spraying campaign.
2. Information about leishmaniasis and its transmission. MENTOR will run combined information, education and behavioural change campaign (IEC/BCC) to ensure households are empowered to prevent leishmaniasis transmission within their household. This will be done by various media and delivery strategies, to maximise uptake and correct utilisation of leishmaniasis prevention and case management services.

4 - Needs

Leishmaniasis is a neglected tropical disease, and an estimated 0·6–1 million new cases per year are reported from nearly 100 endemic countries. It is transmitted by the bite of a sandfly, which breed in uncollected waste, cracks and crevices, and in the rubble of destroyed infrastructure created by conflict and displacement. Furthermore, the most common species of the leishmania parasite use host reservoirs such as humans, rodents, and dogs.In 2019, MENTOR diagnosed and treated 39, 051 new cases of CL in northern Syria. 50.3 % of cases were female, and 66% were children. 21% were children <5 years. MENTOR’s data show high incidence of leishmaniasis across all governorates, with > 1 in every 1,000 people infected.As stated in the WASH assessments were conducted in June-August 2019 to inform the 2020 HNO ((There is a significant need for vector-control activities including solid waste management in some areas, particularly in areas with high prevalence of leishmaniosis)) MENTOR’s project directly addresses this need in implementing prevention intervention to reach out and provide protection to the people in need in the accessible areas of Idleb, Aleppo, Hassakeh, Raqqa and Deir ez Zor (DeZ) governorates.

5 - Implementation of activities

Indoor Residual Spraying (IRS): The IRS campaign will be strategically conducted prior to or just inside the disease transmission season. Sandflies require a minimum temperature of 16.5°C to prosper, although larva development can take place from 9°C and above. Generally, this means a transmission season lasting from March to October, with peak sandfly numbers in September. As IRS lasts 6-9 months, depending on the substrate sprayed, correct timing ensures maximum benefit. The sandfly is 1/3rd the size of the mosquito and silent, but the residents perceive the benefit of IRS because of the reduction of more conspicuous nuisance pests such as cockroaches, bed bugs, flies and mosquitos. This is also an added health benefit. MENTOR will use ICON & FICAM as the insecticide for IRS in 2020. Spraying will include animal houses and outbuildings, and a minimum coverage of 80% of households will be achieved following WHO guidelines to ensure community level protection. Community resistance to IRS in a country where suspicion of chemical reagents is at a height can understandably be a problem. MENTOR has worked to overcome this issue through effective awareness campaigns; the employment of local workers, engendering the trust and confidence of the local community; the recruitment of female workers to facilitate access to homes when the male head of household is absent; printed IEC material accompanied by verbal explanation during the house to house visits, also highlighting the general benefits of eliminating a range of household pests; spraying animal houses, beneficial to both the animals and the household by the elimination of nuisance flies; and above all, a friendly and courteous manner. MENTOR IRS teams are now requested in over 90% of accessible households in northern Syria when conducting campaigns. Primary quality control is provided by management supervision.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

MENTOR ensures throughout the entire period covered by any award that safety of beneficiaries and staff recruited for the campaign is addressed in an inclusive manner, i.e. beneficiaries and recruited staff are informed and are given the opportunity to discuss safety and the reasoning behind safety decisions. To this end, MENTOR provides a thorough briefing to beneficiaries prior to the start of an IRS campaign to cover, but not limited to, the following points: Instructions to home owners to ensure safeguarding of foodstuffs, water, kitchen utensils, children’s toys etc. Covering of any items remaining in the house during spraying. Actions to be taken by residents during spraying, and instructions to the home owners pertaining to poultry and livestock. Explain to residents what is being done and why No persons, animals, poultry or livestock to be inside during spraying Time needed before re-entry to the home is permitted. Post-spraying instructions to include, but not limited to, the following: minor cleaning to be done before touching light switches, door handles etc., Remove from premises all foodstuffs, water and utensils plus children’s toys Cover with plastic sheets all belongings remaining inside No re-entry until insecticide deposit dry (2 hours) Mop-up any run-off Wipe down light switches, door handles etc.actions to be taken if residents find dead or dying insects post-spraying, no washing of walls. Inform occupant to sweep up all dead or dying insects and to dispose of them in latrine or by fire Walls should not be washed down Further, the very design of MENTOR prevention campaigns reduces insecurity as prevention workers (male and females) are recruited locally - regardless of gender, ethnic origins and religious beliefs - to provide protection within their own communities. Trained prevention workers are not imported from outside of the area, as this provokes suspicion, reducing trust and access.
