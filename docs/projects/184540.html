Project Summary

According to the October –Dec 2021 FSNAU and Somalia/2022-somalia-humanitarian-needs-overview, the current and growing humanitarian crisis is compounded by heightened political tensions, poverty, drought, floods, forced displacement and human rights abuses coupled with COVID 19 pandemics. The HNO asserts that without humanitarian assistance, nearly 3.5 million are expected to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes through the end of 2021. There is a serious risk that the combined impact of consecutive seasons of below-average to poor rainfall can develop into a major drought by early to mid-2022. According to the HNO, ‘’Somalia was among the countries hardest hit by the worst desert locust upsurge in decades that started in 2019’’.
DMO strategic intervention calls for WASH intervention targeting 10,000 HHs ( 6,500 Pregnant and Lactating Women and Girls, and Child-led Households along with Persons with Disabilities , and 3, 500 Men including the Elderly, Persons with Disabilities through. :
• access to tenable water trucking through water vouchers
• safe and secure latrine and sanitary facilities,
• increased awareness on safe hygiene practices especially on covid 19 pandemic
• provision of hygiene kits ( include soap, aqua tab, bucket, Jerrycan, and provision of sanitary pads to Households including those affected with AWDs
• Provision of COVID 19 mitigations ( masks, sanitisers and thermometers) to extremely high-risk families, i.e., families with malnutrition children
• Rehabilitation, chlorination, de-silting and fencing of wells and boreholes
• increased community awareness on WASH intervention among the Minorities, IDPs, host communities, returnees, and persons with disabilities being the extremely vulnerable and affected by war, drought, floods, poverty and food insecurity.
• Temporary and sustained access to water will be delivered by providing water vouchers and rehabilitation of strategic water points. Hygiene kits will be distributed to targeted high-risk families, (Persons living with disability, families with malnutrition children, affected by AWD cholera, IDPs, Returnees, Host communities and minority clans).
• Focusing on strategic catchment points such as schools, water points, and health/nutrition centers in disaster-affected areas.
• Disability disaggregation data collected throughout the project.
• DMO sets up the WASH management committees (WMC) comprising of Persons with Disabilities, women, Youth, the Minority Clans, IDPs, Returnees, Host Communities, the Village Elders and the experts in Water, Sanitation and Hygiene.
• Construction of gender-sensitive latrines with hand-washing facilities raised to avoid flooding and fitted with locks from the inside to curb GBV and enhance dignity.
• Construction of accessible WASH facilities for People with disabilities and the Elderly.
• Initiation of CLTS approach to encourage good hygiene and sanitation behaviors aimed at achieving ODF free villages.
• Distance, safety and security, and time are critical factors determining accessibility of WASH services and facilities for all the targeted segments of the beneficiaries
• Direct consultation and engagement with women, men, girls, boys, the people living with disabilities, the Minorities and the Elderly in the Project design, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation.
• Mainstreaming protection and GBV risk reduction through trainings, sensitization and setting up monitoring and reporting platforms and beneficiary commitment for the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse within the intervention cycle.
• Mobilisation, sensitisation and meetings with all project stakeholders, Actors, and targeted communities informing them on objectives, scope, beneficiary selection criteria, and beneficiary entitlement...
• Setting up an Accountability framework to the affected population supported by feedback and complaints mechanisms
• Generator support & the installation of solar panels for pumping water

1. - Projects activities

Project activities1. To increase sustainable clean access to safe water, hygiene and sanitation services and facilities for the disaster-affected populations DMO calls for :A. Provision of water vouchers for water –trucking to 10,000 HHs ( 6,500 Pregnant and Lactating Women and Girls, and Child-led Households along with Persons with Disabilities , and 3, 500 Men including the Elderly, Persons with Disabilities.B. Construction of 10 new wells/ boreholesC. Rehabilitation and fencing of 10 wells/ boreholes as per the cluster standards. D. In addition to generator support, DMO calls for the installation of solar –panels to power water from wells into water tanks and water kiosks for distribution considering time, safety and distance among the targeted beneficiaries including people living with disabilities, women, and girls far from exposure to violence, kidnapping, sexual and gender based violations. Generators can be on standby to support the solar powering in terms of cold weather. This enhances water flow and distribution.D. Constitution and training of 100 Water management committee (WMCs) members on WASH, Gender, Disabilities, Environment and Climate in relation with WASH. Women, men, and people living with disabilities will be equally represented in these committees. 2: Enhancement of sustainable access to adequate sanitation facilities and reduce the risk of violence against women, girls, and vulnerable people. Designing the latrines will consider the needs of people living with disabilities and the elderly. This entails:A. CLTS triggering campaigns in 15 villages where no latrines will be constructed. B. Construction and desludging of 60 gender-sensitive lockable latrines with handwashing facilities and security lights in IDP camps, C. Solar –panel security lights in 7 IDP schools and nutrition/health centers. The security lights are solar-powered.3.Pre-positioning of hygiene kits A. Distribution of 5,000 hygiene kits to the most vulnerable households. B. Constitution of the school and village WASH clubs represented by Persons with Disabilities, Girls and Boys, Religious leaders, Women, Minorities, IDPs, Teachers and ProfessionalsC. All-stakeholder composition and training of 200 hygiene committee members (at least 100 are female) on the PHAST method and protection mainstreaming. D. Provision of COVID 19 mitigations (masks, sanitisers and thermometers) to extremely high-risk families.E. Hygiene and sanitation sensitization campaignsF. Provision of sanitary kits to targeted women and GirlsG. Sensitisation on best practices and policies on environmental waste disposal especially COVID 19 wastes such as masks and bottles to enhance sanitation. 4. Chlorination of 100 wellsA. Consultation with Health and Nutrition Clusters for referral of AWD and nutrition cases.5. Enhance the early warning system in flood-affected areas in view of the impact on health, nutrition, food and hygiene impact on the populations. DMO will coordinate with local authorities and Actors to enhance flood watch and interventions.6. Project monitoring, reporting and evaluation on project performance indicators, setting up accountability to affected populations framework, feedback and complaints mechanisms.7. Project signing off, and handing over WASH structures to the targeted beneficiary communities and the project committee upon completion. 8. Mainstreaming of PSEA and Persons with Disabilities, while standing against Sexual and Gender Based Violence, discrimination and marginalisation.
