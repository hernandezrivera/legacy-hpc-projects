Project Summary

Over 2022 and 2023,the action seeks to deliver formal and non-formal education to 5434 IDP out of school children (OOSC) :3260 girls, 2172 boys aged 6-18 in 11 camp schools in tents and in buildings built with bricks and metal roofs in the camps themselves. The proposed project contributes to the HRP Objectives through:
-Increase safe and equitable access to formal education for 5434 crisis affected students:3260 girls, 2172 boys (aged 3 15 years)
-Improve the quality of formal education for 5434 crisis affected students: 3260 girls,2172 boys (aged 3 15 years)
The camps are located in rural Idleb, Harim district. The action will focus on access and quality .Each camp school will have around 494 children from both genders, in addition to155 teachers(35 Pupil Teacher Ratio), 11 head teachers, 11 cleaners and 11 guards who will be recruited based on a fair hiring criteria. Teachers will be paid monthly incentives according to days worked as tracked by timesheets. As a plan A, Syria Relief will deliver physical education based on the EDs decisions taking into consideration the protective measures for COVID-19.However, home-based education will be as a plan B if an emergency situation occurred, where teachers will deliver lessons to children in groups in safe camps if ,for instance, if one of the camps was shelled or evacuated or flooded The plan C can be applied through the distance learning in which the children can attend online sessions over the smart devices. SR will provide contextualized and adaptable capacity building through trainings and improved learning circles to school staff as relevant and needed and further coaching and mentoring to improve professional development of teachers within each school, with the collaboration of teachers, school management and relevant education authorities.
For formal education, the project will use the Syrian Interim Government (SIG) curriculum.For non-formal education, a BLN curriculum developed by SCI and IRC will be used. In the school year, provide inclusive formal education in the targeted locations: in case schools remain shut, the formal curriculum education
will be provided through online learning for children with access to smart devices, or
through SLP work papers for children with no access to smart devices. SR will ensure equal access of siblings to smart phones, through mobilizing parents and communities and close monitoring and follow up. SR will provide remedial classes in the summer season in order to help children who are falling behind their peers at the class (due to school suspension) in addition to SEL and PSS Recreational activities/sessions. In case school suspension continues, SR will provide 5 remedial classes (2 Arabic, 2 Math and 1English) in collaboration with the ED, in addition to at least 1 recreational PSS session and1 SEL skill session that will be done either online using
online platforms for children with access to electronic devices, or through SLP for children with no access to electronic devices.
BTL campaigns will target the 11 camps for key messages on the importance of learning.There will be classroom Observation for overseeing the teachers and support them.Teachers will form Teachers Learning circles in each school for exchanging the experiences and teaching
CWD will have an access to education through including them in the schools among their peers.there will be ramps and reels for them in addition to special WASH gender-sensitive facilities.

4 - Needs

In reference to HNO 2022,''The protracted nature of the conflict, economic duress and the Covid-19 pandemic, continue to impact overstretched education services. With 25 verified attacks on schools and seven verified instances of military use of schools in nine months, attacks affecting schools remain high.At national level, the data reveals that 18 per cent are out of school and half of the children who are not in school never enrolled. Rates of non-attendance were highest in Ar-Raqqa (35per cent), Al-Hasakeh (30per cent), Idleb (28 per cent), Aleppo (26 per cent), and Deir-ez-Zor (25per cent). Over a quarter of households with school aged children indicated that their children are not in school because there is no school to send their child to. In 1,017 assessed camp sites in northwest, there are only 196 schools''. Syria Relief conducted a needs assessment in Idleb governorate prioritizing 11 camps in Idleb which have IDP out of school children who fled recently from southern Idleb, Hama, rural Aleppo or when the conflict erupted from all over Syria. The 11 camps have 5434 children (3260 girls, 2172boys )aged 6-15 (including 19 children with disabilities).Children are out of school for 9months at minimum. The assessment revealed that there is an urgent need to support the IDP camps aged 6-15 by providing formal and non-formal education as well PSS activities as well as emergency food kits in which SR will cover as much of those emergency needs from other donors.

5 - Implementation of activities

The action focuses on the following activities related to access and quality:Light repairs for the existing classrooms and WASH facilities,supply camps with school furniture : heater, desks and board,provide safety items as 1st aid kits and extinguishers,provide disabilities with rails and ramps,deliver non-formal sessions for girls and boys whose ASER score are 0-1 using BLN,Conduct Back-to-Learning campaigns and convey key awareness-raising sessions and messages,Hold meeting with stakeholders and local actors,enroll children without discrimination,conducting ASER test to measure the learning outcomes,provide children with remedial classes during Summer School,provide students with learning materials ,provide teachers with teaching kits,provide referral pathway services to affected girls and boys,conduct child-centered and CP related trainings to teachers, pay monthly stipends to education personnel, provide girls and boys with recreational materials, distribute fuel and heaters in cold months,pay running cost for schools,establish parent teachers associations in each school,* establish teacher learning circles in each schools,printing formal SIG textbooks , BLN for children,conduct PSS semi-structured activities for the children in the camps,conduct MEAL activities related to the project such as monitoring the distributions,trainings,complaints,assessments.The Head teachers will be responsible for general management of the school, e.g. all documenting.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Attacks on Learning facilities by parties so facilities can be de-conflicted using the de-confliction mechanism administered by UN-OCHA.1 De-confliction does not guarantee that a learning facility will not be attacked.Teachers or other education personnel participate in physical punishment or bullying so enforced code of conduct against all forms of violence and unacceptable conduct, including bullying, corporal punishment, and physical, verbal and sexual abuse. Teachers and school personnel should be trained on prevention and reporting of, and issues related to, violence, physical punishment and bullying and the use of positive disciplineChildren are over-exposed to the risk of, or experience, harm by teachers, school personnel, organization staff or related entities so CSG polices and systems in place by the organization should be regularly monitored and corrective action taken when needed Aid workers or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they should be serving so PSEA policies should be in place, practiced and monitoredLearning facilities or their surroundings are structurally unsafe or do not provide sufficient protection from the elements (rain, snow, cold, dust, etc.) or from public health risks including Covid-19 and other communicable diseases so : improvement to ensure that buildings and grounds are safe and protected. This may include stairs, walls, roofs, flooring, electric, water,WASH facilities


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SR has PSEA Code of Conduct, and Child Safeguarding policies and mechanisms in place to ensure all effort is made to protect all Program participants. SR’s PSEA Mechanism is designed around four core components: Awareness Raising,Prevention, Reporting, and Response. A similar mechanism is available for CSG where the staff are familiar with how to approach and report CSG cases of childabuse, exploitation or violence. In addition, all forms of sexual harassment and exploitation by staff who work in and for SR are unacceptable, both during and outside of work. In addition to PSEA, SR reporting system in place to prevent andtake actions against such behaviors, which clearly stating zero tolerance for sexual harassment. All staff, contractors, cleaners, etc. must adhere to these, and SR offices have been already trained on the latter policy.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Through the selection of the personnel ,few issues may be faced: camps management may order to hire from the campsonly,undergarduates,campers refuse to hire from outside their camp Mitigating Measure:Selection criteria will be clear with no chance for personal judgment taking into consideration the participation of the local communities as references, exit interviews 2.Difficulty in securing textbooks or printing them in Syria education materials Mitigating Measure: Contracting with printers in Turkey, communicating with interested donors 3.Delay in the disbursement Mitigating Measure: Teachers can work as volunteers since they were told that a delay may occur, SR can pay from its own fund until the disbursement is transferred 4.Attackson schools Mitigating Measure: Safety procedures,plans,awareness 5.Duplication when selecting the locations Mitigating Measure:coordinating with the Education Cluster and organizations 6.Dropout of children Mitigating Measure:Awareness to parents, back toschool campaigns and mobilize the community
