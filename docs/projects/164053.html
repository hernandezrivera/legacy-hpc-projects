Project Summary

1. Support to water, sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems to ensure regular services for affected people in Syria.
1.1. Repair and rehabilitation of water systems.
- New HH connections and pipeline repair
- Rehabilitation of water stations including installation of water treatment
1.2. Support to water quality assurance and operation and maintenance of water supply systems
- Operational and maintenance support to boreholes in camps (diesel, spare parts ,maintenance, operators)
- Water safety planning, water quality surveillance and distribution of aqua tabs
- Support with operation and maintenance costs to water stations including training to operatives
- Support to water committees with establishment of cost recovery mechanisms
1.3 Repair, rehabilitation and operational support to sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems
- Sewer network rehabilitations and new connections
- Latrine installation, establishment and support of cleaning groups
- Regular solid waste removal activities within camps and informal settlements, including training and support to local councils
- Latrine de-sludging service support
- Construction of waste water treatment facilities
2. Deliver humanitarian WASH supplies, services and improve hygienic behavior and practices of most vulnerable people
2.1. Distribution of essential WASH NFIs & Hygiene promotion
- Hygiene promotion activities with communities in coordination with civil society organizations.
- Provision of WASH NFIs to the most vulnerable
2.2. Improved access to humanitarian lifesaving/ emergency WASH facilities and services
- Water provision via water tankers to clusters quantity and quality standards

4 - Needs

There are 10.7 million people in need of emergency WASH in Syria. Access to sufficient and affordable safe water, adequate sanitation, solid waste management and/or hygiene supplies remain a challenge for newly displaced populations. Disruptions of services in IDP sites and their quality will have direct negative impact on people’s physical and mental well-being as well as protection consequences, specifically for women and girls. Countrywide, 42 per cent of the population is relying on alternative and often unsafe water sources to meet or complement their water needs, and in extreme case of Idleb up to 85 per cent . Communities not served with public water networks are in general at higher risk of unsafe water consumption, use less water than people well served via free/low cost water networks and spend significantly more of their incomes on purchasing water. At least 80 per cent of sewage is discharged untreated while at least half of the sewerage systems are not functional, 23 per cent of garbage is inappropriately disposed within communities . Such factors pose significant public health risks and have negative cascade effects on communities in terms of public health, inequality and poverty. The FAST consortium conducted an RNA in Idleb and Aleppo between the 19th and 21st of January. Interviews were undertaken with HHs and key informants. 88% of respondents said that water trucking was their primary source of water and only 1% of these stated that the water was treated. WASH NFIs, water and latrines were stated as the top 3 priority concerns. 47% stated that their latrine was not functional and only 12% of latrines are gender separated, which gives rise to sever protection concerns. 91% of HHs stated that handwashing stations were not functional or lacked soap and water. 85% of respondents stated that open defecation was an issue due to a lack of latrines and individuals not feeling safe to use them.

5 - Implementation of activities

Selection and validation tools will identify target populations and their priority needs. Beneficiary lists will be coordinated with local councils and the field team will verify HHs via assessments. Responses will place emphasis on vulnerable households (persons with disabilities, elderly PLW etc). Registration will identify beneficiary’s needs and the response given will be appropriate. Projects will be implemented either direct or via partner implementation. Staff and partners will be selected who have experience in WASH for the targeted communities in Syria and will consist of engineers, assistants and hygiene promoters which build better acceptance from the community and increase the understanding for the essential needs of those communities. Technical teams will prepare all feasibility studies, technical studies, BoQs, schematics and all other needed documents. Contractors will be required to undergo a tendering process to ensure high quality and value for all project work. WASH staff will work closely with local authorities and community leaders to make sure the projects impact on the actual needs of the communities but also fit unique and individual requirements where needed. Where required designs will take into account disability access and use and will ensure needs specific to genders are met. Volunteers will be hired for implementing some works inside the camps such as hygiene promotion activities, garbage collection and latrines cleaning.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks- WVI is not able to mitigate risks around shelling and airstrikes. However, WVI will assess the capacity of partners and advise appropriately to protect their staff and ensure that people receive assistance. - Limited monitoring by WVI’s 3PM. - Restriction of enumerators access to areas - Changes in the areas of control between Turkey, GoS or OAGs. - Temporary closure of borders for shipments either Turkey or within Syria, may disrupt humanitarian response. - Potential diversion of supplies - Damage of supplies due to transport and handling. - In case there is a military operation and a resultant escalation of conflict, there may be large displacement of people in need of assistance. - Climate conditions may exasperate flooding or difficult road conditions. - Local markets can become depleted Mitigation Measures: - WVI has a vast experience in delivering services and supplies in conflict situations, especially areas under the control of armed groups. - WVI’s partners are equipped with warehouses in strategic locations and have strategic partnership with local councils. - Partners will provide monitoring plans with specific operational context. - Third party monitoring visits will monitor the progress. - Implementing partners will provide situational feedback. - WVI’s partners are advised to closely monitor utilization of supplies and to monitor what is out of stock in local markets. - WVI and its partners carry out continuous security assessments drawing from multiple security sources including OCHA and has a dedicated security and context analysis team. - WVI will advise its partners to train field staff in security awareness and management. - Partner staff “lock down”, restricted movements or suspension of activities can be required- Partners will liaise with local councils and obtain all necessary approvals prior to commencement of activities.
