Project Summary


HIHFAD seeks to continue integrating nutrition-related activities into the healthcare facilities it is currently operating through screen all children under 5 years and PLW who visit the clinics for nutrition services or other services (RH, or IMCI clinics, etc.), select beneficiaries through MUAC screening and/ or Weight for Height/ Weight for Length (Z-score), provide SAM treatment for children with complicated SAM using therapeutic milks (for use exclusively in hospitalized patients) f75, F100, and RTUF beside clinical management of common complications, provide SAM treatment for children with uncomplicated SAM using Plumpy nut and MAM treatment for children and PLW with MAM using Plumpy sup, provide micronutrient supplementation to pregnant women and children, Provide one-on-one, and one to group counseling on WHO-recommended (IYCF) and maternal nutrition practices, disseminate IYCF messages.
As well as HIHFAD will conduct training to build capacity of frontline nutrition services providers on IYCF and CMAM programs in the context of COVID-19. HIHFAD will receive the needed nutrition supplies for this project from UNICEF through PAC organization
This project will target 4 subdistricts: Afrin, Armanaz, Qourqeena, Dana
the scope of services include: 4 fixed CMAM center within the health facilities :Atmeh pediatric and maternity hospital: ITP, OTP, TSFP, IYCF promotion and micronutrient interventions, Armanaz hospital, Kalbeet PHC, Kafr Arouk PHC: OTP, TSFP, IYCF promotion and micronutrient interventions
the project will provide the appropriate nutrition services to all age and sex groups; boys and girls, women, men, with attention to people with special needs, without any sexual discrimination, among IDPs and host communities, while ensuring that inclusion is mainstreamed throughout these activities to avoid exclusion of the most vulnerable populations such as those with disabilities targeting IDPs , Host communities including PLWs, children under 5 ,caregivers and men , the number of targeted children under 5 years is 53748 and 23890 PLWs.
Project data is collected on a monthly basis from the field, and reviewed by technical and administrative staff to ensure quality control, adherence to the project proposal and sent as 4Ws for Nutrition Cluster by monthly basis, also HIHFAD follows the financial and administrative guidelinesfor financial control.
HIHFAD's M&E team will monitor these project activities based on the agreed log-frame, A detailed monitoring plan will be prepared according to the final version of the log-frame, HiHFAD will ensure its MEAL plan to be implemented fully compliant with ‘Do No Harm’ principles. The MEAL team will be guided and equipped with protection-sensitive interview skills and approaches in order not to damage the dignity of the beneficiaries. The MEAL plan will apply the stratified sampling method in order to obtain sex and gender-segregated data, ensuring the equal participation of women and youth who are quite often under-represented in Syria response.
Gender and age-appropriate survey tools and methods will be developed and the MEAL team will ensure appropriate gender-balance among its staff, ensuring its access to both gender groups. Throughout the project, the beneficiaries are encouraged to provide their negative or honest feedbacks in order to improve the project performance as well as to collect lessons learned for future planning and their feedbacks will be incorporated during the review exercises.
The monitoring and evaluation team will implement the following plan:
1- Field visits: to observe the overall progress of the project and compare the work-plan with exact implementation.
2- Monthly reports: To check programmatic/financial progress, identify obstacles
3- Patient Exit Interviews via patients, parents and companion feedbacks. (Monthly)
4. Receiving and reviewing all complaints with project focal point through the WhatsApp complaint line and the complaints box

4 - Needs

Children with acute malnutrition are exposed to greater morbidity and mortality risks leading not only to wasting but to sub-optimal physical and cognitive development. With regard to chronic malnutrition physical growth in children is delayed leading to stunting while brain development is adversely affected leading to problems including, poor cognitive skills and poor academic performance. Additionally, the immune system is underdeveloped leaving children susceptible to illness. Micronutrient deficiency is another major problem with a notable consequence of iron-deficiency anemia which contributes to cognitive impairment and can accelerate stunting in children. Stunting is irreversible with long-term social and economic consequences causing limitations on educational achievement and employment opportunities in later life. Children under5 and PLW are the most susceptible to malnutrition since their nutritional requirements are relatively high. MAM in women has deleterious effects on the health of the mother and pregnancy outcomes including low birth weight infants(less than 2.5KG)for instance. Stunting in children is linked to poor infant and young child feeding(IYCF)practices which are crucial to child survival and health. According to the SMART Survey(NWS 2021in NWS IYCF practices were found to be sub-optimal. In children(6-59 months)for example, only10.8% consumed the minimal acceptable diet (MAD) while only 53.4% of infants (0-5 months) were exclusively breastfed HNO 2022

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Nutrition is a human right and underpins success in education, poverty alleviation, and the empowerment of women and girls. It is critical that nutrition programs in Syria assess, plan, and implement nutrition interventions with a protection lens and take a Do No Harm approach to ensure affected populations are protected from risks associated with the conflict in Syria as well as avoid unintended adverse effects of nutrition assistance. Therefore, the nutrition sector will carry out both sectoral and project level protection risk analyses to demonstrate thoughtful and safe mitigation measures in all planned nutrition interventions. These protection risks include: • Violence against health and nutrition facilities and health and nutrition providers • Gender Based Violence • Child Protection • Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) • Provision of and equitable access to nutrition services for vulnerable groups • Conflict Sensitivity • Protection of Humanitarian staff, volunteers and associated personnel • Nutrition Data protection • Exposive Hazard Considerations


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Considering the high vulnerability of the project beneficiaries, HIHFAD provides allthe hired staff an induction training on the protection principles, PSEA and GBV sothey become oriented enough to provide the services in a dignified and safe wayfor the beneficiaries. A focal point for PSEA and GBV would be appointed perfacility. In addition, the services will be provided on a free-of-charge basis for allthose in need regardless of their individual gender groupings. HIHFAD will ensurethe privacy of the patients, where special attention will be paid to ensure theavailability of safe and comfortable examination settings that supports thepatient’s privacy. Furthermore, sex-separated toilets and breastfeeding corners willbe ensured available as well. HIHFAD will make sure that the individuals ofvulnerable groups are always accompanied by either their own caregivers or by predefined facility staff, so such individuals are additionally protected against anytype of harassment or abuse while at the supported facilities. The facilities will beensured to be well lighted and ventilated and the electric outlets will be renderedeither high enough or provided with protecting means, so they are inaccessible tochildren
