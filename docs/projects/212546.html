Project Summary

Based on HNO 2024, The February earthquake increased the PiN, compared to last year. There are more than 2.4549 million out-of-school children (OoSC) (46% girls, 54%boys) and over a million who are at risk of dropping out. The governorates with the highest percentages of OoSC include Idleb (69%), and Aleppo (38% governorates). Also, Education for IDPs in IDP sites remains challenging; 57 percent of households in sites indicated that more schools need to be opened to improve education for children aged 5-17 years old.
The education infrastructure was already insufficient before the earthquakes. An estimated 3,700 schools need rehabilitation/repairs. There are 1,054 schools damaged in northwest Syria. Availability of schools is further reduced with sudden onsets of displacements when schools are used as temporary shelters in some areas.
For children with disabilities, barriers to education arise from the physical inaccessibility of schools, lack of school facilities, lack of specialized teaching and learning opportunities, and a non-inclusive culture in schools.
MWL's proposed project aims to establish protective education environments for vulnerable children (both boys and girls) across four sub-districts: Jebel Saman, Afrin, Salqin, and Armanaz. The primary objective is to ensure the expansion of ongoing education services by providing inclusive protective learning spaces. This initiative aims to prevent children from being absent or dropping out of school due to inadequate learning facilities, materials, or financial constraints faced by both the Education Directorate and parents.
The project targets a total of 12,8500 (6200 boys,6200 Girls, 200 men, 250 women), including 600 children with disabilities. Among these beneficiaries,450 teachers, and 4,600 students (Boys: 2300, Girls: 2300) will be enrolled in formal education programs, while 7,000 students (Boys: 3500, Girls: 3500) will receive support through non-formal education initiatives. Additionally, 300 (Boys: 150, Girls: 150)adolescents and youth will participate in Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET) programs. The project also provides an Early Childhood Care and Education (ECCE) program tailored for 500 (Boys: 250, Girls: 250)children aged 3-5 years old. The project strategy adheres to Education in Emergencies (EiE) standards and underscores the importance of inclusivity by focusing on the rehabilitation of schools, provision of necessary equipment, and conducting awareness-raising activities to ensure the participation of children with disabilities.
The key strategies include:
- Rehabilitating 110 classrooms in targeted schools and learning spaces to accommodate more internally displaced students.
- Establishing 10 temporary learning spaces using pre-fabs, rented rooms, or lightly rehabilitated facilities.
- Providing school furniture and students' seating to enhance learning environments.
- Training 450 targeted teachers and administrative staff in classroom management and modern teaching methods.
- Supplying stationery, teaching materials, textbooks, and student kits.
- Implementing government curricula for formal education and alternative curricula for non-formal education programs.
- Offering incentives for teachers and administrative staff.
- Establishing Parents Teachers Associations (PTAs) to oversee student education and address issues.
- Conducting awareness sessions for students in targeted schools and temporary learning spaces.
- PSEA awareness activities for students, teachers, and education personnel.
- Providing technical and vocational education and training (TVET) to 300 adolescents and youth, focusing on out-of-school children and those dropped for more than one year. Recruit 15 teachers to provide the technical training based on context and needed training.
- Provide early childhood evidence-based programming targeting 500 children from 3-5 years old. Recruit, train, and fairly 50 teachers & supervisors for the ECD program.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The MEAL plan is aligned with the project's log frame. Quarterly, Protection Quality and Safety Centers Assessments will be conducted using a checklist to ensure that centers provide a secure environment for children. Distribution processes will be monitored through questionnaires. Monthly satisfaction surveys will collect feedback on implemented activities, sampled at 20% of project participants.MWL emphasizes accountability and ensures community involvement through transparent communication via email, child-friendly complaint boxes, WhatsApp, Telegram, and face-to-face interactions. MEAL integrates accountability into all tools, fostering participant engagement in project design and implementation. Protection Risk Analysis:-Violence against education facilities and providers:Threats: Attacks on education facilities, personnel, or students.Mitigation:Create safer spaces within facilities.Establish safety protocols and regularly practice them.Inform parents/caregivers of safety protocols.-Violence, physical punishment, or bullying in schools:Threats: Physical punishment, violence, bullying by teachers, students, or other personnel.Mitigation:Enforce a code of conduct against violence and bullying.Train staff in prevention/reporting and positive discipline.Reduce pressure on teachers, provide remuneration, and monitor the application of skills.Implement psychosocial support and referral systems.-Insufficiently safe learning facilities:Threats: Structurally unsafe facilities, lack of protection from elements, or public health risks.Mitigation:Improve facility safety.Provide and maintain sufficient water and sanitation facilities.Ensure facilities are well-lit, clean, and maintained.Implement safety protocols and provide necessary resources. PSEA strategy:To this end, MWL will conduct:-Mandatory refresher training for all teachers and educational personnel on child safeguarding and PSEAH.-Signing PSEAH and child safeguarding policies and committing to its articles by all staff members.-Designating a Safeguarding & PSEAH Focal Point.-Conducting PSEAH awareness sessions for all project participants.- Active membership of the PSEAH Network.-SEA reporting channels will be described and made available.-Feedback mechanism in a place accessible for all to ensure effective participation by the affected persons will be by getting their feedback via several channels. Risk Assumptions & Mitigation:-Operational Risks: Delay in implementation activities. Mitigation involves regular follow-up to the work plan.-Exclusion of Persons with Disabilities (PWD) due to lack of access: Capacity building for staff, installation of PWD-friendly facilities, and provision of ramps to ensure inclusivity.-Dropping out of girls: Mitigation includes providing separate toilets for girls, ensuring female staff availability, and addressing the need for a safe environment and non-discrimination.
