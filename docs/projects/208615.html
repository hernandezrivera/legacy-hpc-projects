Project Summary

Thematic Envelope 1: Underfunded Sector Area-Based Protection Response
In the face of escalating protection concerns amongst women and girls in North East Nigeria, and the limited resources available to respond to these concerns which have continued to make women and girls more vulnerable, COOPI Nigeria, in Partnership with Jireh Doo Foundation (JDF), is pioneering an innovative and transformative multisectoral initiative to ensure women and girls rights are no longer violated. This approach will focus on Promoting the rights of women and girls through the establishment of a Hybrid Safe space in Jakusko Local government area, while providing outreach services to parts of Bade Local Government Areas (LGAs) of Yobe State. This is in alignment with the Thematic Envelope 1: Underfunded Sector Area-Based Protection Response. Through this hybrid safe spaces, COOPI/JDF will implement multi-sectorial innovations that directly targets the identified needs thereby bringing life-saving protection support to women and girls in Jakusko and parts of Bade LGA Yobe state.
novative Multi-sectoral Response for Enhanced Community Well-being: Hybrid Safe space: A Holistic Haven for Well-being
COOPI will establish a Hybrid Safe space which represents a sustainable and comprehensive approach to transformative well-being for vulnerable women, girls, and children. This initiative seamlessly integrates components such as Child protection initiatives, GBV prevention and response, Capacity strengthening and empowerment, Nutrition Program and WASH Infrastructure and services. This holistic framework aims to foster community-led well-being and resilience.
Key Components of the Hybrid Safe Space:
COOPI Nigeria will ensure that women, girls, and younger children receive psychosocial support services (PSS), Case management, access to quality education, and skill-building activities through the established Hybrid safe space which is designed to ensure that women, and girls are able to participate in Key protection activities while their children/wards access same in the same premises. This approach therefore ensures that women and girls are not discouraged from accessing life-saving protection services because of the responsibility of caring for their children or wards. As part of the activities to be implemented in the Hybrid safe Space, JDF will implement a Comprehensive and complementary nutrition and WASH activities, with the aim of addressing other strategic needs of women and girls, thereby reducing their total vulnerability and reduce the options of negative coping mechanisms.
Joint capacity strengthening:
The consortium adopts a joint capacity strengthening with community stakeholders across the sectors of GBV, Nutrition and WASH to to enhance effective collaboration among and promote joint response to individual and community needs in the project locations. Joint capacity strengthening will also encourage collaboration at the cluster working groups through case conferencing in mitigating identified concerns in the communities that may require a muti-faceted approach..
Through this initiative COOPI and JDF will be targeting the same beneficiaries across the same locations thereby providing life saving multi-sectoral responses while meeting the Protection needs of women and girls, thereby ensuring value for money. Adhering to eco-friendly standards, partners will collaborate to plant trees in the safe space, promoting a sustainable and resilient environment. To strengthen the collaboration, an internal referral system will be established at the inception phase of the project with stakeholders such that women and girls with WASH or Nutrition needs identified during protection activities can be referred to JDF to prioritize them for either WASH or nutrition interventions as the case may be and vice versa.

1 - Needs

Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) - December 2023: A three-pronged approach was adopted for the needs assessment conducted in December 2023 to inform the design of this proposed action. This included two levels of primary data collection (survey led multi-sectoral needs assessment with Key Informant Interviews, household surveys and Focus Group Discussions) and secondary review of various data sources and previous relevant assessments, lessons learned of COOPI and JDF The multi sectoral needs assessment was carried out to understand the humanitarian needs across Geidam, Yunusari, Jakusko, Bade, Yusufari and Fune LGAs of Yobe State. The assessment aimed to understand the current humanitarian situation and provide guidance for response action in the sectors of Nutrition, WASH, and Protection. The consortium hired 24 enumerators (13 female, 12 male) who were trained to support data collection through quantitative survey interviews and qualitative key informant and focused group discussions. The data collected were from primary sources: men, women heads of households, community leaders, women leaders, youth and the Local Government Community Development Officers. A total of 600 (270 female, 330 male) respondents were interviewed across the 6 LGAs assessed. The MSNA concurred the UN-OCHA situation reports in Yobe State, highlighting the following findingsThe respondents revealed that some services like health are available, however accessing them was expensive due to cost of services and transport, thus their need for support, health and nutrition awareness was also relatively poor. Across the 6 assessed LGAs, protection risks are aggravated by high cost of living, high inflation rate, low farm yields, insecurity within the communities, limited opportunities for development and self-reliance for families, and inadequate access to health, food assistance and livelihood services especially for those in rural areas. Incidents of GBV have been reported, specifically sexual exploitation/violence. Safety risks are related to fear of abduction, trafficking, recruitment attack. To mitigate against these increased risks particularly for women and girls, it is vital to mainstream general protection across the intervention.55% of the responses across the assessed LGAs indicate that adolescent girls are the main target of the GBV. However, only 9% across the assessed LGAs believed that GBV survivors go to the health centers/clinic immediately for help/treatment, while 25% of the responses in Fune and Jakusko shows that the survivors first go to the relevant government agencies for help. Majority of the respondents in Jakusko and Geidam do not know where the survivors go for help.71% of the respondents across the assessed locations indicated that mental illness/instability is common among people in their communities which cuts across five LGAs except in Yunusari with 40% of the response. Majority believed the caused is by spirit possession, while some indicates crisis, loss of livelihood, and no source of food among others as being the caused for the mental disorder for the people in their communitiesOn informal network for women and girls, 52% of the responses across the assessed LGAs are not aware if girls and women have access to nets on protection especially in Fune (70%), Bade (65%), Geidam (56%) and Jakusko (51%).83% of the responses indicate that people across the assessed LGAs go to health facilities when they are sick with figures being highest in Yunusari and Jakusko. 8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Returnees and internally displaced individuals including women and girls, residing in camps and vulnerable host communities often face isolation, being separated from their communities and families. The negative impact of displacement on household economies often affects their decision to seek support and care when needed. For women and adolescent girls, the impact of the crisis often causes some to turn towards negative coping mechanisms, which increases their exposure to sexual exploitation and abuse and other trauma related concerns. The Consortium will integrate the basic principles of protection mainstreaming (meaningful access to assistance and services, prioritizing safety, dignity and doing no harm, accountability and participation, and empowerment of beneficiaries) into service delivery. The Consortium will ensure that services will be provided at no cost to the IDPs and vulnerable host communities to mitigate against use of negative coping mechanism and lack of access to basic services. Training will be conducted for both program staff and community volunteers on protection and safeguarding to strengthen and promote a shared understanding of protection mainstreaming, including Child Safeguarding principles and Adult Safeguarding; prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse and Harassment (PSEAH). Specific attention will be paid to the assistance offered to GBV survivors in terms of protection and mental health care services, priority will be given to their safety and strive to minimize the negative impacts of the project on the local fabric.

In addition, the project will capitalize on the existing accountability units within the MEAL department to ensure that participation, feedback, and complaints mechanisms are integrated in all strategies, plans and program and provides a range of rapid and accessible channels of communication with affected populations. Implement a continuous feedback mechanism to gather insights from participants, mentors, and community stakeholders. Organize feedback sessions and focus group discussions at regular intervals to address concerns and adapt strategies based on real-time input. In addition to the United Nations Organizations toll-free lines, the project will build on the findings from previous humanitarian accountability assessments conducted in the target locations to determine communities’ preference on the type of feedback mechanism to put in place, prior to the set-up/strengthening of the mechanisms for the project. The feedback / complaints whether negative or positive will be timely addressed in a confidential manner and will inform corrective measures in the project. This is done through dedicated Gender and Protection/CFM Assistants whose responsibility will be collecting and responding to complaints and feedback. The project would ensure that its staff adhere to the principles of “Do No Harm” and as such will periodically evaluate actions of the project (including staff and community members) to ensure that harm is not coming to any of the beneficiaries as a result of the project implementation.
