Project Summary

Through a multi sectoral approach, NID aims to ensure a timely provision of adequate access to safe water, sanitation and and increase hygiene to mitigate the risks associated with lack of clean water supply, sanitation facilities and appropriate usage and appropriate hygiene practices and knowledge. The project targets 50000 beneficiaries through rehabilitation and/or establishment of water points to increase access to clean water supply, increase access to sanitation facilities through rehabilitation of existing facilities, establishing/constructing new ones and encourage the beneficiary community to construct their own own household latrine through provision of latrine digging tool kits. In all activities hygiene messaging and promotion and promotion of good hygiene behaviours will cut across. Protection will also be mainstreamed across activities taking into consideration of Sexual Gender Based Violence (SGBV) Protection of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). NID staff will be trained on PSEA and child protection policies and sign and sign code of conduct to ensure accountability to the affected population. The beneficiaries will also be trained on reporting channels of SGBV and child protection. To ensure all these activities are implemented effectively, NID will work with the community (beneficiaries) to identify local people as pump mechanics, community hygiene promoters etc. This will ensure an exit strategy for the project hence continuity and resilience. In terms of food security and livelihood NID aims to improve the immediate food security needs of the targeted beneficiaries through provision of agricultural inputs (tools and seeds) and trainings. This will contribute to the medium and long term needs of the targeted beneficiaries. In all these activities protection will be mainstreamed across to ensure meaningful access and resilience.

1 - Needs

Since 2013, the conflict in South Sudan has continued to impact all parts of the country. In 2016, the conflict shifted drastically from the greater Upper Nile region of South Sudan to the Equatorias. This led to massive displacement of people as well as destruction of properties including institutions like schools and health facilities. Many school were vandalized affecting learning. Water and sanitation infrastructure were vandalized and agricultural activities came to a stand still. Additionally; many people have have either run for refuge or are displaced to urban centers affecting services in the rural areas of the country leading to widespread farming. Because of lack of access to clean water supply and sanitation, people are at risk of water related diseases as a result of the floods in the country and the floods have also destroyed farm lands which will reslt into wide spread famine. In particular, Yei, Morobu and Lainya counties are some of the worst areas affected by the conflict. The people who remained or the new ones that joined are poorly with other not affording two meals in a day. With the prospects of the peace agreement and expected formation of the Revitilized Transitional government of National Unity to be formed in November, many people have started returning to theirs areas only to find that their sources of livelihood and water points conpletely destroyed. This poses a threat of starting from scratch to abandon the teaching profession. This project seeks to support the vulnerable returnees, internally displaced person and vulnerable host community members. to cope up with the situation and integrate.

2 - Activities

The activities of the project include the followingNID aims to improve the immediate food security needs of the targeted beneficiaries through provision of agricultural inputs (tools and seeds) and trainings. This will contribute to the medium and long term needs of the targeted beneficiaries. This will be done by;1. Provision of agricultural tools like hand hoes, pangas, slashers, rakes etc. 2. Provision of vegetable kits3. Provision of cereal kits4. Provision of fishing kits (nets, twines, hooks).5. Training of the beneficiaries in vegetable growing, cereal crop production and fisheries toincrease knowledge on preservation and storage for the lean season. 6. Training of extension workers within the community who will then in turn train the beneficiaries in the own firms.7. Establishment of firm field schools as practical training farms for modern agricultural practices for the farmers. 8. Where applicable, irrigation facilities (treadle pumps and watering cans will be provided for the farmers to ensure vegetable production in the off season. Farmers will be also be trained on local methods of irrigation specifically drip irrigations using local bottles and broken Jerri-cans to encourage off season vegetable growing.9. Establishment of fish farming especially in Yei and Lainya which have seasonal wetlands that can sustain fish pounds in the dry season. This will ensure local production of fish for local consumption in the areas.10. Where schools exist, the project will be lined with education and in this regards school gardening will be established to train the school children in the agricultural business. For WASH, the following activities will be done. 1. Training of pump mechanics, community hygiene promoters, water user committees, and community leaders. 2. Rehabilitation and/or establishment of water points. 3. Establishment and/or rehabilitation of sanitation facilities. 4. Water quality monitoring. 5. Hygiene promotion activities. 6. Establishment and training of school hygiene clubs. 7. Routine project monitoring (PDM, Evaluation)

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes. The project takes into account the safety of beneficiaries by ensuring that the do no harm principle is implemented. Beneficiaries access to information and services are all taken into account and mitigation measure put in place in order to avoid exposure to danger associated with the above. NID takes protection of its beneficiaries as paramount. Safe audits are always done to better inform the project on safety. The protection principles of DO NO HARM, accessibility are all considered most important for the baneficiaries.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes. As a way of sustainability, the project trains locals on ground so that at the completion, all these locally trained individuals continue to support the community and the beneficiaries. The project also will take into account the use of local materials so that there is sustainable repair and maintenance of structures in case of need for repairs. Through the provision of services such as water points, these items are used beyond the project period hence sustainable in the long run beyond the project. After completion, activities are handed over for the community to manage after the project period
