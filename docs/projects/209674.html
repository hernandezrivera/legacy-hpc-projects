Project Summary

The recent and ongoing conflict in Sudan has exacerbated already high levels of food insecurity, acute malnutrition, and stunting. Over 16.2 million individuals, amounting to over a third of the Sudanese population, were already acutely food insecure prior to the conflict, according to WFP’s Comprehensive Food Security and Vulnerability Assessment. According to the most recent Integrated Phase Classification released in August 2023, the number of acutely food insecure in Sudan (IPC Phase 3 and above) has increased to 20.3 million, amounting to over 42 percent of the country’s population. Driven by a protracted conflict, insecurity, and displacement, this is the highest number of acutely food insecure in recent history in Sudan. Over 5.6 million people have been newly displaced, of which nearly 4.5 million are newly displaced and over 1.1 million have sought refuge.
Among the refugees, the food security situation remains particularly dire. 56 percent of refugee households in Sudan were food insecure during the first quarter of 2023 according to the Food Security Monitoring System (FSMS), of which 11 percent are severely food insecure. This was an increase of 11 percent compared to 2022. The levels and severity of food insecurity has likely increased further due to the ongoing conflict, decreased access, and deteriorating macroeconomic situation. The refugees in Kharasana and Elmairam in West Kordofan are the most food insecure at over 95 percent. 31 percent of refugees rely on non-agricultural wage labour while 20 percent rely on informal transfers. 62 percent of refugees adopt negative food-based coping strategies, of which 29 percent are adopting a high level of coping mechanisms. The most commonly adopted food-based coping strategies includes relying on less preferred and less expensive food, limiting portion sizes and reducing the number of meals consumed per day. In addition, 45 percent of refugee households adopt negative livelihood-based coping strategies, of which 9 percent adopted emergency-level coping strategies. The most commonly adopted livelihood-based coping strategies include reducing non-food expenses on health, withdrawing children from school, and spending savings. Finally, economic vulnerability remains high among refugees, with 60 percent unable to afford one local food basket, and 92 percent spending more than 65 percent of their expenditure on food.
This project aims to support settlement-based refugees in Blue Nile, Central Darfur, East Darfur, Gadarif, Kassala, North Darfur, North Kordofan, South Darfur, South Kordofan, West Kordofan, and White Nile in Sudan to meet their basic food and nutrition needs through an integrated unconditional food assistance.
Amid a lack of availability of resources and access constraints, WFP Sudan is targeting with unconditional General Food Assistance (GFA) 705,260 refugees of whom 343,169 will receive life-saving nutritional support. GFA at Full ration of the in-kind food basked depending on resource availability, each recipient will receive 475g of sorghum per person per day, 60g pulses, 30g oil and 10g salt per person per day, meeting the daily minimum dietary requirements . Concerning nutritional support, both children under the age of five years and pregnant and breastfeeding women and girls will receive 100g per day of Plumpy Sup and 50 g per day of Plumpy Doz for treatment of moderate acute malnutrition and prevention of malnutrition respectively. In addition, children aged 6-59 months will receive 0.5g/day of micronutrient powders (MNPs) to prevent micronutrient deficiencies.
This shall be complemented by thorough engagement with Sudan's Commissioner for Refugees (COR), UNHCR, cooperating partners, community and customary leadership, and targeted locations to identify and sensitize beneficiaries to the anticipated assistance, with process and output monitoring undertaken at scheduled frequencies to assess the implementation of interventions on-ground.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

In alignment with the WFP Humanitarian Protection Policy, WFP remains committed to ensure that the assistance does no harm to the safety, dignity and integrity of the people it serves. The key objective is to improve the quality of food assistance programming by strengthening gender mainstreaming, protection, and accountability to affected population (APP) including Community Feedback Mechanism (CFM) as well as strengthen conflict sensitivity and conflict mainstreaming in programming. WFP has revamped its CFM to promote and ensure that we continue to hear the voices of beneficiaries through toll-free help lines. Furthermore, WFP has established robust digitalized help desks at over 75 percent of the ongoing food distribution sites. These infrastructure support community engagement activities to ensure two-way communication.Through dedicated senior conflict sensitivity advisor together with WFP protection and gender officers, WFP engages with communities, including through deployment of staff deep in the field during food distributions to understand the dynamics and challenges faced by our beneficiaries, particularly women, children and the marginalized.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

WFP deploys multiple approaches to share information and collect community feedback. This includes:1) Face to face with individuals (in homes) and at distribution sites (helpdesks) through regular interactions before, during and after food distributions. The feedback received is processed and are resolved either on the spot or through referrals to specific functional units withi WFP or to other mandated organizations.2) Through a functioning WFP toll-free hotline where beneficiaries can contact WFP directly WFP uses flyers and leaflets to raise awareness of the toll-free line (1460). Logged cases are handled in a secure and confidential. The received cases are safely managed in a corporate case management system, SugarCRM to ensuring data protection. 3) PSEA WFP uses a variety of approaches to enforce its zero-tolerance policy on SEA:• Developing the capacity of WFP and partner staff to receive, respond to, and escalate SEA cases.• Sudan-specific PSEA training manual developed to train WFP, CP, services providers, and community workers.• Ensure adequate capacity at the level of the CO and the area offices in support of PSEA efforts.• Enhance community awareness of rights, prohibited practices, and channels for reporting violations, which may include face-to-face dialogue and PSEA posters.• During partner selection, due diligence was performed to ensure PSEA policies are in place for each partner

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

WFP strives to promote inter-agency collaboration in Community Feedback Mechanism. WFP and UNHCR have an MOU to coordinate efforts and facilitate referral systems. WFP will advocate the expansion of this approach with other UN agencies. In addition, WFP works closely with the PSEA advisor and actively contributes to inter-agency forum such as PSEA network at national and state levels. Regular participation in these forums enhanced collaboration and coordination to strengthen WFP’s approach to PSEA throughout the project.
