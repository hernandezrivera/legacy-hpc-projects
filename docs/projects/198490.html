Project Summary

The overall project objective is that vulnerable youth refugees, IDPs, and host community members impacted by displacement crises in urban contexts have access to livelihood opportunities that safely and sustainably meet their needs and contribute positively to their communities. In order to prepare youth for the world of work, the program will provide a variety of employability support and employment readiness training, dependent on the needs and opportunities of each context. The project will deliver activities around three expected results to achieve the project objective; Youth participants acquire market-relevant skills and are prepared for self-employment; New and existing youth-owned businesses are supported to start and grow, Youth participants who are not able or interested in starting a business are prepared for alternative labor market opportunities such as vocational apprenticeships.
IRC will support urban-based host and displaced entrepreneurs to start up or expand home-based
businesses or existing micro and small enterprises (MSEs). Activities will target displacement-affected
youth (age 18-35) who are at increased risk of gender-based violence, exploitation, and
negative coping mechanisms. IRC will also specifically target economically vulnerable host and
displaced youth who have a strong interest in entrepreneurship and skills set to generate income
through self-employment. These youth and MSMEs typically face challenges with general
business and financial management, as well as accessing capital to support their businesses. The
program will support new and existing entrepreneurs to access finance, business development
services, and mentoring needed for their growth, and ultimately support the growing MSME
market in the northeast region and contribute to increased employment opportunities.
A total of 960 youths (540 in Borno state and 420 in Yobe State) will be targeted in this project to receive livelihood support under this intervention.

1 - Needs

As global humanitarian crises are leading to growing displacement and mass movements of populations, the vulnerable situation in which many displacement-affected populations find themselves is becoming less sustainable. As more and more populations flee conflict, disaster, and insecurity, internally displaced people (IDPs) are forgoing traditional camp-based resettlement settings in place of greater perceived economic and social opportunities in cities and urban areas. This situation is the same for crisis impacting northeast Nigeria where the decade-long conflict has led to constant primary and secondary displacements. Majority of the internally displaced people in the Northeast are now living outside of camps. Furthermore, displaced people often resettle in neighboring communities that are already struggling economically. Urban centers across all these contexts represent both opportunity and difficulty for vulnerable populations, in particular youth, wherewith sufficient access, knowledge, and support, economic integration is possible. Without due support, however, youth continue to struggle with some of the highest rates of unemployment, social stigmatization, and an inability to integrate positively in their new markets and communities.The project seeks to address issues related to youth in particular, as youth have continued to feel the brunt of the economic hardships across urban areas in Northeastern Nigeria. From previous intervention experience and data, youth often lack the skills, capital, networks, and confidence to safely and sustainably integrate into local labor markets and are continuously left out of conversations at policymaking levels in the community. Thus, vulnerable youth both IDPs, and host community members impacted by displacement crises in urban contexts will be supported to have access to livelihoods opportunities that safely and sustainably meet their needs and contribute positively to their households and communities.

2 - Activities

The project will deliver activities around three expected results in order to achieve the project objective:1- Youth participants acquire market-relevant skills and are prepared for self-employment. To prepare youth for self-employment, the program will provide a variety of entrepreneurship support and employment readiness trainings. Activities under this will include:+ Business and life skills training –The project will offer contextualized business and life skills training to local prospective youth entrepreneurs. Trainings will build participants’ knowledge in budgeting, business planning, as well as soft skills such as conflict resolution, time management, and customer service.+ Financial literacy training – Targeted trainings will improve financial literacy and knowledge of household/business finances to build skills in managing business profits, reinvesting income into the business, promoting savings, and sound budgeting practices.+ Agribusiness training - Recognizing that different market contexts demand a variety of goods and services, the project seeks diversify the types of entrepreneurs and businesses supported by introducing youth smallholder farmers to the world of agribusiness to encourage market-driven income generation. This strategy is also aligning with the Government of Nigeria’s national agenda encouraging youth participating in agricultural activities. IRC has already developed an agribusiness module that will be leveraged for this.2- New and existing youth-owned businesses are supported to start and grow - In many urban crisis areas, microenterprises make up a large part of local markets and economies. Supporting youth to start and grow their own businesses in local markets will be a key result of the proposed project. Activities under this include:+ Business start-up and growth grants – Youth interested in starting or growing microenterprises will develop business plans and apply for grant funding for their businesses. IRC will establish a committee to review applications for grants based on the strength of the business plan, potential for profitability, financial management skills, and applicant need.+ Continuous mentorship – Evidence has shown that youth-owned enterprises are 75% more likely to succeed if supported through mentorship and coaching. Various mentorship opportunities including 1:1 coaching, group mentoring, and peer-to-peer learning will be offered to youths. 3- Youth participants who are not able or interested in starting a business are prepared for alternative labor market opportunities.For some youth, starting and running a microenterprise may not be a viable option for safe and sustainable income generation. Beyond requisite business skills, support, and a strong business plan, business owners need an entrepreneurial mindset and motivation. Recognizing this reality and seeking to maximize labor market integration, the project will seek to support a small group of these youth to engage the labor market in alternative ways, including through:+ Job counselling – One common barrier for youth to gain access to the labor market is a lack of information about job opportunities and how to find and apply for them. Under this program, youth will have access to employment support services such as job matching, job search skills, CV skills, interview skills, and 1:1 job counselling to help break down the information barrier.+ Apprenticeships – On-the-job training is an essential component to preparing youth for employment in a hands-on, interactive, and concrete way. A small group of youth who do not seek to start a business will be matched with apprenticeship opportunities with local microenterprises in order to gain valuable learning experience while on the job, and paid a modest market-rate stipend to cover the opportunity costs of participation.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Feedback will be collected from the most vulnerable through physical conversation and the provision of feedback mechanisms in the community. The physical conversation would be through community members on KII conversation with their respective representatives and FGD with community focal groups. The Client Responsiveness Mechanism (CRM) of the IRC M&E unit will also be used to collate feedback while also leveraging on the IRC toll free line made available to the community members. Community structures such as community committees and community engagement meetings will also be put in place to ensure that there is an avenue to communicate and exchange information.
