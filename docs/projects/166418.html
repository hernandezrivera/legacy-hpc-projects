Project Summary

This project takes into consideration the gender dimensions of the impact of COVID-19 due to the increased burden of care, hygiene and sanitation needs this outbreak poses on women and girls. The result framework responds to the immediate and long-term consequence for women and girls of the current crisis by providing crucial GBV and WaSH services at a time when women’s risks are increased as most humanitarian actors decrease GBV service provision in favour of other health services.
The main objective of this intervention is to contribute to prevent the spread of COVID-19 in Monguno through early information sharing in different local languages while maintaining access to critical services through Improved hygiene practices and access to relevant and appropriate life-saving WaSH interventions for at least 4,000 individuals. Interventions focuses on increasing awareness on the COVID-19 symptoms and prevention through mass campaigns on messages including hand hygiene and social distancing, while expanding access to safe water and basic hygiene items including IPC materials and consumables.

1 - Needs

Nigeria has not been spared by the CoVID19 pandemic which has hit several countries around the world since the beginning of the year. This pandemic which has started in the SW part of the country has now gradually moved to the northeast, including the BAY states in general and Borno state specifically. This is more worrying considering the overcrowded IDPs camps established mainly in Borno state where the disease can easily spread and cause a lot of consequences. Moreover, due to its proximity with Cameroon where the CoViD19 is spreading also fast, Monguno LGA is among the most vulnerable LGAs.Furthermore, according to the WaSH services gap matrix, more than 9,000 individuals are lacking access to basic WaSh services such as safe water supply (less than 6 l/p/d in some wards), awareness on COVID-19 and basic hygiene items (more than 1545 HHs in need). Considering that access to adequate and sufficient WaSH services is essential to prevent COVID-19, this situation increase the vulnerability of IDPs in Monguno.

2 - Activities

To respond to the needs identified, hence reducing the risk of COVID-19 spread among vulnerable IDPs, various WaSh activities will be implemented.1. Strengthening the surveillance mechanism through establishment, training and regular follow-up of a COVID-19 committee comprising both women men communities' leaders. This committee will be briefed on COVID-19 symptoms and behaviors to adopt when someone in their community presents such symptoms.2. Increase water supply through water trucking (20 m3/daily) and rehabilitation/upgrading of at least 2 existing water points. Water monitors will be recruited from the target community then trained and equipped to ensure crowd control at water trucking distribution points promoting the respect of social distancing by users when collecting water, carry out water chlorination and control the FRC levels before allowing people to fetch water.3. Briefing of Boreholes' operators on COVID-19 mitigation measures at water points. They will be informed on key actions to be promoted at water points to minimize the risk of COVID-19 transmission between users when they fetch water.4. Provision and installation of 30 foot operated handwashing points at public places, regularly refilled with soap and water.5. Door to door COVID-19 awareness raising sessions to be conducted by 20 hygiene promoters (40% men and 60% women) to be sourced from target communities. All these hygiene promoters will be trained on animation techniques and how COVID-19 barriers gestures (especially social distancing from householders, no hand shaking, coughing etiqueetes...). They will be provided with refillable pocket hand sanitizer and encouraged to disinfect their before and after each HH visited.6. Provision of soap to 420 vulnerable HHs for hand washing and general personal hygiene. The soap will be, in priority, procured from the women groups already supported with soap making materials to strengthen their incomes and the viability of this livelihood mean.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

NCA will establish a CRM committee and complaints box in the project location. Regular FGDs will be organized with beneficiaries and their leaders to get their opinions about project activities and find appropriate solutions to the concerns they can raise. Beneficiaries will be informed, from the project onset, about their entitlements and how they claim their rights (various complaints collection means). Regular discussions will be maintained with the communities to evaluate the effectiveness and relevance of the complaints collection means so that appropriate readjustments can be made.
