Project Summary

The proposed project aims to provide urgent shelter and non-food item (NFI) assistance, including specific winter-season critical needs, in North West (NWS) and North East (NES) of Syria to almost 50,000 people targeted: newly displaced, protracted IDPs, returnees and hosting communities, according to their vulnerability. Under NFI sub sector, the response will target the distribution of new arrivals kits and kitchen kits (and top-up kits for both). For shelter sub-sector, sealing-off, partition and emergency shelter kits will be provided. To ensure sustainability, SI will intervene for the rehabilitation of public infrastructures and buildings. The strategy of intervention is designed on evidence-based needs taking into consideration this Partner’s long field presence in targeted areas and its strong community acceptance, humanitarian access and operational capacities. This Partner has four approaches to S-NFI in Syria:
(1) provide a sustainable and cost-eﬃcient shelter response for returnees with limited means of incomes whose house has light to medium-level damages;
(2) provide a sustainable and cost-eﬃcient shelter response to support communities where public buildings and infrastructure has been damaged;
(3) provide shelter kits, including the distribution of sealing-off, partition and emergency shelter kits to respond to ongoing urgent needs and winter-related needs; and
(4) provide NFI kits, including new arrival kits, kitchen kits (and top-up kits for both) to respond to ongoing urgent needs and winter-related needs

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2020 (draft) key figures, 5.65 million people require assistance in Shelter, 3.8 million (69%) are in areas of severity 4 or higher and 3.42 million in NFI. Geographically, the areas reporting the highest severity are concentrated in Idleb, Aleppo, Rural Damascus, Raqqa city and camps in the north-east. Up to one million IDPs reside in last-resort informal and spontaneous settlements. Concerning NFI, the needs in the sub sector remain acute. There is still ongoing displacement, with roughly one million people expected to be displaced in 2020. NWS: According to CCCM Cluster (19 Jan 2020) between Dec 2019 to mid-Jan 2020, 9,605 new IDPs arrived in Azaz, making 198,380 IDPs, the highest in Aleppo governorate. Of these, over 100,000 are in need of NFI and shelter in Azaz (severity level 4 for NFI and level 5 for shelter) where this Partner implements. These large-scale movements has led to critical levels of overcrowding, increasing pressure on camp management, local authorities and host communities. The main needs reported of all newly displaced persons are S-NFI, and as returns to Idlib are unlikely because of the security and protection risks and the border crossings into Turkey remain highly restricted. NES: According to OCHA figures, at 1 Jan 2020, 246,432 IDPs are reported in Raqqa, Hassakeh and Deir er Zor governorates. Out of these, 84,738 have been reported after the events of October 2019, as result of the violence. The ongoing volatility continues to put millions of people at risk of new/protracted and multiple displacement.The updated HNO 2020, shows that in north-east Syria ongoing displacement coupled with a lack of safe or adequate shelter, and explosive contamination is a significant obstacle. This lack of access to basic services increases the vulnerability of households which forces negative coping mechanisms, especially for female-headed households, households including youth, and those with functional difficulties.

5 - Implementation of activities

The partner strategy of intervention will focus on the following activities: i) Provision of core and essential NFIs; ii) Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs and shelter assistance iii) Support to sustainably repair/rehabilitate housing and public infrastructure and facilities. Activities will target newly displaced, protracted displacement, hosting communities and residents on the basis of vulnerability analysis. Priority will be ensured to the most vulnerable and in accordance with Gender and Protection mainstreaming related issues. The distribution of NFI and Shelter kits include first-round distribution and top up. Concerning modality of intervention, the feasibility and pertinence of the available modalities, in-kind, cash and/or vouchers modalities, will be defined case by case. This Partner aims to include local communities and local authorities as much as possible to foster a sense of self-autonomy. The scope of repairs for private housing will be limited to ensuring the structural integrity of the building and providing adequate covered living space; for public buildings a holistic neighborhood-based approach will be applied covering critical WASH and Health infrastructure. Thanks to the past lessons learnt, the partner disposes of selected pre-identified referenced contractors. Daily monitoring, regular visits by the field team and by the partner technical management staff will be ensured all along the implementation period. This partner will follow Working Group selection and vulnerability criteria guidelines. In NES and NWS both, this Partner’s SNFI intervention also focuses on responding to emergency needs, including the provision of shelter kits in IDP sites, including sealing-off kits and emergency shelter kits, to address acute shelter needs in camps and informal settlements; both in response to ﬁrst phase displacement, and prolonged sub-standard shelter situation.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The key protection risks associated are in line with the Protection Risk Assessments/Analysis Matrix. For the provision of NFIs, there are risks related to increased risks of inter- and intra-community tensions, abuse of authority by implementing actors, risk of physical harm during distribution, risk of intervention by military and non-humanitarian actors, challenges regarding documentation, risks of corruption, over-crowding leading to GBV and other protection issues, evictions and forced removal of IDPs, safety of beneficiaries in unsafe conditions, access restrictions for this Partner to areas in need, coordination challenges leading to gaps or duplication in assistance, risk of dependency on humanitarian aid if markets or income-generating opportunities remain restricted, and risk of creating spontaneous new settlements without access to necessary services or support. Mitigation measures include but is not limited to: following standardised procedures of selecting beneficiaries, (blanket distributions or following sector/cluster guidance); consultation and communication with community (women and girls, people with disabilities and older people) regarding all activities to ensure the specific needs, risks and considerations are applied to activity planning and implementation; establishing and communicating clear and safe distribution plans to communities; develop a clear distribution route and plan after safety assessment; ensure strong coordination, acceptance and partnership with relevant authorities; provide information to beneficiaries about their right to receive assistance; sufficient training to all staff involved in distributions; develop strong monitoring and feedback mechanisms that is accessible for all beneficiaries; establish strong links with local authorities and other humanitarian actors to promote equity and reduce gaps/overlaps; ensure proper registration for all involved in programs; and always consider specific risks/needs of women and girls

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The main risks include but are not limited to: impact of the UN cross-border resolution on humanitarian access, cross-line humanitarian access is further restricted; authorities in areas of intervention do not allow cross-border or internal access; registration delays or refusal by GoS for INGOs in receiving authorisation to work; local authorities’ control and monitoring of humanitarian assistance jeopardizes the ability to respect core humanitarian principles; border closures in neighboring countries inhibit the flow of good or personnel; escalation of conflict; shift of frontlines; potential conscription measures coming into effect that would impact the work and movement of national staff in areas under SDF control. Assumptions for this project include but are not limited to: humanitarian workers and programs are not directly targeted; dormant cells from radical groups preventing access to the intervention area are not activated; the security situation does not deteriorate; cross-border humanitarian access and intervention continue to be permitted and feasible; borders remain open with neighbouring countries; market prices, inflation rates and exchange rates remain stable and accessible. Mitigation measures include but are not limited to: providing support to advocacy efforts by the existing coordination forum at national and international level if cross-border implementation is hindered; continue the existing partnership with national NGOs as well as identifying new potential partners, in tandem with launching the registration process with GoS in Damascus and identifying alternative solutions to completing activities in case of delays in the registration process; maintain strong relationships with local authorities to ensure acceptance, information sharing and strong communication channels between parties; monitor the context closely, continual updating of internal contingency planning; monitor security context closely and ensure continual security plan update
