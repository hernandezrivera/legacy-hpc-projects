Project Summary

IOM’s Child Protection Project in 2021 aims to continue to prevent and respond to child protection issues and empower vulnerable girls, boys, parents and caregivers in camps and in the host community by strengthening the protective environment.
To achieve the objectives IOM’s activities will focus on enhancing community-based protection including capacity building, engagement, awareness raising and promoting participation by supporting community-led activities; specialized child protection services, such as case management, structured PSS for children and MHPSS for parents and caregivers, supporting child survivors of GBV in the camps and in the safe shelter; strengthening Community-based Child Protection Mechanisms and capacity building of child protection duty bearers in local government level (Union, Wards).
IOM CP programming incorporates age, gender, and diversity needs of the affected population to respond to specific needs, especially to enable girls and boys, children and adolescents, children with disabilities to be instrumental in improving the quality of their life and mitigating risks and dangers in their community and living environment.
Services will be provided through implementing partner Terre des hommes (Tdh) in camp 13, 20, 20 extension, in the host community in Ukhia/Palong Khali, IOM directly implement in camp 9 and 23, in Ukhia (Ratnapulong) and Teknaf (Nhila, Sabrang) and in camp 7,9,17, 18, 20 extension, 23 in the Child Friendly Corners of the Women and Girls Safe Spaces.
With a cross-cutting approach IOM CP closely cooperates with other sectors (Health/MHPSS, Shelter, WASH, Site Management, Transition and Recovery Division ) to mainstream child protection, disability, to coordinate disaster preparedness and emergency response and to strengthen the PSEA and Community Feedback Mechanisms to ensure accountability for the affected population.
IOM CP is committed to child protection inter-agency efforts and contributes to the CP Sub-Sector, CP Case Management Technical Working Group, MHPSS Working Group and PSEA Network.

1 - Identified Needs

Entering the fourth year of the Rohingya’s humanitarian situation in Bangladesh, the needs of the refugee and host community population remains dire. The estimated number of 862,277 Rohingya refugees who have fled systematic violence in Myanmar settled in the camp and around host communities in Cox’s Bazar. Children represent 52 per cent of the refugee population. The environment remained congested coupled with the socio-economic disadvantages such as restriction of movement, lack of livelihood and educational opportunities, escalating safety and security issues, environmental hazards including cyclone and monsoon conditions, and the COVID pandemic all has contributed to a rapid increase the vulnerability of children to violence, neglect, exploitation and abuse. Due to the prolonged period of uncertainty about the situation of Rohingya community, the physically and mentally challenging living environment, new protection risks emerged and further needs has been identified.Furthermore, as a consequence to the preventive measures of Government of Bangladesh to address the COVID pandemic, the protection environment weakened due to suspension of non-essential humanitarian support, decreased the presence of protection staff by 50%, shutting down of educational, child protection and GBV facilities, restricting face-to-face interaction with children and families. Children,adolescents, caregivers (including elderly caregivers) were negatively impacted by the closure of facilities, children wellbeing affected due to loss of daily routines and lockdown measures, psychosocial distress, child marriage, child labour, violence against children, child trafficking, intimate partner violence and GBV has increased. In the same time access to services (including protection) has been limited due to reduced humanitarian presence, which decreased the ability of quick identification and timely response for child protection cases and the quality of intervention declined. Based on the researches, adolescents boys and girls (including Hijra children and children with disabilities) remain particular vulnerable and lacking access to services. The potential long-term impacts for girls dropping out from education and other PSS / life skills activities is less likely they would return or continue to engage but staying in the shelter, doing household chores and getting married earlier. Over 30% of male adolescents and 5% of female adolescents reported attending a CFSs, Adolescent Friendly Space, or Multiple Purpose Child and Adolescent Center on a daily basis. For the boys, they are most likely to continue to engage in child labour and illegal work. Marriage continues to be the primary means of attaining social and economic security for Rohingya women and girls, who are traditionally encouraged focusing on caring about the family and house chores. Married girls are at increased risk of a range of physical and psychological consequences including child labour, early pregnancy intimate partner violence.For prevention and responding to child protection concerns, the focus remains in community-based child protection to promote inclusion, participation, meaningful access to services in a dignified way and for a safe transition of sustainable protection mechanism. Capacity building, empowerment of refugee community and religious leaders, volunteers as well as children/ adolescents to enable them for a greater participation in decision making concerning their life situations; strengthening community structures are primary goals to achieve a safer environment for children. Community-led awareness raising and delivering key messages through community members targets a wider audience to reach high-risk adolescents and their families. Life-saving individual case management services and MHPSS activities need to be increased to address current high volume of protection concerns.

2 - Activities or outputs

1. IOM will strengthen Community-based Child Protection (CBCP) Mechanism in the camp and host community to enhance the capacity of families and communities to care for and protect children with the participation and inclusion of children and youth. Support will be provided to CBCP Committees, Adolescents’ (age 14-17) and Children committees (age 10-13) , Parent Groups, Religious leaders’ and Teachers’ networks through regular meetings to learn to identify risks, gaps, barriers to access to services, promoting positive social and behavior change to address CP issues. Groups will be formed separately for boys and girls and ensure equal participation, including children with disabilities.Accountability to Affected Populations initiatives will continue with satisfactory surveys of individuals who received CP services. The Community Feedback Mechanism (CFM) of IOM will be promoted and CP team will respond to CP issues referred by the CFM system. 2. IOM will raise awareness and sensitizing community members on key CP issues to mitigate and identify risks in the community through supporting community-led activities for prevention of and response and child safeguarding to strengthen the protective environment with participatory approach, involving the community, children and caregivers.IOM continue the PSEA Champions in the camps, training staff, partners about PSEA, the obligatory SEA reporting system and continue raising awareness in the community.3. Supporting system strengthening of CP mechanism in Upazila and Union level in host community, providing capacity building and technical support for local government and law enforcement on CP prevention and response on CP principles, identification and referral mechanism, relevant legislation related to child abuse, child labor, child marriage and other key CP issues in the area.4. Provision of MHPSS services will be provided to support vulnerable children (age 2-7) in Child-friendly Corners in the Women and Girls Safe Spaces in cooperation with PULSE NGO. Existing static and mobile Child-friendly Spaces will provide safe access to age-appropriate, disability and gender-sensitive structured psychosocial, recreational and cultural activities for children.Counselling services will be provided for distressed individuals through Problem Management Plus (PM+) developed by WHO, focusing on caregivers to promote positive parenting and reducing harmful practices.5. Case Management (CM) services are provided to children at risk and survivors of child abuse, neglect, violence and exploitation. The service includes alternative care considering age and gender appropriate solutions, services for female and male child survivor of sexual abuse, safe shelter for emergency and rehabilitation purposes for girl survivors and family tracing and reunification services, comprehensive support to boys and girls with disabilities, their carers and families through CM and MHPSS services, capacity building for carers.CP team reaches out to vulnerable groups and disseminate information about available services. For DRR, CP Team being part of the PERU, makes sure beneficiaries have re-established meaningful access to humanitarian services and monitor CP trends for new or emerging threats and risks and apply mitigation measures.6. IOM CP continue CP mainstreaming and safeguarding to strengthen the protective environment through multi-sectoral and participatory approach, involving the community, children and caregivers, as well as promoting “Do no harm “ principles in all IOM programming to prevent further harm to the community.Regular joint monitoring will be carried out with boys and girls in different groups (incl. persons with disabilities) in cooperation of other sectors like Site Management, Site Development and WASH to support the community to develop risk mapping and mitigation plans, inclusive to disaster risk reduction in monsoon and cyclone season, including children and carers with disabilities
