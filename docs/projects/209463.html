Project Summary

In line with the UNICEF Humanitarian Action Appeal and the Health Cluster Humanitarian
Response Plan For The 2024 Humanitarian Program, the project is aimed at achieving
equitable health outcomes for the vulnerable, most-in-need and hardest-to-reach children.
This will be achieved through addressing the evolving humanitarian health needs while building the
resilience of local communities during this crisis against health emergencies and linking with development
interventions. UNICEF will respond to emerging health emergencies in accordance to the
Core Commitments for Children in Humanitarian Action. The key modes of interventions
in this project are service provision including frontline health workers payments, availing medical supplies and vaccines, public health emergencies response , national and state level capacity building, community mobilization and advocacy in coordination with national/international actors and neighboring countries through cross-border operations.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Accountability to the affected population is one of UNICEF core commitments in humanitarian actions. UNICEF is consistently encouraging its partners at the national and sub-national level to design and implement beneficiary-centred interventions. Efforts will be made to advocate for establishing efficient feedback and complaint mechanisms at implementing partners project sites to ensure that targeted beneficiaries have suitable means and methods to raise their voice and reflect their ownership of the services provided. Different modalities will be used, such as establishing hotlines, dissemination of a mobile number of focal points for feedback and complaints, provision of complaint boxes at health facilities, community feedback logbook and patient exit interviews. UNICEF is also an active member of theinteragency platforms and is committed to making significant-quality contributions to it. At the global level, the UNICEF’s Executive Director has assumed the role of Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Champion for the prevention of SEA and SH for one year. As part of this role, UNICEF has led the development and adoption of an IASC Champions Strategy on the protection from and response to SEA and Sexual Harassment.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

UNICEF is keen to improve the quality of humanitarian assistance provided through different implementing partners as well as to protect the beneficiaries and ensure that they are receiving basic social services in a safe and participatory manner. Therefore, the establishment of two-way communication channels between the service providers and the targeted beneficiaries is encouraged and highlighted as a priority within UNICEF-supported interventions (taking into consideration that there are variations among the humanitarian actors in terms of the practice and use of different complaints and feedback mechanisms). UNICEF will continue advocating and encouraging different implementing partners to adopt feedback mechanisms appropriate to the context aiming to optimize the ownership and engagement of the communities to UNICEF-supported interventions. Several feedback mechanisms will be adopted such as hotlines, complaint boxes at health facilities, community feedback logbooks, patient exit interviews, and focus group discussions. All received complaints will be documented and verified by UNICEF staff through field visits to the sites to ensure the implementation of the appropriate corrective actions.
