Project Summary

IOM as Shelter and NFI (S-NFI) Cluster Lead Agency (CLA), plans to reach up to 227,879 vulnerable individuals (41,433 HHs) whose capacity to anticipate, cope with and recover from the drought stresses has been eroded. Ethiopia Somali and Oromia regions have experienced 3 consecutive dry seasons since 2020, and have affected an estimated 3.3 million people. IOM will target the drought-induced displacements as per the cluster prioritized locations across Somali and Oromia regions where emergency Shelter and NFI have been reported as one of the priority needs among other sectors. IOM have a static presence in the targeted areas and has already been responding to assist the drought-affected population. To respond to the lifesaving humanitarian needs faced by the drought-affected population across the two regions. These most vulnerable individuals are the ones who is unable to support themselves with lifesaving shelter and non-food items. The targeted communities include newly displaced persons, IDPs living with host communities, and affected non-displaced communities. The proposed activities are designed in line with HRP strategic objectives 2022, as well as the S-NFI Cluster objectives, key activities with essential protection mainstreaming, and a gender-inclusive approach. In addition to including measures that mitigate the risk of gender-based violence (GBV) and sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA).
IOM has extensive experience in Shelter and NFI responses in both emergency and post-crisis environments. IOM has a static presence across Drought affected areas of Oromia, Somali and SNNP Regions and currently responds to humanitarian needs. IOM is the ES/NFI Cluster lead agency that conducts assessments and training for local authorities and Cluster Partners on ES, NFI, cash assistance. IOM supports SNFI Cluster partners through the provision of in-kind ES and NFI kits as well as supportive supervision.
IOM provides integrated programming alongside health, CCCM, MHPSS, Protection and WASH responses. Delivering Shelter assistance, Housing, Land and Property (HLP) support including ES/NFI assistance to vulnerable communities maximizes the impact of the project in these locations.
Improve access to ES and lifesaving NFIs through in-kind and Cash-Based Interventions. IOM response will provide SNFI assistance to new IDPs, protracted IDPs in camps and collective centres, returnees, PWSN (including Disability and Inclusion (DI) kits) and host community members requiring assistance;
Support SNFI Cluster partners through the provision of in-kind ES and NFI kits (including DI ES and NFI kits), funding for their response, focus on SNFI Cluster partner capacity building and focus on support to NNGO partners;
•Upgrade/rehabilitation/maintenance of shelter, in camps and CCs, through both in-kind and CBI;
•Strengthen evidence-based programming through improved monitoring, evaluation, accountability and learning;
•Mainstream gender, protection/GBV, HLP, environment and AAP in all activities. The specific needs of women will be taken into consideration in the provision of SNFI supplies to populations, IDP in camps, returnees and host communities. IOM will prioritize households headed by women for in-kind and CBI SNFI support;
•Strengthen the capacity of SNFI Staff on emergency and settlement programming, CBI, gender, data quality assessment, AAP and Protection;
•Undertake market assessment and multi-sectoral risk and needs assessment
•Provision of in-kind life-saving ES and NFIs based on need assessment and verification
•Provision of cash for rent and cash for emergency NFIs
•Undertake Monitoring and evaluation through baseline, post-distribution monitoring and end-line assessment
•Undertake Safety Audits and Protection Mainstreaming awareness raising
IOM will target 10 per cent of vulnerable host communities in need of support with essential ES/NFI. Including vulnerable host community members within caseload as part of needs-based targeting

1. - Projects activities

Emergency Shelter and NFI support:• Emergency Shelter (ES) and NFI assistance targeting 39,111 vulnerable HHs (215,108 individuals); the ES and NFI kits provision (in-kind, cash and Disability and Inclusion ES and NFI kits) in Drought affected areas of Oromia, Somali and SNNP Regions, including ES and NFI pipeline supplies to support implementing partners. • Information, Education and Communication (IEC) material for ESNFI will be provided along with the kits to ensure beneficiaries are aware of how to use the items.• With this project, IOM will seek to target 10 per cent of vulnerable host communities in need of support with essential ES/NFI.• Beneficiary selection will follow the Shelter Cluster guidelines for vulnerability criteria. Beneficiaries will be verified 100% by the IOM and Implementing Partners to ensure vulnerability criteria are met. The process of identification, verification, and registration, along with distribution will take longer than in previous distributions in order to follow the COVID-19 mitigation procedure. The beneficiary identification will be done through a joint assessment between IOM, local committees, local leaders, and returnees following agreed criteria, including specific cases highlighted by protection actors. HLP assessments will be conducted in conflict-affected areas, along with an analysis of the national HLP framework and relevant legislative provisions and administrative structures. IOM will raise awareness of issues related to HLP among stakeholders & beneficiaries and strengthen the capacity of government, humanitarian actors and other stakeholders to identify and address HLP issues to effectively assist affected populations to have secure enough tenure arrangements.• Gender-inclusive, PWSN-inclusion and GBV mitigation measures will be incorporated into the action. Several pieces of training have already been conducted in distribution methodologies to ensure teams are aware of the potential threat and make sure mitigation measures are taken ahead of time. Persons with disabilities or specific needs will be supported by porters. The teams will be equipped with PPE and a handwashing station will be placed at points of distribution.• Exit surveys and complaint feedback mechanisms will be established during pre-and post-distribution. Compliant and feedback mechanisms will be set up, when possible BoWCA staff will be included, to increase government engagement and strengthen the link between referrals and case follow up by the government.• Conduct Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM): post-distribution monitoring is planned after distribution. This is conducted to measure the satisfaction level of the beneficiaries of the distributed materials, to measure accountability and to identify gaps and needs which helped identify beneficiaries' list of preferred items and quantities for a response.• Conduct periodical and rapid assessments: IOM will conduct, bilateral as well as with other humanitarian partners, various assessments including protection risk assessments before the ESNFI assistance begins to identify if conditions are safe and establish the need. • Due to the COVID-19 pandemic, the proposed project will take public health needs into account. The project will deliver assistance to 215,108 vulnerable individuals (39,111 HHs) through the provision of ES kits, ES construction, shelter repair, cash for rent and NFIs with essential protection mainstreaming, and a gender-inclusive approach. In addition to including measures that mitigate the risk of GBV and SEA.

2.1.1 - Is it better to use cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

IOM implements Cash-based shelter/NFI responses in various regions in the country. Similarly, IOM will use both in-kind and cash-based shelter and NFI response depending on cash feasibility assessment recommendations. Hence, IOM will consider cash for NFI, shelter rent and shelter repair and maintenance. In locations where cash is feasible, and preference from the beneficiaries IOM will prioritize Cash as a response modality as compared with in-kind assistance cash will give beneficiaries the flexibility and dignity to choose and spend the cash as per their NFIs need and cover the shelter needs. The cash distribution will have a multiplier effect on the local economy by boosting the local market and economy; hence considering Cash as a response modality will revive means of livelihood and income generation, local financial capital will be developed, and the local community will relate to local government system and accessibility to local resources.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

The Emergency Shelter and NFI process will ensure community engagement at all stages. This process will consult and include groups such as female-headed households, people with disabilities and other vulnerable groups in addition to the men, women, boys, girls and elderly people in order to ensure that their needs are adequately met. Additional support requirements for vulnerable beneficiaries will be identified by teams and context-specific assistance provided.In coordination with the IDPs, focal persons and the government BoWCA office and Complaint feedback mechanisms will be established during, pre and post-distribution. Compliant and feedback mechanisms will be set up, when possible BoWCA staff will be included, to increase government engagement and strengthen the link between referrals and case follow up by the government.IOM, in coordination with community members and local authorities, will carry out 100% verification to ensure vulnerability criteria are met and inclusion errors are minimized. Next, registration processes will be conducted, and coupons will be issued to beneficiaries for records and indication of assistance.Upon completion of the ES and NFI assistance, using the Shelter Cluster SoPs; a joint evaluation with relevant partners including the donor, government departments and Post-distribution monitoring will be conducted after the assistance to evaluate the actual impact of the assistance. The project will coordinate closely with National and sub-national Shelter NFI clusters as well as National and Sub-National Cash Working Groups. Participate in the periodical cluster meetings, provide updates, pipeline, and stock for better coordination and to avoid overlap in assistance.
