Project Summary

The proposed action aims at strengthening the community protection mechanism to prevent and mitigate the risks of violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect as well as providing an adequate and holistic GBV and CP service through case management to children in need (Most Vulnerable and excluded children) affected by the conflict. The objectives of this intervention are multiple, including strengthening basic social services for an equitable access to all vulnerable children; strengthening community child protection mechanisms to be accessible and functional in order to prevent all potential risks; establishing and maintaining a feedback mechanism adapted to children for accountability; promoting the involvement of girls and boys, including those living with disabilities, in the decision-making processes that could affect their lives and well-being. Considering the socio-cultural factors and gender inequalities in the Far North region, the project plans to increase advocacy efforts to promote the rights of children, especially those of young girls who are exposed to diverse forms of discrimination and gender inequalities, accentuated by the prolonged armed crisis in the region.
The project targets both children (0-5yrs; 6-9yrs) and adolescents (10-13yrs and 14-17yrs) and youth (18-24yrs) from refugee, IDP, returnee and conflict-affected host communities.
This project aims to reach a total of 9000 people: 1800 children (0-9y) of whom 972 are girls and 828 are boys; 2700 children (10-13y) of whom 1458 are girls and 1242 are boys ; 2700 children (14-17y) of whom 1458 are girls and 1242 are boys as well as 1800 parents and guardians (972 women and 828 men). The project proposes to prevent and respond to violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect of children in the three target divisions of the Far North region, including Mayo Tsanaga, Mayo Sava and Logone et Chari, through the revitalization of community child protection mechanisms, the provision of direct services and an accessible and inclusive referral system. While the primary target group of the proposed action is most vulnerable and excluded girls and boys in need affected by conflict, the project also aims to reach out to parents and caregivers, community leaders, administrative and traditional authorities, religious and community leaders, service providers, and other influential people at the local and national levels to create a safe and inclusive protective environment for vulnerable children.
In addition, through mental health and psychosocial support (M/F) this project aims to ensure care for children in need of mental health care and psychosocial support. Finally, the project will make a significant contribution to ensuring the recognition and enhancement of children's rights, with a focus on girls, so they can achieve their full potential.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Girls and women remain particularly vulnerable to violence including sexual and gender-based violence due to the combined effect of pre-existing cultural and traditional discriminatory norms and practices, gender discrimination and socio-economic vulnerabilities caused by the crisis. From January to October 2021, a total of 229 GBV cases (100% female with 79% 18 to 49 years old, 17% 0-17 years old and 4% 50 years old and over) were reported to GBVIMS. In order to better address these different protection needs and risks for children in the different target divisions of the projects, to reached our main results, we will carry out specific activities based on the indicators identified below. All project activities will be focused on prevention and response to cases of abuse, exploitation, negligence and GBV identified in the communities. All activities will be implemented in such a way as to ensure the mainstreaming protection and thus guarantee significant access to the various services available to the girls, boys including those living with disabilities. Following compliance with the barrier measures to fight the COVID-19 pandemic, prevention activities will revolve around: R1: Strengthen the protective environment: children, parents know how to identify and respond to potential risks of CP and GBV: -Build and support the capacity of local CBO groups to strength the community-based child protection mechanisms- Revitalizing the community child protection mechanisms, training them and support them with materials to carry out their activities and to be accessible -Influence parents & families to reduce social norms, challenge cultural beliefs and harmful practices by awareness raising- Organize life skills sessions and parenting sessions, prevent family separation- Guide all CPCMs to the child protection policy, safeguarding, gender and inclusion policy and encouraging them to sign the Code of Conduct.- Establish child friendly feedback mechanism and backup: for each complaint, a response will be shared in a timely manner through appropriate information channels tailored to girls, boys, men, women considering confidentiality and security. Based on the complaints and comments received, decisions will be made to improve the quality of life of the community. Outcome/R2 : Promote access to age- and gender-sensitive services: children and families at risk have access to timely and quality services to respond to child protection issues. - Case management: child protection and GBV case management will be provided for a holistic response to child survivors; Establishment of birth certificates for children without birth certificates. Separated or unaccompanied children will receive the appropriate care services. -Capacity building of social workers for case management of child protection and GBV children for a better management cases identified in the target communities. Building capacity of the sector agents (MINAS and MINIPROFF) in charge of child protection and GBV. FMO’s will be including to discourage cases out-of-court settlement. -Distribute recreational KITS, dignity kits, NFI / FI to girls, boys and families with urgent needs, to IDPs or to host populations who have welcomed unaccompanied children into their families -Provide psychosocial support, mental health services for girls, boys including the differently abled children in need, but also ad hoc support to cases of protection identified in the communities (medical support, school support, material support for the neediest families). -Establishment or strengthening of reception centers and the foster family system for vulnerable children and their family’s victims of exclusion, separated or ENA which will provide psychological support, socializing activities. -Refresh referral circuits and protection services to ensure that the necessary coordination between actors is effective and that the identified cases receive a holstic response.-Information sharing on service mapping
