Project Summary

The overarching objective is to provide agricultural based livelihood support to women and youth in herding households affected by man-made and natural shocks.
This project falls under Country Strategic Objective 3: “Strengthen the ability of acutely-vulnerable Palestinian households to cope with protracted threats and shocks” of the HRP 2018-2020 as it improves the coping abilities and reduces shocks and environmental hazards of herders and specifically women in herding communities.
The project falls under Strategic Objective 3 of the Humanitarian Response Strategy "The capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with and overcome protracted crisis, including
from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of
threats and shocks are pursued." And Food Security Cluster objective 1 "The resilience , and food security and productive capacity of vulnerable households are restored, enhanced and livelihoods protected".

1 - Needs

Findings from a 2018 post-distribution exercise of FAO assistance to herder communities West Bank and Gaza Strip highlighted findings that herders suffer from limited access to public services, weak adaptive capacity and asset bases. Poor herder families, particularly who are run by a female headed household are particularly affected. Female-headed households are most at risk of suffering from lack of economic capacity to obtain the necessary inputs and basic requirements for meaningful farm and animal health management compared to their male-headed counterpart. Similarly, youth are also adversely affected by restricted access to land which is contributing to high unemployment rates of more than 50% of young people (as of January 2019, PCBS) This situation of limited and restricted access to productive assets and inputs have compromised the productivity and profitability of meat and dairy production. Of the limited resource available, the bulk of the processing and marketing workload is burdened particularly by women, who are negatively impacted by a decline in food production, productivity, and quality, ultimately resulting in reduced ability to meet minimum food security and livelihood needs of their households

2 - Activities

• Activities for this project include: Output 1: Coping capacities enhanced and resilience against environmental shocks improved for women and youth in the targeted communities in the WBGS. Activity 1.1 Distribution of drought tolerant seeds, in accordance to MoA procedure, in Yatta, Dura, Hebron, Bethlehem, Jerusalem, Ramallah, Tubas, Nablus, Jenin. Activity 1.2 Rehabilitation of rangeland in Yatta, Dura, Hebron, Bethlehem, Jerusalem, Ramallah Nablus. Activity 1.3 Rehabilitation and/or construction of 200 water cisterns in Yatta, Dura, Hebron, Bethlehem, Jerusalem, which would particularly lessen the burden of women in fetching water.from locations far from their house . Activity 1.4 Rehabilitation of animal sheds in Yatta, Dura, Hebron, North Hebron, Bethlehem, Jerusalem, Ramallah, Tubas, Nablus, Qalqilya, Jericho, Jenin, Rafah, Khan Younis, Middle area, Gaza and North Gaza, which allows easier cleaning of animal barns. The rehabilitation of plastic sheds will be on existing ones that need rehabilitation due to existing damages. Activity 1.5 Distribution of small scale farm management inputs (disinfection preventive material, salt blocks, wool and hooves secateurs) in Yatta, Dura, Hebron, Bethlehem, Jerusalem, Rafah, Khan Younis, Middle area, Gaza and North Gaza. Most activities in farm management help to prevent animal disease and support animals’ wellbeing, accordingly the toolkit will support this purpose of animal disease prevention. Activity 1.6 Distribution of dairy processing equipment (milk jars, milk shakers, and cheese tanks) in Yatta, Dura, Hebron, Bethlehem, Jerusalem, which will save time and efforts on women as they are the main contributors for producing of dairy products. Output 2: Access to market is improved for herders’ households engaging women and youth in WB Activity 2.1 Enhancing and enabling the market accessibility for women and youth through up scaling the capacity of the livestock market in Al Dahrieh through promoting their participation in local exhibitions and providing them with spaces to sell their products. FAO has previously implemented two phases in Al Dahrieh market under two different projects. This intervention will complete the third stage of the market. The third stage includes booths for women, quarantine area, additional animal cages and sheds and a parking area. This work on the A Dahrieh market was a recommendation from the MoA.

Post distribution monitoring will be conducted by interviewing a sample of female and male beneficiaries in order to monitor use of the inputs distributed, prospects for sustainability of the intervention as well as overall satisfaction of beneficiaries as part of FAO’s accountability to beneficiaries. Collected information will be gender dis-aggregated. Project activities and achievements will be monitored consistently to ensure continued alignment with the FSS response plan and the FAO Country Programme Framework 2018 2022. All project activities and results will be coordinated with the FSS to avoid duplication of emergency support efforts. Relevant results, analysis and lessons learned shall be shared with all Food Security Sector stakeholders. In addition, FAO will maintain coordination with the protection cluster and the Humanitarian Country Team in order to ensure alignment with and support of advocacy initiatives. A Grievance Redressal Procedure on the basis of FAO guidelines and taking into account local best practices has been established and is currently functional. It includes clearly defined responsibilities and follow up channels, starting with the officer in charge of receiving the feedback/complaint, and the proceeding processing procedures, up until final decision and the subsequent follow up. The procedure includes several well-defined steps tied to specific time frames including preliminary review for eligibility, verification process and resolution and will be publicized among targeted communities in the context of beneficiary selection processes, post distribution monitoring as well as in the context of partnerships agreements if and when relevant. Major monitoring and evaluation milestones: 1) Successful completion of the project’s activities should be accomplished 12 months into project implementation. 2) All inputs should be fully operational 12 months into project implementation. 3) Beneficiaries will be contacted for an impact evaluation after project completion, to measure the overall sustainability of the intervention as well to verify whether all structures are in use. Impact evaluation will specifically target females in focus group discussions, and female experts if/when needed will conduct these.All monitoring, reviews and reporting will be based on SMART and gender sensitive indicators and targets ensuring accountability and coherence with gender mainstreaming throughout the project. Mainstreaming gender and environment within FAO’s programming is based on socio economic/environmental assessments and analysis, utilizing a range of tools and analytical modules that are tailored to the WBGS context. FAO’s gender focal point, supported by FAO’s gender committee will play a key role in assuring that women and men associated with the project stakeholders have equal opportunities to participate in and benefit from the project’s activities at all levels.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The intervention proposed by FAO aims to support marginalized and vulnerable herders whose livelihoods are under the threat. The selection of herders is solely based on needs and the most food-insecure and does not consider any other difference or discriminatory factors. The interventions proposed under this project do not put beneficiaries in risk, however, FAO aims to ensure that all efforts will be made to prevent and minimize as much as possible unintended negative effects of the interventions. As part of the project inception phase, FAO will develop a risk log identifying all potential risks, their probability and ways of mitigation. The assessment and analysis of risks will be followed by a coordinated application of resources to monitor, minimize and control the probability and/or impact of risks. External risk factors will continuously be considered in order to ensure an adequate understanding of the context of FAO operations during the implementation of the project. Through FAO’s participation in the UN Country Team (UNCT) and Humanitarian Country Team (HCT), the development of the political situation in the WBGS will regularly be monitored and reviewed in line with project objectives. FAO contributes to policy-level initiatives to protect Palestinians from further reduction in access to land, sea and livelihood resources (e.g. water and agricultural inputs) owing to the political situation, through its participation in the UNCT Commendation Group and HCT Advocacy Working Group. All advocacy efforts will be conducted under the banner of the UN in the WBGS to ensure that FAO’s mandate is not compromised.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

FAO’s approach provides a framework for addressing and integrating issues such as gender equality, protection, the inclusion of the aged and people living with disability, and the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse by FAO and partner staff. Throughout the process of beneficiary selection, disability and age will be a factor taken into consideration. Beneficiaries with this vulnerability will be prioritized. The project also seeks to engage with targeted stakeholders and mainstream FAO’s commitments in each phase of the project cycle. FAO maintains its commitments through regular stakeholder engagement and participation, two-way communication, transparent disclosure and a grievance mechanism.
