Project Summary

A developmental and socio-economic project that adopts the principle of microfinance developed by the Sham Humanitarian Foundation, provides lending services to men and women on easy and recoverable terms without interest, establishes a work culture and the principle of self-reliance,provides free lending and secures job opportunities
The project aims to support livelihoods by providing the opportunity for those with the skill, ability and desire to work to obtain the necessary liquidity to accelerate and develop their own activities, which helps to create new job opportunities and increase income for the beneficiaries
The project will provide financial inclusion and lending services to300 new clients, and the loan amount will reach $600as a first stage, and from the monthly redemption amounts loans will be provided to other clients, and it is expected that the number of clients will increase by 90additional clients/borrowers in the following year.
The project consists of3phases that together form an integrated plan to get out of poverty that gradually moves customers from extreme poverty to the poverty line and then to the middle income group
These loans are also granted under the umbrella of a group guarantee that aims to:
Not to resort to traditional guarantees imposed by banks on guarantors and to transfer their salaries into mortgages
The Karim Microfinance Project is part of the Humanitarian Response Plan in the early recovery sector, as the project provides income-generating tools and tools that aid in extreme poverty, improve the standard of living, and enhance resilience.Which(provided support to 300small projects that benefited 80 families directly and30,000 families indirectly per month)and was implemented in 2015-2016 & had a positive impact on the living conditions of the beneficiaries and the surrounding community
In Al-Bab City during(2019-2021),the Careem project provided financial mergers and lending to more than300new beneficiaries,so the monthly installments were very successful at100%and the continuous rate of repayment during phases1and2of the project
This proposal is an extension and development of the current Karim microfinance project, and AHF hopes to develop it by increasing the number of borrowers and targeting new beneficiaries and new areas if possible, to spread the culture of work and sustainable development in the Syrian society for the sake of those affected by the conflict that has spanned more than10years.
The city of Al-Bab in northern Syria was chosen because it borders the Syrian-Turkish border and is considered a commercial and gathering center for many displaced Syrians from all governorates, including migrant workers and professionals who carry their professions and skills, and requires incubators to start work
The establishment of the project in the establishment stage requires the following:
1-The preparatory stage includes signing agreements with local councils and preparing cadres and labor offices
2-The executive phase,which includes selecting300new beneficiaries in the first phase of the Careem project by granting them loans worth$600
This number will increase one year after the loans are granted at a rate of30%of the monthly refunded installments,which will be granted to new customers
AHF will work to build staff capacity by conducting training in (Communication and Negotiation Skills-Marketing),Client Training in Financial and Management Services(Small Business Administration, Accounting,Account Control,Bookkeeping and Financial Expenditures),and providing advice. Advice on working and marketing their projects and providing support to inform them through(cards, storage boards, promotions, brochures for the project)
The project will contribute to attracting Syrians to enter the labor market, reduce unemployment, integrate the displaced into the host community, and empower women targeted to contribute to strengthening the local economy, combating poverty,and supporting livelihoods

4 - Needs

Ten years into the Syria crisis, humanitarian needs are deepening. Ongoing insecurity and the compounded impact of displacement, combined with worsening socio-economic conditions characterized by sharp currency depreciation, record-level price increases and cuts in subsidized goods have prompted an almost 20 per cent increase in the number of people in need compared to early 2020 - with a disproportionate impact on women and children. As of January 2021, around 13.4 million people are estimated to require some form of humanitarian and protection assistance, including 6 million in acute need, due to a convergence of factors arising from a sharp reduction in purchasing power, the loss of essential livelihoods and income, mounting food insecurity, limited access to basic services such as health, WASH, education, nutrition and critical protection services, inadequate shelter conditions as well as the immediate and longer-term effects of the COVID-19 pandemic.(HNO-2021)The project targets the Al-Bab area, which is one of the areas that have witnessed many changes in the dominant powers over the past ten years and is now stable and safe and is a haven for many waves of displacement from many regions. The project will work to integrate the displaced families with the host community.This project is an expansion of the Karim microfinance project, which has so far supported nearly 300 clients in its first and second phase in Al-Bab.

Late paymentTaking actions towards delinquent clients according to systematic lending policies3. Total deficiency in paymentThe probability is about 3% from our previous experiments4. Resignation of some employeesTraining of a new cadre of volunteers during the project5. Problems in dealing with familiesTraining of staff to better deal with families6. Security risksTaking immediate action according to the changes on the ground (suspension of work - postponement of a visit - remote follow-up ...)7.
