Project Summary

Project Summary:
The project responds to the urgent winter needs of 8086 households in Idlib and Aleppo governorates, prioritizing vulnerable groups. With a total individual target of 40430 persons, It focuses on providing shelter assistance and upgrading sites to mitigate flood damages, particularly for households headed by females, children, persons with disabilities, or the elderly. Special attention is given to recently displaced individuals and those in overcrowded conditions.
Activities include:
1. Winterization assistance: Providing heating materials to 6,068 families, prioritizing vulnerable households, both in and out of camps.
2. Rehabilitation of household-level shelter units: Renovating 99 damaged houses to improve safety and privacy, targeting vulnerable areas.
3. Construction of 818 HH dignified shelters: Building concrete units in targeted districts for IDPs, with infrastructure improvements.
4. Distribution of 1,101 HH core NFIs: Providing essential items in selected villages and camps.
Needs:
After 12 years of conflict and earthquakes, there's increased demand for shelter and NFIs, with 8.6 million in need of shelter support and 2.6 million requiring NFI support. Economic conditions worsen, driving displacement and reliance on humanitarian aid.
Implementation:
Activities will be implemented systematically, including assessments, beneficiary verification, and distribution. Renovations and constructions will adhere to SPHERE standards and vulnerability criteria, ensuring inclusivity and quality. Regular monitoring will ensure effectiveness.
Protection Risk Analysis:
Risks include conflicts among beneficiaries, inclusion errors leading to exploitation, mistreatment of beneficiaries, and safety concerns around infrastructure. Mitigation strategies involve community engagement, staff training, and consultation with vulnerable groups.
PSEA Strategy:
IR Syria field offices adhere to PSEA policy, ensuring staff awareness and establishing mechanisms for feedback and complaints handling. Special attention is given to potential incidents related to gender-based violence and child protection.
Risks, Assumptions, and Mitigating Measures:
Risks include funding shortfall, resource burden, insecurity, political instability, and weather instability. Assumptions include cooperation with local partners and authorities. Mitigation measures involve continuous financial assessment, market monitoring, enhanced security, and strategic implementation in stable areas.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

This project, addressing the winter needs of 6,068 households in Idlib and Aleppo, aims for a holistic impact by prioritizing vulnerable groups. The winterization assistance activity is not just about distributing heating materials; it follows a systematic process involving collaboration with the CCCM and SNFI clusters. Assessments and beneficiary verifications ensure aid reaches the neediest, emphasizing inclusivity for pregnant women, lactating mothers, the elderly, and those with special needs. The rehabilitation of 99 houses extends beyond physical repairs. Detailed technical assessments and adherence to SPHERE standards prioritize protection, safety, and the needs of occupants. Targeting areas like Harim, Afrin, and Azaz, community feedback guides the project framework, ensuring relevance and sustainability. The construction of 818 dignified shelters is transformative. With an average cost of $5500 per unit, the project involves assessments, coordination with local authorities, detailed designs, and rigorous monitoring. Prioritizing vulnerable households, it aims to relocate them to organized camps with enhanced infrastructure, contributing to their long-term stability. The provision of 1,101 core NFIs goes beyond distribution. Strategically targeting villages and camps in specific sub-districts ensures essential items reach critical areas. The distribution process is meticulously documented, fostering transparency and accountability. The project recognizes the economic drivers of displacement and aims to create a ripple effect by addressing deep-rooted issues contributing to escalating needs. The Protection Risk Analysis and PSEA strategy underscore the commitment to safety, dignity, and well-being. Mitigating measures for identified risks, coupled with a robust PSEA strategy, create an environment of accountability and responsiveness. Facing potential challenges like financial shortfalls, environmental impact, and geopolitical uncertainties, the project relies on strategic assumptions and mitigation measures. Cooperation with local partners, continuous financial assessments, and flexible operational plans demonstrate a commitment to adaptability and resilience. This extended summary encapsulates the project's comprehensive nature and commitment to fostering sustainable positive change in the lives of those affected by conflict and displacement.
