Project Summary

The vast majority of the Rohingya Refugees have been living in extremely difficult condition with no or limited access to basic humanitarian facilities including food, shelter, WASH, health, education, protection, nutrition, health etc. Considering the vulnerabilities of the Rohingya refugees, the project “Shelter and NFI Assistant for the Rohingya refugee” is designed to improve the quality of the lives of the Rohingya communities through building shelters and distributing non-food items among the Rohingya refugees.
Specific activities under this project include: HH level site management and improvement of land preparation of 500 shelters, construction of 500 new mid-term shelters in camp 6 block A & B. New Solar and porter support (include household pack) among 350 EVHHS at camp 6 (block A & B). Repair and maintenance of 150 damaged shelters in camps 18 block E. Our Australian based donor has 150 shelters in Camp 18 block E which needs to repair and maintain. We submitted project proposal to repair these 150 shelters.
New MTS in camp 6 will be built based on the Shelter/NFI Sector assessment to target only completely damaged shelters and if it may not reach the planned target and in this case SAWAB will coordinate with the Sector to include other camp based on needs and discussion with the Focal Point organizations to reach the target.
Moreover, we have a plan to distribute Non-Food-Items (Blanket and Cloths Items) which will cover approximately 21,000 Rohingya HHs in Camp 19 (block A, B, C, D), Camp 18 (block A to E), Existing Camp 15 (block A, B, C, D, E, F, G and H) and 4,000 HHs of host communities in Palong Khali union of Ukhia upazila, Cox’s Bazar. These are the plan of SAWAB for the Rohingya refugees and host community. In future, we may change the location according to the demands of donors and beneficiaries coordinating and discussing with SNFI sector and Focal Point organizations.
This project will help to promote the dignity and well-being of conflict-displaced communities through shelters that offer privacy, safety and protection from the elements, support the refugee’s health, dignity and safety through providing Non-Food Item (NFI) assistance through in-kind distributions.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Based on the existing funding gaps; field observation, discussions with local people on the situation & SAWAB’s recent experience of implementing different projects, this project proposed to address the priority needs of Shelters and Non-Food-Items in both Rohingya Camps and Host Communities. According to report of Shelter/NFI (SNFI) 4W dashboard as of 31 August 2022, 10% of households assisted with emergency shelter support and 59% of Rohingya households reached with shelter assistance. Since the very beginning of the influx of Rohingya refugees in Bangladesh, the temporary materials like bamboo, tarpaulin, rope and wires have been used by UN agencies and other NGOs and INGOs. For this reason, the shelters need to maintain and repair regularly. During the cyclone and monsoon season throughout the year, the Rohingya HHs need the essential materials to repair the shelters. On the other hand, extreme weather events such as heavy rains and winds, or incidents such as fires occurs every year in the Rohingya camps. To reduce the suffering of the Rohingya, necessary preparation should be taken. According to the previous J-MSNA, 72% Rohingya HHs reported that their shelter had some issues, most commonly shelters leaking during rain (as reported by 66% of households). At the same time, 36% of households continued to report not having made improvements or repairs to their shelters despite having reported shelter issues, most commonly because of insufficient shelter support and a lack of means to access materials otherwise. Ref: Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (J-MSNA), December 2021. Considering the mention above issues with the shelters in the refugees’ camps, it is mentionable that shelters are not capable to resistant to natural calamities, even not resistant to heavy rain. In host community, shelter is relatively better compare to the Refugees but overall structural condition is not up to the minimum standard- not resistant to natural calamities like rain, storm, land sliding and cyclone. Women and elderly people don’t feel safe in living these houses.Based on the previous experiences since august, 2017 to 2022, the shelter and site improvement interventions are made by bamboo which is a low-durability construction material, these interventions are easily damaged over time and due to any kind of disaster, especially natural disaster. So the shelters and site improvement interventions need regular repair/ maintenance or sometimes construction of new shelter with durable materials like treated bamboo, reinforce cement concrete (RCC) pad footing, nuts and bolts etc. These services are changing and being refined over time to improve the quality of living and accessibility considering the needs of the beneficiaries.Following this, in 2022, under the shelter project of SAWAB, program department will use the improved construction material for construction of Mid Term Shelter to ensure the durability, safety and security of beneficiary households, except for emergency shelters and regular shelters following the approved design of RRRC.Given the situation this project will focus on improving shelter conditions in Refugee Camp and distributing NFI (Blankets and Cloths Items) both in Rohingya Camp and Host communities.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

To ensure equitable access of all Rohingya refugees to all the basic services in our targeted Rohingya camps, SAWAB’s shelter sector will be aware to predict the future events and take necessary steps for bringing solution with efficient manner on time. Regular communication and meeting will be arranged with the Areas of Responsibilities (AoRs), Sector/WG Coordinators, and other stakeholders following the common strategies, system, protocols, and tools are used across all the camps to provide equitable services and avoid the gaps and overlaps in the Rohingya camps. Before starting any project related activities, SAWAB team may receive the consultation from the relevant sectors/working groups in order to ensure the equitable services in the locations/camps. This year-2023, SAWAB has a plan to implement the projects in an integrated approach (e.g.: shelter, food, education and WASH project will be implement at the same camp at a time and maintain regularly for the continuity of the running projects through avoiding the camps where the different organizations are active to discharge the same services). SAWAB maintain regular communication with Shelter/NFI Focal point to improve the quality of assistance. If it is necessary, SAWAB will review and revise the annual plan to fulfill the requirements of the needs for the emergency time. The project will involve beneficiary household members, of especially vulnerable HHs in construction of shelters. Emphasis will be given for especially women headed households and extremely vulnerable individuals (EVI) families/HHs. This is to avoid the exclusion of certain groups from humanitarian services in Shelter and NFI. Beneficiary selection will be through house visits considering people in extreme vulnerability, prioritizing women headed HHs, elderly, person with disability etc. Split of the assistance per camp will be coordinated with other actors especially IOM, implementing similar activities to avoid duplication.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

SAWAB will adopt a community-based approach with proper plan to ensure the benefits of the Rohingya refugees focusing the specific needs of older persons, persons with disabilities, women and children with the aim of mitigating the sufferings of the vulnerable group and targeted beneficiaries. During the implementation of project, team SAWAB will identify the needy individuals and groups with particular attention to the disabled people, pregnant women, lactating mothers and older people to ensure proper access without any obstacles. With the cooperation of SNFI actors and the camp protection focal point, alternative residence opportunities will be arranged for the dwellers of the targeted shelters, notably individuals with disabilities, the elderly, children, pregnant women, and breastfeeding mothers. To avoid the possibilities of violation and clash, cooperation of the law enforcements agencies of Bangladesh government will be taken.

3.2 - Gender

A gender-sensitive, participatory approach at all stages of the project cycle will be ensured and women, girls, boys and men will be involved equally in the process to bring a positive impact. Shelters will be built in accordance with the GoB-approved design, which will safeguard the privacy and security of women and children The safety of shelters will be enhanced in response to protection concerns from male and female beneficiaries (e.g. lockable doors, partitions, lighting). Distribution point and time will be separated to reduce in sexual violence against women and girls during the distribution of relief items. As per SAWAB’s strict safe-guarding policy, all staff will be oriented on PSEA/safeguarding and gender mainstreaming, to ensure a safe environment for both beneficiaries and staff, and interventions tailored as per needs of the vulnerable groups. Gender sensitivity and inclusiveness will be considered as an indispensable part in our entire project implementation strategy.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

To reduce the child safeguarding gaps, SAWAB has its own policy which will be followed and action will be taken according to principles of SAWAB’s policies. Our main aims to reduce all forms of abuse including sexual abuse, physical abuse, emotional abuse, neglect and exploitation. Action against child labour will be taken to reduce the deprivation of children of their childhood, their potential and their dignity, and that is harmful to physical and mental development. At SAWAB, we have a culture of zero tolerance for all forms of abuse and mistreatment, including Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, Harassment, Intimidation and Bullying. We will hold our people to account against the same standards and subject them to the same processes, as everyone else regardless of their position or reputation within the organization. The connection of all staff, representatives and third parties will be ensured in order to increase awareness among them against all forms of child abuse.

3.4 - Diversity

People with gender-based diversity won’t be victimized any way by any of our project intervention. People will be guided and motivated not to be violent against gender based identity. All diversified group will be treated respectfully. To decrease in sexual violence against women and girls during the distribution shelter materials and NFI including distribution of hygiene kits to females of reproductive age, time and venue will be separated and female participation in community activities will be increased (e.g. women in committees and public meetings, girls attending school). Services/aid will be provided equally irrespectively creed, caste and culture. During the implementation of project, all religions, caste, creed and culture will be treated equally for maintaining good relation with Rohingya communities. 500 persons (men and women) with having working ability will be involved. In this regard, the close cooperation of SMSD will be taken.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

SAWAB’s M&E team will conduct Post Distribution Monitoring of shelter/NFI materials distribution and shelter quality. SAWAB’s community feedback process collects information from beneficiary household in design, implementation and completion stages based on which the shelter sector responds. In this process, the concerns, needs and ricks will be identified and it will also help to build their capacities and reflect their perspectives and priorities. The project will remain fully accountable to the affected people having included their voices in our project. We will arrange periodic review meeting with concerned affected community. To make sure that the voice of affected people is heard some regular meeting including FGD and courtyard meeting will be arranged.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

During the implementation of the Shelter project, team SAWAB will use disposable materials. The sites for shelter will be selected the empty space where is no chance to harm the trees and animal. In this regard, the soil for the improvement of site will be brought from the long distance or uncultivable land. The soil will not be collected from hills or near the shelters. The treated bamboo will be used to build the shelters so that the Rohingya refugees can protect themselves from the natural disasters including cyclone, heavy rains and cold waves. One member from every HHs will be selected to work before and after any natural disasters. This volunteer group will play a vital role to reduce the damages of natural disasters.
