Project Summary

The project, “Communication with Communities (CwC) initiative as part of integrated humanitarian aid for Refugee and host communities in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh” aims to serve 574,481 beneficiaries between 1 January 2019 and 31 December 2019 with USD 956,134. Through this project Action Against Hunger’s humanitarian programming will cover both old and new refugees and the host community.
The project will strengthen coordination and advocacy towards the integration and reinforcement of Communication with Communities capacities and community engagement to ensure comprehensive accessibility to life-saving information and knowledge on rights and available resources by affected populations, across sectors, establish and reinforce needs-based, actionable, two-way and culturally appropriate multi-sectoral communication resources and strengthen dissemination and access to information, and strengthen participation of, and accountability to affected populations through effective community engagement, participatory approaches and functional feedback and complaint response mechanisms.
The project is designed based on the fact that, as JRP 2018 mid-term review revealed, 69% of Rohingya refugees are yet to receive information assistance from agencies. The acute need to provide lifesaving information to communities in a language and format they understand is very vital to enable access to services, promote behavior change, reduce risks and bring social cohesion. Information hubs have worked as a catalyst in 2018 humanitarian response to the Rohingya crisis. Considering the poor literacy and access to information, information hubs have disseminated messages in person and through easy-to-understand audio-visual communication contents, which have helped to casket down vital information to the refugee communities. Simultaneously, information hubs have contributed in meeting the essential need of functional complaint/feedback mechanisms.
Observing the growing tensions, low levels of social cohesion and interaction between the Rohingya and host communities, Communication with Communities (CwC) including behavior change initiatives are very much needed to avoid potential conflict and bring social cohesion among new refugees, old refugees and host community. This project directly addresses information needs by scaling up its initiatives, such as increasing numbers of information centres, creating easy-to-understand content for both Rohingya and host communities meeting their concurrent information needs and strengthening the established complaint/feedback mechnisms.
Empowering Rohingya refugees and host communities to provide feedback is key to enable a relevant, conflict-sensitive and localised response. Using established feedback systems supplemented with additional feedback through targeted outreach and engagement, such as perception surveys and focus group discussions, will help provide data that can be consolidated and can support sectors’ in decision-making.
To address the needs and gaps in integration of gender, age and disability perspectives into the response, Action Against Hunger will: continue to collect disaggregate data by sex, age and disability; ensure gender balanced teams (staff and volunteers); use mechanisms and create opportunities to engage community members and ensure equal access of women, girls, boys and men in the design, implementation and evaluation of Action Against Hunger’s integrated programs.

Activities or outputs

• Increased access to information- awareness and knowledge of rights for both refugee and host populations.• Opportunities provided for women and adolescent girls to co-design protection, WASH and nutrition programs and services. • Establish mechanisms through Communication with Communities (CwC) initiatives to systematically gather input from women and adolescent girls regarding their needs and proposed solutions.• Collaborate with women and adolescent girls to co-design programs and services that respond to their self-identified needs and priorities.• Established data and monitoring system with disaggregated by sex, age and disability (SADD).• Develop information content on GBV and SRHR in addition to other integrated programme content (eg. Nutrition, Health, WaSH, Mental Health, Care Practices)• Provide information and support related to GBV and SRHR through women and adolescent-friendly spaces, spaces for elderlies, home visits (for persons with special needs, eg: PLWs and persons with disability), Mens’ Forums, school-based sessions.• Ensure functionality of complain and feedback mechanisms.• Use referral mechanisms to coordinate among sectors and working groups.• Achieve and ensure 100% gender balance in information centres/hubs staffing (including volunteers).• Capacity building activities: refugee and host community• Outreach activities including community mobilisation on disaster preparedness (DRR).• Active participation in coordination meetings and sector initiatives (eg. for advocacy, communication)
