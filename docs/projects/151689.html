Needs

Water supply continues to be a major priority for 2019. From the HNO: “In 2018 the number of people living close to the contact line experiencing frequent water shortages, either daily or weekly was 27%.” The general disruption of water supply networks which were functional prior to the conflict has caused multiple settlements along the contact line to look for alternative means to provide water supply to the population. Furthermore, there remains a gap between centralised water supply interventions and household access to water, with many families relying on shallow wells or boreholes for their daily water needs. While local administrations and water utility managers explore options to supply water to their communities, they lack the material, financial and sometimes technical inputs needed to address this need. Wastewater treatment, sewage networks and desludging services have been severely disrupted over the last several years, due to multiple causes. Some are 70 years old and are simply in need of repair, some have been crushed as a result of heavy machinery driving over the networks, shelling, and in some instances desludging trucks are fearful of driving too close to the line of contact for fear of being mistaken for military vehicles. This has caused massive inflation in costs for desludging services and left families looking for other answers. In rural areas 69% of families have an outside toilet (REACH) and with difficulties in finding services to empty their tanks, many families are simply desludging manually. Previous years have shown a difficulty in towns and settlements near the frontline in dealing with their garbage, and reduced income levels across the line of contact means village administrations are having difficulties in providing this service within their communities. There is an obvious need for better household level sanitation systems (done in coordination with Shelter Cluster activities in 2019), but this will only work I if there is a linkage to desludging services. There currently is not a market analysis of existing waste water treatment facilities and possible connections to settlements along the contact lines. Athough most households understand and apply good hygiene practices, in the conflict affected areas people do not have sufficient access to hygiene materials or adequate water supplies to practice safe hygiene continuously. Furthermore, younger children, especially, have grown up entirely in the context of conflict, and they now need hygiene promotion support, alongside psychosocial first aid.

Activities & outcomes

Based on permanent observation and monitoring of its intervention, NRC will continue an ongoing responsive readiness for conflict-affected households and targeted institutions isolated to basic needs access issues with the provision of general and specific items. Within the frame of its response plan for 2019, NRC will aim to provide WASH assistance to 20,610 persons residing in conflict-affected area of Luhansk oblast (GCA). In addition, the project will have capacities for emergency response (contingency plan) so NRC will ensure appropriate stock and response planning for different situations/needs in order to be in position to deliver effective essential assistance. Based on experience, NRC will ensure response planning and timely/quality assistance for next year winter. This WASH component is incorporated into and coordinated with NRC’s shelter programme as coordinated with the WASH cluster and carried out with the approval of the relevant authorities. Shelter and WASH work in institutions will be coordinated with authorities at oblast and/or municipal level to ensure a cohesive approach and adherence to relevant standards and norms. Completed works will be handed over to the authorities to carry out operation and maintenance, where and as required. Repair, extension, or rehabilitation of water supply in communities and institutions located near the line of contact GCA. Working with the local administrations and utilities, conduct neighbourhood and community repairs, through labour costs (using cash for work from a preferential list of workers living in poverty wherever possible, giving preference to women’s groups for trenching, cleaning drainage and community cleanups) and/or material inputs as appropriate. While providing technical supervision, and education on water treatment options with low environmental risk. Restore broken links with desludging service and waste water management, by mapping existing services currently operating in Luhansk Oblast, and providing vouchers for families for services to cover costs for 2019-2020. This activity will be done in coordination with household level sanitation solutions (proper septic tanks) in coordination with Shelter programming. Hygiene Promotion, NRC will seek to collaborate with organizations, either to help distribute hygiene materials, NFIs, or establish referral systems for trauma related issues. Solid waste management improvement in the locations of contact line GCA. This will include distribution of garbage bins to the communities, mapping local landfill options, and organizing garbage removal works on-site involving local residents (once again, giving preference to women’s groups to manage the community clean ups) that will be closely linked with cash-for-work activities. NRC will coordinate its activities with local authorities of the villages located in the contact line area in frames of identifying communities in need of water and sanitation systems improvement. Relevant assessment activities will be conducted at the beginning of the program including focus group discussions and interviews with focal points in those pre-identified areas. NRC will be taking a community engagement approach to all WASH programming in 2019. Representatives from each age group, gender, and especially marginalized communities will become part of a committee who helps identify and guide our programme at a micro level. Although this is a labour intensive approach, it will afford the opportunity for all members of the community to have a “voice” in how NRC conducts its programmes. Water and sanitation issues usually have impact on the lives of women over and above those of men and mostly affect the elderly and disabled disproportionately. Therefore, such groups will be prioritized targets within project framework, and will be considered for specific help. (HNO 2018)

Indicators & targets

• Number of people provided with access to sufficient quantity of water (disaggregated by sex and age where possible) (Target: 6672) • Number of people provided with access to sufficient quality of water (disaggregated by sex and age where possible) (Target: 6672) • Number of people provided with improved access to adequate sanitation (disaggregated by sex and age where possible) (Target: 1250) • Number of people provided with critical WASH-related awareness (disaggregated by sex and age where possible) (will be done in 28 settlements). Targeting small and isolated communities where institutional WASH programming has yet to impact families at the household level, the project will use a cash for work, community engagement, local administration partnerships and in-kind procurement and distribution modality. Targeting will focus on a range of vulnerable household categories: elderly, disabled people, single woman headed household, pregnant and lactating women, large number families. Also a specific and unusual target group is the approximately 30-55 years old category of unemployed persons with dependent children, a group not typically dependent on welfare and without access to it, and with more expenditures related to food, health, education and basic daily goods. It is believed that the current situation has reduced basic traditional coping mechanisms, while more evidence will be created under this intervention to determine exact vulnerabilities.
