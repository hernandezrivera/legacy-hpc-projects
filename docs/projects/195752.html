Project Summary

CRS and local implementing partner Healthcare Foundation Organization (HFO) will scale their current activities in Fangak and Ayod to ensure that children under five years and pregnant women use more equitable and better quality essential maternal, newborn and child health services. CRS and HFO will target a population of 392,124, focusing on girls and boys under five years of age, adolescent girls and women to increase equitable access to quality health services and adoption of appropriate health behaviors. CRS and HFO will collaborate with county health departments (CHDs) to improve consistent delivery of low-cost, high-impact, people-centered, equitable primary health care services to the most vulnerable populations through an agile mix of essential front-line health services, regular outreach to hard-to-reach populations, community-based health services through the Boma Health Initiative (BHI), safe motherhood initiatives, and emergency preparedness and response to bolster community resilience and continuity of basic services provision during times of shock and inaccessibility. Interventions will align with the South Sudan National Health Policy (2016-2026) objectives, Boma Health Initiative (BHI), the 2023 South Sudan HRP, and the UNICEF South Sudan 2023- 2025 Health programme. CRS and HFO will coordinate with UNICEF-funded nutrition cluster partners, conduct malnutrition screening for children under-five and pregnant and lactating women and refer cases to the nearest nutrition facility. Health and nutrition services will be co-located in health and UNICEF funded nutrition facilities to maximize efficiency and strengthen coordination at the lowest level of health service delivery.

1 - Needs

South Sudan as the world’s youngest nation has faced decades of sub-national violence and is experiencing three consecutive years of widespread flooding and a protracted macro-economic crisis. The country is home to some of the worst health indicators with Under-five, infant mortality, and neonatal mortality rates estimated to be 98, 63 and 40 per 1,000 live births, respectively. This reflects a heavy disease burden that continues to be worsened by the secondary impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic as well as the ongoing food security crisis. In South Sudan, 75% of under-five deaths are directly attributed to malaria, diarrhoea, and pneumonia with malaria accounting for 50% of mortality. 40% of under-five deaths are estimated to occur within the first month of life and WHO estimates that 5% of deaths are attributable to low birth weight, reflecting an age-adjusted death rate of 23/100,000 population and ranks the country at 18 in the world. Nationally, only 39% of children under one are fully immunized for age. In Ayod and Fangak counties, immunization coverage for children within the same age group is 15% and 31% respectively and dropout rate is 33% in Ayod county and 9% in Fangak county. This leaves children highly susceptible to vaccine preventable diseases and has caused the building up of cohorts of unvaccinated and under vaccinated children. Ayod and Fangak Counties in Jonglei state are among the most heavily affected and efforts to increase coverage continue to be constrained by the COVID-19 pandemic and limited health workforce. The 2022 South Sudan Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) estimated that 5.5 million people are in dire need of lifesaving health services with Fangak county ranked among the most severely affected or catastrophic counties while Ayod is one of the second most affected or catastrophic counties. According to UNICEF baseline values for April-June 2022, only 17 of the 26 facilities (65%) in Ayod and Fangak provide at least 75% of essential health services package. In addition, only two of the 26 health facilities (8%) in the two counties had essential medicines available within the same period. 8 of the 26 facilities in Ayod and Fangak are operating in flooded areas, and there is significant risk this could increase as flood water levels continue to rise and spread. The nearest secondary referral facility for Ayod and Fangak is in Malakal and is only accessible via river or helicopter. This makes referral difficult and increases the risk of complications and death for cases that require urgent secondary care.

2 - Project activities

CRS and HFO will target the entire population of both Fangak and Ayod Counties and will support 26 facilities or Mobile-Medical Units (MMUs) to ensure access to quality health services to the population within their respective catchment areas. Communities with impeded access will receive health services using BHI and outreach strategies, focusing on the particular health needs of children under 5, pregnant women and those of reproductive age, internally displaced populations and people with physical disabilities. Services will be targeted to need and provided with confidentiality and dignity and without discrimination. Patients with complications will be referred to the nearest hospital. The proposed project will reach an estimated direct and indirect population of 172,620 individuals in Fangak and 219,504 in Ayod, for a total of 392,124 beneficiaries (188,200 male / 203, 904 female) as detailed below: CRS will implement activities in Ayod county and HFO will implement in Fangak County, building on their understanding of the operating context gained through implementation of health programs in those counties in the last 3 years. CRS will lead the consortium and report to donors, providing grant management, quality assurance, technical oversight and capacity strengthening to HFO. CRS and HFO will employ context specific, cost-effective and adaptive approaches to support facilities and CHDs in the delivery of a comprehensive package of quality essential primary healthcare services that are child friendly, safe, high-quality, aligned with the Ministry of Health basic package of essential health and nutrition services (PEHS), and compliant with COVID-19 precautionary measures. Collaborating closely with the CHD, communities, and relevant stakeholders, activities will include direct support for essential service delivery at health facilities or MMUs; support for outreach activities in stable and accessible areas; and implementation of the BHI to increase coverage and equitable access to services alongside capacity building of health workers on integrated community case management, disease surveillance and reporting. Complementary facility-based and community-based services will maximize cost-effectiveness, accountability for results, and sustainability, and prioritize hard-to-reach communities. CRS and HFO will bring services closest to communities, using local canoes and engine speed boats where necessary to conduct mobile outreach. Through BHI, the project will empower communities to play a leading role. CRS and HFO will: • Ensure that facilities have adequate water, sanitation and solar power; coordinate minor repairs or upgrades. The project proposes to utilize Safari tents for relocated facilities or those in areas prone to flooding, rather than constructing permanent infrastructure; this approach was successfully implemented by CRS in Ayod. • Deploy Clinical Officers (CO) and midwives to 13 Primary Health Care Centers or MMUs (PHCCs); COs will provide clinical oversight to Primary Health Care Units (PHCUs) and continuous supervision and medical education to seconded CHD clinical staff. Using a shift duty roster, facilities will provide services at all times and will maintain satellite phones for timely Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR) reporting, referrals and security updates. • Maintain county pharmacies in existing infrastructure to receive and distribute supplies, maintain cold chain systems and manage routine vaccination. Pharmacists and medical logisticians will manage pharmaceutical distribution and reporting. Health facilities / MMUs will maintain immunization schedules and supplies and mobilize communities on vaccination days. • Ensure quality control and continuous improvement through the MEAL approach, including joint-monitoring with CHDs .

3 - Project indicators

CRS and HFO will develop and implement a dynamic MEAL system to capture sex- and age-disaggregated data; monitor progress on key deliverables; assess the appropriateness and effectiveness of activities; ensure quality control; and remain accountable to community partners, UNICEF, MoH and key stakeholders. CRS MEAL systems focus on multi-directional accountability and transparency from the community to the donor through its proprietary SMILER approach, which includes a manual and data flow map that ensure smooth flow of quality data among partners, UNICEF and external stakeholders. The MEAL system will adapt standard MOH HMIS tools including facility registers, summary forms, and reporting protocols to ensure full compliance with UNICEF requirements and contribute to national monitoring and reporting on the DHIS 2 system.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CRS and HFO will build on existing systems and practices and consult with diverse groups within beneficiary communities to ensure robust feedback and response mechanisms that reflect local context and community preferences are functioning and are used by participants (particularly vulnerable project participants) for project improvement and to voice protection concerns. CRS will continue to communicate proactively to ensure that all facility staff and project participants have knowledge of reporting channels and feel empowered and encouraged to offer feedback and report protection and safeguarding concerns. Ongoing review of feedback will form part of the learning and the project will ensure timely provision of feedback to community members during field monitoring, community engagement sessions, meeting with key stakeholders, and other appropriate channels. CRS South Sudan FCRMs combine a mix of face-to-face, technology-based, static and active feedback and complaints channels to gather feedback, complaints and perspectives from different segments of the population, including marginalized and vulnerable groups. FCRMs currently being used include the use of local structures such as community health workers, lead mothers and FCRM focal person at health facilities. To uphold safeguarding requirements and inform adaptive management practices, CRS strives to ensure that at a minimum, three feedback and complaints channels are in place, and at least one channel that is safe and accessible to all members of the community is available to receive anonymous feedback and complaints. CRS uses a standard set of categories to ensure that all programmatic and sensitive feedback and complaints received are appropriately managed, referred or escalated as needed, and responded to according to agency Safeguarding Policy and program management and MEAL requirements.

Protection and gender mainstreaming will be fundamental and cut across all project activities to ensure that men, women, boys, and girls and other members of vulnerable groups have equitable access to safe and gender sensitive services in the two counties. The project will promote equal opportunities for men, women, boys, and girls and will seek to address existing traditional norms that lead to sex and age biases and inequities through the substantial and deliberate involvement of women in the design and implementation of the project.CRS will train CRS and partner project staff, MoH staff, volunteers, and selected community leaders/influencers on key gender and protection issues. Project data will be disaggregated by sex and age, and project monitoring activities will intentionally engage women, girls, and other vulnerable groups to gather their feedback and perceptions. CRS will aim for gender balance in frontline staffing to facilitate women’s safe and dignified access to heath services. Health facilities and community activities supported by the project will help build awareness of and ensure safe and dignified access to GBV services. Community engagement activities will be segregated by sex and age to create safe spaces for more honest dialogue. Since women and men do not have the same degree of access nor use the same channels of information, these separate community engagement activities will foster an environment through which all groups can meaningfully contribute. CRS plans to tailor communication approaches to help meet the different information needs of various groups. For example, CRS will utilize community groups through the BHI, translate materials into local languages as appropriate, engage female volunteers and health workers, use picture messages, and share information in spaces where women are likely to frequent. As women and girls are less likely to be involved in leadership roles and decision-making at the community level, CRS will purposefully engage them while explaining the importance of their active involvement to communities, family members, and key influencers, to promote gender equality and reduce potential risks associated with shifting power dynamics. CRS will leverage the different project structures as opportunities to tackle gender inequalities and will work with government officials, religious leaders, community leaders, and village health teams to increase awareness about the importance of women’s role in decision-making and access to/control over resources, address inequitable food sharing practices/norms and promote positive coping strategies to enhance the nutrition and wellbeing of all family members.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

CRS understands the operating challenges in South Sudan where the risk profile is elevated. CRS mitigates risk through strong community engagement to ensure acceptance, long-term relationships with trusted partners, well-developed program design and monitoring systems, high-quality regular audit coverage, and comprehensive policies enforced pursuant to strict internal controls and operating procedures. CRS updates its risk analysis matrix regularly and maintains a risk register for every project. CRS South Sudan Country Program is supported by regional and global teams with dedicated experts in security, compliance, PSEA, finance, HR, supply chain and logistics. CRS carries out extensive screening of potential employees, partners, vendors prior to engagement. Security Risks to project staff or participants:CRS South Sudan has a Field Safety and Security Plan for CRS and its partners’ activities in South Sudan which details specific guidelines and procedures to ensure the safety and security of personnel, programs, and assets both nationally and by locationThe locations of outreach services will be informed by security assessments that ensure areas are accessible for staff and beneficiaries.Risk of Fraud, Misuse, or Diversion: CRS has comprehensive policies and procedures and robust internal control mechanisms in place to prevent misuse or diversion of funds or assets, built on strict segregation of duties in all operational processes. CRS and partners have an established robust supply chain, and use the Logistics Cluster vendors with proven track record over many years of engagement with CRS to deliver supplies at the target locations. CRS’ Sub-Recipient Financial Monitoring Policy (SRFMP) requires comprehensive in-person review of subrecipients’ accounting standards and internal controls before engaging themRisk of abuse or exploitation of project participants especially children or vulnerable adults (PSEA and safeguarding): All project staff, MOH staff, volunteers, and community leaders will be trained on safeguarding, including Protection Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). CRS will clearly communicate to beneficiaries expected conduct of staff, services to which they are entitled, and how they can provide feedback or complaints. CRS’ Code of Conduct and Ethics reinforces CRS’ commitment to the highest standards of moral and ethical integrity with respect to harassment, exploitation and abuse, as well as discrimination, conflict of interest and financial controls. All CRS staff members must report any concerns or suspicions of violations to CRS: there are several safe and confidential reporting mechanisms available: a global hotline toll-free with local numbers in each country, an anonymous web-based reporting system (ethicspoint), or directly to a supervisor or the Country Representative CRS will investigate allegations of misconduct and protect those individuals who in good faith report such allegations. CRS is committed to working only with organizations—including partners, vendors and suppliers—who are equally committed to the dignity of individuals and equally vigilant in preventing and addressing harassment, abuse and exploitation. Per its Partner Safeguarding Policy, CRS adopts a robust methodology to support and monitor its partners’ compliance with comprehensive safeguarding minimum standards, including a structured assessment prior to signing any agreement, monitoring, and capacity strengthening cycle to ensure compliance.
