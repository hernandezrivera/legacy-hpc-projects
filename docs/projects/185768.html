Project Summary

Caritas Bangladesh (CB) has been operating 5 Multi-purpose Women and Girl’s Centers (MWGCs) in camps The project goal is “Forcibly displaced people of Myanmar, especially women and adolescent girls live in safe and supportive environment and protected from violence”. GBV interventions provide essential psycho-social support and enhance the protection of vulnerable women, girls, elderly, gender diverse population, persons with disabilities, and other vulnerable groups. They can participate safely and in culturally-acceptable ways, and provides outreach from these structures to the HH by volunteers drawn from within the community. The project follows the “Do No Harm” principle and aims to ensure safety, meaningful access and dignity of affected. CB’s Safeguarding Policy, AGD, Inclusion, Anti-Trafficking guidelines will be reflected across project activities. Community engagement to understand their perception of protection and psycho-social needs and risks, and identify appropriate, safe and adaptive responses that complement and strengthen the community’s existing capacity to enhance the protective environment and the networks. Facilitate are access for all to gather and information sharing, indoor activities, and a range of relevant services to prevention of COVID 19. Women-led GBV preparedness and mitigation action plan will prepare, identify GBV problems, causes and solutions and where they can bring their capacities to improve the GBV preparedness and mitigation actions. Portable skills development training for women and girls on different trades, help them to give service inside the camp for earning. Structured PSS activities roll out for helping them to heal from stresses, to know more functional coping mechanisms to help them adapt with the evolving nature of the stresses in camps. Psycho-social counselling with clinical supervision by MHPSS Specialist and Case Management support provide to the affected.
The project will identify the persons with disabilities using Washington Group Short Question (WGSQ) and provide aid and supportive devices and develop/improve the infrastructures as required.
Meeting with social and religious leaders, centre management committee, male and boy’s engagement influenced community participating. Capacity strengthening of women leaders for community-based approach.
Remote case management system and community-based approach will be continued and strengthen to meet-up the needs, if COVID- 19 comes back again.
A systemic MEAL mechanism will be followed to ensure AAP principles. CB follows it’s FRM and stakeholder consultation to adjust approaches based upon community feedback and information on emerging challenges/needs.
CB conducts safeguarding and PSEA training for staff, volunteers and orientation to visitors, vendors. CB have one PSEA Focal and maintain a reporting channel.
CB has been working with PERU team if preparedness and response to emergencies like- cyclone, flood, heavy rain, strong wind. Conduct session on environment and homestead gardening. CB keeps facilities full of green trees and garden, which inspire the children and community people to make the environment green and fresh.
CB closely works with Government like- NGOAB, Deputy Commissioner, CiC and sectors for approvals and coordination. Regularly submits 5Ws and Sitrep to sectors and participated PWG Coordination, Task Team meeting and ATWG, ADWG meeting at Cox’s Bazar level and camp level CiC meeting and protection sector meetings. CB is the Protection Focal of camp 4 Extension and regularly conduct camp level protection coordination meeting in direction of CiC and guidance of PWG. Any probable duplication of interventions in camp will be managed through well coordination and consultation with Focal Point, CiC office and site management.
The program will participate in established referral pathways for protection services and will strengthen these pathways through its community reach.

1 - Identified Needs

Rohingya women and girls are at risk of GBV, including abuse, neglect, domestic violence, forced and early marriage, and sexual exploitation. Response to trends of IPV is rendered difficult by the lack of access to justice and the perceived impunity in the camp. “In the Shadows of the Pandemic: the Gendered Impact of COVID-19 on Rohingya and Host Communities,”- a Rapid Gender Analysis conducted by the humanitarian community launched in October 2020, shows that GBV has significantly increased, including IPV, child marriages, polygamy, violence against transgender persons, while access to support is more restricted. Support and funding is needed to expand activities that are focused on the prevention and response to GBV and promoting gender equality. Rohingya-led participatory action research found that 88.7% reported experiencing symptoms indicative of depression, 84% reported symptoms indicative of emotional distress, and 61.2% report symptoms indicative of PTSD. (“The Torture in My Mind: The Right to Mental Health for Rohingya Survivors of Genocide in Myanmar and Bangladesh” (Participatory Action Research Report). Fortify Rights. December 2020. Pp. 10-11). Restriction of women and adolescent girls’ movements in camps and public spaces further hinder their ability to access entry points that provide information on GBV response, services, information and other possibilities of support. This hinders their recovery and healing. Therefore, safe spaces remain crucial entry points for them to come together and heal in a safe space as well as to receive vital information. The lack of opportunities for women increases their vulnerability, their exclusion of the community life, and adoption of negative coping mechanisms that are potentially harmful. There is a need for women to be engaged in developing life skills activities that build their resilience and decrease their vulnerability to foster social and economic empowerment. There is a pressing need to engage male and boys in an accountable manner to prevent and respond to GBV. The need for continuous and structured engagement with male and boys is important to ensure the sustainability of GBV programming as well as to mitigate backlash and resistance to gender transformative work that forms the core of GBV prevention activities. There are limited opportunities for refugees women and girls with diverse age and identities, to participate and influence policy decisions affecting their lives. Some of the challenges in participation of women and girls in decision-making and activities are low levels of literacy and gender norms which restrict women and girls from public spaces or from taking a public role. Predominant camp governance structure involves unelected male block leaders, with a total lack of female representation. Abuse of power by block leaders and fear of repercussions to be a major obstacle for survivors’ access to specialized services, which has resulted in a culture of impunity that seriously affects women and girls’ health and well-being, security and dignity.” Various factors constrain refugee women and girls’ timely access to multi-sectoral services, which include social stigma and rejection, feelings of shame and self-blame, threats of retaliation, and low awareness of services and their benefits.Improving the quality and accessibility of life-saving GBV response services, particularly specialized case management and mental health and psychosocial support that addresses the specific needs of different vulnerable groups. Engaging in continuous advocacy efforts to enhance survivors’ access to justice and security services, without compromising the safety and dignity of survivors. Reinforcing community mobilization strategies through Community-based protection approach that promote positive transformation of social and gender norms that fuel and normalize gender based violence.

2 - Activities or outputs

1. Develop women led GBV Prevention action plan: Multipurpose Women and Girls Centre (MWGC) will aim to support affected women and girls to recovery and healing from Gender based Violence in a safe and timely manner. Women led GBV reduction action plan will be prepared with the participation of women and adolescent girls. Total 5 no. of action plan will be develop and a total of 250 women and adolescents, elderly, persons with disabilities and gender divers population participated in developing the action plan. Approx. direct beneficiaries will be 625 women and girls of the targeted areas of 4 camps. COVID 19 prevention activities will be integrated in all activity planning and implementation. Review workshop will be held to harmonize the action plan.2. Capacity building of women leaders at Majhi blocks for the implementation of action plan: Caritas Bangladesh (CB) will provide training for capacity building of the Rohingya women leaders from Majhi blocks for the implementation of the action plan. These activities will be planned according to the issues being faced by the women and girls currently, and where possible, these sessions can be supported or conducted by other actors with expertise in the subject, rather than (or alongside) MWGCs staff.3. Conduction of life skills and awareness sessions: Dedicated Facilitators deployed to conduct awareness sessions and community discussions and raise. The sessions and discussion might be determined by the context, what services are available, and what the women are interested in, but could include: how women can participate in decision making, available services, including GBV services, and referral pathways, stress management and coping strategies.4. Meeting with religious, social, elders and other traditional leaders for sensitization of GBV: Meeting will be organized with religious & social leaders, elders and other traditional leaders for sensitization of GBV mitigation issues.5. Providing of vocational training to the women and adolescent girls: To become resourceful in portable skills CB will provide Technical & Vocational Education (TVE) to the interested women and adolescents girls. The women and girls will gain various occupational (job/business-oriented) skills such as handcraft, fashion design, dress making and electronic gadgets repair works. The training will help them to develop and implement ideas that will allow them to serve their communities and earn a livelihood from their skills. Need based Instructors from Rohingya and Host community will be hired to conduct the TVE.6. Psychosocial support and case management for different age groups: Project provides counselling support due to GBV, violence in partnerships, neighborhood disputes or instances of child marriage. Caritas staff will support the community volunteers in providing integrated case management and psychosocial support for individuals and families at risk. The volunteers gradually perform these tasks themselves. Trained volunteers ensure support to affected receive high quality and timely PSS services. If the situation requires it, this is organized in a safe and secluded room in the community center, in order to protect the privacy of those affected and refer to specialized services while required.7. Maintain liaison with CiC office, other sectors, sub-sectors and other service providing agencies in camps for accessing services and referral.8. Identify and provide supportive devices to the persons with disabilities: CB will organize campaign for identifying the needs of supportive devices for the persons with disabilities among the women and girls in collaboration of specialized actors working at camps. It also provides need based supportive devices as per experts advice. The facilities will be renovated and improved to disabilities friendly in collaboration with specialized actors.
