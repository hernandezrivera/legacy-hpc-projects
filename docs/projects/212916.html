Project Summary

The 2024-2025 Syria Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) sets out the framework within which the humanitarian community will respond to large-scale humanitarian and protection needs in Syria, flexibly leveraging the various response modalities.
Capturing these capacities and efforts under a single strategic framework, the plan aims to increase the effectiveness of the response by improving the identification of needs and gaps, reducing overlap and duplication, and monitoring context changes and progress against collective agreed response results. The Humanitarian Coordinator for Syria and the Regional Humanitarian Coordinator for the Syria Crisis lead and coordinate humanitarian action inside Syria. OCHA, in line with its global mandate, supports the humanitarian leadership in effectively coordinating the response inside Syria. To do so, OCHA maintains country offices in Syria and Türkiye, and a regional office in Amman.
OCHA Türkiye coordinates cross-border humanitarian assistance delivered from Türkiye to Syria as mandated by the UN Security Council resolutions 2642 under the leadership of the Deputy Regional Humanitarian Coordinator (DRHC).
OCHA requires sustained levels of funding to be able to maintain and strengthen effective humanitarian response coordination from these various offices, facilitate strategic and operational coordination platforms, provide information management services, manage pooled funds, support humanitarian access analysis and negotiation, ensure civil-military coordination, and provide public information and advocacy on the humanitarian situation. Funding requirements for this project reflect the needs of OCHA's three offices directly involved in the Syria response.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Entering its 13th year of crisis, Syria grapples with a catastrophe of unprecedented magnitude, with new emergencies adding to existing crises and taking a heavy toll on the people living in Syria. Factors driving humanitarian needs amongst the population in Syria have increased included but not exclusive of protracted displacement, economic decline, global inflation, escalation of hostilities and the aftermath of the earthquakes that struck south-eastern Türkiye and northern Syria in February 2023 are some of the biggest challenges Syrians currently face. An estimated 16.7 million people continue to require some form of humanitarian assistance in 2024, an increase of 1.3 million from 2023. Across the country, the long-standing needs of an estimated 7.2 million IDPs in informal settlements and planned camps, as well as of overstretched host communities and hundreds of thousands of people having returned to their places of origin in recent years have remained staggering and become more deeply entrenched.Syria remains a major protection crisis, with civilians exposed to multiple protection risks related to ongoing hostilities; conflict and military operations have again escalated between non-state armed groups, and government and pro-government forces, affecting north-eastern, south-western, and central parts of Syria in particular. Civilians have suffered death and injuries as a result of air and ground strikes, drone strikes, targeted killings, attacks using various types of improvised explosive devices (IED) and gunfire. The effects of new and protracted displacement; and the depletion of socio-economic resources triggering negative coping strategies. The large scale of needs requires dynamic and flexible coordination support and systems to facilitate effective humanitarian response.Provision of humanitarian assistance across Syria by numerous Syrian NGOs, international NGOs, the Red Cross/ Crescent Movement, and United Nations agencies occurs through Syria-based humanitarian program, interagency convoys, and cross border response. The different response modalities provided by the diverse humanitarian actors working on the Syria response, stress the heightened need for ensuring effective and efficient coordination structures. OCHA recognizes the risks of instrumentalization of aid in the Syrian context and the effects this may have on the protection of vulnerable people. OCHA also recognizes risks related to GBV, SEA, and safety of humanitarian workers and the potential negative affect this may have on affected populations. OCHA will continue to support inter-agency efforts to mitigate protection risks for humanitarian workers, such as the SSG-endorsed Duty of Care Framework for humanitarian organizations working in Syria.
