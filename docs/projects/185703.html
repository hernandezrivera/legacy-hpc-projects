Project Summary

"The project intends to provide the lifesaving/humanitarian assistance to refugees in camps, livelihood recovery and resilience-building support for host community population, targeting most vulnerable population such as poor households relying on wage labor, households with no or very limited productive assets, households affected by disasters. Households with high dependency ratio, headed by women, children, chronically sick persons and households having an individual with special needs like PWD, PLW, elderly persons will get priority while targeting beneficiaries. The ultimate goal of the project is to contribute to ensuring food and nutrition security through emergency assistance, livelihood recovery support to promote income for both Refugee and host community populations. To achieve the goal, the project intends to undertake food security and livelihoods interventions such as (1) Food assistance (emergency cooked meals) (2) Livelihoods assistance (support for homestead based food production and IGA promotion with nutrition education, food safety, and hygiene sessions to improve dietary diversity) (3) Self-reliance (support for kitchen gardening, food processing, and small business, etc.) (4) Skill development (support for skill-based technical training and inputs) (5) Disaster Risks Management through small scale risk mitigation measures. CFW will be linked with small-scale risk mitigation measures to reduce disaster risk as well as to provide temporary employment for the affected population.
The project will ensure a safe environment by upholding gender equality as per IASC Gender and Age Marker. Protection mainstreaming principles are some of the measures taken to ensure gender-responsive programming. The project will give priority to special groups; female-headed households, PLW, under 5 children, PWDs, and the Elderly during the implementation of projects interventions according to their needs.
The interventions will ensure the active participation of the community in different activities. The project will have a contingency plan to respond to man-made and natural disasters (monsoon floods, landslides, and cyclones) with close collaboration with all stakeholders.

1 - Identified Needs

"Cox’s Bazar District is one of the poorest districts in Bangladesh, highly susceptible to environmental and economic shocks such as natural disaster, manmade humanitarian crisis, and COVID-19/health pandemics. Almost 50% of the population in this district belongs to lower-income groups who basically rely on unreliable income sources mainly wage labor. These populations are the main victim of these disasters and which makes them more vulnerable both socially and economically. The proposed project will be implemented in the areas included in Joint Response Plan for Cox’s Bazar district coordinated by ISCG in where, women and girls constitute about half of the population (50.57%). SMART survey (December 2020 for refugees), shows a stagnant rate of malnutrition with slow recovery rate (GAM 10.9% - CI 8.0-14.7%). The coverage survey (SQUEAC Nov 2019) indicates a significant increase in access to and coverage of nutrition services [SAM: 76.9% (95% CI: 72.1-81.7%), MAM 61% (95% CI: 57.5-64.6%)], it is still below Sphere standards (>90%). Stunting among children 0-59 months (>30%) remains a major concern. While for the Bangladeshi host community the SMART survey 2021 conducted in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upzila showed a GAM rate of 9.3% (7.5 - 11.5 95% C.I) with a SAM rate of 0.7% (0.3 - 1.4 95% C.I.). According to REVA-IV 2021, levels of vulnerability remain high in refugees. 96 % of all Rohingya refugees are highly and moderately vulnerable and in continued need of humanitarian assistance to meet, their basic needs. Despite the current level of humanitarian assistance, 49 percent of Rohingya households cannot afford the minimum expenditure basket (MEB). Compared to 2019, economic vulnerability has slightly increased among Rohingya (3 percent) and host communities (7 percent). Two-thirds of Rohingya and one-third of host community households are engaged in crisis or emergency livelihood coping strategies to cover food and/or their basic needs compromising their resilience and future productivity. Among Rohingya households, 36 percent reported no ways to cover any unforeseen future emergency expense. Over 31,000 of the camps’ population are still living in areas considered to be at high risk of deadly flooding and landslides, with drainage congestion and waterlogging. The situation has been further aggravated by the COVID-19 crisis with a severe impact on the income and earning opportunities. According to the J-MSNA report 2021, Needs prioritized by households included shelter materials (reported by 63% of households among their top three priority needs), access to food (60%), and access to income-generating activities (IGAs) (41%). According to REVA-IV 2021, an overall vulnerability in the host community also increased, with 51 percent of the population being moderate to highly vulnerable in 2020 compared to 41 percent in 2019. This increase is also reflected nationally where poverty rates were found to have increased in 2020. The economic contraction and decline in economic activity across most sectors during the COVID-19 lockdown in a population highly dependent on daily wage labor was the main driver. The pandemic and its subsequent containment measures impacted food security and livelihood execrating existing poverty in the host community.
