Project Summary

Due to the recurrent operation of insurgency in the Northeastern region of Nigeria, which has rendered a large number of the vulnerable population. The three states of Borno Adamawa and Yobe have been crossly devastated, with thousands of people affected leaving them without homes or means of livelihood, while being exposed to diseases and health challenges.
This project is aimed at strengthening the Provision of Primary Health Care Services and closing the gaps in Cholera Response to IDPs and Host Communities in Girei, Shelleng, Yola south and Fufore LGAs, Adamawa state.
CPPLI will focus on strengthening the readiness and response actions for Cholera Outbreak response in Adamawa State. CPPLI in partnership with the SPHCDA plans to reach the communities selected in Adamawa through the provision of an integrated package of health services to children under five years old, women and their families in IDP camp clinics as well as facilitation of referral services to secondary and tertiary health institutions. The funds will be used for addressing health needs including scaling-up of the emergency health services delivery, medicines and supplies in these overburdened health facilities to effectively cater to vulnerable populations most of whom are women and children.
CPPLI will also directly reach out to selected population with the provision of integrated package of health services that focuses on the emergency control and response to the recurrent upsurge of epidemics (cholera, yellow fever), the treatments and prevention of widespread common and communicable diseases (Hepatitis, TB, RTI, Malaria), as well as the immediate and sustainable access to life saving immunizations and vaccinations (DPT, HBV, Measles, PENTA, Yellow Fever) , thereby decreasing risk for morbidity and mortality related to vaccine-preventable disease/death amongst children under five years old, women and their families in IDP camps, returnees, inaccessible and host communities. CPPLI will work with the Rapid Response Teams, Adamawa State MOH, SPHCDA and other partners to address the critical gaps hindering access to emergency primary health care services including addressing emerging challenges and critical needs. These needs include: inadequate number and distribution of health care workers, renovation/reconstruction of health facilities because most of the facilities have been damaged, poor storage capacity and dilapidated warehouses, poor logistics management system, lack of emergency drugs and supplies and absence of referral systems due to lack of ambulance and security. In Adamawa state, CPPLI in partnership with the SPHCDA and the State Ministries of Health will ensure that basic supplies and commodities required for effective Cholera OBR are available and prepositioned to reduce mortalities associated with the outbreak.

1 - Needs

In line with 2022 humanitarian response plan, the conflict stemming from the insurgency of non-state armed groups (NSAGs) in north-east Nigeria continues as intensely as ever. The attacks and insecurity have displaced millions of people, devastated agricultural production and other livelihoods, cut off essential services, and caused a crisis of protection. Some 8.4 million people in the north-east states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY states) will need humanitarian aid in 2022, only slightly fewer than a year ago. Of these, 2.2 million are internally displaced; 1.5 million are returnees who lack essential services and livelihoods; and 3.9 million are members of communities affected by their hosting of internally displaced people. This figure also includes the majority (an estimated 733,000) of the 1 million people in areas currently inaccessible to international humanitarian actors. Hundreds of thousands of IDPs are concentrated in ‘garrison towns’ where the Nigerian Armed Forces defend perimeters that are risky to venture beyond. This creates congested, unhealthy living conditions, and constrains displaced farmers’ livelihood options. A major cholera outbreak in the BAY states in 2021 has underlined affected people’s vulnerability and the difficulty of precluding grave threats to life and well-being. Displaced population in Adamawa state continue to experience challenges in accessing quality health services largely because indigenous IDPs hardly adapt to the culture of dwelling in formal camps but rather prefer to lodge with relatives, rent low profile accommodation depending on the income of the household. This compound health challenges except in recent times (December 2019) when a bill which considers unemployed youth, IDPs and other vulnerable groups to have access to medical care services. • Available at; Nigeria: Northeast Response - Adamawa Health Sector Working Group Bulletin No. 10, October 2019 | Humanitarian Response• Data Displacement Matrix

2 - Activities

Activity 1.1: Identification, training and supportive supervision of 30 members of the Rapid Response Teams and & 50 patent medicine vendors and 30 village heads to be able to reach ,7,000 persons in host communities and hard to reach population with surveillance, prevention and response actions against outbreaks.Activity 1.1.2: Training of 150 healthcare workers in public and private health facilities on cholera case identification, reporting, Infection Prevention and control in Shelleng and Girei LGAs.Activity 1.1.3: Procurement and distribution of IPC materials to 10 health facilities on two monthly basis in Girei and Shelleng LGAs. This will equip healthcare workers with the right tools to protect themselves, the fellow workers and the communities from disease transmission. Activity 1.1.4: Quarterly meetings with patent medicine vendors, village heads and the RRT members to identify successes, constraints and way forwards Activity 1.2.1: Training of CHIPS and deployment of ODK/Kobo collect Activity 1.2.2: Carryout outreach services in communities for RCCE, ACS and support for cholera case management and identification of other priority diseases Activity 2.1.1: Training of 30 HCWs from 6 facilities on minimum service package of service deliveries for a period of 2 days Activity 2.1.2: Conduct regular supportive supervision to the facilities and during outreach services to ensure compliance and provide on the job training by supervisors Activity 2.1.3: Weekly outreach services to communities within the catchment area of the PHC to provide minimum service package of healthcare services to the targeted populations.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

CPPLI will prioritize safety and protection of all project stakeholders during the implementation of the project. CPPLI have guiding policies on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, safeguarding and child protection in line with 6 core principles of PSEA from the interagency standing committee (IASC). Staff and volunteers from health facilities including community health influencers promoters and services (CHIPS) and any other relevant staff supporting the project will be oriented on child safeguarding and PSEA policies and sign the policies as part of compliance. This is to ensure beneficiaries are not subjected to harm as a result of their interaction with CPPLI staff or volunteers. CPPLI is always conscious of the local norms and conflicts dynamics and will work with all the relevant stakeholders to identify any key concerns and factors that could lead to resistance.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

CPPLI will ensure that project beneficiaries are part of the decision making process and that community will take lead in the process of engagement of CHIPS. CPPLI will organize inception meeting with community leaders and others relevant stakeholders and personnel to share key information about the project so that community will take ownership of the project and promote sustainability. CPPLI will establish appropriate and context based feedback mechanism so that project beneficiaries will provide feedback about the project implementation. Satisfaction survey will be conducted with beneficiaries in form of focus group discussion and key information interview so that affected beneficiaries can amplify their satisfaction or dissatisfaction about the project implementation in line with the services provided to them.
