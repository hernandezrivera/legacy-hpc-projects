Project Summary

The project activities are aligned with the GBV sub cluster specific objectives 1 of ensuring the provision of critical protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas and Specific objectives 2 of prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience. Therefore, CHADO will provide Comprehensive GBV Case Management to extremely vulnerable 300 women, 50 boys, 200 girls and 50 men, distribute 4000 dignity kits accompanied by discussions, information sharing, awareness raising on GBV and RRH including available services to survivors. CHADO will establish 3 Women and Girls Friendly Spaces in Pibor, 3 in Rumbek North and Improve 6 WGFS already established in Nasir and Koch counties. CHADO will also provide individual, group and specialized PSS and other related services (e.g recreational activities, livelihood activities) to 2000 girls, 1200 boys, 800 men and 2500 women. CHADO will further conduct awareness raising activities on GBV including Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) on challenging harmful cultural practices such as early/forced marriage. COVID-19 prevention messages including sharing of information on where to access services targeting 3,000 men, 2,000 boys, 2,000 women and 1,400 girls will also be included in the awareness raising sessions. CHADO will train 1000 women and 800 girls ToT on the production and use of fuel-efficient stoves as a means to prevent GBV and also a means of livelihood as the moulded stoves can be sold in the market or exchange for goods. In addition, CHADO will train 150 program staff including volunteers and community workers on GBV guiding principles and GBV referral pathways including protection risks mitigation and early warning and further establish and improve 4 GBV referral pathways in Nasir, Koch, Pibor and Rumbek North. CHADO will lastly train 100 community frontline staff on GBV case management and PSS.
Pibor County has an estimated population of 222,297, the PIN are 73,129 people and GBV Sub cluster targets 51,191 people. CHADO will be targeting 5% of the GBV sub cluster target which is 2,560 people (1000 women, 300 men, 800 girls and 460 boys). Among them are an estimated 384 of people living with disabilities. Similarly, Nasir County, with an estimated population of 286,628 people has 46,544 PIN and GBV Sub Cluster targets 23,272 people. CHADO will be targeting 15% of the GBV Sub Cluster target which is 3,490 people (1,200 women, 500 men, 1000 girls and 790 boys). Among them are estimated 524 people living with disabilities. Likewise, Rumbek North has an estimated population of 70,949 people, and the PIN are 21,754 and GBV Sub Cluster targets 10,877 people. CHADO will be targeting 10% of the GBV Sub cluster target which is 1088 people ( 500 women, 100 men, 338 girls and 150 boys). Among them are an estimated 163 people living with disabilities. Koch County has an estimated population of 96257, and the PIN are 31311 and GBV sub cluster targets 21,918 people. CHADO will be targeting 1262 people (100 men, 600 women, 300 girls and 262 boys). Among them are an estimated 189 people living with disabilities.
CHADO is currently on ground in Pibor, Nasir and Rumbek North implementing GBV and CP in Pibor and Nasir and Communications for Development in Rumbek North which has child protection components and has volunteers and community staff conducting basic GBV activities in Koch retained after our project ended in 2019.

1 - Needs

Years of conflicts coupled with floods and the outbreak of the COVID-19 pandemic have made worse the humanitarian situation in South Sudan. As the country struggle's with the implementation of the revitalized peace agreement, the delay continues to impede on humanitarian response as the country still remains a protection crisis in 2021. Some 1.6 million people remain internally displaced and another 2.2 million people as refugees in the region (HNO, 2021). With its limited resources, the nation is paralyzed by corruption, reports of embezzlement of state funds with very weak accountability from its core leadership. The unwillingness and commitment from both sides to form a working government has caused uncertainty in the peace agreement, limiting investment in basic services which are holding people back from sustainability and development. As the people continue to suffer from conflict, covid-19 and floods, OCHA reports that about 8.3 million people over two thirds of the total population are estimated to need some kind of humanitarian assistance (OCHA, January 2021). Another 5.82 million, almost half of the population are likely to face IPC phase 3 or worsening acute food insecurity in January, including 11,000 people in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity category in Pibor County, Nasir and Koch Counties are at (IPC Phase 4) and Rumbek North at (IPC Phase 3). The situation in Pibor is particularly an alarming one as stated by the UN agencies on 18th December 2020. FAO, UNICEF and WFP released a joint press release in Juba titled “UN agencies’ alarm at worsening hunger in South Sudan. Conflict, floods and COVID-19 push more people to extreme hunger. The UN agencies called for immediate humanitarian access to Pibor County, Jonglei state where people have run out of food and are facing catastrophic levels of hunger according to the newly released Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC). Gender Based Violence is one of the most critical threats to protection and well-being of women and children. GBV was already rife prior to the conflict but recent reports indicate that it’s gearing up towards epidemic proportions. As a result of structural gender inequality and unequal gender power relationships, with women and girls and especially those who are internally displaced or living near cantonment sites, or with a disability continue to suffer from GBV. Flooding, armed conflict in pockets of areas, sub-national violence and COVID-19 are escalating the risk of GBV including Conflict- Related Sexual Violence (CRSV) and sexual exploitation and abuse (HNO, 2021). Based on GBV assessments conducted by CHADO in Pibor, Nasir, Koch and Rumbek North, GBV cases such as sexual assault, physical assault, rape including denial of resources and harmful cultural practices including early/forced marriage were reported. GBV risks are associated with women and girls walking long distances to collect firewood and water, poverty, unequal division of roles and responsibility leading to denial of resources and fair involvement of women and girls in decision making.Based on nationwide FSMNS+ assessment, the risk of GBV is identified high as 49% of surveyed households expressed concern on GBV and 34% of households travel more than 30 minutes to fetch water. Similarly, 49% of affected women and girls who are in need of services have no access to GBV services and only 12% of the community have awareness on available GBV services. Based on the 2020 GBV IMS data analysis, 29% of GBV survivors in need of safe house did not obtain the service because of lack of safe houses. CHADO is on ground in Pibor, Nasir and Rumbek North implementing GBV and CP in Pibor and Nasir and C4D in Rumbek North which has child protection components and has volunteers and community staff conducting basic GBV activities in Koch retained after our GBV project in 2019

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse lie at the core of CHADO strategy to combat Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. As such, CHADO has a strict PSEA policy that is signed by all staff and contractors before engaging in any business with CHADO. It’s implementation and adherence are undertaken in a comprehensive and proactive manner in collaboration with all project beneficiaries and CHADO partner agencies. CHADO screens and vets all hired staff for any misconduct in any former humanitarian organization, when a person is found to be in violations, this person is not hired by CHADO. Any person that is found to have committed an act of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse; such a person is immediately terminated. CHADO will conduct training to staff and partner agencies on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. This training is mandatory before a staff starts to work with CHADO and will continue regularly on refresher sessions. CHADO will also conduct awareness and outreach activities on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse including how to report, where to report and why it’s important to report. CHADO will regularly conduct risk assessment on PSEA including safety audits and taking all measures in preventing Sexual Exploitation and Abuse

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will use complaint and feedback mechanisms at child friendly spaces,schools, CHADO offices and other locations which community members can come and use. Focus group discussions, one on one interviews and monitoring visits will also provide opportunities for people to give feedback.Feedback will be equally collected from girls, women, boys and men. This will include making feeback questionaires and easily readable, questionnaires will be read to those who can't read and translated to all who can't read.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Experience from South Sudan highlights key risks that require careful and measured approaches. These include: Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and Older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities and risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms. CHADO will ensure that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and older persons, women, boys and girls equally participate in consultations or decision making processes by equally involving all persons of concerns including persons with disabilities, elderly, and other persons with special needs in all consultations and decision making processes. This will be done by ensuring services points are equally accessible to all persons of concerns. In meetings and all decision making panels, CHADO will advocate for women and girls including PWDs and elderly to equally participate and be involved. CHADO will further conduct groups disaggregated FGDs and community meetings such as meeting with women, men, girls, boys, PWDs and other persons with special needs. Furthermore, CHADO will advocate for equal representation of women, girls and PWDs in leadership roles that will boost their involvement and participation in decision making processes. With the risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms. CHADO will ensure beneficiary feedback and complaint mechanisms are easily and readily available in locations that beneficiaries can access without causing any further harm. Complaints and feedback mechanisms will be placed in Women and Girls Friendly Spaces, churches and women groups meeting points. CHADO will work with community based protection mechanisms to ensure that barriers to inclusive access to feedback and complaint mechanisms are discontinued, CHADO will continuously work to ensure that all persons of concerns have equal access to complaint and feedback mechanisms and further prioritize safety and dignity, and avoid causing harm: By preventing and minimizing as much as possible any unintended negative effects of CHADO intervention which can increase people’s vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. CHADO will ensure meaningful access is arranged for persons of concerns to access assistance and services in proportion to needs and without any barriers (e.g., discrimination). CHADO will further pay special attention to women, girls, PWDs and others with special needs who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services.Lastly, CHADO will ensure equal participation and empowerment of women and girls including PWDs and elderly and support the development of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights. CHADO Will;i. Ensure CHADO presence at humanitarian coordination meetings ii. CHADO will ensure strong and extensive on-ground monitoring of activities.iii. CHADO will maintain daily assessment of security situation. iv. CHADO will meet with key stakeholders regularly as part of AAP.v. Consult staff on ground as they are from within the communities, formally and informally on a regular basis.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

All vulnerable population will have safe and equitable access to CHADO services including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons. CHADO will adopt systematic approaches to ensure the inclusion of older people and people with disabilities. Both (overlapping) groups are affected by many of the same or very similar barriers to access and participation. CHADO will ensure that women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons are recognized to ensure they receive assistance that is appropriate and relevant to their needs and have access to the health services they need. CHADO will also ensure that they are not negatively affected, and are more prepared, resilient and less at-risk as a result of CHADO action in the provision of health services including ensuring that they have access to information, and participate in decisions that affect them on an equal basis with others. CHADO will also ensure that they receive assistance from competent and well-managed staff and volunteers who are skilled and equipped to include them in humanitarian responses, and they have equal opportunities for employment and volunteering in humanitarian organizations.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

CHADO will coordinate with Health and Nutrition cluster partners in the micronutrient supplementation for pregnant women, nutrition education during ANC, promotion of breastfeeding and appropriate complementary feeding, child growth monitoring and promotion (GMP), management of severe acute malnutrition (SAM), nutrition education within integrated management of childhood illnesses (IMCI) packages, vitamin A supplementation for children; and treatment of diarrhoea with zinc.
