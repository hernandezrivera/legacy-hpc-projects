Project Summary

The Survivors of explosive accidents, their families and communities need specialised services to reduce the impact of explosive hazard.
In order to reduce the immediate threat and long-term impact from explosive hazards to at-risk communities, SAMS through this project will work on achieving the following objectives:
1. Provide physiotherapy/rehabilitation care to the survivors in Termanin center
2. Provide MHPSS services to the survivors and their families in Alnafs Almotma’ena facility

Needs

Entering its eighth year of crisis, Syria continued to experience widespread hostilities in many districts. Various weapons types have been used since 2011, leaving behind a range of explosive hazards, including mines, explosive remnants of war and improvised explosive devices (IEDs).According to the 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 10.2 million men, women, boys, and girls live in the 1,980 communities reporting explosive contamination. There is immediate and destructive impact of explosive hazards on communities, the death and injury resulting from accidents lead to long term physical and psychological trauma for victims and their families, these injuries and deaths can have severe long term socio-economic implications on the family structure. For each known explosive accident, an average of 1.5 people is killed and 2 people are injured, with approximately 1 in 3 survivors suffering at least one limb amputation. By the same token estimates are that 2.9 million people inside Syria are living with a permanent disability and 30,000 new conflict-related trauma cases per month are leading to thousands of permanent disabilities that will require long-term physical rehabilitation care (HNO 2018), in addition to the consequence of war-related injuries, gaps in non-emergency care, and lack of treatment, result in a growing number of long-term disabilities.Before the crisis, Syria's health system was inadequate to address injury and disability-related needs where prosthetics and orthotics services were lacking in Syria and after seven and half years of conflict the gap in the provision of rehabilitation care for war injured became larger, with only 2 public physical rehabilitation centers in Syria provide prosthetic limbs – one in Damascus and one in Homs, in addition Northern Syria is left with few physical rehabilitation centers due to health facilities targeting. This severely restricted access to medical care causes many people’s injuries to turn into lifelong impairments, which otherwise would have been prevented with proper and timely care. The centers in Northwest Syria are in dire need of support to operate, training and capacity building for health workers to enable them to provide high quality services.Moreover, the growing number of casualties from explosive hazard accidents has steadily increased pressure on the health sector, in particular, on the provision of specialized services, including trauma care, physical rehabilitation, and psycho-social support on the short and longer term.

Implementation of activities

o reduce the immediate threat and long-term impact from explosive hazards, SAMS will work on Victim Assistance in Idlib governorate, Harim district. SAMS through this project will work on the following activities:Activity 1.1: provide physiotherapy consultation to the war related injuries such as spinal cord injuries, amputation, orthopedic and nerve injuries in Termanin centerActivity 1.2: Provide Physiotherapy treatment sessions to the survivors in Termanin center. After the assessment of the patients, which done by the specialized physiotherapist, there will be a treatment plan for each patient, each patient will need about 15 treatment sessions in average and the treatment session normally take about 30 minutes in average. The sessions may include some training to the families in how to take care of the patients especially the patients with disabilities like spinal cord injury and amputation patients.Activity 1.3: Build the capacities of the service providers in Termanin facility, a training will be provided to the service providers in Termanin center (physiotherapist, rehab worker and nurses). The aim is to provide comprehensive treatment and refer the patients to the advance services, when neededActivity 1.4: Provide medical consumables to the survivors, the physiotherapist according to the assessment will identify the patients which need to the medical consumables, especially walking assistants like wheelchairs, walkers, crutches. Other type of consumables will include neck collars, splints, orthosisActivity 2.1 Provide individual mental health consultation to the survivors, this service will be provided through two ways: home visits by PSS workers for the cases with disabilities, they will provide the consultation and follow up sessions for the survivors in their homes; health facility based consultation by PSS workers thereActivity 2.2 Provide group sessions to the survivors, weekly group sessions will be conducted in the health facilities, each group session will consist of 15-20 survivors and two PSS workers as facilitators.Activity 2.3 Provide self-care sessions to survivors and their families, SAMS will provide self-care sessions to the survivors and their families, each session will target at least 20 family members.Activity 2.4 Provide PSS in three communities in Aldana sub-district and refer the survivors to Alnafs Almotma’ena center, the mobile team will conduct field visits to the targeted communities. Each team will conduct about 4-5 visits per day, each visit will take about 60 minutes.Activity 2.5: Develop the referral pathways, after the proper training which will provided to the PSWs, they will be able to identify and refer the survivors in need to the specialized center (Alnafs Almotma’ena).also there will be activity for self-care and staff care. The project will be implemented in:1. Termanin rehabilitation center, Idleb, Harim.2. Alnafs Almotma’ena MHPSS, Idleb, Harim.SAMS will track the performance of the project activities in terms of achievement and percentage of progress against the targets, through tracking the number services provided and number of beneficiaries reached, number of staff trained in addition SAMS will track the quality of services through survivors satisfaction survey on the services provided and medical consumables received and Employees satisfaction on the training provided. SAMS will also technically monitor the quality of services through International guidelines and standards and SAMS standards of quality of PSS and Physiotherapy services. SAMS will also evaluate the quality of assistive devices depending on the standards SAMS quality assurance for assistivedevices .

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Sexual Exploitation and abuse by Facilities staff toward beneficiaries. Possibility: high. Effect: high. Mitigation measures: 1. Set PSEA Policy and effectively implement the policy in Hiring the staff and oriented them on the policy, training them, and make sure every staff sign the policy, 2. Effective complaint mechanism in place, 3. Awareness raising for beneficiaries on PSEA, 4. Clear complaints channels for beneficiaries and distribute brochures and leaflets in all places in the facilities (waiting rooms, entrance, Etc.).Risk: Sexual and Gender Based Violence in the facilities between beneficiaries. Possibility: high. Effect: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Put cameras in the entrance and every floor of the facilities and corridors, 2. Use or establish separated toilets for men and women, 3. Establish spacious waiting rooms to host huge number of patient. 4. Put keys for the empty rooms if any. Risk: Local population may become reliant on victim assistance provided, eroding long-term sustainability. Possibility: Low. Effect: Low. Mitigation measures: Build the capacity of local populations. Risk: Beneficiaries do not receive sustained or sufficient support. Possibility: Medium. Impact: Medium. Mitigation measures: 1. add sustainability criteria to the project. 2. Beneficiaries requiring long-term or more comprehensive assistance to be referred to other health facilities.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Mitigation measures: Anti-Terrorism Check (ATC), Pause / Cessation; Information security SOP; Strengthening the relations with the local communities; Relocating the activities locations.Risk: Attack by GoS and its allies / others.Mitigation measures: Selecting fortified locations; Security assessment (site & building); Evacuation SOP; Sourcing alternative locations, in case of need for relocation.Risk: Potential high turnover of staff receiving salariesMitigation measure: Potential high turnover of staff receiving salaries; Repeating the trainings regularly to ensure that all new staff delivering it; Adopt appropriate salary-scale to retain the staff as possible.Risk: Abduction (kidnapping)Mitigation measure: Hostile Environment and Safety Management Training; Access and movement SOPs; Local networks to monitor security situation and trends; Communication plan and local acceptance; Security Briefing; Counter Abduction; Abduction Management training for staff and partners.Risk: TheftMitigation Measures: Safety and security upgrades for SAMS facilities, including CCTV cameras and guards; Photo verification of received shipments, signed waybills, and notification if any missing items noted in HF count within 72 hours; Review of stock/inventory records during monitoring visits; Hostile Environment and Safety Management Training; Access and movement SOPs; Local networks to monitor security situation and trends; Communication plan and local acceptance; Quarterly physical inventory of warehouses; review of monthly consumption reports; Security Briefing for staff and partners.
