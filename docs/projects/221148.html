Project Summary

In 2024, IMC entered into a collaboration agreement with a national organization called FEPLEM (FEMMES POUR LA PROMOTION DU LEADERSHIP MORAL) through a selection process. The selection was made following a call for expressions of interest with well-defined selection criteria and with the support of UNHCR. FEPLEM's mission in this agreement was to carry out prevention activities on GBV issues and community engagement as part of this project.
The collaboration will be maintained in 2025, and the missions will also. FEPLEM will be deployed in the East region covering Garoua-Boulai, Gado-Badzere, Mandjou, Gbiti, Timangolo, Lolo, Mbile, Kentzou, Yokadouma and in the Adamawa and Northern regions covering Borgop, Ngam, Touboro and Mbai-Mboum. This national organization will carry out Gender-Based Violence prevention activities in their implementation area with the support of community-based protection committees already established in said regions, notably the women's committee, the Husbands' School, the gender clubs in secondary schools PSN support groups and Local Protection Committees. For the community engagement component, the NGO partner implementing this project will focus intervention on the identification, referencing and monitoring of people with specific needs. However, members of these organizations will receive training in the first quarter of the project on general GBV, mobilization and community awareness techniques, EA$E/VSLA, peer education and EMAP approaches.
As a prelude to the transfer of skills, IMC accompanied by the UNHCR, MINAS` and MINPROFF will carry out joint supervision missions in the different areas of intervention in order to identify and ensure the proper implementation of prevention activities in their different areas.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The socio-political crisis in CAR is unfolding in the North, Adamawa and East regions, which are considered as some of the poorest in Cameroon. With the complex and dynamic context of massive movements of refugee populations fleeing violence in CAR since 2014, there are currently more than 327,976 CAR refugees living in the Eastern, Adamawa and Northern regions. The situation has greatly weakened the already precarious socio-economic atmosphere in these regions and at a time when humanitarian assistance is being drastically reduced. This situation negatively affects the whole protection system of the population of concern, including CAR refugees and vulnerable host communities, thereby justifying conjoint humanitarian intervention of international and national organizations. With regards to community engagement, participation of the population of concern and the inclusion of women, men, girls, and boys in the humanitarian program cycle remains a weakness despite past interventions in community mobilization. There are limited opportunities for the crisis-affected population to seek durable solutions to their problems and to improve their self-reliance. UNHCR, which has an international mandate for the protection of refugees, has partnered with organizations to provide them with multifaceted assistance in order to meet their primordial basic needs. IMC UK is one of the organizations working with UNCHR since July 2014 providing a refugee health and Protection assistance project in East, Adamawa, and North regions of Cameroon. Several hundred thousand refugees of various nationalities but mainly Central African refugees have found asylum in East side of Cameroon over several decades. Despite the voluntary repatriation that took place in June, July and August 2022, Cameroon continues to host—as of October 2022—327,976 Central African refugees in the East, Adamawa and the North regions.Without the basic infrastructure to absorb the influx of refugees, the North and Adamawa regions specifically recorded some of the highest rates of poverty in the country in 2019, at 68% and 47% respectively. Poverty in the region disproportionally affects women and girls, who must spend additional time carrying out income generating activities to support their subsistence. Forty-four percent of the surveyed households in the 2019 UNHCR & WFP JAM were headed by females with an average household size of six with four children under 18 years. These female-headed households experience a double load of caring for the family while engaging in income generating activities. Women's participation in community self-management structures was low and there was a slight growing acceptance and tolerance for women's leadership in communities concerning young adult women aged 18-30 years old. Similarly, women in committees do not most of the time hold leadership positions and there are not opportunities for refugee women to be integrated into the local associations set up by the women of the host community. However, a great move has been observed from 37% in January 2022 to 60% in October 2022 with respect to the participation of women in decision-making positions in community groups and committees since sensitization and advocacy activities on women leadership are carried-out by IMC. Gender-Based Violence (GBV) remains widespread and one of the main rights violations affecting women and girls in the regions covered due to the crisis as well as the patriarchal system and cultural barriers. Moreover, this situation has been exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic leading young girls and women to have reduced access to protective services. Adolescent girls aged 10 to 17 are particularly at risk and can face multiple protection violations simultaneously including GBV, exploitation, and early or forced marriage. The coordination mechanisms for a holistic approach for case management of GBV survivors through security and legal services remains weak.
