Project Summary

The proposed project seeks to provide critical Food Security emergency assistance to an estimated 3500 vulnerable conflict-affected civilians of eastern Ukraine, on both sides of the contact line including 2000 in GCA and 1500 in NGCA.
The overall project objective is to mitigate the negative effects of the protracted conflict and the COVID-19 pandemic on the most vulnerable and at risk population in eastern Ukraine. The specific objectives are: (i) to prevent the deterioration of the food security status and build resilience of the most vulnerable people through immediate access to food, agricultural inputs and short-term income generation activities; (ii) to enhance community engagement and accountability to the affected populations.
The defined project objectives will be achieved through the following expected outcomes:
Outcome 1. Food security levels of the most vulnerable conflict- and COVID-19-affected population is ensured through the provision of critical food assistance, income generation opportunities and agricultural inputs. The outcome and project targets will be achieved through provision of rapid and critical emergency assistance as follows:
(i) 3000 most vulnerable at-risk beneficiaries (1500 in GCA; 1500 in NGCA) will be supported with critical food assistance for three months through provision of electronic vouchers (e-vouchers) in GCA and in-kind kits in NGCA. TGH innovative and effective e-voucher modality will ensure geographic coverage, easy access to hard-to-reach areas, rapid distribution, real-time monitoring and timely decision-making. It allows limiting contacts between individuals which is extremely relevant in the current pandemic context. The target also includes 500 Cash for work (CfW) beneficiaries, 70% of whom will be women.
(ii) 500 vulnerable community individuals of working age notably women affected by unemployment or loss of income in GCA and NGCA will be supported through CfW activities. CfW activities will prioritise women and will include community rehabilitation activities contributing to improving community infrastructure and home-based care (HBC) ensuring protection of at-risk groups such as elderly and persons with disabilities affected by social fragmentation and the disruption of the traditional solidarity networks. The project will establish an informal network of community-based female volunteers to ensure access to protection of the at-risk individuals residing along the contact line.
(iii) 500 vulnerable subsistence farmers will be supported through cash transfers for agricultural inputs such as seeds, fodder or tools. Provision of agricultural vouchers will ensure efficient, sustainable, and durable solutions aiming at building resilience of beneficiaries and subsequent decrease of beneficiaries’ dependence on humanitarian aid. Priority will be given to female farmers.
Outcome 2. Community engagement and accountability to affected populations (AAP) is enhanced. Community engagement and AAP is an integral part of the project. Representatives of the affected population and beneficiaries will be included in all stages of the project cycle. Engagement of communities in CfW rehabilitation activities will ensure project ownership and effective delivery of results. Engagement of community members in provision of HBC activities will ensure tailored assistance to at-risk groups, strengthened community links and enhanced AAP. A project Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) with multiple feedback channels will ensure better access and inclusion.
The project will be implemented in partnership with TGH 4 national partners (3 in GCA, 1 in NGCA) ensuring geographic coverage, community engagement, beneficiary identification and project monitoring. In NGCA, activities will be conducted in partnership with CF HADC, based in Donetsk and registered with de facto authorities. TGH will ensure its presence by having a national Project Officer in Donetsk.

1 - Needs

The effects of the protracted conflict in Ukraine continue to have negative consequences on the socio-economic status of the population on both sides of the contact line. The COVID-19 pandemic with subsequent economic crisis resulting in high inflation rates and increasing prices and unemployment deteriorated the already fragile context and had dramatic consequences on the economic situation of vulnerable households along the contact line. According to the Preliminary Humanitarian Needs Analysis 2021, under the less severe scenario, absolute poverty is expected to increase from 27.2% to 43.6%. Lack of financial resources and income generation opportunities affect the ability of people to access essential products and cover their basic needs. TGH Post-distribution monitoring (PDM) demonstrated that the average income per beneficiary per month was 1570 UAH which represented 74% of the official minimum subsistence level (2118 UAH/month/person) and only 41% of the actual minimum subsistence level (3799 UAH/month/person defined by Ministry of Social Policy, January-April 2020) (TGH COVID-19 Project GCA, July 2020). In September 2020 average income of TGH home-based care beneficiaries who are elderly and people with disabilities was 2,754 UAH per household, with 85% of beneficiaries having income inferior to the actual minimum subsistence level. A number of sectoral assessments demonstrate an overall increase of prices of staple commodities (NRC, TGH, REACH) as the result of the COVID-19 pandemic. High prices for food items have an important impact on households’ capacity to access a satisfying level of food security. Facing increasing prices and having lower purchasing power people tend to use negative coping strategies. PDM of TGH food security assistance provided from December 2019 to July 2020, demonstrated the following negative coping strategies used by beneficiaries: 40% of beneficiaries purchase cheaper or lower quality products, 14% borrow food or rely on help from others, 13% buy products on credit, 12% reduce number of meals per day, 12% reduce size of meals, 9% reduce food intakes in favour of children (TGH, September 2020). According to NRC’s FSL Assessment (May 2020), 66% of households reported reduced consumption of essential items. In NGCA, among the priority concerns identified by REACH Multi-sector Needs Assessment (MSNA, June 2020), were unemployment (69%), followed by curfew (24%) and high prices (36%). According to TGH Needs Assessments in Donetsk oblast in NGCA conducted in February 2020, the main concerns identified by respondents were high prices for basic needs (28.3%), low income (17.3%), and lack of employment (10.9%). The data was collected prior to the lockdown and closure of EECPs which further deteriorated the situation. REACH Presentation “Overview of findings from new and ongoing activities”, September 2020; MSNA GCA 2020 preliminary findings) showed that 19% of households residing within 0-5km area in GCA had a Poor or Borderline Food Consumption Scores. The latter assessment identified humanitarian assistance for food as number one priority by 82.8% of respondents above 66, 78.3% by 50-65 of age, 29% by respondents between 18-49 in NGCA. The agriculture sector, traditionally one of the major sources of food in rural areas of Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts, has been negatively impacted by the protracted conflict and the pandemic. With unemployment, reduced incomes and increased vulnerabilities, rural households residing along the contact line rely on backyard farming for food security. According to a Food security and Livelihoods assessment conducted by TGH in 2019 in GCA, lack of agricultural inputs was perceived by rural households as the most important aspect limiting their potential to secure and increase households’ income generation and food production activities. The situation remains critical in 2020 with the pandemic affecting all sectors of the economy.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

Outcome 1: A1.1: Selection of project beneficiaries. Beneficiaries of food and agricultural vouchers assistance (500) will be selected in coordination with local authorities based on FSL cluster criteria. For beneficiaries of agri-vouchers availability of backyards used as a home garden for food production will also be considered. CfW beneficiaries (500) will be selected as per the following criteria: (i) unemployed or individuals with low income, (ii) motivation to work in the project, (iii) persons trusted by communities. For agri-vouchers and CfW preference will be given to women.A1.2: Workshops for CfW beneficiaries. Workshops will be tailored to (i) provision of home-based care to persons with special needs, (ii) community rehabilitation projects. Training conducted by TGH will cover programmatic, administrative, Do no harm including PSEA and conflict sensitivity aspects.A1.3: Distribution of food e-vouchers (GCA) and food kits (NGCA). Selected beneficiaries will be provided with three rounds of emergency food assistance. In GCA TGH e-voucher modality and a network of over 110 TGH partner shops will be employed. New shops will be identified in new locations. Prior to distributions, prices will be fixed for priority food items. In NGCA, assistance will be provided in-kind. Home deliveries will be organised to persons with disabilities. The amount of food kits will be 1000 UAH/month/person, to cover the Minimum Food Expenditure Basket requirement of the food basket at 2100 kcals per person per day. A1.4: Distribution of agricultural vouchers (GCA). The value of the voucher is set at 3000 UAH to ensure sufficient funds to purchase quality agricultural inputs such as seeds, fodder, tools, etc. Beneficiaries will have a choice in procuring inputs as per their needs. Vouchers will be distributed through cash transfers via Ukrpochta. The use of vouchers will be monitored by TGH in cooperation with local authorities/community members. A1.5: Implementation of CfW activities (GCA, NGCA). CfW activities will include provision of home-based care (HBC) during critical winter months and rehabilitation works. 500 vulnerable individuals majority of whom will be women will be engaged under CfW activities of which 100 individuals will be engaged in HBC and 400 in rehabilitation works. Common HBC activities may include insulation of houses for winter, fetching water and wood, cleaning yards from snow and ice, shopping, paying bills, etc. Rehabilitation activities may include renovation of premises, clearing community infrastructure, etc. Each CfW beneficiary will provide 40 hours of work per month as per agreed plans. Remuneration will include UAH 1000 for 40 hours of work and an e-voucher kit/food kit with the same value. TGH in partnership with local authorities will monitor the implementation of activities. Monitoring activities: The activity is a cross-cutting and will be implemented based on TGH Results-based Monitoring (RBM) framework. Baseline data collected at the project start will be used as a reference to measure project outcomes. PDM will be conducted 2 weeks after each distribution. Recommendations will be taken into account to ensure quality and relevance of the assistance provided. Outcome 2:. A2.1: Project monitoring and learning. Results of PDM, best practices and lessons learned will be regularly shared with relevant stakeholders.A2.2. Establishing a project CRM. Beneficiaries will be consulted in all stages of the project. A project Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) will have multiple feedback channels to ensure access and inclusion. Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA): TGH PSEA Safeguarding Policy and tools will be used throughout the project. All staff will receive PSEA training. Beneficiaries will be sensitized and informed on the project CRM. COVID-19 preventive measures: The COVID-19 Ukraine Assistance Delivery Protocol will be strictly followed throughout the project as required.
