Project Summary

This project includes several types of intervention, such as water system rehabilitation, boreholes rehabilitation and solar systems installation in locations where household-level access to water is limited due to electricity outages, WASH in schools' repair, debris removal in areas where it poses a significant health and protection risk to the local population, especially in Deir-Ez-Zor, sewage pumping station or where the sewerage systems that are damaged and pose a significant risk to the local people as well as WASH assistance in camps and distribution of hygiene kits.
WASA will give approximately 560,000 beneficiaries (85000 households) access to safe water and sewage services. The beneficiaries will be selected on a detailed needs assessment, and those with the highest need and underserved by the humanitarian community will be prioritised for assistance. The targeted category includes newly returned returnees, IDPs and vulnerable host communities across the governorates of Homs, Aleppo, Rural Damascus, Idlib, and Deir-Ez-Zor. Depsit of Homs low severity scale of Homs ADRA had to include the WASH activities as a complementary for the Shelter project in Al Quseir.
The project will use a participatory and integrated approach to ensure that the needs of men, women, boys and girls are included from the project design stage through to implementation and post-implementation monitoring of the work. Children, adolescents, young adults, middle-aged adults and older adults will be solicited during the initial needs assessment for the project and post-implementation. Each age and gender group will have an opportunity to provide timely feedback to ADRA on the intervention through regular contact with ADRA monitoring staff.
The needs of young children, children, adolescents, young adults, middle-aged adults and older adults will also be taken into consideration as children, adolescents, middle-aged and older adults may require a higher amount of water for cleaning cooking and for children who play outside and require more frequent hygiene practices.

4 - Needs

The WASH needs remain critical across Syria, with approximately 13.2 million people in need of WASH assistance (HNO 2022 draft ), increasing over one million PiN from 2021. It is estimated that 27% of the Syrian population remain at WASH severity levels 4-5, 47% of the population relies on unsafe alternatives to piped water to meet or complement their water needs. At least 70% of sewage is discharged untreated, and at least half of the sewerage systems are not functional. Over 30% of garbage is inappropriately disposed or collected with low frequency (HNO 2022 draft), posing significant health and protection risks to men, women, boys, girls, the elderly and people with disabilities (PwDs). Based on ADRA's needs assessments, ongoing presence in the area and current interventions, priority WASH needs were identified in Rural Damascus, where some communities still rely on water trucking, which sometimes comes only every 15 days, or private boreholes with unverified water quality (Shabaa, Sahnaya, Qatana, al Moaadamieh and Al Sbeineh) due to a limited power supply or damages to the network. In Al Mayadeen, eastern rural Deir Al Zor in Deir-ez-Zor Governorate, low water pressure at the household level or network damages in (DeZ) city, Dablan and Abo Kamal causes many already impoverished families to purchase water with unverified quality or resort to negative coping mechanisms.
