Project Summary

In 2022, South Sudan continues to grapple with covariate climatic, conflict & economic shocks ranging from floods, dry spells, high food prices and the continued disruption of livelihoods due to inter communal violence or the fear of violence. This has been exacerbated by existing acute food insecurity and acute malnutrition with an estimated 8.3 million people (including refugees) expected to experience severe food insecurity by the depth of the lean season (May – July) 2022. (FSNMS round 27 report)
With the hope of a hunger free South Sudan and lowering the IPC, RCO seeks to provide Agricultural support to populations affected by crisis in Jonglei (Bor South, Duk, Pibor & Twic East counties), Unity (Koch, Leer & Mayendit counties) and Upper Nile (Melut, Renk, Ulang & Panyikang counties) The support will be delivered through provision of main and seasonal crop kits and agricultural tools to food insecure and displaced households and with an additional input of either vegetable or ﬁshing kits depending on the suitability of the additional inputs to speciﬁc locations. This will be coupled by capacity building on modern agronomy practices as well as capacity building of fish handling and preservation whilst formation of farmers’ producer groups and formation of fisher folk groups.
The project will target 10,000 HHs (10,000Men, 10,000Women, 20,000Girls & 20,000Boys) vulnerable populations by building their resilience and restoring their assets that were depleted by drought, floods, or conflicts. This will be done through supporting the identified beneficiaries with conditional cash-for assets activities, including cash for restocking. Target group will also be supported with agricultural inputs including Crop and Vegetable kits and tools taking into consideration the seasonal calendar activities whilst riverine beneficiaries will be supported with fishing kits.
RCO will also promote the use of improved agriculture technologies through the distribution of early maturing nutritious dense vegetable kits, high yielding, drought-tolerant & disease tolerant crop varieties to gain short term results coupled by capacity training on improved agronomic practices and ﬁsh preservation/handling techniques and training of farmers on land –harvest, storage and market linkages that will develop communities’ knowledge and skills that will improve food access, increase and build their resilience and future self-reliance by creating and expanding delivery models that support community empowerment and accountability.
Being a key FSL actor in the country, RCO is cognizant of the various gender challenges in the context and will duly target specific groups particularly identified as being vulnerable such as IDP households headed by women and children. In addition, an inclusive community-driven approach will be applied to ensure that women and men are involved in the planning, selecting, implementation, and approaches of programmes, especially on decisions of the selection of cash programming schemes, taking into consideration the different obligations, challenges, time constraints and priorities of women and men across the seasonal calendar.
Lastly, RCO will integrate and synergize multi sectoral response by coordinating with counterpart agencies implementing Protection, Health, WASH, Nutrition, WFP GFDs to ensure effectiveness and referrals of unmet objectives in addition to mainstreaming cross-cutting programmatic approaches.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation across the country continued to deteriorate between January and September 2021. A range of compounding natural, man-made, and macroeconomic shocks have resulted in mass displacement and have limited access to crops, livestock, and markets. These shocks have simultaneously eroded the capacity of food insecure households to employ coping strategies, exacerbating already widespread, severe, and multisectoral vulnerabilities. In Jonglei and Unity states, the persistent communal ﬁghting and spats of tit for tat revenge killings in have forced people to ﬂee their homes, many of them multiple times. The compounding effects of widespread violence and sustained economic decline have further diminished the capacity of people to face threats to their health, safety and livelihoods. With an estimated 8.3 million (includes refugees) people are expected to experience severe food insecurity by the depth of the lean season (May – July) 2022. (FSNMS round 27 report). Further IPC April - July 2021 projects that 7.24M (60% of the population) people are likely to face severe acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3+) and clearly identiﬁed as likely be at least one phase worse without current humanitarian support. In Jonglei, the situation continued to deteriorate for the better part of 2021 and even the first quarter of 2022 where several people have been displaced in Bor South following the inter-communal conflict in February 2022. These shocks have simultaneously eroded the capacity of the already vulnerable and food insecure households to employ coping strategies, exacerbating already widespread, severe, and multisectoral vulnerabilities. The 2021 harvest is unlikely to result in any substantial or long-term improvement in the food security situation and, moving forward, humanitarian FSL response will be essential to offset a continued and widespread deterioration.This frequent displacement witnessed in Jonglei and Upper Nile states coupled by the third consecutive year of massive floods have undermined already compromised agricultural production with floods submerging crops and granaries as well as drowning livestock of the already vulnerable communities and causing food shortages. These have forced the affected populations to adopt to negative coping strategies by reducing meals, going without food, surviving on wild fruits or selling their last livestock which has led to food consumption gaps, reduced dietary diversity and increased household hunger scores. (REACH_SSD_Situation-overview-Jonglei-state-2020). Unity state has not been spared either, the situation there has been compounded by a recurrent flooding, communal conﬂicts, prevalent cattle raiding incidents, below average harvests due to recent Fall Army Worm, prevalent and fresh outbreaks of livestock diseases such as Foot and Mouth Disease. (FSNMS Round 24, HNO 2021). These events continue to negatively impact food security and livelihood options among the most vulnerable populations in Unity state with a signiﬁcant proportion of populations at risk of sliding into IPC Phase 4 and beyond (June 2020 FEWSNET Food Security Outlook). Concern is highest for the many HH in need who do not have access to assistance. It is expected some HHs will exhaust the capacity to cope during the outlook period and slide further in the IPC as hunger and malnutrition have escalated on an unrelenting course with about 1.5 M children under age 5 estimated to be acutely malnourished. In light to the above, Food insecurity is expected to be more severe during 2022 as conﬂict persists and macroeconomic conditions further deteriorate which calls for immediate and unhindered humanitarian support and close monitoring.

1. Beneﬁciary Identiﬁcation registration and distributing of staple crop kits, nutritious dense vegetable kits and tools to 10,000HH in target Counties. 2. Capacity development and training of 150 CAHWs on livestock handling, treatment and vaccinations and safeguarding basic animal health. 8. With the aid of the trained CAHWs, distribute 10 solar powered cold chain facilities in the targeted locations and vaccinate 120,000 livestock in the target locations.9. Formation and training of 30 fisher folk groups of 25 members each on fish handling and preservation methods and sales and marketing in the target counties.10. 20 schools in the target counties children trained on good farming techniques, preservation and storage through demonstration plots. 11. Provide unconditional cash transfers/ Voucher system for seed procurement targeting 2000 households.14. Post Distribution Monitoring and Evaluation of the Project.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

RCO as in the past and will be transparent with the beneficiaries by providing sufficient information; guidance and support that will enable them understand and influence key decisions in the project including consulting with the beneficiaries to enquire on how they want to communicate (feedback, complaints, general inquiries) with the organization before putting in place a feedback and complaints mechanism/desk. Moreover, RCO has established clear guidelines and practices on engaging the target population by ensuring that the most vulnerable in hard-to-reach areas are represented with full participation and influence. Beneficiaries’ feedback especially during project inception and assessment visits will be an integral part during the project development and implementation as it will provide a platform to accommodate new ideas and different perspectives and improve programming. In doing so, RCO will have an opportunity to demonstrate its accountability to the community and ensure that the project reflects local needs, has local support and does not have any adverse unintended consequences.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

RCO will be flexible with its mechanism models to accommodate the people with special needs and those whose access to feedback and complaint mechanism is limited. For instance, the beneficiaries with various disabilities will be visited at households’ level and communicated to in their local languages to ensure that their ideas are heard and considered during an assessment at the time of intervention. For the illiterate, RCO will encourage use of local language so that every person understands and contributes in the decision making. Equal consideration and participation during all sessions will be a priority so that the minority and vulnerable group can feel to be part of the group. The project will avoid, minimize or reduce any unintended negative consequences or impact of their assistance or interventions are committed to a ‘do no harm’ approach to programming. The project team in consultation with community leaders will engage in regular discussions with the most vulnerable groups to receive their feedback on all aspects of the project. RCO will ensure equal access of the most vulnerable including persons with disability older people to tangible assistance and implementation, all the implementing staff member will be aware of their specific needs, sex and age sensitivities within the targeted community. The project locations, to and from where the project is implemented will be safe and accessible to everybody. The provision of assistance will not put the beneficiaries at any kind of risk but reduce the threats (Do No Harm) and will ensure vulnerable and those who cannot go through usual procedures will be given equal chances. The project will further ensure that vulnerable groups and individuals are able to access services equally without any discrimination and service prevision process does not generate any Sexual Exploitation and Abuse against girls, boys, women or men. The feedback complaint mechanisms will be easy to use and impartially accessible by all including the marginalized and those who are illiterate.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

RCO has internal mechanisms in place to provide beneficiaries information on PSEA during project implementation. This is achieved through periodic PSEA sensitization to all staffs and volunteers after which they all sign the PSEA policy as a commitment to adhere to it. During project implementation, RCO will place hkey contact information they can confidentially and confidently report. All staffs are expected to sensitize the beneficiaries on SEA in order for them to understand the adverse effects SEA may have on them and to the organization and also to help them know their rights and entitlement of service delivery especially during beneficiary selection for the distribution of FSL inputs. Child-headed HH, women-headed HH, the elderly, PLW and people living with disabilities will be prioritized and identified as primary beneficiaries to ensure their dignity and safety is guaranteed including advocacy against Gender Based Violence/ Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. The beneficiaries will also be provided with information on how to report cases of SEA whenever it arises through our established complaint mechanisms in the areas of operations. Notably, PSEA information will be shared to the beneficiaries at the inception of the project to ensure they understand it and can give feedback and make complaints.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

RCO has in the past and will be transparent with the beneficiaries by providing sufficient information; guidance and support that will enable them understand and influence key decisions in the project including consulting with the beneficiaries to enquire on how they want to communicate (feedback, complaints, general inquiries) with the organization before putting in place a feedback and complaints mechanism/desk. RCO will form AAP committee that will conduct monthly focus group discussions and community meetings and participatory activities to share information and solicit feedback from beneficiaries about the support they receive, to generate useful information for quick decision-making. The Project will ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members, through: - Surveys and FGDs with specific age groups, women, men, and potentially vulnerable or marginalized youth (e.g. with disabilities) will be conducted on a regular basis. Monthly FGDs will be conducted with children and communities to assess protection knowledge, attitudes, and practices; including focused FGDs with girls and young women with the participatory assessment. Moreover, RCO has established clear guidelines and practices on engaging the target population by ensuring that the most vulnerable in hard-to-reach areas are represented with full participation and influence. All the beneficiaries and other key stakeholders including the local leaders will be directly involved throughout the entire project cycle. The beneficiaries will be taught on their roles and responsibilities before the launch of the project and they will have an opportunity to ask questions and get clarifications concerning the project. In addition, RCO will establish a multi-layered feedback mechanism which include a complaint desk, suggestion box and providing the primary contact number which will give the beneficiaries a multiple avenue to channel their concerns and complaints. RCO has been doing this so that the community gets timely information which enhances involvement and sense of ownership of the projects.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

• Risks that beneficiary are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services; MITIGATION: Activities such as inceptions, registrations and distributions will be conducted closer to the population to avoid too long-distance walk - Locations/sites for registrations will be selected in consultation with local communities and authorities. RCO will involve all the affected populations in the response and their views will be taken into considerations too. The choice of distribution sites, access to those sites and distance will be taken into account to ensure safety of the affected populations. Some distributions, if possible, will be done around households to enable female double up in routine domestic chores and to ensure their protection from risk of sexual abuse is taken care of.• Risks of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance; MITIGATION: RCO will ensure the safety of all women and girls enrolled in the program are able to access project services sites without challenges. The safety and dignity of the beneﬁciaries is very key aspect of this project. RCO is alive to the fact that protection and safety issues are a big challenge for many affected populations in South Sudan.• Risks that the project does not have sufficient internal mechanisms to combat and prevent child safeguarding and PSEA; - MITIGATION: RCO will conduct safeguarding for existing and new staff to increase their knowledge on its zero-tolerance policy, ways to detect and report concerns and remain with a duty of care in dispensing their duties - All staff must sign RCO's code of conduct and safeguarding policies. Further RCO will put in place locally feasible mechanisms for reporting complaints and feedback will be strengthened including toll free line, word of mouth & written suggestion boxes through staff, community structures among others. • Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents; MITIGATION: - RCO will consult all groups including people with disabilities, women and children on the project modalities and share relevant information to ensure they are aware of their rights including rights to protection assistance and can claim their rights and lodge complaints when aggrieved. RCO will also work with protection actors to collectively identify actions to reduce GBV and child protection risks and improve accessibility for all people as well as create referral pathway and train our Staff on how to receive, refer and report protection concerns safely, ethically and with confidentiality. • Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms; MITIGATION: - RCO will set up community engagement and feedback desks at targeted sites, which will be manned by male and female beneficiary representatives to collect feedback that shall be reviewed on a weekly basis including holding consultations and discussions on issues raised and way forward. These will then inform programming including relevant adjustments upon consultations with other key stakeholders including line ministries. • Risk of interference from external stakeholders; MITIGATION: - At the initial stage, all stakeholders and other parties will be involved during the assessment, project initiation, work plan, direct and indirect beneficiaries, and during the closure of the project in order to make them understand the nature of the project to avoid doubts and interference during project.
