Project Summary

Despite the enormous benefits to children, societies and entire countries, education is often the first service suspended and the last to be restored during crisis. UNICEF works to provide uninterrupted education for every child affected by humanitarian crisis in Sudan – especially girls, children with disabilities, internally displaced children and adolescents.
The armed conflict which started April 15th resulted in large scale displacement and shattered access to basic services in conflict hotspots including education. Yet far, more than 250,000 people were forced to flee across the boarders and nearly one million people displaced internally.
This project aims to support 2,821,200 children (1,763,249 girls) to access safe learning spaces, cope with the trauma of crisis, receive psychosocial support, recreational activities, engage with their peers, and to be equipped with learning materials. The project aims to establish learning spaces to enable children and adolescents’ access to learning, and to engage in skills and recreational activities.
The project is nationwide targeting localities most affected by the humanitarian crisis, displacement, and armed conflict. UNICEF Sudan -as the co-lead of the education sector- will strongly advocate for a child’s right to education and a protective learning environment, forging partnerships at the national and local levels to safeguard learning for every child. UNICEF will explore numerous implementation modalities to engage children in learning and recreational activities through educational material and supplies distribution, as well as, E-learning and digital learning provision.
UNICEF Sudan is in urgent need for USD 100,033,082 (USD 85,762,245 total programme cost) to support approximately 2,821,200 of the most vulnerable in need girls and boys with inclusive access to learning opportunities across 9,404 safe learning space.

1 - Needs

The People in Need of life-sustaining humanitarian support to continue or resume their formal education increased from 3.7 million to 8.6 million following the April 23 armed conflict which broke in several states across Sudan. By the end of the 2021-2022 academic year, an estimated 6.9 million school-aged children 6-18 years of age (39% of the overall population in Sudan) were estimated to be out of school, many of whom live in the most vulnerable or conflict-affected communities. Most likely, the number of out of school children has increased since early 2022.On the backdrop to this conflict, literacy and numeracy skills remains extremely poor. The majority of students progress through the school system without the requisite basic skills to comprehend increasingly more complex subject matter and effectively participate in learning. The schools’ infrastructure continues to fail students to foster a conducive learning environment. The infrastructure continues to deteriorate as it faces poor government spending on rehabilitation, annual floods, over crowdedness, use of schools by displaced population, and occupation of schools by armed personnel. The situation is exacerbated year-by-year due to the escalation of conflict in some areas, compounded by annual floods, which causes additional disruption of learning and render children out of school, and currently with the armed conflict in several states across Sudan. However, despite these high needs, government investment in education remains extremely low, ranging from 0.6 to 2.3 per cent of GDP committed between 2017-2021, with a dramatic fall to only 0.1 per cent of GDP committed to education for 2022. In addition to affecting the quantity and quality of school facilities, and availability of quality teaching and learning materials, this long-standing underinvestment in education is also a key driver of teachers’ strikes, resulting in fewer school days for children and accompanying learning losses. The needs increased tremendously following the armed clashes that erupted across many states in Sudan and particularly Khartoum in April 2023. The armed clashes have reportedly resulted in the displacement of more than one million individuals and more than 200,000 households in the first six weeks. The displacement has affected the IDP caseload in almost 17 out of the 18 states of Sudan. The revision of this project follows the planned UNICEF emergency response plan for this crisis.

2 - Activities/Outputs

UNICEF will target 2,821,200 children with an integrated package of education interventions. Through a holistic approach to its response, UNICEF will support establishment of temporary learning spaces that provide learning, skills, recreational activities and enhance safety, psychosocial support and protection measures for children. These learning spaces will enable a range of activities and interventions targeting displaced children and adolescents, distributing learning and recreational materials, training teachers, facilitators and education personnel.Following the armed conflict which erupted in April 2023, this project activities and interventions has been revised to align with UNICEF wider crisis response plan. In contribution to the education sector humanitarian response plan, UNICEF aims to establish 9,404 learning spaces that are safe and child-friendly with a comprehensive support package, benefiting a total of 2,821,200 girls and boys in Sudan. UNICEF activities will target certain accessible states during the first stage, including Red Sea, Kassala, Gedaref, Geziera state and others. The second stage will target hotspots states, including, Khartoum, Darfur states and others. Overall, the response will target both displaced populations and host communities, targeting more than 300,000 displaced children. Lastly, UNICEF broader education response plan aims to reach more than three million children in more than 11,000 temporary learning spaces and digital learning centers.
