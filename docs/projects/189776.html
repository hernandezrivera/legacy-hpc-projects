Project Summary

The project will provide access to food, agriculture inputs and improve agriculture production to prevent famine and support vegetable production and fishing kits to improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations. The project will target 57,430 (33,309 female and 24,121 male) beneficiaries in Aweil North, Aweil South and Aweil West. The integration with other multi-donor funded programmes will help in reaching this target. Concern will provide access to agricultural inputs and trainings to the 3,000 vulnerable HHs who rely on subsistence agriculture as their main source of food and livelihoods in Aweil West, Aweil North and Aweil South in NBeG. Concern will engage with the County Agriculture Department (CAD), extension workers and lead farmers for training on agriculture extension services and Climate Smart Agriculture practices, informal seeds systems, pest and post-harvest management. The activity will support vegetable production Aweil West/North/South targeting the nutrition sector beneficiaries to increase HHs dietary diversity and contributing to improved complementary feeding practices among children 6-23 months of age. The vulnerable HHs practicing fishing in NBeG will receive fishing kits and training to improve dietary diversity and income source. Considering asset depletion, crop losses due to flooding, inflation and limited access to livelihood, hunger is expected to deepen due mainly to depleted food stocks and high food prices therefore Concern in partnership with WFP in NBeG continue to provide emergency food assistance through General Food Distribution, School Feeding Program and Cash/Food for Asset program.

1 - Needs

South Sudan remains one of the worst humanitarian crises in the world, the number of people in need (PIN) is growing; with 8.9 million people (70% of population) projected to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2022. The primary needs reported in the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) are food insecurity or lack of food, which weakens people’s health and nutritional status. The protracted conflict and recurrent floods, which killed livestock, destroyed food stocks and damaged crops ahead of the main harvest season, have aggravated the food security situation increasing the risk that vulnerable communities could slip into IPC 5. According to latest context analysis conducted by Concern in January 2021, over 80% of the population depends on farming to meet their food and income needs however the population is facing the highest recorded level of food and nutrition insecurity. This is mainly due to poor access to agriculture inputs, lack of improved production skills, and poor water management skills, thus the agricultural productivity is among the lowest in the country. As result of recent flooding, FAO estimated 17,691 hectares of crop land affected with an estimated 7,764 tonnes cereal production loss in 2021 in NBeG alone. The recent WFP huger hotspots analysis warn that the acute food insecurity is likely to deteriorate further and there is risks of hunger and famine in part of country. This project will be integrated within Concern’s on-going food security and livelihood (FSL) programme in Aweil North, Aweil South and Aweil West County, Northern Bahr el Ghazal (NBeG) State. The recent food security analysis indicated that Aweil North and Aweil West Counties will face severe food insecurity during the lean season (April-July), while Aweil South is projected to face extreme food insecurity. The project will contribute to Concern’s on-going FSL interventions to support agriculture production to reduce dependency on food aid, reduce hunger gap and improve nutrition.

2 - Project activities

CO1:Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5, 4 & some 3): In partnership with WFP, Concern will improve food security through increased food access and improve livelihoods through temporary employment and building of community assets. During the lean period, Concern will ensure food access through conditional cash transfers and food distributions to vulnerable populations. Temporary employment and asset creation will be provided through Cash for Assets (CFA) where vulnerable households will receive cash for construction on community-prioritised assets. Similarly, Concern in partnership with WFP in NBeG continue to provide emergency food assistance through General Food Distribution (including more hybrid rations in kind plus cash component) and School Feeding Program. CO2: Enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing and livestock support (in IPC 3, 4 & 5): The project will provide emergency access to agricultural and fishing inputs to improve agriculture production, strengthen local seed systems to increase access to quality seeds for vulnerable HHs and to build community resilience to weather calamities with overall aim of improved food security and reduced malnutrition. Emergency access to agricultural inputs will be provided to vulnerable households who rely on agriculture as their main source of food and livelihoods to address the severe food insecurity. In Aweil South, Aweil West and Aweil North, NBeG, targeted emergency seed distributions for both vegetable crops and main season cereals and legumes will be conducted among HHs whose means of livelihoods is agriculture but whose production levels are chronically low. Concern will continue to provide support on vegetable growing and kitchen gardening activities in Aweil South, Aweil West and Aweil North. The nutrition sites supported by Concern in Aweil North and Aweil West will be supported with kitchen gardens. Targeted beneficiaries will be encouraged to practice kitchen gardening at their homesteads as it provides available low-cost vegetables to meet dietary needs of households and improve livelihood by selling surplus produce. Beneficiaries will be encouraged to identify and allocate suitable land for vegetable production. For those living along rivers, fishing has the potential to be a significant form of diet and livelihoods. Concern will target vulnerable fishing households in NBeG with fishing nets, hooks and fishing line. CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks (implemented across all 78 counties); Concern will continue to collaborate with County Agriculture Department (CAD) through supporting them and engaging Community-Based Agriculture Extension Workers (CBAEWs) from CAD to deliver services and agronomic trainings. Concern will continue working with CBAEWs who reside within the targeted Bomas to extend services aimed at promoting Climate Smart Agriculture practices such as Conservation Agriculture, Good Agronomic Practices in vegetable production, and increased crop diversification for both improved household incomes and nutrition as well as strengthen household’s ability to absorb shocks particularly related to changing weather pattern. Concern will identify lead farmers in the community to support other farmers in their locality. Concern will train beneficiaries on the farmer group approach, establishment of demonstration plots, farmer-to-farmer learning, and on mass agricultural campaigns. Concern will conduct refresher trainings to CAD and CBAEWs. The training is aimed at reinforcing skills and knowledge so that government extension staff and extension workers can reach out to farmers with properly structured and quality agriculture extension messaging over a sustained period of time.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Concern engage with the target community and relevant state authorities during the needs assessment, project design and monitoring to seek their feedback and adapt project assistance/delivery accordingly. The broader consultations with the community during the assessment will inform about the project design, priority activities and modality of assistance. The project/villages committees including most vulnerable such as women, elderly, disable will be formed at inception stage to engage in project delivery and monitoring. The project/villages committees from target beneficiaries share feedback to adapt the assistance accordingly. The regular monitoring and quarterly reviews will collate the feedback, lessons and inform about necessary adaptation to project assistance. Similarly, the Complaint and Feedback Mechanism and regular consultation with beneficiaries during monitoring will provide opportunity to beneficiaries to share their feedback which will be considered in adapting the program accordingly. Concern conduct project end-line survey to review the progress on project outcome and seek feedback from future improvements. The lesson will be document and shared with cluster and other relevant stakeholders.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Concern understand different vulnerabilities within target communities that may exclude target beneficiaries in their access to feedback and complaint mechanism. Therefore, Concern will consult with the target community, the local chief and state authorities to understand the overall target population profile, access to communication channel and nature of vulnerabilities. While establishing the Feedback and Complaint mechanism, Concern will consider such vulnerabilities and access constrained therefore the target population will be consulted on their preferred mode of complaint/feedback channel. Different mode of feedback such as complaint boxes, phone numbers, email, verbal feedback/complaint during Concern monitoring visits, in-person to Concern office and Help desk at distribution points/beneficiaries registration points will be used to avoid exclusion of access as for as possible considering the operational challenges in target locations. Concern will use both pictorial, written and verbal channels in local languages to communicate the feedback mechanism to target beneficiaries. The individuals living with disabilities, illiterate, women and girls can also share their complaint/feedback through their local representative and relatives. Concern will maintain a help and feedback desk at distribution points. In addition, during monitoring and support visits FGDs will be conducted separately with women and men to seek feedback and provide opportunity to discuss any concern/issues or complaint.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Concern has a Code of Conduct and its associated policies including Program Participant Protection Policy (P4), Child Safeguarding, and Anti-Trafficking in-person Policy. Concern staff and any one engaged by Concern including consultants, contractors, partners and visitor are required to understand and comply with Concern safeguarding policies. Concern’s Code of Conduct and Associated Policy is consistent with the six core principles of IASC Task Force on PSEA in Humanitarian Crises. During project inception and regular consultations with the beneficiaries and relevant stake holders, Concern informs them about their right to assistance, entitlements, right to appropriate behavior including Concern’s commitment to zero tolerance on Sexual exploitation and abuse by humanitarian workers. The beneficiaries will be informed about the Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) to raise their voice in case of any issue pertaining to PSEA. The monitoring mechanism will ensure that the beneficiaries are aware about PSEA and how to complaint in case of any exploitation and abuse. In PoC sites Concern refers complaint to the camp manager and protection concerns to the Protection partner. In all locations, Concern has a Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) where beneficiaries can register complaints about gender related or protection concerns which are referred to Protection partners. All recruitments of staff and trainings ensure that there is equal access to women and men to ensure balanced representation. Monitoring will collect data on any information on safety and protection concerns and to improve future programme designs.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Concern commitment to APP and Core Humanitarian Standard ensure participatory and inclusive approach. Prior to the initiating project activities and delivery of assistance, Concern will conduct an inception meeting with the target beneficiaries and stakeholders to inform them about the project scope, target criteria and their entitlements. Similarly, during the inception meetings and during regular consultations with the community, Concern will inform the beneficiaries about the feedback mechanism, their right to assistance and complaints. Depending on type of assistance, context and target population profile, Concern ensure that the banners with information on complaint channel and feedback mechanism are displayed broadly in the target areas and at the distribution points.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

During assessments, Concern will coordinate with Protection partners and identify safety, security and protection risks and findings will be used to adapt the programme. The interventions will prioritize gender and protection mainstreaming, and ensure inclusion of older people and PWDs based on our experience and learnings from previous programs. Concern will use the Gender-Age Marker toolkit as well as protection guidelines during the assessments, ongoing monitoring, and evaluations of projects. The project targeting criteria and entitlements will be shared with the community and Concern will ensure that the program priorities are always identified by the affected community, beneficiary selection criteria are always inclusive, and the community is always involved in project design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. Prior to distributions, Concern will consult with communities to identify distribution sites to ensure they are in safe locations such as churches, schools and health facilities and close to beneficiaries to minimise travel times. Concern will conduct sensitisations on risk reduction measures before distributions such as travelling to sites in mixed groups and during daylight hours. Dates of distributions and fairs will be on a “need to know” basis to minimise information circulating. Help desks will be used to identify and refer protection cases to Protection partners and to adapt the programme to minimize further risks. Concern safeguarding policies require all staff and any one engaged in program to comply with code of conduct and reporting of breaches. The beneficiaries will be sensitised on CRM and their right to share feedback and complaint. Concern will ensure that the staff were trained on GBV, aware about referral pathways and sensitise the beneficiaries on reporting and use of GBV pathways.
