1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Increased attention is warranted to the Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), a cross-cutting issue and joint responsibility of all humanitarian partners. Risks of SEA, which could be perpetrated by humanitarian workers as well as local leaders, are a result of the increased humanitarian presence in Cabo Delgado and Nampula, coupled with underlying gender inequalities, under-representation of women in community decision-making structures, prevalent GBV uneven access to resources and growing desperation of the affected population. The 2021 Joint SEA Risk Assessment identified critical gaps of the operation to fully mainstream PSEA across the whole operation. This is not least due to gaps in coordination, training as well as human and financial resources. While in 2022 inter-agency and sector specific PSEA efforts have increased considerably to address these risks, it also saw a significant increase in reports of sexual exploitation and abuse, linked to beneficiary registration as well as aid delivery. Proposed project will aim to support effective PSEA coordination and provide technical support to humanitarian actors, with a view to improving survivors’ access to assistance while increasing local humanitarian and goverment actors PSEA capacities. Activities will be caried out in Cabo Delgado, Nampula and Niassa and target women, men and children.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

1. Coordination and technical support to PSEA efforts across humanitarian operation in Cabo Delgado, Nampula and Niassa as well as to national coordination to support implementation of minimum standards, Joint Risk Assessment recommendations and PSEA Action Plans; 2. Improving capacities for victim assistance and referral, provision of legal aid and other support to survivors by building GBV and PSEA actors capacities in line with Victim Assistance Protocol, in all districts covered by HRP 3. Strengthening local humanitarian actors from government, civil society and community leadership to prevent and address SEA at province and district levels 4. Reinforcing PSEA integration and operation of community-based feedback and complaints mechanisms at sites, host communities and return areas through trainings, community consultations and provision of IEC materials in all districts covered by HRP 5. Development of innovative, inclusive and participatory methods and tools for PSEA awareness raising in communities, using drama and arts, in all districts covered by HRP

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Sexual exploitation and abuse by humanitarian workers is a violation of international principles and a grave protection concern. The targeted PSEA actions foreseen in this project will actively contribute to ensuring the protection of people affected by the crisis in northern Mozambique, in particularly woman and girls, suffering or at risk of SEA. Raising humanitarian staff capacities and mainstreaming PSEA throughout the whole operation is a measure to ensure that no further harm is caused by the humanitarian presence. Improved investigation and victim assistance capacities of humanitarian organizations will enhance their safety and dignity. Ensuring that PSEA is systematically mainstreamed into community-based feedback and complaints mechanisms and reporting barriers for children and persons with disabilities are lowered will improve meaningful access of victims and communities to information about their rights, but also redress and protection. Communities will be encouraged to actively participate in the development and implementation of awareness raising and define the reporting and complaints mechanisms which best suit them. The PSEA Network cooperates closely with the Protection Cluster on community-based actions and protection monitoring as well as with the GBV and Child Protection AoRs on mainstreaming PSEA into GBV referral pathways and trainings for service providers.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Differential of power and access to resources between the affected population and humanitarian aid workers are among the root causes for SEA. In northern Mozambique, women and girls, especially those heading households or caring for unaccompanied or separated children are at most risk to suffer from SEA, as confirmed by the Joint Risk Assessment and the analysis of SEA trends. It is important to ensure that all survivors of SEA, regardless of age and gender, can equally access services. Women face specific barriers in access to reporting and will be closely involved in the advancement of community-based activities to ensure that their specific concerns are taken into account. Attitudes of male staff, particularly but not only coming from local communities, are strongly influenced by cultural norms and beliefs regarding early unions, transactional sex, gender relations and women’s control over their bodies. Together with the predominance of male humanitarian workers across the operation, these may facilitate a normalization of exploitative and abuse behaviors. It is therefore necessary to increase staff training and engagement, but also to involve male staff as change agents raising awareness of PSEA Principles among their peers and contributing to the implementation of the Zero Tolerance Policy.Community-based project activities respond to gaps and needs identified during community consultations by the PSEA Network and the Joint Risk Assessment, as well as GBV safety audits. Communities’ level of knowledge of their rights to humanitarian assistance, possible reporting channels and access to existing complaints mechanisms were assessed by the MSNA and found to still be low. In particular a gap was revealed in access of women and children to the existing hotline and community-based complaints channels, for persons with disabilities these barriers were identified to be even higher. The project will apply innovative and inclusive methods to raise communities’ PSEA awareness by involving them in the development of arts and drama based tools to be implemented at community levels. Where the project will target community leaders and/or committees, they will be trained as trainers and empowered to be involved in information provision on the rights to assistance, PSEA and case reporting. The PSEA Network is working closely with the CE/AAP to integrate PSEA into the existing CFMs and CBCMs to lower reporting barriers. National non-governmental and humanitarian actors expressed the strong need to increase their PSEA capacities, as they see themselves and communities they serve at high risk.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Persons with disabilities, especially women suffering from mental disabilities, have been found to be at high risk to suffer SEA in Mozambique. The project will specifically address gaps in access to reporting and services for persons with disabilities, who wish to disclose or have suffered from SEA. Members of the PSEA Network who specialize in disability inclusion will be involved in the development of tools and methods for community engagement as well as in the integration of PSEA into CFMs/CBCMs to ensure that those are inclusive and targeted. They will also be consulted where the provision of specific services to survivors is needed.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

No specific environmental coniserations are foreseen for the project. Wherever possible, PSEA activities will be integrated into ongoing actions and programmes and not cause any additional impact.
