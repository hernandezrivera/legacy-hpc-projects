Project Summary

The project will contribute to the efforts of improving the physical safety and psycho-social well-being of girls and boys affected by emergencies in Sudan which are still compromised due to exposure to violence, disruption of social support systems, increasing poverty and the lack of access to quality social services. Through this Child Protection (CP) project, FPDO aims to support girls and boys in critical need of care and protection, including those with disabilities. The project prioritizes children facing severe protection concerns and imminent threats living in the targeted localities of ALrusayris – al tadamon –Geisan, Blue Nile state, The project`s focus will particularly be on children experiencing or at high risk of violence, abuse, exploitation, neglect, including family separation, severe psycho-social distress, physical and sexual violence, early marriage. The project will involve advocacy and mobilization of caregivers, communities, local service providers, and duty bearers for improved protective environments for girls and boys; improved well-being, capacity, and resilience with age- and gender-appropriate community-based protection services and accessibility of quality, specialized child protection services to the most vulnerable and at-risk girls and boys. The project will coordinate a production of awareness and behavior change community messaging, including through mass media, to promote family unity and family-based care for all children and discourage harmful traditional practices. It will also advocate with duty bearers to ensure birth registration and introduce juvenile justice considerations for children in contact with the law, including the release of detained children as well as those associated with armed conflict, if any. It will as well ensure that specific needs of girls and boys are addressed while monitoring of grave child rights violations to promote accountability will timely be supported by the project. Child protection committees and networks at the community level will be strengthened and expanded to facilitate more sustainable, community-led CP risk mitigation and response measures. In its targeted conflict-burdened area, namely Blue Nile state, FPDO will integrate group activities to reinforce children’s well being at community and family levels.

1 - Needs

About 1.8 million people are estimated to need humanitarian protection in 2020. The most vulnerable among them include unaccompanied and separated children, survivors of sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV), women-headed households, elderly people, people with disabilities and people with specific health concerns. Both displaced people and refugee returnees face risks in areas of return. The government’s protection capacity is limited, while conflict, displacement and the ongoing economic crisis have profoundly impacted community-based social protection and resilience capacities. About 46,847 Children in the targeted localities are severely affected by conflict and natural disasters and they continue to experience grave violations of their rights, including sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV). Multiple factors such as localized displacement, tribal clashes, cyclical floods, malnutrition, social and political instability, gender inequality, and the effects of the current economic situation, contribute to the absence of protective environments and monitoring mechanisms. According to UNICEF, Sudan has the 16th highest rate of child marriage in the world, with approximately 34 per cent of girls married before the age of 18, and 12 per cent married before the age of 15. Child marriage is most prevalent in South and East Darfur (56 per cent of girls), Central Darfur (55 per cent), Blue Nile (50 per cent) and Gedaref (49 per cent). About 10,000 boys and girls are living outside of a family environment as either unaccompanied or separated children, in addition to 13,000 unaccompanied or separated children who are in need of protection support, including those living in the targeted localities of Blue Nile state.

2 - Activities/Outputs

1. Provide Case Management and referral services: at least 1000 boys and girls out of the 37,422 targeted through the CP-HPC will be identified, including adolescents, children with disabilities at risk to receive individual case management services that meets their unique needs. Social work case managers act according to the situation of children and needs. Case managers will be assigned to help children and their families evaluate their options, determine what is best for the needs of the individual, implement the best possible options to realize the individual potential and fulfill their expectations. Case managers act as advocates for the children and their rights in efficient, effective and equitable manner. 2. Establish or construct semi-permanent safe spaces/multi-purpose (child and adolescents):Two semi-permanent safe spaces will be established in the two accordioning to minimum standards. They will be safe places where children can meet to play, learn to deal with the risks they face, be involved in some educational activities, and relax. They will be established and managed in a manner that support all children, without discrimination to promote inclusion and equality. They are expected to encourage social cohesion among girls and boys of different ages, ethnic and religious backgrounds, living situations and abilities.3. Provision of Mental health and psychosocial support services to children and adolescents:1000 girls and boys, including adolescents, children with disabilities (500 in each locality), will reached with age, and gender sensitive structured mental health and psychosocial support services by specialized persons.4. Provide Structured and sustained family based psychosocial support services: Seventy-five caregivers will carefully be selected from each locality to receive psychosocial support, parenting messages, positive parenting and other kind support leading to improved relationships with the children under their care. This service will be delivered by well-trained volunteers from the respective communities in the two localities.5. Community engagement and awareness: This will involve utilizing the existing community structures such resistance and change committees, women and youth groups and traditional leaders to reach at least 400 people (200 in each locality) in small groups (10-15) and convey messages on key child protection risks, social norms, harmful practices, MRE and related information on CP service and mitigation measures. The active will also include supporting existing and establishing new early warning systems.6. Capacity building activities, including mentoring and coaching, for government, NGO staff and community based child protection structures:100 CP actors [social workers, community volunteers, Government officials, field coordinators] and non-child protection actors equally selected from both localities will receive 3-day CP training with the aim of improving their knowledge and skills in providing age and gender- sensitive child protection services.7. Assistance to children with Disability:Fifty selected children with disability (boys and girls) will be identified and reached with direct services to improve their care and wellbeing in the two targeted localities.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

FPDO will be abiding by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Policy on Protection in Humanitarian Action (2016) which defines the Protection Mainstreaming as the process of incorporating protection principles and promoting meaningful access, safety and dignity in humanitarian aid. The following elements have been taken into account by FPDO and project stakeholders during this phase and the implementation one:1. Prioritize safety & dignity and avoid causing harm: prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of the intervention which can increase people's vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks.2. Meaningful Access: arrange for people’s access to assistance and services - in proportion to need and without any barriers (e.g. discrimination). Pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services.3. Participation and empowerment: support the development of self-protection, capacities and assist people to claim their rights, including - not exclusively - the rights to shelter, food, water and sanitation, health, and education.
