Project Summary

Sudan is expected to continue to face major political, security, and socio-economic challenges. As peace negotiations continue to fluctuate, access to international financing will take time to materialize. Furthermore, the economic crisis, compounded by the COVID-19 pandemic hyperinflation, continues to negatively impact purchasing power. A significant proportion of Sudan’s population is struggling to meet their basic needs and access essential services, especially security and peace addition to the implementation of humanitarian and development programs.
Since the beginning of 2022, protection space has been negatively affected by large-scale violence and lack of rule across the Darfur States, South Kordofan, and Gadaref and affected Khartoum that leading to further displacement and reduced opportunities for sustainable and durable solutions for IDPs, Returnees and host communities priorities in 2023-2025, therefor focusing on protection monitoring, provision of basic protection services and Strengthening of Community Capacity to mitigate these protection risks in Al-Genina.
The challenges remain across Darfur, including lack of and/or destroyed infrastructure due to years of conflict; lack of access to basic services, and limitations of the Federal and State governments in upholding the rule of law due to the limited resources and presence of police and courts given the geographic expanse of Darfur.
The target state West Darfur has witnessed relative conflict resulting in larger numbers of displaced hundred thousand, other locations continue to undergo cycles of violence, and many women and children are at high risk of Gender-Based Violence and Child Protection issues, mostly instigated by long-standing tensions between community groups over key resources, IDPs, and IDP/refugee returnees face a complex of protection concerns, including limited access to durable solutions, livelihoods opportunities, and assistance.
The proposed project is to contribute to the protection and safety of IDPs, Returnees, and host communities focusing on women, girls, and children by strengthening community rights as well as child protection structures and services at community and local government levels. The project will take place in Al-Genina and rural Al-Genina. The project will target a population of 110,000 people and 20,000 children through strengthening community-based protection mechanisms, and 40,000 targeting GBV as well as the coordination and management of community protection and GBV issues across four states.
The project has been designed based on RCDO's previous experience which has taken place as part of our project. which engaged closely with the Ministry of Social Welfare and supported the establishment of community-based protection mechanisms through Community-Based Protection Committees.
One objective aims to contribute towards strengthening community base protection, and the other to strengthen the formal state response to community protection.

1 - Needs

According to the OCHA report, Humanitarian Needs Overview identified 12.8 million people (28% of the population), including 7.4 million women and girls and 3.03 million IDPs in need of humanitarian 50% of those women /girls are in need protection of Gender Based Violence assistance major political developments, insecurity, violence, conflict, civil unrest, weak rule of law and lack of basic and adequate social services represent persistent challenges to the Protection of Civilians, particularly IDPs and returnees, and for some members of the host community. The overall political situation remains fluid, and the country is facing a continuing economic downturn which has worsened since the military takeover in October 2021, when the Sudanese government lost a great part of its international financial assistance. During the reporting period, markets experienced a remarkable increase in the prices of fuel, food, and supply shortages of basic goods, including medicine, wheat, and agricultural inputs. The increase in fuel prices is contributing to increasing in living and transportation costs, compounding an already dire food security situation across the country. During 2021 and as a result of 84 conflict-related incidents across the country, 442,427 Individuals have been displaced deteriorating further their living conditions and compromising access to lifesaving services. During the reporting period, a wave of security incidents took place, including attacks on and looting of warehouses/premises of humanitarian organizations, robberies, and theft in Darfur. These acts of violence are affecting the timely delivery of humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable people in Sudan. The aforementioned increased risks make imperative the need for flexible support so as to prevent further deterioration of the humanitarian crisis in Sudan.Pervasive Conflict-Related Sexual Violence (CRSV) continues to be regularly reported by the Protection Sector partners. Women and girls suffer disproportionally from GBV while collecting firewood, engaging in farm work, fetching water, or traveling. The widespread social stigma associated with GBV can create barriers for survivors to accessing services, leading to social exclusion, isolation, and self-blaming. The underreporting of GBV of also attributed to this social stigma and the inability of authorities to bring perpetrators to justice, and provide legal remedies and recovery services. The continued economic deterioration and COVID-19 increased GBV and gender inequality by stressing family incomes and increasing women and girls’ household responsibility
