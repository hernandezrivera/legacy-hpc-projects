Project Summary

The project herewith contained focuses on the restoration of the ecosystem and on provision of timely actions to mitigate/prevent the impacts of natural hazards in alignment with CCCM´s Objective No. 4 "Provide life-saving shelter, NFIs and LPG support to households affected by disasters and other sudden onset events and ensure emergency preparedness and response to natural and manmade hazards while protecting and rehabilitating ecosystems.", specifically furthering the Humanitarian system´s contribution to further integrate emergency preparedness and response, climate change adaptation, and improving camp planning through access and safety and Disaster Risk Management/Reduction activities.

The first set of activities aims at stabilizing the slopes in the most hazard-prone areas putting at risk the lives of FDMNs in the camps, as well as reforesting the wider environment within and surrounding the camps to restore the resilience of the ecosystem. Cox's Bazar is inherently prone to several types of natural disasters due to its geographical location, such as cyclones, landslides, flash floods, and drought. The influx of the FDMNs in 2017 and the continued population growth inside the camps have put considerable strain on the once-reserved forestland, which has reduced the ability of the region to cope and bounce back from the numerous natural disasters. To mitigate the impacts of these natural disasters, overall ecosystem health needs to be restored. For overall ecosystem health improvement, FAO will focus on rehabilitating degraded land and streambanks, supporting roadside and riparian plantations to minimize soil erosion, and implementing slope stabilization through nature-based solutions in line with a landscape-based watershed management approach. The project will reforest 250 hectares of land inside the camps by planting various indigenous tree species, including hardwood and medicinal trees. This will not only rehabilitate the environment but also provide accessible and affordable sources of traditional remedies for illness for the Rohingyas. Plantation, fencing, care, and maintenance activities will also provide financial incentives to Rohingya volunteers. Through the landscape approach, FAO will identify multi-hazard risk-prone areas (e.g., landslides, floods, cyclones) within the watershed units and develop a community-led and resilient watershed management plan.
The second set of activities aims at validating the landslide Early Warning System developed by FAO, as well as implementing the necessary Anticipatory Actions to mitigate or prevent the damages to FDMNs in the camps. This will come as a result of the groundwork developed by FAO since 2020 together with BMD, Red Cross, and other partners which assisted in the operationalization of the system. The Landslide Network of Volunteers has been fully developed for host communities during 2023 and during the early months of 2024, it will be expanded to the camps to complete the Warning System on the camps and provide communities with evidence-based forecasting of landslides. FAO will provide awareness and training on landslide risks and preparedness to these volunteers, which in turn will trickle down the training to community members. The project will use the landslide early warning system for anticipatory action, whose activities are to be defined in the early stages of 2024.
Additionally, FAO will support partners in monitoring and implementing ecosystem restoration and biodiversity conservation by developing specifications and guidelines on tree species selection, watershed management, landslide awareness training.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

In response to the Rohingya refugee influx, the Government of Bangladesh allocated more than 2,500 hectares of protected forest land for shelter construction. The demand for cooking fuel from both host communities and refugees further expanded forest degradation to over 7,000 hectares. According to the MSNA 2023, 44% Rohingya households are suffering either because of running out of LPG before the next refill or did not receive refill in last 3 months. Moreover, 85% reported that they are dependent on firewood from forests as an alternative source of cooking when LPG refill is insufficient This scale and rate of degradation resulted in increased frequency of flash floods, soil erosion, biodiversity loss, and increased human-wildlife conflict. To restore basic ecosystem services in the camps, at least 25 percent, or 625 hectares, of forests must be replanted or rehabilitated. However, due to the growing population, increasing need to build shelters, and demand for land for livelihood activities, reforestation is a constant need because of continuous disturbance from the community. From 2001 to 2022, Cox’s Bazar lost 9.95 kha of tree cover, equivalent to a 23% decrease in tree cover since 2000. (Note: The global standard for a healthy ecosystem is 25 percent of the total land area of a community/country is forested.) According to the RIMA Round-2 study conducted by FAO in 2022, 34% households from the Rohingya community are exposed to natural shocks and the resilience index of the households affected by shocks is lower (32) compared to the households not affected by the shocks (38). Shocks especially cyclones and landslides have negative impacts on the resilience capacity of the Rohingya households. Therefore, to make the households more resilient to shock, early warning and anticipatory action initiatives should be taken. According to the MSNA 2023, the shelter of the camp area is having issues like damage to walls, floors, or leakage during rain which makes the households vulnerable to landslides, stormy wind, and cyclones. Camp 18 has the highest proportion (85%) of shelters with issues followed by Camp 7 (82%) and Camp 2W (80%). Water quality and quantity have been compromised inside the camps since 2017. Depleted groundwater due to over-extraction by both Rohingya and host communities, salt-water intrusion, and water logging are only some of the issues affecting water quality and quantity. Therefore, a holistic watershed management plan is needed to maintain the balance between meeting water demands for economic growth and environmental and social development.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

FAO, in collaboration with UN partners, the Inter-Sector Coordination Group, national and local government agencies, implementing partners, and other stakeholders, will ensure the application and implementation of knowledge-based interventions to restore the fragile socio-ecological systems, which includes technical guidance and capacity-building, monitoring, research, advocacy and communication, and mainstreaming climate actions in environmental restoration, DRR, and livelihood activities. FAO will implement activities related to natural resources management and DRR inside the camps in line with the Shelter-CCCM sector's strategic objectives, specifically SO4, "Provide life-saving shelter, NFIs, and LPG support to households affected by disasters and other sudden-onset events, and ensure emergency preparedness and response to natural and man-made hazards while protecting and rehabilitating ecosystems." The project will complement and provide technical backstopping to other sector partners' ongoing activities. The community-led approach will ensure the sustainability and continuity of the interventions and their benefits.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

FAO ensures the participation of gender-disaggregated (sex, age, and disabled) and vulnerable individuals in the design, development, implementation, and monitoring of program interventions to eliminate barriers and make sure all activities target and benefit all individuals, thus enhancing safety and dignity.FAO has established a dedicated hotline number for beneficiaries to directly share feedback, and complaints, raise voices to concerns, and seek resolutions regarding the interventions and assistance. Several awareness sessions will be conducted during the implementation of the project interventions. FAO strongly maintains confidentiality on feedback and complaint processes and ensures timely responses. Based on the feedback and analysis of context, FAO identifies needs, re-adjusts, and tailors the interventions accordingly to ensure the safety and dignity of all individuals. In addition, FAO follows PSEA guidelines and internal referral pathways to report sexual exploitation and abuse and other forms of sexual violence. Also ensuring gender, age, and disabilities are taken into account and acknowledging the fact that climate change and natural disasters will affect different societal groups differently. All targeted beneficiaries are systematically registered, where analysis of household resilience including gender, age, and access to productive assets (using the FAO RIMA tool) will inform the intervention design. For example, landslide early warning, search and rescue, anticipatory action and preparedness, and age will play an important role in an individual’s vulnerability, and the possibility of evacuation.

3.3 - Age & Disability

FAO ensures all individuals (Sex, age, disabled, and vulnerable) have equal access to information, resources, services, and decision-making processes. Mainstreaming actions incorporating the needs and priorities as core components of the overall strategy and activities ensure that age and disability are integrated into the mainstream decision-making processes, policies and practices. By adopting a mainstream approach, FAO strives to create inclusive environments and promote equal opportunities for all individuals. Children who come into contact with FAO as a result of the project or programme activities must be safeguarded to the maximum possible extent from deliberate or inadvertent actions and failings that place them at risk of child abuse, sexual exploitation, injury, and any other harm. FAO has zero tolerance for such behavior and pursues rigorous policies to prevent and respond to these issues.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

FAO strictly enforces a policy of AAP which ensures that all information relevant to project stakeholders is shared in advance of commencing project participation FAO will organize feedback session with the targeted community in a regular interval to take the community perception regarding the service. If any changes is required based on the community feedback, FAO management will do it and consultation/dialogue will be organized with the community to inform about the changes. At the time of beneficiary selection each beneficiary will be informed about the beneficiary selection criteria as well as the service throughout the project period what they are going to be entitled for. All types of services will be delivered taking proper consent from the beneficiary and any form of refusal will be counted and taken into consideration at the time-of-service delivery. FAO will do yearly evaluation of the project and the findings of the evaluation will be shared with the community representatives organizing workshops. According to the MSNA 2023, 48% households from the Rohingya camp still are not aware about complaint and feedback response mechanism. Taking this into consideration FAO will ensure a safe, accessible and responsive complaint mechanism for the project. A dedicated hotline number will be set up where the beneficiary can directly access any complaints and feedback to FAO. The implementing partner will be sensitized the beneficiary with the hotline number and AAP messages when delivering services at the field level. Moreover, in organizing input distribution events or awareness-raising sessions the AAP message dissemination to the beneficiary will be a mandatory task. If the beneficiary comes up with any complaint through the hotline number then the FAO MEAL team will immediately go for the field investigation to verify the complaint and report to the senior management. The investigation and reporting will be done by maintaining a high level of confidentiality.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

FAO has a well established PSEA mechanism which follows zero tolerance for any form sexual exploitation and abuse. All FAO staff and implementing partners must know and comply with FAO’s Policy on the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and FAO’s Code of Ethical Conduct. Furthermore, all staff and implementing partners are obliged to treat beneficiaries, partners, and colleagues with respect and dignity. As part of ensuring safety and dignity and upholding the voice of the beneficiary, FAO will set up a dedicated hotline number where beneficiary can reach out to share complaints related to sexual exploitation and abuse. Upon receipt of complaint by the designated focal point, it will be forwarded to the office of the inspector general within 24 hours who is responsible for investigating the case. For wider dissemination of the PSEA policy and complaint mechanism, flyer/poster will be designed and attached to the fixed points.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

FAO is developing a landslide early warning system and is cognizant of similar efforts by other UN agencies and partners. Therefore, as already practiced, FAO is ensuring close coordination and collaboration, where applicable, with other agencies and partners. For example, FAO is coordinating with UNDP in the development and expansion of the landslide early warning system, providing technical guidance to IFRC and CRS on landslide awareness raising, and providing support to the Bangladesh Cyclone Preparedness Programme on landslide awareness and preparedness. In cases where other partners have similar activities, FAO will proactively seek expansion of coverage and impact rather than duplication.
