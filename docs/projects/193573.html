1 - Needs

Coordination: The prolonged conflict context of oPt demands maintenance of strong cluster coordination to ensure accessible, acceptable standard and quality of humanitarian health interventions requiring more robust mechanisms to coordinate, monitor and provide support to Health Cluster partners. With the large membership of the Health Cluster there is need for a solid cluster coordination structure providing leadership and technical guidance to maximize rational allocation of resources and benefit to the most vulnerable amongst the affected population. This especially more important now when there is a huge scarcity of funding. Information management and sharing: Monitoring healthcare facilities and resources availability is important thus the investment in tools such as the HeRAMS which in 2022 started to be expanded to West Bank. There is massive health information generated from daily operations, assessments that need to be done to ensure that the ever-changing needs of the population are identified and met. Information needs to be properly collected (including disaggregated by gender, age, disability and any other relevant intersection and social determinant) and analyzed to highlight the health needs of those groups in a situation of vulnerability and for a more evidence-based approach to cluster priority setting and identification of gaps within interventions. There is need to ensure access to information through sustaining the Health Cluster website. Greater collaboration and information sharing between the Health Cluster and other clusters is needed to ensure the integration of inter-sectoral issues in all aspects of health interventions Capacity building: There is increased demand for capacity building and support on cross-cutting issues such as gender, age, disability, PSEAH, protection and AAP as well as cash and voucher assistance for Health Cluster partners. With 40% of membership being national NGOs, it is important that the Health Cluster fosters stronger partnerships, complementarity, and capacity building especially considering the operational challenges they experience due to the occupation. The health care systems face challenges in providing effective, gender-responsive services. The package of services is not adequately tailored to meet the health care needs of children with disabilities and functional difficulties, especially young children. Many women do not consider themselves having safe access to health facilities, affecting their overall physical health (Morrar, R. and Taweel Jibril, R. (2020). “The Gendered Impact of COVID-19 Pandemic in Palestine”), a gender analysis is being carried out (end of 2022) to guide the interventions in the upcoming years and help address inequalities across service delivery, specially for women, girls, men, and boys, and people living with a disability. Platforms such as the technical working groups and the Strategic Advisory Group offer opportunities for the partners to benefit from technical expertise. Platforms such as the technical working groups and the Strategic Advisory Group offer opportunities for the partners to benefit from technical expertise. Advocacy: As a consequence of the occupation and accompanying restrictions there is a need for advocating for patients and companions to be granted access to Patients and their companions inthe West Bank, outside East Jerusalem, and Gaza Strip continue to face majorbarriers to healthcare access due to the delay and denial of permits required by Israel tomove between areas of oPt, as well as to Israel and Jordan. Through advocacy andnegotiations with the relevant authorities the cluster will work with partners to ensure thatthese communities have access to essential health services. The cluster will also monitor and document barriers to accessing health care services and attacks on healthcare advocating for the removal of barriers and respect of health care facilities and workers.

2 - Activities

The Health Cluster Coordination Team will aim to enhance coordination and the humanitarian health response of its membership . At the heart of the Health Cluster response is the aim to enhance the resilience of the health sector and disaster risk reduction as a critical cross-cutting element in all activities. Key activities such as the monthly meetings, monitoring of health indicators, and setting of minimum standards for health, will continue to take place. In addition to this, the Health Cluster will focus its activities on key areas that help drive an operational response, particularly through inter-sectorial coordination and linkages. Across the oPt, the Health Cluster activities will be to: 1) Produce Health Cluster Bulletin quarterly and times of crisis SitReps or contribute to inter-cluster SitReps. 2) Maintain Health Cluster Contingency Plans to ensure a timely response to emergencies, whether it is conflict-related or disease outbreak.3) Continue with the expansion of the HeRAMS (an approach to monthly monitoring of health facilities and resources availability) to encompass all health facilities in Gaza and the West Bank.4) Promote monitoring of Health Cluster activities through online platform, that include the PARP, HRP periodic reporting and other trackers. 5) Promote monitoring for attacks against healthcare, including barriers to accessing healthcare. 6) Support partners in conducting health needs assessments and sharing results within and outside the cluster.7) Mobilize resources through donor engagements via quarterly briefings, disseminating information the impact of funding shortages. 8) Enhance gender, age, disability, protection, PSEAH and AAP mainstreaming through having a dedicated focal point to support the cluster and partners to improve mainstreaming of these key areas in health interventions, coordinating and linking with key agencies and stakeholders working on the issues (UN Women, Protection Cluster, GBV sub-cluster, PSEA Network, Local women rights’ organisations) and reporting as well as providing specialised trainings to partners and visibility materials such as posters on reporting mechanisms and PSEAH9) Support partners in enhancing community engagement in health through encouraging the active participation of beneficiaries.10) Continue the work of the technical working groups i.e. Trauma, Nutrition, Mobile Clinics and Advocacy11) Conduct monitoring and support field visits to partners12) Support partners in incorporating cash and voucher assistance in health as well as provide inputs to the MPCA related to health.13) Advice and support partners in the effective deployment of Emergency Medical Teams (EMTs)
