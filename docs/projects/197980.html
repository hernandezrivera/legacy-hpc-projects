Project Summary

The proposed project will address gaps in EORE and Victims Assistance in three affected LGAs (Damaturu, Bade and Gujba) of Yobe State as identified by this call and in the rapid need assessment of UN OCHA as well as the Humanitarian Response Plan strategy 2023. This project will build on lessons learnt and successes of previous EORE project implemented by AFRYDEV in Geidam LGA that targeted 4,000 direct beneficiaries with messages on EORE safety. AFRYDEV will build the capacity of vulnerable population which include men and boys, women and girls and survivors including persons living with disabilities from host communities and IDPs settlement that were not reached with EO messages in the previous project to mitigate the threats of explosives by enhancing their knowledge of EO and safe behaviours. AFRYDEV will employ a mixed-method approach that combine security sensitivity to deliver and achieve this project strategic objectives.
AFRYDEV recognized that Explosive ordinance hazard is a life threatening and EORE remain a top priority of the protection sector and well as the Mines Action SSWG, AFRYDEV is adopting a tested and proven community engagement strategy as it approach to deliver risk education 30,000 people in the context of socio-cultural setting of Yobe State, this will involve translation of the risk communication materials into Hausa, Fulfulde and Kanuri languages which is commonly spoken in the focal LGAs to provide options for engagement by the participants in a more suitable local language of their choice with the facilitators/trainers.

1 - Needs

Nigeria is one of the top five countries globally for landmine casualty rates. The use of explosive weapons during the ongoing conflict, mostly Improvised Explosive Devices (IEDs) by NSAGs have resulted in deaths and severe injuries of civilians. It impedes access to basic social services and socio-economic activities, and hampers safe return and reintegration of refugees, IDPs and free movement of Host Community members. The Mine Action (MA) AoR has identified approximately 1.7 million people in need of Mine Action interventions in 2023, out of which the MA AoR will target 400,000 individuals for MA interventions based on LGAs with most severe needs. According to data obtained from the United Nations Mine Actions (UNMAS), two-third of all victims are civilians, with 65% of civilian victims being injured and 35% killed. Over the last five years, 7,833 people were killed and another 1,878 injured by explosive ordnances, out of which 1,163 civilians were killed and 677 civilians injured. In 2020 alone, 247 explosive incidents were registered in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY) states, with 216 improvised explosive devices (IED) incidents, and 31 explosive remnants of war (ERW) incidents, resulting in 166 civilian casualties and 175 casualties from defence personnel. Nigeria has been reported in the Landmine Monitor Report 2020 as the state party to the Anti-Personnel Mine Ban Convention with the fifth-highest number of explosive ordnance casualties globally. Women and children constitute 81% of the overall population affected by the crisis and 87% of new displacements, with 25% of the affected population being under five years of age. The United Nations (UN) Resident and Humanitarian Coordinator in Nigeria; Mr. Matthias Schmale spoke during the 2022 commemoration of International Mine Awareness and Assistance Day; stating that 1.2 million people in the three states needed life-saving assistance. According to him, those at risk were identified during the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview process of the UN agency. He noted that explosive ordnance in their different forms continued to represent immediate, grave and additional threat to already suffering populations in Northeast Nigeria. Schmale further stated that United Nations Mine Action Service (UNMAS) collated 1,532 incidents in the region involving explosive hazards from January 2016 to January 2022 with over 10,000 causalities. In order to mitigate the threats of Explosive Ordnance (EO) to vulnerable populations in North-East Nigeria and ensure Victim Assistance (VA), the MA AoR identified two objectives for 2023 including civilians’ and humanitarian actors’ increased capacity to mitigate the threats of explosive weapons via Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE), and the establishment of a VA mechanism and a national VA strategy and work plan. The MA AoR objectives are aligned with the Protection Sector Strategy and deliver towards the Humanitarian Response Plan objectives.

2 - Activities

AFRYDEV recognized that Explosive ordinance hazard is a life threatening and EORE remain a top priority of the protection sector and well as the Mines Action SSWG, AFRYDEV is adopting a tested and proven community engagement strategy as it approach to deliver risk education in the context of socio-cultural setting of Yobe State, this will involve translation of the risk communication materials into Hausa, Fulfulde and Kanuri languages which is commonly spoken in the focal LGAs to provide options for engagement by the participants in a more suitable local language of their choice with the facilitators/trainers. AFRYDEV will begin implementation of this project with meeting with Government and community stakeholders as well as Mine Action to discuss and agree on project methodology, deliverables, implementation framework and strategy, review of work plan and agree on budget. Activities willl include; Advocacy to government and Community Stakeholders, AFRYDEV will average on existing knowledge of context and working relationship with LGA council, traditional leaders and security agencies to identify and map vulnerable communities and categorized target beneficiaries for EORE intervention. Selection of target beneficiaries will cut across different stakeholders such as Men and women (from the age of 18 years old), Teenagers (girls and boys from the age of 12 to 17 years old), IDPs and returnees, Victims and Survivors of EO accidents including person with disability. In order to sustain and ensure transfer of knowledge, AFRYDEV will select from it pool of trained volunteers who previously received EORE training and engaged them as Facilitators/mentors for training of selected Community Volunteers. AFRYDEV will use strategic community mobilization approach to identify community volunteers from each selected project wards who will be trained on EORE sensitization and KAPB survey in the focal LGAs. Other stakeholders that will be targeted through key informants’ questionnaire include, Village leaders and Bulamas, Religious leaders, Local Medical Health Centre personnel, Representatives of women’s and farmers’ groups and herders.Training of Community Volunteers on EORE and conduct of Peer2peer Sensitization Sessions on EORE in 15 communities of each LGA; 1.Damatutu-Abbari, Kasaisa, Maisandari, Nayisawa and Gujba ( LGAs

Fundamental to the operation of African Youths for Peace Development and Empowerment Foundation is respect for the dignity and basic human rights of people within Nigeria and throughout the world. AFRYDEV has a PSEA policy it ensure strict adherence. A robust communication strategy has been pur in place ensuring every person who represents AFRYDEV reflect the organization principles and values in their professional conduct, regardless of whom they are dealing with, or where they are working. AFRYDEV PSEA policy aims to provide a safe and trusted environment that safeguards everyone from SEA including beneficiary communities, workers, program participants and partner organizations. AFRYDEV is committed to safeguarding the people it helps and who it works alongside. We recognize that the nature of AFRYDEV’s work places our workers and program participants in positions of authority and trust in relation to the communities we work with, especially vulnerable adults and children. AFRYDEV workers and program participants have an obligation to uphold high standards of personal and professional conduct at all times and must not abuse this position in order to exploit or abuse another person. AFRYDEV will actively prevent and respond to SEA and maintain an organizational culture that prioritizes safeguarding against SEA so that it is safe for those affected to come forward and report incidents and concerns with the assurance they will be handled sensitively and appropriately.
