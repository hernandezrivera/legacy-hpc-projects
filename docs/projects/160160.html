Project Summary

In response to the displacements in the Hiran region due to prolonged drought, conflict and expecting flooding, HIWA is proposing response with mobile teams providing combined Protection support services to both IDPs and host communities. In this project, HIWA will provide emergency GBV and child protection services to vulnerable, newly displaced boys and girls in Beletweyne and Buula Burtey districts, in both IDP and host communities.
To ensure the most vulnerable are reached, HIWA will target 16,342 newly displaced women, men, girls and boys from both IDPs and host communities. This project will fill gaps in service provision where government and humanitarian support is currently insufficient. Provision of services in general and protection services, in particular, have been significantly challenged by the limited access. As a result, HIWA aims to support the current situation in Beletweyne while preparing for the possible influx of people following the expected rain.
HIWA is proposing the following interventions to expand lifesaving GBV, protection and child protection services to vulnerable populations in Hiran Region. The complexity of the operating environment requires multifaceted approaches that are tailored to each location including:
1. Mobile GBV and Protection emergency response services in Beletweyne and Buula Burtey during the flooding which will expand protection and GBV emergency response through teams based in both districts. The proposed intervention is designed to:
• Capacity building on Clinical Management of Rape Survivors (CMR)
• Safety audits in priority areas to review cluster response implications for girls, boys, women and men living in the camps
• Increase women and adolescent girls’ access to life saving GBV case management psychosocial support (PSS) services and protections to reduce risks of GBV.
• Integrate protection monitoring and mainstreaming into the GBV intervention to improve the overall context analysis and integration of protection principles in the intervention that will be scaled up to respond the identified needs.
• Enhance understanding and analysis among humanitarian actors on the protection context and needs. HIWA will utilize existing internal emergency response capacity to staff these teams to provide timely, effective and efficient GBV and protection responses during the project period
2. Child Protection interventions in the target districts the child protection intervention has been specifically designed to be integrated within HIWA’s existing protection program in Hiran region, which are aimed at strengthening community¬ based protection mechanisms and facilitating access to information and appropriate services.
• Through existing, Community Protection Committees, provide a platform to provide family tracing and reunification, alternative family care and arrangements including community-based support to identified UASC.

Project Activities

Activity 1.1 Provision of GBV services including case management, safe shelter, basic emotional support and health referral to the main health facilities. Activity 1.2 Provision of life saving material assistance (dignity kits, shelter kits and transport cost) to GBV survivors based on individual assessments by GBV case workers. Tailored NFI/Shelter items will be provided to the survivors’ host to assure hospitality in a dignified manner. Provision of life saving material assistance refers also to emergency food (rice, flour, sugar and oil) for GBV survivors (based on the IASC guidelines on Mental Health and Psychosocial support in emergency settings Activity 1.3 Capacity building on Clinical Management of Rape Survivors (CMR) for 20 health staff from the two Beletweyne and Buula Burtey as well as from the MCHs present in the district (1 formal training and 2 mentoring sessions). Among the participants GBV focal points will be identified and support to provide case management and referral at the two hospitals and in well-functioning MCHs of both districts Activity 2.1 To conduct 10 GBV field coordination meetings with elders, village leaders, etc to link them into the GBV referral system. Activity 2.2 Conduct inter-cluster IDP/host community villages/camp safety audits in priority areas to review cluster response implications for girls, boys, women, and men living in the camps, prepare cluster action plans to implement GBV minimum standards, support joint-monitoring of action plan implementation, and conduct an internal evaluation/lessons learned on GBV mainstreaming training and implementation. Activity 2.3 Establish community-based mechanisms to provide protection assistance and psychosocial support to children and adults affected by conflict. HIWA will engage with the community to identify qualified community members who will be trained on survivor-centered approach and basic psychosocial support for timely response to SGBV survivors, unaccompanied and separated children within the community. Activity 2.4 Train frontline service providers (Case managers, police, community leaders, community support teams) on GBV, referral pathways, survivor-centered approaches and psychosocial first aid. Frontline service providers will be training for effective response and support to survivors of GBV and enhance proper coordination in support to the survivor needs Activity 2.5: Provide family tracing and reunification, alternative family care arrangements including community-based support to identified UASC. The Identification and documentation of UASC will be done through the Case Workers who will identify emergency alternative care arrangements, and provide tracing and reunifying services and best interest determinations.
