Project Summary

Street Child will work to improve access to quality, protective education for out-of-school children, quality of teaching and learning for formal schoolchildren to address acute learning and retention barriers for 70,000 conflict-affected children in northeast Nigeria. The project will mainstream protection and Safe School Declaration aspects, in line with State and Federal Government education strategies and planning, through teacher training and continuous learning. Street Child will strengthen community and national NGO/government partners to build capacities in education programming and improve sustainability and systems toward the humanitarian development nexus.
Street Child aims to operate in the following LGAs under the 2023 Humanitarian Response Plan:
Borno: Bama, Damboa, Dikwa, Gwoza, Jere, Mobbar, Monguno, Ngala
Adamawa: Hong, Madagali, Michika, Mubi N, Mubi S

1 - Needs

The 2022 Nigeria Humanitarian Needs Overview targets 1.2 million out of 2.1 million people who need education support, the majority being children with adult teachers requiring training and professional development. The education crisis is primarily driven by long-term poverty, COVID-19 linked restrictions, and systematic attacks on education facilities and people by armed groups. Formal schools are unable to absorb displaced children, and other families choose to keep their children at home (particularly girls) or engaged in income generating activities. Both boys and girl have limited access to education in the northeast, for different reasons, although the proportion of boys accessing education is higher than for girls. Dropout is particularly pronounced among adolescent girls, who are often expected to be married or carry out housework. Children with Disabilities (CwD) also experience low rates of access to education due to physical restrictions (school infrastructure being inadequate to facilitate learners with disabilities), untrained teachers who cannot effectively teach CwD, social stigmas, and high costs for specialised/accessible materials. Many children, particularly children from households in poverty, enter the Almajiri system to receive Quranic education. Almajiri children are often illiterate and innumerate when leaving the Tsangaya, and are also exposed to considerable protection and safeguarding risks while living there. Learning outcomes, including foundational literacy and numeracy and numeracy are low due to difficulty in hiring and retaining trained teachers who have skills in pedagogy and classroom management. The key needs for the education system in the northeast are improving access and quality of education, made sustainable by strengthening and partnership of the State and Federal education structures and agencies. Schools require additional investment to make them safe and protective environments that support children's psychological and cognitive development, both physically and through provision of GBV and protection mainstreaming and awareness-raising. Communities require support and sensitisation on the importance of school and learning, particularly regarding girls, CwD and Almajiri, and behavioural changes. Teachers and government stakeholders require continuous professional development and training on pedagogical skills, psychosocial skills, child protection knowledge, curriculum development, etc. through localised communities of practice that offer resilient and sustainable continuous professional development in line with local realities.

2 - Activities

The project will support access to quality education for 70,000 children in northeast Nigeria as well as supporting the development and strengthening of 1000 teachers and 320 SBMC/CEC members. Key activities will include construction of safe and accessible semi-permanent learning centres or classrooms, in alignment with state government requirements, to support formal schools which do not have space to absorb newly enrolled out-of-school learners (or communities where no formal school exists). Learners will receive accelerated learning programmes in local language via Street Child trained and supported Community Volunteer Teachers. The project end cycle will facilitate mainstreaming activities for both children and learners to fully re-enter the formal system. Government teachers and CVTs, together with School Based Management Committees (SBMC) or Community Education Committees (CEC) will be supported to form communities of practice and create spaces for regular review and reflection to identify best practices. Street Child will also deliver targeted training sessions in protection, gender, and CwD. Schools and TLSs will also be supported to develop clear, comprehensive safeguarding mechanisms that facilitate childrens’ safety in school.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The prioritisation of protection, safety, dignity, accountability and minimising unintended negative impacts ensure that the communities where Street Child works can access meaningful, dignified support. Street Child is committed to centring and mainstreaming protection throughout its activities and projects, and has pre-emptively identified potential protection risks within the project as well as taking active steps to integrate mitigation and management strategies. Protection reporting, response and prevention mechanisms are integrated into the design, and protection mainstreaming indicators are included within the monitoring and evaluation framework. At the beneficiary selection stage, Street Child will use tested vulnerability assessment tools and clear selection process to ensure that beneficiaries are selected based on need and through predetermined and clearly communicated criteria of vulnerability. In order to ensure participation and minimise inter-group tensions, targets have been set to include different communities (IDP, host community and returnee) as well as both men and women. Community leaders and members will be informed of the selection process and criteria during the inception stages, and continuous sensitisation of the selection criteria will be used to ensure that possible intra- and inter-group tensions are minimised. Sensitisation on these topics through regular community visits and feedback sessions has proven successful during past projects, where dissatisfaction about not being included in the project has been reduced by clearly explaining the aims of the project and how beneficiaries were selected. As part of our commitment to accountability and protection, Street Child commits to work towards the elimination of exploitation and abuse in the communities where we work and in our engagement with vulnerable populations. Street Child has a clear safeguarding policy in place which applies to all personnel and affiliates, including implementing partners and community members who are affiliated to our work. All Street Child staff, partner staff, and project-affiliated community members such as volunteers and committee members receive comprehensive training of the Street Child safeguarding policy. This comprehensive approach, which is also intended to be rolled out to vendors, ensures that all actors within SC projects are operating under the same rules and procedures and creates a harmonised safeguarding environment. In addition to training key community members on the Street Child safeguarding policy, wider community outreach and sensitisation is carried out via hosting of community forums and meetings, or use of visual aids including posters and murals which offer accessible and culturally relevant and appropriate key messages to empower community members on understanding and reporting concerns. Finally, Street Child is an active participant of the PSEA working group in Borno, as an extra level of accountability, and follows the best practices and lessons learned shared through this working group.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Feedback mechanisms will be contextualised to the community in question. Street Child has a dedicated CRM Officer with the responsibility of implementing and managing accessible and relevant feedback mechanisms, based on community information. Previous interventions have utilised toll free lines, complaint desks, anonymous boxes, and similar modalities. Safeguarding and complaint mechanisms can be contextualised through altering images and languages to suit the community, both for audio and print material. Project staff will also attend regular coordination meetings with community structures. M&E teams will conduct baseline, endline, and regular monitoring, including FGDs/KIIs with community members, to gauge satisfaction with the implementation. All feedback received will be fed into review sessions and management decisions, allowing activities to be adapted to respond where appropriate in line with Street Child's commitment to accountability and evidence-based learning.
