Project Summary

Overview of the existing problem:
According to the humanitarian need overview of 2019, Unreliable access to a temporary and/or inadequate WASH facility remain an important feature of the humanitarian landscape in Somalia. More than half of Somalis lack access to adequate sanitation, and water supply facilities, particularly in central and northeastern parts of the country. The overall humanitarian outlook is thus expected to worsen the Gu rainy season that began in April 2019 failed in intensity and regularity, a scale-up of humanitarian response will be required in order to prevent further deterioration into food insecurity for households that rely heavily on agriculture and livestock production.
Buurhakaba district is located 60 Km of East of Baidoa town. It has a population 160,356 of persons.The population of town is estimated at 18,900 persons. The town has no deep underground water sources, as there were no successful borehole drilling operations in the past due to natural limitations. Burhakaba district is generally 57% in WASH Gaps according to the WASH Assessment in South West State. For that reason, all the people in the area depend on shallow dug wells and surface water catchments. Both these types of sources depend on rainfall for recharge and susceptible to both biological (pathogens) and mineral (salinity) contamination during water drawing/water collection; times of delayed recharge; low levels of water during the dry season; and extended drought seasons.
During the Deyr and the ongoing Gu’ season, low rainfall resulted Buurhakaba district facing serious water scarcities. Severe drought, insufficient, unsafe water and poor sanitation, has increased the risk of communicable disease outbreaks. Furthermore, Warta Dawladda, which is the only main water source/catchment in Buurhakaba, has rapidly dried up increasing water scarcity for domestic and livestock use and continued environmental degradation. This water crisis has further been exacerbated with the constant rainfall deficit. In 2021, BTSC will ensure that safe water is available and accessible in sufficient quantities, accounting for barriers and risks faced by women, people with disabilities and the elderly, for IDP settlements and communities in need of assistance. BTSC will ensure inclusion of disabled groups. BTSC will also contribute to reducing the incidence of WASH-related disease through the safe management of excreta and through the promotion of safe hygiene practices. Under this objective, there are no geographical targets; all disaster-affected people in need of life-saving assistance will be targeted. The second priority of the cluster is to deliver sustainable assistance in prioritized locations, where the context is favorable, to improve living standards of beneficiaries. These locations are identified in the targeting guidance of the cluster and are well-known AWD/cholera hotspots, drought hotspots or long-term IDP settlements at low risk of eviction. In 2020, BTSC will continue to deliver sustainable.
The new cholera outbreak in South West state regions started at the beginning of May 2020 among IDPs that were displaced following flash floods that displaced 50,000 people. A total of 196 cholera cases including 3 deaths (CFR 1.5%) have been reported between week 22 and 23 of which 60 (31%) are aged 2 years.
Buurhakaba district is one of the districts of South West state that has been affected AWD cases due to lack of sufficient of safe and clean water.

Mitigation Measures

a. Exclusion and marginalization: Members of marginalized community/ minority groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance. BTSC adopts a broad-based consultative approach in its assessments, project design and implementation. Broad-based in the sense that every section or group of the community members are consulted. Special attention is given to members of marginalized community/ minority groups in a manner to create animosity and draw hostility from the majority groups. People Will disability are not excluded and this project will be designed giving high priority to people with especial needs.b. Exclusion and marginalization: Women, girls, and boys are not included in consultations and decision-making processes. BTSC engages broad-based gender-inclusive relief community committees at village levels and project sites. Women, girls, and boys are part of these project committees and they are coached/mentored and encouraged to articulate and bring out challenges facing them to the decision-making table at the village level. c. External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance. BTSC’s Staff and partners are trained on PSEA. Protection is mainstreamed in all BTSC’s interventions and there are dedicated staffs who conduct high-level awareness campaigns on prevention of all forms of Gender Based Violence (GBVs) at the community level. d. External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: Exposure of displaced women and girls to sexual violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements, distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and services. BTSC ensures access to humanitarian assistance and services does not result in the exposure of displaced women and girls to sexual violence in terms of distance and location of the assistance and services. The project team will consult all gender and age groups when selecting the site for water and Sanitation facilities. Water and sanitation facilities will be located in sites that are close proximity to the homesteads. Latrines will be located in properly lit areas and with a good view of all sites.
