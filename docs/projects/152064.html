Project Summary

This project is aimed at strengthening integrated primary health care services delivery in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe States. These states bordering Niger, Cameroon and Chad are expected to receive many returnees from hard to reach locations within Borno, Chad and Cameroun and host over 1 million IDPs. Over the last several months and with increasing activity of the military, these locations, especially in Borno state, are also overburdened by an increasing congestion within the limited available IDP camps.
UNICEF, in partnership with the primary health care agencies, plans to reach these groups through provision of an integrated package of health services to children under five years old, women and their families through IDP camp clinics and health facilities in host communities.
UNICEF will work with other partners to support the Government to provide rapid response activities desperately needed in locations with high humanitarian needs. We will also work in the 16 (13 Borno and 3 Yobe) LGAs with ongoing protracted crises while supporting HSS work in the rest of the stable LGAs. We will focus on how to reach people in need including returnees, we will support states to implement interventions that will enable transition from emergency phase to development in some LGAs, partner with INGOs that are present on the ground and provide strategic TA in building capacity for resilience at the community level. We will review our supplies and focus on the critical component. We hope to leverage resources through convergence with other sectors at the state, LGAs and community levels.
In all we have the following objectives
• Meet the immediate health needs of conflict-affected populations;
• Restore essential health services;
• Rehabilitate the health system; and
• Transit into government led/operated health systems strengthening program

Needs

The armed conflict has significantly disrupted the health care delivery system in the three states, and the existing health facilities are overwhelmed. The health care delivery system in the Borno previously relied on a fragile network of LGA-level health facilities comprising of General hospitals, PHCs, health posts and dispensaries; but as a result of the conflict about 497 of the 755 health facilities (66%) were destroyed, looted or damaged (Health Resource Availability Mapping System (HeRAMS), February 2018. Findings from IOM DTM data Round XXIV of August 2018, Health Sector Bulletin of April 2018 and routine data collected by UNICEF show that health facilities are constrained by inadequate staff that are mal-distributed, lacking appropriate mix of skill as well as insufficient supplies and equipment to meet the health needs of the population. In addition, the overcrowded living conditions in the IDP camps where, there is limited access to safe drinking water and sanitation, significantly increases the risks of preventable disease outbreaks. There is an active outbreak of Cholera affecting many communities in several LGAs which is stretching available resources even further. Before the conflict, the northern states, especially Adamawa, Borno and Yobe have been characterized by extremely poor health indicators, which were significantly worse than the national-level indicators. While the national maternal mortality ratio (MMR) was 576 per 100,000 live births, in the 3 states MMR was as high as 1500 – 2000 per 100,000 live births. Under-five mortality (U5MR) was also above the national average of 126/1,000 live births with over 160/1,000 in the 3 states (DHS 2013). These trends are reversing with improved high impact health interventions in the Northeastern states especially in Borno. U5MR in Borno stands at 82 per 1000 live births compared to a national 120 deaths per 1000 live births (MICS5 2017). Sustaining this achievement will require strengthening and scaling up on-going interventions especially maternal, newborn and child health services at PHC level. Furthermore, there is an uneven distribution of trained health personnel. Following the conflict and the consequent displacement of populations, many trained health personnel fled the area. This has left many of the returnees, IDPs and refugees without quality health services rendered by qualified health personnel. Of the total number of health personnel available in Borno, only 3% are doctors 11% nurses and 4% midwives, with the majority of these concentrated in Maiduguri, the State capital. (WHO HeRAMS February 2018). The following are emerging challenges and critical needs:• Inadequate number and distribution of health care workers.• Majority of the available health workers lack updated technical capacity to render quality high impact services.• Absence of adequate accommodation for health workers.• Poor storage capacity and dilapidated warehouses, and weak logistics management system. • Insufficient stocks of emergency drugs and supplies.• Absence of effective referral systems due to lack of ambulances and security issues.

Activities

In order to adequately address the gaps mentioned above in locations with high humanitarian needs, UNICEF, through her implementing partners will ensure availability of essential drugs and supplies, improve on the existing logistics capacity to deliver essential commodities to end users at the last mile.UNICEF will partner to improve the technical capacity of skilled health workers and support their deployment to locations where they are most needed. This will done by supporting training and provision of technical support in supported health facilities and IDP camps. Limited rehabilitation of damaged health facilities and construction of semi-permanent health facilities will be supported in worst affected LGAs according to the 1 PHC per strategy of the FMOH. UNICEF shall, in partnership with other health sector actors, participate in ensuring that a robust referral system is supported by improving pre-referral treatment in PHCs and the revitalization of ambulances to meet the demands of vulnerable populations.These activities shall be guided by SPHERE standards and the policies as directed by the state. They shall also be tailored to satisfy cultural sensitivities while preserving the tenets of human rights and fostering gender equality.
