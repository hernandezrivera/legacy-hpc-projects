Project Summary

In the aftermath of 12 years of relentless conflict, the Syrian population finds itself grappling with immense and prolonged suffering. A staggering 4.6 million individuals currently inhabit the tightly controlled region of NW Syria, facing the arduous challenge of rebuilding their lives. As of the second quarter of 2023, a distressing 4.1 million people in northwest Syria are in urgent need, with approximately 3.7 million experiencing acute food insecurity, making the dire situation a call for immediate and strategic humanitarian intervention.
The ramifications of the protracted conflict have significantly impacted the agriculture sector, a cornerstone of livelihoods for many smallholder farmers in Syria. The sector faces multifaceted challenges, including damaged infrastructure, difficulties in accessing water for essential purposes such as irrigation and livestock, scarcity of inputs, deteriorating pastures, exorbitant costs of processed animal feed and water, limited access to veterinary services, and the unfortunate consequence of internal displacement.
Recognizing the integral role that agriculture plays in the lives of Syrian communities, the initiated project stands as a beacon of hope. This meticulously designed intervention aims not only to address immediate food security concerns but also to uplift the livelihoods of both internally displaced persons (IDPs) and host communities in Idlib Governorate. With a specific focus on supporting wheat and livestock value chains throughout 2024 and 2025, the project unfolds in a staged and comprehensive manner.
Stage One: Supporting Wheat Value Chains:
This phase of the intervention is a lifeline for 50,000 households. It goes beyond traditional aid by seeking to empower communities to rebuild their primary livelihoods through personal wheat crop cultivation. Essential components include providing $700 vouchers for purchasing agricultural inputs like seeds, fertilizers, and fuel, not forgetting the coverage of harvesting costs for cultivating five dunums of wheat crops. A crucial aspect involves capacity-building projects for farmers and local community members. Furthermore, the project strategically supports the wheat value chain by directly purchasing wheat from farmers, facilitating their access to markets. A unique project involves assisting in bread production, milling wheat, and supplying free flour to bakeries. This flour is then distributed at no cost to IDP families in camps following Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) cluster standards.
Quality control for bread production involves ensuring ingredient quality, maintaining strict hygiene practices, and conducting regular testing. Monitoring mechanisms include production scheduling, record-keeping, and inventory management. Efficient distribution is ensured through optimized delivery routes and real-time tracking of delivery vehicles.
Livestock and Agriculture Activities:
Beyond wheat value chains, the project extends its impact by addressing vulnerabilities among households. Those with less than 25 dunums/2.5 hectares of agricultural land and limited livestock are the focus. Livestock activities aim to benefit around 5,500 households, encompassing vaccination campaigns, treatments, artificial insemination, and estrus synchronization. The overarching goal remains clear - to increase agricultural productivity, protect and stabilize the livelihoods of vulnerable smallholder farmers and herders, and comprehensively enhance the wheat value chain.
ATAA places significant emphasis on community engagement and capacity building. By involving local councils, the project ensures that it aligns with the aspirations and needs of the community. Capacity-building projects extend beyond immediate project activities, empowering farmers and community members with the knowledge and skills needed for sustained productivity and resilience.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The project is strategically designed to uplift vulnerable groups, including households led by women, those caring for elderly persons or orphans, larger families, and those with members facing disabilities or chronic illnesses. By tailoring interventions to the unique needs of these groups, ATAA aims to create a more inclusive and equitable impact. In essence, this comprehensive project orchestrated by ATAA signifies more than just a humanitarian response; it symbolizes a beacon of hope for a community that has weathered the storms of conflict and displacement. By addressing immediate needs and fostering sustainable livelihoods, this project emerges as a testament to the resilience and strength of the Syrian people. As they strive to rebuild their lives, ATAA stands as a partner in this journey, dedicated to creating lasting positive change.Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E):To ensure efficacy and transparency, a robust M&E department conducts assessments, identifying gaps and bottlenecks within the value chains of strategic crops. This data-driven approach enables ATAA to design interventions that precisely target these gaps across various stages of the value chain. Working hand-in-hand with local councils, ATAA identifies, verifies, and profiles target beneficiaries based on predefined eligibility criteria, including factors such as vulnerability, household composition, and income levels.Prior to project initiation, a comprehensive baseline assessment refines beneficiary selection criteria. Adherence to ATAA procurement procedures ensures that all requirements meet the highest quality standards. The M&E team plays a pivotal role, communicating tools such as a logical framework and indicator plan to the project team. This not only outlines the project's objectives and expected outcomes but also provides a roadmap for performance evaluation through means of verification.The project's impact will be continuously measured through spot visits to projects and interviews with beneficiaries. A commitment to refining and adapting interventions based on real-time assessment ensures that the project remains responsive to the evolving needs of the community. The goal is not only immediate relief but sustainable, long-term transformation.ATAA's unwavering commitment to (PSEA) is evident throughout the project. Active participation in PSEA networks and the establishment of an internal framework and control system underscore ATAA's dedication to upholding the highest standards of accountability. Prevention measures are seamlessly integrated into the core operations of all humanitarian interventions.ATAA employs a scientific risk management approach by developing a risk matrix during project design that considers all potential hazards and mitigation measures. As part of the management of this risk, the S&S department depends on the strategy of achieving community acceptance during all phases of the project in targeted locations
