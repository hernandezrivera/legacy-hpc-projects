Project Summary

Initiated by UNFPA and implemented by national partners in Cox’s Bazar, this programme will contribute to Government of Bangladesh & Inter-agency efforts to actively promote skills and livelihood’s development amongst Rohingyas Refugees and Bangladeshi host community. For UNFPA it is an opportunity to facilitate improved health and protection outcomes through livelihood skills development programs.
The programme framework is holistic. It captures agency-led technical expertise in Gender Based Violence (GBV/Protection sector), Sexual Reproductive Health (SRH/Health sector), and Youth (A&Y Working Group) and is operationalised through national knowledge, technical expertise and experience. It is a multi year approach anchored in the livelihoods & skills development sector while achieving cross-sectoral impact.
Developed in-line with the Government of Bangladesh-United Nations Framework on Skills Development for Rohingya Refugees, as established with the Government of Bangladesh.
A few components that make this livelihoods response programme unique:
- SRH/GBV services provision to livelihood activities centers.
- Leveraging “pre-conditionalities” to accelerate women and youth-led community engagement/ SBCC.
- Linking Antenatal Care & nutrition services to livelihoods opportunities/access.
Outputs:
- Improved health (SRH) and protection outcomes across service users (i.e. trainees, skilled workers).
- Project level sustainability over space & time (i.e. sustainability).
The program will consist of project level outputs and will scale according to financial and operational capacities of partners. Project cohort numbers are defined at project level by both curriculum needs and capacity to accommodate participants. Capacity building will be given based on consultations and then using practical methodology for curriculum in handicrafts, batik print, tailoring, weaving workshop, shoe workshop, benne (yarn) making, flormat workshop, fabrication of eco-responsable menstrual hygiene management (MHM) kits. Training curriculums will align with Myanmar Curriculum & ASEAN standards to ensure sustainability and credibility to participants receiving the intervention. UNFPA will work to ensure this by engaging in the Livelihoods sector on adherence to this & aligning all training coordination, curriculums, and trainers to the standard from the design phase to close out .
This programme will have priority target groups:
- Vulnerable households and/or individuals (i.e. pregnant and lactating women & girls; caregivers).
- Referred individuals or households (SRH/Nutrition/GBV pathways).
- Youth between 18-24 yrs (men/women) w/potential for “pre-conditionalities' '.
- vulnerable groups of 80+ yrs for men, boys, women and girls, with an emphasis on PWDs, LGTB, and other vulnerable communities.
The application of a “pre-conditionality” is not mandatory. “pre-conditionalities” would include:
- Previous Girl Shine / Champions of Change experience/completion;
- Members of Women’s Support Groups, Community Watch Group & Self-help Group;
- Previous relevant experience and knowledge of volunteering, community engagement;
- Readiness to learn/support and participate in SBCC SRH/GBV focused skills/activities development/outreach;
The programs will take place in A&Y Centers (F/M ages 10-24 + caregivers) and Women Lead Community Center (WLCC) (all ages F/M). The A&Y Centers promote knowledge transfer on SRHR and GBV via supporting educational and recreational programmes, enhancing youth participation by empowering Rohingya youth with life skills and life enhancement methodologies. WLCC contributes to advancement of gender equality & facilitates empowerment of women & girls through life skills development & women’s leadership.
Between the two centers, UNFPA facilitates comprehensive case management services, sexual and reproductive health services, emergency referrals, life skills development, and structured psychosocial support activities.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

As 85% of incidents of violence were perpetrated by intimate partners, while 10% by a family friend, caregiver, & neighbors (1), many Rohingya refugees are at high risk of intimate partner violence, and are simultaneously adjusting to factors which contribute to risk of GBV. This includes increased overcrowding, stress due to the uncertainty of the protracted situation, limited privacy & lack of lighting across camps (2). Additionally, adolescent girls face significant barriers to accessing GBV services and are particularly vulnerable to child marriage, trafficking, and sexual violence in the camps. Similarly, adolescent and young boys show levels of distress due to memories of violence before their forced displacement, as a result of lack of opportunities for schooling and the limited recreational activities available to them. This is coupled with constant vulnerability to SEA & inaccessibility to complaint & feedback mechanisms (8). Further emphasis & concerns are extended to intersectional vulnerable groups such as PWDs, older persons, religious minorities & GDPs as they face discrimination & particular barriers to accessing services. Negative impacts of GBV are detrimental not only to the survivor but also the society as a whole. As GBV perpetuates gender inequality, it leads to higher rates of poverty (3). The linkage to violence prevention, health and livelihoods is crucial. This works as an intersecting relationship where addressing all issues within the same intervention will lead to the most positive outcome in reducing vulnerabilities, addressing gender and human rights, and facilitating positive health outcomes (in particular, reproductive health) (3), while also supporting communities to develop life long self reliance and income generation opportunities. WLCCs & A&Y Centers open to men and boys apply a feminist based approach to adressing the livelihoods needs (4). As over 35% of perpetrators are unemployed (1), lack of livelihoods may have a correlation to increased incidents of GBV within the Cox’s Bazar camps. WLCCs, & A&Y Centers (open to men+boys) offer self-reliance activities also provide SASA! Together GBV prevention methodology & the Champions of Change Programs. Doing so within the spaces will work in a holistic approach to combat the multitude of issues the Rohingya and host community experiences while sustaining long term empowerment for vulnerable communities (youth + women and girls) is essential to the Cox’s Bazar community. Finally, when it comes to community resilience through livelihood, women's leadership roles are crucial to achieve the resilience objective and because there is a lack of women playing leadership roles, this project will also train the targeted beneficiaries on leadership. In addition, the project will enhance the capacity of women and girls to transcend the traditional gender roles in livelihood and income generation activities. There is a need for a Women Led Community Center which is open to women, men, boys and girls. As it offers livelihood activities such as tailoring & handicrafts & promoting self-reliance & women’s empowerment activities working as a community lead GBV prevention & risk mitigation strategy.(1) GBVIMS Quarter 2 Fact Sheet, 2022(2) Needs & Priorities of Rohingya refugees & Host Communities in Cox’s Bazar since 2017:What has Changed?, 2022(3) How can food security interventions contribute to reducing gender-based violence?, 2017(4) https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2405844022007538

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

As the recognized partner of the Cox’s Bazar Rohingya response, and in line with rationalization exercise and its organizational mandate, UNFPA has been coordinating with all relevant appealing partners that are under Protection, Education, Health/Nutrition and Livelihood , to avoid overlapping of services and ensure that there are no current actors providing similar service within the targeted area. UNFPA has been one of the active partners in the inter-sectoral coordination mechanism since the beginning of the influx and always maintained respectful partnership and networking with relevant stakeholders and government officials. Moreover, UNFPA has been prioritizing multi-year programming and funding when advocating for and fundraising resources to ensure continuity of interventions and bringing out the optimal positive impact of the programme. Simultaneously, UNFPA’s implementing partners are also equipped with the skillsets, and institutional capacity to operate on the ground. Given its mandate and policies on transparency and accountability to affected populations and donor communities, UNFPA has been active in publishing Sit Reps, Fact Sheets, and Appeal sheets which included operational elements of the programming such as information about implementing partners, funding needs, mobilized resources and programming targets. UNFPA ensures that information is accessible to all including ISCG. Finally, all UNFPA IPs are part of the current JRP process and meet the minimum PSEA related requirements while being active members in the Cox’s Bazar PSEA network, all of whom have appointed focal points and Child Safeguarding Focal Points. These needs become even more imperative in the current protracted humanitarian situation, given the absence of a sustainable solution for Rohingya refugees, and where interventions must take into consideration the humanitarian-development-peace nexus, so as to ensure peaceful co-existence among community members.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

The project will continue to ensure protection mechanisms & principles are in place by: - Applying protection mainstreaming assessment tool kits, safety audits, & related IASC protection standards. - PSEA is a serious initiative for UNFPA & IPs; PSEA IEC materials will be piloted to beneficiaries & community based PSEA module will be developed. - Providing lifesaving services incorporating protection mainstreaming principles & approaches. - Applying the “do no harm” principle to affected people. - Applying survivor centered approaches in programming & specifically through case management services. - Providing GBV related data to GBVSS/Protection sector while abiding by GBVSS Information Sharing & data protection protocols. - Applying a holistic approach to promote the engagement of rights holders at individual, community, & institutional levels through community-led social mobilization; SASA! Together. - Providing comprehensive referrals to specialized protection related services.

3.2 - Gender

The following contributes to UN-SDG 5 - “Achieve gender equality & empower all women & girls” - WLCCs are established to contribute to the advancement of gender equality & facilitate empowerment of women & girls through life skills development, gender-GBV & women’s leadership. - UNFPA will continue to apply programmatic improvements by engaging women & girls in feedback & complaints mechanisms & programmatic decision making processes which dismantle barriers, & promote women’s leadership in the community. - UNFPA-supported 10 women-led “Self-help Groups” will continue to contribute as consultation experts to inform programing & promote women's empowerment in the community and WLCCs. - UNFPA supported 39 Women Support Groups, 39 Community Watch Groups, & 4,040 SASA! Together Community. - Activists & Leaders continue working to ensure women’s leadership & participation in the decision making process. - UNFPA will consider GBV case management & SRHR services for men & boys in all WLCCs

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

Under the SASA! Together approach, A&Y will continue as community activists & ensure peer-group support for one another and their communities. - “Girl Shine” and “Champion of Change” programme for A&Y will provide targeted sessions to equip participants with a clear understanding of gender equality, safety,child marriage, ASRHR and empower them to act as a change agents for the community. - WLCC will continue the life-skills GEMS curriculum alongside the regular sessions on GBV, SRHR, PSEA, DRR & emergency referral pathways which are tailored to the specific needs of A&Y. - GBV case management service provision for adolescent girls will be strengthened with special focus on caring for older persons & child survivors, & survivors of child marriage.

3.4 - Diversity

With the results of UNFPA’s 2021 Vulnerability and Infrastructural GBV Assessment, & through the guidance our Disability Inclusion Specialist, UNFPA will continue to work on the following to promote diversity: - Strengthen partnerships with PWD & GDP focused organizations - Dedicated “tom-tom” referral services & referral money are ensured for PWDs & caregivers. - Programmatic improvements in consultations with ethnic & religious minorities through FGDs. - Pictorial PSEA related IEC materials in programming - Enhancing the capacities of IPs on GBV, PSEA, referrals, & case management services for PWDs & GDP. - Improving data gathering by capturing GDP, PWDs, & ethnic minorities, to ensure their needs are recognized & programmatic improvements. - Project indicators will be disaggregated by sex, age, disability & other relevant diversity factors - The project will serve ethnic minorities and PWDs through ensuring their engagement, participation & ownership in every phase of the project.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

UNFPA works to ensure program planning includes needs assessments, FGDs & consultation with relevant stakeholders when developing programs. Alongside any curriculum or IEC materials in development involves consultations with community members: - The following UNFPA tools are used to ensure feedback amongst all communities: Community Feedback Mechanisms, Grievance Response Mechanisms, Client Satisfaction Survey, PSEA complaint boxes. - Based on the feedback, actions are routinely taken to improve service provision within facilities. These are also taken into account in capacity development of staff, case workers & volunteers so that service & programmatic gaps are addressed. - UNFPA conducts FGDs on a regular basis with affected populations and minority groups in A&Y & WLCCs to gather feedback on programme activities & updates on security concerns - For data transparency UNFPA M&E team will continue its existing activities of field assessment.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

UNFPA will ensure adherence to DRR/DRM standards including the Bangladesh government National Plan for Disaster Management (2021-2025) & Action for Disaster Risk Management Towards Resilient Nation. - UNFPA's IP staff are actively engaged & well presented in the PERU & receive training by UNFPA including GBViE. - UNFPA Social Mobilizers are trained in responding to disaster related emergencies - UNFPA IPs preposition & store customized Dignity Kits to be utilized in the acute response of an emergency & reduce the vulnerabilities for affected women & girls. - It will continue community based awareness programs on climate change through art based activity, & audio-visual content. - It will provide DRR training A&Y programming including the distribution of micro-gardening kits - It will explore & adopt modes of production based on contemporary understanding & best practices in the application of the circular/donut economic models (i.e product value / waste reduction / carrying capacity)
