Project Summary

As part of the early response to climate-induced shocks, WFP will ensure availability of specialized nutritious foods, other essential supplies, trained staff and field capacity for early identification, referral and management of moderate wasting in children and PBWG to prevent morbimortality due to malnutrition in the most-affected districts. WFP will work together with government and key partners to monitor and analyze the nutrition situation to identify the areas more nutritionally vulnerable and response needs through massive screening and nutrition assessments.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

WFP aims to prevent and mitigate the effects of natural disasters in districts severely impacted by weather extreme phenomena and with the highest prevalence of food insecurity and malnutrition. The focus will be on stabilizing food consumption and nutritional status of the most vulnerable population groups, with special attention to pregnant and breastfeeding women and girls, and children under five years of age, including people living with HIV/AIDS.WFP will ensure availability of specialized nutritious foods, other essential supplies, trained staff and field capacity for early identification, referral and management of moderate wasting in children and PBWG to prevent morbimortality due to malnutrition in the most-affected districts. WFP will work together with government and key partners to monitor and analyze the nutrition situation to identify the areas more nutritionally vulnerable and response needs through massive screening and nutrition assessments.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

1- Generating evidence to guide the nutrition response: WFP is working with the SETSAN, MISAU and nutrition partners to establish a comprehensive and sustainable nutrition information system to anticipate nutrition needs and inform responses to prevent and address malnutrition, particularly in humanitarian crises. Additionally, WFP will support the adaptation of the nutrition vulnerability index to help identifying the most nutritionally at-risk districts potentially impacted by floods/cyclones in addition to the index recently develop for drought-induced El Niño. Moreover, it will continue supporting nutrition assessments in the most shock-affected areas, followed by an IPC Acute Malnutrition analysis.2- Ensure nutrition adequacy of food assistance to prevent against malnutrition: To prevent malnutrition during times of emergency or crisis, it is critical that household assistance is adequate and well-balanced to meet both quantity and quality needs, such as essential nutrients. Therefore, food assistance transfers should meet the nutritional needs of beneficiaries for both macronutrients (Energy, Fat and Protein) and micronutrients (Calcium; Folate; Iron; Magnesium; Niacin; Pantothenic Acid; Riboflavin; Thiamine; Vit. A; Vit. B12; Vit. B6; Vit. C; Zinc, and Iodine). This will be ensured (only if needed) by complementing the food ration with food supplementation through fresh food vouchers or fortified blended foods prioritizing children under 2 years of age or PBWG living in highly vulnerable conditions. 3- Active-case finding of wasting cases and referral to treatment services: To ensure early identification and increase access to and coverage of PRN services, WFP will support regular active case finding through nutritional screening and referral of wasting cases to PRN services in the accommodation centers and schock-affected communities and districts. Static health brigades as well as mobile health brigades will be implemented by health professionals from nearby government-led health facilities with involvement of community health workers or by trained health staff from NGOs. These health teams will also provide treatment and management of wasting and other critical health services. WFP will provide technical and financial support to the district health authorities and NGOs.4- Targeted Supplementary Feeding for Management of Wasting: In the most nutritionally vulnerable shock-affected districts, WFP will ensure the pre-positioning of Specialized Nutritious Foods (SNF’s) and non-food items to ensure local capacity for timely identification and management of moderate wasting cases in children < 5 years of age (CU5) and of moderate and severe undernutrition in pregnant and breastfeeding women/girls (PBWG), including those living with HIV and Tuberculosis (TB), through the implementation of Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programme (TSFP). The TSFP is an integral part of the National Nutrition Rehabilitation Programme (PRN). WFP will procure and supply health facilities and accommodation centers with SNF’s and non-food items (e.g. screening tapes, job-aids, registration books) and ensure health facility staff is trained to provide quality PRN services.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

WFP is committed to taking action to facilitate safe inclusion in food and nutrition assistance to victims of SEA and GBV (where targeting criteria are met) with cases referred through the PSEA network and GBV actors. WFP is furthermore working closely with the recently established social and environmental safeguarding division of INGD to ensure synergies between WFP and the Government on integration of protection, AAP and PSEA in the humanitarian responses in Mozambique, including supporting strengthened commitment and capacity to address abuses of power in the humanitarian response recognizing that the most visible perpetrators are local leaders acting on behalf of the Government. Recognising the identified risks above, WFP Mozambique is committed to ensuring protection-centered and principled interventions across Mozambique and in this regard maintains a targeted protection and accountability structure, with a particular focus on the Northern region. The protection and AAP team works with senior management, programme, and supply chain colleagues as well as cooperating partners (CPs) to incorporate mitigation and prevention measures for the identified protection risks associated with the food assistance and operational context. Furthermore, they actively engage with the affected population and implementing partners to handle individual cases such as abuses of power, sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), exclusion/ inclusion errors, food quality concerns among others reported through the available community feedback mechanisms. As a preventive measure to protection risks and to best ensure clarity on beneficiary rights and intervention objectives WFP works with CPs to ensure accessible community engagement which is supported by the provision of accessible communications tools and regularly updated communication-with-communities documents containing key messages regarding the intervention, which are disseminated to WFP and CP staff, CFM teams including the tollfree inter-agency hotline, as well as the broader humanitarian community and government where possible. Furthermore, recognizing the challenges faced by people living with disabilities (PWD) to access assistance, WFP is taking targeted action to address this through several methods. For instance, by ensuring inclusion and participation of PWD in community committees, by engaging the disability working group to directly refer PWD and their families to food assistance, and by working with the Trinity College of Dublin to develop communications supports with the dual goal of tackling both communication barriers caused by disability and language generally due to high illiteracy and mixed language needs of the displaced population. As an active member of the protection cluster, PSEA network (co-chair at national level), WFP And CPs are enabled to access protection actors and Government referral pathways for GBV and child protection, for example, enabling a strengthened rights-based approach and access to vital services beyond food assistance.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Where there is overlap between beneficiaries that are disabled and those that are acutely malnourished and the same quality of services will be provided regardless of disability status. Additionally, the targeted supplementary feeding programme for prevention is blanket for all children 6-23 months, including those children with disabilities. Active screening and treatment through regular mobile brigades occur in isolated or hard-to-reach communities to ensure services are closer to those unable to reach health and nutrition services.
