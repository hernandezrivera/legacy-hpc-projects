Project Summary

This project will be implemented in Terekeka County of Central Equatoria state and Raja County of Western Bhar el Ghazal state, targeting a total of 176,910 beneficiaries. The purpose of the project is to increase access to essential quality nutrition services among vulnerable children, pregnant and lactating women and adolescents’ girls including protection and GBV services. The project activities include; routine screening and treatment of malnutrition cases, static and outreach/mobile nutrition services, training and mentorship of frontline workers (CNVS/BHWs, Nutrition and Heath workers), MIYCN counseling for pregnant and breastfeeding women, and caretakers of children 0-23 months, defaulter tracing and follow up, awareness creation on nutrition, vegetable/kitchen gardening and animal rearing, cooking demonstrations, and provide ECD services including GBV and protection, management of nutrition supplies, mother-to-mother support groups’ activities, strengthen community feedback mechanisms to enhance accountability for affected population, family MUAC (FMUAC) screening, micronutrient supplementation (Vit A supplementation) and deworming, rapid nutrition assessments/surveys, safety audits, supervision and monitoring including malaria and immunization screening, distribution of LLITN, hygiene and sanitation education, provision of safe water and soap for hand washing.
The project intends to reach 52,283 boys, 54,417 girls and 69,210 Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) and adolescent girls with 6,721 SAM, 10,905 MAM, 5,941 PBW with SAM, 70 MSGs) and 282,025 shall be reached through preventive activities (40,435 caregivers received MIYCN counselling, 20,880 PLW received MIYCN counselling, 106,700 VAS, 95,469 deworming and 18,541 BSFP) in all the four Counties. Additionally, 433 CNVs, 50 BHWs and 88 nutrition and health workers will be trained/oriented on MIYCN/Prevention of malnutrition. The estimated project cost of $343,538 will be required for a period of 12 months.
To ensure effective and quality implementation, AFOD will adopt a mix of integrated strategies to increase access to essential nutrition services through sustained static and community prevention activities integrated with other routine health, WASH, education, protection and FSL interventions for synergy and cost effectiveness. AFOD will coordinate with other partners and clusters, carry out joint monitoring and reviews, engage relevant Government and community stakeholders in planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the project including strengthening complaint feedback mechanism to obtain community feedback about the services and address Sexual Exploitation Abuse (confidential, accessible, responsive), participation for sustained ownership of the project.

1. Needs

Malnutrition and acute food insecurity situation in South Sudan is deteriorating because of the economic crisis (currency depreciation, high food prices), and climatic shocks (especially flooding, dry spells), conflict and insecurity, low agricultural production and this situation is further worsened by the influx of returnees and refugees from Sudan (IPC November 2024). Limited dietary diversity, food consumption and relatively low coping strategies of population contribute to consumption gaps and indirectly to limit childcare and access to health services significantly contributing to acute malnutrition in children and pregnant and breastfeeding women. Based on the IPC report of 2024, severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) cases are estimated at 650,000 and 1.43 million people by June 2025 respectively. The core contributing factors for the acute malnutrition include high prevalence of diseases, poor access to sanitation and drinking water compromises hygiene practices and increases the risk of high incidences of illness, Inadequate feeding practices of infants and young children and elevated levels of food insecurity. Terekeka County in Central Equatoria state and Raga Counties in Western Bhar el Ghazal state are facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and in stressed (IPC Phase 2) acute food insecurity respectively (IPC November 2024). An estimated 637,000 people in Central Equatoria state and 230,000 people in Western Bahr el Ghazal State are facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, with key drivers being high food prices, low households’ purchasing power, prolonged dry spell, and localized insecurity incidences including intercommunal violence (IPC November 2024). The anticipated continued influx of returnees and refugees fleeing from Sudan is likely to worsen the food insecurity situation in these Counties. However, between December 2024 and March 2025, it is estimated that 187,000 people in Western Bahr el Ghazal State and 620,000 people in Central Equatoria state will face Crisis (IPC AFI Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. Evidence indicates that an estimated 0.53 million children with acute malnutrition and 0.8 million pregnant and breastfeeding women will need treatment for acute malnutrition in 2025 while for preventative interventions, it is estimated 2 million children aged 6-59 months and 2 million pregnant and breastfeeding women, and adolescent girls will be reached. High exclusive breastfeeding practices increased to 70% although still below 75% target and relatively low diarrhea cases were reported ranging between 12% and 7% (FSNM Round 30). AFOD will adopt a static and mobile approach for delivering quality nutrition services for optimum coverage robust monitoring, and utilization of data to inform decision making, integration of services for cost effectiveness, to prevent duplication and overlap. The need for management of acute malnutrition is still huge despite on-going humanitarian assistance. Acute malnutrition has a direct undesirable impact on children under 5 years, women of childbearing age (15-49yrs) including Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLWs). Therefore, the need for management of acute malnutrition in under five, pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) in Terekeka and Raga Counties is critical. It is on this basis that AFOD is submitting a proposal to implement management of acute malnutrition to prevent further deterioration in GAM prevalence and averting complications related to acute malnutrition and promote health in under 5years.

2. Project activities

The project include; • Conduct community awareness and sensitization on health and nutrition facts including WASH, Education and protection services promote positive social norms and behaviour change • Routine screening and treatment of children and pregnant and lactating women with Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM). • Screening and vaccination of children eligible for immunization at nutrition sites and management diarrhoeal cases in the health facilities.• Support the functionalization of mother-to-mother support groups to strengthen community prevention and care networks.• Training and capacity building for community Nutrition workers, Boma Health Workers (BHWs), and other health workers on CMAM, MIYCN and Integrated Management of Neonatal and Childhood Illnesses (IMNCI) guideline.• Provide MIYCN counseling for Lactating women, breastfeeding women, and caretakers of children 0-23 months • Established and strengthen kitchen gardening/backyard farming to promote food production among beneficiaries.• Carry out cooking demonstrations in both health facilities and communities to enhance knowledge and skills of mothers and caregivers.• Established and strengthen community feedback mechanisms (CFM) to enhance accountability for affected persons.• Provide safe water for drinking and hand washing, and soap at nutrition sites to promote good hygiene practices.• Support in distribution of long-lasting insecticides treated nets (LLITNs) for malaria prevention • Provide testing, treatment and referral for malaria cases at nutrition sites. • Train frontline nutrition staff and community on PSEA, aid diversion and GBV facts, including how to safely and appropriately respond to issues. • Provision of early Childhood Development (ECD) services at nutrition sites to promote learning and development of children.• Support and strengthen family MUAC (FMUAC) screening activities by caregivers of children to ensure early detection of malnutrition in children.• Support micronutrient supplementation (Vit A supplementation) and deworming for children. • Conduct routine Supportive supervision to ensure quality implementation• Carry out rapid nutrition assessments and surveys to generate evidence to inform decision making and guide intervention. • Documentation and reporting on weekly, monthly, quarterly and annual basis (on dashboards, provide situational reports, NIS, 5Ws etc). • Carry out safety audits.
