Project Summary

This Organization will contribute to strengthening the overall protective environment and resilience of the most vulnerable internally displaced people and host community members in six communities in Northeast Syria. This will include the provision of integrated protection services, including specialized gender-based violence case management, advanced and focused psychosocial support, community awareness raising activities, empowerment activities, training for service providers and community members protection topics. The most vulnerable will be targeted, including people with disabilities, people facing mental conditions, psychological distress and trauma, and/or those who are survivors or at risk of gender-based violence and abuse – especially women and girls. The program will target at least 35,000 beneficiaries through various services offered. Attention will be given to appropriate entry points such as existing women’s groups and support networks.
The Organization’s GBV services will contribute to the Protection Cluster’s and GBV sub-cluster efforts to provide GBV prevention and response to the areas where conflict–affected Syrians, especially girls and women, are most vulnerable. By including community members and marginalized groups, such as persons with special needs, female heads of households, disabled people, adolescents, and older persons during project design and implementation. Through its programs the Organization will ensure contextually appropriate and tailored interventions that meet the unique needs of each group. The intervention will include support for static women’s centers and GBV mobile teams to provide specialized protection assistance, prevention and support services for women and girl’s survivors of GBV in Ar Raqqa, areas around Deir Ez Zor and Al Hasakah. In order to mitigate GBV risk and prevent further violence, this NGO will conduct community engagement activities. In addition, we will also deliver sessions from the Adolescent Girls’ Toolkit Curriculum, designed to empower girls living in North East Syria through the delivery of Life Skills, Reproductive Health, Gender Based Violence (GBV), Financial Education and Leadership information
As part of its specialized psychosocial support activities, the organization will offer wide range of services that will reduce prevalence of MNS disorders and increase psychosocial resiliency for the affected population affected by trauma as a result of war in the NES. Services will include; Case management services, Psychosocial support, mhGAP detection and referral to paraprofessionals, mhGAP HIG for case managers and general practioners, structured peer group support to clients who will be referred and identified to require MHPSS services. There will also be upscaling of problem management plus intervention through training of trainers and providers, who will implement to the clients in order to reduce mild and moderate conditions of anxiety, depression, stress, alcohol and drug abuse etc. The project will also conduct community outreach activities that will encompass door to door and education sessions, which will reach out to the household level and create awareness of MHPSS services, thus reducing stigma. The project will target all community members, making a special effort to target and increase access for the most isolated and vulnerable through outreach, integrating MHPSS services into primary healthcare facilities MHPSS services will be available for all community members, both host and displaced, regardless of age, gender or disability status. This will improve the health of the vulnerable clients and reduce effects of post traumatic war effects.

4 - Needs

Since the start of the civil war in Syria in 2011, an estimated 5.6 million people have fled Syria to other countries, while 6.2 million have been internally displaced (IDPs) within Syria. Gender-based violence (GBV) remains a major protection risk, particularly in forms such as domestic and family violence, forced/early marriage as a coping mechanism and the fear of sexual violence and harassment. The lack of understanding for the need for GBV services in some communities remains high, indicating a need to strengthen GBV awareness, referral pathways, as well as outreach and prevention activities. Disruption of traditional safety nets and of livelihood opportunities, in addition to sudden displacement, put many women and girls at risk of various types of GBV that affect their lives in different ways, aggravating protection concerns such as psychosocial stress, limitation of movement in some areas of the country, situations and potential for increased recourse to negative coping mechanisms. This context highlights a continuous need for increasing the availability of specialized GBV services, including for older persons and persons with disabilities. During humanitarian emergencies as has been experienced in North East Syria, the rate of common mental disorders (depression, anxiety, post-traumatic stress disorder) came double to 20% of the population, while 2-3% of the population with existing severe mental disorders are especially vulnerable, often having no access to care. According to W.H.O, in emergency settings, it’s estimated that more than one in five people (22.1%) in post-conflict settings has depression, anxiety disorder, post-traumatic stress disorder, bipolar disorder, or schizophrenia and that almost one in ten people (9.1%)in post-conflict settings has a moderate of severe mental disorder at any point in time. Further studies have identified a sustained presence of high prevalence of mental disorders in conflict-affected countries.

5 - Implementation of activities

This NGO will provide a comprehensive and integrated GBV prevention and response and MHPSS services in Raqqa, Deir Ez Zor and Al Hassakeh within supported facilities. Protection services will be integrated into primary healthcare through: provision of GBV case management services using the survivor centred approach, adolescent girls sessions, recreational, vocational, risk reduction activities and different skills building activities at the identified WGSS as part of PSS. Capacity Building training on different GBV topics for frontline staff, and community members will build capacity, raise awareness, improve referral mechanisms and strengthen protection mechanisms. MHPSS activities will entail Comprehensive Case management provided through trained and supervised Psychosocial Workers (PSWs). Including referral/linking to other services, as well as ongoing targeted interventions such as training on coping, self-help and problem management plus (PM+) techniques in order to improve functionality and wellbeing. Community-based MHPSS group sessions will be designed to meet the needs identified by community members. Selected MHPSS trainings will be conducted for staffs on Psychological first aid, Problem management plus, detection and referral, stigma reduction, self-care, case management, basic counselling skills among others. Key monitoring and evaluation activities through qualitative and quantitative monitoring will include: baseline & end line survey, client satisfaction surveys per month, pre and post training assessments; routine monitoring visits; observation; updating and use of the gender based violence information management System (GBVIMS) database to collect GBV case related information confidentially and to analyse GBV trends to modify activities and to share information as relevant, safe and appropriate with other partners, including advocating for improved protection services in areas of high risk, documentation of success stories and lessons learned.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk: Gender relations in the country continue to be extremely fragile. Widespread discrimination and risks for women and girls (e.g. kidnapping, human trafficking, sexual violence and targeted killings) remain and are exacerbated during times of displacement, turmoil, and conflictMitigation measure: To ensure their meaningful involvement, the Organization will work with staff, local authorities and community members, including women, adolescent girls, persons with special needs, and others in particularly vulnerable situations, throughout the entire project cycle to ensure gender and protection mainstreaming into all activities by prioritizing consideration of specific and often sensitive issues related to gender, disability and protection among the beneficiary populations. This will be emphasized in the collection and analysis of data disaggregated by age and sex, focus group discussions and key informant interviews, GBVIMS throughout the project cycle with both mixed and single-sex groups In this way, The Organization will ensure the impartiality of program services enabling the project team to identify individuals and families with specific vulnerabilities related to age, sex, disability, family size, health status, socioeconomic status. Protection risk: Legal protection for the rights and safety of women and girls is lacking, leaving women vulnerable to multiple forms of GBV and social and economic disempowerment, with few or no channels for recourse or changeMitigation measure: The Organization will advocate for appropriate and adequate assistance to be provided for the survivors of GBV and vulnerable women and girls. Service mapping exercise will be conducted periodically to map out partners who can provide this support to the women and girls. Participation in the coordination meetings will see to it that information is shared amongst the different actors and possible solutions found to provide efficient assistance to the PoC’s.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions• The NGO continues to have approvals from local authorities to provide services in targeted locations• The NGO can maintain access to targeted locations to deliver the assistance• The security situation remains stable to implement the programs on a day to day basis• Non-interference by local population/groups in delivery of proposes assistance. Risks:• Security situation remains volatile where inter armed group violence, criminality, military operations by major political actors could lead to destabilization of the current status negatively impacting safety and security of staff and beneficiaries• Insufficient level of community participation in the intervention areas. • Program locations are taken over by Government of Turkey or allied forces or Government of Syria; leading to restrictions in access or implementation of activities without specific approvals Mitigation Measures:• The physical security of the organizations facilities to be regularly assessed and upgraded when needed. Close security monitoring, security training for staff which would hopefully limit/lessen exposure, contingency and evacuation/withdrawal plans in place, alternative implementation methodologies depending on situation. Duty of Care, families of killed or injured staff, severance pay for sudden closure of activities resulting in termination of staff• Community participation is a key aspect of the design of this project with community leader’s, women and girl’s groups consulted needs assessments. This NGO will continue to work closely with the community, building capacity of national staff based in areas of intervention, as well as the key community stakeholders, encouraging community participation. If community participation decreases advice from local leaders and representatives to find alternative ways of engaging the community will be explored.• The NGO has contingency and exit strategies in place to respond to loss of access or restrictions to program areas.
