Project Summary

The security, political and economic situation in the Gaza Strip has a deep immediate and long-term impact on children and their families whose resilience capacities are eroded. According to the Protection Cluster, 350,748 Gazans have been affected by the Great March of Return (GRM)-related violence, while 248,111 children show an increased level of psychosocial distress (Humanitarian Needs Overview-HNO 2019). Whist disability is a source of vulnerability, in May 2018, HI conducted a rapid needs’ assessment with MHPSS working group members to identify the needs for including children with disabilities in MHPSS programs. The results showed that children with disabilities remain barely visible, with an extremely limited access to safe and quality service-provision, including, but not limited to, health, mental-health, education and psychosocial support.
The Project aims to ensure that protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the conflict related violence, especially for children with disabilities in the Gaza Strip. To do so, the project builds on a pronged approach. On one hand, mental health and psychosocial support services will be provided to conflict-affected children, with a focus on children with disabilities and injuries. In a responsive action, the project will support protection and MHPSS quality services provision to children and their families through individual and collective psychological care, provision of assistive devices, case management and referral. On the other hand, the project will improve the capacity and participation of community members in addressing child protection needs, through the establishment of a child protection committee, coupled by awareness raising and the development of code of conduct. The implementation method relies on a strong partnership with two community-based organizations (CBOs), which will ensure access to the target beneficiaries, localization, and sustainability.

1 - Needs

According to the last situation report of the World Health Organization (WHO) from Gaza -July 2019, over half of conflict-affected children are likely affected by post-traumatic stress disorder; while an estimated 210,000 people -1 out of 10-, suffer from severe or moderate mental health disorders. The HNO 2019 states that an estimated number of 323,000 children in Gaza are in need of protection interventions, including psychosocial support. Because of the GRM violence, an additional 10,420 people is expected to face severe mental health problems, while 41,678 people will likely suffer from mild to moderate problems requiring MHPSS. The last Humanitarian snapshot reported the casualties in the context of demonstrations and hostilities in Gaza between 30 March 2018 and 31 July 2019. The number of Palestinian killed amounts to 311 including 55 boys and 4 girls, 34,185 have been injured, while 7,653 of them are children including 7,104 boys and 549 girls. Recently, the Palestinian Center for Democracy and Conflict Resolution - 2018 study on GMR needs of persons with injuries indicates that 75% of children with injuries 22.5% aged (12 - 14 ) & 52.5% aged (15 - 17 ) are facing emotional difficulties including the following psychosocial symptoms and disorders; stress, depression, nightmares, crying, aggressive, and inability to participate in social activities. In addition, 43.8% of the targeted children indicate that they are facing negative attitudes and behavior from others including laughing, teasing, rejection, social isolation due to the fact of being injured. In regard to the involvement in MHPSS activities, the study shows that 53.8% of the targeted children with injuries aged 12 to 17 did not participate in any PSS, 16.3% aged (12 - 14 ) and 37.5% aged (15 – 17 ). while, 75% of families of children with injuries didn’t participate in any support session on how to provide care to their children. Back in 2018, Islamic Relief in Palestine conducted a vulnerability assessment and mapping in the Gaza Strip. The level of vulnerability was defined as the level of access to basic service provided by municipalities, ministries, and other actors. The main sectors were assessed: economic, environmental, protection, education and health. As a result, the study revealed that the average of vulnerability rate in protection per governorate scores 66 %, where the most vulnerable governorates are Gaza and Rafah. A situation report (titled “Impact of the ‘Great March of Return’ on Gender-based Violence”) issued in June 2018 by the Gender-based violence sub-cluster in Gaza, shows the negative impact of the blockade on women's conditions especially concerning psychological stress, which threatens the stability of the family. More specifically, mothers of children with injuries have shown increased psychological/emotional high distress in addition to the further burden as a caregiver of a child with injuries. Within HI emergency intervention launched early in May 18, so far, HI’s partners’outreach teams reached 2,235 persons with injuries among whom 464 children of persons with injuries (12 girls and 452 boys) were targeted to receive multidisciplinary post-operative care and rehabilitation including psychosocial support at the individual and family level. The project was able to conduct almost 1,902 individual PSS specialized assessment, that indicate a real need for psychosocial support as most of these children are facing difficulty overcoming the daily stress due to the fact of being injured. The analysis of the project database indicates that more than 80% of children with injuries have difficulties to emotionally manage their feeling of experience, 155 children (151 boys and 4 girls) out of 464 (12 girls and 452 boys) aged 8-17 having difficulties to move, see, hear, speak and to emotionally manage their feeling of experience.

2 - Activities

HI has been working alongside children with/without disabilities, persons with disabilities in the Gaza Strip since 1996, offering them assistance and supporting them in their efforts to become self-reliant. The proposed project will benefit from the current networks and coordination established by HI with local and international actors including the CP MHPSS working group members, a fine understanding of the context, and long term investment from previous projects. The overall objective of the project is to support protection mechanism in order to prevent and mitigate the effects of the conflict related violence for children. Specifically, the project will contribute for women, girls and boys exposed to conflict related violence to have access to mainstream protection and MHPSS services (Project Outcome). To do so, the project will be using a two-pronged approach that includes responsive, remedial and resilience activities to reduce the vulnerability and risks behind exclusion of children with disabilities/injuries and their caregivers from accessing services. The first component (output 1) will ensure the provision of mental health and psychosocial support services will be provided to conflict-affected children, with a focus on children with disabilities and injuries. In a responsive action, this will be achieved through individual and collective psychological care, provision of assistive devices, referral to case management . In addition, the project will enhance the accessibility of the selecting CBOs through adapting its facilities, coupled by the capacity-building of the staff on inclusive protection and MHPSS strategies. The second component will seek to improve the capacity, representation, and participation of community members in addressing child protection concerns/needs. This will be achieved through the establishment of the local child protection committee inside the partner CBOs. The committee members will receive training on facilitation skills and protection related issues to allow them organizing awareness raising sessions in their communities. Besides that, the role of committee will be to participate in the development of the child protection code of conduct at partner CBOs supporting the creation of the protective environment. The logic of intervention is presented hereafter: Output 1: Protection and MHPSS needs of vulnerable children, including those with and without injuries/disabilities, and their caregivers are adequately addressed by CBOs and service providers. For the individual support sessions, the problem management plus – PM+ manual will be adopted; 1.3 Provision of outreach / home based protection and PSS services for children with disabilities/ injuries in particular girls and their caregivers living in ARA facing barriers to access center-based services. 8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

HI is implementing a safeguarding policy including an internal code of conduct, a policy on protection from Sexual Exploitation, a Child Protection policy and an Abuse and Anti-fraud, bribery and corruption policy. To enable beneficiaries, affected populations and partners to provide complaints, different mechanisms are in place including the availability of HI’s Safeguarding Focal Points. Focusing on partners, training and awareness sessions will be provided for the selected CBOs’ staff. In addition, HI will support CBOs in developing complains mechanisms in their entities as complains box. Partners’ Child Protection workers are entitled to inform beneficiaries through information and awareness sessions about the complaints mechanisms, the safeguarding policy and their rights to report about any suspicion or any inappropriate behavior. These mechanisms will develop safe ways of working and promote beneficiaries’ well-being, including the protection of children from maltreatment, abuse and violence, and deterioration or drop in children’s health or development.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The project is designed to provide protection to the targeted populations, children with and without disabilities/injuries with focus on girls and their female caregivers. The monitoring and documenting of gender-based violence cases reached by the project will allow to provide specialized services through the referral system, previously developed by HI. As well, case management pathways will be used to handle cases that in need for interventions. This will allow to improve prevention and protection specialized response. Data generated will be shared with the protection cluster members. In addition, the protection mainstreaming principles have been considered at the project’s design stage for its implementation. Because of the proposed training and awareness raising session on safeguarding, the two CBOs will offer a coordinated, confidential and safe attention to ensure dignity and avoid causing harm to the targeted beneficiaries. The target groups will be selected based on transparent selection criteria that ensure project services are delivered without bias. HI with its partner CBOs will ensure the access of all beneficiaries to a protective and safe environment where we all work together reducing and removing barriers to access taking into consideration disability, gender and age markers. HI will train partner staff on the identification of protection concerns and signs to support the identification of beneficiaries most in need for protection & MHPSS specialized services. This will ensure their referral through the existing referral pathways and networks where they can receive appropriate care within a safe and protective environment

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Accessibility is at the core of the project and will be ensured to all beneficiaries considering theirs needs and gender, through physical access to services and through consideration of their specificities by professionals –especially interacting with children with disabilities/ injuries. Additionally, all project and technical tools/materials will be tailored to suit Gaza’s context and needs of children with focus on children with disabilities.
