1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project aims to improve equitable access to relevant and inclusive learning opportunities for children exposed to conflict, with a particular focus on expanding access for children with disabilities. The main objectives of the project are: (i) to improve access to education for affected children through the removal of barriers, (ii) to strengthen equity and gender equality, (iii) to ensure better continuity and sustainability of education through improved quality of education for children with disabilities, and (iv) to promote a safe and protective environment in a more welcoming and appropriate setting.This project will specifically target boys and girls with disabilities between the ages of 5 and 15, their families/caregivers, their teachers, and communities. However, developing inclusive education practices benefits all children and the community as a whole. Because there is no clear data on children with disabilities between IDP and host communities, HI calculates the goal based on overall numbers: it is usually accepted that 15% of the population, and 10% of children, have a disability (WHO) and that about 14% of them are out of school. In this project, at least 50% of the total children are female, 70% of total children are IDPs and 30% are part of host communities.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The main activities that will be implemented in the districts of Ancuabe, Chiure and Mueda are:a. Identification of in-school and out-of-school children with disabilities: initial screening of children in and out of school, identifying children with disabilities and those who are at greatest risk of exclusion from a learning system.b. Barrier and facilitator assessments: Evaluate the inclusiveness of the schools and service provisions, including accessibility, social environment, staff and teacher competencies from the perspective of users and providers.c. Community sensitization, mobilization and awareness raising focused on (i) the importance of children attending school and learning opportunities: emphasis on ensuring enrollment of children most vulnerable to exclusion or neglect; (ii) children's rights, child protection risks and psychosocial advice on how to cope with reactions to severe stress or trauma.d. Addressing educational barriers through (i) identification and implementation of safe and inclusive TLS and provision of basic educational kits for teachers and students; (ii) teacher training on Inclusive Education; (iii) support to teachers through specific peer support groups and PSS training.e. Direct support to children with disabilities and their families through (i) referral to health, social and rehabilitation services and PSS; (ii) specific educational support to children with disabilities regarding their needs including inclusive remedial classes and provision of specific adapted materials

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Violent conflicts and disasters disrupt the lives of those who are forced to seek shelter from harm. Children are the most vulnerable population during crises and the accumulation of risks for girls and children with disabilities increases dramatically. Their development can be slowed, halted or even reversed if they are not given the tools and protective spaces to understand and heal from their experiences. While all children face threats of exploitation, violence and child labour, girls and children with disabilities often face additional barriers that preclude their access to education. Without supportive policies, the exploitation of children can worsen. To protect children from abuse and exploitation, HI adopts a PSEA policy which aims to protect children from intentional and unintentional harm or abuse. Safe and accessible learning environments help provide the protection children need: schools help foster a sense of normality and routines that promote children's positive development, and they can also receive life-saving messages and life-skills education, such as healthy WASH practices and the importance of inclusivity. The project aims to support the development of a safer learning environment for all children, including children with disabilities, where the well-being of all is considered. Protection actions aim to prevent violence and its disabling consequences and to provide assistance to survivors of violence or threats of violence, exploitation and abuse. Protection mechanisms embedded in inclusive education will be implemented and protective and psychosocial issues faced by children with disabilities and their families/caregivers will be addressed by supporting the school community to be more inclusive. Teachers will benefit from consultation and training.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

All project activities are based on a participatory and collaborative approach with the direct involvement of the local population and local educational authorities. Continued cooperation with children, children with disabilities, families and most severaly affected people is expected at all stages. Specifically the project:- ensures participation of all targeted groups in the assessment of barriers and facilitators; - plans to consult with prospective male and female teachers, male and female students, children with disabilities, parents, and community leaders on key action decisions, such as identifying a temporary safe learning spasafe TLS e and the best learning program they need;- staff will be recruited with local language skills;- a proportionate number of male and female teachers will be recruited, both from among the displaced and the host community;- a variety of learning activities will be offered, depending on the characteristics and needs of the participants;- the creation of a community-based education committee will be encouraged: half of the committee members will be women and the committee include representatives of the students. The main role of the committee will be to monitor the focus and performance of the school and to propose solutions to any problems that arise.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

This project supports equal access to educational opportunities for girls and boys with disabilities, as well as for children most severely affected by the crisis. However, they are not a homogenous group and face a wide range of barriers when trying to access the education system. The promotion of safe and child-friendly schools/learning spaces, personalised support and awareness-raising activities are designed to significantly reduce barriers to acess, as well as teacher training and support and the provision of adapted materials will be useful to strength the capacity to provide quality education. Through the initial assessment and focus group discussions, HI will identify out-of-school children and encourage school participation through direct family and child support and awareness campaigns. Then working with community leaders, OPDs and community volunteers, as well as protection actors, additional children will be identified. The use of WGQs or CFMs will identify children who have functional limitations in a variety of domains and who are at risk of experiencing limited participation in an uncomfortable environment, and are likely to have a disability. Children with mild to severe difficulties in any domain would be referred for further assessment for a medical diagnosis and/or an assessment of their specific needs.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The environment will have little or no impact from the project. Potential negative effects mainly relate to the social and human environment, where gender equality efforts challenge traditional norms and can cause backlash, including violence against girls and women. Successful behaviour change and awareness-raising efforts can lead to long-term positive effects in the community environment, as equitable behaviour increases over time and social norms and values change. An assessment of environmental barriers that prevent access to education for conflict-exposed children, especially girls and young women with disabilities, will be conducted at the beginning of the project. However, the identification and removal of these barriers will take into account sustainability and environmental impact. Safe and inclusive spaces will also be identified that will allow out-of-school children to have access to a temporary educational programme that will be created using local materials.
