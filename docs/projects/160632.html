Project Summary

The project seeks to support the provision of and rehabilitation of emergency shelter and life-saving Non Food items to vulnerable populations in Ulang and Panyikang Counties in Upper Nile State.The core of this 1 year intervention is to improve the living conditions of vulnerable Displacement affected populations.Needs assessment in the target counties have been done and documented.The 8 – 13 May 2019 IRNA mission to Ulang County covered the following locations: Kuic,Yomding,Ying,Pakuay,Nyangore,Ulang,Riang & Doma payams.SSS lead a team of partners in June to come up with the Kiechkuon Returnee Report and the Ulang Flood Disaster Report, and all these assessments exposed huge needs in the affected populations including lack of shelter for newly arrived returnees and IDP's,dilapidated shelter,lack of blankets and mosquito nets,lack of water cans/pots to fetch water,lack of cooking utensils,lack of dignity kits for girls and women.This assessment report coincides with the needs assessment in Panyikang County,where displacements,inter clan fighting and floods have continued to wreck havoc among the host and Displacement affected populations.Continued armed crashes between government and oppositions forces continue to impede humanitarian efforts and displace civilians throughout and within the target counties.These counties have for the last two years hosted and received a huge number of Displacement Affected Populations from neighboring states and counties. The affected populations in these 2 counties are in dire need of humanitarian assistance. Refugees and South Sudanese locals have fled their homes, carrying their belongings and seen gathering together on little islands of dry ground.We remain concerned that the flooding may increase the risks and spread of diseases.This year's situation gives us a broader picture of worsening climatic changes and disaster preparedness is a major necessity. This project targets 15,000 direct beneficiaries composing of newly displaced people in protracted displacement,returnees and host communities unable to meet their S/NFI needs.Further,lactating and pregnant women,households with malnourished children,unaccompanied children and unaccompanied elderly and persons with disability will be targeted.The project will undertake timely delivery of life-saving assistance through the deployment of rapid response teams in line with humanitarian priorities for 2020 as outlined in the HRP.
Panyikang and Ulang Counties in Upper Nile State are among the most severely conflict affected counties since the 2013 and 2016 crisis.This project will be implemented in close collaboration with the shelter cluster and project activities will be crafted taking into account the gaps identified through the multi-sectoral assessment missions.SSS will dynamically adapt the project to the specific needs of men,women,girls and boys,elderly and respond accordingly.
The distribution of SNFI's will take into consideration the minimum protection standards and be undertaken at sites to be identified together with the beneficiaries.Steps to protect women and girls against SGBV will be undertaken including positioning distribution points within short walking distances from residences.Special attention and priority will be accorded to the elderly,people with disabilities,pregnant women and children to enhance reach and access.Feedback desks will be provided at each distribution point.SSS will work closely with TADO,FYF,SSUDA,DRC and others on SNFI and other clusters partners for better inclusiveness,effectiveness and efficiency in distribution & Monitoring.SSS will complement its distribution to community awareness campaigns and information sessions on proper usage of the distributed items.

1 - Needs

This project is designed in response to the 2020 (SSHF) cluster strategies.The project targets vulnerable people in geographically priority areas in line with the cluster priorities.Protracted internal conflicts in South Sudan and unpredictable weather pattern that is now extreme for this year,has worsened the situation of host communities subjecting them to vulnerability and loss.There have been increased displacement in almost the whole country due to either Inter-Communal Conflicts, signing of the Revitalized Peace Agreement and Continuous Flooding that have rid the whole country. SSS intends to apply in-kind based intervention to assist the identified beneficiaries since there are no credible markets or the existing markets have been destroyed and submerged by floods or abandoned due to displacements. Since SSS does not have MOU with Pipeline,we will work closely with S/NFI Cluster,Logistics Cluster and a sister NNGO ,TADO that is also on ground and already has an MOU to make a request for the supplies from pipeline on our behalf.The areas SSS intends to target are Ulang (Kurmout 1,Kurmout 2,Kerchoat,Barmach), and Panyikang (Kaldak,Atar,Agurgo,Athakong,Kwogo).The 8-13th May IRNA report gave the following number of IDP's & returnees (Kurmout 1 ---2,984,Kurmout2---2,035,Kerchoat ---2,750,Barmach---1,110).The organization intends to target 15,000 beneficiaries segregated among the identified counties as follows; Ulang (12,500), Panyikang (2,500).The situation on ground is alarming and it is estimated that more than 20,000 beneficiaries are in dire need of the S/NFI based intervention,and this number is expected to have risen higher due to the current floods that has submerged villages ,markets and farmlands.Cases of communal conflicts and government and opposition campaigns to take over Malakal town has also contributed to displacements.As per the above, SSS intends to intervene in some of the severity locations identified by the cluster.SSS will engage already existing women groups,youth groups throughout the project for more accurate information gathering.This project will reduce suffering,save lives and enhance self reliance and improve coping capabilities of the beneficiaries by provision of life saving emergency shelter and NFI to vulnerable populations in an open,accountable and participatory manner.This will be better achieved by involving the beneficiary communities in the planning and implementation of interventions in accordance with the (HAP) principles.The project team will adopt conflict sensitive approaches embracing Do No Harm principles.The distribution of Shelter and NFI items will be guided by standard vulnerability criteria,meeting the needs of large families,households with children under 5,pregnant or lactating women (PLW) unaccompanied elderly persons (above 60 years),people living with disabilities (PLWD),female headed households,unaccompanied minors and the chronically ill.The distribution points will be planned and implemented in the presence of all beneficiaries and authorities,women and youth groups and done near settlement areas to avoid long treks to access items which would otherwise expose women and girls to sexual harrasment.SSS will engage communities on GBV and HLP with assistance from local authorities,having in mind that in humanitarian work its unanimously accepted that anyone with needs has the right to assistance, regardless of their right to the land they are occupying or using when they receive it. Conversely,SSS maintains her neutrality from engaging in controversies of a political, racial or religious or ideological nature.We will employ ‘do no harm' approach while engaging GBV & HLP approaches to inadvertently reinforce and even legitimize unjust land claims during implementation.With this in mind,SSS will work closely with other agencies with expertise,cluster,community leaders,religious leaders for better coordination and information dissemination.

2 - Activities

1.Initial Community Engagement to understand the community and enhance their capacity to participate in the project.Training and orientation sessions on the processes of engagement will be emphasized (Do No Harm,protection,HLP rights and gender considerations).A conflict sensitive framework for programming will be put in place.Training communities and staff in Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP) Principles to enhance safe programming and accountability to affected populations will be undertaken.2. Setup community feedback mechanism including formation of Beneficiary Accountability Committees and other feedback and complaints mechanisms during project implementation (especially during assessment /registration and distribution of S/NFI's).A feedback desk will be setup during distribution. Committees will take into account gender and age,ensuring that committee members are represented by women,youth and elderly as well as people with special needs.3.Initial assessment including conducting needs assessments both direct and inter agency to identify beneficiaries needs for Emergency Shelter and Non Food Items paying attention to the needs of women,men,girls and boys.Existing lists by WFP will be considered but verified and validated.This will be done in cooperation with state authorities and beneficiary committees and in consultation with other S/NFI partners.SSS will identify and engage already existing Women/youth Groups in needs assessment and through out the implementation process.4.Carry out verification and registration of persons in readiness for distribution (focusing on specific needs of children,women,men,girls and boys).Household interviews will be used where feasible.Beneficiary Accountability Committees will be formed and will participate in all the stages of implementation.State authorities will be engaged in identification,listing,and during distribution to the beneficiaries.5.Awareness raising including mainstreaming protection and gender.Beneficiary Accountability Committees formed will be trained on generating and dissemination of key messages.Do No Harm principles will be adopted.Awareness creation teams will be made up of local members of the community,trained and led by a member of staff.6.Distribution of Emergency shelter and non food items as per identified and verified needs of the beneficiaries.Efficient and equitable distribution approaches will be planned and implemented in consultation with the affected populations through the Beneficiary Accountability Committees and in coordination with cluster and state authorities. Logistical arrangements will be made with core pipeline and ensure that there is adequate storage and security arrangements.SSS will make use of Kodok's Humanitarian Hub for operations in Panyikang County,and Mandeng Humanitarian Hub for operations in Ulang County.SSS's reliance on these 2 humanitarian hubs is out of:- 1. They are strategically placed in our target counties and their proximity to our target locations. 2.The need to access reliable communication and internet for report writing. 3.Networking with other agencies implementing similar or various other projects and easier involvement in joint missions 4.Safety of staffs and safety of S/NFI items.With effective communication Relevant stakeholders will be consulted on which distribution locations are most suitable for safe and dignified access to and receipt of shelter and NFI's,Monitoring and evaluation and learning including post distribution monitoring.This will be ongoing activity culminating into post distribution monitoring.This will be done at least 3 weeks after distribution to evaluate effectiveness,appropriateness and coverage.The beneficiary Accountability Committee will take an active role in collaboration with project staff in carrying out monitoring and evaluation.8. Reporting to funding partners and Beneficiaries as well as sharing will cluster and implementing partners will be an ongoing activity.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

The project is legally binding according to the laws of South Sudan, It targets each segment of the population differently depending on the needs identified. Women and girls are more prone to vulnerability in comparison to males and this is taken into account when designing protection interventions. For instance, Women & young girls walking long distances to access water or forage for food are exposed to greater risk of abduction and SGBV compared to men and boys. Protection interventions must be alive to such realities. Further, the aged and disabled are more vulnerable. Interventions are informed by risk analysis that takes into account gender, age and cultural practices that increase or reduce vulnerability to risk.This project is designed to consider protection of the beneficiaries from all forms of risks associated with its implementation. This would be by and ensuring that information acquired from a beneficiary would be kept confidential and could only be released to the third party at the consent of the originator. Parties would be required to adhere to the rules before any intervention is carried out.SSS in collaboration with Protection clusters/partners on the ground and the local authority will make sure the areas are safe from any danger to the populations/beneficiaries and environment for successful implementation of the project.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Beneficiaries will be instrumental in the identification of needs and in determining the appropriate locations for distribution. They will be actively involved in giving feedback to the project team through direct engagement with the Beneficiary Feedback mechanisms and also through the Beneficiary Accountability Committee.SSS collaborates with the local community especially during assessments, where the communities are hired as part of the assessment team. Communities will also participate during the distributions and identification of beneficiaries. Communities also participate in focus group discussions, orientation and feedback mechanisms. Local communities are also employed during the distribution process especially in crowd control, registration, mobilization, arranging items/NFIs and offloading.Awareness messaging and basic project information material will, where necessary, be translated in to local language for the better understanding of beneficiaries. A community leader will always be involved, complaints will always be handled and immediate feedback given to beneficiaries. Beneficiaries will fill the complaint forms and will be provided with help if required for record purpose. Beneficiaries will be trained in project messaging and dissemination to enable them reach out to other members of the community in order to create awareness and enhance project ownership.This project will be implemented in close collaboration with the Shelter/FSL Clusters, beneficiaries’ community leaders, ROSS/Protection cluster and partners and local authority and project activities will be crafted taking into account the gaps identified through the multi-sector assessment missions. SSS will dynamically adapt the project to the specific needs of men, women, girls and boys and respond accordingly. With regards to the cluster objective, the proposed project aims to distribute shelter material for setting up or improving dilapidated shelters and NFIs.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Save South Sudan's SNFI project is embedded into the existing government and community system at all levels from village to Boma to payam to county, state and national levels.SSS will network with all stakeholders:- partners, beneficiaries and the informal sector in planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation for community ownership and project sustainability. This will be done in a consultative and coordinated manner for inclusiveness. Selection of beneficiaries will be done using the set criteria so that the right beneficiaries are targeted, community leaders, local authorities, church leaders will be involved at all levels of S/NFI cycle.SSS will work hand in hand with county authorities on how the beneficiaries will acquire poles for erecting shelters,and avoid involving women going to the forests as this would also expose them to GBV and sexual harassment. The involvement of county/local authorities will be of utmost importance as this will quell disputes of harvesting/exhaustion of locally available resources which the host community might not feel well contented with .This is bearing in mind that some of the Displacement Affected Populations do to hail from these exact locations.SSS will work closely with other agencies with HLP expertise on ground,regional land alliances, community leaders, church leaders and peace commissioners during implementation of the project.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Peace is a vice that forms the driving force to development,social justice and creates a platform for satisfaction,national cohesion and healthy inter relationships amongst communities.Provision of basic services is a factor that is inter twinned to conflict resolution and healing.This project in full knowledge of the dire needs of communities,will work as a tool to peace dissemination,which is a national concern.The ongoing peace resolution and settlement of warring communities is the back bone to success of this project,banking on the willingness of the stakeholders to settle disputes amicably.The Government of the Republic of South Sudan, is committed to assist in finding durable solutions for IDPs in the country through the Transitional Government of National Unity (TGONU) Government of National Unity (TGoNU) and its partners to ensure and provide: •Secure access to civilian populations in need of emergency humanitarian assistance and protection. •Create a safe and secure environment by establishing the rule of law to achieve durable solutions, including return, reintegration and relocation •Implement relief and development programmes in line with internationally accepted human rights and humanitarian principles and standards. •Provide special consideration to conflict-affected persons -including children, women, widows, persons with disabilities, etc. in the provision of public service, including access to health and education services and granting the host communities the same protection and humanitarian services. •Foster community dialogue and reconciliation not only at the political levels but also inter-communal levels. •Implement early recovery and stabilization interventions especially in communities where individuals are returning or being relocated, to include; by restoring the capacity of local governments, rule of law institutions, and creating value chains for sustainable livelihoods.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

SSS Human Security Policy recognises that Displacement Affected Populations and vulnerable people are exposed to a constant series of threats and risks and focuses on the need to retain a minimum quality of life,defined as Freedom from Fear,Freedom from Want and Freedom to take action on one's own behalf.SSS seeks to empower affected conflict communities to claim their rights and emphasizes the need to protect ,respect full people's rights.This includes the provision of safety gear while at work,messaging on safe use of services provided.The project will liaise with the existing government structure to ensure that beneficiaries are protected from harm, mainstream LHP and conflict sensitive approaches throughout all stages of S/NFI response. Beneficiaries will be exposed to LHP messaging to reduce harm and build resilience by building on existing systems and community dynamics, mainstream GBV throughout all stages of S/NFI response. This will ensure that gender and protection risks are addressed directly, mitigating chances of exacerbated vulnerabilities Mainstream LHP issues throughout the stages of S/NFI response as land is the most single factor of production and livelihood for sustainability, Community engagement and consultation in the implementation of S/NFI response will be critical as a way of feedback mechanism. The project team will adopt conflict sensitive approaches embracing “Do No Harm” principles. Community engagements on GBV, HLP and integrating the work across other clusters where SSS and other partners are active. The project will be part of an integrated response that will not only address the shelter needs but also support populations facing greatest protection risks. Gender principles of active and equitable participation in community level distribution committees will be followed and supported and at the same time distribution activities will be planned in such a way as to facilitate women involvement.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

At the moment, the proposed intervention can only complement and justify SSS willingness to intervene on Education,WASH,Food Security and Health in Emergencies in the target Counties. The provision of NFIs and shelter including fishing kits will go a long way in enhancing access to education to children and youth across the board from IDPs, returnees to the host community. SSS works closely with other implementing partners, jointly planning interventions. In particular, for the S/NFI intervention, SSS will actively reach out to other S/NFI partners such as SSUDA, TADO,World Vision and jointly plan for the intervention ensuring that there is collaboration in implementation and minimizing duplication. SSS will seek to learn from more experienced S/NFI partners and seek to actively participate in the local S/NFI cluster deliberations. At every stage of the process, SSS will seek to learn and collaborate with existing partners including state authorities. Protection experts will be brought on board to ensure that programming takes protection issues into consideration and that there is adherence to the “Do No Harm” principle.
