Project Summary

World Relief Sudan (WRS) is currently providing integrated humanitarian emergency response, recovery and development programs in Food Security Livelihoods, health, nutrition, education, protection, peace building, WASH and activities funded bilateral and multilateral donors including SHF, OFDA, DFID, SIDA, UNICEF, BMZ, WFP, MoFA of Netherlands and private donors.
This Food security and livelihoods (FSL) project is designed with the overall objective of saving lives and building resilience of the most vulnerable HHs and reducing acute food insecurity and malnutrition through ensuring availability of nutritious food to 188,432 HHs (942,161 individuals 565,297 female & 376,864 male) (in 12 localities in the four states including (El Kurmuk, Geissan and Baw localities) of Blue Nile, (El Geniena, Jebel moon, and Kreinik) in West Darfur, (Gharb jabal marrah, Shamal jabal marrah and Wasat jabal marrah) of Central Darfur and (Abassiya, Abu jubayhah and Al rashad) of South Kodrdofan . Besides, this project is prepared in alignment with three FSL strategic objectives (SOs). The project also focuses on two major areas, the first focus area is ensuring conflict and other disaster affected people in the four states including IPDs, returnees, host communities and nomads receive timely, adequate and required assistance during and in the aftermath of a shock. The other focus area is improving availability and accessibility to sufficient, quality and nutritious food to individuals impacted by protracted crisis. To achieve these, WR conducted Community Based Participatory Planning (CBPP) in Blue Nile, West and Central Darfur. In South Kordfan, WR team conducted key informant interviews in the three targeted localities. To this end, project plan has been prepared with active involvement of the community and activities are prioritized for implementation based on community priorities and triangulated with data from different sources. IDPs, returnees and extremely vulnerable host communities are the central focus of the project. Provision of agricultural inputs, improving livestock veterinary services & water schemes, income generating activities, conditional and unconditional food assistance, various capacity buildings, saving and loan schemes, restocking and gender & environment are the main focus areas.
Even though women constitutes significant portion of the households, they are often hindered by many traditional norms from making real progress which make it difficult for them to access new agricultural technologies as well as improved livelihood opportunities. This project will mainly target women headed households in order to build their capacities which allows them to sufficiently increase their productivity beyond consumption.
The organization recognizes the importance of the human rights of refugees, returnees, IDPs and especially the most vulnerable groups such as women, girls and minority groups. While implementing this project, the organization will make conscious and intentional efforts to avoid all situations that expose beneficiaries to risk, threat, violence and abuse of their rights.
All interventions will be done in partnership with government line ministries, community-based committees, local authorities, community leaders and beneficiaries themselves. As described further below, interventions are designed to meet immediate emergency needs of the beneficiaries mainly women, girls and children while employing approaches that increase community ownership and thus sustainability.
Furthermore, this project is designed to not cause negative impact on the environment. Appropriate protection measures including seedlings production and plantation, community awareness on environment, safe disposal of wastes, germination tests and focal on local verities.

1 - Needs

According to Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC 2021), an estimated 942,161 people face crisis or worse levels of food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 and above) in West Darfur, Central Darfur, Blue Nile and South Kordfan which need urgent action to save lives. The figure, the highest ever recorded in the history of the IPC analysis in the country, represents an increase of 22 per cent compared with the same period last year. 381,056 people are facing emergency levels of acute food insecurity (IPC 4), while around 561,105 people are facing crisis levels of acute food insecurity (IPC 3). The increase number of people under stress and any additional shock could push them to severe levels of hunger. Some localities in areas affected by shortages of water during the rainy season have shifted to higher levels of food insecurity compared to 2021 projection; all localities targeted by FSL/WR may be shifted from IPC phase 3 to phase 4. Based on various food security assessments, the most vulnerable groups are IDPs, returnees, refugees and host communities. The highest severity of acute food insecurity is recorded in all four states. The number of households facing crisis impact is expected to remain high among IDPs and people recently affected by tribal clashes in Darfur, Kordofan flood-affected households, Ethiopian and South Sudanese refugees, poor households in eastern and western Sudan who have exhausted their food stocks, and urban poor households. Intercommunal conflict incidents have significantly increased in Darfur and some other parts of the country during July and August 2021, with the start of rainy season cultivation and seasonal movements of nomads into the northern wet season grazing areas. The violence is likely to track with the seasonal movements of nomadic groups into southern grazing areas at the start of the transhumance in October/November 2021, which coincides with the agricultural harvest period. In last September Food Security Technical Secretariat (FSTS) of Blue Nile reported that a combination of food security triggers created mixed scenarios on the three-food security contributing factors (Availability, Accessibility and Utilization). Because of stressed food security situation and multiple effects of the lean season, outcome data on nutrition showing a worsened nutritional status especially among children under-five. Trends show unpredictability of rainfall patterns in the localities with adverse effect on crop production, pasture development for animals, and market prices which undermine efforts of vulnerable people (women, children and other people with special needs); Poor production of crops due to lack of productive seeds, farm tools, agricultural technologies and extension systems; Lack of food/nutritional diversification due to the major use of Sorghum/millet as staple food. Poor post-harvest management and marketing of cereals and vegetable crops caused Signiant loss of the production and quality; Shortage of water for livestock which gets pronounced during dry season forcing households to travel more than 10km to get water for livestock and becoming the cause for conflicts; Absence of organic fertilizers usage to improve the chemical (nutritional) and physical properties of the soil; Communities poor access to income sources due to lack of skill and market networks; Environmental degradation exacerbated by firewood need, burning charcoal as a means of income, cutting trees for construction and income, and overgrazing the grassland contributed to degradation of the natural vegetation and soil, which also counter affected the agricultural production and depletion of water sources; Poor agro forestry, soil & water conservation practices contribute to soil erosion; limited skill of agricultural extension worker.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project provides maximum care not to cause pollute the environment. For activities having an impact on the environment, the project design required mitigation measures, which will be implemented parallel to project activities implementation. Environmental markers guideline was reviewed to ensure that the project is designed sensitive to the environment and necessary mitigation measures will be to reduce the risks. The project will not encourage the use of toxic chemicals and commercial fertilizers, rather the communities will be trained and supported to apply biological and cultural pest control and organic farming. To ensure environmental compliance and adhere to no-do-harm principles WR will provide training on integrated water resources management and create awareness to the community on environment. The irrigation facilities and the hand dug wells for livestock will be accompanied with drainage ditches to avoid water stagnation. The project works together with the MOA so that the different types of inputs provided to the community pass through quality check based government standards and other quality check measures. In addition, the seeds and related inputs will be inspected for presence of pests and weed seeds not to introduce to the localities. In this regard, the project will not import or introduce planting materials out of the Ecological areas. Through other programs, WR will support with several tree seedlings in the project proposed localities which will be distributed to individual households, planed in public places, and provided to different institutions including health facilities, schools and social centers. Moreover, there will be environmental protection campaign at villages’ level.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

WR will ensure that all activities to contribute to improving the wellbeing of women and children and contribute towards improving women access to and control over assets created by the project. Women and girls are overburdened in domestic and agricultural activities and are sole responsible for child care in the household. Besides, women have almost no right to make a decision on the utilization and transfer of the major assets. This project has analyzed all the constraints that affect women and girls and develop strategies that ensure their active participation and benefits. Women and women headed households will get priority to address their needs. More than 60% of the beneficiary households will be women. Improved technologies that reduces the burden of women are included in the project so that women get enough time for domestic and other reproductive activities. WR recognizes the importance of the human rights of refugees, returnees, IDPs and especially the most vulnerable groups minority groups. While implementing this project, WRS will make conscious and intentional efforts to avoid all situations that expose beneficiaries to risk, threat, violence and abuse of their rights including SGBV problems. Mobile veterinary services will be conducted, reaching out to populations who are unable to access the static facilities, thus ensuring that individuals afraid or unable to move to the static facilities are not denied assistance or services. This is particularly important for elderly and disabled beneficiaries who may be unable to walk the required distances to project sites. The organization will also be sure to promote conflict sensitivity by ensuring that all community groups including IDPs, host community members, returnees, pastoralists, and varying tribal and ethnic groups have equal access to project services by selecting project sites in areas accessible to the different groups. This minimizes the risk of conflict due to competition over social services. The possibility of discrimination by locally hired staff against beneficiaries who do not hold the same tribal or political affiliations will be mitigated by ensuring that program teams that consist of combinations of locally hired national staff representing all ethnic groups, national staff hired from outside the programmatic area and international staff. In addition, the M&E system segregates data by gender and age groups to measure and analyze how the project is affecting different groups. The organization will focus on building the capacity of the staff to strengthen their understanding of the fundamental human rights and serving beneficiaries with dignity. In addition, the organization ensure that all staff, volunteers and contractors working with the organization sign code of conduct.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The actions proposed by WR are prepared based on the needs and priorities of the communities, which has been collected and compiled through needs assessment, field visit and discussion with community leaders and line ministries. In addition, the intended communities have also confirmed their commitment to fully participate through the entire project life cycle. Overall the community will be involved in the project as depicted below. • Through their representatives the community had been involved in needs assessment and formulation of this project; • WR established three layers of community engagement in the project proposed locations. The first layer of engagement with the community is through community-level volunteers in project design, implementation and monitoring of activities in their respective locations. The second layer is through strengthening and working with community-level development committees and extension workers. These committees established to oversee the work of WR in their respective areas or locality. In addition, CAHWs and extension workers, who contribute to the strengthening of livestock and agriculture-based livelihoods of the communities, and provide feedback through various reporting and communication mechanisms. • The community level engagement materialized through conducting a regular meeting with community-level committees and volunteers. Sector officers and area coordinators conduct monthly meeting at the community level and discuss progress, challenges, future plan and opportunities, community concerns and risks. WRS displays hotline reporting mechanisms so that the community members can report their level of satisfaction on the services provided by WRS and partners to relevant staff in the organization. Furthermore, WR will continue using the existing suggestion box to get the feedback from beneficiary communities. The beneficiary and community will be informed to put their feedback anytime in the suggestion box and WR coordinators will collect information from the suggestion box on a quarterly basis and discuss with all concerned including higher management. Also, the actions taken to the community findings will be shared back to the community.
