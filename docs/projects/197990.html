Project Summary

Due to the high insecurity in the BAY states, we observe a lot of displacement, which creates a great need for shelters and NFI. Moreover, the climatic conditions (wind, rain, etc.) often washes away the shelters and basic items of the population living in the camps and host communities. To meet these needs, SI plans to distribute shelter repair kits, construct, with the help of skilled carpenters, BAMA shelters. SI also wishes to have a longer-term impact by repairing transitional shelters. Finally, every household that will receive shelter assistance will also be given a NFI kit. These actions will benefit 8,360 individuals.

1 - Needs

Due to harsh weather, displacements and overstay in the place of displacement and shelters’ short lifespan, many IDPs in camps are living in completely or partially damaged shelters and require shelter assistance. The condition of these shelters exposes them to the extreme climatic conditions and protection risks induced by the absence of privacy. Host communities are considered as more resilient to cope with future shock / disaster as 53% of HC members are living in concrete-building shelters in Monguno and Ngala compared to 19% of IDPs. Indeed, IDPs are mostly living in make-shift shelters (32%) and emergency shelters (27%) with 36% of IDPs who are staying in these locations for more than 1 year and show the willingness to stay are living in transitional shelters. Land acquisition for the construction of new shelters is the real challenge in Borno state. Most people are unable to acquire new land for shelter construction. Furthermore, displacements and the subsequent lack of income-generating activities prevent IDPs from buying essential items. Access challenges to shelter are also matched by dire needs of shelter NFI like beddings, kitchen utensils and water containers

2 - Activities

In order to answer the needs assessment in our areas of intervention, SI will distribute shelter repair kits to the most vulnerable households. SI will also construct emergency BAMA shelters, helped by skilled carpenters recruited within the communities who will be given a tool kit. Part of SI’s strategy is to also have medium/long term shelter impact through the rehabilitation of transitional shelter. Finally, all the household who will benefit from a shelter assistance will also receive a NFI kit. Given financial budgetary constraints, SI shall not be able to access all deserving cases , hence priority shall be accorded to households with SAM cases, persons living with disabilities, most elderly and pregnant/lactating mothers. 8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

All SI staff are experienced and trained on mainstreaming protection as a cross-cutting theme across all programs to increase the efficiency of humanitarian assistance. All staff also sign a Code of Conduct, ensuring they provide the program services to all members in the targeted communities, regardless of gender, age, religious belief, and are inclusive to people with additional or special needs, such as disabled or elderly people. In addition, SI prioritizes the needs of various vulnerable groups in their programming (children, women, people with special needs, elderly). SI strategy program is based on a community approach and the organization is used to organize gender and disability inclusive Focus Group Discussion (FGDs) in order to better identify the specific needs of the most vulnerable beneficiaries. In addition, it has already developed monitoring tools that include protection mainstreaming indicators and has included questions related to the protection mainstreaming in all its surveys to measure that humanitarian assistance is delivered safely, accessible, accountable, and participatory. This monitoring tool will be used to monitor this project’s activities. Indeed, where ever possible, daily workers will be recommended by their own communities and grouped respecting gender equity, to increase local visibility and accountability, and reduces risks.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

SI will use a mixed research approach, and both quantitative and qualitative data will be collected as guided by the logical framework to ensure the collection of reliable, credible and valid data for the project. The outcome and process monitoring of the project will comprise of the following key activities: • Beneficiaries registration surveys;• Verification of beneficiaries;• Feedback and Complaints Response Mechanism (FCRM); • Regular Field Monitoring visits (quality check list of workmanship)• Community and stakeholders’ feedback.SI will ensure that the beneficiaries know the project deliverables, mode of assistance, selection criteria, entitlements, and standards of behavior they must expect from SI staff and project. Specifically, in order to strengthen participation and accountability to beneficiaries in the implementation of this project, an entry point assessment will be conducted in the project areas, with the participation of community members and humanitarian actors both in camps and HCs in order to update the accountability channels and adapt them to the context and to the beneficiaries' capacity to feedback and have their views adapted to improve implementation . Beneficiaries shall be consulted on planning, selection, award criteria, siting, installation/construction of shelters and PDMs conducted for feedback on quality of installations as well as Shelter NFI materials. Any complaints shall be recorded and handled as per SI FCRM.
