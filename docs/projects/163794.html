Project Summary

REACH, a joint initiative of ACTED, IMPACT and UNOSAT, has been providing assessment and information management services to humanitarian actors in Libya since 2011, initially in the aftermath of the first conflict and then later establishing a full-time presence in the country in 2015. In light of the dynamic context in Libya, it is necessary to maintain the flow of updated evidence in order to develop efficient and adequate programming to address the needs and vulnerabilities of affected populations. Through this project, REACH will ensure that critical information on the humanitarian situation in Libya is provided through a Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA), through the delivery and coordination of Rapid Needs Assessments and through technical support to sector-led assessments, thereby informing more effective planning and immediate responses to sudden crises.
Multi-Sector Needs Assessment: The basic methodology of the MSNA is expected to stay consistent in 2020 with previous years, to ensure the comparability of data. The main component will be a quantitative data collection exercise (i.e., a HH survey) conducted in partnership with local CSOs, followed by a smaller qualitative data collection exercise that will triangulate and build on the survey’s findings. Also, for consistency’s sake, the 2020 MSNA is expected to target the same three population groups as in previous years: IDPs, returnees and the non-displaced. That said, REACH will consult and coordinate with the sectors, the IMWG, the ISCG and the HCT around the details of the 2020 MSNA methodology, including geographic coverage and the choice of indicators. Once the survey data is collected and analysed, presentations of preliminary results will be given to the sectors, so that sectors may use the data as they develop their strategic plans for 2021. Subsequent presentations of results (e.g., to the HCT) will include more advanced analysis and will be conducted in time to feed into the 2021 HNO/HRP process. The final, written outputs will include a final report, plus a set of fact sheets for each sector and targeted mantika.
Rapid Needs Assessments: REACH proposes to conduct rapid assessments at the onset of humanitarian emergencies in Libya, to inform effective and rapid humanitarian action. This activity will directly benefit the humanitarian community, including the inter-sector coordination group (ISCG), UN agencies, and international and national NGOs, and ultimately vulnerable communities in need of assistance in Libya. In coordination with humanitarian coordination structures such as the ISCG, REACH will activate its network of Key Informants to collect information and release findings in the form of a report and (if relevant) accompanying maps on an accelerated timeline after the sudden crisis. These findings will provide humanitarian rapid responders with a timely and up-to-date situation overview, as well as information on priority needs, allowing humanitarian actors to implement an informed response.

Technical Support to sector-led assessments: REACH proposes to provide technical support and capacity building to the humanitarian sectors active in Libya, as they plan, design and implement their own assessments in 2020. The objectives of this support will be to help ensure that the assessments that REACH supports produce high-quality, relevant and actionable outputs, as well as to strengthen the sectors’ long-term capacity to collect, analyze and share data. The ultimate goal in providing such support will be to assist the humanitarian sector in producing a more diverse pool of up-to-date data and analyses to draw upon when planning and implementing activities in Libya. This technical support is not meant to replace the sectors’ important leadership role in conducting these assessments, but rather to enhance it by providing sectors with a valuable resource that will help them achieve their assessment objectives.

1 - Needs

Crucial humanitarian information gaps remain in Libya: the political, economic and social landscapes are constantly evolving. To ensure that programming quickly responds to the rapidly changing needs and environment on the ground, it is thus crucial that timely and up-to-date information is maintained and reinforced. REACH proposes to conduct assessments (MSNA, rapid assessments, and support to sector-led assessments) for an informed and coordinated programming of the humanitarian response. Multi-Sector Needs Assessment: Data available on the humanitarian situation in Libya beyond the REACH MSNA often relies on key informant rather than HH-level surveys, and frequently does not have the comprehensive geographic and thematic coverage necessary for making decisions about the allocation of scarce resources. The MSNA intends to complement these and other sources of information such as the IOM DTM. The proposed 2020 MSNA intends to provide an overall understanding of HH vulnerabilities, their most pressing needs and their severity, both within each sector and from a cross-sectoral perspective. Rapid Needs Assessments: In the Libyan context, there have been periodic outbreaks of armed conflict which have spurred population movement, a pattern which is anticipated to continue into 2020 thanks to 2019’s escalation of the conflict in Tripoli. This conflict is now spreading to Sirte and other areas between LNA and GNA forces. To enable an informed and rapid response, information on the situation needs to be collected in a timely matter. Technical support to sector-led assessments: Currently, many of the humanitarian sectors in Libya rely heavily on data from the annual MSNA. However, the MSNA is not intended to provide an exhaustive analysis of all dynamics and vulnerabilities for every sector. Rather, the MSNA should serve as one of a range of information sources that sectors rely on when deciding where, how and for whom to target their resources. To help make this a reality, REACH proposes in 2020 to begin providing technical support to sector-led assessments. This would include support in research design, analysis and final output production, as well as capacity building. REACH is uniquely positioned to assist the sectors in this regard, and to help the sectors achieve quality assessment outputs that strengthen evidence-based decision-making in the Libyan humanitarian response.

2 - Activities

Multi-Sector Needs Assessment: The main component of the MSNA will be a quantitative data collection exercise (i.e., a HH survey), followed by a smaller qualitative data collection exercise that will triangulate and build on the survey’s findings. REACH will consult with the sector, the IMWG, the ISCG and the HCT around the details of the 2020 MSNA methodology, including geographic coverage and the selection of indicators. To enable access to hard-to-reach areas, REACH will collect data for the MSNA and Rapid Assessments through a combination of trained enumerators (gender-balanced as far as possible) and local data collection partners (typically local CSOs). Data collection for the household survey should last approximately two months, and the qualitative data collection exercise should last about 2-3-weeks. Once the MSNA data is collected, it will go through several rounds of analysis and presentations of results, including to the sectors, the ISCG and the HCT, in time for the data to feed into the HNO/HRP process. Rapid Needs Assessments: Upon the onset of a humanitarian emergency in Libya, and in coordination with humanitarian coordination structures such as the ISCG, REACH will activate its network of Key Informants to collect information and release findings in the form of a report and (if relevant) accompanying maps on an accelerated timeline after the sudden crisis. Technical Support to Sector-led Assessments: REACH will have one dedicated Assessment Officer who will work closely with the sectors. Upon request from the sectors, this Assessment Officer will help sectors plan, design, and implement assessments. This will include support in assessment data analysis and final output production. If needed and requested, the Assessment Officer may also provide tailored, complementary support to build the sectors’ capacity around assessments and data analysis.

4 - Protection risk analysis

As REACH is not a direct service provider, the primary risks associated with these activities relate to the ethical practice of research, and the protection of respondent anonymity. REACH relies in all its data collection activities on Libyan respondents, and the data collected may include sensitive information such as household income and medical conditions. If this data were not kept anonymous, it could lead to negative repercussions for the respondents. Additionally, enumerators must often travel from location to location to collect data, and given the evolving security situation in Libya, they may need to manage safety and security risks connected to this travel.

5 - Mitigation measures

REACH-led data collection is undertaken within the framework of a do-no-harm approach, and in accordance with REACH policy on the collection, storage and dissemination of personally identifiable information. All enumerators receive training on REACH policy and procedures around data collection. Also, any information that could potentially violate respondent anonymity is removed from datasets prior to publication. Respondent contact information is not shared with any other actors unless there is a specific, pre-planned purpose and the respondent household has given informed consent – and even in such cases, only the absolute minimum data is shared. Regarding enumerator safety, enumerators receive training on both their and REACH's responsibilities around ensuring safety and managing travel. Throughout all data collection exercises, REACH remains in daily contact with enumerators and local partners. In the event of any security incident or potential security risk, REACH closely consults with the Tunis-based ACTED Security Manager, as well as the affected local partner and other local sources, to determine how to react, and whether it is safe to continue. In particularly volatile areas, REACH may periodically suspend data collection in reaction to upticks in fighting.

6.1 - Other accountability mechanisms

Libyans who serve as respondents in REACH-led data collection activities may provide feedback on their experience through the ACTED-managed Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM). REACH successfully rolled out the use of the CRM with the 2019 MSNAs, providing CRM cards for every respondent household and individual. REACH will continue and build on the integration of the CRM into data collection activities in 2019.
