Project Summary

As the Rohingya refugee crisis enters its sixth year, Bangladesh finds itself grappling with a critical situation, facing adverse impacts on the promotion of child rights and the fulfillment of basic needs. Hosting a staggering 965,467 Rohingya individuals, including 501,143 children below 18 years old, who fled from Myanmar to settle in Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh bears the weight of a substantial population, straining humanitarian services amid a global funding crisis. This overwhelming demographic challenge has led to a decrease in funding for humanitarian operations, exacerbating the vulnerabilities of communities, especially children, adolescents, women, and persons with disabilities. The repercussions manifest in various forms of violence, neglect, and psychological issues. Discrimination, violence, sudden natural calamities, including cyclones and fire incidents, amplify the potential crisis, make the community especially children and adolescents more vulnerable. Caritas Bangladesh is set to address these pressing issues through the implementation of a project in Rohingya camps 1E, 1W, and 5, focusing on promoting child rights and ensuring child protection. The project has four primary goals. Firstly, the project aims to strengthen community-based child protection activities by fostering ownership among community members through capacity-building initiatives. This involves the formation of Community-Based Child Protection Committees, engagement of paid child protection volunteers, and the establishment of adolescent and youth forums with equal gender and diversified group participation. This group will work for structured and sustainable child protection activities. They will identify the child protection risk and concerns and take appropriate measures after receiving the capacity building training. The second goal focuses on preventing child protection risks and promoting early childhood development environments. Eleven multipurpose child and adolescent centers will be utilized to provide child protection and prevention messages. The project ensures the active participation of diverse children, including those with disabilities, and provides non-food items such as dignity kits to adolescent girls. Also, positive parenting session will be conduct with the parents and caregivers. Awareness messages will be disseminated through puppet shows and interactive popular theatre, addressing the identified gap in recreational activities for children. The third goal involves that to bolster child protection, Caritas Bangladesh will undertake advocacy efforts with relevant forums, sectors, agencies, and personnel, strengthening the system for child protection. The fourth goal involves responding to services with sustainable approaches, particularly in alleviating child protection concerns through case management. Trained child case managers will employ the Child Protection Case Management System, incorporating mental health services and positive parenting sessions to address complex cognitive issues. The project also emphasizes capacity building of children & adolescent in life skills, including problem-solving, decision-making, peer communication, and social cohesion. Sustainable activities, such as vocational training in tailoring, will be provided to empower children as key contributors to promoting child rights. These comprehensive efforts aim to reduce inherent risks and lay the groundwork for a more secure and empowered future for Rohingya children.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Caritas Bangladesh is committed to enhancing community-based child protection activities, placing a significant emphasis on fostering ownership through capacity building. Within the fixed six camps, participants from four camps reported a 73.4% increase in knowledge regarding child protection issues following awareness sessions [Caritas Emergency Appeal interim report, 2023]. However, there are identified gaps at the practice level, indicating the need for further intervention. To address child protection risks, the project targets early childhood development (ECD) and essence of learning (EoL) across all six camps. Thirteen centers will disseminate essential messages, addressing issues such as neglect (35.91%), psychological distress (11.73%), physical and emotional abuse (7.95%), and child marriage (4.13%) [J-MSNA, 2023]. Utilizing puppet shows and interactive theatre, the initiative aims to bridge gaps in recreational activities for children. Caritas Bangladesh recognizes the imperative role of advocacy in creating a protective environment. Recent statistics reveal a 12% increase in trafficking among adolescent boys compared to 2022, leading to violence and emotional abuse. In response, Caritas will focus on influencing practices and collaborating with relevant stakeholders to ensure a safer environment. In responding to child protection concerns, the project incorporates case management. Trained child case managers utilize the Child Protection Case Management System, addressing cognitive complexities. Mental health services and positive parenting sessions are integral components, providing comprehensive support to vulnerable children. The project prioritizes life skills development, including problem-solving, decision-making, peer communication, and social cohesion. Sustainable activities like vocational training in tailoring aim to empower children as essential contributors to their communities, considering that 31.9% of children left shelters for livelihood opportunities. Highlighting the active involvement of 2,160 diverse children, including those with disabilities, the project ensures inclusivity in child protection activities. This active engagement fosters a sense of belonging and support, particularly crucial for children with disabilities who face fewer opportunities for focused case management support, making them more vulnerable. Caritas addresses the specific needs of adolescent girls by providing non-food items, including dignity kits and warm cloth etc. Given that 1,467 adolescent girls across the six camps require these kits, this targeted support aims to reduce inherent risks and create a safer environment for this vulnerable population [Caritas Emergency Appeal interim report, 2023]. The multifaceted approach of Caritas Bangladesh reflects a comprehensive strategy to address child protection concerns and create lasting positive impacts within the Rohingya refugee community.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Caritas is deeply committed to addressing the unique and substantial safety and dignity risks faced by Rohingya individuals, particularly those with heightened vulnerabilities such as children, adolescent girls, boys, and persons with disabilities will be engaged by non-discriminated way. The establishment of Multipurpose Child and Adolescent Centers prioritizes safety, strategically located away from dark footpaths, risky slopes, and isolated areas. Moreover, these centers feature separate, clearly labeled latrines for girls and boys, ensuring a secure and dignified environment. A comprehensive approach such as mainstream with WaSH, shelter and education sector that guarantee equitable access to services. Embracing a community-centered methodology, Caritas strives to ensure active participation, empowerment, and the strengthening of local communities. The community-based planning approach actively diminishes risks and hazards while fostering resilience. This proactive approach acknowledges the dynamic nature of the situation, allowing for agile adjustments that enhance the effectiveness and relevance of the project over time. Caritas remains dedicated to mitigating risks, upholding dignity, and ensuring the well-being of Rohingya individuals through a comprehensive and adaptive humanitarian response.

This project places a central emphasis on engaging the community, encompassing children, adolescent girls and boys, men, women, elderly individuals, and children with disabilities, along with other diverse groups. The objective is to provide effective remedial development, protection, and comprehensive services to facilitate the recovery of losses and enhance overall welfare. CBCPC will identify the children with disabilities and their parents and provide child protection related message and based on the needs they will be involved in the case managements and referral session. The implementation of Individual Improvement Plans specifically tailored for children with disabilities aims to promote inclusive services, addressing their unique needs and fostering holistic development. This approach reflects a commitment to ensuring that the project is responsive, inclusive, and impactful across various segments of the community.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project incorporates a comprehensive Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) mechanism, integrating Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) principles, Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM), and regular stakeholder consultations. Stakeholder engagement is prioritized to enhance the overall quality of protection initiatives. AAP is a core responsibility and central to Caritas Bangladesh’s (CB) mandate. Refugees participate in deciding the priorities for activities implemented by CB and lead their own initiatives through community-based mechanisms throughout the operational management cycle. CB conducts regular community consultations in all camps to identify priority issues and obtain feedback in order to adapt its protection programming as needed. CB also operates community feedback and complaints boxes, email service, and a helpline in order to provide information to and elicit feedback and complaints from refugees. CB ensuring that individual complaints are responded to and that communities are provided with collective feedback. A fundamental part of CB’s work on AAP includes its commitment to the Code of Conduct (CoC) and a zero-tolerance policy towards sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) and harassment for its staff.
