1 - Needs

According to Ministry of Education and UNICEF data, the majority of schools attended by IDPs and returnee children suffer from serious deficiencies, which affect the right to adequate standard of education every child should be entitled to. Among those deficiencies, the lack of drinking water, lack of latrines facilities, qualified staff and the poor physical conditions of school facilities. An estimated 50% of the population has no access to latrines and hand washing facilities, particularly at schools and markets. In January and February 2019, NEF conducted a targeted rapid needs assessment in Jebel Marra, focusing on water, sanitation, and hygiene. NEF's assessment identified villages most in need of humanitarian assistance due to the degradation of their hygiene situation, water supply, and sanitation infrastructure. The assessment confirmed that current humanitarian responses are few and fragmented. Field agents conducted interviews with government and non-government stakeholders in each locality and made direct observations of village and basic services. The assessment team triangulated data with Funga Sug Inter-Agency Humanitarian Mission Report, Sector/Cluster, IOM, OCHA, FEWSNET and existing humanitarian assessments. The assessment also revealed that primary schools suffer from serious deficiencies. Among those deficiencies, the lack of drinking water, lack of latrines facilities and the poor physical conditions of school facilities are among the most appalling. Limited funding available to schools has an additional dire impact on access and children have limited school supplies. At the same time, a high rate of children out of school and drop-out rate has been recorded: this is mainly due to the lack of water and sanitation facilities within targeted schools. Additionally, waterborne disease outbreaks have occurred in the past and improved sanitation facilities and knowledge will help mitigate future outbreaks related to poor hygiene and sanitation.

2 - Activities / Outputs

NEF intends to: 1) conduct training programs for school personnel and community leaders on sanitation management, child friendly school concept, gender issues and hygiene promotion; 2) improve access to water and sanitation facilities at schools through the rehabilitation of water points for human consumption construction gender sensitive sanitation Facilities; and 3) promote Gender Balanced Hygiene Practices and Awareness Campaigns in schools so as to improve the hygiene practices and to prevent diseases outbreaks. This supports the Education Sector's strategy to: Ensure schools and learning environments are protective and responsive to the needs of conflict affected children and adolescents. The construction/rehabilitation of latrines at schools will be accompanied with activities that promote positive hygiene practices and minimize health risks. Each latrine block will be equipped with hand washing facilities and a source of water. NEF will provide soaps and bins for safe disposal of toilet paper and hygiene materials. Jointly with Water Safety Committees( WSC) , NEF will implement Children’s Hygiene And Sanitation Training (CHAST)methods– based on PHAST methodology. WSCs will undergo a training to facilitate the implementation of CHAST at schools. Facilitators will organize water and sanitation clubs at schools and educational games (e.g., school posters, puppets, role plays) to raise awareness about (1) personal hygiene practices,(2) the links between personal hygiene and good health, and (3) the importance of using latrine and hand washing with soap. We will work with to promote school latrines and improve sanitation infrastructure through trainings, awareness campaigns. This will be supported by targeted hardware interventions to construct or rehabilitate school latrines. This approach can improve personal safety, dignity, and school attendance, and minimize lost employment opportunities, health risks, and environmental contamination due to a lack of inadequate sanitation and hand washing facilities, particularly for girls. The project will also integrate hands on training on small school-based gardens to provide children with practical nutritional information and diversified food sources. Additionally, tree nurseries will be included as part of the school based health and hygiene clubs. These activities will complement the gardening and tree-planting components of our FSL project and are also components that NEF has long experience with.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The planned intervention will assure that sanitation services that will be provided in the selected schools will be the appropriate distance from the class rooms, kitchen and are equipped technically to ensure do no harm approach to children. Also the project will assure, schools fencing will not be done with trees and parent and teacher associations together with the school population will be given workshops about natural resource management and children will be open to create nurseries at their schools by linking them with the National Forestry Cooperation. NEF identifies, analyzes, monitors and mitigates risks in a participatory and accountable way across multiple categories (i.e. fiduciary, context, delivery, safeguarding, operational, etc.). For each risk, NEF identifies a risk owner, a description, inherent risks in terms of probability and impact, mitigation approach, and residual risks in terms of probability and impact.NEF facilitates a participatory and collaborative approach that engages local community, local organizational stakeholders as well as line ministries in risk management.NEF’s participatory approach is employed based on the assumption that project components necessitates ownership and leadership by local communities needed in identifying and collaboratively working toward addressing project risks. As per NEF’s experience, the participatory approach supports local communities to develop actions to mitigate against identified risks and controls that can be put in place or exercised to manage risks.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

NEF guiding principles: NEF mainstreams protection through training and enforcement of its Safeguarding Policy and Code of Conduct. Through these principles, and its adherence to Protection Principles as per the Humanitarian Charter and Minimum Standards of Child Protection in Humanitarian Action (CPMS), NEF resolutely ensures the protection of children and at-risk adults. NEF further ensures the safety and dignity of all beneficiaries through its strict adherence to the principles of Do No Harm (DNH). NEF actively looks for ways to minimize any threats and vulnerabilities of every group of the affected population. Protection is mainstreamed throughout project design and implementation by NEF’s promotion of meaningful, safe, and dignified access. Contractors and project partners need also to adhere to NEF’s guiding principles. NEF applies DNH and Sphere principles to its selection of participants, guaranteeing fair representation, regardless of their ethnicity, religion, or gender. NEF further reflects Sphere’s belief that all individuals have a right to assistance in our commitment to working with marginalized and vulnerable populations, including female head-of-households, survivors of sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV), youth, older people, and persons with disabilities. These considerations guide all of NEF to prevent exposing vulnerable and marginalized people to further harm. Consultation, feedback, and response. Through confidential and secure feedback mechanisms, NEF ensures that all beneficiaries are able to raise concerns and complaints. NEF responds to beneficiary feedback by adopting the DNH principles of constantly learning and improving. In addition to responding to and reducing risk, NEF assists affected populations to claim their rights and access appropriate remedies. As appropriate, NEF will establish community mechanisms (example, WASH committees) that are diverse and representative of the community. Women and girls are at disproportionate risk of GBV and SGBV. NEF strives to mitigate these risks through consultation in project design and implementation. NEF has a formal gender policy as well as a dedicated gender officer and it seeks to mainstream the participation of women in all activities. Throughout the project, NEF will give special attention to gender-based constraints and opportunities to ensure that activities and decision-making processes are accessible to women and girls and that they promote appropriate access to and safe use of NEF services.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

NEF projects are designed, implemented and monitored with the participation of the beneficiary communities, government line ministries, HAC, donors and local partners. NEF ensures that affected populations are engaged at all levels of the project cycle. To help operationalize its commitment to actively seek, listen to, consider, engage, protect and act on feedback from the communities and individuals that are the direct recipients of assistance, NEF maintains an organization-wide Accountability to Affected Populations Framework linked to its organizational policies and practices and is built upon the 5 Commitments to Accountability to Affected Populations. NEF expands upon this framework to include principles of protection, do-no-harm, appropriateness, and coordination. Leadership & Governance. NEF works in partnership with local NGOs and CBOs throughout the lifecycle of our projects. Where there is need and demand, NEF devotes resources to capacity building of local entities, aligning projects with relevant policies and priorities. All project staff, regardless of organizational affiliation, will work together as a single team. As the lead, NEF takes full responsibility for project management and implementation, for coordination among partners at an organizational level, for delivering results and for reporting. Transparency. Outreach events include information about the organizational stakeholders, criteria and processes for beneficiary recruitment, participation, and access to project benefits, project approach and objectives, and project sponsors, all in local languages, displayed in public, accessible locations. Community-based project association meetings and trainings will use culturally and linguistically appropriate communications. Feedback and Complaints. Beneficiary feedback mechanisms will be incorporated into program implementation, particularly through monitoring, evaluation and learning (MEL) processes. NEF will transparently communicate the purpose of all MEL activities with beneficiaries, and seek their feedback. NEF will consult communities to establish an appropriate feedback and response mechanism. All methods of feedback collection will involve sensitization of staff, partners, volunteers, and beneficiaries on the purpose of methods used and will communicate clear roles, responsibilities, and processes. Feedback will be collected legally, ethically, and with due regard to the welfare of those affected by the results. In addition, the feedback system will uphold confidentiality and non-retaliation principles as per NEF’s Safeguarding Policy and Code of Conduct. Participation. To verify needs, gauge acceptance, and ensure the target population understands the project, community consultation and orientation meetings will be held at the beginning of the project. These meetings will raise awareness of the communities’ participation roles and responsibilities and of the program partners’ responsibilities toward them.
