Project Summary

Contribute to improve the food security status and reduce the negative coping mechanisms of food insecure people in Northwest Syria through the distribution of
1) Monthly Cash for Food ($60 per household as per the Cash Working Group’s current recommendation, regardless of household size; subject to change) for vulnerable displaced persons and host community members in need;
2) Monthly food baskets for vulnerable displaced persons and host community members in need. One food basket (25kg) per household per month regardless of household size, based on the monthly needs of a family of five
3) One-off short- term food assistance through cash ($100 per household as per the Cash Working Group’s current recommendation, regardless of household size; subject to change) for newly displaced households and / or crisis affected vulnerable people as part of a rapid response mechanism;
4) Ready-To-Eat-Rations (RTERs) assistance, one-off, for newly displaced or crisis-affected households as part of a rapid response mechanism. Two RTERs (14kg each) per household for one month regardless of household size, based on the monthly needs of a family of five.
For beneficiaries living in camps, blanket selection will be applied. Before implementing CVA, IOM will conduct a market functionality assessment to ensure local suppliers are able to absorb the extra demand for goods and services. IOM will also monitor local prices to mitigate risks of inflation as a result of the injection of cash in the community.

4 - Needs

The economic situation in NWS is dire, with 99 per cent of the population of several districts in Idleb and Northern Aleppo Governorates in extreme poverty (defined as living on less than 1.90 USD per person per day) and 3.1 million individuals being food insecure (OCHA NWS Factsheet, Oct 2021). The top priorities for newly displaced households in NWS as of November 2021 were livelihoods (for 63% of households), basic services (59%), and food (56%) (HNAP NSAG and TBAF MNM, Oct 2021). Currently, 3.6 million out of 4.4 million people in northwest Syria (82%) are in need of food assistance (OCHA NWS SitRep, Sept 2021). Alarmingly, this includes everyone currently residing in IDP camps. Food insecure households are also highly likely to adopt food-related coping strategies, including reducing the number of meals and the size of portions. Additionally, the ever-increasing prices of food and other necessities further compound the vulnerability of households. The Northwest Syria Cash Working Group (CWG) has reported that the price of the food component of the Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (SMEB) had increased by over 18 per cent over six months up to December 2021, when the SMEB cost reached its highest level in over a year (133 USD) (NWS CWG market monitoring, Dec 2021).

5 - Implementation of activities

IOM FS activities will be as follows:• Selection of implementing partner(s).• Selection of locations based on the updated needs and gaps in food severity ranking and the FSL Cluster’s gaps analysis.• Registration of beneficiaries by IPs. Verification of 20% of beneficiaries by IOM M&E through commissioned TPM. • Initial assessment of FCS and CSI scores.• Food baskets and RTERs: Selection of food vendor via competitive tendering, production of food baskets, laboratory testing of food samples at source to ensure safety and quality and storage of food baskets in vendor warehouse if required. Transportation of food baskets into Syria, Storage of food baskets in IP warehouse selected in coordination with IOM.• Conduct sensitization sessions with site managers, IDP committees and IDPs.• Distribution of food assistance under the observation of IOM’s TPM.• Cash: contract a financial service provider (FSP) in Syria approved by IOM Compliance.• Distribution of cash to beneficiaries from the FSP’s funds; IP to reimburse the FSP within three days of the distribution.• Conduct distribution monitoring by IOM TPM in 100% of distribution sites as well as exit interviews.• Conduct satisfaction survey (PDM) at the end of the project to measure the change in FCS and RCSI.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks: 1. Beneficiaries can be exposed to violence, theft, or other kinds of abuse at or after leaving the distribution sites. 2. Cash can be used for anti-social purposes such as the purchase of arms or alcohol. 3. Corrupt diversion or seizure of cash by armed groups may expose beneficiaries to security risks.4. Pressure from the local authorities on who should or should not receive the assistance. 5. The selection of beneficiaries can lead to tensions among the communities.6. Risk of Covid-19 spreading at distribution sites, or of distributions being cancelled due to Covid-19. Mitigation measures: 1. Set the point of delivery close to a beneficiary’s home or provide transport to ensure safety.2. Financial service providers are vetted prior to each project and never have access to the beneficiary list so as to mitigate any interference from local actors.3. Thorough needs assessments and targeting ensure that the beneficiaries are likely to use the cash assistance primarily to cover their food needs.4. Raise awareness with implementing partners about the importance of principled humanitarian assistance, and reporting any incidents of interference.5. Explain the selection and vulnerability criteria clearly to the communities via the IPs and IOM AAP.6. Enforce all required hygiene measures including social distancing, sanitation, PPE, spread distributions over a longer period with fewer beneficiaries per day, or distribute door-to-door.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risks:- Abuse or exploitation of beneficiaries by IP staff during beneficiary selection and distribution processes.- Lack of staff awareness and code of conduct on PSEA.- Lack of availability of information on reporting mechanisms for beneficiaries.- Lack of female IP staff in the field. Mitigation/prevention measures:- Code of conduct includes PSEA language.- All IP staff are trained on PSEA.- Internal SEA reporting mechanism for staff and investigative capacity exist and are known within the organization. - PSEA awareness-raising activities and materials for beneficiaries and local communities are conducted and shared during the activities and reporting mechanisms for beneficiaries are available. - All field activities are supervised by humanitarian staff, including female staff members to mitigate PSEA and other protection risks.- Beneficiaries regularly receive information regarding the free and unconditional nature of humanitarian aid and know about and have access to complaint mechanisms and hotlines.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:1. Security situation in Aleppo and Idleb allows the implementation of activities.2. Local authorities cooperate with IOM.3. UNSC will continue to permit cross-border humanitarian access.4. Storage conditions for food commodities in Syria remain satisfactory. Risks:1. Security situation in Aleppo and/or in Idleb doesn’t allow for viable IOM response activities to be undertaken.2. Local actors influence IOM response activities or beneficiary selection.3. Food commodities in storage are exposed to weather conditions.4. C19 prevention measures are not followed by IP staff. Mitigation measures:1. Establish a security and accessibility plan for targeted areas and a process that allows to consult communities on how to proceed with proposed response in a deteriorating security environment and develop a ‘duty of care’ strategy for local partners.2. Implementing Partners ensure full coordination with and prior approval from local councils. 3. Wider and transparent call for beneficiary registration by IPs. Conduct 20% beneficiary verification by IOM Third-Party Monitors. Beneficiary selection based on cluster-recommended beneficiary scoring system.4. Complete the distribution of commodities within 10 days from off-loading at warehouse, conduct warehouse verification pre-off-loading and during the distribution of commodities.5. Close coordination with field team to ensure all C19 preventive measures are taken.
