Project Summary

The intervention will target HHs, primary schools and rural clinics in unserved communities in Area C to contribute at the improvement of the water quality consumed and hygiene practices. Water filters at the point-of-use and water-saving hand-washing stations will be installed, and good hygiene/sanitation promoted in institutions and HHs, especially those practicing open defecation. Furthermore, the project aims to complement the interventions that other organizations foresees to implement. Capacity building of local authorities and institutions will be built, in order to be more effective and coordinated. Data will be analyzed and shared in a systematic way, with the development of scientific papers and evidence-based advocacy material

1 - Needs

Access to adequate sanitation and safe household / personal hygiene is a concern in communities facing water shortages in the West Bank. Despite the foreseen realization of the “Technical Master Plan” in Area C elaborated by PWA, AAH and GVC in coordination with UNICEF, 76 communities in Area C or some parts of them will still remain unserved and not connected to a piped water network. In addition, the number of communities awaiting to be connected to the network has been reducing, but still not negligible. For their water supply, the HHs in these communities mainly relies on rainwater harvesting (runoff) or/and trucked water from filling. A systematic water quality surveillance Area C communities in Area C in April 2018 - September 2019 and proved that – regardless water source and quality at the point of delivery – water for human consumption is very often contaminated when consumed in a HH. Indeed, it is affected by cross contamination during collection and storage (presence of TC in 99% and EC in 50% of samples), likely related to both equipment/assets used and their cleanness. Additional contamination is present also inside the HH before consumption when a household treatment takes place (presence of TC in 89% and EC in 25% of samples). The latter is due to poor hygiene practices, and exacerbated by inadequate hand-washing, inappropriate cleaning and storage of cups and dishes, and poor attention to protect water quality. The study excluded that water treatment was not effective (treatment effectiveness always assessed and only few cases of residual contamination registered). Consumption of biologically contaminated water may have relevant health impacts, especially on vulnerable groups. Children under the age of 5 are particularly vulnerable to waterborne diseases, as well as pregnant women and breast-feeding women who could expose fetus to impair growth or contribute to stunting. Approximately 75% of families in the area of intervention (estimate based on data collected in sample communities) have children under the aged of 5. Various primary schools and clinics in Area C are unconnected and have access water that does not meet safe drinking standards, and lack of proper hand washing facilities or hygiene education increasing their risk to public health diseases. These facilities are relevant not only due to consumption of potentially contaminated water by vulnerable groups, but also as meeting points and strategic places to promote awareness and better practices. In September-October 2018, January and August 2019, 575 households were assessed in Area C of Hebron governorate. The assessment confirmed that Open Defecation was a common practice. Indeed, 14.2% of people had no latrines at all, while 64.1% very basic latrines, often not properly used. 69.3% of the people reported not to have possibility to handwashing in the toilet, confirming a hygienic risk for public health. It is assumed that the practice of Open Defecation is common for 10% of beneficiaries, also confirmed by Oxfam Area C assessment– entailing approximately 340 individuals/45 HHs, who do not have/will not have connection to the piped water network and are located in isolated rural areas, an assumption confirmed by WASH Cluster partners active in this location. Finally, currently water treatment experiences at the HH are quite limited and not well documented, due to some failures in the past and a certain skepticism related to O&M and acceptability. Data in this regard are few, methods for analysis and information management in use different and inconsistent, and sharing limited.

2 - Activities

Activities are designed and will be undertaken in coordination with WASH cluster members and relevant strategic documents. Output 1 Water quality for human consumption and hygienic/sanitation conditions are improved at the HH level A1.1 Baseline assessment that will evaluate access to water, sanitation and hygiene practices in HHs in unconnected communities in Area C, to identify those at risk of open defecation (OD) and poor hygiene. A1.2 Installation of HWTS and training in 550 HHs Household Water Treatment and Safe Storage (HWTS) unit with Gravity Driven Membrane (GDM) technology minimizes risk of cross contamination before consumption. GDM filtration is a physical and biological treatment, needs very limited operation and no consumables nor chemicals. It has successfully been tested for years in Africa and introduced in Area C in 2018 with very good results. A1.3 Collection and analysis of water samples in HHs covered Water samples will be regularly collected from raw water, treated water and at the point-of-use (at the cup), and analyzed to assess water quality (biological and physical parameters). A1.4 Promotion of hygiene and sanitation practices with kit distribution, especially for HHs practicing OD. Promotional activities with behavior change approach (RANAS) will be carried out in a culturally appropriate manner to systematically analyze and tackle OD and practices contaminating water. Specific sessions – with hygienic kits distribution – will target mothers and children (5 18Y). A1.5 Construction of Latrine 40 latrines will be built in the most vulnerable HHs in the targeted communities. The action aims to reduce the practice of OD, in combination with the Activity A1.4. The construction of the latrines will be implanted through a beneficiary-driven construction modality, implemented through cash for work. Based on experience from Cesvi, in line with the WASH Cluster approach, the final design will be defined using participatory methodology. CfW will increase ownership. Output 2 Water quality for human consumption and hygiene are improved at the institutional level A2.1 Baseline assessment Primary schools and clinics in the area of intervention will be assessed, focusing on availability of water treatment and appropriate Hand-Washing Stations (HWS) A2.2 Installation of HWTS and training in 10 schools/clinics HWTS units for institutions – designed according to the number of users – will be installed in identified locations. Personnel will be trained for basic O&M. A2.3 Installation of HWS with water consumption minimized and water treatment and recirculation will be installed, training and hygiene promotion in 10 schools/clinics. Users–both children and patients–will receive dedicated hygiene promotion sessions, tailored on various user groups. Output 3 Capacity of Authorities and Institutions in water quality treatment and surveillance, and data management and analysis are enhanced A3.1 Capacity building about methods for water quality analysis, and design of a surveillance system and field visits will be organized. 3 workshops about theoretical and strategic aspects of water quality surveillance for rural and isolated communities will be organized at 3 different levels and in coordination with main stakeholders. A3.2 Capacity building and experience sharing in the WASH cluster about water treatment 1 workshop to share relevant findings–national and international level–of water treatment technologies for unconnected and unserved communities will be organized. A3.3 Open Data sharing for evidence-based advocacy using innovative data management Data in open access with data disaggregation will be published, allowing evidence-based advocacy by WASH cluster members. 1 workshop about open data with the applicability for WASH in oPt Data are collected with a methodology developed with international experts, checked and triangulated. An independent data verification process will ensure the reliability of the findings.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The intervention mainstreams protection following indications provided by the Protection Cluster and relevant guidelines and tools (e.g. Protection Mainstreaming toolkit, Gender & WASH Toolkit). Both activities and implementation modalities are designed in order to prioritize safety and dignity, and avoid causing harm. Possible risks - security, safety, physical, and psychosocial - were considered, including unintended negative effects as much as possible. Accountability is ensured by a dedicated tool - project complaint and feedback mechanism (CFM) - specifically tailored on the project as per Cesvi procedure. It will also support - along with other specific activities (e.g. workshops, regular monitoring with questionnaire) - participation of beneficiaries and their empowerment. The action improves the resilience of the communities, due to an improved access to WASH services, and a better coping capacity. In particular, water filters are not at risk of confiscation, thus can improve the quality of water consumed even in the case of forcible transfer. They do not need any electricity, consumable nor chemical, thus they will work with no additional cost for the household. Cesvi commits to:- Prioritize safety & dignity, and avoid causing harm: Prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of the intervention, which can increase people’s vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. For instance Cesvi will ensure that the location of EH facilities and routes to them are away from actual or potential threats such as violence; especially the risk or threat of gender based violence (GBV); use discussion groups and observation to detect/discuss this and ask the community to come up with solutions; Latrines and HWTS design will preserve the safety and dignity of its users facilities design must preserve the safety and dignity of its users- Accountability: Set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints. Cases of violations should be referred promptly and in accordance with standard operating procedures established in the area. Eventually, Cesvi staff will be trained on protection procedures for referral of eviction and demolition orders. Based on the safeguarding policy Cesvi commits to do all the needful for preventing programmes having any negative effect on children. Cesvi takes all the complaints and concerns about violence against children seriously and it initiates rigorous investigation of complaints indicating a possible violation of this Policy as well as of Cesvi Code of Conduct and of Cesvi Ethical Code. The safeguarding policy complements, without replacing: Cesvi Ethical Code, Cesvi Staff Code of Conduct, Cesvi PSEA Policy, Cesvi Policy on the Use of Images of Children and Young People and Human Resources Policy.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The proposal is based on the need assessment and context analysis carried out by Cesvi or other stakholders. The action proposed met the recognize needs alone and provide an appropriate answer in proportion to need. Needs were identified household per household before the submission of the proposal, but will be confirmed during the implementation, in order to crosscheck again conditions of beneficiaries, and confirm the selection based on vulnerabilities. Filters, latrines and handwashing will be adapted and introduced in order to guarantee a meaningful access for all the beneficiaries, removing any barrier.The above mentioned project complaint and feedback mechanism (CFM) will support the project staff to adapt the response to the emerging needs or to correct the ongoing actions. Furthermore, it will be provided information, in accessible formats and languages, about entitlements and the complaint-feedback mechanism in place. Cesvi commits to: - Meaningful Access: Arrange for people’s access to assistance and services – in proportion to need and without any barriers (e.g. discrimination). Cesvi will pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services, for instance the installation of ramps and railings to EH facilities- Participation and empowerment: Support the development of self protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights, including – not exclusively – the rights to shelter, food, water and sanitation, health, and education.
