Project Summary

Cyclical climate shocks, including drought and flooding, which is challenging traditional rural livelihoods, as well as ongoing conflict, together contribute to growing internal displacement in Somalia, with an estimated 2.6 million women, men, girls and boys currently displaced. Between January and August 2020, conflict, flooding and lack of livelihoods contributed to the displacement of 893,000 people across the country. IDPs comprise 30% of Somalis in IPC 3 and 4 requiring lifesaving assistance in 2021. Poverty rates among the urban poor is 64% with an additional 10% of the population at high risk of falling into poverty with any sort of climate, economic or health related shock. Somalia is currently grappling with multiple shocks that exacerbate existing acute food insecurity and acute malnutrition. Desert Locust affected rural livelihoods in Northern and Central regions and presents a risk (serious) to the South. The 2020 Gu seasonal flooding (river flooding) contributed to loss and damage to property, housing, farmland and infrastructure. The riverine areas of Hiran, Middle Shabelle, Lower Shabelle, Gedo, Middle Juba and Lower Juba were some of the worst-affected areas. The COVID-19 pandemic and associated socio-economic impacts also contributed to the food insecurity situation in Somalia.
Based on the cumulative, combined impacts of multiple shocks, FSNAU and FEWS NET projected in May 2020 that around 3.5 million Somalis would be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) through at least September 2020. An additional 3 million Somalis were anticipated to be Stressed (IPC Phase 2) and engaging in negative coping strategies to meet their food and nonfood needs.
The project is aimed at supporting the most vulnerable persons in Banadir particularly in the Mogadishu and Afgooye with food interventions. The project will improve the lives of the IDPs by providing food vouchers to improve the immediate food security, health and nutritional well-being of IDPs and host community, mainly by tackling the immediate food needs and reducing the percentage of people in severe acute food insecurity phases (crisis and worse). Food security interventions in camps is aimed at preventing malnutrition in the population, especially among women, children, older persons without support, persons living with disabilities, female or child headed households and pregnant and lactating women. Priority will be given to households with malnourished children, female headed households (widowed divorcees, separated and without adequate support from extended family members), elderly headed, child headed households and Socially excluded groups (abandoned women, intellectual/mental disability all with no economic active members. The project will use the most appropriate form of food assistance through vouchers depending on the market establishment and accessibility and vendors will be selected from within the selected target areas with close proximity to the beneficiaries to reduce protection risks.
SSWC will conduct market assessment after every voucher distribution and cash transfers to see what are the market prices for the predetermined food ratio for the beneficiaries. This will track the availability of food and the fluctuation of the market prices. SSWC will coordinate the distribution of the food through the qualified vendors who meet the standards to distribute food to the beneficiaries and on the Cash transfer SSWC will use the available e-money transfer services within Somalia. SSWC will buy SIM cards that will be distributed to the beneficiaries to receive the money. There will be complaints and feedback registers at the camps and the distribution centers beneficiaries will call a free toll number that will manned by the project staff. Immediate solutions will be given to the beneficiaries who have complaints regarding the cash transfer and the distribution of food by the vendors.

Project Activities

Distribution of food through voucher to 10,000 (1667HH) beneficiaries (1200 girls, 800boys, 4800women and 3200 men) Distribution of non-food items to 10,000 (1667HH) beneficiaries (1200 girls, 800boys, 4800women and 3200 men) Cash transfers to 4000 (667HH) beneficiaries (480 girls, 320 boys, 1920 women and 1280 men). The project will be kicked off with beneficiary selection and registration. The proposed project will target the most vulnerable households in the selected IDP camps and host community as per SSWC and cluster selection criteria. Priority will be given to households with malnourished children, Female Headed HH (widowed divorcees, separated and without adequate support from extended family members), Elderly Headed HH with no economically active members of the HH looking after young children (parents have died), Child Headed HH and Socially excluded groups (abandoned women, intellectual/mental disability. Vendors will be selected from within the selected target areas. Since 1667 vouchers will be used in redeeming food items from vendors the potential vendors will be selected based on the proximity to the beneficiaries to reduce protection risks. Printing of 1667 IDs and vouchers with features (i.e. watermarks) to make it reduce risk of malpractices by some unregistered persons. Voucher distributions will take place in the target communities, ensuring that the elderly, pregnant women and women with young children all have easy access to the selected distribution points. The food items will then be distributed to the selected 10,000 (1667HH) beneficiaries (1200 girls, 800boys, 4800women and 3200 men) In regards to Cash transfer SSWC will use the available e-money transfer services within Somalia. SSWC will buy SIM cards that will be distributed to the selected beneficiaries to receive the money.

Mitigation Measures

During delivering assistance, exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance is possible and SSWC will prioritize safety & dignity and avoid causing harm. Save Somali Women and Children will ensure the location of and access routes to distribution points and services are safe with enough space for orderly distribution. The distribution centers will be central and the distribution will take place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall.During the project implementation the Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents may be identified . SSWC will ensure that all selected beneficiaries are aware of all the mapped out referral services through its field monitors. Te beneficiaries will also be encouraged to listen to radio programs sponsored by SSWC which highlight GBV issues and inform people on the available referral services in Somalia .The Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms may arise. SSWC will set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Eg. feedback box at distribution site, phone lines , setting up mechanisms for submitting feedback that do not require the beneficiary exposing themselves to project staff. Responding to complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can/need to be put in place and organizing awareness raising sessions so that people know how it works. SSWC will also provide clear information to distribution actors and affected population explaining that beneficiaries do not have to pay or provide services/favours to anyone in exchange for receiving food assistance.

SSWC will involve the community in decision making at all the levels of the project so that the community fees their worth in implementing the project and attaining durable solutions. On the hand SSWC will ensure that local authorities and host communities are informed, consulted and included in decisions on site location and planning in order to reduce tensions between host communities and displaced populations. Ensure that women, men, girls and boys are fully involved in decisions relating to their situation, such as voucher distribution, registration of beneficiaries, training of the beneficiaries. SSWC will further establish appropriate and sustainable mechanisms for meaningful dialogue with different age, gender, diverse groups of beneficiaries. To incorporate the views of persons with specific needs in decision-making processes, designate focal points within camp/site management structures and beneficiaries ‘committees. The distribution of food to the emergency affected population of the this project is itself the beginning of contributing to the durable solution which is to make people resilient to the shocks. Save Somali Women and Children will engage the government and the communities on this project which are all curently working on a durable solution in the IDPs.
