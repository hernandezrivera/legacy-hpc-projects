Project Summary

Under the HRP 2021, UNICEF will endeavor to support the most vulnerable and marginalized particularly, women and children refugee population. UNICEF positions itself to work in deprived communities with the highest level of vulnerability aiming to address the underlying as well as root causes. Based on the comparative advantage of UNICEF’s programming and hands-on experience of 60 years in Sudan, it is evident that a meaningful impact and results only be attained by adopting inter-sectoral approaches.
UNICEF aims to deliver an integrated package for children by adopting a “whole child” approach improving programmatic complementarity under a common result framework thus, realizing the rights of the most vulnerable children in the areas of health, education, WASH and protection. This will allow UNICEF to enhance sustainability within the projects and build the resilience of the affected population and thus, bridging the humanitarian and development. UNICEF in partnership with UNHCR has developed a modus operandi for refugee response both for in-camps and out-of-camp population. UNICEF is ensuring the delivery of an integrated maternal child health and nutrition package along with WASH, education, and child protection in camps. Provision of out-of-camp services has been mainstreamed with the service delivery for host communities. In the spirit of partnership and the new way of working, UNICEF submits an intersectoral proposal – a progressive step in the direction of achieving integration.

1 - Needs

Currently, Sudan hosts around 800,000 South Sudanese refugees (SSRs) who were fled from the major conflicts in South Sudan. They are settling in or out of camps with the local hosting communities in White Nile, East Darfur, South Darfur, North Darfur, South Kordofan, West Kordofan, and Khartoum states. New SSRs are still arriving in Sudan as in 2019, an additional 19,000 arrived at Sudan, mainly to East Darfur State (UNHCR) . The existing and the newly arriving SSRs constitute a heavy load on the existing WASH facilities, which are already overstretched and need expansion, rehabilitation, and operation and maintenance support. They also lack the required personal and environmental hygiene practices. The current armed conflict in Tigray Region in Ethiopia has resulted in the displacement of more than 45,000 people to Kassala and Gedarif states. The number of refugees to the two states is increasing daily. As the areas in which they have arrived are already with inadequate basic water and sanitation facilities, the arrival of the Ethiopian refugees constituted a real emergency in terms of the availability of fop water and sanitation facilities. While interim solutions are currently being supported, major efforts must provide the required WASH services for the Ethiopian refugees and their hosting communities.Gender inequality is considerably existing among the project targeted population, especially about decision-making processes, division of labor among family members, and gender-based violence. Standard Expanded Nutrition Survey (SENS) conducted in White Nile, East Darfur, South, and West Kordofan indicate ‘critical’ rates (>15 percent) of GAM and SAM (>3 percent) among children 6 to 59 months of age. The SENS also indicate high rates of anemia (>40 percent) among children and women (aged 15-49 years), and low antenatal service and measles immunization and Vitamin A supplementation coverage for refugees in all above refugee-hosting states. Other surveys conducted also showed significant gaps in access to primary health care services for refugees' children, including newborn care, immunization, case-management of the common childhood illnesses, and PMTCT. The surveys' findings and recommendations emphasized the need to strengthen the provision of health interventions to refugees living in and out of camps to reduce this unacceptable in-equality. Improved immunization coverage for children (up to 15 years of age) against measles, polio, and other childhood illnesses will remain a priority, support for new-born care, and case-management for childhood illnesses for children under-five years of age. Access to education for refugee and IDP children remains one of the key challenges in Sudan. Of the three million children out-of-school in Sudan, refugee and IDP children are among the most vulnerable. While the International Convention for refugees provides that all refugee status persons are eligible for elementary education from the host countries, only a few refugees are enrolled in school. In the 1,271 public schools currently hosting refugees throughout the country, refugee enrollment does not account for more than five percent of the total enrolment.The current armed conflict in Tigray Region in Ethiopia has resulted in the displacement of more than 45,000 people (50 % are children) to Kassala and AlGadarif states in Sudan, where more than 200 children have already been identified as separated and unaccompanied. The number of refugees to the two states is increasing daily. The displaced children and their families have been left with limited access to basic services while exposing them to violence, exploitation, sexual violence, malnutrition, and diseases. Children escaping conflict have experienced and witnessed violence, fights, deaths of parents, siblings, and relatives, resulting in deep negative psychosocial impact. Also, the children that arrived unaccompanied orseparated from their families are more vulnerable.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Climate change is one of the causes of increased vulnerabilities among Sudan's population to natural disasters, including floods, droughts, and their consequences such as the eruption of conflicts related to competition for resources causing displacements, increase in vector-borne diseases outbreak and others. This project aims to strengthen the health system's capacity in Sudan to make it more resilient to shocks and detect early signs of shocks before they happen to minimize their risks. Through this project, UNICEF will target localities with vulnerable communities related to the impact of climate change. At the community level, the project will contribute to the community’s resilience to and ownership of their own health as part of the mitigation measures.Furthermore, to reduce the impact of waste generated from RUTF and nutrition supplements packaging materials, UNICEF will ensure the appropriate disposal of RUTF packaging through following GoS waste disposal policy. Also, Incinerators and/or waste disposal facilities are generally available at the health center level. Water supply sites will be selected, considering IWRM and climate change adaptation measures for balanced and rationalized water resource utilization. Refugees and host communities’ awareness of IWRM and climate change adaptation will be enhanced. Environment-friendly water supply motorized solar systems, hand pumps, and concrete instead of wooden latrines slabs will be widely adopted.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project will work across sectors to ensure that child protection is mainstreamed inthe education system by training teachers on Psychosocial First Aid andenabling teachers to identify children with psychosocial needs and refer them to servicesto be provided by trained social workers who are part of the social welfare system. theMHPSS will also be mainstreamed within the health units, where health workers will betrained on the provision of PSS to affected children and their families who might besurvivors of GBV land mines and other grave violations. the Child Protection Sub-The sector will work closely with the WASH sector to ensure that WASH services are safelyaccessible to children and adolescents, especially adolescents girls. WASH facilities will be constructed in an obvious location (protection against gender-based violence). Gender segregated and separated institutional toilets with Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) components, lockable doors (privacy and protection). Disabled people-friendly toilets with ramps, handrails, and seats (access and ease of use) Beneficiary's safety and protection are one of UNICEF's key priorities in its all programming. It has zero-tolerance for violence, including sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment. For the last couple of years, UNICEF has been training and sensitizing (both online and classroom pieces) its staff and implementing partners to raise awareness on Prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). UNICEF has put an internal mechanism for detecting, mitigating, and reporting any such violence not only that may be happening within its staff but also with its beneficiaries. Because of its focus on this, UNICEF’s highest level (representative) is held accountable at the organizational level to place this mechanism. Also, UNICEF has started screening its implementing partners for their sensitivity to the matter, including assessing the mechanisms they have put in place to detect, mitigate, manage and report any violence, be it sexual or otherwise, against beneficiaries. The following principles underpin and inform UNICEF’s strategy: -The children, women, men, and communities that UNICEF serves have the right to be treated with dignity and respect and receive assistance without threat of exploitation and abuse; -UNICEF has zero-tolerance for SEA and SH. As such, it has a responsibility to create and maintain an environment that prevents sexual exploitation and abuse and a responsibility to provide timely, confidential, and effective investigation, assistance, and support to survivors; -All UNICEF personnel have the right to work in an environment free from sexual harassment; -All UNICEF personnel are expected to act with tolerance, sensitivity, and respect for diversity. They have an obligation to ensure that they do not engage in, condone, or tolerate behavior that would constitute sexual harassment. The following are its strategic focus on preventing SEA and SH.
