Project Summary

The intervention aims at supporting vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities through the rehabilitation of private shelters or apartments. A total of 850 women, men, boys and girls will be supported with 1) upgrading of unfinished buildings and 2) rehabilitation of damaged houses.

Needs

The situation of shelter in Syria has worsened during 2018. The damage caused by the ongoing conflict has led to millions of homes being destroyed, and millions of people had to find alternative accommodation with host families, in camps or in collective shelters (shelters converted from schools, unfinished buildings, mosques, or other public buildings). Main constraints are in terms of the available shelter capacity, the adequacy of shelter conditions, the ability of people to repair or rehabilitate their own damaged shelter, and also the affordability of rent. Displaced populations and host communities struggle to find safe and sustainable solutions and they are living in inadequate shelters with lack of necessary bathing or cooking facilities, inadequate space, lack of privacy, lack of heating and lack of insulation mentioned as the main reason of distress. According to assessments carried out, in many houses the structural safety is maintained but the level of destruction and looting is so extensive that requires rehabilitation works above the average cost to, for example, rebuilt completely partitions and facilities with the aim of ensuring safe, decent and dignified housing conditions. In general, the needs for safe shelter are urgent across Syria with more than 4.7 million of people in need.Moreover, returnees continue to gather pace, with 1,219,874 million Syrians having now returned to their place of origins, especially in Rural Damascus, Homs, Hama, Aleppo, and Deir ez Zor. Many areas are still largely lacking safe, dignified and sustainable conditions for returnees. Emergency shelter rehabilitation as well as WASH infrastructure repair to areas in reception of influxes of IDPs, as well as those to which people are returning in high numbers, will continue to be a critical life-saving imperative. The majority of people living in damaged shelters cannot afford repairs due to the high cost while the upward trend in rent prices can be expected to further degrade the ability to make safe and adequate repairs.

Implementation of activities

AHH will improve safe and decent housing conditions of vulnerable conflict-affected populations selected based on vulnerability criteria, with priority to most vulnerable families such as women-headed HHs, households with PLWs and/or children under 5, elderly, a member chronically ills and/or with physical/mental disability, hosting IDPs for more than 3 months, and/or with more than 7 members. The two types of shelters targeted for rehabilitation works are private shelters (which are unfinished buildings that needs finishing or upgrading) and damaged apartments ( rehabilitation of damaged houses for returnees/host community). The identification process of shelters differs according to the type of shelter that requires rehabilitation. The privacy and dignity of separate households will be considered during the design and implementation of the works and essential shelter needs will be covered through basic civil repairs. Depending on the type of targeted shelter, a different set of works will be considered, among which internal partitioning for larger spaces; sealing off for unfinished buildings for insulation and protection against the elements; rehabilitation/provision of windows and lockable doors; provision of locks for toilets/bathrooms; carpet/floor covering; rehabilitation of stairs and provision of railings where needed; installation of ramps where possible and needed; repair or installation of electricity cables, connection to electrical networks, provision of lights to households and communal areas, provision of emergency lamps; plastering/other minor structural repairs; internal WASH facilities (latrines, water tanks, showers) and kitchen facilities; insulate external walls and floors; electricity battery supply. The rehabilitation works activities will take into account the unique needs of men, women, children, elderly and disabled persons; the technical assessment and the residential unit design will be completed to ensure safety, privacy and dignity for all targeted beneficiaries.Priority will be given to female headed families, households with large number of children, elderly and disables and in general AAH will ensure that relevant age/sex groups are considered with all data sex disaggregated throughout the project cycle from assessment and implementation to monitoring and evaluation. AAH has capacity and willingness to intervene in different areas, therefore several sub-districts are selected as potential targets. The definitive areas of intervention will be defined on a later stage based on needs assessments, according to which the support will target the most vulnerable.

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk: Physical safety of beneficiaries/communities is at risk due to structural integrity of the building and/or the presence of ERWs in or in close proximity to potential work sites. Likelihood: high Impact: highMitigation: Structural assessment of buildings is done by qualified personnel. AAH engages in repair/rehabilitation of buildings only if the building is found structurally safe. AAH works closely with local authorities, communities and agencies involved in ERW clearance to ensure that the building/area is free from ERW; contractors/workers are aware of risks associated with the presence of ERW and safety protocols are in place. Protection risk: selection criteria is influence by local authorities may contribute to nepotism, tension in the community and risk of most vulnerable being left out and/or abuse of Authority by distribution actors (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion)Likelihood: high Impact: mediumMitigation: In general, assistance package is in line with sector standards and guidelines. AAH engages with community people (both men and women), to support in disseminating information in the communities. AAH creates feedback and complaint mechanism at community level taking into consideration sensitivities in the communities and allowing women to express their complaints in safe manner. AAH staff is trained on conflict mitigation techniques, awareness of relevance of social structures for aid programs, neutral acceptance of AAH programs and red lines when outreaching social structures. The selection process for private shelter or rehabilitation of damaged houses follows sector guidelines ensuring all steps are followed in a transparent and compliant way. When hired, all AAH staff receives and reads the organization’s Code of Conduct. AAH has an already established country commission to identify and act in case a staff is identified as perpetrator of abuse and exploitation and act accordingly. Protection risk: Lack of security of tenure document excludes benefiting from shelter repair/rehabilitation works Likelihood: high Impact: mediumMitigation: AAH communicate to beneficiaries list of alternative documents to prove ownership, and engages with the local authorities to establish a mechanism to deal with such issues at local level.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: The security situation in targeted areas is unstable and AAH has no access to implement the activities; there are additional unforeseen administrative procedures imposed, and restrictions on access to beneficiaries. Likelihood: medium. Impact: highMitigation: AAH works and coordinates with authorities and Ministries to maintain access to target areas and beneficiaries, and the capacity to implement activities. AAH will reach out to local authorities and line ministries with trust building activities to further develop relationships and capacity to avoid or mitigate restrictions in AAH capacity to operate in Northern and Southern Syria. Advocacy and coordination with the humanitarian community and humanitarian diplomacy activities on the local level are also considered to contribute to this mitigation measure. Risk 2: Substantive devaluation of the EURO or USD against the Syrian pound; International bank transfers to Syria are fluid and timely.Likelihood: medium. Impact: highMitigation: The eventuality of significant changes in the value of the Syrian currency will be closely monitored and, if materialized, timely communicated to donors and options discussed. Risk 3: Aid diversion, fraud, and corruptionLikelihood: medium. Impact: highMitigation: diversification of supplier, triangulation of information, standard monitoring procedures in place, security plan and risks assessments tables updated and followed. Risk 4: Information gathering activities hampered by suspicion or misunderstandingLikelihood: low. Impact: medium Mitigation: Transparency, trust-building, developing relationships with municipalities, district authorities and facilities managers
