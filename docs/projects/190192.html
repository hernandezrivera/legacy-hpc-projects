Project Summary

The proposed project will contribute to protection and sustaining livelihood of vulnerable households affected by conflict , drought and other shocks (COVID-19, flood,) in Oromia region ( Bale and Borana) and Afar regions . Livelihoods of 134,000 pastoral, and agro-pastoralist individuals in these regions will be protected, livestock morbidity and mortality in the target areas will be reduced by at least 30%. At least 1,000,000 heads of livestock from two regions will be vaccinated against sporadic and contagious diseases like Contagious Bovine Pleuropneumonia (CBPP), Contagious Caprine Pleuropneumonia (CCPP), Peste des Petits Ruminants (PPR), Lumpy skin disease (LSD), sheep and Goat pox, Bovine and pateurellosis. 34500 heads of affected livestock population from 11500 in the targeted areas will be treated against specific diseases . 6200 HHs will be provided with fodder. 500 HHs in the targeted area will receive animal restocking assistance while 11700 HHs receive multipurpose cash . The capacity of 136 Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) will be strengthened through training and provision of essential equipment to support in the livestock vaccination campaigns and treatment livestock. In addition, 9850 Agro-pastoralist households will be provided with agricultural input (emergency seed and farm tools).
Linkage with nutrition: The project will also improve food and nutrition security status of affected communities by improving the production and productivity of livestock through provision of Fodder and medical services to core breeding livestock that can recently produce milk to the households. The agricultural inputs supply will also improve production and productivity of crops for agro-pastoral households contributing to food security and reduction of malnutrition rate of to the affected communities.
This response focuses on maintaining and restoring basic assets/needs of the targeted households to enable them to cope with the current situation within the shortest possible period of time. The multiple burden and overlap of disasters caused by the Drought, conflict , Covid-19 restrictions and the flood among other challenges heightened the loss of the assets of the households and makes them extremely vulnerable. Therefore, a quick response is the highest priority to sustain livelihoods and to tackle/minimize the negative consequences. The selected activities contribute to protect, maintain and restore assets of the vulnerable households at the 5 target woredas which have lost their scant resources drastically due to the various calamities that forced them into displacement, unplanned sale of animals, and sending children to other places

1. - Projects activities

1.Support government livestock Vaccination campaign through community mobilization and logistics One-time vaccines of PPR, LSD, Bovine, CBPP, CCPP, and sheep and goat pox will be provided during the campaigns. In all target woredas of Afar and Oromia there are 1000000 livestock population (from 150,000 HHs ) and this the project will reach 100% animals in the two selected regions and woredas in ( Afar and Oromia)2. Providing curative livestock treatment for poor HHs in selected disease hot spot kebeles. In all 4 zones zones of the 2 target regions , the following drugs will be distributed to targeted community members :-Oxy TTC 10/20, Ivermectin, Alebandzole 2500 mg, Alebandzole 300 mg, Teteraclozian 3400 mg for sheep for cattle, Teteraclozian. 9000mg, lcomitamidem 1g, Malti Vitamin, Deminzam ,Diazinol. 60%, Wound Spray. To minimize risk of fake deworming drugs, the project will involve the regional veterinary professional during the procurement process.3.Provision of livestock feed (Concentrate Animal feed) to regions highly affected by drought and conflictThe key intervention under this activity is the provision of emergency livestock feed for 18600 animals from 6200 HHs with 2.5 kg of concentrate (1 quintal at $20) for 90 days for 3 core breeding animals per household. Under this activity, type and number of livestock per household is decided based on participatory planning process with targeted community, HRP Agriculture sector standard and local authority aiming at ensuring that the selected livestock reflects the needs of vulnerable groups which are affected differently by a shortage of feed. Likewise, when duration of intervention is set, existing resource size has also been taken in to account. Cows and oxen will receive 2.5kg of supplementary feed concentrates per day. 4.Providing emergency crop seed and farm tools for poor HHs in selected disease hot spot kebeles.This project will provide crop or vegetable for seeds for 9850 poor households. The Emergency seeds are determined by multiplying total targeted people by an average of 0.75 hectares with the unit price of different seeds varieties (price differs per type) and farming tools at a total cost of $20 per HH. e. Providing animal restocking assistance The most vulnerable 500 HHs households will be provided animal restocking assistance; which will be determined based on types of animals recommended during the assessment (price differs per type) for households at risk. Restocking is recommended for 5 shoats at a cost of $98. f. : Providing Multipurpose Cash to 11700 HHs selected households at rate of $40 for three months The households selected for the multipurpose cash support will receive one time cash at rate $40 per household which is equivalent to about 90 percent of the Productive Safety Net Programme [PSNP] wage rate. Households for this activity will be selected based on the probability of selling their livelihood assets due to forced drought related problems. Capacity of woreda government and CAHWs strengthened through provision of veterinary equipment and trainingFacilitating refresher training for 136 CAHWs in total who will be trained on treating affected animals, vaccinating livestock, diagnosing symptoms, etc. During the trainings, social distancing will be ensured by reducing the number of participants and allowing enough ventilation in the meeting hall. In addition, all participants will be provided with face mask and hand washing /sanitizer to ensure low/no transmission of C-19. The costing Capacity building activities CAHWs (venue for training, per diems, materials etc.): $60 per participant. Besides, consumable veterinary equipment such as automatic vaccination syringe for vaccination, treatment syringes, and ice boxes for drug storage, and glass barrels will be provided for the Animal Health Department in 2 regions of the four zones in Afar and Oromia .

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

At federal (Addis Ababa), regional (Oromia), and zonal (Borana and Bale ) and zonal (Awsi and Hari in Afar ) as well as woreda levels, WHH and its partners actively participates in the Agriculture, WASH, COVID-19 task force, and shelter clusters (working groups in zonal/woreda levels), to share updates and lessons learned, and coordinate with other actors. WHH and partners regularly shares the 4W matrix for the Agriculture cluster. WHH regularly coordinates with clusters FAO and OCHA and attends FAO and OCHA meetings at federal level. WHH is a member of the Humanitarian INGOs (HINGO) Forum, which convenes on a monthly basis to discuss strategic issues, among which are coordination of humanitarian responses and promotion of joint partner initiatives. Strong participation of WHH and its partners at regional and federal clusters will ensure no duplication and facilitate coordinated approach in the action. As with all other interventions, WHH will closely coordinate this project with all relevant stakeholders such as - I/NGOs (Ayuda En Accion, CARE, Save the children , World vision, Action Against Hunger , IRC, Ethiopian Red Cross), UN agencies (esp. FAO) currently engaged in emergency and development activities in the targeted area. All agencies are actively participating on zonal level agricultural cluster and has provided updated information on humanitarian situations in its intervention areas. The Foundation will take this opportunity to update clauster member on this humanitarian intervention funded by country humanitarian Fund through our lead applicant WHH. The foundation will also share the experiences it will learn during the implementation of this actions to the cluster members at zonal and regional level. The foundation will also mobilize woreda and kebele levels disaster risk management committee to bring them on board during implementation of this project. The participating communities (beneficiary households) of this intervention will also fully engage in implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the impacts of this intervention

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

WHH and its partner will establish a Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) together with the affected communities. The CRM includes various channels depending on the preferences of the affected communities, at least one of which is anonymous. Possibilities are for example 24/7 hotline, community help desks and suggestion boxes.As a pre-requisite for a functioning CRM WHH and its partners ensure that the affected communities will receive timely and clear information about the overall project and its activities, targeting and process as well as the behavior that they can expect from the project staff (e.g. Code of conduct). Moreover, WHH and its partners repeatedly and clearly explains the purpose of the CRM and how to access or use the system to the affected community, beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries alike. The various channels of the CRM remain available throughout the project. When a complaint is received, the CRM focal person processes the information and refers it according to set standards. Especial care for confidentiality and professional handling of complaints is given in case of sensitive cases, here WHH has a professional system in place at head office level. Depending on the matter of the complaint response is given in a set timeframe and feedback is given to the complainant or the community in total. In general, complaints or feedback are welcome by the WHH and its partners and seen as an opportunity to learn and improve the project and communication with the community, this culture is supported by the leadership and trough capacity development for the project team. The foundation will also establish a compliant handling mechanisim that can easily be accessable to all participants of the project. Compliant hearing committee will be established at kebele levels and receive any complains raised by the participating communities and share it with WGF focal person so that the focal person will take immediate measures to correct the complains or will share the issue with WHH/WGF /CIFA/FSA management team for immediate actions.
