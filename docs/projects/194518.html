Project Summary

The humanitarian nutrition overview for 2022 estimates that 2 million children, pregnant and lactating women (PLW) will require treatment for malnutrition. In the same year, around 1.4 million children under 5 years of age (U5) will need to be provided with nutrition services out of which 302,078 will be admitted for acute malnutrition. 675,578 PLW are expected to be acutely malnourished, which is a significant increment when compared to 2021 needs. The need for humanitarian assistance is increased due to emerging issues related with increased food insecurity, flooding and the continuous impact of the COVID-19 pandemic. The severe flooding in Jonglei State has impacted health and nutrition service provision in Fangak and Canal/Pigi counties. IRNA assessment conducted in August 2021 in the area, indicated that various locations in the counties have been completely submerged in water including nutrition and WASH facilities while the rest remains under the big threat of being flooded. Communities continue to be displaced to relatively dry high lands in Fangak and Cana Pigi counties. In Bentiu IDP, while official counts have shown fluctuation in the population counts over the years, the recent influx of displaced community from surrounding areas due to flooding is likely to continue causing much stress on existing humanitarian services in the IDP camp. Rehabilitation of nutrition and WASH infrastructure is needed to continue providing life-saving services in flood affected counties. A recent SMART survey done at Canal/Pigi and Fangak counties reveals, a GAM rate of 19% for each of the counites. Mass MUAC screenings conducted by WR in New Fangak in February 2020 show a proxy GAM rate of 11.6% and a proxy severe acute malnutrition (SAM) rate of 2% for children U5, and a proxy GAM of 8% for PLW. In Koch County, mass MUAC screenings conducted by WR in 2020 revealed a proxy GAM rate of 10.6% for children U5 and a proxy modern acute malnutrition (MAM) of 11% for PLW. SMART survey done in August 2021 in Bentiu IDP, reveled the prevalence of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) is 2.4%, and GAM prevalence is 9.7. To strengthen life-saving nutrition services, WR will scale up qualitative service and coverage for community-based management of malnutrition (CMAM), extending services hard to reach sites with underserved communities. The project will target 26,064 children U5 and PLW as direct beneficiaries who show signs of SAM and MAM. Care givers of children will be reached through Maternal and Infant Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) programs in Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) and Therapeutic Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP) sites. This project will establish four new outreach units in each county to provide outreach nutrition services to communities with limited access to services due to displacement. In Fangak County, WR plans to support 5 new OTP and TSFP sites in Canal/Pigi County. A gender-targeted and nutrition-sensitive service delivery approach will be used and nutrition will be integrated with WASH, health and FSL programming. The aim of the project is to reduce morbidity and mortality among the most vulnerable children and women while promoting and engaging in recovery and resilience activities in the community. The project purpose is in line with Nutrition Cluster Objective and sectoral strategies. Project activities will be monitored through supportive supervision done together with the County Health and Nutrition Department, MOH and UN Agencies. Activities will be monitored by nutrition project staff with support from the WR Juba M&E department and other program team. Relevant reports will be shared to nutrition cluster and other reporting partners. Community based compliant mechanisms will be established within the nutrition sites and at community level to ensure accountability.

1 - Needs

The need for humanitarian assistance is high due to emerging issues related to increased food insecurity, flooding, inter-communal conflict and the continuous impact of the COVID-19 pandemic. Protracted shocks on top of flooding in 2021 has disrupted livelihoods, availability & access to food which has contributed greatly to food security deterioration at household level and has impacted health and nutrition service provision in Jonglei and Unity states. Nutrition sites in Fangak and Koch counties have been submerged and relocated to higher ground because of flood waters in 2021, IRNA conducted in august 2021 states in Fangak county most locations are devastated by the floods and highlights the very high trends of malnutrition in the counties. 52,827 individuals have been displaced in Phom and Manajang payams in Fangak County where WR is implementing nutrition services .Communities in Fangak and Canal pigi continue to be displaced due to ongoing flooding, lose livestock, damaged crops and other natural resource-based livelihoods. This population is in urgent need of humanitarian assistance. Food is the most requested provision by the community, and it was noted that sanitation and hygiene practices were ‘poor’ in the counties since most WASH facilities have been affected by the flood. Ongoing flooding and intercommunal conflict in Koch have negatively impacted in delivery of nutrition services. In the last Quarter of 2021, three quarter Rubkona town got submerged, an estimated 151,000 population from Rubkona and Guit settled in Bentiu IDPs sites A, B, C, D and E. High magnitude of flooding led to increased prevalence of acute malnutrition among U5 children as per the MSF-H mass MUAC screening report sites A, B and C with a proxy GAM of 19.3%, 24.8% and 16.6% respectively. Recent SMART survey done at Canal Pigi and Fangak counties revels a GAM rate of 19% for each counites. In koch county Mass MUAC screening conducted by WR in 2020 revealed a proxy GAM rates of 10.6% for children U5 and a Proxy MAM of 11% for PLW. In Fangak county 6 out of the 9 nutrition sites where WR is implementing has been submerged in water and been relocated. To improve the quality of nutrition interventions and increase coverage, Nutrition and WASH facilities will need to be rehabilitated to ensure continuity of life saving service delivery. WR is planning to scale up the program with approaches to reach the vulnerable communities affected by the ever-increasing humanitarian crisis through integrated mobile outreach units to flood affected sites. There is a need to strengthen the capacity of staff and empower the community to improve the quality of nutrition service provision. Impact of protracted emergency affected household food security levels in addition to increasing malnutrition rates among children U5. WR will continue to strengthen COVID-19 preventive measures and work on risk communication and community engagement strategies for integration of preventive activities at all levels. Community outreach activities for MIYCN counseling and mother-to-mother support groups will be conducted under the guidance of COVID-19 protocol as per the countries and WHO guideline to effect behavioral change in the community.

2 - Project activities

WR is supporting OTP, TSF, MIYCN and other nutrition sensitive interventions in 8 static nutrition sites in Koch County, 9 static nutrition sites and an integrated mobile outreach in Fangak County and 2 nutrition sites in Bentiu IDP camp. To respond to the increasing demands of vulnerable communities, scale up the Nutrition services, improve quality and coverage in pocket areas with no nutrition service. Objective-1 activities include: Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) counseling, support and outreach activities approach will be used to improve optimal MIYCN practices for caregivers of children aged 0-23 months. Mothers’ Support Groups (MSGs) through Lead Mothers (LM) in Bentiu, Fangak and Cana Pigi counties will be providing training on MIYCN and IYCF-E activities. LM will conduct individual and group counseling within the community involving 10-15 mothers who live nearby to each other. Care Group Volunteers in Koch will be trained regularly and deliver messages for groups of 8-10 mothers every 2-4 weeks. Kitchen gardens, cooking demonstrations, and referral of children and PLWs to FSL programming activities will be part of community outreach activities. Vitamin A and deworming campaigns will be conducted as per MOH guidance. Objective-2 activities include: Project staffs and CNVs will conduct MUAC screening at facility and community level for early detection and treatment of malnourished children under 5 and PLWs. All nutrition sites will strengthen Family MUAC approach through continuous capacity building for mothers. SAM, MAM children U5 and PLWs will be identified using national criteria of CMAM protocols, and WHO reference standards. Admitted children will receive medical investigation, and routine medication based on National treatment protocol. Supplies for the OTP/TSFP program will be received from the Nutrition cluster core pipeline and regular monitoring and early request of supplies will be done. Support stabilization center in Koch PHCC and plan to reintegrate a stabilization center in Phom PHCC of Fangak county. Health, nutrition and WASH integrated outreach units will be established to provide service to flood displaced communities. In Koch county one outreach unit will be supporting Thoriak and kueet payam which is hosting displaced population of Boaw Gauang and Bou. In Bentiu town the outreach unit will provide nutrition service for flood displaced community settling in camp sites in Bentiu town. In Jonglei state two outreach units will reach to Thalier, Kuerkan, Dor, Toungdiok, wuntur and Bielnyang payams in Fangak and support 5 new OTPs /TSFPs in Canal Pigi county. Post flooding rehabilitation will be done for 4 Nutrition sites in Koch and 6 sites in Fangak. Supportive supervision at nutrition sites will be done by project staff, standard and on-job trainings will be given as per National protocol. Objective-3 Activities include: Conduct one mas MUAC screenings in the post-harvest season in each location, and results will be shared with Nutrition Technical Working Group. Facility-based data collection will be strengthened through capacity building of staff. Objective-4 Activities include: under 5 children and PLWs admitted in nutrition programs will receive service on malaria prevention, treatment activities, supplementation of ORS and zinc for cases with diarrhea. They will also be targeted for WASH kits. All staff will be trained on PSEA and a clear referral mechanism will be put in place for mainstreaming child protection and GBV at nutrition sites. Accountability to affected population through raising awareness by providing information to beneficiaries on their rights and entitlement in the nutrition sites by placing a suggestion box. Provide training for staff to understand how to serve best the beneficiaries using the QAAP approach. WR will coordinate with nutrition cluster and MOH for delivery of standard nutrition service in all the counties.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The community level engagement is initiated from the very beginning of the program. WR will hold a launching of programs at county and payam levels to ensure awareness of available services and targeted population. The community engagement will also be consistently followed through by conducting regular meeting with the volunteers and community-based committees as well as focus group discussions with beneficiaries on sites. WR sector staff and area coordinators regularly conduct meetings at the community level and discuss progress, challenges, future plans and opportunities, community concerns, and risks. This feedback is discussed with the management team in the country office and used to adapt the project to meet the changing needs. A variety of mechanisms are used to give beneficiaries a choice in how to provide inputs and express concerns. • First, local staff hired at the community level, are trained on how to receive feedback from participants, serving both as staff and as trusted community members. • Second, program management staff at the field level conduct regular Focus Group Discussions with project participants and relevant community members and leaders. To ensure the comfort of project beneficiaries, various discussion sessions are conducted with segregated groups of individuals with particular sex, age, and minority (e.g. non-dominating clan or sub-tribe) identities. • Third, CNVs/CHPs are able to receive feedback from participants one-on-one, both at the nutrition centers and during home visits in settings where participants may feel safer sharing their comments in the privacy of their own home. This also ensures that elderly, disabled, or other people confined to their homes have the opportunity to submit concerns. • Fourth, beneficiaries are informed of their right to submit complaints during community-level meetings in case they feel more comfortable in a group setting or directly to a WR staff member at a project site.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

World Relief takes Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) seriously at all levels. All staff are trained on PSEA and must sign on to the organization’s established policies and code of conduct. WR is engaged in a continuous capacity building of technical and supportive staffs in the health facilities and nutrition sites to treat women and girls who are survivors of rape and other incidents of sexual or gender-based violence and be very vigilant on proper referral mechanism set in place by cluster . For the community, be aware and be engaged in reporting of any incident of PSEA, WR has put in place a community-based beneficiary feedback mechanism and create platforms that encourage discussion and consultation with community members and community leaders on reporting mechanism in place, on proper referral pathway and on handling complaints on confidentiality terms. The community will be clearly informed that any reported case will be kept confidential and will be dealt by only responsible individuals at WR. The possibility of discrimination by locally hired staff against beneficiaries who do not hold the same tribal or political affiliations is mitigated by ensuring that program teams is a combination of locally hired national staff, national staff hired from outside the programmatic area and international staff. WR will continue to leverage its relationships to advocate for the safety of beneficiaries regardless of their tribe or political affiliation. WR will ensure that feedback mechanisms are safe, accessible, and the preferred option for beneficiaries by offering a variety of options. Beneficiaries will be sensitized on the different people to whom they can report an incident. By allowing for multiple choice contacts based on the beneficiary comfort level.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

A quick change in activities will be in effect to improve the suitability and desired outcomes for the affected population through Local staff, clinical staff and the Area Coordinator based on the feedback collected from beneficiaries. Projects activities including distribution days and sites will be adjusted according to the feedback of the beneficiary. WR has put in place a Complaint Response Mechanism on all nutrition implementing sites to be used. Project staff will take time with community volunteers to understand the concerns of the community to which the service is being provided for. Feedbacks collected through different ways will be delivered to the Quality Specialist and the Program Director for analyzing and monitoring the data collected from field sites to suggest quick changes that can be made to improve the project and pick trends of complaints for better implementation. Apart from this, on launching and during implementation of the project period community sensitization meetings will be conducted for influential community leaders, church leaders, the CHD, and women leaders to build trust and confidence toward the project and to orient beneficiaries on CRM pathway and get feedback from the community on the services delivery.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Women and young children being the main target of this project, the implementation of activities in particular might put them at risk as they seek the services. unaccompanied travel to nutrition sites might expose woman and children to possible sexual abuse which is of concern for WR. WR project staff will create the platform with Boma nutrition teams to create awareness among the community especially men to escort women and girls during travel to Nutrition facilities to reduce any incidence of possible GBV and other protection concerns. WR will actively engage with communities during site selection for static nutrition centers, relocated sites due to flooding and mobile nutrition outreach units to ensure accessibility distance to the targeted community. The nutrition team with coordinate with protection partners on the ground to strengthen an effective community awareness on identification and referral pathway. Maternal infant and young child feeding (MIYCN) counseling sessions will be used to target and address wrong perception related with malnourished children, improper usage of RUFT/RUSF by selling or sharing among household. Community outreach activities will be implemented through the care group volunteers and lead mothers with regularly group meetings and individual household visits. women, children, elderly and people with disabilities are likely to be discriminated against in accessing nutrition services, distance being a possible barrier for inclusion. The project team will give priority for particular individuals with special needs during registration and implementation of any project activities. The project will employ one protection officer and two assistants to ensure inequalities are minimized, inclusion in project activities is enhanced and all population groups are protected from exploitation and abuse. This will be achieved by ensuring equitable gender inclusion, targeted inclusion of people with disabilities and other vulnerable groups including children and elderly individuals. With the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic WR will continue with Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE) activities for implementation of preventive measures. World Relief has a security focal point who works closely with other protection actions; a security risk analysis will be conducted to ensure safety of staff and beneficiaries. WR will liaise with Boma and county management committees for advices related to recruitment, distribution safest mode and venue for training and inputs delivery. The project will conduct post distribution monitoring which will measure among other parameters how accessible the inputs were to beneficiaries. The project will measure timeliness of inputs, ease of access of beneficiaries to Nutrition centers and time taken in queuing for inputs. The project team will serve beneficiaries in the most respectful and dignified way possible with sensitivity to the communities culture and way of life. Beneficiary will always be encourage to make their own decision based on full and proper information given through project staff specially for activities to do with mode of cultivation or consumption of other foods, this will be explained in the most respectful manner and beneficiaries will be allowed to make their own decision.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

The three target locations for WR implementation have been facing flooding for three consecutive years. Most of nutrition sites and WASH facilities in these counties have been submerged in water for most of 2021, the likelihood of flooding continuing in this counties is very high. WRSS will work with communities to institute disaster risk mitigation measures across the target locations. the project will build the capacity of the community to institute early warning signs that will trigger response before the flooding occurs. Through this early warning signs, the community can respond with appropriate measures. The project will work together with community to conduct flood control measures, mainly berms and dykes. The community will identify and prioritize areas prone to flooding. The community will then be facilitated to establish dykes or berms that will be used to control the flooding water. This will help to reduce flood related displacement in the target locations

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

WR follows an integrated approach in service delivery at nutrition sites. WR runs a stabilization center(SC) in Koch PHCC of Unity state and plans to establish one SC in Phom PHCC of Fangak County in Jonglei state. In Benitu IDP camp OTP/TSFP sites are attached to sector 2 PHCC. In these two counties, WRSS runs both PHCU and PHCC services. WRSS intends to expand and strengthen timely and quality case management of GBV in coordination with protection cluster and lead partners on the ground. WRSS also ensures protection to children, women at risk and GBV survivors. WRSS will also able to work with protection partners to provide minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV. 1) These services will include: counseling-empathetic listening, inquiring about needs and concerns, validating, and ensuring safety of the victims. 2) Treatment of injuries associated with GBV 3) STI screening and treatments 4) Rapid HIV testing and counseling services and referral to care and treatment as needed 5) Post exposure prophylaxis (PEP) within 72 hours for sexual exposure and to be taken for 28 days 6) Emergency contraception within 120 hours 7) Referral and linkage to other services:-child protection, legal counsel, police, psychosocial support, shelter) . Through these referral pathways children, women at risk and GBV survivors will be able to access information on prevention and management of GBV. WRSS is also able to handle clinical management of rape within the PHCC and where such cases would be beyond WRSS capacity, WRSS is able to refer such cases to other facilities with better capacity.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

This nutrition project will integrate FSL activities through targeting direct beneficiaries that have been admitted in nutrition programs and targeted with MIYCN/IYCF activities. kitchen gardening education and cooking demonstration will be conducted in the nutrition facilities for care takers of malnourished children under five and pregnant and lactating mothers , these activities contribute in the overall effort of food availability through increased crop production and consumption of available variety of foods. Lead mothers in the community are responsible for looking after kitchen gardens in nutrition sites which will help in providing hands on practice for the mothers themselves and for the community they conduct counseling on such activities. A combination of inputs and training will be integrated in community outreach activities to enhance crop production for the community. A behavioral change on addressing food taboos and other cultural barriers related to nutrition will be tackled on community outreach activities through care group volunteer, lead mothers and CNVs keeping in mind the cultural set of the community the project is being implemented.
