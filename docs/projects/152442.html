Project Summary

This project aims to establish seven (7) women and girls friendly spaces (WGFS) using local building materials in four (4) payams of Kapoeta North County namely (1) Chumakori Payam, (2) Najie Payam (3) Paringa Payam and (4) Mosingo Payam and three (3) women and girls friendly spaces in three (3) Payams of Ibba County namely; (1 )Ibba center Payam, (2) Madebe Payam and (3) Manikakara Payam to enable women, girls, boys and men to have access to safe, reliable and confidential services for mitigating the consequences of GBV and to allow for enhanced community reintegration. The seven women and girls friendly spaces will:
1)Provision of age and sex appropriate case management to survivors of GBV targeting 200 women and girls.
2)Provision of quality individual and group psycho social support services such as bead making, bed sheets making, drama, storytelling, embroidery, crocheting through newly established women and Girls Friendly spaces using UNICEF Guideline targeting 280 women and girls. And another 2100 women and girls assessing WGFSs for information session in introduction to GBV,education,hygiene etc. this will make a totaling of 2380 women and girls assessing the WGFSs.
3)Conduct training targeting 14 case management workers on GBV Guiding principles.
4)Formation and Training of 2100 community members which compose of women, men, boys and girls as GBV champion through Focus Group Discussion (FGD) to conduct information sessions on introduction to GBV, gender role, education and on how to support GBV survivors to assess GBV services within 72 hours in WGFSs as per GBV standard guide.
5) train 14 community mobilizers to strengthen on protection risk mitigation and preparedness and early warning.
6) Provision of Mentoring sessions to 14 case management workers, 14 community outreach workers on GBV prevention and responses.
7) Conduct out-reach awareness raising sessions on GBV prevention and response targeting 6,200 women,men,girls and boys to strengthen referral pathway systems to improve prevention and response on GBV and risk mitigation across community structures in Ibba and Kapoeta North Counties. The overall beneficiaries of this project are 10,908 persons comprising 70% (women and girls) and 30% (men and boys) to work together to prevent and respond to the abuses of sexual gender based violence in four (4) Payams i.e. Chumakori, Najie, Mosingo and Paringa Payams in Kapoeta North County and in the three(3) payams of Ibba center, Medebe , Manikakara Payams in Ibba County . Women and girls in 7 (WGFS) will effectively undertake group activities such as bead making, bed sheets making, drama, storytelling, embroidery, crocheting .and other activities that may be decided by women to learn new income generation skills to become self-reliant. They will also have the opportunity for interacting and building peaceful communities.

1 - Needs

Since the eruption of the violent conflict in South Sudan in 2013 and renewed fighting in Juba in 2016; South Sudan has been undergoing catastrophic humanitarian crisis. Millions of the South Sudanese population were/are displaced both as internal displaced persons and refugees in neighboring Countries in dire needs for humanitarian assistance. There have been total breakdown of rule of law. The crisis have made millions vulnerable and depending on humanitarian relief aid. The economy has since collapsed with hyperinflation. Humanitarian workers have been targeted by armed groups. There is huge humanitarian needs all over South Sudan including great Kapoeta and Ibba. for which this project is targeting. In Greater Kapoeta area the major humanitarian issues include recurring food insecurity to thousands of civilians most of them internal displace persons due to on-going insecurity in surrounding villages caused by cattle raids, fighting among the communities in Pibor areas forcing many people to seek refugee in Kapoeta.The infiltration of arms in the hands of undisciplined youth posed serious threats especially for girls and young women. This is very serious because women and girls have faced the brunt of inhumane treatments during the conflict. In this crisis context, the GBV situation in Kapoeta north and Ibba remains dire: There are well-documented and frequent reports of rape cases, girl child labor, forced abduction, sexual exploitation and abuse , early and forced child marriages and bad cultural practices that disadvantage women and girls. Women and girls continue to suffer everyday where the social system of support has broken down. These GBV survivors who might have escaped from their captors do not have access to counseling, medical and legal services. Community members believe that indignity women and girls going through every day is culturally acceptable and therefore to change these mindsets requires massive awareness rising. Prolonged conflict and lack of rule of law and security structures have traumatized and psychologically stressed the local population with no redress options. The Women and girls have minimal access to services, GBV survivors have inadequate support, and many women and girls rights violated are not reported. High levels of illiteracy among the community and the community lack information on GBV has resulted to many condoning biased cultural practices against women and girls. Survivors/Victims and families know most of the perpetrators of sexual violence. However, survivors often fear that if they speak openly about their ordeals, they will become a disgrace to their families.This social stigmatization means that many choose to suffer in silence. Lack of community structures that provide GBV services to support women and girls have continued to be serious concerns. Women and girls continue to be exposed to sexual violence and limited medical services. Where limited GBV- related service do not exist they must travel long distance exposing them to further risks. There are no specialized agencies rending GBV services in Ibba and Kapoeta North and the needs continue to exceed the available services in the two counties. This project will enable GREDA to address the critical gaps in GBV programming in the two targeted counties. GREDA's experience of working successfully on GBV project in Kapoeta North indicates that the majority of adolescent girls are out of school, experiencing growing levels of sexual violence, early and forced marriage, sexual exploitation and abuse. women/girls have limited access to GBV services especially in the far away payams. the local cultural norms do not allow women to freely discuss GBV issues publically. the fear of cultural norms contributes to failure of women to report cases of GBV.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

GREDA HR policy considers code of conduct, PSEA and AAP training are mandatory for staff including volunteers and contractors. GREDA have in place channels for reporting abuses. There will be very strict follow up on code of conduct for the staffs of the project and their relationship with the beneficiaries. They should maintain official relationship with the beneficiaries without intimidating the beneficiaries by promising false. The beneficiaries should be informed by management and the staffs that all services are free for all targeted population without discrimination. Notifications and code of conduct will be deployed for the beneficiaries to be informed. GREDA will develop preparedness and contingency plans to deal with any change or fluctuating security situation to safeguard staff security and safety and minimal disruption on project activities overall. These plans will ensure minimum services remain in place even in escalation of violence. Being a national organization, we conduct our recruitment locally and most of our staffs are not relocatable. They are available to offer services in the event of limited access or evacuation. They move with the population in the event of displacement or disasters. These local national staff members can easily be provided with support remotely. In volatile situations, this means limited interruptions in service delivery. As part of GREDA contingency plans, key triggers will be identified for a variety of likely scenarios in all locations. Security management plans are in place in all locations and are regularly reviewed and updated based on emerging threats and changing security. GREDA has various checks and balance including anti fraud and whistleblower policies among others. .GREDA will also work in coordination with other humanitarian agencies working in the project areas and also follow-closely the NGO Forum and UN Security alerts and updates to be aware of eminent risks.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

GREDA’s accountability to the affected population (AAP) is central to our programming. Our approach in our AAP is influenced by the Do No Harm principles. Beneficiaries are at the center and part of our decision making. The project will engage the beneficiaries throughout its implementation as part of our efforts in strengthening AAP. In our project implementation phase, the beneficiaries will be sensitized about their rights, decision-making and leverage they have in the project. Beneficiaries will be engaged on choice of locations of key project activities, considering their access, safety and distances especially taking account of, women, girls and child-headed households. Our actions are guided in three steps which are in line with IASC 5 commitment, and they are: (1) Taking account, we give communities meaningful influence over decision making in a way that is inclusive, non-discriminatory and accounts for the diversity of communities.This means before implementing the project, GREDA will organize a meeting with stakeholders to inform them about the services to be rendered under this project and different channels they will use to communicate to express either satisfaction or complaints through feedback boxes to be placed in all WGFS. To ensure that information shared is not tampered with and it will be locked in a safe. Periodically, a senior manager on an M&E mission will collect and bring the feedback to head office where the small team will review and report the findings. (2) GREDA adhere to principle of giving account to our beneficiaries by sharing information in an effective and transparent way across communities that we serve and this includes providing beneficiaries with information about GREDA and about how to provide feedback or how to raise complaints. (3) GREDA is guided by the principle of being held to account and include involving communities on what they think about the quality of response provided by us.
