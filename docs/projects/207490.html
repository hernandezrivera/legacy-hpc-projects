Project Summary

The goal of the project is to provide life-saving child protection Assistances for both host and Rohingya Communities. In order to achieve the project’s goal, WVB intends to achieve the outcomes which are:
Children and adolescents have access to structured and unstructured Psychosocial support and age-appropriate Life-Skills based knowledge. To ensure community based protection mechanisms for children wellbeing and community strengthen. Children and adolescents have also access to quality case management and referral to specialized services with Outreach Activities and Child Protection-Systems strengthening and Capacity building.
To begin with, WVB will dedicate itself to providing robust psychosocial support and life skills to children aged 0-10 and adolescents aged 11-18. This support will aim to strengthen their mental health and resilience through structured and non-structured programs within safe, inclusive, and high-quality learning environments. They'll conduct training sessions in leadership, social cohesion, and resilience for these young individuals. Additionally, caregivers will be equipped with positive discipline techniques and receive parenting guidance tailored to the needs of vulnerable children and adolescents, including those with disabilities.
Recognizing the importance of community involvement, WVB will identify and empower local child protection groups and networks. Project will conduct capacity-building exercises focused on various risks, such as gender equality and harmful social norms. Improving existing referral systems and establishing stronger community-based mechanisms for at-risk children and adolescents will be key priorities.
Moreover, WVB will be actively engaged in providing case management services to children who are at at-risk and children who have experienced protection incidents including, child laborers, child survivors of SGBV, and unaccompanied and separated children, and conduct referrals to multi-sectoral services.
Community engagement will lie at the heart of the project to ensure better environment for children and adolescents. WVB will conduct awareness campaigns, workshops, and use social media platforms to educate children about child protection and gender-based violence risks. They'll also distribute dignity kits to vulnerable groups, aiming to boost protection and diminish social stigma. Collaborating with faith and community leaders will be crucial in this effort.
WVB will be committed to strengthening the overall system and capacities involved in child protection. Project team will train both child protection and other sectoral staff and volunteers on integrating child protection principles. Through participatory community mapping exercises, they'll work closely with various stakeholders. Regular updates and sharing of local child protection referral pathways with stakeholders will ensure a more efficient system with actively participate in humanitarian coordination groups and establish child-friendly feedback mechanisms.
Through MEAL part WV is committed to ensuring that the project is achieving and will collect data on the project indicators through a variety of methods, including surveys, spot-check assessments, satisfaction survey focus, satisfaction surveys for case management services observation spot-check, ECHO Questions for dignity kits, feedback summary sheet, CVA & NFI distribution group discussions, and interviews. WV will produce regular project reports which will be required by CPSS and internal to improve the project's effectiveness and frequently report. The project team will ensure the Feedback mechanisms by seeking input from students, parents, and community members to ensure a safe, accessible, accountable, and participatory manner for children.
In essence, WVB’s project will be a comprehensive approach aimed in child protection for vulnerable children, empowering communities, and establishing sustainable mechanisms for child protection in Cox's bazar.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Cox's Bazar is a socio-economically vulnerable district in Bangladesh, with the situation of children and adolescents exacerbated by the influx of Rohingya refugees since 2017, the COVID-19 pandemic, and the global fund crisis. Currently, over 931,000 Rohingya refugees reside in 33 congested camps in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazila of Cox’s Bazar District, with 51.6% being children aged 0-17, including 25.18% girls and 12% living with disabilities (Source: JRP-24 Refugee population fact sheet). Child marriage and child labor persist as critical issues. Child marriage poses health risks, disrupts education, and perpetuates gender inequality. The severity level for child marriages in the region is catastrophic, with an incidence rate of 5 (Source: Refugee/CPIMS+/Expert Judgement, 2023). The Multi-Sectoral Need Assessment found rates of child marriage at 2% among refugees and 0.3% in the host community (Source: J-MSNA, 19 October 2023). Child labor is also a signification concern in child protection issues where 3% of host communities and 3% of refugee children ages 5-17 were reportedly contributing to household income. 3% of the host community and 4% of the refugee households reported that one of their children (under 18 years old) was not residing with them (JMSNA-2023). Also, 3% of the host community and 3% of refugee children ages 5-17 were reportedly contributing to household income. 16% of the host community and 25% of refugee households reported that their children participated at least once in the last month in a community-based child protection program (JMSNA-2023). The report and data sources show that the Rohingya refugee crisis has created a unique and challenging environment for child protection. Rohingya children are particularly vulnerable to child labor, child marriage, and trafficking due to their displacement, poverty, and lack of access to education and other protective services. The host communities in Cox's Bazar are also facing significant challenges in protecting their children. The influx of Rohingya refugees has put a strain on local resources, and host children are also at risk of child labor, child marriage, child trafficking and other forms of exploitation. To address these challenges, WV is focusing on community engagement, awareness raising, coordinated interventions, and establishing child-friendly spaces. Additionally, WV will provide specialized child protection services, psychosocial support programs, recreational activities, life-skills education, early childhood development interventions and positive parenting skills programs for caregivers. Given these challenges, WV aims to actively engage communities in protection services, conduct awareness programs, coordinated interventions and establish child-friendly spaces. To address child protection and case management issues, WV will provide comprehensive child protection services to both Rohingya refugees and host children. These services will include prevention, interventions and case management activities. WV is continuously supporting for strengthening of the capacity of local child protection actors and the community-based Child Protection Committees. This includes training government officials, NGO staff, and community members on child protection issues & referral mechanisms. WV will integrate these concerns across programs, conduct regular awareness sessions, and empower communities as agents of change rather than mere recipients of services.In order to ensure the CP services among the targeted community peopls both in Host and Camps, WVB will maintain strong networking, collaboration and coordination with CIC, Site Management, CP Sectors and other sectors.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

World Vision BRCR will Prioritize the Safety and Dignity of affected populations in all interventions including specific guidelines. Our Child protection projects will embrace the Protection Mainstreaming Framework's eight core components to comprehensively address the protection needs of children. WVB always approaches to ensuring that the rights and well-being of children are protected throughout all aspects of the project. Project activities will be designed and implemented in a way that minimizes harm to children and enhances their well-being. We have already existing steps to reduce children's vulnerability to physical and psychosocial risks. Our existing CFS center and MP Center have equitable access to the project's activities and services. We have established CFRM mechanisms for children to assess the project's activities, voice their concerns, and seek resolutions. Our children will be empowered to participate in the project and make decisions about their own lives to express their views, develop their skills, and assert their rights which will ensure that children are safe, dignified, and empowered. Our Effective information-sharing mechanisms empower communities and refugees with knowledge of their rights and service access. The integrated CP project will empower and engage the community to take ownership of child protection issues and provide them with the tools to prevent, mitigate, and respond to protection risks within their communities. We have a plan to more collaboration with CPSS to equip staff with the necessary knowledge and support to enable them to uphold humanitarian principles while maintaining their own safety. We have already existing CP service mapping and strong referral systems to facilitate access to existing services, maximizing the utilization of available resources. Finally, coordination and advocacy efforts will strengthen protection responses, minimize duplication, and effectively address emerging risks.

3.3 - Age & Disability

In a Child Protection Project focusing on age and disability, the project team plans to execute a thorough needs assessment. This assessment will specifically address the vulnerabilities and requirements of children across various age brackets and those with disabilities. Its aim is to ensure that services provided are accessible, pertinent, and fitting for children of all ages and abilities. To enable inclusive communication, the project aims to develop strategies that encompass children with disabilities and those in different age groups. Currently, there are 16 safe spaces and child-friendly environments established. There's a plan to enhance community sustainability and ownership by implementing materials such as braille, sign language, and simplified resources easily comprehensible by everyone. An integral task within the project is Child-Centered Case Management, which involves implementing case management strategies sensitive to the age and disability of children. Training for case managers will focus on identifying and addressing the distinct needs of each child. The project emphasizes the establishment of a robust Monitoring and Evaluation Function. This mechanism will track the participation and progress of children across different age groups and those with disabilities. The data obtained will drive adjustments and enhancements to project activities. For comprehensive community engagement, the project will undertake Community Awareness and Sensitization efforts. This involves conducting awareness campaigns within the community to foster inclusion, diminish stigma related to disability, and encourage active participation of children with disabilities in community life and activities.

World Vision has the Humanitarian Program Accountability Framework (PAF) and Accountability Operating Guideline (AOG) aligning with global standards covering CHS, IASC, etc. Projected people's needs will be given priority without any discrimination or bias. A detailed AAP plan and progress tracker will be followed over the project duration. Our current CP project has an active involvement with the affected population, including children, caregivers, and local stakeholders, in the project's design, implementation, and decision-making processes. Our Project seeks the community people’s input, listens to their concerns, and incorporates their suggestions to ensure that our project is directly based on the findings of participatory assessments and joint analysis of priority needs. Our project has a strong focus on Complaint and feedback mechanisms in designing a coordinated plan to ensure clear and accessible feedback mechanisms that allow beneficiaries to raise concerns. In Transparency and Communication, we have already World Vision’s mechanism to monitor the affected population's perception of the humanitarian response regularly ensuring decision-making for the community people. World Vision has a clear accountability framework that outlines the organization's commitment to being accountable to the affected populations. 3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

World Vision is an integral part of the Rapid Protection Assessment Team, Protection Emergency Response Unit (PERU). This ensures an immediate response to the needs of people affected by crises and disasters. They maintain close coordination with Site Management, Shelter Management, and the Camp-in-Charge (CiC) offices and provide support to Disaster Management Unit (DMU) volunteers, offering training on Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and safe referrals for gender-based violence (GBV). World Vision Bangladesh will outline the Disaster Risk Management and Reduction section focus on ensuring effective coordination, compliance with government regulations, and maintaining contingency measures. Coordination with Sector Leads World Vision Will Ensure that climate change considerations are integrated into sector-specific contingency plans and Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) plans in Rohingya refugee and host communities. By adhering to the government's Standing Order on Disaster 2019, agencies indirectly contribute to the broader national disaster risk reduction and management strategy. World Vision also remains committed to raising awareness about child Protection Key Messages, with the aim of helping the community mitigate the impact of disasters before, during, and after they occur. This effort includes house-to-house visits conducted by trained volunteers to ensure that individuals with disabilities and other vulnerable populations receive the necessary support. Moreover, WVB will continue to preposition clothing items and Dignity Kits as part of their emergency preparedness and response activities. As part of contingency plans and EPR plans, Community-based disaster preparedness programs will be implemented including training to community members on evacuation procedures, first aid, and other essential skills to enhance their resilience in the face of disasters and awareness programs that build the capacity of communities to adapt to climate change impacts.
