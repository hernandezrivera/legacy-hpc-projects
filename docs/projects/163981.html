Project Summary

This project is aligned with the priority of protection of population affected by the crisis is improved through community-level approach and individually targeted protection interventions, and through advocacy with duty bearers, relates to strategic objectives
Project contributes to prevent and response Child Protection (CP) concerns through implementing Community-level child protection activities as the following:
The first specific objective of the program is to strengthen community-level child protection services. The main activities that will be:
1- Case management (survivors or at risk of UASC, CAAFAG, Child labor & Early marriage, dangers and injuries).
2- Referral pathway
3- Family tracing and reunification.
4- Psycho-social Support structured and semi structured.
5- Mine Risk Education
6- Parenting skills
7- Establishing for CP community committees, supports Community-level child protection
8- Inclusive programs (For children with disabilities, PSS, and life skill).
9- Adolescents life skills
10- Ensuring the safe access for PSEA for girls, women.
The second specific objective of this project to strengthen the capacity for humanitarian worker, duty bearers and community member to respond to child protection concerns. As the staff and CPN member will be trained on CPMS, CPIE, Communication Skills, PFA, PSS, first Aid and case management. (BDC) will focus on building the capacity of all staff to be first responder to CP concern in targeted areas. Training will include basics of child rights, identification and referral, CM for new staff, parenting skills and life skills training for workers how to provide these programs.
SOP will be developed for project aligned with child protection sub-cluster guidance, ToRs and agreed criteria. While, BDC will determining “vulnerability” and “vulnerable groups” typically according to analysis of multiple dimensions based on age, gender, diversity, specific needs, displacement status, and other characteristics.

4 - Needs

Since December 2019, the violence in north west of Syria has been taken a hard way against the civilians in mentioned region. Which an intensive military operation has been escalated again in northwest of Syria, with a catastrophic impact the civilians specially against vulnerable parts like children, and women. “With nearly one million people displaced in Idlib and surrounding areas since 1 December, the vast majority of them women and children, it is imperative that the UN uses all modalities to reach people in need, whether from inside Syria or across borders.” Statement by Kevin Kennedy, Regional Humanitarian for Syria Crisis conducted in Hatay, Turkey 2nd March 2020. According to HNO 2020, Violence, displacement and worsening socio-economic conditions leave children, adolescents, and youth exposed to a range of protection concerns and grave child rights violations. Children continue to be adversely affected by the crisis, with 2.5 million displaced, 5.7 million in need of protection, and 6.6 million in need of education assistance. “The crisis continues to impact children’s mental well-being through prolonged displacement, exposure to violence, poverty and challenges in access to basic services. 42 per cent of surveyed households report signs of psychosocial distress – nightmares, lasting sadness and anxiety, amongst others – in the last 30 days, suggesting that many girls and boys are in a situation of more prolonged distress.”“Of the over 948,000 people displaced in northwest Syria since 1 December, 81 percent are women and children (some 570,000 children). Ongoing protection monitoring highlights the desperate situation of displaced civilians, with many households in western districts of Idleb governorate indicating that they have run out of options to seek safety and security.

5 - Implementation of activities

1-BDC will provide structured PSS activities in established CFSs using the Palestinian model for psychosocial support programs and will adopt (little fellow) curriculum for ages 6-10. In the centers (g/b), including adolescents ages 6-18, Two shifts will be operated daily to make sure more children can access to 14 CFS 5 d/w. 2-Caregivers and parents (m/f) will be given parenting sessions to help them understand, prevent and respond to protection risks for children. Outreach team will target adolescents outside the centers with life skills, leadership skills. 3- Deploy mobile teams to deliver semi-structured PSS interventions to children and caregivers who cannot access services in the CFS. One caseworker will be part of each team to identify and refer children that need specialized support. Children and caregivers will also get general CP awareness on prevention of family separation, child rights Activity. 4-Community-level CP will be supported through establishing 15 CP Community Committee in proposed locations. Each CP committee consists of 6 (m/f) members in 14 different locations. It includes members from the LCs, NGOs, community leaders, and youth. 5-Specialized child protection services (case management) for girls and boys are available in targeted locations BDC will work with CPNs to identify entry points in accessing survivors or at-risk children. Case management will facilitate case monitoring and referral to services, and respond to high urgency child protection risks, including CAAFG, UASC and survivors of violence, abuse and exploitation.6-Follow-up support will be provided to all reunified cases on a monthly basis. The CPNs will support with follow up visits.7-Strengthen human resource capacity to respond to child protection concerns. To conduct a thematic training courses for 200 employed staff (m/f) on the following topics: PSS, CPiE, CPMS, Child Rights mainstreaming, and PFA. In addition to 90 members (m/f) of CPN.
