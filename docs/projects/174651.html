Project Summary

Children aged 12 to 17 years in the Rural areas of Fako division who are drop out are 1.7 times more likely to be exploited and violated due to the fact that they are out of school and with no skills or low skills as a result of the crisis in the South West and North West Regions of Cameroon (PAID-WA 2019). This project aimed to improve on the welfare of at risk school dropout youth (12-17 years) through sustainable livelihood pathways and provision of local based revolving startup capital in Vulnerable communities in Fako Division in Cameroon. The Pan African Institute for Development West Africa (PAID-WA), Buea, seeks to achieve this through:
a. Capacity building of 500 (250 boys and 250 girls, aged 12 to 17) school drop outs in vocational and entrepreneurial skills for income generation through distance learning mode and field base mode in vulnerable communities in Fako Division;
b. Mentorship and incubation of 500 school drop out with business plan by experts;
c. Provision of Start up capital to 500 drop out youths through a revolving micro credit schemes

1 - Besoins / Needs

Children aged 12 to 17 years in the Rural areas of Fako division who are drop out are 1.7 times more likely to be exploited and violated due to the fact that they are out of school and with no skills or low skills as a result of the crisis. (PAID-WA 2019) Starting and expanding a small business remains a serious challenge, especially for these disadvantaged youth. According to a need assessment conducted by PAID-WA in 2019, Obstacles include the lack of access to information, skills, finance, land and property rights, pre-/post-start up orientation and business development support as well as administrative hurdles. Even where public and NGO support services for self-employment exist, they are usually targeted to youth with a minimum of completed middle-school (e.g. Advance level and Diploma holders) or even university level education (e.g. PAID-WA 2018). School drop-outs and low skilled youth working the informal sector, on the other hand, currently have almost no opportunities to increase their productivity. Therefore, the need to enhance economic and social inclusion, supporting vulnerable youth (school dropout) in the informal sector and tailoring the design and delivery of training in income generating activities and enterprise support towards the particular needs of drop outs between the ages of 12 to 17 has emerged as a key priority that is currently unmet in the two crisis region of Cameroon, especially in Fako division.
