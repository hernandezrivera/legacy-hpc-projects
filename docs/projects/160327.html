Project Summary

The overal purpose of the project will be to build resilience against factors that cause perpetual food and nutrition insecurity to the communities of Nasir, Ulang, Maiwut and Longechuk County of Greater Upper Nile State in South Sudan by supporting sustainable livelihoods, improved productivity and increased household incomes to attain the four pillars of Food Security (Availability, Access, Utilization and Sustainability). ADRA will use the Lead farmer and groups approaches as its strategy for sustainability. The groups will be comprised of men, women and the youth from food insecure households, to avert effects of Climate Change, negative attitude towards crop production and poor skills and knowledge in crop production.This purpose is envisaged to be achieved through Disaster Risk Reduction, improved agricultural and veterinary practices, income generation, natural resource management and peace building with a focus on gender parity and community engagement. The other key issue under this purpose will be strengthening local capacities in farmer support, Disaster Risk Management and increment of household self reliance.
ADRA will target Host, IDP and Returnee communities resident in the four Counties of Upper Nile State of South Sudan. 40% of the target group will be households from the Host community while the categories of the IDPs and Returnees will each constitute 30% of the total households targeted. A total of 17,000 households comprising of women in vegetable production, men and women in Crop production, men and women in livestock production and youth and community members in fishery and Small business activities. ADRA will have a deliberate bias to targeted the most vulnerable households who have the willingness to participate in the project for two years. The households targeted for agricultural activities will have at least one adult who is non-labour constrained (able to offer labour).

1 - Needs

Upper Nile, like most parts of South Sudan experiences perennial food insecurity that is fueled among other things by poor or lack of resilience to impacts of Climate Change on food production, processing and utilization. Siting the impacts of Climate on Food Security in South Sudan, the WFP 2014 VAM report states that Climate variability and change disproportionately affect the poorest and most food insecure through a combination of decreasing crop production, increasing human and animal diseases, flooding,.... dry spells and unpredictable weather. The incapacity to create household income from livelihoods is another noted reason for continued food insecurity in Ulang as employment continues to be limited and markets unreliable. The findings by joint WFP and FAO Crop Assessment of 2018, states that only 50% of the Households of Nasir are in crop farming but are drawn back by erratic rainfall patterns and inadequate inputs and poor agronomical practices. This contributed to cereal deficit production of 6,700MT for the year 2018 . Intercommunity conflicts have led to poor coexistence between communities and among the IDP, returnee and host populations . This has caused instability leading to population movements and that is not conducive to sustaining food security. Fractured local coordination and farmer and entrepreneur support systems lead to continued food insecurity. The highest need for the populations of Upper Nile is to be food secure, ie to be self reliant in making adequate food available in their reserves, access it, properly utilize it and create incomes for sustainability.

2 - Activities

ADRA’s activities will focus on vegetable production, agro-forestry crop production, livestock management, fisheries and household economic development. Protection, Peace building, gender and environment protection will be implemented as cross cutting in order to ensure sustainability and inclusion. In a bid to bring new Climate Smart technologies and capacity to fight perennial food and Nutrition insecurity, ADRA’s implementation of this intervention will work around:-- The group and peer to peer approach by establishing Farmer Field Schools.- Enhancement of business and entrepreneurship, value chains and markets linkage.- Strengthening of the local systems and the extension service structures.- Strengthening community cohesion. ADRA and the respective communities will adopt complementary, market driven, transparent, inclusive, pro-poor and pro-gender-balanced approaches in agricultural production through the promotion of tested extension and advisory approaches. ADRA will ensure the selection of the most deserved beneficiaries in a transparent and participatory method with a focus on smallholder women and men farmers, fisher folk and small business entrepreneurs. The implementation strategy in all modalities will focus on community capacity building, emphasizing that long-term independence is based not just on human rights, but also on the participants’ capacity to sustain families without assistance. Farmer to farmer strategies will be employed with the objective of increasing knowledge, promoting agricultural productivity, increasing food security, improving rural livelihoods, and promoting agriculture as an engine of pro-poor economic growth, participation and inclusion. The Lead farmer approach, Farmer Field Schools, demonstration plots will be part of a series of peer to peer or farmer-to-farmer extension methods to be used by ADRA to strengthen the capacity of Upper Nile communities, livelihoods and food security asset creation. ADRA SS strategy encourages social and community enterprises that are both sustainable and market driven to ensure they meet community needs and are based on fiscal responsibility. ADRA South Sudan will build and strengthen partnerships with communities, knowing that independence and the freedom to live, work and succeed are constant fights that require the commitment of great individuals and great communities. In this spirit, ADRA SS has a proud history of welcoming international and national partners in its work with communities. ADRA Animal Health Workers, technicians, community mobilisers, participating producer groups, government agricultural extension officers, fisher folks and other community members will be required to cascade and disseminate the information to their peers using various platforms. Environment protection will be key in safeguarding land and reduction of the impacts of Climate change. Tree planting will be promoted through all the selected farmers as well as other members of the community. Local Early Warning systems will be strengthened through formation and strengthening of the DRR committees and Payam and county levels. These will come up with Community Action Plans (CAPs) on Environment Protection and Disaster Risk Reduction. ADRA will advocate and facilitate that the CAPs are adopted into by-laws at State and county levels.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Following the ongoing peace process in South Sudan, the protection environment has significantly improved in Upper Nile like other parts of South Sudan. This is seen by the number of returnees back to Nasir, Ulang, Longechuk and Maiwut mainly from the neighboring Ethiopia and Sudan. ADRA has also been continuously present in Nasir for the past six years where they have implemented FSL, and are implementing Protection and Education in Emergency projects. Communities have generally managed to implement projects with ADRA to completion.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The project is designed in a way that Community level structures will be capacitated throughout the project life. These structures will include Disaster Risk Reduction Committees and Payam and county levels, VSLA groups, Fisher Folk groups, Lead Farmers while also including Community leadership. Proper project handover planning will be done and implemented together with community based structures.
