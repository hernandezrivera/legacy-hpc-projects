Project Summary

The project will contribute to protection and promotion of rights and durable solutions for people affected by the conflict in eastern Ukraine, in close coordination NRC Ukraine’s LFS and S&S programs. Within this goal, ICLA’s specific objectives are to: (1) ensure that a greater number of the most vulnerable conflict affected people in hard to reach areas have access to basic rights and humanitarian assistance, reduce their vulnerability and build resilience; (2) Support economic and social well-being as durable solutions by providing IDP with equal access to, and enjoyment of their right to an adequate standard of living without discrimination, including shelter, livelihoods and employment, effective and accessible mechanisms to restore housing, land and property rights, access to personal and other documentation and freedom of movement (3) build the capacity of government and civil society legal service providers to adopt the maximum feasible case load for IDPs and other such conflict-affected populations in need of assistance (4) support advocacy in pursuing the adoption of policies and practice which promote durable solutions in line with international standards. ICLA will endeavour to reach the most vulnerable within the above target groups, notably women/single headed households, especially with a large number of children, elderly (particularly living alone), people with disabilities or limited mobility, extremely poor and no-income families, people of pre-retirement age, people living in rural or remote areas. This will include persons from GCA and NGCA where possible.

Needs

As a result of the outbreak of hostilities in eastern Ukraine in 2014, the State territory has been split between government-controlled areas (GCA) and non-government controlled areas (NGCA). As a result, a once-integrated community is now divided by a 457-km long frontline (the so-called “contact line”), with a system of entry-exit checkpoints (EECP) installed on both sides by the parties to the conflict. 4.4 million people are affected by the crisis, of whom 3.4 million require humanitarian assistance and protection (600,000 people living along the contact line, 1.8 million in non-Government controlled areas-NGCA and 1 million in Government controlled areas-GCA). The Government of Ukraine (GoU) has registered 1.5 internally displaced people (IDP), of which 57% are women; 18% are over 60 years of age; households with children made up almost half (46%), while 15% of households reported members who are persons with disabilities.Armed clashes, which continue to the present day, have resulted in large-scale loss, destruction or damage to the property of the conflict-affected civilians. More than 40,000 objects of private property were damaged or destroyed due to shelling. Lack of adequate mechanisms for assessment and recording of, and restitution and compensation for damaged, destroyed, looted or occupied property and limited access to (shelter) humanitarian assistance in the absence of property title documents, are primary issues of concern for people living along the contact line.Further, due to the loss of control over a part of its territory and accompanying security concerns, Ukraine withdrew State bodies and services from NGCA and introduced control measures over the movement of people across the contact line (comprising permits and a verification process). Having officially declared all the bodies or structures created by the authorities in NGCA as illegal, Ukrainian authorities do not recognise any act issued by such authorities, including civil status acts or identity documents. Many IDPs continue to face bureaucratic impediments and discrimination due to various legislative acts and policies adopted since the beginning of the conflict such as the introduction of residence, identification and verification procedures, imposition of payment of pensions and social benefits through one State bank only - and solely on the basis of an IDP certificate. Internally displaced women are more vulnerable than men, often more financially dependent on other family members than men, more likely to be taking care of children (a 14.5 per cent gender gap), less active on the labour market and in starting their own businesses, and much more frequently faced with insufficient funds and forced to economize than internally displaced men. The escalation of hostilities, withdrawal of public services from NGCA and linking access to pensions and social benefits with IDP status, restriction of freedom of movement and transfer of goods across the contact line, “nationalisation” of Ukrainian companies by de facto authorities, economic blockade of NGCA, failure to relocate a large number of State-owned and municipal enterprises and institutions and bodies of local self-government, as well as disruptions to industry transport and small and medium-size enterprise activity have led to decrease in economic activity, widespread job loss, problems of salary arrears, suspension of pensions and social benefits and record-high prices. This in turn has caused increased food insecurity levels, loss of access to services and livelihoods and the application of negative coping strategies from food reduction to depletion of savings to illegal or high risk jobs for people living both in NGCA and in GCA.Overall, the conflict-generated discontinuation of State services in NGCA, coupled with the Stateˈs often discriminatory approach to addressing the needs of residents of those territories, have placed a disproportionate burden on the affected civilian population

Activities & outcomes

• ICLA will continue to provide information, counselling and legal assistance in:o ICLA Legal Aid Centres (LACs); o During mobile (home) visits for the most vulnerable;o ICLA desks at local municipal, council or village premiseso Employment centres, o Collective and community centres, o Educational or cultural institutions, o Partner officeso At the crossing points along the line of contact. • Information and counselling will additionally be provided remotely throughout Ukraine (GCA and NGCA) through: o Telephone hotlines & email o Multimedia including Radio, TV, social networks and Internet/websites. • Information provided through hard copy and electronic publications and group information sessions. • Legal representation and assistance before the courts and administrative authorities will be provided through partners. • ICLA will expand activities on strengthening the capacity of duty-bearers and other stakeholders to ensure protection and provide durable solutions to targeted conflict-affected people. Capacity building will include financial, management and technical support to local authorities, NGOs and SLACs.• In close coordination with NRC’s Advocacy team, ICLA will strengthen the use of research and advocacy resources to address systemic issues of concern for targeted CAP beneficiaries. Evidence-based research and technical support with NRC advocacy activities will feed the major protection actors, donor community and relevant interlocutors at the local, national and international level with the respective analyses of the current protection situation, strengthen the networks and seek allies in order to influence policy formation and decision making processes in relation to the targeted population. • NRC will continue to lead the HLP Technical Working Group, and ensure that resources are used most effectively in coordinating the efforts of humanitarian actors with respect to HLP issues. • As part of the effort to support evidence on the most relevant and important protection issues, NRC will engage with relevant actors in order to create unified dataset that documents claims for compensation of damages by conflict-affected persons ICLA will scale down directly implemented legal assistance service provision in communities where government and local civil society have the capacity, or can be given the capacity, to serve existing caseloads. This project will establish links with past/present humanitarian responses (HDN). Furthermore, project activities will be aligned with/contribute to relevant GoU strategies in eastern Ukraine (e.g. IDP Integration Strategy/Action Plan, State Targeted Recovery/Peacebuilding Programme, etc.), as well as create synergies with activities of recovery/development actors. Accountability: NRC policy places a heavy emphasis on ‘rights based’ programming, in which accountability plays an important role. NRC will adhere to the Ukraine Accountability Framework as recommended by the STAIT as both HLP TWG lead and as a program implementer. NRC has a comprehensive Complaints and Feedback mechanism which is independent from programme operations.

Indicators & targets

In 2019:• 12,000 beneficiaries receive accurate, timely and relevant information and counselling on HLP, LCD, IDR and employment/livelihoods through group or individual information sessions conducted by a lawyer (primary legal aid)• 2,000 beneficiaries receive legal assistance on HLP, LCD, IDR and employment/livelihoods (secondary legal aid)• 1500 persons referred to authorities or humanitarian partners for assistance in areas outside of ICLA or NRC Ukraine competences (secondary legal aid)• 2 Secondary Legal Aids Centres will receive direct capacity building and human resourcing• 2 Ukrainian Legal Assistance NGOs receive management support resourcing and core financial support• 100 duty bearer and other stakeholders beneficiaries receive training• 1 Database is created and maintained to document claims for compensation of damages by conflict-affected persons• 4 bilateral and group meetings / roundtables / workshops / conferences carried out with national authorities to advocate for systemic issues of concern regarding equal access to HLP, LCD, IDR and employment/livelihoods • 20 advocacy interventions (polling, coalition and network building; media outreach and partnerships; briefings /presentations; issuing analysis/research; strategic litigation; lobbying; policy proposal development) carried out at local, national and international level on HLP, LCD, IDR and employment/livelihoods In 2020, the project will be amended to an updated needs assessment. Potential activity, outputs, and outcomes are projected at no more than 50% of the 2019.
