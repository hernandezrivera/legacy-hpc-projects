1 - Needs

The context in the occupied Palestinian territory (oPt) remains a protracted protection crisis, where too many Palestinians struggle to live in dignity. The crisis is characterized by more than 54 years of Israeli military occupation, lack of respect for international humanitarian and human rights law, internal Palestinian political divisions, and recurrent escalations of hostilities between Israel and Palestinian armed groups. This year has witnessed a significant deterioration in the humanitarian situation in the occupied Palestinian territory (oPt), marked by the most serious escalation of hostilities between Palestinian armed factions in the Gaza Strip and Israel since 2014. In addition to the high human toll of 260 Palestinians killed and almost Palestinians injured, the 11-day conflict in May aggravated Gaza’s chronic shelter, infrastructure and power deficits. At its height, there were 113,000 internally displaced people (IDPs) of whom about 8,250 remain displaced. Although the ceasefire is largely holding, the main factors driving the fragile humanitarian situation in Gaza are still in place, primarily the blockade, imposed by Israel, citing security concerns, which impedes the access and movement of person and goods into and out of Gaza, the implementation of infrastructure projects and delays economic recovery. May 2021 also witnessed extensive clashes between Palestinians and Israeli security forces in the West Bank, focused around the threat of eviction of Palestinian families and access restrictions in the Old City. Overall, 2021 has witnessed a significant increase in conflict-related Palestinian casualties in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, and in settler-related violence against Palestinians and their property. The restrictive and discriminatory planning regime applied in Area C and in East Jerusalem continues to prevent Palestinians from addressing basic housing, livelihood and service needs: there has been a significant increase in demolitions and seizures of Palestinian structures on the grounds of a lack of Israeli-required building permits, marked most prominently by multiple mass demolitions of residences and livelihood structures in the Jordan Valley community of Humsa al Bqai’a. Demolitions, evictions and settler violence are some of the ongoing occupation-driven policies and practices, many linked to the presence of Israeli settlements, which intensifies the coercive environment on vulnerable communities in Area C, East Jerusalem and the Israeli-controlled part of Hebron city, The Covid-19 pandemic has intensified, with Gaza experiencing its third and the West Banks its fourth wave, overburdening the already-stretched healthcare system and aggravating socioeconomic conditions across the oPt. The intra-Palestinian divide between Hamas and the Fatah-led Palestinian Authority (PA) remains unresolved, deepening territorial fragmentation and reducing the capacity of local institutions in Gaza to deliver basic services to the population. The PA’s cancellation of planned Palestinian Legislative Council elections, which had been scheduled for May,1 has deepened this divide, while Palestinian security forces crackdown on domestic protests In the West Bank, following the killing of a prominent Palestinian critic of the PA during his arrest in June, has raised serious concerns of human rights abuses.2 The Israeli authorities continue to impose physical and administrative restrictions on humanitarian programmes, including constraints on the delivery of materials needed for humanitarian projects and limitations on the implementation of projects that involve building, expanding or rehabilitating infrastructure in the Gaza Strip, Area C, the H2 area of Hebron and in East Jerusalem. Humanitarian operations in Gaza are also increasingly impeded by restrictions imposed by Hamas. These limits on operating space are accompanied by unprecedented shortfalls in funding, which are generating an increasingly constrained operational context, hampering their ability to provide assistance and protection to Palestinians throughout the oPt.

2 - Activities

OCHA plays a number of critical roles in the oPt humanitarian context which are unique ascompared to other OCHA country oces globally. Within the politically-charged context ofthe oPt, OCHA plays an authoritative role in ensuring an unbiased common situationalawareness of the humanitarian needs – and the drivers of those needs. OCHA’s work inthis regard is widely recognized and relied upon by other UN agencies and oces, byMember States in country and at the capital level, and by operational humanitarian actors.To achieve this common situational awareness, OCHA provides thorough informationcollection, analysis, management, and reporting services. Information is triangulated andanalyzed in detail by OCHA staff, and further verified through OCHA’s extensive networkwith operational partners, Israeli and Palestinian authority counterparts, civil society andaffected people, to ensure accuracy and to mitigate for bias. Information is then conveyedthrough factual reporting, operational coordination to ensure there is timely and evidence- based emergency response, as well as coordinated humanitarian advocacy. There is alsodeep demand for OCHA’s coordination of operational interventions which is based on asound evidence base resulting from strong eld presence and robust IM capabilities. OCHAplays the key role in convening the humanitarian community around strategic emergencyreadiness activities for macro-emergencies, as well as in the development of localizedemergency response plans for micro-emergencies arising largely from protectionviolations, affecting individual communities at risk in the West Bank, including EastJerusalem. OCHA supports the development of a prioritized humanitarian responsestrategy, enabling six clusters, and more than 100 operational partners to deliver targetedassistance and protection to the most vulnerable across the oPt and to ensure effectivemonitoring and reporting on the national humanitarian response strategy. OCHA supportsthe HC in mobilizing system-wide funding and in ensuring pooled funds to achieve thegreatest results for the highest number of people possible. 10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The ‘Do No Harm’ approach is mainstreamed in all aspects of OCHA’s work. Data and information collected from communities is done so with consent and a clear description of how the information will be used. Community consent, in coordination with the Protection Cluster, is obtained prior to organizing any advocacy (be it through the HCT AWG, OCHA senior staff, the ICCG, etc.) potentially impacting communities, be in public advocacy or diplomatic visits to affected communities. Field visits are organized to ensure a diverse spectrum of persons speak. Databases are built to ensure that sensitive information (phone numbers, ID numbers, details of incidents, etc.) are stored in a secure manner. Coordination between Clusters also takes place to ensure that advocacy advised by one Cluster (e.g. protection) does not place at risk the assistance provided by another to communities, or if it does, informed consent to proceed is obtained. OCHA also ensures that AAP , inc. PSEA, is mainstreamed throughout the HPC, including in development of the HNO and HRP.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The Humanitarian Programme Cycle, coordinated by OCHA through the project,ensures the ecient delivery of humanitarian aid in line with humanitarian principles,including impartiality. OCHA ensures that sound needs assessments form the basis ofthe HNO and HRP , with attention to sex and age disaggregated data that informsanalysis on the different needs of girls, boys, women, and men, as well as persons withdisability and the elderly. OCHA ensures Cluster agreement on priority vulnerablegroups to be targeted with humanitarian responses. OCHA manages a number ofcritical data sets that ensure that the humanitarian community has access to factbasenecessary to ensure a needs-based response (e.g. Gaza early warning indicators, Gazacrossings, Gaza electricity tracker, West Bank demolitions database, etc.) OCHA alsomanages the 5Ws database that contributes to monitoring the equal and impartialdelivery of assistance.
