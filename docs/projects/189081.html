Project Summary

As of the Humanitarian needs overview of 2021, Aweil East and Aweil North Counties of Northern Bahr El Ghazal have been highlighted as some of the priority counties with multi-sectoral needs education included. in CEDS is proposing to carry out an education in emergency project in these counties (Aweil East and Aweil North) aimed at improving access to education for children of school going age in these counties. This project targets to reach a total of 4,404 beneficiaries including 4,226 children of school going age (2,286 boys, 1,940 girls) and 178 adults (112 males and 66 males). This project will primarily targets to reach in and out of school children of school going age, the teacher, the school management committees and the PTA's as well as key education stakeholders within the communities. The project proposes to carry out construction and or rehabilitation of temporary learning spaces, facilitate the capacity building of teachers, school management committees and PTA, facilitate access to water sanitation and hygiene through rehabilitation of latrines, provision of hand washing facilities and distribution of WASH NFI's including MHM kits as well as facilitating back to learning campaigns throughout community based initiatives and activities. CEDS will intentionally integrate GBV prevention efforts including capacity building and awareness as well as promoting accountability ti affected population initiatives specifically timely sharing of information with the targeted communities, enhancing community participation and consultation as well as collecting and acting on complaints and feed back from community members and targeted beneficiaries including school going children. These activities are envisioned to contribute to increased enrolment and retention of children in school as well as facilitating of strengthening of teachers capacities to support education of the children and schools managements to properly manage the schools.

1 - Needs

Since the conflict broke out in South Sudan in December 2013, the nation has been plagued by a myriad of security and economic challenges that have adversely affected education. However, the standard of the education sector was already extremely low even before the outbreak of civil war in 2013. (NRC 2018 Publication). To date, the education sector in South Sudan is still plagued with significant challenges. Among these challenges include: high nationwide insecurity; limited teaching and learning materials; limited accessibility to the schools and remoteness; poor school infrastructure; school closures; the interruption of education days during the school year; limited availability of WASH facilities and soap in schools, low enrolment and attendance rates; and high drop-out rates, and in some cases, inadequate support from the government, the community, church and private individuals (Education Cluster Assessment South Sudan October 2018)The desk-based review study of the OoSC for South Sudan by UNESCO in 2018, found out that an estimated 2.2 million children in South Sudan are out of school. It further projects that if fit-for-purpose and targeted interventions are not put in place by 2020, the number will reach 2.4 million. If these estimates reflect reality on the ground, it is an indication that above 50% of the school age children are out of school and that the Republic of South Sudan may be next to none on the world stage.As of the Humanitarian needs overview of 2022 (SS HNO 2022), an estimated 3.6 million girls and boys between ages 3 and 17 in conflict- and crisis-affected areas of the country are estimated to have inadequate or no access to education in 2022, including more than 125,000 teachers and members of school management committees requiring humanitarian assistance. Additionally, about 139,000 refugee children will need education support in this same year. Increasing climate change, food insecurity and localized conflict additionally exacerbate the existing barriers, including inadequate school infrastructure, costs associated with accessing quality education, untrained education personnel, and irregular and insufficient teacher payments. Furthermore, distance to school, inadequate learning spaces, lack of school supplies and untrained teachers are other barriers to learners accessing education. Furthermore, fees associated with education, such as school contributions, volunteer teacher payments, uniforms and examination fees, are some of the most significant barriers to regular school attendance and drop-out. The barriers to education are more amplified in girls as compared to boys. For girls, early and forced marriage, and pregnancy are some the main reasons for school drop-out. During school closures, evidence suggests daughters were married to alleviate household economic stress through the “bride price”. Children with Disabilities (CwDs) also face numerous challenges in accessing quality education. The status of the school infrastructure in Northern Bahr El Ghazal is not any different from the general picture of the country with majority of schools in the periphery of the state lacking classrooms where students can study and learning in such schools take place under trees and are prone to interference by the elements of weather and contributes to low school enrolment and attendance.

2 - Project activities

Construction and rehabilitation of temporary learning spaces (TLS's) - CEDS will Construction new TLS's and also rehabilitate existing TLS's both in Aweil East and Aweil North. CEDS will ensure that climate change resilience is taken into consideration in both the constructions and rehabilitations. Facilitate storage and distribution of learning materials - Since CEDS is currently hosting the education materials for NBeG state on behalf of the state government, We will continue to facilitate the storage of the education materials and will facilitate the distribution of these education materials to the targeted schools. Training of school management committee and other education stakeholders - CEDS will facilitate the training of SMC', students leadership and other key community on education stakeholders on key aspects including psycho social support, schools management, complaints and feedback management, basic maintenance of schools structures as well as disaster reduction. Promoting access of water sanitation and hygiene in schools - CEDS will facilitate contraction and rehabilitation of sanitation facilities in schools alongside provision of hand washing facilities and other WASH NFI's including soap and MHM kits for girls in schools. Organising and facilitating back to learning campaigns - CEDS will failitate and support the key community stakeholders to facilitate community based back to learning campaigns. this will include but not limited to radio sessions, back to learning community events as well as village to village and houses to house initiatives.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

On a monthly basis, the collected beneficiaries feedback will be analyses and shared with the programmes team for further action. The project implementation team will develop feedback actioning tool to ensure that the feedback collected from the beneficiaries and the community members are not only actioned and acted up but the project is routinely improved and adjusted based on the feedback. on a quarterly basis, the project review meeting will be organised in which the project implementation team and the organisations management will review the feedback from the community and beneficiaries and how these feedback has been used to improve the projects. In the course of the project implementation, CEDS will organise for a lessons learn event in which the feedback collected from the beneficiaries will be incorporate as part of the learning, documented and used not only to improve the ongoing project but as well for future programming.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CEDS will ensure that the most vulnerable and hard to reach member of the community members are able to give their feedback which is acted on, response given and the feedback utilised for continuous project improvements. as part of the feedback committees, CEDS has and will continue to ensure that there are representative of women, persons living with disability as well as other special groups. CEDS has community feedback volunteers who are able to reach the hardest to reach members of the communities to collect thier feedback and ensure that these feedback are acted on. to ensure that both literate and non literate members of the community members are reached, multichannel feedback mechanisms including community meetings, open and closed community feedback forums, direct feedback to project staff as well as community leaders will be utilised.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Through the already existing feedback channels, CEDS will ensure that the information about the organisations PSEA initiatives are provided to the community members and the project beneficiaries. CEDS will organise and facilitate training for this proposed project staff on PSEA and ensure that this staff are aware of and share with the community members on the organisations PSEA efforts. CEDS will train the school PTA's and the pupils leadership about the organisations PSEA guidelines and ensure that they are aware of the organisations PSEA commitments and are also aware of channels or reporting PSEA concerns. Through community meeting, community leaders, and other avenues as pre distribution meetings, CEDS will utilise the opportunity to inform the community members as well as the beneficiaries about the organisations PSEA commitments and channels available to report suspicion or actual PSEA violation cases.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Guided the the organisational accountability framework, CEDS will utilise multichannel to provide information to the targeted beneficiaries and the community members about this project. Through community leaders, the schools PTA's, the pupils leaders, community meeting, open forums with children, community members as well as parents, CEDS will ensure that all the community members including the hard to reach are reached with the information about this project. CEDS will incorporate the information about this project to the already existing community information sharing checklist and ensure that the project staff and other key stakeholders are provided with this information.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

CEDS has in place a Zero tolerance PSEA policy to guide the action of personal and all the organisations stakeholders in relation to PSEA. CEDS will continue to capacity build staff and stakeholders on this policy to ensure that they are aware of the policy and adhere to it. in the onboarding process of the new person, stakeholder or contractors, CEDS will carry out awareness session on the policy and ensure that they consent to adherence to the same before engagement or direct interaction with the beneficiaries. CEDS has ensured that the PSEA policy is publicly displayed in the office with the channels on how the suspicion of the same can be reported to enhance the ability of all the stakeholders to report the same. At community level, CEDS do and will continue to create awareness of the organisations zero tolerance to SEA in community meeting, through community stakeholders and key informants to ensure that they are aware of and report on suspicion and actual cases of PSEA. CEDS has in place feedback mechanisms at community level and will continue to encourage community members to report suspicion of SEA through the community based feedback mechanisms. CEDS will ensure strict adherence to the organisations AAP framework to ensure that community members including school going children participate and consulted throughout this project. to mitigate the risk of non participation, the AAP coordinator will ensure that community consultation and participation meetings are integrated as part of the routine project activities carried out by this project. CEDS will continue to promote the usage of the current feedback mechanisms established at the community level to collect and act on the community feedback but to also continue to improve these mechanisms to meet the contextualised needs of the community members. CEDS has in place data management protocols that details the data management procedures in custody of the organisation including the beneficiaries information. CEDS will ensure that this protocols are continued to be adhered to and informed consent is sought before collection and release of data and information. CEDS will ensure that the data in custody of the organisation is well protected in lockable cabinets, with only access to authorised officers and are back up in an alternative location as a risk management procedure.
