Project Summary

Gender Based Violence (GBV) remains a major protection concern among women and girls across the Country. The proposed intervention seeks to ensure access to timely and safe services for GBV survivors which include lifesaving, GBV case management and psychosocial support (PSS) services at the women and girls safe spaces. In particular, IRC seeks to continuously determine barriers to access of GBV services through development of functional referral pathways, continuous consultation with the women, girls, men and boys through focus group discussions (FGDs) to ensure such barriers are addressed through collaborative advocacy with different sectors (health, protection, WASH and livelihoods). Community engagement through information dissemination and capacity development will be conducted utilizing the program adaptations for COVID-19 to ensure that beneficiaries and staff adhere to the measures put in place. In addition, regular inter-sectoral safety audits will be conducted in the project locations to determine emerging GBV risks and mitigation initiatives developed through community participation aimed at creating safer environments for women and girls. IRC will continue to work closely with humanitarian and local service providers to mainstream GBV response and prevention into emergency humanitarian services and strengthen the capacity of the community to support and protect women and girls. IRC also seeks to utilize its field tested unique and innovative programming curriculums such as engaging men through accountable practices (EMAP), Girl Shine and EA$E), aimed at addressing the complex needs of women and girls in the proposed project locations.

1 - Needs

Violence, abuse and exploitation is still the greatest protection risks for women and girls reflecting on the continued gender inequalities and discrimination across most communities. The humanitarian needs overview (HNO) 2020 indicates that armed conflict, sub-national organized violence, COVID-19 and flooding have additionally exacerbated the already extreme levels of GBV with over 6,000 GBV incidents recorded between January and September 2020. The GBVIMS statistics (2020) further indicate that 97% of the reported GBV incidents survivors were females (women and girls) while a fifth of survivors were less than 18 years old with intimate partners accounting for more than half of the alleged perpetrators. Access to GBV services across most of the communities remain a major challenge owing to the limited availability of the services. The global COVID-19 pandemic has further caused devastating socio-economic impact on people, including severe economic contractions, spikes in prices of basic commodities, loss of livelihoods, increased protection risks, and disrupted access to basic services. Measures adopted to curb the spread of COVID-19 have also contributed to a rise in GBV incidents. A multi-sector needs assessment conducted by the IRC in December 2020 covering Koch and Rubkona Counties indicated that GBV is more prevalent within the households and mostly perpetrated by intimate partners. In addition, majority of the community members who participated in the key informant interviews and FGD acknowledged that the prevalence of GBV was high in their communities as they mentioned to have witness GBV related incidents perpetrated almost on a daily basis. This is largely attributed to the deep rooted cultural beliefs, attitudes and perception about women and girls that contribute significantly to unequal power dynamic among men and women, hence resulting to normalizing violence against women. In addition, fear of retaliation through revenge killings was mentioned to be a major barrier in accessing the available services by survivors. Across the four counties the GBV situation has further been exacerbated by the insecurity situation, flooding and COVID-19 pandemic. Closure of schools which serve as a safe havens for children have impacted significantly on the well-being of adolescent girls who have been subjected to early marriage in addition to other forms of violence while boys have been exposed to crime and other forms of exploitation in the communities. The increased risks, coupled with gaps in GBV service provision, necessitate the urgent need for strengthened GBV prevention, risk mitigation and response services. Therefore IRC will scale up its current GBV programming in the proposed areas through incorporation of the community engagement activities to strengthen the community based mechanisms in prevention and response to GBV.

2 - Project activities

The project aims at providing GBV interventions which include both the response services and prevention activities in Koch, Rubkona, Aweil East and Aweil South Counties targeting 26,000 (13,000W, 8000G, 2,500M, and 2,500B) beneficiaries from the IDPs and host community. Through this project, IRC will scale up and promote access to lifesaving GBV services through the following interventions:1. Case management and PSS.Provision of comprehensive case management, referrals, individual and group PSS services at the existing women's and girls’ friendly spaces (WGFS) across the four counties. The intervention will emphasize on the provision of and access to holistic survivor-centered services through strengthening of the referral pathways to ensure effective collaboration with service providers so that GBV survivors who include women, girls, men and boys receive appropriate care in response to their needs, and in line with global best practices as well as the GBV minimum standards. The IRC will continue to use the existing WGFS to host age appropriate group psychosocial and skills-building activities, weekly information sessions and recreational activities to promote healing, strengthen social cohesion and support networks for women and girls from both the host community and IDPs. Given the COVID-19 pandemic, program adaptions developed in 2020 at the onset of the pandemic will be implemented to ensure that beneficiaries accessing the services and the staff adhere to the measures such as observing social distance, wearing the face masks, washing hands and are well informed of the COVID-19 messaging. 2. Adolescent girls engagements.With the closure of schools, the vulnerability of adolescent girls has significantly increased and therefore, IRC aims at engaging the girls through age-appropriate services including information sessions on reproductive health done collaboratively with the health program and life skills using the Girl Shine curriculum which will be contextualized to address the prevailing needs of the girls in each project sites. While Adolescent boys will be referred to the IRC child protection teams for a more comprehensive tailored support. The sessions are meant to mitigate their vulnerability to multiple forms of GBV and offer support systems and access to information. This will be pivotal in reducing women's and girls’ isolation and boosting their self-confidence. 3. Community engagement activities through information sharing.Cognizant of the contextual gender dynamics, women, girls, men and boys in the community will be engaged through GBV information sharing and awareness raising sessions done through the local media and utilization of megaphones during the outreaches. In addition, EMAP sessions targeting the women and men will be carried out to address social norms that influence the perpetration of violence and build a community that holds each other accountable for their actions. 4. Economic empowermentTo promote resilience and enhance economic well-being of the community, women will be consulted and supported with livelihood interventions through the utilization of IRC ERD model (EA$E) to improve access to food and basic needs including hygiene and sanitary items and reduce vulnerability. 5. Safety Audits.The IRC will work to mitigate and respond to the needs of women, girls, men and boys affected by conflict by reducing GBV risks through frequent joint community-based safety audit assessments, address risks identified, and strengthen community-based support mechanisms, creation of safe and meaningful access to relevant services, and advocacy with other humanitarian actors on GBV mainstreaming. 6. Provision of dignity kitsThe IRC will also provide material support such as dignity kits modified to include hygiene commodities to curb the spread of COVID-19 targeting the most vulnerable women and girls.

3 - Project indicators

At the project design, indicators to measure the project success will be identified together with the targets. Data resulting from the project implementation will be routinely collected, analyzed and compared to the targets set during the project design. WPE program will continue utilizing available monitoring tools including GBVMS to record and store GBV survivors data, psychosocial functioning tool to monitor and evaluate survivors psychological wellbeing, WGSS participation, satisfaction, and knowledge survey to assess women’s and girls’ experience in WGSS, EMAP and Girl Shine monitoring tools to evaluate knowledge, behaviors and altitude of participants before, during and after engagement in the respective interventions. Senior WPE manager will provide technical support to the field teams on using these tools while the WPE manager will do regular follow-up to ensure the tools are consistently used and data is recorded in the right database. Analysis of data will also be done on regular bases to inform the program on progress towards the milestones, adjust implementation and reporting to donors, beneficiaries and other stakeholders. In addition to that, indicator tracking table will be developed to ensure indicator’s progress is updated in a monthly basis. Similar to that, the monitoring and evaluation work plan will be developed to keep the project team well informed on the time-frame for conducting the activities contributing to indicator success. 7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

IRC will strictly adhere to the protection principles throughout the project period across the proposed locations. All project staff will be trained on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) messaging will be incorporated in the community awareness raising activities to ensure the community members are aware of what PSEA entails, the available reporting mechanisms and the importance of reporting. IRC will build the capacity of PSEA networks or focal point (s) who will be included in the community sensitization activities to ensure that beneficiaries are well informed of existing reporting mechanisms. IEC materials with PSEA messages will be developed, translated into the local language, and distributed to the communities.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Utilizing the Women and Girls Friendly Spaces, monthly FGDs and listening sessions will be held to collect feedback from the women and girls in regards to the project and IRC services in general. This sessions will also serve as avenues to provide response on the feedback received from the previous sessions.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

IRC has established multi-sector community feedback and response mechanisms (CFRM) in all its operational sites including the proposed project locations through which the beneficiaries will be informed on their rights to launch complaints or give other forms of feedback in regard to the implementation process and the quality of services they are receiving from the IRC. The existing complaint and feedback mechanism will be used during the project period with beneficiaries being sensitized on beneficiary accountability standards. Communities will also be informed about the focal point in the IRC to handle any compliant report and feedback. IRC will explain its procedures, decisions from the information and the feedback obtained from the beneficiaries to allow women, girls, men and boys to hold IRC accountable.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The feedback will inform the proposed project activities to ensure that the project responses to the community needs for women, girls, men and boys. In addition, the feedback will be incorporated for future projects if the available resources are not sufficient enough to address the identified needs.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

IRC will continue to strictly adhere to the protection principles throughout the project period across the proposed locations. PSEA messaging will be incorporated in the community awareness raising activities to ensure the community members are aware of PSEA which includes the available reporting mechanisms and the importance of reporting. The humanitarian workers through the various coordination forums will be reminded of the protection principles to ensure that their interventions are not causing more harm to the beneficiaries. IRC will scale-up the community sensitization activities to ensure communities are well versed with the available GBV services and in addition, enhance their participation in developing the community referral pathways from the onset of the project. The existing complaint and feedback mechanism will be used during the project period with beneficiaries being sensitized on beneficiary accountability standards. Communities will also be informed about the focal point in the IRC to handle any compliant report and feedback. IRC will explain its procedures, decisions from the information and the feedback obtained from the beneficiaries to allow women, girls, men and boys to hold IRC accountable.
