Project Summary

“• Since late 2017, long-running tensions in Cameroon’s North-West and South-West regions have escalated, and the crisis has increasingly shifted into armed conflict., • The humanitarian situation has fast deteriorated, 1.3 million people are in need of assistance., • Insecurity has forced more than 430,000 people to flee their homes. More than 380,000 people need shelter, and some 418,000 people NFI assistance., • An estimated 3,000 children suffering from life-threatening severe acute malnutrition (SAM) require urgent treatment., • There is a high risk of a rapid increase in new HIV infections due to the interruption of ARV medication., • About 3,700 unaccompanied or separated children need urgent psycho-social care and reunification support. • Access for humanitarian actors to reach the most vulnerable, and for affected population to needed services, remains difficult. And Humanitarian partners provided shelter assistance to 23,635 people, and 100,150 benefited from NFI distribution” Source: Cameroon: North-West and South-West Crisis, Situation Report N°3 as of 31 January 2019.
CHRAPA aims at implementing this project with great focus ensuring that people affected by displacement due to this crisis are provided with emergency individual/community shelter provisions in the NW Region Cameroon and they can live in dignity; have their rights respected; and access participatory, equitable and quality services that build upon their resilience through all phases of their displacement until a durable solution is achieved.
Within this context, CHRAPA’s innovative and sustainable strategy is to ensure that, sustainable coping mechanisms are established at village or chiefdom levels/as per the coverage of each religious scope. This strategy covers the aspirations and perspectives of all stakeholders to the crisis that is the government and non-state armed groups (NSTAGs), including lessons learned during the implementation of cluster shelter activities within Boyo, Menchum and Momo Divisions of the NW Regions with all challenges affecting shelter situations within communities taken into consideration. CHRAPA intends to set up sustainable community shelter provisions generally accepted safe spaces that is churches ( Baptist, Catholic, Presbyterian, and other credible Pentecostal churches), traditional institutions like palaces, community institutions generally considered safe by all stakeholders to the crisis.
GREAT NEED FOR A COMMUNITY/INDIVIDUAL EMERGENCY SHELTER SCHEME:
CHRAPA will be with its grassroots access and experience during this crisis period proposes as a best practice to establish emergency community/individual shelter schemes. This very innovative strategy permits the establishment of community coping and sustainable schemes to cope with internally displaced persons perpertually, while awaiting a solution to the crisis. That is enabling host communities, often at sub divisional headquarters to be equipped with systems of receiving and supporting IDPs regularly without external assistance as the crisis might deteriorate/improve. This project intends to use its access to various crisis affected communities in Boyo, Menchum and Momo Divisions.
Within the context of the strategy already very successfully piloted in Fundong and Mbengwi Sub Divisions of the Boyo and Momo Divisions of the NW Regions sponsored by the UN Women and UNDP. We successfully realized selected safe spaces in various traditional palaces or council areas, trusted religious institutions and trusted IDP host regular arenas/host families are the most reliable areas to provide sustainable community shelter provisions (Temporal Lodging Tarpaulin, Mosquitoes nets, mads, blankets, matrass, Bucket, pots, wooden spoon, bowls, plates, spoons, Jar, 5 cups, tablets of savons etc) at the community level where IDPs can use and while they are settled other new sets of IDPs still continually use them and the this strategy also integrates individual shelter support .
