Project Summary

In response to the implications of the spiraling multi-faceted crises on the health sector which include the shortage of medications and medical supplies, and the exponential increase in prices due to the removal of subsidies, Anera plans to support more than 205,070 (54% females and 46% males) vulnerable Lebanese, migrants and Palestinian refugee patients with the provision of a comprehensive package of chronic medications, hygiene material, and medical supplies as needed (sutures, blades, syringes, insulin needles, gowns, masks, gloves, etc.). The disaggregation by age group of the beneficiaries is as follows: Below 5 years old (1%); 6-18 years old (1%);19-49 years old (21%); 50-59 years old (40%); Above 60 years old (37%). To ensure the provision of an improved service that accounts for all beneficiaries from different backgrounds and cultures at the healthcare facilities, Anera plans to provide primary healthcare centers' staff with the needed training on topics including: cultural sensitivity (inclusion, barriers to care, provider-patient communication) along with workshops on wellbeing,, and establishing strong PSEA and child safeguarding systems, and warehouse and logistics. Anera will use different outreach approaches to reach out to target beneficiaries from different nationalities. Anera will highly coordinate with the Ministry of Social Affairs (MoSA) to reach out to the most vulnerable Lebanese targets at a national level. Anera will also coordinate with IOM and Migrant Community Centers to identify vulnerable migrants. Anera, which has extensively responded to the health needs of patients in Lebanon during 2022 through securing more than 20 shipments of medications and supplies, will ensure the delivery of an average of 10 shipments throughout 2023. The procurement of the medications and medical supplies will be following international procurement from authorized suppliers. Anera will expand the pool of vendors to ensure finding the needed variety of medications and supplies requested by HCCs, while following all national policies to ensure the shipment and smooth clearance of permitted medications. Following the clearance, Anera will transport the medicines from customs to a designated area in its certified distribution centers. The procurement of Medications is done through a subaward agreement with our partner in the USA. below are the detailed steps for the procurement process:
1- we assess the need locally and send the needs list to our sub-awardee
2- Sub-awardee send RFQ for their list of vendors to get quotations and do bid analysis
3- Sub-awardee sends Anera a consolidated analysis for Anera's review
4- Anera goes over the items and approve the requested
5- Sub-awardee sends POs based on Anera's approval
6- Sub-awardees shares shipping documents
7- clearance process takes place
Anera follows the Guidelines on GSDP of Pharmaceutical Products in Lebanon, which align with the Order of Pharmacists guidelines. Anera will select a total of 60 HCCs, vetted against the OFAC and SAM USG vetting systems. Anera’s selection of health facilities based on: MoPH license, compliance with GSDP standards, religious and political neutrality, the unavailability of other chronic medication programs at the facility, the demographics of the community they serve (high concentration of Lebanese and Migrants), the availability of infrastructure and qualified staff including legal pharmacists, and the capacity to operate 5-6 days per week.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Anera's extensive experience in the implementation of health programs in Lebanon, has allowed the identification of gaps in the operation of PHCs. Thus, Anera through its experienced health teams will provide 75 staff (Admin Staff) and 125 medical staff from 60 PHCs with capacity building and awareness sessions, on wellbeing, cultural sensitivity, establishing strong PSEA and child safeguarding systems, and logistics and warehousing. The outreach and enrollment of the beneficiaries will occur through Anera’s reliance on its connections with Public hospitals, Karantina Warehouse, partners in Palestinian camps, Ministry of social affairs (MoSA), and Migrant Community Centers. Anera will use its well-developed vulnerability assessment tool to select the most vulnerable beneficiaries. Anera's multidisciplinary team of public health workers, health professionals and communication experts, will implement several health promotion initiatives including health messaging on a wide variety of health issues targeting 40,000 beneficiaries (54% females and 46% males) all over Lebanon.These health messages will be inclusive while taking into consideration beneficiaries’ cultural differences, language barriers, and communication style. Anera will distribute acute, chronic, and cancer medications and adult diapers to more than 205,070 beneficiaries. Anera will procure and deliver hygiene kits including menstrual items to 5,000 Lebanese and Palestinian girls and women.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

The targeted PHCs will receive training on detecting potential protection cases, providing psychosocial first aid, and referring cases for available services. Anera will prioritize PSEA and child protection as mandatory training themes for selected partners. Anera will work with professionals who have experience within the PHC context and are well-equipped to support PHC staff with the necessary tools to manage the stressors that come with their profession. This is part of Anera’s dedication to empowering PHCs and enhancing their resilience. Additionally, Anera uses a Referral Information Management System (RIMS) for protection referrals and notifies inter-agency referral focal points immediately of any protection cases as a matter of priority. Anera has proven PSEA and child protection policies that are compliant with UNHCR and UNICEF requirements and will require that all partners in this program, including clinics, sign its Code of Conduct and adhere to anti-harassment policies.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Anera accepts high quality shipments that are compatible with the needs of populations based on HCCs lists. Once received Anera has effective monitoring to ensure the beneficiaries receive quality services. Recipients undergo a certain vetting process prior to distribution of donations:1.OFAC’s port of sanction list 2.System for Award Management (SAM) 3.Assessment sheets.Tools & Monitoring Visits: A- Assessment sheet: Filled out after approaching or being approached by a potential partner, including: administrative details, type of centers, # of centers, the nationalities of their end beneficiaries, the partners’ vision & services provided. Monitoring Visits/year & Survey: After the distribution of a donation, partners are visited by a medical donations (MD) Team representative. During the visit, Anera checks handling/storage/dispensing, handling/storage practices. Beneficiaries submit their feedback through satisfaction surveys, HCC monitoring visits and inventory checks, & hotline.
