Project Summary

The project aims to provide improved access to nutrition services to the most vulnerable population in particular women and children U5. It includes the increase of the capacities of HCF in the provision of Integrated Management and Prevention of Acute malnutrition and to support community-led nutrition specific and sensitive activities.

1 - Activities

One HCF per district will be rehabilitated and equipped with IPC minimum standard required material (1.1).This number of facilities can be increased according to the needs and through specific WASH interventions led by ACF and its implementing partners. 1080 staff of the selected HCF (4 nurses and nurse aids *30 wards * 9 district) will be trained on Integrated Management of Acute malnutrition and Covid19 (1.2). In the 9 districts, a support will be provided to the supply and management of the essential nutrition commodities for prevention and treatment (1.3). Nutrition data management will also be supported (1.4). An integrated approach of sensitization on maternal and neonatal health, nutrition and prevention of COVID 19 will be implemented. Each entry point will be used to ensure nutrition messaging and screening (such as ante and post-natal consultations or gender/age specific groups of discussions) (1.5). A support will be provided to community outreach health and nutrition services in hard to reach communities in rural areas (1.6). A minimum of six (6) outreach activities per year will be supported in every 30 wards of the 9 districts. At community level, 2.1) sensitization on good nutrition and food practices will be ensured through mass sensitization during food and cash distributions held by ACF and NAZ through their FSL projects (can be extended to other cash interventions in the area) and through community groups discussions. Alternative means of communication will be identified while the COVID-19 pandemic persists to ensure compliance with relevant protocol. 2.2) Breast feeding community groups will be put in place to improve practices with the support of community members in all wards of the 9 districts. 2.3) 2700 Community health workers (300 per district) will be capacitated to provide IYCF-E counselling and support services to Mothers and Care giver of children Under 5. 2.4) and will be trained on screening 2.5) Screening will be ensured through family-led MUAC and community health workers for early referral to HCF. Per district, 1200 mothers and caregivers will be trained and supervised by the 300 CHW. 2.6) Malnutrition trends will be closely monitored such as the management of cases through community health workers.

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

The project will be implemented in areas where ACF and its implementing partners have already worked closely within communities through multisectorial approaches including nutrition activities in HCF and at community level. Key government and community stakeholders have been widely consulted in the context of ACF’s current projects in particular through on going monitoring mechanisms such as baseline, endline assessments featuring Focus Group Discussions and Key Informant Interviews. Discussions with provincial and district authorities, local leaders and gender and age specific groups led to define priorities and the current project address the main needs. The project will ensure AAP through: The direct participation of communities in the selection of beneficiaries through community committees. It ensures a community ownership of the intervention,sustainability of the outcomes and AAP in the prioritization of the affected population and their needs.  A community based approach focusing on health care facilities and community-led nutrition sensitive activities through CHW capacitated and family-led MUAC screening. It ensures that the Community is at the center of the intervention. Training of CHW and family members will ensure to have the community as the main actor of the intervention and being capacitated to improve their resilience to future shocks Existing feedback and complaints mechanisms such a toll free line and suggestion boxes which will allow to receive all complaints and ensure to address them in a limited timeline, providing feedbacks to the beneficiaries on the solution provided. The toll free number is usually preferred by the communities. But the mechanism should be chosen by the communities themselves according to their preferences. The tools will be adjusted to fit to the expectations and use of the project communities. All communications, sensitizations, feedbacks provided and discussions will be translated or held directly in local language to ensure the understanding and accountability to all communities

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The environmental impact of each activity will be considered in project design phase to minimize negative impact. This includes ensuring adequate disposal systems are in place for medical (or other) waste produced as a result of the project. Minimising unnecessary movement of vehciles and personnel and, where feasible, making use of remote monitoring mechanisms that have already been put in place as as result of COVID-19 for example.
