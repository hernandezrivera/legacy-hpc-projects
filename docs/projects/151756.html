Project Summary

In the 2019 HRP project for health, IOM will increase access and utilization of primary healthcare services among the most vulnerable and crisis-affected populations in the country. IOM will operate 27 static health facilities with integrated community outreach, as well as 4 mobile medical teams; many of the facilities are in areas that are hard-to-reach, remote, conflict-affected and isolated, lack other health actors, and where the population does not have access to basic life-saving health services. IOM will provide comprehensive primary healthcare services, continuously build the capacity of health workers to ensure quality of services, and preposition medicines and medical supplies in regional hubs to ensure continuous supply to all of IOM’s health facilities, as well as to the region at large in the event of a sudden emergency. IOM will upgrade health posts to provide basic emergency obstetric and newborn care (BEMONC) services, particularly in regions where there is no other facility for safe deliveries. To mitigate against recurrent and expected shocks and the effects of continued conflict, IOM will conduct mass vaccination at the entry points to IDP sites, thus increasing immunization coverage and reducing outbreaks of communicable diseases. IOM will utilize health education in communities to effect behavior change in health and hygiene practices that contribute to high excess morbidity and mortality, and to increase demand and utilization of preventative services such as antenatal care, facility delivery, and immunization. IOM will address gaps in mental health care and psychosocial support (MHPSS) through mainstreaming and integration of MHPSS into primary healthcare, coupled with community-level approaches. The project will benefit 511,500 individuals, including 309,313 children under 18 years. The project will be part of IOM’s integrated emergency response program which includes shelter and NFIs, camp coordination and management, WASH, and displacement tracking and monitoring.

Needs

There are 2.6 million IDPs in Somalia as a result of continued conflict, drought, flooding, forced evictions, and an influx of returnees. Many IDPs lack access to lifesaving and essential healthcare services meeting minimum quality standards. Low routine immunization coverage (less than 65%) and the effects of massive flooding and Cyclone Sagar, compounded by the country’s poor water and sanitation infrastructure, led to concurrent outbreaks of measles, diarrheal diseases (cholera/AWD) and vaccine-derived polio in 2018.The health system is highly fragmented, marked by weak institutional capacity, inadequate funding, and inequity in access to services. Health facilities, supplies and workforce are limited. The country’s infant mortality rate in Somalia is 137 deaths/1,000 live births. The leading causes of infant and child mortality are preventable and treatable illnesses. The maternal mortality rate is 732 deaths for 100,000 live births. Only 33% of births are attended by skilled health personnel, contributing to high infant and maternal mortality. There are major gaps in management of non-communicable diseases, especially mental health care, where 1 in 3 Somalis suffer from mental illness. Lack of access to routine primary healthcare, malnutrition, and communicable disease outbreaks drive Somalia’s high morbidity and mortality rates. IDPs, especially those displaced by conflict, have lower access to healthcare (JMCNA 2018). Significant numbers of people reside in hard-to-reach areas and are exposed to violence from ongoing conflict. In 2019, IOM will provide critical lifesaving interventions to prevent avoidable morbidities and mortalities among vulnerable and displaced populations in Somaliland, Puntland and south-central Somalia. IOM will support integrated static and mobile clinics and facilitate delivery of emergency medical supplies to hard to reach areas, while addressing gaps driving poor immunization, mental health, and maternal-child health outcomes.

Activities

1. Support 22 static health facilities which deliver front-line primary healthcare services in crisis-affected communities. Establish 8 new static facilities in regions which lack access to primary healthcare services. Deploy 10 mobile rapid response teams in response to acute emergencies and to large, hard-to-reach areas with dispersed populations. 2. Preposition essential medicines, medical supplies, and equipment in 6 strategically located regional hubs which supply all of IOM’s PHC service delivery sites.3. Upgrade 4 current health posts to health centres by providing staff, equipment and infrastructure for provision of Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care in locations lacking maternity and delivery services.4. Conduct training and capacity building for 80 health workers on outbreak response, national treatment guidelines, emergency management, maternal child healthcare, screening and management of malnutrition, travel health, and health promotion strategies.5. Provide health education at health facility and community level to 223,200 individuals to effect behavior change in health and hygiene practices that contribute to high excess morbidity and mortality, and to increase demand for preventative services.6. Conduct training on psychological first aid, MHPSS guidelines, identification of MH symptoms and behaviors, MH treatment guidelines, referral mechanisms, and psychoeducation for 50 primary healthcare workers at 12 static clinics.7. Provide mass vaccination of IDP children arriving at entry points at 6 large IDP to increase immunization coverage among IDP populations. Children missing immunizations will be vaccinated at the entry point to the IDP sites and linked to the nearest health facility for follow-up and completion of immunization series. 8. Support travel health services including provision of health screening, transit healthcare and fitness to travel for emergency movement of 10,000 displaced and IDP returnees.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

This protection environment in this project's context is sufficiently stable to enable durable solutions. This project is linked to government and community systems, as it is implemented in partnership with the Ministry of Health, utilizes Ministry of Health workforce, is based on national development and humanitarian response frameworks, and includes community engagement throughout the project process including accountability to affected populations This project has inbuilt sustainability. Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

In Somalia's context there are inherently risks of exclusion based on displacement. However, this project is largely focused on IDPs, bring services to where IDPs are, involves consultation with the government to ensure that IDPs are fully accessing services as intended, and involves consultations with communities from project inception through implementation. IOM's memorandum of understanding with the Ministry of Health for this project will include provisions specifying that all services provided to IDPs are free of charge, and that no restrictions are to be placed on individuals accessing healthcare based on any other factor.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

There is risk of gender-based violence (GBV) against women and girls, which is prevalent in Somalia especially among displaced and vulnerable populations. In this project, IOM engages community leaders/gatekeepers before and during the project to ensure maximum mitigation measures are put in place, and IOM's health programmes incorporate mobilization and awareness of communities around GBV, training of health providers on screening and referral, and establishment of referral pathways from health facility/community to sources of legal/social assistance as established by the Protection Cluster.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

This project is not implemented directly in areas of armed conflict, so this is not a protection risk for beneficiaries in this project.
