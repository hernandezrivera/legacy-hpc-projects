Project Summary

Since 2011, Libya has been affected by political, security and economic volatility resulting to a governance vacuum, which has created significant security, rule of law, and social and economic consequences.
An estimate of 897,000 people (including internally displaced persons, returnees, non-displaced conflict-affected people, refugees and migrants) are considered to be in need of humanitarian assistance (HNO, 2020). The majority of people in need are found in urban areas of Libya while many of the most severe needs are in the southern Mantikas such as Al Kufrah. Key humanitarian needs reflect life-threatening risks from exposure, vulnerability and inability to cope with human rights violations, conflict, and deprivation of essential services and commodities. Thus, Libyan crisis is largely characterized by major protection issues. Based on its past years of experience, PUI places the protection in a focus of concern and has adjusted its programming to better response to the context’s evolution. To this end, PUI keeps taking concrete steps to reinforce the centrality of protection within its response with the goal to contribute to alleviating the suffering of conflict-affected populations in East of Libya. Specifically, the Action will aim to enhance the protection environment and improve access to essential services. During the 8 months, PUI will effortlessly continue to reach out and assist conflict-affected communities via the development of continuous protection need’s assessments, and the provision of health and protection-based direct assistance through the deployment of 4 outreach teams.
Throughout the Action, PUI will still work closely with authorities and partners to improve local capacities, acceptance and coordination of the intervention. Humanitarian principles will guide the Action to promote safety, dignity and rights of affected people, and mitigate exposure to additional risks through conflict sensitive and do not harm approaches.

1 - Needs

In 2019, Libya has continued to suffer from political, economic and security instability driving to a governance crisis, which will persist and might deteriorate in 2020. Spikes in violence, such as the escalation of conflict in Tripoli since April 2019 and clashes in Murzuq in August 2019, have resulted in increased civilian casualties and further displacement deepening people’s need for protection assistance. Across the country, over 301,000 Libyans remain displaced, including 128,000 people due to the Tripoli conflict while Benghazi remains the main IDP-producer municipality with 27,080 people, reversing the declining trend in displacement. As displacement has increased, so has the number of Libyans who are returning to their homes, around 447,000 people (with Benghazi being the top 1 of the regions with returnees (189,025)). Living conditions, including access to clean drinking water, medical services, and safe housing have all degraded due to the protracted situation in the country, particularly for women and children (Libya, IOM DTM, IDP & Returnee Report, Round 27 (Aug-Oct 2019)). Despite the crisis, Libya remains an attractive destination for migrant workers due to an economy that relies on foreign labor, higher salaries and historical ties, as well as being a transitory route for people seeking opportunities or asylum in Europe. At least 636,426 migrants are in Libya (IOM, Libya’s migrant report 27 –Aug-Sep 2019) and exposed to protection risks, human rights violations, exploitation and abuse. Out of them 10% are women and 7% are children and 22% declared to be unemployed. The majority of identified migrants comes from Libya’s neighbouring countries Niger, Chad, Sudan and Egypt, accounting for almost two-thirds of Libya’s migrant population. Migrants were identified in all 100 municipalities, within 567 communities with 26% of them located in Eastern Libya and 5% in Al Kufrah region (IOM, DTM,LBY MVHNA, Report. Dec2019).The no protective legal environment for migrants combines with the lack of refugee framework, which complicates the situation of non-Libyans that flee from conflict in their countries of origin and could potentially be considered asylum-seekers and/or refugees. Key humanitarian needs in Libya are linked to i) protection, ii) access to critical services such as healthcare and education, and iii) access to basic household goods and commodities including food, safe drinking water, sanitation facilities, and essential non-food items. These humanitarian needs reflect life-threatening risks from exposure, vulnerability and inability to cope with human rights violations and abuses, conflict and violence, and deprivation of essential services and commodities.Protection needs are amongst the most commonly reported needs in Libya for all people affected by the crisis, whether Libyan or foreign nationals. They are primarily driven by exposure to conflict and violence, human rights violations and abuses, contamination from explosive hazards in urban centers, breakdown of the rule of law, and major challenges related to impediments to access critical services and essential goods and commodities. Refugees and migrants face similar protection challenges as Libyans, primarily due to direct exposure to conflict and increased criminality throughout the country. However, they also face additional protection issues because of racism, discrimination, lack of tribal and family support networks, impunity for perpetrators of criminal acts that target them, and their irregular status and poor socio-economic standing in Libya.

4 - Protection risk analysis

Protection risk analysisThe present project does not specifically target certain groups but the most vulnerable victims of the conflict regardless their status and origin living in conflict-affected zones through a vulnerability approach.Beneficiaries will include IDPs, refugees, returnees, host communities, and migrants’ men, women, boys, and girls with protection concerns and particularly deprived of access to critical essential services and to basic commodities due to continuous political instability. To this end, PUI’s action does not select individuals but do identify pockets of vulnerability where to deploy its mobile team based on a protection need's assessment conducted throughout the project via an outreach approach. The most vulnerable communities and therefore areas of intervention will be identified based on systematic protection needs assessments. Within these communities, survivors or at risk of rights violation incidents men, women, girls and boys will be identified by the protection outreach workers, members of PUI’s mobile team. Specific attention will be provided to survivors of (sexual) gender-based violence.

5 - Mitigation measures

Mitigation measuresThe project will conduct protection needs assessment in targeted areas to identify the most at risk communities in order to define where and when the medical staffs and protection staffs will provide direct services. Targeting will be based on evidence gathered during these assessments, and assistance will be provided to the most in need regardless of their status or background (IDPs, migrants, refugees, returnees, and host communities women, men, boys and girls).These assessments will enable PUI teams to identify the protection risks and incidents the targeted communities are exposed to, to better inform PUI’s and other actors’ response in these areas, and to identify individuals survivors or at risk of right violation incidents for enhanced access to services. During these assessments, attention will be provided to specific protection concerns such as gender-based violence, physical and emotional violence, but will also highlight barriers face by women, men, boys, and girls in accessing critical lifesaving services such as primary health care services. When existing, PUI teams will closely collaborate with other actors’ established protection community networks to gather critical information on the protection situation of targeted populations, and to safely and timely identify individuals in need to specific assistance. Protection needs assessments will be conducted directly within the communities using observation, key informant interviews, focus group discussions, and household surveys to draw a comprehensive understanding of the overall protection situation, the specific risks to which communities are exposed, and the barriers faced to access public and humanitarian services. Specific templates and forms will be designed and contextualised following the Protection Cluster and international guidance before the start of the project by PUI Protection team with the support of HQ protection and gender advisor.

6.1 - Other accountability mechanisms

In its Institutional Program Quality Policy, PUI has committed itself to being accountable towards stakeholders linked to the implementation of its humanitarian projects. This commitment is a crucial part of what defines the notion of program’s quality in PUI. Thus, program accountability is based on three main pillars that are participation, information and feedback processing. In the light of this, PUI Libya mission has put in place several mechanisms to ensure accountability to the affected populations at different stages of the project cycle.-At the design phase: the identification of needs is based on a participatory approach combining household surveys, key informant interviews toward national and local authorities and stakeholders, and focus group discussions held within the communities. Additionally, the present Action being a continuity of former requests, has been designed based on beneficiaries feedback-At the inception phase (and continuously): information on the Action is communicated with key stakeholders at various levels to ensure full participation of the targeted beneficiaries through informative leaflets or posters disseminated in key locations, local coordination and information sharing with partners, and communication through social media or radio, etc.-At the implementation phase. Established community-based networks (local contacts, community leaders, community health workers) and population/needs tracking mechanisms (FGD, surveys) will ensure relevant coverage and increase project efficiency and targeting by involving those directly affected. Additionally, the existing PUI feedback mechanisms will be pursue both at real-time through PUI local hotline and email address for feedback collection and, at evaluative level with the conduction of the mobile team satisfaction survey held on quarterly basis.
