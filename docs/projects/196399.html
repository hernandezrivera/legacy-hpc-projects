Project Summary

IMC will continue its current intervention at its community center in Jaramana and mobile team deployed in Yalda, Rural Damascus, WGSS in Beit Sahem and medical mobile teams in Damascus and Rural Damascus. IMC is planning to establish another community center in Beit Sahem. At its community centers and protection mobile team, IMC will provide the following CP-related activities: case management, non-formal education, skills development, social and recreational activities; inclusion program targeting children with disabilities and their parents; Speech therapy services will also be provided based on the specific needs of each child; parenting skills programs and peer to peer support groups; Youth Empowerment Program (YEP) for youth and adolescents; IMC also plans to establish community-based committees to facilitate the identification of children at risks and/or survivors, raise awareness and discuss main CP concerns and solutions. Regarding GBV prevention and response programming, IMC will provide case management, psychosocial and referral services, and structured and non-structured activities to enhance awareness, prevention, and response to GBV concerns. Women and girls and the community overall are consulted to better understand the main risks and the existing community support systems. IMC WGSS offers the following activities, aimed at building resilience and fostering social interaction, reducing protection, and more specifically GBV risks and thus improving beneficiaries’ safety and well-being: PSS activities and basic life skills programs; Conflict resolution education, a new program targeting girls and focusing on understanding rights and identify solutions, applying different techniques. Health services are provided at the WGSS to ensure access to safe and comprehensive services for GBV survivors, in line with a survivor-centered approach. Dignity kits will also be provided based on needs. Individual Protection Assistance (IPA) will be available for beneficiaries to address the immediate consequences of GBV incidents and protection risks. GBV Case managers will provide assistance through assessments of each specific case and established criteria. Additionally, IMC will provide Information and awareness sessions on CP and GBV-related topics, and capacity-building activities to community members and volunteers.
IMC protection intervention will also be linked with health and livelihoods activities, specifically: 1) GBV and CP case management services are provided in static and mobile health facilities; When dealing with CP cases, the case managers will focus on the needs of the child and his/her family, in consideration of the best interests of the child and building upon the child and his/her family resilience and ensuring the child’s meaningful participation and family empowerment throughout the process. 2) Vocational training courses and related start-up kits will be provided to selected beneficiaries, at risk or survivors of CP and GBV, with the aim of reducing protection risks and increasing wellbeing. Courses will be selected based on market surveys and discussions with targeted communities.
Furthermore, IMC plans to expand its work by supporting national actors through providing technical support and capacity building to MoSAL-supported centers, specifically the care centers of juveniles and people with disabilities in Damascus, Rural Damascus, Aleppo, Idelb and Hama.

3 - Needs

According to the HNO 2022, 14.2 million people are in need of protection humanitarian assistance. This number is expected to rise up to 15.1 million in 2023. COVID 19, the deterioration of the economy, the severe water crisis affected millions across the country, amplified by the worst drought in Syria in 70 years, and the new burden of the Cholera spread is forcing families to adopt negative and harmful coping mechanisms. Children continue to be exposed to a variety of risks and forms of violence and their prevalence is reported at increasing levels in all governorates. Child labor was reported as occurring in 84% of the assessed communities, family separation in 36% and psychosocial distress was reported by 26% of the surveyed households. Girls are more at risk of early marriage (reported in 71% of the assessed communities, against 62% in 2021), harassment, sexual abuse and movement restrictions; boys are more exposed to heavy child labor, negligence and harmful discipline methods, recruitment, detention, family separation and engagement in illegal activities. Women and girls are disproportionately affected by GBV and despite under-reporting (due to stigma, social and traditional norms, lack of awareness and support services), IMC teams confirm they are on the rise, 21% of beneficiaries reached case management services were survivors of GBV, 62.9% were exposed to violence/ IPV, while 53% were children, 44.3% had emotional or psychological abuse and 26.5 were physical abuse.

4 - Implementation of activities

IMC will carry out GBV and CP case management activities in static and mobile health and protection facilities. The activities will be complemented by referrals (internal and external) as per the protocols and principles of safety, confidentiality, and a survivor-centered approach. Survivors may also be referred to specialized services such as psychotherapy and psychiatry for severe mental health issuesIMC will implement PSS activities such as non-formal education, life skills, empowerment programs, and social and recreational activities based on a combination of pre-determined standards and beneficiaries’ preferences. Additionally, IMC will establish a portage approach-based inclusion program for care-givers and children with intellectual disabilities including speech therapy services. IMC will also provide parenting skills programs (such as ECD) to pregnant women and mothers in line with the nutrition-related themes. IMC will run education and awareness raising activities in all IMC sites and vocational training facilities. The activities are designed through the usage of contextualized IEC (Information, Education, and Communication). The topics of the IEC will follow the top frequent risks faced by the target population.IMC will provide vocational training programs and start up kits through private institutions. IMC will also integrate capacity-building activities to IMC staff members, other organizations, and community members.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Lack of/low quality of services and referrals expose survivors to further harm, discrimination, stigmatization. Mitigation measures include: training and supervision of case managers; service mapping including only providers implementing ensuring survivor centered approach; protected and confidential information systems. Resistance from the community on GBV/CP messaging and activities, resulting in low participation, stigmatization or harm to target groups. Mitigation measures: community and leaders’ engagement on all phases of the project cycle; adapting awareness and information messages to the specific context; only female staff working in WGSS; engagement of volunteers from the target communities.Risks related to the location of service provision (safety of movements for beneficiary, contamination from remnants of war, proximity with sensitive buildings. Mitigation measures: choice of location done in close consultation and agreement with the community, conduction of safety audits; awareness and mine education. COVID 19: Not adherence to preventive and IPC related measures can increase the risk of transmission to beneficiaries. Mitigation measures include: monitoring of adherence to preventive measures; reduction of beneficiaries numbers for group activities; provision of PPE; trainings/refreshers; suspension of service provision if increase in the number of staff with confirmed/suspected infection.PSEA: please refer to the dedicated section


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SEA related risks include abuse and exploitation both towards staff and beneficiaries seeking services at IMC facilities, either in exchange for services/assistance or not. At organizational level, all acts falling under the definition of SEA are prohibited and considered a gross violation of IMC Code of Conduct, which all IMC employees must sign and commit to adhere to. The Code is supported by IMC Safeguarding and Whistleblower Policy. All employees and volunteers are required to complete a mandatory training on the Code of Conduct & Ethics and Safeguarding Policy, and on reporting mechanisms, with refreshers provided on annual basis. IMC HQ has established a Safeguarding Task Force while at country level there are designated focal points in charge of training and awareness to staff and beneficiaries and ensuring safe and appropriate referrals in case of any incident being reported and confirmed by the investigation. Furthermore, IMC Syria MEAL team conducts mandatory and refresher training on the community-based feedback and response mechanism for all IMC staff and volunteers. Awareness and information sessions to beneficiaries regarding PSEA and reporting mechanism are also provided, together with information on how to safely report any incident


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1: Delayed/lack of approvals. Mitigation: Regular follow-up with relevant authorities, and advocacy for uninterrupted access. 2: Resistance and non-acceptance of IMC activities from the community. Mitigation: Community engagement and awareness through culturally and context-adapted tools and communication strategies, especially for new areas of intervention. 3: Delays in international bank transfers. Mitigation: Maintain more than one bank account; advocate for dedicated channels for humanitarian transfers; info sharing with partners and donors. 4: COVID 19 limits the capacity to deliver services either because of lockdowns or infection among staff. Mitigation: waivers to allow movement of staff and mobile facilities; adherence to preventive and isolation measures; temporary suspension of services to protect beneficiaries from transmission, remote case management services. 5: Deterioration of the security situation. Mitigation: monitoring and info sharing with relevant stakeholders and temporary closure of facilities if risks raise; security/surveillance at facilities, and premises.
