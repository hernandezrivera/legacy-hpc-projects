Project Summary

This project will address the issue of SGBV from three different but interlinked components:
a. Component 1 – Gender and Rights education: this component will equip SGBV survivors on the issue of gender and its interaction with other factors that discriminates against women. It is hoped that gender education will demystify socialization and social norms that women and girls have accepted as normal. Rights education will equip women on their rights and ability to know when these rights are being violated. Training on the skills to demand for a community that is safe and secure will be part of this component. Evidence has shown that gender and rights education have led to change in mind sets and reduction in IPV, PSEA and community-wide violence.
b. Component 2: Transforming negative gender and social norms through whole community approach through mobilization, sensitization, gender and rights education to change kAPB on SGBV to engender zero tolerance to SGBV and safety and security for women and girls. It is hoped that these trainings will change mind sets of men to be favourably disposed to changing negative gender and social norms that accepts and tolerate SGBV/VAWGs. Other aspects will include establishment and training of Gender Dialogue Committees to facilitate Gender Dialogues to address controversial issues such as early and forced marriage, FGM, harmful practices, gender equality, women’s participation and decision making. Engaging men/boys through accountability practices (EM/BAP) will comprise another aspect. This aspect is based on the fact that men and boys are the major perpetrators of SGBV and so must be part of the solution to the plague. EM/BAP will reduce harmful behaviour through providing men and boys the tools and knowledge to rethink belief systems and prevent VAWG through individual behavioural change that will promote transformational community change that is accountability to the voices of women. The targeting of adolescent boys has the objective of catching them young to stop patriarchal mind sets before they become deeply entrenched. EMAP will also provide female participants with opportunities to reflect on SGBV/VAWG in their lives and community.
c. Component 3 – Access to services and justice for SGBV survivors: Aspects of this component would include the following: Mapping of GBV reporting channels and creation of awareness on same; Mapping of service providers in the target locations; Operation of Women and Girls Friendly Spaces. The project will establish WGSS in target locations where none exists and leverage in locations where they exist. The activities in the WGSS will include case management, PSS, MHPSS, SRHR information, material assistance such as provision of dignity kits, EASE+ activities ;skill acquisition for income generation to build the independence and agency of women and amplifying their voices on issues that affect them. For effective and professional case management, GBV officers and case workers and service providers will be trained to ensure that their approaches are survivor centered; Establishment of camp /community Leaders to deliver monthly innovative SGBV awareness and prevention activities/outreaches and social media platforms using IEC/BCC materials and animated video clips on SGBV and PSEA to change KABP; Access to Justice – this will involve mapping of legal service providers in target communities; Establishment and training of Community Paralegals (CPs) to support survivors in their quest to access justice; Awareness creation on the VAPP Laws of the BAY states – the offences and the punishment so that survivors and at risk women have knowledge of these and report; High level advocacy with other WLO/WROs and MWASD for the implementation of the VAPP laws that will lead to the reduction SGBV/VAWGs.

1 - Needs

GEPaDC’s end line assessment in some LGAs in Borno state indicates the following needs that has to be tackled for the reduction of SGBV in camps and communities in Borno state for survivors holistic and comprehensice access to services and mechanisms. These needs include inadequate knowledge of reporting channels and service providers and stigma and shame that inhibits survivors from reporting; lack/inadequate capacity of survivors and vulnerable at risk women and girls on their rights; Inadequate or lack of women friendly spaces where survivors can obtain case management, PSS, MHPSS, SRHR information and services as well as material assistance such as provision of dignity kits and economic empowerment; lack of women’s leadership to drive the fight against SGBV; Lack of access to justice in target communities arising from lack of survivors’ knowledge and the technicalities of accessing justice in the communities especially rural communities where law enforcement institutions are almost non-existent. Also, there is dearth of support system for SGBV survivors that can support their quest to access justice even in the face of hostilities from perpetrators, families and community members; lack of awareness of survivors on the most important law against SGBV in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States – the Violence Against Persons Prohibition law

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Gender and Rights education: An understanding of gender and concepts & human/women’s rights education, known change in mind sets and reduction in IPV, PSEA and community-wide violence. 5,000 survivors and vulnerable females shall benefit from these trainings.Activity 2: Transforming negative gender and social norms through whole community approach: Involve sensitization and training of community stakeholders (38,540) on SGBV, gender, women’s rights, reporting and referral pathways and role of community in SGBV responses using animated videos, IEC/BCC materials and monthly community Gender Dialogues by CGDC (120).Activity 3: Engaging men/boys through accountability practices (EM/BAP): EMAP model is used to change patriarchal mind set and negative social norms. The activity will include: 16, 8, 27, 10 sessions respectively for men, women, boys and girls in each community. Beneficiaries will comprise of 1000 each of the 4 categories. The beneficiaries will share with peers their learning. Project staff will be trained on EMAP approach in a 25 days training using the training manuals developed by IRC (2013). Arising from this will be the mixed Gender Dialogue Committee.Activity 4: Access to Quality Services for SGBV survivors: Involve mapping SGBV reporting channels and service providers and creation of awareness on same. Posters with this information shall be placed in strategic locations for survivors and WGAR for quick access to service providers trained on SCA.Activity 5: Operation of Women and Girls Friendly Spaces: this activity will involve construction of 3 WGSS (Damboa, Damaturu and Numan) and leveraging on 1 in Konduga and their furnishing for increased access of 8,020 survivors to Case management activities (PSS, MHPSS, SRHR information, PSS and MHPSS sessions). 12 case workers will be recruited and trained in case management. A referral/emergency fund shall be established for referrals and prescribed drugs.Activity 6: Provision of dignity kits for SGBV survivors and vulnerable women and girls and provision of SRHR services including material assistance to 6,000 women and girls of reproductive age for 11 months. Activity 7: Economic and Social Empowerment (EASE) plus approach: 1,325 SGBV survivors and other vulnerable women and girls will benefit from the EASE+ approach through 8 days training on business skills and start-up grants, weekly savings, monitoring and organization into VSLAs (45 VSLAs). For effective EASE+ intervention, an EASE+ officer and 8 Livelihood caseworkers will be recruited and trained for 15 days on EASE+ approach. The VSLAs will be trained for 5 days on VSLA operations; 2 days shall be devoted to couples’ training on joint financial management, conflict management and joint household decision making. Activity 8: Establishment and training of female community Leaders (80) that will double as community paralegals and support network will be trained on SGBV, women’s rights, leadership, case identification, reporting, community engagements, outreaches, one-on-one sessions, paralegal activities, campaigns and dissemination of BCC/IEC material; 100,000 IEC/BCC materials shall be produced. There shall be 12 monthly outreaches and campaigns reaching 4,000 people every month.Activity 9: Access to Justice: this will include mapping of legal providers and together with other service providers shall be trained on survivor centred approach at a 2-day training. The community paralegals shall play a role in survivors’ access to justice. Creation of awareness on the VAPP Laws – the offences and the punishment for community members and high level advocacy with other WLO/WROs, MWASD for the implementation of the laws will be part of this activity and training of law enforcement agent on the law and SCA investigating & policing.Monitoring and Evaluation: One MEAL Officer & 4 CB MEAL assistants shall carry out M&E. 4 Accountability boards & CRM boxes shall be placed in each community for AAP.
