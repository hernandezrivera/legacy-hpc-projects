Project Summary

The humanitarian crisis in the Northeast Nigeria specifically in the BAY (Borno, Adamawa & Yobe) states is expected to persist in 2021 and will severely affect millions of people (HRP March, 2021). About 5.1 million school-aged children are in need of humanitarian assistance.
Education in Emergency Working Group (EiEWG 2020) said that about 1.2 million of school-children are in need of basic education services in the Northeastern states most affected by the crisis.
GHIV Proposed 12-months intervention aims to provide access to education opportunity to children affected by crisis in Ngala and Bama Local Government areas in Borno state. The intervention will be implemented in both formal and in non-formal education system

1 - Needs

About one hundred thousand and five school-aged children are in need of education assistance in the 2 proposed LGAs for the project (EIEWG, April 2020). This is as a result of infrastructural damage and systemic collapse which was brought about by the more than ten years of crisis in the region and the neglect by the government. Many teachers have left these communities affected by crisis and went to reside in a safer town in the state. This Action seeks to support the effort of the government through the rehabilitation of formal school classroom and the establishment and rehabilitation of Temporary learning structures in the 2 proposed LGAs. The Action shall also aim to strengthen the education by providing capacity training to the school teachers and the school-based management committee on issues of management, mobilization of resources to be able to continue the work and on the safety and security of school within and outside the school environment. The 2 proposed LGAs are some of the major under-served communities in the state. Education in emergency sector identified that more sixty thousand (60,000) school-aged children are in need of education in the two LGAs proposed for this intervention. Four thousand (4,000) children would have opportunity to attend, remain in school and transition to a higher level through this intervention while about above 10,000 children are expected to indirectly benefit from the intervention through campaigns and peer to peer learning.5 classrooms selected for renovation in would provide education access to 1,000 children that will graduate from the NFE in a protective and safe environment and address huge gap in infrastructure in the LGAs earmarked for the intervention. Aside the challenges identified above, another challenge facing these 2 selected LGAs is the issue of rising security tension although not peculiar to these 2 LGAs which has resulted to school dropouts in the communities. The school-based management committee and other education actors will be trained on safety and security of school children to ensure adequate protection for children while they are in the school or when the school has closed, most especially the girls who are been adopted for sexual gratification and used to conduct attacks. Covid-19 has further exacerbated the impact of the crisis and led to the closure of the schools for about a year. The intervention would capacitate the teachers and the community structures on the preventive approaches to adopt in schools and out of school premises. The project shall also provide radio learning to ensure that there is no obstruction during learning.

2 - Activities

The project will contribute to the education sector's priority objective of “ensuring access and continuity of learning during and after schools’ closure in safe and protective school environment”. Through this intervention, GHIV Africa will rehabilitate Ten (10) TLS to provide 1000 out-of-school children with access to seven-months non-formal education delivered in a Temporary Learning Space (TLS) managed by GHIV Africa in partnership with the community structures. To ensure the sustainability of the action, after the completion of the seven-months curriculum, children will be mainstreamed into formal schools to continue their learning. The primary school where the children will be transitioned to will be supported to provide safe inclusive and quality teaching and learning for 3,000 pupils (1,800 girls and 1,200 boys) through the renovation of five (5) classrooms and the provision of teaching and learning materials. Eighty (60) formal school teachers and twenty (20) community education volunteers will participate in capacity-building training, coaching, and learning review circles in order to enhance their knowledge and skills in EiE Pedagogy, MHPSS, and classroom management. Moreover, the project will strengthen SBMCs through training and support to develop and implement school development plans and action plans for effective school management. In the view of providing the children with a safe learning environment and with the objective of counter the spread of the COVID-19 virus, cholera and viral diseases, the learning spaces will be upgraded with WASH facilities as well as disability-sensitive WASH facilities, including the construction of latrines, and the installation of handwashing facilities in the schools and TLS. Community awareness raising events will be organized to increase knowledge of community members and stakeholders on the importance of education and the need to ensure children are enrolled, stay in school, and learn. In addition, the project will support 600 young girls in Non-formal Education (NFE) Spaces with training on Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM), provide with dignity kits, and enable them to participate in child-to-child hygiene education activities. The project shall also provide life skill training to 500 out of the 1000 children targeted by the project in the non-formal education system.Lastly, the project will provide training on safety and security of schools and develop protocols when schools are attacked.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

During the implementation of this intervention, GHIV Africa will focused on the dynamics and circumstances that threaten the safety and dignity of people affected by armed conflict, displacement, and other crisis situations especially amongst the most vulnerable groups. We will be using crisis-specific strategies that prioritize issues of greatest concern to affected groups, while investing in relationships and initiatives that safeguard the space needed to uphold humanitarian values.GHIV Africa is a women led organization that prioritizes the needs of girls and boy, women, boys, and other persons most affected by crisis or those in dire need of humanitarian assistance. In view that, girls of menstruation age will be trained on Menstruation Hygiene Management (MHM) and will be provided with dignity kits. In addition, all the latrines constructed will be gender segregated. As the sector lead for disability inclusion, the project will ensure access to learning opportunities for People with Disabilities (PWD) thus making inclusive. In this frame, a fair access to education will be emphasized during the awareness sessions on education and the classrooms in the formal schools, as well as the WASH facilities in both the TLS and the formal schools will be constructed with ramp access. To empower the different groups, GHIV Africa will identify volunteers and hygiene promotor speaking the local dialects. Furthermore, the recruitment of the volunteers and the hygiene promoters will be gender-balanced and ensure a fair access to job-opportunity and to provide culturally sensitive services to girls, boys, women, and men.GHIV Africa will follow its policies on child safeguarding and protection from sexual abuse and exploitation (PSEA) in line with International best practices. These policies set out the importance and requirements for the good conduct of staff and establishes a systematic obligation for reporting any behavior that contravenes it. More specifically, teachers and education will be trained on prevention of violence against children, positive discipline, PSEA, Psychosocial support and Psychological First Aid, Mental Health and Psychosocial Support Services (MHPSS), School GBV concepts and prevention- gender norm, safe identification and referrals, Child protection mainstreaming, child protection minimum standards, teachers code of conduct, life skills training and safe school.As previously mentioned, GHIV will put in place an Accountability to Affected Persons framework. Through this framework, community members will have the opportunity determine the appropriate and relevant mechanisms for feedback, participation, complaints, information sharing, and monitoring based on the context and to use them when necessary.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

At GHIV Africa we know that the extent of local participation exerts a strong effect on the prospects for successful peacebuilding and reconstruction efforts in the wake of humanitarian interventions. Even though the population in target communities may sympathize with the goal of the intervention, local populations are unlikely to feel a personal attachment to a solution externally imposed unless actively consulted or involved in the intervention strategy. Humanitarian interventions without some forms of local participation are likely to create cognitive dissonance among the local population between the outcome and the means chosen to implement it. It is therefore important to understand that failure to consult or actively integrate local parties in target communities during the intervention phase can create problems of cognitive dissonance in humanitarian interventions: a population that is largely uninvolved in a humanitarian intervention is less likely to cooperate with the intervening parties or expend efforts to make the intervention successful.Participation of beneficiaries is rooted in GHIV Africa methodology of implementation and as such beneficiaries will be involved at all stages of the project. Beneficiaries will be deeply involved in the project implementation to enhance the ownership of the project, strengthen the community-based mechanisms, and reinforce sustainability. In this frame, volunteers will be identified amongst the community in consultation with SAME and/or the community leaders. Volunteers will be trained on the efficient implementation of the activities and their capacity will be enhanced to become agents of change amongst their peers. In addition, GHIV Africa will provide local structures composed of community members with trainings and on-the-job coaching to reinforce their capacity and autonomy.
