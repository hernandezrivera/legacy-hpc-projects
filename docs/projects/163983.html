Project Summary

This project aims to contribute to a safe and equitable access to education and to guarantee quality and sustainable formal and non-formal education as well as psycho-social support for crisis-affected children. The project would like to soft rehabilitate 4 schools in the selected area, providing them with school furniture, providing specific trainings for teachers (ex. Active Learning), providing BTL campaign for the communities, developing remedial classes for pupils in schools and catch-up courses for children temporary out-of-schools. It is finally planned to provide to children of the community sessions on PSS in the perspective of increasing community protection. The project will take in high consideration access to education for disable children and gender equality.

4 - Needs

The conflict in Syria is lasting more than 8 years and nowadays over 5.8 million school-aged children and about 245.000 education personnel are in need of education assistance inside Syria, 61 % of whom are in acute and immediate need. 2.1 million children in Syria are out of school, and further 1.3 million are at risk of dropping out, with more than one out of three schools damaged or destroyed (UN HNO 2019, p.61). Despite considerable education investments and efforts, access to safe and quality educational opportunities remains a challenge for children and youth in Syria, especially in new accessible area as the Governorate of Idlib. Due to a shortage of functional classrooms, classes are still overcrowded, teaching capacity remains overstretched and the barriers to access quality and relevant learning for children inside Syria are complex (UN HNO 2019, p.61-63), especially for disabled children. The proposed intervention is included in the framework of the Education 2020 sector' response, objectives 1, 2 and 3, in line therefore with the No Lost Generation (NLG) Initiative and the Education Cannot Wait (ECW) Investment (UN HRP 2019, p.62), and therefore it is designed to complement the efforts of the Education sector in Syria (SO3 of HRP 2019). In Idlib Governorate (Education Sector data 2020) there are 949,131 PiNs and 30,617 teachers in need and the percentage reached so far this year is nearly zero percent. Hostilities and safety concerns of households particularly in the north-west remain a key barrier to accessing education. Even those who are attending school are exposed to a pervasive culture of violence which can in turn contribute to dropout rates. Education needs are highest in areas of the northwest where conflict and displacement are concentrated (HNO draft 2020, p. 71). Urgent interventions are therefore needed in the region proposed.

5 - Implementation of activities

The activities proposed would like to sustain a safe and equitable access to formal and non-formal learning opportunities and to guarantee quality and sustainable education for crisis-affected children in an area affected recently by a deep humanitarian crisis. In brief the action will support a) the rehabilitation of four selected schools in the area (at least 30 classrooms), in agreement with MoE, and the provision of school supplies and equipment such as pupils' desks, with a specific focus on green technology (solar panels), gender equality and disability inclusion intended (as per IASC guidelines) environment, attitude and institutional inclusion. The soft rehabilitation of schools will benefit 1000 children (6-17 years old) in those areas; b) specific trainings on Active Learning and PSS for teachers, educational staff and AVSI/SARC volunteers (at least 20 teachers trained) c) BTL campaign with SARC volunteers and catch-up course in the summer (we plan to reach at least 100 out-of-school children) d) Remedial classes and support to children who risk to drop-out (we plan to reach at least 400 children at risk of drop-out) e) PSS activities community-based for children (500 children benefiting) and adults (50) i) parents-teachers meetings.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

All staff involved will received a specific training on CP and PSS according to AVSI international Psycho-social Approach Manual and its toolkit and they will sign and adhere to the AVSI Code of Ethics and Child Protection Policy as well as Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) . Moreover, a psychological test will be appointed for all staff involved with children. A specialized psychologist will be available periodically in all schools in order to help staff, volunteers, teachers, disabled children and pupils with their parents in their problems and complains, in collaboration with SARC and MoE. All activities will be tailored to be inclusive and accessible for girls and children with disabilities and specific structured interventions are planned to allow this. All beneficiaries identified for need of specialized services that require referrals will be asked to sign an information release consent form. This would also apply in regards to field staff sharing information among each other. The discretion of beneficiary information will be protected, as it will only be disclosed upon consent of the beneficiaries. Continuous activities' monitoring, trainings to teachers and staff and whistle-blowing procedure will prevent misconduct and potential cases of harassment. There are other risks to be concerned like for instance the possibility that teachers or students participate bullying the students, mitigated by the trainings to teachers and the PSS activities with children in this project, or the fact that learning facilities or their surroundings are structurally unsafe, mitigated by a clear programme of rehabilitation of the schools, target of the intervention. The project will follow the Educational Cluster Education Protection Risk Analysis (PRA) Matrix in order to mitigate risks and to accomplish the aims of the project itself.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The situation in the area of intervention is relatively stable and the area is accessible. AVSI is in continuous contact with its main partner SARC and UNDSS and it receives periodic specific reports about the situations in the country. All possible threats found in schools will be reported to UNDSS and to the Attacks on Education Reporting Mechanism. AVSI has specific Security Guidelines and Procedures for Syria (updated in August 2019) and specific measures are in place for daily field missions (use of specific AVSI driver and car; security check during the trips, WhatsApp updates). Field monitoring missions for international staff, or other extra staff, will be done only after obtaining the security clearance from SARC. Thanks to SARC and MoE the access to the beneficiaries is guaranteed, the identified areas are selected according to needs of the population and to AVSI capacity to reach them. HR is managed by a specific AVSI HR officer (currently working) to avoid delays and to face internal queries. Finally, from a financial point of view, all the expenditures related to the project are sent on a monthly base to HQ and to the regional office to enable the revision of the accounting in order to avoid financial delays and problems. AVSI staff will conduct field visits on a regular basis to assess implementation (using the AVSI M&E work plan tool) while ensuring active participation of beneficiaries through establishing feedback mechanisms to increase quality of services and especially accountability to beneficiaries. External consultants and experts will carry out trainings, evaluation and follow-up missions to evaluate the level of learning of the field staff, the correct implementation of educational and psycho-social activities and humanitarian assistance. There are planned periodic monitoring missions from Damascus, as well as from the Beirut AVSI MENA office during the implementation of the program to ensure the effectiveness of the proposed action.
