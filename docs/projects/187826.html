Project Summary

In more than a ten-year conflict, the security, safety, and well-being of the civilians are widespread, regularly violated. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview across Syria, an estimated 11.7 million people are in need of various forms of humanitarian assistance, with certain population groups facing particularly high levels of vulnerability.
The overall conservation situation remains dire, defined by the protracted crisis and characterized by complex and interconnected conservation concerns. Women and girls are particularly vulnerable to GBV due to: Gender inequality and discrimination in Syria. In the households, women, and girls traditionally took on additional roles, including as heads of household. This contributes to increased pressure and stress for women to provide for their families, often in absence of any other support from family members. Additionally, Children of all ages face violence and protection concerns in many life situations. Adolescence brings exposure to new forms of violence due to harmful coping mechanisms within the family.
The project is designed to improve the protection of the population affected by the crisis through community-based and individually targeted protection interventions such as the following:
 Outreach workers provide referral of protection/GBV cases and awareness on health hygiene and protection.
 Referral teams provide referral service to the GBV/protection cases assisting them in getting specialized assistance (vocational training, PSS, etc...)
 Distribution of dignity kits to women and girls in need and vulnerable
 Engagement of male community leaders including religious and local leaders.
 Raising awareness of the risks of exploitation of children and developing sensitization work on this issue.
 Raising awareness sessions on COVID-19 including associated GBV risks and GBV key messages.

4 - Needs

11 years of crisis have caused huge damage to the Syrian population eroding its resilience and causing immense suffering. The needs are now gigantic. 12.4 million people are now in need of health assistance. The project will be implemented in order to address the most urgent needs of the communities of North-western Syria in particular. According to the HNO 2021, “Dramatic increases in food insecurity can worsen already elevated levels of anaemia in Women of Reproductive Age (WRA), posing a risk to adverse health outcomes for both mother and child, and, when coupled with diarrheal disease, can exacerbate the risk of poor malnutrition.”According to the “Voices of Syria 2022” report, “Expand the number and accessibility of static and mobile GBV specialized services”. This is due to “Accessibility of GBV services emerging strongly as a theme that women and girls in Syria are concerned about, in recognition that WGSS are sometimes too far and not always easy to visit for women and girls, and particularly those facing compounding forms of discrimination and violence. This will be addressed through the use of referral pathways services supported under the proposed project. Low awareness and high importance of the healthcare workers in the provision of support to GBV survivors is an important gap in the proper management of GBV cases. This creates the need for the training of healthcare workers in the proper management of GBV cases using a survivor cantered approach.

5 - Implementation of activities

IRT will provide awareness-raising and psychosocial assistance to persons with specific protection needs and other community initiatives, through the activity it is expected to reach 50,000 people. Additionally, 5000 will be assisted through individual assistance for specific protection needs and with referrals to further assistance such as PSS, health and material assistance. Around 20 advocacies with duty bearers and key stakeholders will be taken place to inform and enhance the response to protection risks. The activities from the scratch will be monitored via the Meal team in order to identify protection needs. 4 communities will be reached and assessed for the protection needs and risks. The intervention also plans to provide life-saving GBV response services, including case management and psychosocial support, enhance vulnerable groups' access to these services. A number of male and female GBV actors will be trained on GBV. Outreach workers will be supported to provide case management support in a survivor cantered approach. The workers will be based in 4 health facilities mainly serving IDP camps. Outreach workers will support GBV survivors in a survivor cantered approach and will provide referral assistance enabling them to provide access to other specialized GBV support. 17 outreach workers will be trained on GBV and case management in a survivor cantered approach. 40 professional health staff will be trained on GBV and protection risk mitigation and in inclusion.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

GBV cases engaged by project staff In order to prevent such events, outreach work is being conducted on issues such as protection and GBV in order to increase the awareness on such issues. Furthermore, location as well as the surroundings of the health facilities to be supported will be chosen to be suitable and safe for all groups especially women and girls. Furthermore, referral vehicles will be tasked with picking up from home any community member at risk in need of transportation.Safety of the outreach workers jeopardized Outreach work will be coordinated fully with the local councils/camps management of each community beforehand in order to prevent negative reactions from the community members seeing strangers in their community. Furthermore, the outreach work will be followed up closely by the safety and security department of IR.Confidentiality of GBV survivors in particularIn order to prevent any harm or conflicts, all staff dealing with GBV cases or survivors will abide by strict confidentiality measures preventing any information pertaining the survivors from going public. Mitigation measures: will include name hiding, private archiving of cases, case management rooms isolated and general confidentiality measures preventing the divulgation of information to non-relevant staff or persons.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

IR Syria field offices comply the PSEA policy and prior to the project start as part of the orientation of the project staff that is recruited, they will be informed of this policy. The collected feedback and complaints will be received and analysed by the complaints officer/focal point and will be addressed according to IRW CFM policy, special attention will be given to the GBV, PSEAH, CP, etc, potential incidents and complaints, such cases will be directed to the country director to take the required actions. IR Turkey will make sure that the health workers of the supported health facility have adequate knowledge of the CFM and the related subject. IR Turkey has provided training on the CFM to the staff in the supported health facilities in the past two years IR team will make sure that all the health staff in the supported health facilities have adequate knowledge regarding the CFM and there are no gaps in this regard. In the context of Covid-19 IR will try to adopt suitable approach that will allow IR team to conduct trainings\sessions to strengthen the capacity of the CFM and safeguarding for the catchment population and main stakeholder. IR will be very clear about its roles and responsibilities as well as roles of the main stakeholder and target communities during the implementation of the project.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The existence of many checkpoints inside Syria, which may limit the access to the target area.IR will coordinate with local partners and local committees who will help in facilitating the movement of the items to the target areas. All required documentation will accompany all shipments of goods along with experienced staff who are familiar with the situation and the checkpoints.COVID-19 in project area becomes out of controlThis will greatly reduce the number of beneficiaries visiting the supported centers due to the lockdown measures that will be implemented. During this phase, the prioritization of PPE distributions to the health facilities will be key to prevent a shutdown of the health system due to infections.Diversion of aid : IR team is implementing a big health programme inside Syria by involving all the relevant stakeholders to ensuring that there is no external influence in aid delivery.
