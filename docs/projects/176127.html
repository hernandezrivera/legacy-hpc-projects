Project Summary

According the Health cluster response plan 2021, the people of South Sudan have limited access to optimal health services due to ongoing conflict, flooding and an underdeveloped health care system. About 2.4 million people in South Sudan are in need of health services. The proposed project shall extend health services to vulnerable persons form IDPs, returnees and host population in Leer, Mayendit, Panyijair and Pariang counties of Unity State and Maiwut and Longechuk counties of Upper Nile.
This project is inline with
CLHEA/CO1 objective to reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic-prone diseases and health insecurities, CLHEA/CO2 objective to Improve access and utilization of integrated life-saving quality health care services to crisis-affected and vulnerable populations through static, mobile and community-based health service delivery systems CLHEA/CO3 Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled, mental health The project will Target 125,000 beneficiaries and support them with life saving emergency health care services through 12 Primary Health Care Units (PHCUs) and 6 Mobile Clinics in the targeted Counties.
The emergency health care services will be provided through static PHCUs and PHCCs and Mobile clinics across the targeted locations to ensure vulnerable persons are given adequate health care services.
Coalition for Humanity through the PHCU/ PHCC and the mobile outreach shall strengthen the existing surveillance system to pick the first case and raise the alert to the cluster and other implementing partners in timely manner. The project will also build capacity of the community Health workers to report outbreaks timely and provide life-saving emergency. Vaccination campaigns such as EPI, Case management, rapid response and risk communication in the outreach locations earmarked for the project activities in unity and upper Nile states. The project therefore aims to improve access to and utilization of essential health care services by all people in need, in order to reduce excess morbidity and mortality. Disease outbreaks will be detected early, responded to in a timely manner and adequate surveillance systems will be in place through Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR) and Early Warning Alert System (EWARS). The project will provide health services adapting to the current COVID-19 context and ensure COVID-19 prevention and appropriate response activities are integrated while continue providing essential lifesaving health services. Referral pathways for mental health and psychosocial support, sexual and gender-based violence survivors and for maternal and child health emergencies will be streamlined and strengthened. Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) and services for persons with disabilities will be integrated and strengthened.

1 - Needs

The prolonged conflict and limited Government investment have had a devastating impact on the country’s infrastructure and basic service delivery. Health facilities are poorly equipped and staffed. Out of approximately 2,300 health facilities, more than 1,300 are non-functional. Of the functioning health facilities, 57 per cent are supported by humanitarian and development partners and many remain in areas that are not easily accessible by the communities, i.e., close to a quarter of IDPs and returnees live in settlements further than 5 km from a functional health facility. The weakened health system compounded by multiple shocks has impacted the health and wellbeing of an estimated 4.9 million South Sudanese in 2021. This is a 36 per cent increase from the 3.6 million people in 2020. In addition, 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers continue to need support with health care. The people of South Sudan have limited access to optimal health services due to ongoing conflict, flooding and an underdeveloped health care system. Only 7.5 per cent of the 1,763 reporting health facilities provide basic health services. According to South Sudan Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response System, Malaria remains the top cause of morbidity (64 per cent) and mortality (45 per cent). Between 9 and 15 November, there were 89,403 reported cases. Six out of ten states have been affected by vaccine-derived polioviruses outbreaks in 2020 among which are the Maiwut and Longenchuk counties of Upper Nile state. The ongoing flood situation has further aggravated the health situation, the spread of existing outbreaks and increased likelihood of new outbreaks in 2021. This highlights the critical need to strengthen disease surveillance and response system to prevent, detect and respond to future outbreaks. The absence of basic health care including vaccination services has resulted in low immunization coverage throughout the country, increasing the risk of vaccine preventable disease outbreaks. Planned mass vaccination campaigns were disrupted by the COVID-19 containment measures in place, which worsened the vaccination coverage and related health risks. According to WHO and UNICEF estimates of immunization coverage from 2019, more than half of children are not fully immunized and the transmission of vaccine-preventable diseases such as measles continues unabated. This will result in higher health needs in 2021. In Upper Nile, diagnostic capacity is poor and there are significant pockets of kala-azar disease. Snakebites pose a risk during the rainy season, and timely treatment is crucial. These multiple shocks compounded peoples' psychosocial distress. Stressors at the family level such as conflicts, substance abuse and COVID-19 related fears increased the risk of suicides, domestic violence and GBV. Specialized mental health services are scarce and limited to a few urban areas like Juba and the disruption in accessing medication and treatment aggravated the already poor health condition of people with mental disorders.According to the findings of an assessment on people’s priority needs conducted in 2020, health was among the second-most identified need for women after food assistance. For children, health was the second-most identified need, after education. For men, health was the third-most identified need.

2 - Project activities

• 100,000 persons provided with outpatient services to manage un complicated Malaria, acute diarrhea and un complicated pneumonia within PHCC, PHCU and mobile clinics.• Provide EPI services to children below five years for child preventable diseases at the PHCU’s and Mobile clinics.• Provide antenatal care (ANC) services at the health facilities • Provide children with Nutritional services by conducting screening, deworming and referrals. • Conduct community-based outreach services in the PHCU’s and Mobile Clinic’s.• 300 community Health Workers provided with communication skills and approaches to reach out to community with key messages of Health.• Provide Integrated disease surveillance response service (IDSR) • Promote family planning through distribution of pills and condoms• Establish village Health committee to support the health facilities and staffs in out reach activities • Strengthen Health information system (HIS) at implementation sites • Provide weekly continuous medical education at the Health Facilities.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Coalition for humanity has a PSEA code of conduct signed by all project staff including support staffs, all those who shall be directly involved with the project implementation including humanitarian staff, community leaders, Health workers shall be taken through 1 day training on PSEA and the community members sensitized on their right to access the humanitarian assistance without being exploited.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

There is a risk of external stakeholder interference, Coalition for Humanity will conduct a stakeholder engagement meeting at the start of the project to define objectives of the project, their role and responsibility. There is a risk of minority, people with disability, women, girls and boys not participating effectively in the project. Coalition for Humanity will conduct focus group discussions targeting people with disability, boys, girls, women separately to make sure their needs and concerns are captured. Coalition for Humanity has a PSEA code of conduct, all field staff are trained on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, Community leaders are also trained on PSEA to raise awareness in the community so that beneficiaries can know they have a right to humanitarian aid without their dignity being compromised.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Coalition for Humanity shall engage with the communities through baseline survey, focus group discussions, community consultations before and during the development of this Project. Moving forward, Coalition for Humanity will continue engaging the communities through risks assessment, Health related risk discussions, community engagement meetings/Consultations (CEC) involving the affected community before and during the project implementation. The community needs will be verified through focus group discussions and Key Informant Interviews with leaders. A project inception meeting will be held with local authorities involving women, men, boys and girls to ensure participation and feedback. Village Health Committees shall be set up. Coalition for Humanity will adapt participatory monitoring and evaluation.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback from community will inform the Health risks, most needy Payam’s, Bomas, and this will inform prioritization at community level. Feedback on needs beyond this project will be referred to other partners in the county who have capacity to handle cases beyond this project. Further feedback will inform change in strategy, and future proposals.
