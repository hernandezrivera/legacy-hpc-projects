Project Summary

The project aims to provide a range of protection services to migrants in need, strengthen links between targeted community groups and partners, improve the quality of services available, and enhance knowledge on migrant population groups in Lebanon. Based on IOM Migrant Presence Monitoring 2022, services will target both male and female migrants, with a higher caseload of the latter group due to their increased number in country. Beneficiaries will typically be working age, between 18 and 40 years old, however the project will also support families (including one-parent families). In line with IOM’s localization strategy, IOM will work with one or more implementing partners, enhancing capacities of local CSOs to provide protection services for migrants at the local level. Under this project, IOM will continue to promote a holistic approach to protection assistance, with case management as an entry point to support the accurate identification of protection needs (including MHPSS) and to support necessary referrals. Building on the partnerships established in 2022, IOM will build partnerships with existing partners as well as explore new partnerships. The services that IOM will deliver, either directly or through partners, include awareness on legal and protection topics, legal assistance, case management support including mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), individual counselling, safe and temporary shelters for vulnerable migrants and referral to community hosting program, provision of non-food items, financial support and assisted voluntary return and reintegration. These services will be made accessible to migrants by scaling up IOM’s pre-existing case management services, supporting enhanced capacity of partners, and referral to other partners, strengthening culturally appropriate outreach to large migrant groups, including translation and interpretation, and expanding community-level networks to strengthen targeted communities’ awareness of and access to IOM and partner services, as well as wider issues of concern. Provision of or onward referral to multisector assistance such as food or health support will be mainstreamed across the project.
Additionally, IOM will develop a training curriculum to increase the understanding of cultural sensitivity and migrant inclusivity and build the capacities of service providers. On the data collection and improved understanding level, IOM plans to conduct data collection activities including its third round of the Migrant Presence Monitoring program to update and precise further its baseline assessment on migrants in Lebanon. The MPM informs and builds the sample for the MSNA 2023 which IOM plans also to lead on. Additionally, a nationwide mapping for services available for migrants in Lebanon is planned to take place; IOM piloted this exercise in 2021 in North/Akkar and wish to update findings and expand to the whole country as it would support the Migrant Sector response in assistance to migrants. On another note, IOM plans to support the sector in implementing a protection monitoring /qualitative research with the aim to fill the information gaps from the existing assessments and gaining an in-depth understanding of the migrants’ situation in the country from a protection lens. For instance, topics as children of migrants, migrants in detention, migrants in the workplace, live-in vs. live-out migrants protection concerns, etc. IOM's targets under this proposal include those targeted under preexisting funding, such as the Lebanese Humanitarian Fund (LHF).

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

SO1 Legal awareness. Migrant groups will be given access to community-based protection and services and/or MHPSS. Legal counselling, aid and representation. Case management. including MHPSS. Individual screening and/or counselling.SO2 Shelter via community hosting programs. House assessments to identify hosts; training to hosts. Financial and in-kind assistance to hosts.. Provision of Non-Food Items (NFIs), including hygiene items, and blankets and other winter items on standby. Provision of cash assistance to those affected by ongoing crisis. Cash for protection following assessments and part of the broader case management process. The frequency of cash support (recurrent/non recurrent) will depend on the severity of needs. The amount will be in line with the sector’s guidance.Develop training curriculum on cultural sensitivity. Curriculum will be translated in different languages depending on needs of communities. SO3 Provision of AVRR including counseling, pre-departure health checks, liaising with embassies and General Security to secure travel documents and exit permits respectively, airport escorts, in-flight and post-arrival escort, cash and reintegration assistance. SO4 Nation-wide data collection and assessments on migrants through the third round of the MPM program to inform and build the MSNA 2023.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

IOM aims to strengthen the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and support interagency response. It is critical to ensure people are informed on their rights and protected from sexual exploitation and abuse. It is essential to train staff and ensure beneficiaries understand their rights, and are aware on reporting mechanisms on sexual misconduct. IOM is member of interagency PSEA network. IOM’s Risk SEA risk mitigation plan is the following:- Awareness raising and capacity building of IOM Lebanon’s staff (All staff/volunteers/non-staff had been trained on a full day PSEAH training)- Key messages and awareness raising sessions on PSEA towards the affected population.- Visibility materials on PSEA to be set across all IOM offices.- Active participation and co-leading role in the PSEA national network in Lebanon- Securing a referral mechanism for SEA survivors whenever needed

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

IOM’s accountability to affected populations (AAP) framework gives overview of IOM’s commitments on AAP, definitions and explanations, policies shaping the IOM’s approach. IOM adheres to the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC)’s definition of AAP as "an active commitment by humanitarian actors to use power responsibly by taking account of, giving account to, and being held to account by the people they seek to assist." IOM Lebanon is currently in the processes of revamping it’s complaint/feedback & response mechanism. IOM’s current communication channels on CFM include a dedicated email address, global platform, and support line, which was reactivated in January 2023, and comment boxes in common areas. IEC materials are being developed and disseminated towards staff and the affected population. IOM Lebanon is drafting its communication channels’ SOPs, referral mechanism, database and the hiring of a dedicated support line operator(s) under the AAP/PSEA unit.
