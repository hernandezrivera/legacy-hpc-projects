Project Summary

In 2025, NRC will be operating in 25 camps in Ukhiya and Teknaf and extending Housing, Land, and Property (HLP) services to eight additional camps, ensuring prioritised service delivery to the vulnerable groups including women, elderly, persons with disabilities, and diverse genders. ICLA’s response will focus on improving access to rights through legal identity and residency documents, access to essential services and addressing HLP challenges. The approach includes raising awareness, tailored legal aid, and due diligence to ensure uninterrupted service access. ICLA will mainstream HLP training and Collaborative Dispute Resolution (CDR), addressing HLP challenges and enhancing community-based dispute mechanisms to promote access to justice option complemented with digital hubs that are close to host communities to enhance social cohesion. Additionally, mobile clinics will expand HLP services across camps to reduce tensions. Using a gender-sensitive approach, following NRC gender dynamic study on HLP issues in 2024 NRC translates these findings to scale and modify its supports on HLP rights and access to justice to address challenging social and cultural norms and administrative hurdles against men, women, girls and LGBTQI.
Legal identity documentation remains challenging, as many refugees lack essential documents like birth and marriage certificates, necessary for HLP claims, healthcare, education and other services. ICLA will help bridge this gap by facilitating access to Family Attestation Certificates and Smart Cards, improving refugees' access to services. NRC will work with RRRC, UNHCR, and stakeholders to identify and resolve bottlenecks streamline suitable solutions for civil documentation processes.
Counselling and awareness sessions empower refugees to secure vital documentation, enabling access to civil justice and essential services. Information sessions will raise awareness of documentation needs and HLP rights, with assisting in completing documentation for CiC submission and reducing exploitation risks. Community leaders, Majhi and Imams trained in CDR will mediate HLP conflicts, facilitate communication, and ensure effective collaboration between the community and stakeholders. The Hub will serve as a Dispute Resolution Centre, supported by trained and mentored Rohingya youth men and women paralegals. NRC will work with RLOs, refugee volunteers and community networks to extend access to H2R target population within camps for delivery of vital services.
Additionally, NRC’s protection from violence programme addresses humanitarian needs due to intercommunity conflict in Teknaf, especially in camps 26 and 27. This programme provides case management, individual assistance, and self-protection activities, involving community members in identifying and addressing risks through capacity building. This initiative complements NRC’s ongoing humanitarian efforts, responding to increased displacement due to conflicts and upholding Principles of Engagement to enhance community approaches, local decision-making, and support networks. Projects focus on prevention and response to conflict and violence, e.g. supporting communities to develop communication trees, early warning systems, emergency and contingency plans. These activities focus on soft components to reinforce existing plans by communities that are exposed to cyclical displacement and violence. Community leaders will be trained in conducting service mapping and engagement with humanitarian actors, coordination mechanisms functions and training and assertive communication to improve their ability to meet the needs of their communities.
NRC collaborates with local authorities to enhance climate resilience, supporting community-driven initiatives to foster self-reliance and reduce aid dependency. Disaster risk reduction strategies address climate vulnerabilities, improving safety and preparedness for both refugee and host communities.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The Rohingya refugee population in Cox’s Bazar exceeds 967,000, facing challenges in accessing essential rights and services. The Norwegian Refugee Council (NRC) addresses critical needs related to Housing, Land, and Property (HLP) rights, legal identity documentation, and justice access. NRC’s assessment revealed that 48% of the host community lost documents, 16% lack proper documents, and 38% struggle with obtaining or renewing legal identity and civil documents. Barriers include high costs (25%), lack of procedural information (33%), and lengthy processes (40%). A significant issue for refugees is the lack of essential documents, such as birth and marriage certificates, vital for legal recognition and access to services like healthcare, education, and livelihoods. Marriage registration poses challenges due to verification requirements and informal costs imposed by Majhis, affecting women particularly. Child marriages, polygamy, and domestic disputes are prevalent, leading to intimate partner violence and unsupported divorces. NRC’s protection monitoring indicates recurrent barriers to Legal and Civil Documentation (LCD) and HLP rights, with many unaware of the benefits of identity documents. Varying registration practices and administrative limitations within Camps in Charge (CiCs) worsen these issues, with reports of corruption and abuse further complicating access. Protecting HLP rights is essential, as many families reside in temporary shelters on public or private lands without formal agreements, leaving them vulnerable to eviction and financial exploitation. Roughly 90% of Rohingya renting land lack formal agreements or rent receipts, risking eviction without notice and arbitrary rent hikes. In the past year, about 350 families were evicted, and nearly 900 faced eviction threats. Major eviction drivers include rental disputes and back payments (56%), security issues like forced recruitment or abduction (18%), and other reasons (26%) such as landowner demands, family disputes, and environmental risks. Incidents in Teknaf’s camps 24, 25, 26, and 27 are increasing, affecting social cohesion between refugees and host communities. Armed groups employ coercive tactics, like false promises of payment or repatriation, to recruit refugees for conflict, endangering the vulnerable population. Knowledge gaps on legal rights limit refugees' engagement with protections. The October 2024 report on HLP Rights for Women highlights that many, especially women, lack awareness of their HLP rights and how to assert them. Enhanced justice access is crucial, as fear and limited legal understanding hinder refugees from seeking redress. According to the Q3 Joint Protection Monitoring Report, refugees face barriers from discrimination fears and legal unfamiliarity. In Teknaf and Ukhiya, 79.8% of respondents know about informal justice systems, yet awareness among women (68.3%) lags behind men’s (77.1%), especially in Teknaf. This limited engagement among women reflects barriers in male-dominated justice systems like the “Shalish.” Economic dependency, mobility restrictions, and inadequate legal knowledge increase women’s vulnerability, exposing them to HLP rights violations. Women and gender-diverse individuals heavily rely on legal aid groups, with 30.3% in Teknaf and 27.6% in Ukhiya using these services. Resource limitations in legal aid particularly challenge women, with 26.9% in Ukhiya citing poor allocation and restricted legal support as significant barriers. Only 1.6% of women feel they have equal opportunities to acquire HLP rights, facing barriers such as social status, mobility restrictions, and patriarchal norms. Cultural norms around inheritance and gender roles deepen dependency cycles. Additionally, LGBTQI and Hijra individuals in the camps encounter obstacles to HLP rights due to social discrimination, safety concerns, and lack of formal support systems.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

To ensure protection is central to the protection project in Cox's Bazar, NRC will mainstream protection principles throughout the project cycle, from assessment and design to implementation, monitoring, and evaluation. NRC already conducted a SiP and gender analysis in 2024 identifying the key areas to enhance accessing to services through ensuring safety, dignity, active and seamless participation of community in programme and accountability at each stage with a risk matrix mitigation measures to reduce risks for beneficiaries and uphold their rights. Additionally, NRC on yearly basis conduct a protection-focused needs assessment, including consultations with vulnerable groups (e.g., women, children, persons with disabilities) to understand their specific protection needs. These insights will shape project design to ensure that all activities are safe, accessible, and responsive to the distinct risks faced by different groups.During roll-out, project staff will receive training on protection principles, including confidentiality, do-no-harm, and safe referrals. This will help ensure that all team members can identify and address risks during project activities. NRC will implement age- and gender-sensitive approaches, such as gender-segregated spaces for services, to promote inclusivity and safety.We will strengthen our existing complaint and feedback mechanisms that allow participants to report protection concerns or provide input on the project. This feedback will help us address emerging risks, improve our approach, and make real-time adjustments.Post-implementation, we will evaluate the project’s protection outcomes, assessing whether it effectively mitigated violence risks and promoted safety and dignity. Lessons learned will inform future programming, enabling us to refine our protection mainstreaming approach based on community feedback and protection monitoring results.

2.3 - Age & Disability

NRC protection programming embedded the Age, Gender, and Diversity (AGD) approach to report disaggregating data by age, gender, and disability in its programme at implementation, monitoring phases and promotes beneficiary participation during community consultations. This is to remain responsive to the unique needs of adults (ages 18–59), the elderly (over 59), children under 18, and persons with disabilities, across different gender groups providing each group with tailored support in housing, land, and property (HLP) rights, obtaining legal identity documentation and to overcome barriers to essential services.NRC’s Safe and Inclusive Programming (SIP) promotes assistance that aligns with the diverse needs of affected populations. SIP prioritises safety, dignity, accessibility, and participation through all stages of the project cycle. This approach minimises risks of unintended harm and creates inclusive response enabling age groups and individuals with disabilities to access services and exercise their rights. NRC has established a child and gender safeguarding policy and provides regular training in Psychological First Aid (PFA) for staff, ensuring that they can sensitively handle cases involving children, women, and other vulnerable groups. Regular refresher sessions on safe referrals, and utilising the Washington Group Questions (WGQ) to identify persons with disabilities, enables NRC to deliver targeted assistance and tailored referrals.NRC’s inclusive service delivery supports primary beneficiaries and benefits to children and other vulnerable populations, improving housing security and access to documentation and services. NRC’s outreach strategies includes hubs, help desks, community network and mobile team aim to ensure that all community members, including women, the elderly, and those with mobility challenges, have equitable access to services which is amplified with collaboration with specialized agencies and coordination WGs.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

NRC ensures that the perspectives and needs of affected populations- men, women, boys, and girls, are integral throughout every phase of programming. This commitment is evident through structured feedback mechanisms, transparent operations, and ongoing engagement with beneficiaries. Accountability is reinforced through regular community and partner meetings, which disseminate essential project information, clarify objectives, and set expectations around timelines and outcomes. These meetings offer a platform for beneficiaries to voice concerns, ask questions, and influence project adjustments, strengthening trust and ensuring that project goals align closely with community expectations.Community representatives and volunteers play active roles in processes such as beneficiary selection, targeting, verification, and distribution, ensuring that these actions remain fair, transparent, and inclusive. By involving local voices in these critical stages, NRC enhances community ownership and social cohesion, addressing specific needs through shared decision-making. Feedback and complaints mechanisms are further strengthened to ensure culturally sensitive methods for collecting and responding to feedback, with prompt action taken on all types of feedback. NRC actively uses trends from feedback analysis to adapt service delivery, demonstrating a commitment to learning and responsive management that continually enhances program effectiveness.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

NRC is committed to integrating the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) into all levels of its protection programming. As an active member of the PSEA network in Cox’s Bazar, NRC participates in coordination meetings to enhance the safety and integrity of all supported environments. This commitment is reinforced through aligned messaging, the use of information, education, and communication (IEC) materials, and regular reporting.The comprehensive PSEA strategy includes mandatory training for all NRC staff, volunteers, and partners on ethical conduct, the Safeguarding policy, and reporting procedures in accordance with NRC’s Code of Conduct. This training equips personnel to uphold high ethical standards and understand their responsibilities in preventing exploitation and abuse. Additionally, NRC will remain actively engaged with PSEA network and will be sharing the bi-annual reporting for the year 2025. NRC implements robust measures to address incidents of sexual exploitation and abuse, reflecting its dedication to ethical conduct and community trust. Guided by a survivor-centred approach, NRC provides confidential and accessible reporting channels that empower individuals, especially women and children, to report incidents safely. The ICLA team conducts awareness campaigns to inform communities about their rights and the protections available to them, ensuring individuals are well-informed on how to report concerns.Continuous training on PSEA principles for all NRC staff fosters vigilance and awareness across the organization. By embedding a zero-tolerance policy within its operational culture, NRC creates a safe and ethical environment for both staff and beneficiaries, ensuring that protection services are effective and trustworthy.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

NRC’s protection programming in Bangladesh integrates climate action by prioritising eco-friendly and resilient service delivery methods, which directly support climate adaptation and mitigation efforts. Currently, NRC operates five digital hubs that offer essential legal services in a paperless format, greatly reducing resource consumption and emissions. This approach underscores NRC’s commitment to sustainable interventions that meet the needs of affected populations while minimising environmental impact.Through its protection programming, NRC strengthens climate resilience by ensuring that individuals have access to essential legal identity documents and housing, land, and property (HLP) rights, both of which are crucial for the adaptive capacity of communities affected by climate change. By providing secure HLP rights, individuals are better positioned to invest in resilient housing that can withstand environmental shocks like flooding or cyclones. The programme's mobile teams and digital platforms enhance access to these services while reducing reliance on physical resources and supporting a low-carbon delivery model, further aligning with climate-conscious operational standards.NRC plans to expand its climate-friendly practices within protection programming. In 2025, NRC will lower its carbon footprint through using recycle materials specifically during awareness raising campaigns, the solarisation of digital hubs, sustainable hub management practices- green hub initiatives, using digital platform for case management, training and workshop. The planned launch of KOBLI- a digital case management platform, will facilitate a fully paperless workflow, embedding climate-conscious practices within NRC’s service framework. By incorporating these innovations, NRC’s protection programming will deliver essential services in a manner that aligns with the organisation’s mission to promote resilience and environmental responsibility across its operations.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

NRC Bangladesh has implemented Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) measures to ensure continuity in legal assistance, civil documentation, and housing, land, and property (HLP) rights during emergencies. These measures include business continuity strategies, emergency response Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs), and contingency funds tailored to the needs of communities dependent on NRC support.A continuity plan has been established to maintain critical legal and counselling services during disruptions caused by natural disasters or displacements, ensuring refugees and host communities can access essential legal aid and documentation. This plan aligns with NRC's goal of delivering life-saving and sustainable assistance. NRC’s SOPs detail procedures for activating emergency interventions, allowing staff to promptly address legal needs in crises. They clarify roles within the NRC team and include protocols for conducting Rapid Needs Assessments (RNAs) to evaluate legal and HLP needs following emergencies. The Emergency Response Roster ensures standby capacities for conducting RNAs and deploying mobile legal clinics to support vulnerable populations.Contingency funds are available for emergency-related legal and documentation needs, enabling swift mobilization of resources to address immediate community needs during crises. Through these EPR measures, NRC supports the continuity of essential services, ensuring affected communities retain access to their rights and protections. To further enhance operational capabilities, NRC will develop a Business Continuity Plan (BCP) focused on preparedness and resilience, identifying potential threats that could disrupt operations and prioritising critical protection services.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

NRC protection programming in Bangladesh prioritises engaging youth to foster community resilience and address critical protection concerns. This engagement empowers young people while mitigating risks of exploitation, particularly labor exploitation prevalent in displaced and economically unstable settings like Cox’s Bazar.NRC's utilises Collaborative Dispute Resolution (CDR) training to equip youth with the skills to mediate conflicts, especially around Housing, Land, and Property (HLP) rights. This involvement enhances their leadership abilities and promotes social cohesion and stability within communities. NRC ensures that all youth engagement adheres to international child protection standards, particularly for those under 18, by creating safe and supportive training environments.The program actively raises awareness about legal rights, labor laws, and protection mechanisms, assisting youth recognise and report exploitation. For young adults aged 18-24, NRC encourages participation in community decision-making, enhancing their capacity to contribute positively to social and economic stability.Additionally, NRC addresses structural vulnerabilities by connecting youth with skills-building and livelihood opportunities, thereby reducing dependency on unsafe labor. Gender-sensitive programming acknowledges the unique challenges faced by young women, providing safe spaces and support to ensure equitable participation.Overall, NRC's protection programming exemplifies a holistic approach to youth engagement, integrating conflict resolution, rights awareness, and socio-economic empowerment. By equipping youth with the skills and knowledge needed for active citizenship, NRC supports both immediate protection needs and long-term development outcomes, fostering a future where youth are central actors in community recovery and sustainable development.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

NRC’s project in Cox's Bazar promotes self-reliance and resilience among Rohingya refugees through livelihood support, skills training, and community-led safety initiatives. By offering locally tailored vocational training and conflict resolution skills, refugees can enhance their economic opportunities and reduce dependency on aid.ICLA’s efforts focus on sustainable community empowerment, including mediation training, establishing dispute resolution committees, and setting up paralegal centers for community self-sustenance. NRC’s ICLA and education units are conducting studies on labour laws and market opportunities to expand livelihood options in the informal sector. Community-led initiatives empower refugees to address safety and violence, with training in mediation and violence prevention fostering conflict resolution skills. These actions support immediate protection needs while building resilience by cultivating leaders who can sustain community safety.
