Project Summary

In line with CWG guidelines and drawing from previous experience, SI plans to aid 980 households (5,880 individuals) in Al Hassakeh and Ar-Raqqa governorates with regular Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA), using a referral system from various health, nutrition, and protection organizations. SI aims to deliver 3-round cash assistance to 880 HHs (5280 individuals) and 6-round assistance to the most vulnerable 100 households (600 individuals), aligning with CWG recommendations. Vulnerability criteria include health issues, malnutrition in children under 5, high health expenditures, and low income. SI will verify households based on these criteria, conduct visits, and initiate monthly cash distributions. For three-round MPCA, SI will target inter-sector referrals, providing $120 per round, while six-round MPCA will support households with chronic needs, with beneficiaries receiving $120 per round for six months. it is important to note that based on the CWG guidance, SI budgeted $130 for monthly assistance while providing an assistance of $120. This practice was adopted to anticipate any changes in the monthly amount of assistance suggested by the CWG. Distribution points will be accessible and secure, with gender-separated lines and tailored financial literacy messages. Beneficiaries will be pre-registered with a Money Transfer Agent, with identity checks during distribution, and a help desk for feedback established. There are no conditions attached to cash distributions, though health referrals will receive information on available medical facilities. SI may refer malnutrition cases to WASH teams for additional support, and market assessments confirm the viability of MPCA.
The Syrian conflict has severely devalued the Syrian pound, leading to hyperinflation and a significant decrease in the purchasing power of households in Northeastern Syria (NES). The past 6 months witnessed an increase of 37% of the SMEB cost in USD, primarily due to increases in water, bread, eggs and vegetables, reaching a record high of 150 USD. This economic strain has pushed even non-displaced Syrians into vulnerability, with 93% of households in Al Hassakeh city struggling to access sufficient food, mainly due to financial constraints. The inflation and loss of SYP value, also was noticed in a sharp increase of 58% of the SMEB costs in the past 6 months. Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) emerges as a vital tool to aid vulnerable households, preserving their dignity, autonomy, and aiding in local market recovery.
In the areas of intervention, SI continuously monitor the market functionality ahead of providing cash assistance, ensuring the availability of the main food, WASH and NFI commodities and goods in the market. Additionally, SI with other Cash Actors in NES regularly participate in the JMMI exercise led by REACH by providing continuous inputs on market functionality and goods availability.
Through its referral system, and adapted methodology for the assistance, SI gives further focus on health and PWD cases, with adapted assistance. A protection risk assessment is conducted, in addition to community consultations ahead of the initiation of the project, to ensure the cash assistance is provided in a safe manner.
Considering the complexities of referrals and the higher risk of assistance duplication, SI consults with relevant working groups (Cash & FSL) to map the geographical presence of humanitarian actors. If any risk of duplication is identified, SI engages with the CWG and relevant partners in coordination meetings, respecting data protection policies to mitigate any risks of duplicated assistance.
