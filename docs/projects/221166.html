Project Summary

Since 2016, the "Anglophone crisis" has caused immense suffering in the South-West and north-west regions of the country. The economic, social and political marginalization of these regions has generated to resentment, violently repressed, since 2016. The number of displaced persons has been steadily increasing since November 2017. This crisis, which has become a humanitarian crisis, affects displaced populations and hosts mainly in the South-West accentuated during the presidential elections of October 2018 with new population movements in the North West and South West regions and to Nigeria. In December 2018, it is estimated that 246,000 people are internally displaced in the Southwest, more than 105,000 people in the Northwest, while 86,000 have fled to the Western and Littoral Border Regions (OCHA). In line with our emergency response strategy being revised, Better Earth International (BETA) plans to strengthen its operational presence in Muyuka and Tiko subdivisions of Fako Division in the Southwest region to meet the urgent needs of a worsening humanitarian situation. An exploratory mission was carried out in September 2020 (self-funds from BETA) then a partners’ sectoral evaluation in Gender Based Violence (GBV) / protection in the big summer holidays in August 2023 (self-funds from BETA) with field partners in Muyuka (AFCI, DEN PLANET) and partners in Tiko (Humble Friends and Mukoko Foundation) to understand the complexity of the context and the urgent needs of the affected population (host and displaced).
The finding is worrying:
 Lack of training of health personnel and other actors in terms of types of GBV, clinical management of rape cases.
 Lack of access to dignity kits for women and girls
 Lack of access for pregnant and lactating women to health facilities due to costs for transport and consultations medical and psychosocial support structures for survivors of GBV do not provide safe and confidential care. These survivors prefer to stay at home and avoid stigmatization.
 The use of prostitution as a survival mechanism to feed the household, pay rent and provide shelter has increased, with an increased risk of contracting sexually transmitted infections.
 The increased presence of armed groups has amplified the vulnerabilities of host and displaced populations with increased risks of kidnapping, sexual assault, rape, physical violence etc
Vulnerable populations, especially children, women and girls, are the first victims of widespread violence and worsening living conditions. The 2023 Humanitarian Needs Overview indicated women and girls and children are the greatest population in need of humanitarian support, with protection an urgent need. The 2024 Humanitarian Response Plan, the protection cluster also recalled the urgent need to implement awareness-raising activities on sexual and gender-based violence, case management, psychosocial support, capacity building and GBV training. Though more Returnees have been registered since the beginning of the conflict especially in 2024 (Reasons for return mentioned during the Multi Sectoral Needs Assessment 2024 included improved security in area of return, access to cultivable land, lack of assistance in area of displacement, Health care and protection facilities, supplies and workers also continued to be targeted in Fako division, while civilians in need of protection and medical services continued to be affected by attacks and movement restrictions, with increased protection and GBV risks. BETA has positioned itself on emergency support for the GBV / protection needs of the displaced populations and host communities of Fako division, in connection with recognized expertise, particularly through the support of the affected populations in the South West
currently, our partners in Muyuka: AFCI, DEN PLANET and Humble Friends and Mukoko Foundation in Tiko, with whom we have collaborations since 2020, implement in the South-West.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Gender-based violence continues to be one of the major protection issues in the North-West and South West (NWSW) regions. From January to October 2022, 8,668 cases of GBV were reported. The NWSW continue to face restricted access to life-saving services due to constant lockdowns and roadblocks that prohibits meaningful service delivery. About 90 per cent of the survivors are females, 19 per cent are children, and 6.5 per cent are persons living with disabilities. Better Earth International (BETA) plans to strengthen its operational presence in Muyuka and Tiko subdivisions of Fako Division in the Southwest region to meet the urgent protection needs of a worsening humanitarian situation, through a coordinated approach of protection service delivery with other protection actors. An exploratory mission in September 2020 (self-funds from BETA) then a partners’ sectoral evaluation in Gender Based Violence (GBV) / protection in August 2023 (self-funds from BETA) with field partners in Muyuka (AFCI, DEN PLANET) and partners in Tiko (Humble Friends Association and Mukoko Foundation) to understand the complexity of the context and the urgent needs of the affected population (host and displaced). The finding is worrying: Lack of training of health personnel and other actors in terms of types of GBV, clinical management of rape cases. This limits GBV survivors access to comprehensive first line GBV prevention and response services such as medical, legal, MPHSS support Lack of access to dignity kits for women and girls. The use of prostitution as a survival mechanism to feed the household, pay rent and provide shelter has increased, with an increased risk of contracting sexually transmitted infections. The increased presence of armed groups has amplified the vulnerabilities of host and displaced populations with increased risks of kidnapping, sexual assault, rape, physical violence etc .... conflict-affected children without a legal identity and so in great support for documentation services such as birth certificates Unaccompanied and separated children who live caregivers that do not provide supplementary care for the children. Many Children grow with poor attitudes and lifestyle due to lack of parental care Vulnerable groups especially heads of households who are persons living with disabilities not usually targeted and reached with support. They are left out from humanitarian supports, and their dependents, children, are thus vulnerable and exposed to sexual violence and other protection risks  Many cases of arrests due to lack of documentation services for adolescent boys and girls, which further cause harm to the adolescents due to lack of identification documents. The partners within these communities do not have efficient capacities to respond to the urgent protection needs in the communities especially to provide targeted protection assistance to the most vulnerable people (men, women, boys and girls, PWD)Through this proposal and in line with our emergency strategy, BETA wishes to consolidate its partnership approach and thus an operating model that strengthens the participation of local stakeholders, promotes innovative approaches and improves the effectiveness of the emergency response. BETA then commits to begin its effective deployment in the South West, starting in Buea and Limbe, and begin 1) capacity building of partners, 2) support to partners for the implementation of GBV case management, prevention, mitigation of risk of violence in targeted communities 3) implementation of a long-term partnership strategy, 4) strengthening of coordination efforts under the protection cluster lead by valuing the contribution and accompanying the active participation of local partners.
