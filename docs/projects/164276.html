Project Summary

The project provides food security assistance through the distribution of food baskets to households affected by the conflict in ( Marrat Tamsrin ) which is related to Idleb, Syria.
The food baskets will secure food security for the internal displaced households for 3 months, beneficiaries will be selected as per the vulnerability criteria set by Al Ameen ( AHS ), the selection criteria include:
1- House headed by a woman, child or elderly.
2- HH with Disabilities “one of the householder members”.
3- Host communities and IDPs living under poor shelter conditions (i.e. destroyed houses or no shelter), and/or living in collective centers “vulnerable groups”.
4- HHs with more than five members.
5- HH has at least 1 Pregnant/lactating woman.
6- HH has three or more kids under 10 years.

4 - Needs

Humanitarian access: There is a major shift in humanitarian access compared to the previous years. The number of people residing in hard to reach locations declined from 4.1 million in January 2017 to 1.5 million people in July 2018. Similarly, the number of people in formerly besieged areas decreased from 643 800 to none. These locations represent a high human and economic cost, as they were exposed to heavy fighting and widespread destruction. • Displacement: Population displacement continues to be the main driver of food insecurity in the country. Currently, there are about 6.2 million internally displaced people, including 1.3 million new displacements of people since January 2018, some of them several times. The largest number of IDPs within the country is hosted in Rural Damascus, followed by Idleb and Aleppo. Returns: About 963 600 IDPs have returned to their places of origin, 58 percent more people compared to 2017. Furthermore, 23 400 refugees also returned to the Syrian Arab Republic from neighboring countries. - According to also to “HNO,2019” the total population of Maaret Tamsrin, district, including Ariha, Ehsem, and Mahmbel subdistricts, is 250,121 individuals, about 86,520 are people in need (PIN), around 51% of the total population are females and 17% are children under 5. The total number of children between 6-59 months are 42,520 children, and the total number of PLWs is 19,759 women.

5 - Implementation of activities

1400 Food baskets distribution in Maart Tamsrin to the IDPs and host families of widows, spinsters, and poorest families, food baskets content depending on materials available in the local market, and in line with the implementing region habits. 1- Al-Ameen will maintain coordination with the humanitarian actors having similar operations in the project area to avoid assistance duplication. 2- pre-registration: Targeted families will first be identified. Families will be selected based on WFP beneficiary selection criteria, this includes determining their existing need under food & monthly income, the family profile & category, displacement condition and time, and food consumption score. 2-1 Al-Ameen will undertake pre-registration activities.3- Filtering the collected data: Al- Al-Ameen will identify the beneficiaries based on WFP criteria. Then Al-Ameen will apply filtration for the collected data 4- conduct door to door visits to verify the collected information and prepare the final list of beneficiaries, that according to each field visit and interview with families who already provided information during the pre-registration process. 5- Each family identified as the beneficiary of the food baskets will be given an electronic barcoded card which they can utilize to obtain FR each month at the FDP. 6- The final distribution list & beneficiary contact list will be printed for each location and the available Excel format list will be prepared. 7- Transport the food baskets “including ready to eat items” to Al-Ameen warehouses.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Although all Syrian population has been heavily affected by the current crisis, due to traditional local norms, women are exposed to additional protection related risks. All types of violence are cited as occurring in opposition areas of Syria, with domestic violence, sexual exploitation and child marriage as top three. While sexual exploitation is somehow new, child marriage and domestic violence are often normalized and ingrained in the culture. Women and girls are exposed to structural and societal risks as the lack of safe spaces and public transportation, limited freedom of movement, poverty, unemployment, displacement, stress associated to war, gender role change, lack of documents. All these poses enormous pressure, men feeling they failed in protecting and securing a good life for their families. With men perceiving women as a threat to their control due to gender roles change, domestic violence has increased in Syria. Whereas men actions are explained as a result of stress, women are often blamed for provoking the violence or allowing it to happen. Parental violence against children has also been reported as increasing. Displacement and living in camps are noted as being particularly unsafe environments for women/girls due to the lack of privacy (in tents or in latrines), overcrowding and mixing of people, poverty, financial desperation, and lawlessness.The WoS Protection analysis reveals that 17 sub-districts in Idleb and 23 In Aleppo are facing severe, critical and catastrophic situation regarding protection. While some services are available, GBV cases are less addressed and the assistance is focusing mainly on survival pathway support and less on community-based interventions, mandatory for mind-set change. Women and girls occupy a central role in AlA’s strategy for 2018-2023. Part of the development approach, AL-AMEEN FOR HUMAINTERAIN SUPPORT seeks to empower women to become more resilient in the conflict setting while securing their safety all level
