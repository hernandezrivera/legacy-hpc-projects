Project Summary

1.27 million individuals are exposed to the risk of explosive remnants of war (ERW),
landmines of an improvised nature, and other improvised explosive devices (IEDs) in Borno,
Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY) states. Over a decade of the ongoing conflict between the Nigerian army and non-state armed groups (NSAGs) in north-east Nigeria has resulted in the widespread contamination of the area by explosive ordnance (EO).
UNMAS Nigeria leads the mine action response in north-east Nigeria to mitigate the threats of EO for civilians and humanitarian actors, as well as to develop capacity for national and state authorities and civil society actors to manage humanitarian mine action activities, based on an accurate understanding of the scale and scope of EO contamination.
UNMAS conducts explosive ordnance risk education (EORE) for IDPs, returnees, refugees and host communities, and implements data collection, mapping of services to establish referral mechanisms for the victims of EO incidents, persons with disabilities and persons with similar needs to facilitate access to immediate and longer term medical and psychological support.
UNMAS also conducts capacity building of national and state authorities as well as local non-governmental organizations (NGOs) and civil society organizations (CSOs) with Training of Trainers (ToT) courses for EORE and non-technical survey (NTS).

1 - Needs

The 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview identifies 1.27 million people as in need of mine action in north-east Nigeria, due to prevalent use of IEDs by NSAGs and widespread EO contamination. According to the 2021 Landmine Monitor, Nigeria has the fifth most EO casualties among mine and EO-affected countries. In the BAY states in 2021 alone, 564 explosive incidents were registered, 248 IED incidents, and 15 ERW incidents, resulting in 168 civilian casualties. The presence of explosive ordnance hinders safe resettlement, returns, movements for IDPs, returnees, refugees and host communities, as well as impedes access to basic services and land for agriculture, livelihood and other socioeconomic activities. IEDs or mines of an improvised nature have been the weapon of choice by Boko Haram factions, and the extensive and indiscriminate use of IEDs is a threat to the civilian population and humanitarian community as they remain active over time if not cleared. The capacity of the Nigerian federal, state and local authorities as well as civilian organizations to conduct mine action activities to protect affected populations is currently limited. These limitations include the areas of conducting non-technical and technical surveys, mapping and analysis of contamination to strategically plan effective and efficient mine action, delivery of life-saving EORE messages, IED disposal (IEDD), support to victims and survivors of explosive incidents, including emergency first responder medical attention. In this regard, it has been established that it is crucial to support the capacity development of the Nigerian national and state authorities, as well as local NGOs and civilian organizations, to mitigate the impact of explosive ordnance, as well as to mitigate immediate threats for refugees, IDPs, returnees, and communities as well as humanitarian actors. The effort of the Borno State Government to resettle all IDPs raised further challenges with regards to the safety of the IDPs. It is crucial that EORE is delivered to those IDPs and NTS are conducted prior to their return. Beyond the nearly 960,000 people in need in Borno, there are also more than 66,000 in Adamawa and nearly 148,000 in Yobe. Land where contamination is suspected should be non-technically surveyed to confirm the presence or the absence of EO. Data collected since 2016 indicate that more than 56 percent of all incidents in the BAY states happened in 7 local government areas (LGAs). Three LGAs, namely Gwoza, Konduga, and Bama, account for more than 35 percent of known incidents. In addition, referral possibilities for the survivors of explosive incidents are limited, making it hard to receive medical, psychological and social support. Therefore, a broader disability inclusion is required in order to address access barriers to those services.

2 - Activities

UNMAS Nigeria intends to continue supporting national capacities, including national and state authorities and the civil society, as well as mitigating immediate threats for the IDPs, returnees, refugees and host communities, towards the vision of National authorities protecting civilians from the threat of EO in north-east Nigeria. This project is In line with the HRP 2022 protection cluster objectives 1) to mitigate and respond to the harm caused to persons who have suffered violence, coercion, exploitation, serious neglect or discrimination, and to restore their capacity to live safe and dignified lives, and 2) to strengthen targeted protection services to address the needs and vulnerability of persons at heightened risks of violence, exploitation, serious neglect or discrimination; enhance their capacities and reduce the risk of resorting to negative coping mechanisms, and the associated mine action outcomes 1) a victim assistance (VA) mechanism and national VA strategy and work plan are established, and 2) civilians and humanitarian actors in north-east Nigeria have increased capacity to mitigate the explosive threats. 8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

EO is indiscriminate, but specific threats and impacts vary according to gender, age and other aspects of diversity. Mine action actors maintain a comprehensive and inclusive approach to planning, implementing and monitoring programmes using local sources to include expertise on gender and protection relevant to the community and to ensure protection for all and to guarantee no one, including women, children, persons with disabilities or other marginalized groups are left behind. EORE activities are planned and implemented with tailored sessions and messages for at-risk groups, mainstreaming the gender perspective, in accordance with the EORE National Standards. A technical note on EORE and disability has been endorsed to promote disability-inclusive EORE activities.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Community members are involved in determining their own risks and priorities for mine action assistance, which ensures the project addresses community needs and priorities and participation in design, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of operations. Systematic measures are in place to collect feedback from affected people to understand the usefulness of the messages and to understand how these messages might be effective in changing behaviour of those who live among or will return to areas with EO. A feedback mechanism through pre- and post- activity assessments and community engagement are part of the mine action approach in Nigeria. In addition, during activities conducted in the framework of the victim assistance pillar, victims of EO incidents and other persons with disabilities, including men, women, boys and girls, are consulted on their priorities and the difficulties they face to access services. This consultation process includes focus group discussions, individual interviews, and consultative sessions with organisations of persons with disabilities.
