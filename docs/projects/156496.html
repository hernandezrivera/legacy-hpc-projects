Project Summary

The School rehabılıtatıon project would serve to expand access to education services for children in the age group of (5-17) ın Aleppo; and all District Which has shown a great number of IDP returnees resulting from the demilitarized zone created for the protection of civilians. The intervention will focus on rehabilitating and re-enforcing school sewage systems and the provision of safe and appropriate school facilities / for 5 schools with a focus on availing inclusive gender consideration spaces for children. The basic renovations would cover; a fully functioning school sewage system, establishing secure separate toilets for boys and girls, renovating and making Weather tight windows and doors that allow for free air ventilation, ensuring wider door facilities for children with disabilities, provision of mobile furniture such as desks and chairs to facilitate a flexible learning process and the possibility for children working alone and in groups.
The primary beneficiaries of this project will be school going age children particularly girls and children with special needs, teachers and the community as whole targeting schools in Aleppo-
According to the Education cluster Gaziantep Hub, one in every three schools in Syria has been destroyed or is dilapidated and this has greatly impeded the access, enrolment, and detainment of children in schools especially internally displaced children/families at an alarming rate of 2.10 million children and youth without access to the education system and the 2018 assessment of 226 camps in Aleppo governorates found that 73 percent of camps and settlements have no education services at all and for the few schools that exist or are operational, about one-third of schools have need of extensive repair or replacement and almost two-thirds have at least one inadequate building feature such as substandard plumbing, roofing, or electrical systems. Hence the need for sufficiently flexible instructional educational space for effective teaching to take place bearing in mind that the right to learn in a safe and healthy environment is a child right that can’t be overlooked.
The project will work to support the Syrian students for IDPs And host community who affected by the crisis, in addition, to enable those who are out of school to return to schools, and those are at risk of dropping out to continue their learning.
Where the project seeks to increase the capacity of the school to absorb more children from IDPS and community.

Needs

The eight (8) year-long Syrian crisis has had a devastating impact on all infrastructure especially schools making it impossible and unsafe for children to attend school. The demilitarised zones agreed along the Euphrates shield have opened channels for some form of stability prompting the desire for many formerly displaced persons to return to their homes however, with the vast destruction of influctructure greatly ruining schools and making it impossible and unconducive for children to attend or resume schooling hence driving a dire need for rehabilitation of these structures to create a safe environment that promotes learning, child growth, and achievement. Almost all school facilities do not have sufficiently flexible instructional space for effective learning and with the creation of safe zones along the Euphrates, many families are starting to return to their homes and would need to re-start or resume their lives and essential services such as fully functional schools and child friendly spaces need become more Indispensable. Many of the schools are a hollow reflection of their previous state with broken glass, cracked windows and non-existent or destroyed doors. According to statistics from the Education Hub, one in every three schools in Syria has been destroyed or is dilapidated and this has greatly impeded the access, enrolment and retaining of children in schools especially internally displaced children / families. Of these schools, about one-third of schools had need of extensive repair or replacement and almost two-thirds have at least one inadequate building feature such as substandard plumbing, roofing, or electrical systems. Hence the need for sufficiently flexible instructional educational space for effective teaching to take place. Research has linked conducive school influstructure/ buildings as an important predictor of student attendance, learning as well as teacher retention. Schools continue to represent a form of safety and hope to many of these children who have suffered a great ordeal of both psychological and physical traumas caused by years of instability and without adequate facilities and resources, it is extremely difficult to serve large numbers of children with complex needs. The most important require needs are summarized as;36% establishment or rehabilitation additional classrooms or education centers, and rehab WASH facilities and teaching supplies. 40%of assessed schools in targeted sub-districts operating schools. 48% of them need maintenance, of the schools working two shifts in this 5 school.
