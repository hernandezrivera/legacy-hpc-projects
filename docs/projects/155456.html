Project Summary

Based on the latest nation-wide food security and nutrition assessments highlighting the high prevalence of food insecurity in urban settings, Food Security Partners will provide time-bound pilot food security assistance from January to June 2019 in high-density and peri-urban areas of Harare and Bulawayo with high levels of food insecurity, extremely low levels of formal employment and an infrastructure deficit.
The assistance will supplement the ongoing efforts of 100,000 of the most vulnerable people to cope with the direct impacts of the 2018/2019 lean season as well as the current volatility in markets and food prices. In close collaboration with other sectors, complementary activities will be provided alongside the food assistance, including a livelihoods support and sensitisation/information provision on issues such as Nutrition, Protection (specifically Sexual and Gender-Based Violence (SGBV)), and WASH.
The urban intervention will use a vulnerability-based targeting approach, prioritizing chronically vulnerable households including older people, under-five children, disabled, pregnant and lactating women (PLW) and households headed by children. Partners will apply a context and protection risk analysis to ensure that new tensions are not created and existing tensions are not exacerbated at either the community or household level as needs exceed available resources. Significant efforts will be made to mitigate any protection risks identified and appropriate complaints and feedback mechanisms will also be put in place to ensure that affected populations are able to voice concerns about the assistance as well as protection and other issues impacting their lives.
In addition to providing time-bound direct assistance to chronically vulnerable households, the pilot is being designed with an active learning and research component through comprehensive monitoring and evaluation to allow partners to learn and draw lessons that can inform future joint interventions for responding to chronic and acute vulnerability in urban settings and identify opportunities to work with partners, including government, to develop a proof of concept for urban responses centred on the provision of essential social services, particularly for Food Security and Nutrition safety nets, WASH, Protection and Livelihoods. As such, the pilot will serve to inform partners and advocate towards the Government of how to scale-up urban humanitarian assistance to better address the scale of needs.

Needs

According to the 2018 Zimbabwe Vulnerability Assessment Committee (ZimVAC) and the IPC analysis, an estimated 5.3 million people in Zimbabwe are projected to be in need of immediate food assistance at the peak of the 2018/19 lean season. Of this total, approximately 1.5 million people reside in urban areas, including major towns and secondary cities. While the rate of urbanisation had previously been low in Zimbabwe, this trend now appears to be reversing. While seasonality has a more indirect impact on food security in urban contexts, trends in the labour market and food prices are affecting the food security of urban populations and interacting with food consumption and quality of diets. The scale and complexity of urban settings, combined with a limited understanding of potential approaches for addressing vulnerability in urban contexts, represent significant challenges to traditional response analysis and implementation. Further to the ZimVAC assessment that indicates that 38% of the urban population is food insecure, Food Security Partners conducted a vulnerability urban assessment in Harare in October 2018 across the five high-density and peri-urban locations (Mbare, Epworth, Tafara Mavuku, Highfields, and Norton) and found that an estimated 70% of households were food insecure, ranging from 52% to 88% across the locations. Negative coping mechanisms vary from reducing the size and number of meals, engaging in casual work/ piece jobs, vending and hawking, urban farming, early marriages, selling household assets to buy food, begging, and waste collection for recycling. The dramatic market fluctuations since September 2018 have adversely affected the food security situation of poor urban residents, unable to cope even with even a slight increase in the price of basic commodities. Based on the survey conducted in October, vulnerable households are giving priority to food purchases at the expense of other basic essential needs, including education and health, with increases in school drop-out rates. These challenges were further compounded by the cholera outbreak in September 2018 which has so far claimed 59 lives from the 10,598 suspected cases to-date, of which over 90% are from Harare district. Epworth, Mbare, Waterfalls, Mabvuku, Tafara, Highfields and Chitungwiza of Harare as well as selected wards in Bulawayo (including but not restricted to Makokoba and Mabuthweni) are high density and peri-urban areas that have been found to be vulnerable to: high food prices, high levels of unemployment, high levels of HIV/AIDS, local infrastructure deficiencies, and low levels of educational achievement. These districts and wards are populated almost exclusively by families and individuals afflicted by or highly exposed to these food insecurity vulnerability factors.

Activities

Food Security Partners will provide cash-based and in-kind food assistance to 100,000 of the most food insecure people in selected urban areas. The assistance is intended to supplement the ongoing efforts of the chronically vulnerable to cope with the direct impacts of the 2018/2019 lean season as well as the current volatility of the markets and food prices. The assistance will provide a short-term cushion for the chronically vulnerable, whose situation has been exacerbated by the direct impacts of the 2018/2019 lean season as well food price shocks, through the provision of a cash-based transfer. It will also increase community knowledge and sensitize on key issues including Nutrition, Protection (and specifically Sexual and Gender Based Violence (SGBV)) and WASH (including Cholera-related information). More specifically, the activities will include:• Provision of cash-based assistance to 63,800 food insecure people, or approximately 15,000 vulnerable households; • Provision of food to Pregnant and Lactating Women (8,800), children under five (12,200), and elderly (5,200) in targeted districts The response will be implemented through Partners (DanChurchAid, CARE, Oxfam, WVI and GOAL) with proven capacity to conduct urban food security assistance, and with operational capacity on the ground in the selected areas. These partners have also been operationally involved in the recent response to the cholera response, and as such will reinforce the link between the proposed actions and the on-going assistance provided in WASH and Health.
