Project Summary

REACH aims to facilitate evidence-based planning and programming for humanitarian actors in Syria by providing information management and coordination support to at least 200 humanitarian organisations REACH will be leading on the implementation of project activities. More specifically, this will be done through the following activities:
• Monthly Market Monitoring assessments
• In-depth Market Assessments
• Camps & Sites Profiling and Camp Infrastructure Mapping
• Urban Damage Assessments
• Rapid Assessments and Situation Overviews
• Support to Clusters and Coordinated Data Collection Exercises
• Monthly Humanitarian Situation Overviews in Syria (HSOS)
• Thematic assessments/Trends Analyses
• Information management to the Northeast Syria Response activities
• IDP Situation Monitoring in Northeast Syria
REACH has designed this intervention on the basis of needs identified in the field and successful experiences in implementing similar activities in south and northern Syria over the past five years. The activities proposed herein aim to address the challenges humanitarian organisations face in accessing information and data to effectively identify gaps, plan and implement a humanitarian response.
This project is already fully funded, but may require additional top-ups later in the year.
IMPACT is a leading Geneva based think-and-do-tank, created in Switzerland in 2010 on the initiative of ACTED and firstly operationalised in 2012. While autonomous organizations, IMPACT and ACTED operate together thanks to a formalised global partnership. Together, IMPACT, ACTED and UNOSAT set up the REACH program in order to promote and facilitate the development of information products that enhance the humanitarian community’s decision making and planning capacity. IMPACT is the lead-organization in REACH and hosts the REACH global team in its Geneva HQ. As sister organizations, IMPACT and ACTED coordinate on field implementation. In all field operations of the REACH program, ACTED provides operational support (security management, office space, cars, national HR recruitment, etc.), while IMPACT provides strategic and programmatic oversight through its global team and is in charge of the validation and dissemination of reports, and external relations.

Needs

The Whole of Syria humanitarian architecture has evolved since 2014, to foster and promote a better understanding of humanitarian needs and gaps, through an improved collective response throughout Syria. The REACH program has been engaging with this process extensively, working closely with OCHA, with hub and with Whole of Syria (WoS) cluster and working group leads, NGO fora, as well as with partners more directly, creating a strong understanding of information needs and priorities across all hubs responding to the Syrian crisis. Being present and engaged with actors across Lebanon, Turkey, Jordan, Iraq, and northeast and northwest Syria, has been a critical component of understanding regional dynamics, as well as the capacity of gathering information among multiple stakeholders. Through this engagement, REACH has observed a greater climate for data sharing and improved understanding of the need for evidence-based programming, opening the way for collaboration and harmonisation of approaches. Nevertheless, two broad challenges remain across the Whole of Syria: (1.) the ability to conduct coordinated large scale data collection on a more regular basis – particularly critical in the highly volatile context, and during key humanitarian milestones; and (2.) the lack of information from areas with some of the most acute needs, such as Afrin in northwest Syria, Raqqa and Deir-ez-Zor in northeast Syria, and areas experiencing sudden escalations in conflict, due to accessibility and security constraints. In both regards, REACH brings a flexible and reactive approach to data collection to ensure that data is being gathered and shared across the widest possible area in the most effective and feasible way possible. As the coordination structures across WoS continue to shift, it is necessary to ensure that new structures have access to information and assessment support. With over six years of experience, analysis, and data in the Syrian context, REACH has the responsibility and opportunity to take stock of the information gathered over previous years, bringing the wealth of data that it has collected, in partnership with other humanitarian partners, to other actors and coordination bodies that continue to respond to the Syria conflict. Furthermore, as humanitarian actors move towards the formalisation of cash-based programming, well-established and regular information on market prices and functionality is critical to monitor the impact of cash programming and ensure the feasibility of cash modalities.

Implementation of activities

The REACH program ensures that coordinated efforts have been established to allow for consistency in data collection approaches, and harmonisation of methodologies across hubs, working groups, and clusters, while enabling context and hub-specific modifications to tools and exercises. Having supported a wide range of exercises to date, including HNO data collection, household surveys, rapid assessments, coordinated partner-led initiatives, as well as both sector and multi-sector assessments, REACH has a broad base of knowledge of operating in the context of Syria, well-established relationships, strong local capacity, and institutional knowledge, as well as an established flexible approach to ensure data can be collected in the most inaccessible areas of the country. This project will enable REACH to maintain and increase its strong network of key informants and enumerators, while expanding activities to support acute information needs across northwest and northeast Syria, further enabling the REACH program to maintain its goal objective of informing an evidence-based humanitarian response to enhance the effectiveness of planning and programming. REACH will continue to support coordinated, multi-sector assessments, support coordination and cluster-led efforts through data collection and analysis, as well as build upon the work of previous years to further expand and diversify data collection approaches. Under this project, REACH will implement the following activities: • Monthly Market Monitoring assessments• In depth market assessments in NES and NWS• Camps & Sites Profiling and Camp Infrastructure Mapping• Urban Damage Assessment• Rapid Assessments and Situation Overviews • Support to Clusters and Coordinated Data Collection Exercises• Monthly Humanitarian Situation Overviews in Syria (HSOS)• Thematic assessments/Trends Analyses • Support to Northeast Syria Response• IDP Situation Monitoring in Northeast Syria• Early Warning Early Response (EWER) Pilot

Protection Risk Analysis

In order to collect information that reflects different sub-groups within society, REACH enumerators are selected from a wide range of backgrounds and origins in Syria. REACH aims to identify an equal number of male and female respondents, as well as male and female enumerators who are then subsequently trained on the methodology to understand the importance of reaching individuals from various demographic groups. In addition, teams are trained to review questions for cultural and gender sensitivities, with specific questions administered to women by women only and to men by men only. Nevertheless, it remains highly challenging to assure an equal number of male and female respondents from within Syria due to cultural norms dictating that heads of households, who normally respond to surveys, are typically male, while enumerators who are able to work at this present time in Syria are more likely to be male. Furthermore, indicators and questionnaires are developed to allow for disaggregation of all data per gender and vulnerable sub-sets in order to inform programming that addresses the needs of unaccompanied women and households with particularly vulnerable individuals such as young girls, pregnant or lactating women, and elderly and disabled persons. Based on the data provided by REACH, partners will be better able to tailor gender specific activities to the specific needs of these groups by providing for example, gender-segregated distribution lines gender-segregated toilet and shower facilities, specific health care items required by women, and by addressing barriers to accessing education, health, markets for vulnerable groups, such as those with disabilities, women with restricted movement. Following a review of previous assessment tools, all REACH tools incorporate indicators to allow for identification of vulnerabilities of specific groups, for example, taking into account specific protection concerns for men, women, boys, and girls. This will also consider other vulnerable groups, such as the elderly and the differently-abled.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Deterioration of security situation or encroachment by other actors - The security situation inside Syria is extremely volatile. Security incidents perpetrated by disparate actors active in these areas have occurred periodically and are likely to pose a threat to field staff. These threats can also include long-range arms and airborne weapons systems of the Government of Syria and outside actors. This could cause delay and may require redesign of some project activities. REACH will notify partners immediately in such case, proposing alternatives that will ensure that the project continues in targeted regions and sectors. Limitations imposed by authorities in Syria - The project depends on the willingness of local bodies to cooperate. At present in Syria there are different actors with different levels of legitimacy, often competing between themselves. REACH will seek close cooperation with the relevant bodies to gain access to target areas and acceptability of proposed interventions. While REACH will target areas where relevant bodies are willing to cooperate, unexpected changes in local dynamics may lead to delays and/or relocation of project activities. Should there be a need to halt operations, temporarily or permanently, areas of coverage can be temporarily redefined. Furthermore, assessments do not include sensitive, political or conflict-related questions. Armed groups demand involvement in the program and in operations - REACH abides strongly to the Joint Operating Principles developed by the NGO community in 2015. REACH will not compromise in any case with the ability to operate according to humanitarian principles and without pressure from any armed groups. Long term presence in targeted areas, local staff from the communities as well as provision of essential services will support ACTED to operate in an enabling environment. However, if pressure intensifies, ACTED and partners will seek collective action with other NGOs and OCHA, and potentially suspend activities in the area. Opposition from extremist groups - The conflict in Syria has led to the proliferation of extremist groups. Opposition from their side against humanitarian aid might lead to important delays in implementation and/or relocation of activities to other areas. In this context, minimized visibility and branding will reduce both the likelihood of this issue, and its potential impact on project activities. Limited access to the affected communities - Changes in the security situation and local bodies’ reluctance to allow access to target areas can result in the inability to reach out to the targeted beneficiaries. This could potentially lead to delay and/or relocation.
