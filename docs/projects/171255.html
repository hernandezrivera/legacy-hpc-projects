Project Summary

The project will support 18000 vulnerable beneficiaries (3000HHs) including 12000 IDPs (67%; 2000HHs) & 6000 individuals from HC (33%; 1000HHs). This will be achieved by ensuring their access to adequate & dignified shelter solutions through an In-Kind Response Modality by establishing a New Camp as scale-up of HA’s ongoing Shelter project, where 3000 Basic NFI kits & 3000 cluster standardized Emergency Shelter Kits will be provided. Designed to meet the needs of a HH of 6, the ESK & BNFI are easily available locally & provide cost-effective/flexible solutions to meet the immediate needs of target beneficiaries. Furthermore, these are fast & simple to deploy. Target beneficiaries will include 5184 men (29%), 7776 women (43%), 2520 girls (14%) & 2520 boys (14%). Furthermore, 60% HHs will be women/child-headed; 3000 (17%) of the total beneficiaries will be elderly & 2700 (15%) of total beneficiaries will be PWDs.
The project is based on scaling up the outcomes of HA’s ongoing Shelter Project in Banadir, targeting IDPs & HCs within the region & is aligned with Somalia’s Humanitarian Response Plan 2020 & will contribute towards the achieving of the shelter cluster objectives.
The project will ensure the NFIs & ESK kits distributed are as per cluster recommendations to ensure the provision of a habitable covered living space that provides a healthy living environment; enabling the beneficiaries’ privacy, dignity & protection.
A comprehensive M&E plan will be developed, including a baseline survey, technical tracking sheets for distributions, post distribution monitoring, weekly/monthly progress reports, monthly financial reports & a project completion report. After the provision of the NFIs to the targeted population, a Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) exercise will be conducted to inform and assess the effectiveness and efficiency of the intervention. Furthermore, a Post Construction Monitoring (PCM) will also be conducted to assess the quality and standard of the shelters constructed. This will ensure effective implementation of project activities, assessment of required quality standards & monitoring progress in line with agreed milestones & targets. This will set a framework for ensuring accurate and effective project reporting; provide a basis for continuous improvement & a mechanism to evaluate the successes, challenges & lessons learned. Monitoring activities will include community representatives, & project staff will ensure they clearly explain the criteria for beneficiary selection, type of assistance provided & procedures for complaint mechanisms.
The project will ensure active/meaningful engagement of community groups in all aspects of the project cycle (needs assessment, beneficiary selection, response planning & monitoring). Close collaboration with village/camp leaders & engagement of target communities will be ensured to enable reach to most vulnerable & in need beneficiaries. Furthermore, project team will ensure consultation with PWDs or their representatives to ensure provision of adequate support & for securing their feedback. To ensure effectively capturing & addressing beneficiaries’ feedback & complaints, a comprehensive Complaint & Feedback mechanism will be established, ensuring easy & equal access of all beneficiaries. This will include a Complaint Hotline number, which will be displayed through IEC material, followed by awareness-raising to ensure target population are informed on how they can voice their concerns to project management & raise any positive or negative suggestions. This will also include devising a Community Feedback and Complaint Form, for recording, following-up & solving any reported complaints. The project will ensure equal & easy access of this form for all the project beneficiaries, partners & other relevant stakeholders.
The project maintains a strong focus on gender with 57% beneficiaries being women/girls. Priority will be given to most vulnerable segments of community.

Project Activities

The proposed project will seek to achieve its objectives by supporting the targeted 18,000 vulnerable beneficiaries (from 3,000 HHs) through the following main activities: 1. Supporting 3,600 individuals (600 IDP HHs) by establishing a new camp and installing 600 cluster recommended standardized Emergency Shelters. 2. Supporting 8,400 vulnerable beneficiaries from 1,400 IDP HHs; with the distribution and installation of 1,400 cluster recommended standardized Emergency ShelterKits (ESKs) in their existing camps. 3. Supporting 6,000 vulnerable individuals from 1,000 HHs from Host Communities with the distribution and installation of 1,000 cluster recommended standardized Emergency Shelter Kits. 4. Distribution of 3,000 NFI kits to the targeted 2,000 IDP HHs (12,000 individuals) and 1,000 HC HHs (6000 individuals). Each NFI Kit will include a kitchen set, jerry can, solar light, ropes, and plastic sheets. 5. Reaching all targeted 3000 HHs (18,000 individuals) with awareness raising activities focusing on containing the spread of COVID-19 pandemic and promoting healthy hygiene practices. 6. An effective Complaint & Feedback mechanism will be established allowing easy and equal access to all project beneficiaries, partners and other relevant stakeholders. This will support in ensuring community feedback on their satisfaction with project interventions and will enable the beneficiaries to raise positive or negative concerns in relation to the support provided. The project teams will also actively encourage beneficiaries to share feedback on the project and feedback meetings carried by the outreach teams will be duly recorded and reported for internal learning review.7. Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) will be conducted 2 weeks after completion of NFI kits distribution. This will allow capturing beneficiary satisfaction on the provided support, assessing quality against agreed standards and monitoring progress as per agreed workplans. 8. Post Construction Monitoring (PCM) will be conducted 3 weeks after completion of all construction work on Shelters. This will support in ensuring the implementation is in line with agreed milestones and targets. Furthermore, this will enable the project team to assess beneficiary satisfaction on the provided support. The above-highlighted activities will adhere to the underlying shelter solutions principlesand standards in the following ways: 1. Selection criteria for target beneficiaries will include prioritization of groups with specific needs, including women (in particular women HHs), children, elderly and PWDs. 2. Engagement of relevant stakeholders including Regional Shelter Cluster, Banadir Local Authority, Camp Leaders and Ministry of Humanitarian & Disaster Management. 3. Promoting active community engagement throughout project implementation to empower target population in building their own shelters with the necessary organizational and material support. 4. To ensure cost-effectiveness and supporting local businesses, the project will prioritise locally available materials to the extent possible, to adequately reflect on the cultural preferences and traditional lifestyle of the target persons of concern.5. The shelter designs will ensure considerations in relation to climate, topography, hazards and environmental risks, national and international minimum standards, livelihoods, and the local availability of resources i.e. materials, skills and infrastructure. The project will also ensure minimal possible impact on the natural environment.

Mitigation Measures

Risk 1: Beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services.To mitigate this risk, the project will ensure the following:- Ensure the safety & dignity of target beneficiaries by ensuring that all sites based in safe locations, are free of hazards & are clearly humanitarian spaces.- Ensure easy access of the services offered by establishing distribution locations that are accessible to PWDs & are located in close proximity to where the beneficiaries reside. Risk 2: The Risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity in the targeted project area:To mitigate this & avoid potential eviction of 600 families benefitting from the New camp, HA has ensured:- Engaging local communities & community leaders in coordination with regional & district administrations. - Legally secured the land for Shelters through a formal MoU between landowner & community leaders so that HA is in a good position to erect the tents & construct other infrastructures without further concerns. - To avoid possible eviction of other targeted 1400 IDP families in their present camps, HA will maintain a strong collaboration with all relevant stakeholders including Shelter & CCCM clusters, relevant line ministries & community leaders. - HA will ensure that agreed land tenure is long enough through a legal agreement with landlord in the witness of the government/local authority, community leaders & relevant stakeholders. - HA will ensure securing necessary Land Certificates from relevant localmunicipalities before project implementation is initiated, to ensure sustainability of the infrastructure & to prevent potential eviction of communities supported through the project. Risk 3: Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activitiesTo ensure inclusivity for all the targeted groups of the affected population, the project will ensure:- all specific activities of the project i.e. beneficiaries selection, major decision making on project among others etc.; will ensure there will be the prioritization of groups with specific needs, including women (in particular women HHs), men, children both boys and girls, elderly and PWDs. - HA’s MEAL team will review and update the Age, Gender and Diversity (AGD) commitments, their implementation and reporting against the set indicators. Specific commitments include ensuring staff and local stakeholders are trained on AGD (Age, Gender and Diversity) approaches, assessments and responses to take into consideration the diverse needs of men, women, boys, and girls.- HA will ensure gender balance in the recruitment of project staff. - A gender and age analysis will be included in the project’s needs assessments and reflected in one or more of the project’s activities and outcomes. Risk 4: Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanismsTo ensure easy and equal access of all project beneficiaries, partners and other stakeholder to a comprehensive Complaints & Feedback Mechanism, the project will ensure: - Promoting and enhancing quality and accountability of the project to ensure the success of the project.- Ensure a dedicated Complaint Hotline number, which will be displayed through IEC material, followed by awareness raising to ensure target population are informed on how they can voice their concerns to project management and raise any positive or negative suggestions. - A Community Feedback & Complaint Form will be devised to record, follow-up & solve any reported complaints. Ensure equal & easy access of this form for all beneficiaries, partners & relevant stakeholders. - Actively encourage beneficiaries to share feedback and ensure feedback meetings carried by outreach teams are duly recorded & reported.

Please provide further explanation

- HA initiated operations in Somalia in 2017, and has extensive experience of delivering several Shelter/NFI focused interventions including supporting 387 floods affected households in Bardhere (2018) with emergency shelter kits/NFIs; 489 droughts/conflict-affected IDP HHs with the provision of plastic sheets and blanket (2018); 280 displaced HHs with plastic sheets and blankets (2019); and in collaboration with IOM supported 600 HHs with the provision of NFIs (tent, rope, mosquito net, spoons, mat, plastic water container, cooking pot, aluminium cups, plates, blanket and knife).- In collaboration with the Federal Ministry of Humanitarian and Disaster Management, HA is delivering a similar Emergency Shelter project in Mogadishu, where we are establishing a new Model Camp which will support 600 HHS from poorest communities. The project will also establish complementary services in the camp including water points, communal latrines, markets, child-friendly spaces and solar lights. HA has maintained strong coordination and engagement with relevant line ministries, Shelter and CCCM clusters and the local communities to ensure effectively and quality implementation and following best practices. - To ensure the sustainability of the proposed interventions, the project willmaintain close collaboration with the Somalia Shelter and CCCMclusters (national & regional levels), Ministry of Humanitarian & DisasterManagement (Federal & State levels) and relevant Local Authorities including DistrictsOfficials from Banadir regions. This will ensure that the project activities are inline with local government plans/priorities are delivered with the approval ofrelevant ministries/line departments and do not replicate any services offeredby them. - To promote community engagement, ownership and buy-in on project interventions; the project will ensure close engagement of the target communities. Also, to ensure that the targeted beneficiaries maintain their normal life, the project will support the beneficiaries to have access to a durable emergency shelter where they will not suffer from the ever-changing climatic and weather conditions which will contribute to their resilience. - The principle of Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) will be observed by ensuring an effective beneficiary feedback mechanism.- The proposed project will also contribute to the fight against the spread of the deadly coronavirus pandemic. All project intervention will be delivered in compliance with the Shelter cluster guidelines to Mitigating COVID-19 risk in IDP settlements. Decongestion measurement will be considered while constructing a new settlement to reduce the human density of the settlements in order to maintain social distance and reduce the transmission of the virus.
