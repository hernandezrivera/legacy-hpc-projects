Project Summary

The NGO is working closely with implementing partners in North West Syria to respond to remaining post-earthquake needs in Idleb and Aleppo governorates. The immediate needs including Health & nutrition, WASH, Education and GBV. Regarding livelihoods and needs for MPCA, 15.4M people are in need of early recovery and livelihoods according to the 2024 HNO. The project provides Water trucking, hygiene kists, educational activities for the students, GBV services, MPCA and life-saving health consultations and nutrition services.
MPCA: the NGO supports a local partner to establish a scoring matrix for selecting beneficiaries for cash transfers, with the amount based on a standard food basket. The exact value of the transfers and the number of transfers per family will be determined based on regular consultations with the community (this flexible funding can be used for MPCA, NFI, or direct services, depending on the evolving needs of beneficiaries). The planned reach for MPCA is 2,275 HHs for a single transfer of 150 USD each.
Protection Risk Assessment:
Risk 1: Exacerbating real or perceived inequalities. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigations: Area selection based on evidence-based criteria and conflict sensitivity analysis; establishing a clear communication-with-communities strategy and a feedback mechanism; coordination on area-based response to ensure fair distribution of efforts. Risk 2: Excluding vulnerable communities from access to ER assistance. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigations: Community-level engagement informed by a gender and power analysis and analysis of local conflict dynamics; community-led design through an equitable process that supports participation of marginalised groups (e.g. women, youth, PLWDs, IDPs etc.) through dedicated consultations, affirmative action and gender transformative approach. Risk 3: Exacerbating local tensions. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigations: Dedicated accountability mechanisms; clear communication on the policies guiding SC and partners’ engagement with authorities; clear communication about the benefits of multi-actor engagement for sustainability. Risk 4: Exacerbating local power dynamics. Likelihood: low. Impact: high. Mitigations: Awareness raising to emphasize the temporary nature of CfW activities to avoid creating pull factors; daily rates aligned with sector guidelines and practice in the area of implementation; service rehabilitation based on community prioritisation and plans communicated to local technical authorities.

PSEA:Risks: Women and girls not able to access services or participate in income generating activities due to sexual harassment; exploitation and abuse by aid workers; female heads of HHs and adolescent girls in particular risk of sexual exploitation in exchange to services (Voices from Syria, 2022); risk of not reporting SEA for fear of retaliation; lack of awareness on reporting channels; stigma and shame and fear of losing on the services making it difficult for the NGO to hold staff/partners involved accountable. Mitigations: the NGO has a Child Safe Guarding policy which includes PSEA. To mitigate the risk of PSEA during the assistance delivery, the following will be included: mandatory training and regular refreshers on Child Safeguarding, Safe programming and PSEA policy to NGO and partner staff; working closely with the WoS PSEA TF to strengthen feedback, reporting and response mechanisms for SEA; conducting community awareness and sensitization sessions on PSEA with emphasis on reporting and ensuring safety of survivors through referrals; regular participatory safety audit to identify protection and safety concerns arising as a result of project implementation and developing action plans to mitigate identified risks collaboratively with the community; including PSEA messages and topics in all IEC materials and training sessions conducted under the project; ensuring that centrality of protection is embedded in all projects to mitigate risks and prevent potential harm. Monitoring: NGO and partner staff will continue regular monitoring of key program activities using standardized indicators.
