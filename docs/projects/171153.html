Project Summary

The objective of the project is to provide lifesaving emergency water supply, sanitation and hygiene promotion services for conflict, drought, flood and Covid-19 affected people, vulnerable host communities and people in disease prone areas, mainly women and children including strengthening of communities’ resilience to cope with the impact of emergencies and recurrent shocks. During the proposed emergency WASH project design and implementations women and girls will be consulted and WASH facilities will be designed and located to ensure the specific needs like privacy, safety and dignity of women and girls. In addition, the project is also targeting to reduce the risk of violence against women, children and vulnerable groups while accessing water and sanitation facilities by providing gender segregated and disability friendly WASH facilities and establishing water supply facilities within 500 meters’ distance of the settlements and sanitation facilities within 50 meters. The emergency WASH response will also be implemented by considering the needs of people with disabilities. People with disabilities will be represented in the community consultation process, beneficiary identification and on design and location of WASH facilities. In addition a certain percentage of emergency WASH facilities will be constructed with disability friendly components like ramps and support facilities to cater for people with disabilities

Project Activities

Output 1: 850,000 emergency affected people (178,500 women, 153,000 men, 263,500 Boys, 255,000 Girls) have temporary access to adequate and safe water. Activity 1.1: Provision of water for 500,000 people affected by emergencies (AWD/Cholera, drought, flood and conflict) through water trucking/vouchers, O&M services (15L/person/day for 45 days) Activity 1.2. Provision of water for 350,000 people affected by emergencies (AWD/Cholera, drought, flood and conflict) through water source chlorination/disinfection and HHWTs. Output 2: 175,000 emergency affected people (36,750 women, 31,500 men, 54,250 Boys, 52,500 Girls) provided with access to appropriate sanitation facilities (single sex and equipped with hand washing facilities). Activity 2.1 Construction of 3,334 gender segregated emergency latrines with handwashing facilities for 100,000 people. Activity.2.2 Desludging and repair of an estimated 2,500 latrines / cesspits /septic tanks in institutions and densely populated IDP settlements, to benefit 75,000 people.Activity 2.3: Support clean up campaigns and/or cash for work solid waste management to improve the sanitary conditions in IDP settlements and marginalized communities.Output 3: 1,200,000 emergency affected people and vulnerable host communities (252,000 women, 216,000 men; 372,000 Boys and 360,000 Girls) provided with hygiene and sanitation promotion messages and emergency WASH supplies including Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) kits. Activity 3.1 Procurement, prepositioning and distribution of critical emergency WASH supplies and equipment including hygiene kits to meet WASH emergency needs for water point disinfection and household level water treatment to benefit at least 1,200,000 people. Activity 3.2: Training and deployment of 1200 community hygiene promoters for hygiene promotion and sanitary surveillance at Households and health and nutrition facilities. Output 4: 320,000 people (67,200 women, 57,600 men, 99,200 Boys, 96,000 Girls) provided with reliable and sustained access to basic water services through rehabilitation and construction of new strategic water sources.Activity 4.1: construction of 20 new strategic water supply systems.Activity 4.2: Rehabilitation, upgrading and/or development of existing 80 water supply systems and water points. Activity 4.3: construction/upgrading of 120 shallow wells with hand pumps. Activity 4.4: Train/support community based and public private partnership management structures for 170 water supply schemes for sustainable water supply managementOutput 5: 50 schools and 100 health facilities provided with WASH Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) activities including provision of WASH facilities and hygiene promotion activities benefiting 220,000 people (26,400 women, 13,200 men, 92,400 Boys, 88,000 Girls) Activity 5.1: Construction of new or rehabilitation and upgrading of existing water and gender segregated sanitation facilities in 50 schoolsActivity 5.2: Construction of new or rehabilitation and upgrading of existing water and gender segregated sanitation facilities in 100 health and nutrition facilitiesActivity 5.3: Hygiene promotion and distribution WASH IPC supplies in 150 institutions (50 schools and 100 health and nutrition facilities)Output 6: Efficiency of Emergency WASH response National, Zonal and/or regional authorities, as well as partners, in targeted high risk areas/districts are prepared to deliver emergency response.Activity 6.1 Support WASH cluster team coordination and information management activitiesActivity 6.2. Support WASH cluster assessments at national, zonal, regional and district level with ensuring government involvement at all levels.Activity 6.3: Training of WASH cluster partners and government counterparts to improve knowledge and ability for emergency preparedness and response.

Mitigation Measures

Risk 1- Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services - Provide WASH assistanceincluding water trucking and hygiene kits distribution in the affected populationssettlements - Monitor climatic conditions and provide anticipatory WASH interventionsin chronically affected areas to reduced displacement - Prepositioning of suppliesclose to target beneficiaries. Risk 2- Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance. - Provide WASH facilities within accessible areas in the affected population settlements - Provide gender segregated sanitation facilities especially in IDP camps, schools and health facilities - Equalrepresentation and consultation of women and girls in all WASH committees andselection of locations for WASH facilities, training of implementing partners staff on PSEA. Risk 3-Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities - Include marginal communities on the priority list of WASH support - Include clear strategies for reaching marginalized communities in all the programmepartnership documents with implementing partners, establish specific targets for marginalized groups like people with disabilities in all project documents with partners. Risk 4- Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms - Establish proper beneficiary feedbackmechanisms for all WASH project interventions - Provide complete information tobeneficiaries on the planned WASH support and distribution packages - Includeaccountability for beneficiaries and beneficiary feedback mechanisms in all WASHproject agreements with implementing partners and monitor implementation

Please provide further explanation

The WASH project will contribute for durable solutions targets in Somalia by providing sustainable assistance and improve living standards of people in chronically emergency affected locations including AWD/Cholera hotspots, flood prone areas, drought historical hotspots or long-term IDP settlements at low risk of eviction. This will be achieved through construction/rehabilitation of water points in AWD/Cholera hotspots and long-term IDP sites, provision of WASH facilities in schools and health facilities in emergency affected areas and hygiene and sanitation promotion. Such interventions will also have a positive impact on nutrition, food security, education and health outcomes for which sustainability is impossible without access to sufficient quantities of safe water and improved sanitation.
