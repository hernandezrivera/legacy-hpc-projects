Project Summary

Throughout the project, Save the Children (SC) will target 58,387 Rohingya individuals to ensure access to and use of safe water and sanitation facilities as well as adopting improved hygiene practices. SC’s WASH programming is basically of institutional focused, such as, SC integrates WASH services into learning centers, health posts/centers, child/girls friendly spaces, and several facility points. At community level intervention, SC only targets those blocks/areas of the camps, from which people usually used to visit SC’s health posts and get services. SC aims to mitigate potential public health risks to the refugees through faecal sludge treatment in camps 20 and 25 of Cox’s Bazar adapting to a cost-effective and energy friendly technology in the limited space and flood-prone areas. Safeguarding and gender considerations will be mainstreamed across all the activities under the project. The project will also strengthen community participation and effective community feedback and information-sharing mechanisms.

1 - Identified Needs

Rohingya Refugees are currently residing in overcrowded camps in Cox’s Bazar and highly vulnerable to COVID-19. The overcrowded and unhygienic conditions increase the potential for the rapid spread of disease. As disease prevention hinges on firmly entrenched WASH practices, and there is a critical need to ensure improved water service, sanitation, hygiene promotion and community engagement. The provision of safe water, sanitation, hygienic living and environmental conditions are essential to protect human health during all infectious disease outbreaks, including the COVID-19 outbreak. To ensure smooth water supplies, sanitation and hygiene practices, repairs and maintenance of existing WASH structures along with distribution of hygiene and dignity kits are highly needed for the refugees to limit the outbreak. In Ukhia, lack of clean water is reported as a concern, while in Teknaf, lack of water is only perceived to be an issue by few key informants, while generally people report to be able to meet even additional COVID-19-related water needs using rainwater (J-MSNA, Refugees, 2020). Women and girls face issues of overcrowding and lack of privacy when using latrines during the day, while at night they risk of harassment and violence due to inadequate lighting. MHM has become more difficult during the pandemic due to delays in distribution of materials and increased difficulty for women and girls to wash and dry their menstrual cloths due to taboos around menstruation, resulting in increased risk of infection. Rohingya refugees face a variety of problems when accessing water points, latrines, and bathing facilities. This shows that even if Sphere standards are met in terms of the number of WASH facilities, access to them is not guaranteed. For water points, access issues include waiting times, distance to water points, and water points not functioning. For latrines and bathing facilities, they include lack of lighting, lack of gender separation, and facilities not functioning. Access is a concern particularly for people with disabilities. Due to excess generation of wastes in camps, there is a need for solid waste management in the camps that will prevent access of children scavenging in search of food and other valuables. The waste area requires fencing to prevent access to un-authorized staff and patients and strengthening against the monsoon weather. Solid waste is often dumped in narrow spaces between shelters and in landfill sites. Provision of appropriate solid waste management activities through active community participation is highly necessary in the camps. Based on the need analysis and considering sector priorities, following are key strategies for WASH programming: covering all institutional facilities (health, education, training centers, nutrition, food distribution), hygiene promotion, behavior change intervention and monitoring at community (for refugees and host communities), considering equitable access for vulnerable people (children, people with disabilities, PLW, old aged), durable solutions like water supply system/network using renewable energy, fecal sludge management unit, female friendly sanitation spaces, child participation in planning and monitoring programme, and provision for menstrual hygiene management.

2 - Activities or outputs

(a) Access to Water: Upgradation, operation and maintenance of existing water supply systems; installation of water supply systems with piped network (2 units); water quality testing; repairs of deep tube-wells; (b) Access to Sanitation: Construction, improvement and rehabilitation to durable, gender segregated latrines and bathing spaces; desludging of latrine pit/septic tank; repairs, maintenance and installation of solar lights; operation and maintenance of DeWATS and Faecal Sludge Management Units; pilot or redesign an on-site FSM system adding the separator buffer as ‘innovation features’ with an advance technology; repairs of access roads towards sanitation facilities; (c) Access to Hygiene Promotion: Distribution of menstrual pad production kits, hand-washing kits, dignity kits, latrine cleaning kits and hygiene kits (with soaps); hygiene promotion activities including menstrual hygiene management, children’s session, campaigns and day observations; engaging volunteers for hygiene messaging through door-to-door visits
