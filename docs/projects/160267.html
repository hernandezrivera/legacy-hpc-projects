Project Summary

Within the Somalia Information Management Working Group (IMWG) framework, REACH in partnership with OCHA and Shelter/NFI Cluster, WASH Cluster, CCCM Cluster, Protection Cluster and Health Cluster, has facilitated three rounds of Joint Multi Cluster Needs Assessments (JMCNA) in Somalia since 2017. The JMCNA’s have provided crucial information on humanitarian needs and access to services among displaced and non-displaced population groups across Somalia and has proven to be a key reference document for the development of the HNO/HRP.
In 2018 and 2019, building on the experiences from the first round of the JMCNA in 2017, REACH strengthened the coordination of the JMCNA, working with more than 50 national and international NGO partners, as well as government and UN agencies. However, moving forward into 2020, crucial information gaps remain in regards to the humanitarian response in Somalia and therefore coordinated and harmonised assessments remain vital.
The FSNAU is in charge of the Integrated Phase Classification (IPC) in Somalia. The IPC informs the humanitarian response especially for the Nutrition and Food Security clusters but has limitation for the other clusters. The JMCNA and the IPC complement each other as they are both nationally representative and do not overlap on the scope, allowing for a complete overview of the needs in Somalia. In the 2020 HNO, the JMCNA and the IPC were the two main indicators used for the calculation of people in need (PiN) and severity in Somalia.
In 2020, REACH will support the coordination of the fourth JMCNA across Somalia. Maintaining and strengthening the coordination with clusters and partners throughout the process, REACH will ensure that findings can directly feed into targeted assistance based on each cluster’s needs. In addition, as this will be the fourth JMCNA, findings can provide an understanding of changes over time and across geographical areas over the past four years. The JMCNA will provide statistically significant findings through household-level data collection in a minimum of 50 districts across Somalia (of 73) and capture the needs of displaced, returning, and non-displaced households. Findings will be representative at district level and further stratified to produce statistically significant findings for both displaced and non-displaced populations. While the tools will be based on the previous JMCNA’s, and correspond to other relevant REACH work streams (e.g. DSA, CSA), REACH will engage closely with clusters to ensure key indicators are captured and in line with the current context and cluster response planning.
REACH aims to continue and expand the support not only to the relevant clusters (WASH Cluster, Shelter/NFI Cluster, Protection Cluster, Health Cluster, Education Cluster, and CCCM Cluster) but also during the HNO/HRP intercluster processes, at the same time than supporting the set up of harmonized assessment frameworks, strengthening cluster information management systems and enhancing capacity. With this project, REACH will support 6 clusters as well as the coordination of the HNO/HRP through technical guidance to the IMWG.

Mitigation Measures

1.a: Exposure to landmines: As the JMCNA is conducted across the country, the risk of exposure to landmines is high in certain areas of the country where the REACH teams go to conduct data collection might be high. To mitigate this risk, every data collection team needs to get a security clearance from the security team who evaluates and cross-check information with INSOS and UNMAS regarding areas with prevalence of these devices. Even during data collection, security monitoring of the situation is closely conducted in communications with the field teams.
