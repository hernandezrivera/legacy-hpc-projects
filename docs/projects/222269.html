1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

This project aims to provide lifesaving and life-sustaining sexual and reproductive health, maternal, and newborn healthcare services (the Minimum Initial Service Package) in Mozambique's conflict- and climate-shock-affected areas. The project will be implemented in the central and northern provinces of Mozambique, including Cabo Delgado, Niassa, Nampula, Zambezia, and Sofala. It seeks to deliver integrated sexual and reproductive health services and maternal health interventions to internally displaced people, returnees, and host communities in the selected affected districts. Project interventions will be carried out by the Government, as well as national and international non-governmental organizations (NGOs) and civil society organizations (CSOs), guided by the principles of the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) for Reproductive Health.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The priority SRHiE activities of UNFPA under the HRP 2025 will include: a) Support the government in coordinating the SRH response during emergencies by identifying lead organizations and participating in various SRH platforms, including the SRH Technical Working Group, Health Partners Group, Health Cluster, and other relevant clusters as necessary.b) Equip targeted health facilities providing SRH services with essential reproductive health medical supplies and equipment, such as Inter-Agency Reproductive Health kits, supplies, and truck-mounted mobile clinics, to enable the delivery of quality, integrated SRH services.c) Conduct integrated mobile health brigades in areas hosting IDPs, returnees, and host communities within the affected districts.d) Strengthen the referral system to ensure the timely transfer of complicated obstetric and GBV cases in hard-to-reach areas.e) Raise awareness of the availability of SRH (MISP) services to increase knowledge and access to SRH/GBV clinical care information and services.f) Train health service providers on delivering MISP services based on identified needs.g) Support the deployment of temporary clinics using SRH emergency tents.h) Collaborate with the government to ensure the availability of a health workforce in targeted static health facilities, temporary clinics, and mobile clinics.i) Support the delivery of comprehensive emergency obstetric and newborn care through containerized theaters in selected districts.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

This project places the centrality of protection at its core by ensuring that health staff are equipped with the necessary capacity to deliver services through a client-centered approach. It emphasizes the full application of humanitarian principles and guiding principles to prevent placing target populations at further risk or harm. The project upholds privacy, dignity, and confidentiality during service provision, prioritizing the best interests of the individuals served. In addition, it adheres to medical ethical principles, including "do no harm," "do good," and "do justice," to ensure that interventions are both ethical and protective.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

This project is based on a result from different comprehensive needs analysis, conducted, identifying areas and people in need of essential health services. Building on this analysis, UNFPA engages in ongoing rapid assessments in the targeted provinces, working closely with communities and stakeholders to ensure interventions remain relevant and responsive to the needs of those in need. To encourage meaningful access to assistance and foster participation and empowerment, particularly for women and elderly persons, UNFPA actively collaborates with other agencies and national actors at the grassroots level, not only to implement in hard-to-reach areas but also to establish feedback and complaint mechanisms. These include initiatives such as community dialogues and the "Linha Verde 1458" complaint hotline. These platforms ensure that community voices are heard, feedback is acted upon, and accountability is upheld throughout project implementation.

6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project is committed to minimizing its environmental footprint and mitigating potential environmental impacts. UNFPA Mozambique and its implementing partners will adhere to the UNFPA Social and Environmental Standards (SES) as outlined in the Environmental Efficiency Strategy (EES) 2021–2025. To achieve this, UNFPA will implement measures such as waste management protocols, the use of eco-friendly materials where feasible, and promoting energy efficiency in operations. Health workers' capacity will be enhanced through training in the provision of quality integrated SRH services, with a focus on infection prevention and reducing environmental health hazards. Training will include proper disposal of used sharps to minimize health risks. Additionally, the disposal of expired condoms, medicines, and obsolete equipment procured under this project will be carried out in compliance with government guidelines on environmental protection.
