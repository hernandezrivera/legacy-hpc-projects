Project Summary

The proposed project seeks to address the protection concerns of vulnerable Lebanese and migrants who are at-risk of or survivors of gender-based violence and child protection issues. The intervention adopts a comprehensive approach including both prevention and response activities undertaken by a staff of multi-functional protection practitioners. The targeted areas are within the Governorates of Bekaa, Beirut - Mount Lebanon and the South, based on communities' needs, gaps identification and existing protection response capacities at organizational level. The intervention is structured through a combined approach: individually-tailored specialised GBV and CP response services, including case management, focused PSS and emergency cash assistance, are provided to medium and high risk survivors and at-risk individuals through, in most cases, already established safe spaces at the Community Development Centres (CDCs); while prevention activities such as non-focused PSS, awareness-raising and information-provision services, are implemented through a community-based approach and are provided through mobile outreach and community mobilization.
The proposed project is integrated within and strengthened by synergies with INTERSOS’ current protection programming in Lebanon which includes case management, psychosocial support, legal assistance, protection monitoring and community-outreach and engagement.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

With a proposed staffing of 9 Child Protection case workers and 9 GBV case workers , INTERSOS anticipates that 540 child protection cases and a further 450 GBV cases will be taken into case management over the course of the year; with 85% of those in case management accessing Emergency Cash Assistance (either directly or through the UNICEF project for CP). Focused PSS sessions will be offered by psychologists to 385 boys and girls in child protection case management and a further 385 individuals in GBV case management expected to access the service. Vulnerable Lebanese will constitute 95% of cases receiving individualised protection services while the remaining 5% will be individuals from the migrant community.GBV prevention services consisting of awareness sessions conducted at community level by mobile social workers will target 4,800 individuals adopting a targeting breakdown of 95% vulnerable Lebanese, 5% migrant community of whom 70% will be female (70% women and 30% girls) and 30% male (70% man and 30% boys).

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

INTERSOS’ programmes are designed to follow the principles of participation, do no harm, confidentiality, safety, non-discrimination, and quality and accountability. The services provided do not to discriminate against any persons based on their gender, age, or nationality. Activities will take place in culturally acceptable places. Psychosocial Support activities will include curriculum aimed at addressing bullying through different media, contributing to the development of an accepting and inclusive learning environment. Gender balance will be taken in to account in the selection process of staff. Protection, Age, Gender and Diversity will be taken into consideration throughout the proposed intervention. INTERSOS has a well-developed child protection/safeguarding and PSEA policies in place, in respect of the Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS). INTERSOS will work with and through existing community networks, ensuring representation of marginalized and vulnerable groups.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

INTERSOS’ rooted presence in the targeted areas has bolstered the understanding and knowledge of the context as well as of the needs of populations. INTERSOS will ensure that feedback mechanisms are put in place to tailor and respond to beneficiaries’ needs and suggestions, and community-based focal points will be in place to facilitate the communication of feedback to relevant entities, in a secure and confidential manner. A component of the program entails working with community members; thus, beneficiaries will be directly involved in leading selected activities. Beneficiaries will be informed of the project's objectives through trained staff, information sessions, leaflets and explanation of INTERSOS feedback/complaints mechanisms. INTERSOS staff will ensure that complaint mechanisms are always available and that beneficiaries are aware of them. Feedback boxes will be set up so beneficiaries can anonymously provide their feedback and a hotline will be shared with communities.
