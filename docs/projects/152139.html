Project Summary

Since 2009 when Boko Haram also called JAS began its brutality in Borno State, many families and communities have been affected in numerous ways. This conflict reached its peak in 2013 and early 2014 following the killing of the sect’s leader Mohammed Yusuf in Maiduguri. Since the conflict began, children and their families have been internally displaced, homes destroyed and livelihood loss. According to the last round (round 10) of the Displacement Tracking Matrix, produced quarterly by the International Office of Migration (IOM) and the National Emergency Management Agency (NEMA), 2.1 million people were displaced within Nigeria and 220,000 others were refugees in the neighbouring countries. More than 56% of the IDPs are below age 18 and over 90% of them live in host communities, with only a fraction reached with limited humanitarian assistance. As a result of conflict-related displacement and exposure to violence and abuse amongst children, which have been compounded by severe lack of social amenities including the two-year disruption of education activities, the levels of psychological distress have increased and continue to impact on the lives of all children and their caregivers throughout North-east Nigeria. UNICEF estimates that over 20,000 children were separated from their families due to Boko Haram insurgency and over 7,000 women and girls suffered conflict related sexual violence. Children and their families have been killed and maimed in direct attacks, indiscriminate attacks and through the landmines used as Improvised Explosive Devices and remnants of war. According to Nigeria MRM reports, 3,803 children have been killed and 7,309 others injured since January 2013. There is a real and increasing dangers and risks of UXOs for IDPs returning to their areas recently liberated by the military from JAS control.
Boko Haram also abducted children, men and women for forceful recruitment to be used in combats, sexual slavery and religious conversions through radicalized means. It is believed that at least 2,000 children have been abducted and more than 8,000 children (mostly boys) were recruited and used by Boko Haram and unknown number of them are associated with Civilian Joint Task Force (CJTF). Both Boko Haram and CJTF have both been listed for recruitment and use of children in the 14th and 15th Annual Reports of the UN Secretary General to the Security Council on Children and Armed Conflict, respectively. No concrete actions have ever been undertaken by the armed groups and government authorities to end grave violations against children. In addition to this, children and mostly girls, have been used as suicide bombers by Boko Haram in North-east Nigeria but also across the borders of the affected countries of Cameroon, Niger and Chad. 89 out of the 255 incidents of suicide attacks recorded between January 2013 and July 2016 in the four affected countries involved 56 children. 89 incidents were recorded in 2015 alone in which 21 girls were used. The trend of suicide attacks seems to be on the decrease in 2016.
Children have been left as orphans and others continue to be used by armed actors in different ways. Through the counter-insurgency efforts by the Nigerian security forces, significant number of children and women are being rescued and released. Following military screening, children have been detained by the Nigerian military on alleged association with Boko Haram. Some of these are stranded without anyone providing for their needs in the communities of return. Grave child rights violations continue to be committed by JAS, Civilian JTF, vigilante groups and members of the Nigeria Security Forces. A lot of people including children have been killed and injured in contravention of human rights and international law. Military uses schools and hospitals for their activities while JAS has destroyed several of them. Schoolchildren are not able to go back to schools.
There is an urgent need to respond to the needs of those vulnerable children and their families. The project seeks to strengthen the community-based system of monitoring and reporting of incidents of grave violations by both JAS and CJTF, but also be security forces of the government, provide both short-term and long term socio-economic reintegration assistance to children associated with armed groups and child victims of sexual violence and other conflict affected children in hard to reach LGAs. It will further aim to build capacity of community leaders and faith-based groups to prevent the grave child rights violations from happening with specific focus on ending child recruitment and use by armed groups.
In June 2015, EYN attended one-day MRM training organized in Maiduguri by UNICEF. Subsequently, the organization picked interest in this field of work and has since been participating in similar discussions at state level. EYN is an active member of Borno State Working Group on Children and Armed Conflict. EYN has partner with UNICEF on Child protection in emergency monitoring and response in five selected LGAs of Borno State in Five LGAs from 15thFebruary, 2016. Under the SSFA EYN worked in Damboa, Biu, Bama and Askira Uba, where it had maximum success in the project.
EYN sign a 12-month contract with UNICEF Child Protection, November 2016 to November 2017 on project titled: “Improved community-based monitoring and reporting on grave violations against children and socio-economic reintegration support to children associated with armed groups and other conflict affected children in Borno state” with an achievement of 1556 children 746 boys and 810 girls. All been identified, registered, referred and supported and still are under the reintegration services. Details of support given to this identified children includes, Access to formal education support, skills training activities, livelihood supports, reinsertion packages to meet up their immediate needs, referrals to other reintegration services/support and quarterly follow up to ascertain the condition and the state of these children in the 5 LGAs (Bama, Askira Uba, Damboa, MMC and Monguno) of project implementation.
ON the aspect of Monitoring and Reporting Mechanism we have 62 incidences which are mostly Suicide bombing, abduction and sexual violence, have been reported and confirmed by EYN MRM Officer through a collaborative effort with the security personnel, NEMA, SEMA and the resident community members.
A refresher training on MRM interviewers was conducted in all the 5 LGAs on Monitoring and reporting of grave violation committed against children, how to report incidences and principles or reporting MRM cases (5Males and 5 Females).
A total number of 224 (M87, F137) participate in the training of community based group on MRM across the 5 LGA’s of Project implementation and Community awareness sessions in Four LGA excluding MMC on the Child Rights and grave violations committed against children in the community and other child protection issues e.g early marriage, prevention of sexual abuse and exploitation with an approximate of 2026 (M926, F1100) adult’s men and women in attendance.

Needs

Under the previous UNICEF-funded 12-month project, EYN identified and offer reintegration support and referral services to 1556 (746 boys and 810 girls) CAAFAG in the LGAs under the project. Despite the progress made in more than 12 months of project implementation, several gaps and needs remain. There is an urgent need to strengthen the system of identification of CAAFAG and provide them with quality interim care to reduce their vulnerability to other grave violations of their rights. Sensitive inquiries should also be made to ensure that infants, very young children and girls are not missed or hidden, especially where analysis of data shows a gender imbalance among identified CAAFAG. The previous project highlighted the need to continue to build the capacity of community structures in a way that not only equips them to address child protection concerns now, but also to ensure sustainability of these structures in the near and longer term when displaced persons may return or relocate back to their communities. In the previous project, community based MRM networks were established. Community structures need to be strengthened in order to effectively respond to protection concerns. To achieve these, EYN will continue to build upon the existing structures by building their capacity through trainings, community awareness/sensitization and meetings with the stakeholders and local actors in the community.
