Project Summary

This project is designed to contribute to General protection Sector objectives and is proposed to targets Rokoro, Sabanga in North Jebel Marra in central Darfur and Tawilla locality in North Darfur State from January-December 2020. it is expected to improve access for general protection services for Persons with special Needs (PSNs).
Through training and support to community based protection, reactivation of community protection structures eg. Networks, women centers and child friendly spaces to strengthen case management, direct support to PSNs, support paralegals to provide needed protection services, referral to service providers eg. GBV and child protection services, medical treatment for serious medical condition, psychosocial support, and provision of mobility devises for PSNs disaggregated by gender, age, disability and diversity
ASSIST will sign memorandum of understanding (MOU) and sign agreement with technical departments at the Ministry of Social Welfare women and child affairs, as well as coordination with cluster coordinators, community based organizations (CBOs), and other relevant partners.
ASSIST will set a benchmark to collect, analyses and use sex-disaggregated data, 53% of staff will be women, 60% of staff will complete basic gender training and learning sessions, and 100% of the project reports will have specific gender results and lessons learned.
Secondly, we will ensure that 100% of the project activities have resource allocation to achieve IASC Gender with Age marker code 4(M) by the end of 2020. ASSIST will also have financial tracking mechanism to quantify funds disbursement for promoting gender and women empowerment.
3% of the project grant will be cash based disbursement to PSNs as Unconditional and unrestricted transfers as it gives the PSNs chance to chose and prioritize their needs. In addition, PSNs that will be referred for medical treatment and ensure that survivors of GBV, Separated and unaccompanied children, persons with disabilities and elderly at risk make decision and prioritise their daily needs that meet their protection concerns.
Will ensure functioning complaints and feedback mechanisms as an accountability to affected beneficiaries who receive assistance or participate in the project implementation, gender and age dimensions are integrated into 86 percent of Country Strategic Plan, and in 63 percent of ASSIST guidance manuals

1 - Needs

As per the Inter-agency assessments conducted in Sabanga North Jebel locality and Tawilla locality in North Darfur from June 7-26, 2019, the IDPs in Sabanga told the assessment team that although the new IDPs feel they are secure, there were restrictions on their movement and continuous threat of forceful return to their villages. In Summary, personal safety and security, family separation, women issues, separated and unaccompanied minors, lack of social services welfare for cases of persons with special needs, and non-functional community based protection structure. In addition, most IDPs have no proper documented figures with breakdown of various vulnerabilities such as women at risk, individuals with chronic diseases, elderlies, and unaccompanied minors among others. The difficult economic situation resulted to limited access to livelihoods means/sources, coupled with extremely high inflation, cash liquidity, high prices and scarcity of bread and fuel led to dramatic increase in prices to above 370% which have affected persons with special needs to access basic services and have increased their vulnerability especial persons with disabilities, elderly, women and vulnerable children. Cases of causalities, injuries, and SGBVs were also reported. Which included kidnapping of young male for short period and released, one child was also abducted and not yet released, 2 women were beaten severely. 126 women were harassed, and all the cases were not reported to the police however, the army was informed. While in Tawilla locality, majority are protracted IDPs and vulnerable host communities, who had their worse nightmares due to the heavy rainfall and flooding that resulted to 485 households completely destroyed with a total population of 2472 persons affected, 758 houses partially destroyed affected 3726 persons including persons with special needs (PSNs) that have been seriously affected. The security situation reported to be unpredictable due to absence of local authorities, police, with increasing restriction of movement for the livelihoods and farming purposes. 2 incidents, robbery and attacks against civilians at the western parts of Tawilla occurred over the last week of the assessment including theft of camels and destruction of farms.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

During design and implementation we will ensure consideration for protective spaces for women and children to provide a safe environment where all basic services eg. WASH. Psychosocial support can take place with confidential, complaints and protection reporting mechanism system has been designed and beneficiaries know where to refer or report incidents of rights violations The day, time, and location of the Protection has been discussed with different community members (age, gender, disability) to determine when/where would be the safest, Working hours during which services are provided are appropriate for all persons, including women, children, and the elderly, the day, time, and location of the Protection services has been clearly communicated using a variety of approaches to all, including those who may have difficulty accessing information points.Adequate levels of female staff with skills and experience working with women and with children exist, vulnerable individuals who may need assistance eg. pregnant women, chronically ill, disabled) have been identified and prioritized by placing them first lists or placed in separate lines,Measures are in place to distribute supplies to vulnerable individuals and those who are unable to travel, Access roads between where beneficiaries live and centers/referral points or where services are provided are safe, If the services point is far away from the shelters, arrangements have been made to assist people to transport their goods so they are not forced to pay for transport themselves, All recipients know how to use and are able to use the materials they have been provided. Confidentiality and privacy are respected in consultation, counseling or personal information sharing with beneficiaries, Outreach services are available for areas that are far due to geographic difficulties or in insecure areas for beneficiaries to access. A system is in place for regularly finding out about new needs, Monitoring teams are in place to assess, for example, whether service delivery locations are adequate and can be used in a dignified way.
