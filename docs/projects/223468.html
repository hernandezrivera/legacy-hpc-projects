Project Summary

UNICEF as the provider of last resort, has the accountability for covering critical needs/gaps remaining as a results of new population displacements or diminished capacity/departure of other humanitarian WASH partners in any state throughout Nigeria. The project is aimed at addressing life-saving WASH assistance to the most vulnerable people affected, displacement (new or continued), flood and mitigate the effects of the current cholera outbreak in the BAY States. This action will also provide complimentary and crucial pipeline support to WaSH partners to better respond to emerging crises and reach an estimated individuals with critical supplies, improve sector coordination within the WaSH to ensure quality and timely assessments and analyses, improve coverage and reduce duplication of services.
Over 357,759 people will be provided with sustained access to safe water through regular water point chlorination (shock chlorination and bucket chlorination) and campaign at community level. Water quality control and emergency water treatment of both clear and turbid water for 50,000 households will be emphasized in communities without communal water sources or relying on rivers and streams as their sources of water. Similarly, 357,759 people will be provided with hygiene supplies. The objective of the project is to facilitate the provision of safe water and practice of personal hygiene through sustained chlorination of water points, safe water chain including use of clean containers for water storage and the use of Aqutabs for household water treatment and provision of replenishment hygiene kits and other WASH NFIs.
The envisaged outcome is prevention of disease outbreaks such as cholera, diarrhoea and hepatitis. UNICEF through the common supply will procure water treatment chemicals (Calcium Hypochlorite 65-70% - HTH, Aluminum sulphate, PUR sachet and Aqutabs) and hygiene supplies. These WASH supplies will be prepositioned and used to provide lifesaving WASH services in the hot spot locations affected by displacement (new or continued), cholera and flood. Procured supplies will be distributed to WASH sector partners by UNICEF in line with the WASH sector's needs/gaps analysis and prioritization
in response to emergency needs, at the request of the WASH sector coordinator using the sector supply pipeline procedure to access the stock.
This will be based on satisfactory needs assessment and justification coupled with other compounding factors such as availability of stocks, ensuring no overlapping with other responses, strategy and soundness of the planned response, as well as the sector priority. The annual requirement of WaSH supply pipeline for 2024 is estimated at $ 12,000.00
This estimate was done collaboration with WaSH sector partners and based on the humanitarian response plan (HRP).
To meet the demands of the provider of last resort (POLR) responsibility, UNICEF may need to mobilize 50% of the funding requirement i.e ($6,058,371) to provide the initial lifesaving response to the gaps, prioritizing areas receiving newly displaced people, disease outbreak such as cholera, hepatitis or other WASH-related diseases including covid-19.
UNICEF will work together with the sector coordinator and through UNICEF facilitators to monitor the use of the supplies. These supplies will supplement routine supplies distributed in these locations, and they will target the lifesaving needs of IDP and newly arrivals and host population. UNICEF will ensure that storage is secure, clean, dry, well ventilated, no exposed to direct sunlight and not too hot. WASH sector will maintain an updated common pipeline

1 - Needs

5 million people have been identified in need of humanitarian WASH assistance across the BAY States The findings from WASH Severity Classification indicate 52% in Yobe,36% in Adamawa and 49% in Borno use unimproved latrines and placed majority of the assessed LGAs in the 3 BAY States between severity 3 and 4 in terms of lack of access to improved sanitation with access to sufficient quantity of water in the assessed LGAs stands at severity 3 (Severe).Women and girls accounting for 56% of the population in need, often are tasked with fetching water far away, potentially exposing them to protection issues. Children accounting for 60% of population in need are highly impacted making them susceptible to WASH related diseases and malnutrition. Persons with disability require adaptive WASH infrastructures-water supply and sanitation, through which their dignity and hygiene are upheld. This dynamic context requires strong sector leadership to ensure appropriate humanitarian preparedness and response capacity, adherence to humanitarian principals, develop strategies and technical standards and support the implementation of nexus activities and/or the full transition to early recovery where feasible.There are currently 47 WASH partners providing life-saving services under the 2024 HRP. WaSH service delivery is hampered by high congestion in camps, limited space to source adequate water supply, poor hygiene practices, open defecation, organizational capacities and chronic underdevelopment. Household level handwashing options have been explored but widespread scarcity of basic WASH NFIs such as soap, detergent, water storage containers due to limited quantity available in markets and high prices have further constrained the household to ensure improved hygiene, increasing communicable diseases. In 2024, heavy flooding resulted in displacement and high rates of acute watery diarrhoea, severely impacting access to safe water, dignified sanitation and hygiene awareness. This has led to negative coping mechanisms, including drinking water from unsafe sources, increased open defecation and poor personal and household hygiene. During the flooding, the situation in over-congested camps deteriorated significantly, limiting access to safe sanitation. The cost-of-living crisis and inflation have limited households’ ability to procure soap and other non-food items, leaving vulnerable populations, especially the elderly and children under five at high risk of diseases. Access to menstrual items is still lacking in over 90 percent of households, according to the MSNA. Menstrual hygiene disposal has been a continuous challenge and sector partners are advocating for re-usable sanitary towels for women and girls.Access to hygiene items and hygiene promotion: Over 65 per cent of affected populations reported a lack of adequate soap for personal and household use. Hand washing prevalence is low: only 45 per cent wash their hands at critical times. Most of those with a knowledge of handwashing and personal hygiene practices report the lack of essential non-food items and sufficient water as a major barrier. Lack of essential WASH items has put at risk millions of vulnerable populations in BAY states with consequences of especially a) Increase of Acute Watery Diarrhoea, including health and nutrition concerns, further increasing the risks of children (especially U5) and women. b) The delayed capacity of emergency response; the IDP population who are mostly targeted at the peak of displacement will have to wait longer or miss out entirely on critical WASH items. c) Loss of dignity to schoolgirls and women who benefit from responses conducted through the common pipeline. d) Lack of support for new displacements including returnees. The pipeline offers flexibility to support the influx of IDPs due to conflict. e) Negative coping mechanism including protection.

2 - Activities

UNICEF will implement seven key activities (outlined below) through various partnerships, Long Term Agreement (LTA) contracts with selected partners and State Govt Agencies under the Basic Cooperation Agreement with Federal Government of Nigeria and Annual Work Plans with the state governments in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe. The project has the following main activities to protect the dignity of the new arrivals and to ensure reduction of risk to deterioration of the vulnerability of the displaced persons or of their health and nutrition status upon arrival: a) Enable water quality provision and monitoring through safely chlorinating water and testing it. b) Provision of immediate response WASH kits and Dignity Kits c) Ensure sustainable availability of core items, while ensuring cost efficiency due to economies of scale, enabling uniform, and coordinated responses. d) Offer a solution that addresses the complex logistical challenges through prepositioning improving capacity to respond to critical humanitarian needs. e) Streamline accountability in all processes of needs verification, response, and post-distribution monitoring. f) Timely response and capacity to support urgent needs, especially beneficiaries affected by health, hygiene, and nutrition issues. g) Restore dignity amongst the affected population through the provision of hygiene kits and menstrual hygiene items This project will focus on 31 LGAs with high IDP influx, flood, and cholera in BAY States. In Borno LGAs 19 LGAs (Askira Uba, Bama, Biu, Chibok, Damboa, Dikwa, Gubio, Gwoza, Jere, Kaga, Kala Balge, Konduga,Mafa, Magumeri, Mobbar, Maiduguri,Monguno, and Ngala). In Adamawa, 13 LGAs (Demsa,Fufore, Girei, Lamurde, Madagali, Maiha, Michika, Mubi North, Mubi South, Numan, Shelleng, Yola North and Yola South). In Yobe, 6 LGAs (Bade, Damaturu, Fune, Geidam, Gujba, Gulani and ,Potiskum) where UNICEF will support AWD/flood response.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project is designed based on community needs identified by the various ongoing assessment and surveillance activities including partner surveys, Sentinel Alert, monitoring activities by LGA facilitators or networks of Community Nutrition Mobilizers. Accountability to affected populations is ensured through the set-up of a rigorous beneficiary complaint and feedback mechanism, embedded in the program design. Feedback will be drawn from needs assessments, post distribution monitoring, key stakeholders’ consultations as well as from analysis of data collected under feedback mechanisms.At all stages, UNICEF separately consults with girls, boys, women, and men, including older people and those with disabilities, to ensure that all programmes are designed to enable access and reduce risks of violence. Ccommunities will be sensitized regarding their right to complain/provide feedback and given assurance of confidentiality. The result of the monitoring is used to feed back into UNICEF’s project design, to reinforce accountability and improve the way assistance is designed and delivered, as well as its responsiveness to the preferences of beneficiaries to ensure safe and equitable access to, and use of WASH services.
