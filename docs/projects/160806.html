Project Summary

The purpose of the project is to improve food security and liveihood in particular for most vulnerable groups, by increased food production and promotion of market oriented livelihood diversification, based on community action and facilitated by relevant state and civil society actors in Lainya and Juba west counties. The project is targeting a population of 5,200 direct beneficiaries, that is, 2,600 beneficiaries from Lainya county and 2,600 from Juba west county.
This will lead to increase in agricultural production, enhancement of food security and livelihood of vulnerable groups including recent returnees and internally displaced people, surplus production, improvement of natural resource management and sustainable land use options and enhancing of State and Civil Society Actors capacities to strengthen formal and informal institutions for playing a key role in food security and promote a process of conflict transformation.
The intervention area is Lainya and Juba west counties. The action will target smallholder farmers and include women as key household providers combining their role in childcare, agricultural production and income generating activities. Vulnerable groups, such as lactating women and young children, the handicapped and people living with HIV/AIDS will be targeted either directly or indirectly (by building the resilience of communities to enhance kinship-based support systems). Youth will be targeted by various activities such as life- and vocational- skill training. The proposed action is in line with relevant policies of GoSS, particularly those of the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry, policy of the Central Equatoria State and their Policy statements in line with social protection of the vulnerable groups in the society.

1 - Needs

Generally, South Sudan has witnessed a reduction in armed violence (UN Security council 25 June 2019), after the signing of the revitalized peace agreement on 12 September 2018 (ACCORD Conflict trends 2018). The increased presence of UNMISS security in counties like Lanya and Juba west prompted the return of refugees and internally displaced persons to their homes (United Nations Peace Keeping 19th January 2018). Central Equatoria and especially Lanya and Juba west counties have seen substantial returns of both refugees and internally displaced people. However, the cumulative effects of years of conflict, violence and destroyed livelihoods have left more than 7 million people or about thirds of the population in dire need of humanitarian assistance and protection in 2019 (UN. 2019). The outcomes of the armed conflict like institutional breakdown, loss of human capital, depleted resources and increased level of vulnerability after peoples’ livelihoods were eroded or collapsed, can still be felt. The conflict and associated economic decline have eroded the Government’s ability to provide consistent basic services to its people. Only 40 percent of nutritional treatment centers have access to safe water, a gap that puts more vulnerable people, particularly women, boys and girls at a risk of malnutrition and disease. Agricultural production systems have eroded with basic subsistence agriculture now the norm, trees have been cut and charcoal is produced widely as common coping strategies to earn some cash. Farmers, who are mostly women, and their families have been displaced from their fertile lands. Annual cereal production has reduced to 25 percent from 2014 to 2017, leaving 500,000 metric tons’ deficit for 2018. Over 80 percent of the total population lives below the absolute poverty line and half of the population will be severely food insecure between January and March 2019, similar to the same period in 2018. The number of people in IPC Phase 4 is expected to nearly double from the same period in 2018, hence the need for increased humanitarian assistance especially for food security and livelihood.In addition, Central Equatoria has good agricultural, agro-forestry and animal husbandry potential and both the Ministry of Agriculture, Animal Resources and Irrigation and the Lainya and Juba West Counties’ Agricultural Departments underline the importance of these sectors for economic recovery. This offers good opportunities for recovery and longer-term development programming. Though central and strategically located few NGOs are active in the field of food security and livelihoods. Capacity of local state actors and non-state actors including civil society is still low, fragmented and fragile. There is thus a need for a practical approach connecting the synergies of state action, Civil Society Organisations (CSOs) and communities in support of community based intervention and action. The proposed action will enhance co-ordination between the different actors and stakeholders in the form of multi-stakeholder platforms and promote participatory approaches to ensure that interventions are built on the interests and capacities of the target communities. While most international aid is still provided in the form of relief there is an effort by the donor community to promote rehabilitation, recovery and longer term development. Based on previous experience, SLDA proposes an action with a duration of 1 year and a budget of USD 240,000. The proposed action is particularly relevant with regard to the creation and protection of productive and social assets, economic reintegration and longer-term rehabilitation. Communities have started to rebuild their agricultural production systems but are constrained by a lack of appropriate inputs and technologies and the need and opportunity to integrate nutrition as a cross cutting issue by creating functional linkages with the health.

2 - Activities

The following sets of activities will contribute to the proposed results:- Promote access to improved local seed varieties;- Establish seed supply systems (community based seed multiplication)- Diversify crops and introduce vegetables- provide food security and livelihood sensitization and education- Work with and strengthen farmer field schools, extension workers and lead farmers- Undertake market assessments and promote commercialization with support of credit institutions and village based loan and credit associations.- Provide training on agro processing, packaging and marketing- Introduce or strengthen improved agricultural technologies- Strengthen horticulture and promote agro-forestry- Piloting commercialization of non-timber forest products (e.g. honey and sheanut)- Improve livestock production via village banking systems and improved animal husbandry techniques- Provide formal and informal livelihood skill training including business development.- Sensitize key stakeholders at County, Payam and Boma level.- Provide environmental education at schools and develop curriculum.- Integrate environmental protection in Village, Payam, County and State Development Plans (including management of bamboo and forest resources)- Introduce appropriate Disaster Risk Reduction mitigation components (e.g. introduction of drought resistant crops and improved food storage practice).- Enhance technical and methodological capacity (by use of participatory approaches) of government extension workers.- Provide refresher training of community based counselors- Train key actors in conflict transformation. Note: Interventions will be based on the interest and priorities of the communities themselves which is one of the best guarantees for their effectiveness. Therefore, beneficiary groups have a key role in the design, implementation as well as monitoring and evaluation of the activities to ensure effectiveness. Special attention will be given for transfer of acquired skills and knowledge to be community driven and not be dependent on outside support. Therefore, low external input strategies are pursued while market driven strategies are promoted by revitalizing markets and support of co-operative forms of action.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

YES, the rights of beneficiaries will be respected. The action will give particular attention the inclusion of vulnerable groups in the project activities as well as to a gender-balanced approach. Female membership in the Boma communities will be actively promoted by the consortium and special community sessions will be held to identify female leadership. The consortium will follow on from this identification by promoting special leadership enhancement sessions for these women, as far as relevant to their functioning in community-level development planning and management. Inclusion of of vulnerable groups is done in a number of ways:Directly by targeting the vulnerable households for the project activities. For example, half of the home garden beneficiaries and all of the improved seed distribution will be to the vulnerable.Indirectly by making inclusion of vulnerable groups a key issue in the development of the Boma and Payam Development Plans. The development of such plans is a participatory process whereby the ideas and opinions of the target communities will be solicited for. SLDA will also participate in the formulation and implementation of policies on the rights for all the people including the vulnerable like women, girls, boys, refugees and IDPs.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project is linked to government systems as various institutions will play an important role with regard to the proposed action:• The Central Equatoria State Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry, Ministry of Gender Social Welfare and Religious Affairs and the Department of Co-operatives & Rural Development will provide overall policy guidance and co-ordination on relevant matters. Counterparts at County- and lower levels will be directly involved in and strengthened by the proposed action. • The South Sudan Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (SSRRC) will be consulted regarding matters related to the return and integration of refugees, IDPs and demobilized soldiers. The South Sudan Peace Commission (SSPC) will be consulted on conflict transformation work. • A number of resource institutions have been contacted and will make information and expertise available in support of the action, e.g. FAO (technical expertise and advice); SNV (value chain development); UNICEF (vulnerable groups, esp. women and children) and DED-SONAD. • The action will work with a variety of CBOs on the ground including farmer groups, women associations, youth groups, parent teacher associations, village health committees and VSLA’s.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, The project supports social protection of refugees and IDPs, it will be implemented in line with the National Refugee Return Framework.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Sensitisation and awareness raising among school children regarding sound environmental management is an important foundation for improved natural resource management. And, as mentioned before, improved natural resource management is key to sustainable livelihood diversification. Some interesting work on environmental education has been done by SLDA with her extensive knowledge on non-formal environmental education which it has provided in some Sudanese refugee camps in South Sudan and Uganda for a number of years. This experience will be pooled in the design and implementation of a non-formal Environmental Education programme and piloted in 12 primary schools in the target intervention area. The GoSS Ministry of Education, Curriculum Development Department, will be engaged and a stakeholder meeting organised towards the end of the pilot to share lessons learnt and good practise. Uptake of a non-formal environmental education as an element in the primary school curriculum will be advocated.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Financial sustainability: The proposed action will be based on SLDA’s developmental paradigm paying respect to principles such as appropriateness (low external input), long-lasting results, participation, community ownership and trying cost recovery whenever feasible. Direct relief or basic service oriented interventions in support of most vulnerable groups may be considered only after due consultation with the Project Steering Committee. There is an important focus on awareness raising, knowledge and skills development and transfer and the establishment of co-operative forms of engagement. The action will as such not make large amounts of money available for initiating project activities. In those cases, where money will be made available it will ideally be done via revolving fund schemes, Village Saving and Loan Associations (VSLAs) or via micro credit institutions (SUMI).Institutional sustainability: there will be a strong focus on strengthening and supporting both formal and informal institutions at community level. This will be done by strengthening the capacities of both state actors and civil society actors in terms of capacity training and by putting training to practise over the course of the project period. Sustainability of interventions: interventions are grounded on the needs and realities of the target groups and build on their strengths and capacities (while addressing their vulnerabilities). The strong focus on community participation in developing and implementing project activities will ensure appropriateness of the project intervention and it activities ensuring lasting impact at community level.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

The following risks have been identified:• In the physical domain: accessibility to the more remote payams is difficult due to poor road conditions and possible collapse of bridges. In such cases people from remote areas will be trained and resourced in the more central areas. • In the environmental domain: climatic variability/global warming has resulted in sub-optimal crop yields since 2005 and put pressure on production systems. Appropriate Disaster Risk Reduction strategies will be developed and promoted in various ways.• The political and security domain: outcome scenarios for the 2010 election and 2011 referendum differ and may result in renewed instability and fighting which will affect the proposed action. Flexible planning is a key strategy while preposition of supplies will be considered and project staff may need to keep a low profile. SLDA strongly believes that a community based approach and focus on community ownership will safeguard sustainability of important project components. Local conflicts (e.g. cattle raiding in areas bordering the agro-pastoral communities) may create serious tensions between various groups and will be addressed by the conflict transformation component.• The economical domain: with staff salaries of GoSS staff dependent on timely transfer of oil revenues there is a risk that the government system may be under severe pressure affecting operational presence at county, Payam and Boma level. Working with community leaders, CSOs and CBOs will guarantee project continuation.• Social risks: such risks will be minimised by working with the various ethnic groups, both men and women as well as adults and youths.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will conduct monthly meetings with the Boma Development Committee, quarterly meetings at Payam- and bi-annual meetings with the County authorities. Participants will include LGAs, CSO, local leadership, representatives from women groups, youth, Illiterate and PWDs representatives of CSOs. Progress made against activities and results, and operationalised in the Boma-, Payam and County Development Plans, will be discussed, challenges identified with the next work plans being prepared. The basis for monitoring and evaluating progress will be the quarterly work-plans in line with the Boma, Payam and County Development plan. These internal evaluation reports will be availed to the external evaluation team and is an important source of information for the external evaluation and will help inform the end term report on best practices and lessons learned. The midterm and final evaluation will be performed by external evaluators.
