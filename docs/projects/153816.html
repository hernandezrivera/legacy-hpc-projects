Project Summary

In 2019 Tdh will continue to assist highly affected IDPs and returnee communities in hard to reach areas and areas of displacement. Tdh will provide specialised protection support to children and their communities to improve access to services, promote community-supported child protection dynamics and increase mainstreaming and understanding of CP concerns among services providers. In line with Tdh capacity and expertise in the Country, Tdh is pursuing an integrated CP and Education approach.
Tdh action in 2019 aligns with the HRP Strategic Objective 1 and 2, and with Cluster Strategic Objectives 1 and 2.
Under HRP CO 1, the proposed activities within the CP response are pronged over the following streams of intervention:
-Culturally appropriate case management services for children at protection risk. It will includes, when relevant, assistance in accessing legal documentation and specialised MHPSS support. Dedicated secured programme will targets the needs of children in contact with the law in detention and for reunification and reintegration to their communities.
-Structured and sustained psychosocial support to reduce risk to children’s wellbeing and enhance resilience skills among children. Activities will be organised to be culturally appropriate in line with a gender equality and age lens, but also to be inclusive for children living with disabilities, marginalised children, vulnerable children at protection risk.
-Adolescents-group’s led projects as a mean to boost peer-to-peer dynamics, sense of self-efficacy and ownership, participation and connectedness. Adolescents girls and boys out of school, exposed to child labor and early marriage protection risks will be prioritized.
-CP system strengthening. Tdh response drawn from an ecological model on Child protection and it aims at strengthening multiple layers of responsibility. The action is pronged over a set of activities focusing on enhancing ownership on CP by communities and governmental staff. Some key activities will be carried out under this specific objectives, among them: awareness raising on CP concerns to promote children and communities’ capacity to protect themselves, identify, prevent, mitigate and respond to child protection concerns. Training of governmental staff on selected CP concerns, including MoLSA staff and Education staff (with a specific focus on CM Teachers focal points for safe identification and referral of cases eligible for a CM support). Parenting skills and parents groups. Capacity building of front-lines social workers.

Under HRP CO2 Tdh will continue to focus on capacity building of CP and non CP actors on CP mainstreaming. In hard to reach areas the action is particularly important to enhance the capacity of limited actors to coordinate with Tdh staff and enhance the scope of the direct assistance of Tdh response.
Tdh programming in CP is strongly oriented to mainstream child safeguarding and protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) within the response, as per Tdh’s relevant global policies.
Tdh programming is framed by a Quality and Accountability Framework, which is informed by the Core Humanitarian Standards and actively seek to establish relevant strategies to link relief, rehabilitation and development.

Needs

In the targeted areas of intervention, CP needs continue to be priority among IDPs population both in camp and out of camp, remainees, returnees and host communities. Limited services provision and insufficient social protection floor affect the capacity of most vulnerable children and their family to access services and assistance. Disrupted community-based safety nets and overwhelmed key service providers, such as the education one, put at risk children’s capacity to recover. During a recently led assessment in Hawija, 75% of the parents stated that their child presents negative behaviour change resulting from the crisis. In particular, major signs of distress mentioned are unusual crying and violent reactions. Between 30% and 45% of children interviewed stated to be working, mostly in pharming and selling. Interestingly, according to the parents sexual and gender based violence affects mostly boys more than girls. For the majority of the interviewed population, lack of access to psychosocial services and loss of hope towards the future are the most significant concerns for children and adolescents. Adolescents, also, are considered to be particularly exposed to protection risks and lack of access to socio-economic opportunities. Another assessment recently carried out in Tal Afar district identifies concerns related to marginalisation and stigma , access to services and high presence of out of school children. In particular, stigma and marginalisation run along religious, ethnical and affiliation-based features. In west Anbar and Salah al Din, similar dynamics are affecting cohabitation and social cohesion to be restored. In all areas assessed and monitored, negative coping mechanisms, such as child labor, drop out from school, neglect towards children has been observed. In specific areas of Kirkuk Governorate and West Anbar sexual abuse and early marriage is observed to be increasing. Most vulnerable children and their families struggle to cope and they experience great barriers in accessing basic services and specialised services, including health services, mental health services, support in obtaining legal documentation and socio-economic services. Together with the lack of services, persistent scattered insecurity and incidents, and inter-communal tensions hampered durable and principled return. At the same time, uncertainty, erosion of livelihood resources and barriers to access basic service also affects IDPs in areas of return.

Activities

Activities under Cluster Objective 1- Support children at risks and survivors through case management service- Support children at risk through child protection legal services (detention representation, guardianship determination)- Support children to obtain civil documentation- Support children who require family tracing through tracing and reunification services- Strengthen community based structures to support prevention of and responses to child protection issues- Build capacity of girls, boys, women and men members of Community Based structures related to CP- Provide sustained, structured psycho-social support (PSS) or resilience activities to children- Provide skills and knowledge for parents and caregivers to better care and protect their children- Conduct awareness raising activities on child protection issues, focusing on prevention and behavior changes- Strengthen the capacity of front-line workers and volunteers to respond to child protection issues- Government social work force including DoSA/ MOLSA, MoJ, MoI- teachers and social workers under MoE (CP Integration in Education)- child protection workers of humanitarian organizations Activities under Cluster Objective 2:- mainstream child protection in other humanitarian actions

Protection Risk Analysis

Continuous risk analysis informs Tdh programming all over the program cycle. The following risks for 2019 within the CP response have been singled out as medium-high ranked: 1) operational access: security continue to be intermittent in most of the areas of implementation, with a specific focus on Hawija, Tooz, Sherqat and Tal Afar. 2) access to most vulnerable marginalized groups: stigma and denial of access to services is observed in many areas of intervention for marginalized groups. The identified patterns is drawn over religious, ethnical and affiliation to armed group-based division. 3) acceptance: prioritization on the basis of needs is challenged by acceptance concerns. The access to most vulnerable population in need of assistance can encounter blame and stigma in particular against beneficiaries perceived as affiliated and marginalised groups. Case management services focusing on culturally and conflict-sensitive cases, such as children from alleged affiliated households, children at risk of recruitment, children formerly associated to armed forces and groups, children in need of legal documentation might put at risk the beneficiaries and the social workers, with greater impact on Tdh presence and operational safety.

Mitigation Measures

The risk analysis takes into consideration protection risks of beneficiaries and staff and it is supported at coordination level and field level by dedicated staff such as: Risk Advisor, Field Coordinators, Liason Officers. Also, a Risk Department at HQ level ensure technical guidance and streamline with internal and inter-agency procedures. Also, dedicated risk analysis are designed to ensure compliance with internal Child Safeguarding policies and PSEA policies. Risk analysis takes into consideration specific needs of boys and girls, women and men, persons living with disabilities. Specific needs of vulnerable groups are also considered and in particular marginalised group, minorities, groups non protected by social floor. 1) Operational access is monitored by dedicated trained staff through a set of key actions carried out by a dedicated team at coordination and field level, among them: field coordinator, liaison officer, risk advisor. Daily monitoring of safe access to the area of intervention it is carried out at base level and through inter-base networking. Access is prepared at the beginning of the operations and all along the programme through the establishing of field-based networking of key informants and by continuous coordination with local authorities, communities national and international actors. Incidents are avoided also thanks to a spread efforts in making Tdh’s mandate and action appropriately known by key stakeholders. Security plan and contingency plan are active to respond to crises and incidents. 2) A challenged access to most marginalised groups it is mitigated by continuous risk assessment, that it is designed to take into account expressed needs and concerns of affected communities, local authorities, national and international actors, host communities. Consultations systematically seeks to collect voices from IDPs communities, host communities, returnees, boys and girls, adolescents boys and girls, women and men, marginalised groups and minorities. A conflict-sensitive lens is applied to all proposed activities and screened with key informants, relevant clusters, national and international actors, communities and locally-hired national staff. 3) Acceptance is built on a set a fundamental activities, designed to ensure accountability to affected communities principles to be put into practice. Continuous consultation with communities inform programming. A flexible approach allows Tdh to re-designed activities according to feedbacks and suggestions. Feedback mechanisms are systematically integrated into Monitoring Plan and evaluation plan. Tools and methodologies are expressively designed and contextualised to ensure voice to women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, marginalised groups. Also, Tdh’s approach within the targeted areas of implementation is conceived to build up on existing resources, including community-based resources and governmental resources. Locally hired staff represent a key informant in ensuring that activities and approaches are culturally appropriated and conflict sensitive.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Tdh is accountable to affected population, whose involvement as a key participant in all phases of the project cycle in crucial to ensure meaningful participation of the beneficiary community. Tdh incorporates and promotes the five IASC Commitments to Accountability to Affected Population and framed its entire response within a Tdh Quality and Accountability framework. 1) Leadership/Governance: Tdh promotes leadership and governance, and ensures that staff is aware that programs and approaches must be in line with accountability to affected population principles. This means a commitment to build on the capacities of local population and strengthen the resilience of people affected, whenever possible. Collaboration and consultation with local actors is crucial to ensure that the implementation of activities meet the needs of the affected population and will encourage beneficiaries to actively participate. Participation of boys and girls, women and men and person with disabilities to decision making is systematically sought. 2) Transparency: Tdh ensures the transparency of communication with the affected population to help both Tdh and beneficiaries make informed decisions. There is a focus on providing clear and transparent information about consequences and risks, particularly related to essential services for beneficiaries. At all stages of the project cycle, continuous and transparent communication on Tdh’s activities and objectives will be conveyed to local authorities all other actors operating in the area, with feedback mechanisms also in place to allow for reciprocal information sharing. Tdh will also observe full coordination and information sharing with relevant cluster and sub-cluster actors both at national and governorate levels. 3) Feedback and Complaints: Feedback and complaint mechanisms that are child friendly and adequate to the age and the gender of beneficiaries are set up. These mechanisms are designed to be inclusive, accessible, confidential and will not expose participants to any harm. Feedback and complaints will be systematically considered, and focus group discussions will be regularly conducted with beneficiaries to gather feedback and report on achievement in integrating feedback in the response. This will allow Tdh to assess the level of beneficiary satisfaction and adapt the design of activities and approach to implementation to meet the current needs of beneficiaries. Tdh will take all necessary measures to ensure operationalization of Tdh’s Child Safeguarding Policy (CSP) and PSEA policy, including the identification of a Child Safeguarding focal point, the setup of safe and accessible reporting mechanisms and the adoption of measures to ensure data protection, confidentiality and dignity. All Tdh employees, partners and visitors are carefully briefed on Tdh’s CSP and must read, understand and sign the policy before starting any assignment, collaboration or visit. 4) Participation: Affected populations will play an active role in decision-making processes, ensuring that principles of accountability is incorporated at all stage of the project cycle. To ensure comprehensive representation of beneficiaries, a particular focus will be given to child participation, specifically girls and children with disabilities. During all activities, children will be encouraged to participate and speak out. A wide range of participatory activities will be implemented to make sure that all opinions, and above opinions of those more marginalized, are considered. Community-based design activities are prioritized. 5) Design, Monitoring and Evaluation: A participatory design, monitoring and evaluation of activities is ensured through ongoing consultation with beneficiaries. Focus groups discussion, bilateral and groups informal meetings and other activities will constantly aim at improving the capacity of the project to better meet beneficiaries’ needs and collect best practices and recommendations.
