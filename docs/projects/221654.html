Project Summary

In the context of South Sudan, children in Upper Nile, Jonglei, and Unity states face significant protection risks, including violence, exploitation, and separation from their families. In alignment with the 2025 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) objectives, ensure mobile or static provision of critical protection assistance and services (lifesaving), including specialized response services. Prioritize a collaborative, child-focused approach, with the engagement of local communities, government, and partners to enhance effectiveness. Responses are tailored to address the specific needs of the individuals, considering age, gender, and disability. The project will, therefore, target a total of 24,900 conflict affected people, 14442 children (7510 girls, 6932 boys) and 10458 adult (5438 women, 5020 men) for child protection in emergencies in Jonglei, Lakes, Upper Nile and Unity state.
To ensure that centrality of protection, this project will address risks face by UASC, vulnerable children and families through Comprehensive case management and CVA activities targeting 7500 children (3900 girls, 3600 boys) including alternative care for UASC and children without appropriate will be conducted by trained caseworkers in the priority locations. The activities will be implemented in line with CP, Case management and UASC SOPs for Plan International and respective technical task forces. Interagency forms for case management and FTR will be used. Additionally, CPIMS+ V.2 system will be utilized for uploading all case for timely and quality support of vulnerable children. Additionally, data protection and Information sharing will be observed in all processes. Plan will support identification, support and training of new and existing foster parents to support UASC and other children without appropriate care.
Psychosocial activities will be facilitated by trained 54 PSS team (20 females, 34 males) in child friendly Spaces, Schools and community safe sites through static and mobile PSS modality especially in IDP, refugee and returnees’ sites or settlement areas to reach 17000 (8840 girls, 8160 boys). Plan will collaborate and coordinate with PSS and MHPSS technical working groups for any support required including roll out of revised CPMS standards 15 and 16 on group level activities and promoting wellbeing of children, adolescents and caregivers. Other activities will include structured PSS activities using interagency PSS manual and Plan CFS toolkits, staff training among others. Plan will conduct awareness raising and companied with 300 sessions to reach children and adults with Child protection messages including key messages on family Separation.
Strengthening 400 (208 women, 192 men) community-based structures in communities to support in awareness raising and protection enhancing information dissemination. Key activities include training of the community structures on CP, GBV, and education essentials, facilitation for smooth running of activities, development and validation of key messages translated in local language, printing of safeguarding materials, training of key stakeholders to support advocacy work among others. Key community leaders and chiefs will also be trained on CP legal framework in collaboration with Government to promote gender equality and mitigate risks faced by women and girls. The trained participants will become change agents in communities to influence positive change and renounce violence including negative social norms.

1. Needs

The Protection Cluster's Protection monitoring assessment conducted in October 2024 highlighted that Child protection and GBV are major pressing issue with alarming prevalence rates. The findings also underscored the urgency for interventions focusing on prevention, risk mitigation and response. Children face significant risks of violence, exploitation and abuse due to conflict, forced recruitment, displacement, family separation and limited access to education, nutrition and health care. Out of 11.29 million affected populations (IDPs, Returnees, Residents), it is predicted that approximately 9.18 million people (81% of the affected population) are exposed to at least one of the top five protection risks. Further analysis shows that 98% of returnees (840K people) are the most protected risk-exposed population group, followed by IDPs at 81% (1,58M). Malakal, Renk, Rubkona, Leer, Ayod, Nyirol and Pibor are among the 39 counties classified as priority 1 and 2 counties. The impact of the Sudan crisis resulted in the arrival of vulnerable population that require assistance, increasing market costs and further stretching people’s coping capacities.On 1 November 2024, an interagency needs assessment was led by the health and protection sectors in Renk. It aimed to identify the needs and possible support that could be extended to the refugees and returnees in the area, as well as members of the host community. Some key findings include, Refugees and returnees are in temporary shelters with limited access to essential services such as clean water, sanitation, healthcare, and mental health support, approximately 8,000 returnees from various ethnic groups (Shilluk, Nuer, Lotuko, and Mabanese) and 4,000 refugees are currently in Jerbena, hoping to settle until conditions improve in their homeland, There have been significant security issues, including physical violence, rape, and killings, particularly affecting women, girls, and people with disabilities. To address the above highlighted needs and risks, Plan International will address and respond to the community needs and risks as part of organizational commitment to contribute achieving and protection CPAOR strategy. Key strategies include delivering integrated gender transformative Child Protection. This strategy will enhance accountability to affected persons, ensure they are supported and upscale 2-way feedback mechanisms. On this HRP 2025, plan will support and facilitate Mental Health and Psychosocial Support and Case management activities.

Other activities will include structured PSS activities using interagency PSS manual and Plan CFS toolkits, staff training among others. 3. Key activities include training of the community structures on CP, GBV, and education essentials, facilitation for smooth running of activities, development and validation of key messages translated in local language, printing of safeguarding materials, training of key stakeholders to support advocacy work among others. Key community leaders and chiefs will also be trained on CP legal framework in collaboration with Government to promote gender equality and mitigate risks faced by women and girls.
