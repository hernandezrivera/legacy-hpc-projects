Project Summary

The project consists of conducting population profiling in multiple municipalities in order to collect disaggregated and in-depth data and analyze it at the household level primarily and the individual level secondarily, while adopting a multisectoral approach. UNFPA approach complements REACH MSNA as it zooms into specific crisis-affected areas and provides in-depth understanding of population dynamics while REACH MSNA will allow to compare between different mantikas all over Libya. It will enable the international community to assess the actual situation in Libya based on a multisectoral snapshot of people vulnerabilities and coping strategies in Libya. When compared with previous snapshots in 2014, 2017 and 2018, this project will allow to monitor the trend of a number of core indicators and coordinate the humanitarian interventions in accordance with the accountability to affected population principle.

Needs

As the socio-economic landscape in Libya has been constantly changing with the political crisis affecting the various local and national technical institutions, reliable data that reflects the actual status is scarce. Such data is mandatory for the international community to plan its response, especially during an emergency. Sectoral and Multi-sectoral data has been collected previously with improved quality as the lessons learned are being taken into consideration. However, the challenges faced are different given the changing contextual situations.UNFPA gained experience in the data collection in the Humanitarian context in Libya through multiple household assessments such as the household Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment 2017 (MSNA) and the Rapid City Profiling and Monitoring System 2018 (RCPMS). Both assessments were implemented in collaboration with sister UN agencies such as UN-HABITAT(RCPMS), UNHCR(MSNA), UNICEF(MSNA), WFP(MSNA). The coordination with other data providers (such as REACH and MERCY CORPS) improved synergies and lowered risks of duplication.The Humanitarian Country Team has enhanced the understanding of the humanitarian situation in Libya, building a stronger coordination system between the international stakeholders and the Libyan governmental counterparts to respond to the needs more efficiently. The information management working group (under the Intersector coordination group) has widely contributed to increase synergies between the different data providers and users.The household MSNA 2017 and the RCPMS 2018 were both used for the Humanitarian Needs Overviews 2018 and 2019 respectively. Despite the challenges faced, notably security, accessibility and political sensitivities, the collection of data was done by Libyan partners, mainly the Bureau of Statistics and Census, which enhanced the national ownership of humanitarian data, an asset for the planning and coordination processes. Even though both assessments have provided multi-sectoral disaggregated information advising the humanitarian process, they still have limited geographical scope. Synergies with other partners to cover other geographical areas will continue to be sought. The Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) continued to be the main source of flow monitoring for displaced people and migrants. UNFPA will scale up the collaboration with IOM to run migrant population profiles and avail migrant population disaggregated data. While preparing the Humanitarian Needs Overview, the quality of data and the national ownership have been thoroughly discussed by Humanitarian Country Team members as a vital component for the Humanitarian response. Stakeholders, including governmental institutions, are aware of the lack of data with reliable age and sex disaggregation on people needs and the impact of the humanitarian assistance. With the changing dynamics in the country, appropriate risk management actions and humanitarian interventions cannot be efficiently foreseen or assessed without the necessary information.The project will not only be a tool to assess the humanitarian needs quantitatively but also an instrument to analyze qualitatively and in-depth some population challenges and problems and monitor the impact of the humanitarian interventions through the trending elaboration with the baseline of previous surveys. Consequently, accountability to affected population and community participation including the most vulnerable will be enhanced.

Activities

UNFPA will build on the Rapid City profiling and Monitoring System project as well as the 2017 household MSNA to conduct the Population Profiling in 2019. The project will be as follows: 1- Consolidate the baseline data on the different sub-population groups targeted by the project.2- Set the list of indicators to be collected to be in accordance with the humanitarian and early recovery needs: the IMWG forum will be used as a platform to coordinate with the members including INGOs and UN agencies on the indicators, the scope of the assessment and the methodology. UNFPA will validate the methodology and the questionnaire of the assessment through consultations with the international community as well as the Libyan counterparts.3-Train enumerators and the assessment team on the data collection tools.4- Collect the data.5- Ensure data entry, analysis and dissemination.6- Determine specific issues to be analyzed in-depth based on the updated data collected.7- Conduct FGDs with concerned parties or population sub-groups on the specific issues.8- Correlate the in-depth quantitative analysis with the qualitative data.9- Ensure dissemination.

Access, safety and dignity of beneficiaries

The project will measure access of beneficiaries by comparing the the number of households who respond to the questionnaire to the overall number of households selected randomly for the assessment. UNFPA has a zero tolerance policy on PSEA. Its mandatory for newly recruited staff to receive the PSEA training prior to deployment. In addition, UNFPA ensures that implementing partners staff are trained on PSEA and supported to establish and sustain internal procedures for reporting and investigations. At the interagency level, UNFPA is leading the roll out of SEA prevention in Libya including the establishment of the in-country PSEA network and community based complaints mechanism.
