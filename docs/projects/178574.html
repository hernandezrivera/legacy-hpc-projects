Project Summary

The goal of this project is to provide non-food items for the IDPs in Jabal Kelly, Al Wifak, Al Darablah, Al Rayhan and Dar Alkiram camps located in Kelly community, Maaret Tamasrin sub-district in Idlib. The intervention meets the first HRP 2021 Objective 1 i.e. to save lives by providing life-saving humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable people with an emphasis on those in areas with high severity of needs; and specifically, the project meets Objective 1.1.
According to HNAP Spring/2021, 97% of individuals are extreme poverty in NWS. 80% HHs in NWS report insufficient income. Maaret Tamasrin has a total population of nearly 287,000 (WoS report) and of whom 214,000 are IDPs. The sub-district is category 4 in severity with 68,000 are classed as PINs. The sub district, in other words, has a very high proportion of IDPs. This plan is therefore targeting the IDPs residing in camps in the sub district. According to ISIMM report of February 2021, the 5 camps in total house 7,320 individuals (1,500 HHs). Project will be in 2 phases, with 2 main activity categories – Activity1-Core/Essentials NFI and Activity2-Seasonal/Fuel. Cloth Masks will be added in each distribution.
Outcomes are in four domains - camping stability, family preservation, adult well-being, and child well-being. The outcomes capture intermediate and longer-term results rather than the experiences of families while they were receiving the shelter aid. Impact of the project can be seen in: Living conditions improved for 7,320 IDPs; Increased chances for families to move to more settled areas in or outside current locations; Improved well-being of the most vulnerable, children, infants, disabled, elderly; and Increased number of families staying together thus protection for children. OB can deliver this project on an estimated budget of 679,362 USD over 12 months with 6 field workers to coordinate and manage operation with Management support from HQ in Gaziantep/Turkey. 1,500 kits of Standard NFI items will be delivered in each distribution in Activity 1 at average cost of $168.89 per kit; 1,500 kits of Seasonal/Fuel in Activity 2 at average cost of 250.00 per kit.
The data breakdown as per ISIMM report of Feb/2021 for all 5 camps are as follow:
Jabal Kelly: 2,323 Individuals, 514 HHs, 543 Girls, 779 Boys, 482 Women, 519 Men
AlWifak: 1,420, 250 HHs, 460 G, 410 B, 300 W, 250 M
AlDarablah: 260 I, 54 HHs, 75 G, 65 B, 65 W , 55 M
AlRayhan: 1,892 I, 397 HHs, 518 G, 547 B, 431 W, 396 M
DarAlkiram: 1,425 I, 285 HHs, 423 G, 426 B, 295 W, 281 M
The project is designed to benefit all population of the camps including women and girls. The aid will be provided to all households without discrimination. The project should reduce the risk of vulnerable women and girls from being exploited in any way due to lack of basic life-saving aid. Basic needs will reduce domestic violence within the family and tension within the camp. Women will be able to stay safe in their own tents and feel less vulnerable. In general, OB will ensure that there is sufficient monitoring conducted by OB to avoid any kind of harm done to the beneficiaries in the distribution. Proper measures will be in place to ensure there is no abuse of power by any members of the implementation team, the volunteers or the IDPs themselves. OB will involve the women participants in the operation to decrease the protection risks in dealing with gender sensitive matters. Also, there is no risk of tension between inter-IDPs or with host communities as all beneficiaries are IDPs. OB also have a strict PSEA and CP code of conduct for their staff to adhere to in the course of the operation.
Due to Covid, OB will arrange to bring the goods to the camp. The aid will be distributed tent to tent observing the Covid SOPs.

4 - Needs

According to the Needs and Response Summary (February 2021), with the crisis in its tenth year, staggering levels of need persist for people throughout Syria. Whilst there has been a reduction in violence in many parts of the country over the past year, an estimated 11.7 M people require multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance. According to HNAP Spring 2021, 97% of individuals are extreme poverty in NWS. 80% HHs in NWS report insufficient income. Rates of household engagement in daily labour are extremely high in NWS (41 percent). Individuals are most frequently found living under the survival minimum expenditure basket in NWS. Maaret Tamasrin has a total population of nearly 287,000 (WoS report) and of whom 214,000 are IDPs. The sub-district is category 4 in severity scale of (1 to 5) with 68,000 are classed as PINs. The sub district, in other words, has a very high proportion of IDPs. This plan is therefore targeting the IDPs residing in camps in the sub district. Olive Branch has selected 5 camps, Jabal Kelly, Al Wifak, Al Darablah, Al Rayhan and Dar Alkiram in Kelly community in the sub district. According to ISIMM report of February 2021, the 5 camps in total house 7,320 individuals (1,500 HHs). The majority are under 18 making up 4,246 of the camp residents. There are 85 individuals with special needs, while 2 cases of Covid have been recorded. The project will begin with a reassessment activity to confirm the needs. Taking into consideration the pandemic threat, Olive Branch will arrange to bring the goods to the camp. The aid will be distributed tent to tent observing the Covid SOPs. Considering GBV and other protection needs, OB will include female workers in arrangement and distribution.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will distribute core/essential NFIs and seasonal/supplementary NFIs over the course of the project. Divided into 2 phases. PH-1: Activity1.1: Distribution/Standard INF Kit plus Masks. (one round immediately)PH-2: Activity 1.2: Distribution/Winterization (seasonal/fuel) plus Masks. (September 2021) The NFI materials will be provided to 7,320 IDPs who are living in Jabal Kelly, Al Wifak, Al Darablah, Al Rayhan, Dar Alkiram camps through a voucher mechanism that the recipients can use according to their requirements. Each HHs will be issued with one coupon per phase in the year starting from Jan 2021. The value of the voucher for Phase 1 is $168.89 and Phase 2 is $250.00. Olive Branch will deploy separate approaches in case of COVID-19 pandemic so we have our SOP procedures to follow during the distribution; all beneficiaries will be advised to stay at home and receive the aid at their door to avoid contact with other beneficiaries and staff. OB will be delivering tent to tent observing COVID measures. The project will begin with a re-assessment activity to confirm the needs followed by the main activity to distribute Essential NFI items at start of project. Depending on the timing, Seasonal/Fuel distribution will be done in time for winter. Post distribution assessment will be carried out by Olive Branch team to ensure all documentations are complete.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

By following the cluster guidelines, the project will address potential protection risks with mitigating measures appropriate to the intervention modality of this project. Oliver Branch assesses the risks to be in following areas. A: Inter-IDPs tension due to varied selection of items – Med/Med Since the aid is provided through cash/voucher approach, and all HHs getting the same amount of aid, and the content is based on cluster’s guidelines and standards. C: Abuse of authority by distribution actors - High/High Olive Branch applies Code of Conduct policy/procedure on all staff along with complaint mechanism to prevent abuse. E: Risk to humanitarian nature of the intervention by armed actors – Low/Med Strong coordination with local civil authorities, camp management, and community actors to prevent interference by armed actors. G: For Cash Voucher mechanism can entice corruption – Med/High Staff will be trained on our Code of Conduct and anti-fraud policy to ensure no corruptive practices occur. For accountability and transparency, M&E will be responsible to monitor and document at all stages of the operation from the supplier end as well as the beneficiary end to ensure the quality, quantity, and efficiency, to avoid risk of corruption. OB also have a strict PSEA and CP code of conduct for their staff to adhere to. As part of accountability to the affected population, beneficiaries will have access to hotlines to report any incidents of misconduct or feedback in general.

There is a risk of abuse of authority in this modality with potential risks related to SEA. 8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Olive Branch have assessed the risks to the proposed intervention and can be summarized in terms of Operation, Financial, Security in addition to Protection discussed in the previous section. In terms of security risks, we estimate this to be low in that the district is sufficiently close to the Turkish border and is considered safer with easy access to the border and markets. OB will also coordinate with the local civilian authorities and the camp management to avoid any kind of interference by any armed groups in our operation. Financial risks are low as we have a well-established financial control, procurement, and anti-fraud policy to ensure the duties are sufficiently segregated for the benefit of transparency, control, and avoidance of any frauds. Since the materials will be sourced in the relatively stable local markets or across the border if required, there is low risk of impact from inflation and price fluctuations. In terms of disruption to our operation due to shortage of required items, we estimate the risk to be low in that the required items and tools are available in the market. In addition, the risk is minimized by having more than one supplier for the items. Distributions will be supervised so as not to cause any harm or tension. The project team will organize a schedule considering the place, time, items, and manner of distribution in advance. The timetable will be made known to the residents in advance. For safety and well-being of the vulnerable beneficiaries, aid will be delivered tent to tent due to COVID risks, The distribution team will be gender balanced to reduce GBV risk. Regarding PSEA we will conduct an awareness session to team members as well the beneficiaries.
