Project Summary

The project is to respond to two Health Cluster strategic objectives SO. 2 “Ensure the availability of acceptable and quality essential healthcare services to vulnerable communities in oPt”; and SO. 3 “Strengthen the healthcare system's capacity to respond to emergencies and build community resilience to cope with the impact of current and future crises”.
This project aims to provide an urgent response to immediate health needs by ensuring access to health and nutrition services for the most vulnerable patients (pregnant, lactating women, neonates, and children under the age of ﬁve, both boys and girls) through the provision of medical supplies, malnutrition treatment and targeted outreach postnatal home visiting services. Additionally, the intervention will provide essential COVID-19 medical supplies (laboratory tests, infection prevention, and control items and PPEs) to build the capacity of the health system to timely and effectively respond to the outbreaks of new and re-emerging infectious diseases.
The project will be implemented in close coordination with Health Cluster partners including WHO, UNFPA, international and local NGOs to ensure complementarity of the interventions for the improvement of maternal, newborn, and child health care.
The number of women and children benefitting from the essential health services provided through the project will be 86,000 across the ﬁve Gaza governorates over a twelve-month period. This includes 36,000 high-risk women and caregivers and 40,000 children under the age of ﬁve (20,000 boys and 20,000 girls), 10,000 neonates (5,000 boys and 5,000 girls). Moreover, 15,000 people, (7,500 female and 7,500 male) will have access to COVID-19 testing; and 350 healthcare workers protected with the provision of PPE and IPC (170 female and 180 male). 18,000 people will benefit from awareness sessions and health education
Additionally, 1.6 million people will benefit from the Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE) project component which will be implemented in close coordination with WHO and the rest of the RCCE partners. The project activities will reach out to 100,000 at-risk populations through household visits by mobile RCCE and vaccine teams. The project activities will also reach 1.5 million people with messages on vaccines and adherence to preventive measures across the media and social media platforms and through the on-ground community engagement.
The project will also provide capacity-building training to 2000 community workers on vaccine promotion, community engagement in emergencies, and behavioral change communication.

1 - Needs

The Health Cluster has identified a total of 1.5 million Palestinians, two-thirds of whom in the Gaza Strip, as requiring health-related humanitarian assistance in 2022. The persisting COVID-19 pandemic, recurring flare-ups in violence and escalation, access barriers, along with a weakened, overstretched, and fragile healthcare system in the Gaza Strip because of almost 14 years of closure, internal Palestinian political divide, and limited human and financial resources are the context and key drivers of humanitarian health needs in the State of Palestine. According to WHO Situation Report on occupied Palestinian Territory (June 2020), 232 items (45%) of essential medications were reported with less than one-month supply availability at the Ministry of Health (MOH) Central Drugs Store (CDS), and 219 items were depleted (42%). This has severely impacted the service provision for newborns, young children, and high-risk pregnant and lactating women. The need for COVID-19 laboratory testing, case management, infection prevention, vaccination, risk communication, and community engagement remains a humanitarian priority. It is anticipated that the COVID-19 caseload will continue to burden the healthcare system. Based on 2021 trends, it is estimated that the incident rate could be, on average, around 78 per 1,000 but could be as high as 108 per 1,000. Despite some progress being made in the vaccination of the Palestinian population, the coverage is still well below what would be ideal for reducing transmissions. Data collected by the Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) in July 2021 shows that 72 per cent of surveyed households in the Gaza Strip and 45 per cent in the West Bank (WB) reported that not all members are willing to be vaccinated against COVID-19. Over a year of responding to the COVID-19 pandemic and redirection of resources from the provision of sexual and reproductive healthcare, obstetric and neonatal care, and emergency nutrition services; negatively affected the service provision. According to MoH 2020 Annual Report, there are about 130,000 adolescent girls, 90,000 vulnerable women, and 225,000 children, including 30,000 neonates need the provision of sexual and reproductive healthcare, gender-based violence care, obstetric and neonatal care, and emergency nutrition services. In addition, the escalation of hostilities in May 2021 and the continued sporadic incidences across the State of Palestine highlights the continued need for expanded and improved maternal and child health services. Maternity and neonatal wards in Gaza are overstretched, which leads to the early discharge of mothers and babies within 2-3 hours after birth with no access to post-natal services, which reduces opportunities for the provision of life-saving interventions. Findings of the external evaluation of the Post Natal Home Visiting (PNHV) program (2018) proved the effectiveness of the UNICEF-supported PNHV interventions for high-risk pregnant lactating women. In addition, a mapping study of the Maternal, Newborn, Child Health (MNCH) system conducted by UNICEF showed that the inconsistent supply of medical supplies negatively affects the quality of MNCH services. Hence, the life of 30,000 newborns which require neonatal health care interventions could be severely jeopardized with intermittent and insuﬃcient electricity supplies. The fragile health system in the Gaza Strip will negatively impact the response to high-risk pregnant and lactating women, where medical services, equipment, consumables, and drugs are insufficient and do not cover their needs. Affected people have been consulted through focus group discussion. Many mothers who have been consulted have reiterated the need for targeted PNHV services in hard-to-reach areas of the Gaza Strip, awareness sessions on nurturing care (e.g., health, protection, responsive feeding, early learning), and intervention services for children with disabilities. This project is designed to contribute significantly to gender equality and gender-sensitive programming, focusing on the needs of gender-speciﬁc vulnerabilities faced by high-risk pregnant and lactating women and their newborns and boys and girls under the age of ﬁve.UNICEF will continue to work in close coordination with WHO, relevant authorities, and other health and nutrition partners to protect children and their families from exposure to COVID-19 and to minimize the risk of mortality. UNICEF will contribute to the efforts to strengthen the healthcare system's capacity to respond to emergencies and build community resilience to cope with the impact of current crises through the provision of medical supplies.

2 - Activities

UNICEF will continue supporting the MoH in the coordination of the Nutrition technical working group in the West Bank and the Gaza Strip. UNICEF Jointly with partners will ensure implementation of the updated MNCH Nutrition Protocol.Following a multisectoral approach and building on the humanitarian-development nexus approach, UNICEF will partner with relevant stakeholders to address the need to strengthen the healthcare system capacity to respond to the COVID-19 outbreak, focusing on maternal and child health services. Furthermore, UNICEF will ensure complementarity with the WHO’s, UNFPA’s, and an international NGO partner’s HRP projects and will jointly contribute to the implementation of the Gaza Every Newborn Action Plan 2017-2022. This will include procurement of the necessary medical equipment; drugs and consumables and other medical supplies to support the provision of services for neonate children, children under five years, and women including COVID-19 response-related supplies; diagnostic kits, and PPEs, ICU equipment, and IPC supplies. UNICEF will also ensure leveraging resources through other partners to address issues related to maternal care such as GBV and referral of cases to relevant services.UNICEF will continue to produce, disseminate and broadcast risk communication messages across all the available platforms. UNICEF will also continue to generate and use behavior change data relying on its partnership with Talk walker (social listening platform), Vaccine demand observatory (VDO), and the global partnership with Facebook to drive the risk communication activities.Also, in partnership with Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE) partners and coordination with MoH, UNICEF will scale up the community engagement and reach out to around 100,000 elderly people and people with disabilities through household visits to get them vaccinated.UNICEF will also scale up its on-ground community engagement activities using social and behavior change approaches to ensure the adaptation of preventive measures as a practice. Activity 1Provide access to healthcare, nutrition, and development care that prevent excessive maternal, newborn, and under 5 years children morbidity and mortality by ensuring availability of preconception care, clean and safe delivery, essential newborn care, safe spaces, emergency obstetric and nutrition services including prepositioning of medical supplies. Activity 2Support MoH COVID-19 response and management of other outbreak-related communicable diseases focusing on the diagnosis, case management, infection prevention, vaccination, and Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE).

3 - Indicators

To measure the success of this project, UNICEF and its partners will use the below indicators:Objective 1: Ensure the availability of acceptable and quality essential healthcare services to vulnerable communities in the State of Palestine.1. # of neonates receiving quality healthcare services in NICUs (Target: 10,000 neonates, of which 5,000 are female and 5,000 male)2. # of < 5 children receiving targeted health and nutrition interventions (Target: 40,000 children, of which 20,000 are female and 20,000 male) 3. # of women in reproductive age, pregnant and lactating women receiving quality maternal and nutrition services (Target: 36,000 women) 4. # of people directly benefitting from awareness sessions and health education (Target: 18,000 people) Objective: 2 Strengthen the healthcare system's capacity to respond to emergencies and build community resilience to cope with the impact of current and future crises1. # of people with access to testing to detect communicable diseases e.g. COVID-19 (Target: 15,000 people, of which 7,500 are female and 7,500 male) 2. # of healthcare workers protected with the provision of PPE and IPC (Target: 350 healthcare workers, of which 170 are female and 180 male)3. # of people fully vaccinated against COVID-19 (Target: 100,000 (above 60 years old, people with disability, 50% female))4. # of people benefitted from RCCE activities (1,500,000 people, of which 50% females)5. # of non-medical staff and community health workers benefitted from training, awareness (2,000 community workers, of which 50% females) UNICEF will oversee project monitoring and will ensure timely reporting. Programme oﬃcers will conduct regular ﬁeld visits to the project sites and review progress reports from implementing partners. Quality checklists will be used to ensure the quality of the interventions. Staff will monitor progress against agreed indicators and verify the validity of reports. The programmatic visits will also assess barriers and challenges and lessons learned. Data will be collected, analyzed, and disaggregated by gender and by age group. Gender will also be mainstreamed by working with other clusters to ensure that clinical management of GBV is improved. Early engagement of selected communities will be enhanced by involving communities and their representatives at all stages of project design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. Target beneﬁciaries have been consulted during the project design through focus group discussion and will be continually involved in its implementation. During the project implementation, channels of communication for feedback, complaints and information sharing will be established through satisfaction surveys and recording goods of services delivered to the end-user, so that assistance is delivered in accordance to the needs and in adherence to the principles of “do no harm”.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

UNICEF safeguards beneficiaries’ rights to safe and dignified interventions. Consent of beneficiaries is usually considered and obtained through partners and implementing agencies joining training and community interventions. UNICEF ensures applying international standards, and WHO standards for medical equipment and medical supplies procured. UNICEF developed feedback mechanisms from beneficiaries to ensure accountability toward affected populations and to mitigate any risks identified by end-users. UNICEF also conducts end-user monitoring field visits to ensure the safety and quality of services and supplies provided.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNICEF conducts independent need assessments prior to the distribution of supplies or materials. UNICEF verify needs through WHO and health cluster members to ensure targeting the most vulnerable people. UNICEF applies multi-vulnerability targeting criteria to ensure equity and impartial interventions are designed.
