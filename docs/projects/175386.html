Project Summary

The project aims at reducing the health inequalities among IDPS through provision of quality, inclusive and responsive essential health services and mitigation of immediate and long-term risks and consequences for mental health and psychosocial wellbeing of the IDPs and host communities in Meconta District.
The project will contribute to the following health cluster HRP objectives:
Ensure quality, inclusive and responsive essential health services sensitive to specific needs of affected population
Strengthen resilience among affected populations through Mental Health Psychosocial services and referral mechanisms

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Nampula Province has been recorded recently an increasingly number of IDPs who are seeking refuge. Majority are children, adolescent girls and boys and women. There is a critical and deteriorating access to health services by the displaced girls and women.The IDPs are currently not benefiting from structural health well-being, as a result of broken normal and traditional community structures, such as extended family systems and formal and informal health systems. This is leading to social and psychological problems or exacerbate existing problems. Some IDPs developed negative coping mechanisms that needs to be addressed while they struggle to meet basic needs. The project intends to address the above-mentioned health challenges faced by the IDPs through activities such as supporting communities’ resilience, promoting mechanisms for social support, and offering services to individuals with more complex mental health needs.The target groups are IDPs in the 04 transition centers in Namialo and the ressetlment center in Corrane, estimated at 12,418, out of which 55% women and girls, while 10% include people from the host communities.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The following activities will be undertaken:Support Meconta District Directorate of Health to improve IDPs access to sexual and reproductive health-care services, including for family planning, information and education;• Support implementation of Action Plans from the already established Health Committees in Namialo and Corrane transit and resettlement centres;• Support Meconta District Directorate of Health to train and empower IDPs Health Focal Points at local health units in Namialo and Corrane;• Provide communication and outreach activities from the IDPs Health Committees;• Support Meconta District Directorate of Health to improve M&E data collection, reporting and dissemination;• Establish (or adapt) mechanisms and protocols to govern the delivery of essential health services in accordance with response protocols from MoH;• Establish, revitalize community groups and activities (such as those for children, youth ,women or parents);• Set up common spaces where IDPs can interact and engage in joint activities (such as spaces for children and youth, community centres, and community-based schools);• Support Meconta District Directorate of Health to engage IDPs as ‘peer counselors’ or ‘community psychosocial workers;• Through Meconta District Directorate of Health, train and support IDPs to provide scalable culturally appropriate mental health psychosocial; • Support Meconta District Directorate of Health to provide of clinical MHPSS services, including linkages between clinical services and IDPs committees to ensure timely referral, accompaniment of people to facilitate their access to services, and adequate follow upThe project will be implemented in Meconta District in Nampula Province (04 transition centers in Namialo and resettlement center in Corrane).

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The project will identify and understand the IDPs capacities and coping mechanisms through a community-based approach, which will allow us to be more aware of the broader political context, to recognize and understand changes in roles and power relations between women and men, young and old, and majority and minority groups, and to work with leaders and all members of the community to ensure respect for rights and equality. This will help us to ensure that our interventions do not undermine community structures which can provide protection and support.By working closely with the community, the project will be better informed about the protection risks faced by different groups, depending on their age, gender and background Collaboration with government, national and international non-governmental partners is also essential to provide protection and assist persons of concern in claiming their rights to humanitarian assistance.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

The consultation process has been carried out through meetings with stakeholders and support for public hearings to set up the beneficiary registration committers and selection of the beneficiaries. We are already working with the local authorities and leaders to mitigate risks that arise for women experiencing backlash from community for participation in the project, through appropriate communication and reporting mechanisms, ensuring transparency on the beneficiary targeting.The feedback and complaints mechanism that Plan International uses include suggestion boxes, community engagement meetings, participation in camp meetings, surveys and evaluations, help desk, and the national emergency green line “1458”. In all these methods, we ensure the mechanisms are child friendly. These child friendly mechanisms promote gender and age-appropriate, inclusive, safe and confidential ways for children and young people to receive information, provide feedback and meaningfully participate in influencing humanitarian programming. In Namialo resettlement centers, Plan International already supported the District Directorate of Health to establish a health referral group with participation of IDPs. We have also trained the IDPs Protection Group in Child Protection and GBV referral and complaint mechanisms, including case management.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project will follow the guiding principles of working IDPs with disabilities. These include Rights-based approach, with actions targeting persons with disabilities informed by the human rights principles and standards codified in the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD). These include principles of nondiscrimination, respect for the inherent dignity, autonomy and independence of the individual, gender equality, respect for difference and diversity, and respect for the rights of women and children. The identification of people with special needs will be done through data regularly collected and shared by INGC. The project will validate the existing data through direct engagement with IDPs management committees.To remove barriers to their access to assistance, the project will include people with disabilities, their families and caregivers in all community-based participatory assessments, provision of training and awareness-raising programs on their rights, to empower them to participate in community affairs and decision-making processes.Particular attention will be given to promotion of the inclusion of people with all types of disabilities in camp management structures (e.g., camp committees) and in community leadership positions (e.g., through quotas/reserved seats/awareness raising).

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

We will work closely with the health providers and community structures to assess and mitigate any environmental risks that can also pose a risk on public health, such as the water provision and sanitation, waste management.We will also monitor any natural hazards, such as floods and in coordination with relevant stakeholders prepare a contingency plan to mitigate and manage potential risks and reduce people’s vulnerability.
