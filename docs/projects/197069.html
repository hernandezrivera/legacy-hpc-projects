Project Summary

RI aims to provide prevention and response GBV services as well as empowerment activities through both a standalone as well as integrated approach that promotes access to health services provided by RI in the targeted area. RI will target the most vulnerable women and girls including: people with disability, widowed women, women and girls headed household, adolescent girls and older women among others. RI will continue to support and establish the functioning of two women and girls safe spaces (WGSSs) - one in Kisreh Sub-district (Deir-ez-Zor governorate), and the second in Al-Hasakeh Sub-district (Al-Hasakeh governorate), as well as four mobile protection units: two in Al-Hasakeh (covering Washokani\Tweineh and Al Hol camps), one in Al Kobar Community (Kisreh) and the fourth in Basira - roving between the two communities of Dahla and Jdidet Bikara. In addition, following the integrated approach with its health services, RI will deploy GBV teams in two RI health facilities to provide prevention and response services for women and girls visiting the health facilities in Basira and Kisreh CPHCs. RI is working on strengthening the referral for women and girls at risk between RI health and GBV services in the targeted area either by establishing a WGSS near to RI health facility or through deploying GBV team in the health facility to provide prevention and response services in line with the GBV Minimum Standards in Emergencies, the services provided within RI WGSS are case management following the GBV case management SOPs developed by GBV SC, community based and focus PSS services including psychoeducation sessions, psychological first aid as well as awareness raising and empowerment activities including skills development and life skills. Additionally, RI will provide focus response services such as individual and group counselling through trained and supervised PSS team, case management services by trained and supervised case workers, and Psycho-workshops. Moreover, in order to strengthen the community approach, RI will build on community protective mechanisms already in place. In addition, RI will conduct safety audits to services and facilities, risk mapping, contextualize participatory GBV key messages, integrate awareness raising and psychoeducation sessions for men and boys as well as psychosocial workshops to address behavioral problems such as anger, violence to enhance their engagement in the GBV program. RI will provide integrated approach within its supported health facilities by providing awareness raising and information sessions for women, men, girls and boys on GBV topics in addition to providing focus on strengthening the referral pathway for GBV survivors and women and girls visiting RI health facilities, in addition to provide case management through social assistants based at the C/PHC level. All activities will be implemented while adhering to strict COVID-19 prevention measures such as reduced numbers of participants in group activities, social distancing, and provision of PPEs for both staff and participants at the center’s activities. Additionally, hotline services will be used to provide online information sharing, psychological first aid, individual counselling and case management for women and girls who do not have access to the center. Moreover. RI will also integrate Emergency Cash in case Management, and survivors and women and girls at risk will be available through case management services when relevant in the Case Action Plan to benefit from cash Support to meet and respond to emerging needs. Cash support will be available to all survivors and women and girls at-risk in all the static services or upon referral from social workers and assistants in mobile units or health facilities, as well as from external referrals.

3 - Needs

As per Voices of Syria/HNO 2022 women and girls are specially affected by all forms of GBV potentiated by the 11 years of conflict and its psycho-social impact as well as economic and living conditions deterioration. "IPV and family violence against girls, especially by their brothers and fathers, are among the forms of GBV that continue to affect the daily lives of women and girls in Syria during 2021 included sexual harassment and sexual violence, including in places of detention and within the context of kidnapping”. Women and girls living in NES continue to be exposed to multiple and interconnected forms of GBV throughout their lives, due to several factors such as highly unequal gender norms, often exacerbated by discriminatory attitudes based on age, displacement status, disability and/or marital status, create an environment in which women and girls are devalued and controlled. The protracted Syrian crisis with frequent peaks of emergency and sudden shocks, coupled with the deeply rooted discriminatory social norms and the lack of the rule of law in certain areas, exacerbate Syria's protection needs. This economic situation, coupled with forced confinement and limited freedom of movement leads communities to use negative coping strategies which disproportionately affect women and girls, including child/ forced marriage and various forms of gender-based violence. In NES, also the tribal dynamics, conservative communities, and static gender roles limit women and girls'.

4 - Implementation of activities

RI will continue to promote a gender sensitive and inclusive approach throughout the program cycle including in the MEAL mechanisms setup. RI data is collected and analyzed under an AGD approach (Age, Gender and Diversity) and reporting through 4Ws report will be continued as well as participation of hard to reach groups is fostered in consultation processes by ensuring groups assessed are representative of social dynamics to promote a better understanding on needs, risks, gaps and barriers. Moreover, through the establishment of a risk matrix MEAL can monitor the impact of adopted mitigation measures and how barriers are addressed and access is being enhanced. Among the key measures adopted by RI in the program cycles, one of the key access barriers for persons with disabilities, was transportation and mobility, for that purpose RI has adapted the vehicles supporting the WGSS activities in order to suit the mobility needs of women and girls with disabilities; moreover, RI has adapted the center facilities to ensure dignified access to women and girls with disabilities as well as provided a training on Disability & Inclusion to all the frontline service providers. During the next program cycle RI will continue to enhance the inclusiveness of services and gradually address identified barriers; for example, the project will target among over all beneficiaries at least 510 Persons with disabilities.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Lack of quality services and referral expose survivors to harm, enhancing risk of stigmatization, community and family ostracization and further trauma in addition to possibility of putting survivors at risk if confidentiality is not secured due to service providers’ lack of capacity on information management. Likelihood: High, the impact High. However, RI will implement the following mitigation measures: 1. Case management with survivors to be conducted by trained personnel only; 2. Service mapping including only those that can provide survivor centered services; 3. Mainstream messaging on the risk of the available legal systems which is likely to cause harm to survivors; 4. Self-care strategies as part of programmers and organization HR policy. Second risk: Resistance or backlash from sections of the community and community leaders on GBV messaging, backlash from family or community on women participating in recreational, vocational or empowerment activities. Community and family stigmatization for participating in programming, family resistance to girls’ mobility and low participation of girls because of family and community resistance. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. RI will implement the following mitigation measures: 1.Community engagement from onset of project, from assessment phase and throughout all project cycle. It includes meetings with religious and community leaders; 2. Adaptation of GBV messaging to ensure sensitivity to the context.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The project aims to provide lifesaving GBV services for women and girls. However, such services can increase risks of exploitation by service provider or by other actor in the community, and RI will work on mitigation measures through the following: 1. Consult women and men, boys and girls in the design of the programme; 2. Ensure participants know services where to disclose if violence occurs; 3. Raise awareness on PSEA and other types of exploitation and where to report; 4. Establish good relation with local authorities, clarify humanitarian principles and create internal and external monitoring mechanisms to measure compliance to them; 5. Ensure the compliant and feedback mechanisms are accessible for all women and girls; 6. Mainstream PSEA across key program activities and ensure awareness on SEA is a key component of all the services provided at the WGSS and in MPUs. Moreover, RI has a policy on PSEA and new staff are trained/ sensitized on it, and the code of conduct is signed by staff, vendors, visitors, etc, and they are informed about the policy.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The WGSSs and MPUs are located in relatively safe areas. Risk: Armed clashes, arrest and detention, shelling/artillery, VBIEDs/IEDs. Mitigation: staff to contact the security focal point and alert all staff to be prepared for immediate evacuation/hibernation if possible. Activate an emergency plan, previously designed and explained to all staff members. Being neutral and avoid political action Suspend/Hibernate and/or relocate as per the severity of the risk and based on a contingency plan and in line with “Protection Cluster Guidance: Protection Principles and Minimum Standards for Emergency Relocation Support in Northwest Syria, May 2020”. Staff to Stay away from convoys of military exercises during the movement. Following the steps for ethical closure of the program and ensure continuous updating of the plan to close the program as needed.
