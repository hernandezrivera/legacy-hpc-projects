Project Summary

"This project aims to enhance the equitable provision of humanitarian assistance in refugee camps and host communities, with a central focus on coordination, community participation, capacity building, Complaints and Feedback Mechanism, Accountability to Affected Population (AAP), disaster risk reduction (DRR), Shelter and Non-Food Items (NFI) including Liquefied petroleum-related (LPG) interventions as part of the SAFE Plus 2 initiative consisting of LPG activities and refills, a collaboration with FAO, WFP, and UNHCR, aimed at enhancing climate resilience, food security, and safety for affected community.
SMSD will focus on site management support coordination, information management, advocacy for minimum standards, CFP Mechanism, representation, and refugee population engagement through AAP. Communication with Communities is prioritized to inform residents of their entitlements and will be promoted through capacity-building programs, and the establishment of platforms for meaningful dialogue with camp stakeholders to reinforce community capacity and ownership.
To mitigate multi-hazard risks, the project will implement cash-based site development, Shelter/NFI and DRR interventions, including infrastructure maintenance, environmental restoration, Shelter construction, repair and maintenance, porter support and solar streetlight installation. Strengthening community-based disaster response volunteer capacity and coordination systems at the camp level.
IOM is leading the effort to provide cleaner cooking energy to refugees through LPG activities and refills. This helps ensure food, nutrition security, reduce health risks, gender-based violence exposure, and environmental impacts. IOM shall collaborate with UNHCR in streamlining and harmonizing the LPG supply chain to leverage the combined volume of the LPG requirement against the overall purchasing price. Furthermore, efforts will be maintained in replacing damaged cooking stoves with new ones. Simultaneously, IOM shall empower the Rohingya community by providing training and spare parts to facilitate stove repairs. Continuous training for all beneficiaries on the safe use of cooking fuels and efficient cooking methods will be a priority during LPG distribution and refilling.
IOM plans to implement gender and inclusion activities (17 camps), Second phase of Shelter Upgrade and Maintenance (SUM) (13 camps), construction of new MTS and repairing works. NFI distribution (5 camps), and solar lamp distribution (10 camps), and distribution of emergency shelter kits and NFI to affected refugee households in the aftermath of disasters based on Rapid Damage Verification (RDV), while continuing to be the Shelter Focal Point and directly assist as per the verification in Camp 8E, 8W, 9, 10, 18, 20, 20 Extension, 22, 24, 25 and through its pipeline partners in Camps 13 and 19 (Caritas), 14, 15, and 16 (HEKS) and 11,12 (BDRCS).
For the implementation of SUM, Camp 8E, and 8W will be covered by NRC and DRC, and Camp 15 will be covered by HEKS utilizing the materials provided by IOM through Pipeline. The remaining SUM caseload will be covered by IOM directly. For the NFI voucher programme, Camp 24, 25, 18, 8W, and 10 will be covered by IOM and through Pipeline Partners. Emergency shelter kits and NFI will be prepositioned (i.e., up to 30% for Ukhiya camps and 100% for Teknaf camps) to respond to disasters in IOM AoR.
Needs and Population Monitoring (NPM) will continue to support informed humanitarian and development decision-making through the identification of needs, conditions, and service provision for the refugee and host communities, enabling strong evidence-based response planning by administering thematic assessments, joint multi-sectoral needs assessment, assisting the inter-sectoral coordination group (ISCG) on the post-disaster and preparedness through daily incident reporting, shelter addressing system, facility mapping and joint needs assessment framework.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Since the onset of the Rohingya influx in 2017, emergency responses have been primarily focused on addressing immediate life-saving needs. However, the crisis persists without sustainable solutions. A concerning trend is the decline in funding, with the JRP receiving only 48% coverage in 2023, thereby limiting the scope for enhancing services hence mounting protection risks and heightened concerns regarding safety and security, exacerbated by increasing tensions over resource allocation with host communities. The dynamic nature of services, camp actors, and administrative policies has created a complex humanitarian environment that necessitates the implementation of referral systems and improved information provision, improved access to services and community engagement. High turnover and a lack of adherence to humanitarian principles underscore the need for capacity sharing to ensure equitable access and advocacy. Efforts to strengthen the CFP are imperative for effective and equitable accountability. According to the MSNA 2023, only 56.45% of refugee HHs were aware of a CFM, with 79% refraining from making complaints. Among those who did lodge complaints, 62% reported no action taken. To address this, the focus will be on enhancing the CFM within the broader AAP mechanism. Furthermore, Assessment revealed that 47% of refugees prioritized shelter materials, indicating a 28% reduction in shelter needs since 2021. This underscores the effectiveness of shelter interventions. However, with funding decreases and a worsening camp situation, both shelter materials/NFIs remain critical, constituting 52% of top priority needs in 2024.Considering that the situation in the camp is unchanged, the dire needs of shelter and NFI continue to be top priority needs in 2024. Reference to limited lifespan of shelter materials, coupled with difficult weather conditions, regular repair and replacement is inevitable to ensure decent living conditions, as such shelter assistance through emergency shelter response, SUM, repair, and construction of the MTS is required to continue minimizing the gap among affected populations, contributing to protection, security, privacy, and health of the beneficiaries. The 2023 SCCCM Sector assessment showed that the average number of portable solar lamps per HHs is 0.35 which is far below the minimum standard of 2 lamps per HH exacerbated by a 48% non-functioning SSL, according to the Energy and Environment Network guidelines. The provision of solar lamps coupled with the repair and maintenance activities is required to continue ensuring safety during the night within camps.To minimize the use of firewood and items such as plastic for cooking fuel, the continued provision of LPG refills to all refugee HHs in camps is crucial in preventing deforestation, averting respiratory illness, improve mental health, increase spending on food and maternity care, studies, and reduce violence. The operations of the IOM’s Bamboo Treatment Facility will contribute to providing durable materials to beneficiaries by treating and prolonging the lifespan of the bamboo poles at the same time capacitating host communities’ skills and livelihood. Community engagement and participation lack strong representation. Many participants have reported in the past negative experiences when reporting issues, with minimal involvement in decision making processes, particularly for women, young individuals, and those with disabilities. Refugees continue to be exposed to natural hazards. In March 2023, a major fire incident affected over 3011 households in camp 11 in addition to Cyclones Moha and Hamoon that caused significant damage to camps (NPM). The hilly terrain and poorly consolidated sand and silt deposits in the camps render them vulnerable to erosion, landslides, access impeded and flash floods. These natural hazards are compounded by low socio-economic indicators and limited coping mechanisms and access impediment in the camps.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project aims to ensure operational efficiency and effectiveness in responding to the Rohingya crisis by deploying a single agency for SM, SD and Shelter/NFI in each camp, whilst coordinating shelter and SD partners in IOM AoR. This strategy will be operated directly through IOM or its implementing partners. SM will use service monitoring tools and on-site inspections to improve the response's quality and rationalize partner activities, while Shelter/NFI will conduct post distribution monitoring (PDM) in collaboration with relevant stakeholders such as sector to reduce service duplication and ensure uniform adoption of strategies, guidelines, and tools in AoR.DRR, plantation, shelter, NFIs and energy initiatives will be streamlined through the SD and shelter camp focal points while encouraging partners to concentrate efforts on specific camps to improve coordination. All partners must adhere to Site Planning, shelter/sector guidelines and a multi-year strategy for various activities. For LPG-specific intervention in Camp 25, Nabolok will cover. SD and Shelter/NFI efforts in camps will prioritize urgent needs, ensure coordination among stakeholders, and employ appropriate techniques for environmental considerations including capacity building of Shelter/NFI actors to enhance expansion of the Pipeline system for more efficient intervention with a coherent approach and quality of materials/items provided to the beneficiaries. IOM will prioritize implementing partners aligning with its model with stable programs. IOM will serve as focal point in camps with IOM integrated programming to foster predictability, continuity, and trust while reducing costs. NPM will continue partnering and collaborating with stakeholders by designing and conducting prioritized assessments and providing necessary technical mapping capacity through collecting drone imageries, producing maps and footage, to support the operational and strategic needs of ISCG and actors.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

IOM will continuously monitor, coordinate, and report safety risks and violence through service monitoring, safety audits, and community feedback. It will also integrate with protection actors for emergency response, relocation, complaints, shelter assistance, NFI and information campaigns. In disaster situations while prioritizing vulnerable households in camps, ensuring they receive necessary assistance.Collaboration with protection actors is essential to address safety, security, social cohesion, and other community concerns. IOM will advocate with local authorities and property owners to secure land use agreements, reducing the risk of eviction, conflicts, and high rental costs.All extremely vulnerable individuals (EVIs of different genders) will be provided with porter support to carry materials from the distribution point to their shelters as well as construction support with the received materials. For LPG, porter support will be provided between EVI’s shelter and the distribution point.The project involves installing, maintaining, and repairing SSL (Street Solar Lights, Household) for lighting pathways and open spaces, improving access to services, and reducing protection risks, especially for people with reduced mobility . Capacity building for IOM staff, third-party contractors, supplier’s staff and partners will focus on monitoring, reporting, and responding to protection concerns, emphasizing the principles of Protection, and preventing abuse and exploitation.NPM in close coordination with the Protection and PSEA units will continue to provide support by conducting necessary protection-related surveys and PSEA assessments with the aim to understanding the Rohingya population's knowledge and perception, the information will be used to improve existing complaint and feedback mechanism, and to boost reporting of sensitive issues.

3.3 - Age & Disability

The project will play a critical role in promptly informing communities about specialist services, even though specialized agencies provide referral pathways. The project will work closely with the Age and Disability Working Group and Child-Protection subsector to quickly inform communities about specialized services. IOM's ongoing relationship with Christian Blind Mission will support SMSD's intervention for individuals with disabilities and elders.To employ the Ideas Box spaces as an inventive solution center, the project will collaborate with BSF to promote the larger mobilization of creative youth leaders in the community who will receive training as Human Centered Design approach trainers. Groups will receive support in identifying small-scale initiatives that are connected to specific issues seen in their community and will strengthen their ability to carry out those projects.The Service Monitoring Tool SMS will conduct on-site observations of child-friendly spaces, early childhood care facilities, and learning centers to identify gaps. Recommendations for improvement will be shared with the facility owners. At the time of data collection, IOM ensures that age and sex disaggregated data are collected to inform specific needs. During the implementation of shelter activities, engagement with the youth will be ensured so that they are trained to support the shelter upgrade and maintenance activities in their community. A safety audit will also be conducted for children living in camps, and an accessibility audit will be done for key facilities. Persons with disabilities and the elderly will be involved in every CfW rotation, where adapted tasks will be provided to those with physical challenges and such will include fencing of ponds, slope stabilization, construction of speed bumps, Shelter/NFI and safe construction practices will be implemented to avoid accidents and reduce child safety risks.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

IOM's Communication with Communities Sub-Unit, in coordination with NPM and ISCG's AAP Technical Working Group of which IOM is a SAG member will set standards and tools to enhance AAP and avoid duplications. NPM will manage, maintain, and update the Common Feedback Platform IM reporting outputs on a regular basis, in coordination with steering committee members (IOM and UNHCR). NPM will also implement multiple thematic and needs assessments, interacting directly with the affected population covering as many camps and host community locations as possible, with various assessments to reflect on different needs. At the time of the designing stage of the project, consultation with beneficiaries through focus group discussion and key informants’ interview will be conducted. The orientation and training will be provided to beneficiaries for all programs to explain the program so that eligibility and process are clarified, and beneficiaries are trained to utilize the materials provided properly.IOM has a zero-tolerance policy against sexual exploitation and abuse and ensures that its IPs, third-party contactors, and vendors involved in direct contact with beneficiaries have received mandatory training in PSEA. Designated PSEA focal point and champions in all the camps will have the right skills and capacity to report and handle SEA cases. The complaints and feedback desks will be set at all IOM’s distribution points supported by strong referral pathway to relevant partners such as protection and SMSD. Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) through household-level surveys, FGD and KII will be conducted to capture the beneficiaries’ feedback and effectiveness of the implemented program and reflect the required change in future implementation.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

IOM has a zero-tolerance policy against sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) and ensures that its IPs, third-party contactors, and vendors involved in direct contact with beneficiaries have received mandatory training in PSEA. Designated PSEA focal point and champions in all the camps will have the right skills and capacity to report and handle SEA cases. An annual PSEA and Code of Conduct refresher training will be conducted with all staff, including the Rohingya volunteers. All staff and volunteers working in the camps understand, sign, and adhere to a Code of Conduct Standard. IOM conducts daytime distributions with security presence as needed, prioritizing vulnerable individuals such as households with disabled members, and women-headed, and child-headed households. The Shelter team comprises both male and female staff, all trained in managing Gender-Based Violence (GBV) disclosures and integrating Accountability to Affected Population and protection measures into distributions, focusing on GBV risk mitigation and the principles of Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Distribution points are organized with separate lines for vulnerable individuals, females, and males. Mothers have access to breastfeeding corners, and porter support is provided to extremely vulnerable individuals who face difficulties carrying LPG cylinders. To enhance Accountability to Affected Populations and align with the AAP strategy, the Shelter/LPG team establishes complaint and feedback desks at each distribution point. This ensures all reported cases are captured and addressed, fostering continuous engagement and feedback from the supported communities.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Through the implementation of multi-hazard mitigation measures such as slope stabilization works to prevent landslides and slope erosion, access improvement works to enhance access to basic facilities, drainage network improvement works that encompass cleaning and de-silting to prevent flooding and inundation, plantation works to restore the environment, and watershed management initiatives to improve soil and water quality with a particular focus on nature-based solutions to support the local ecosystem and sustainability while promoting the resilience of the affected population, IOM is committed to aligning the response strategy with disaster risk reduction frameworks and climate change adaptation efforts coherently and effectively. IOM is actively exploring environmentally friendly construction materials, including the potential use of Lime, for small-scale DRR works and shelter to enhance cost-effectiveness and environmental sustainability. The installation and maintenance of numerous SSLs, and provision of solar-powered SMSD facilities serve as a practical and effective solution for reducing carbon emissions and promoting sustainability in the camps. IOM ensures that all borak bamboo poles used in all shelter interventions are treated to prevent the poles being infected by insects and rotting due to the moisture, this also prolongs the lifespan of the poles, decreasing the frequency of the replacement that subsequently contributes to less usage of the resources. Strict quality control measures are applied at the time of procurement of borak bamboo poles and muli bamboo poles to avoid suppliers harvesting premature bamboo poles that are not fit for usage in the shelter program. The provision of LPG prevents deforestation by switching the energy source from firewood. IOM also ensures that plastic packing materials are not used and substitutes with organic materials such as jute bags, bamboo baskets and geotextile.
