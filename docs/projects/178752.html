Project Summary

Through this project Secours Islamique France (SIF) aims to provide WASH assistance to support the immediate needs of the most vulnerable population in Homs, Hama, Rural Damascus, Aleppo, Al- Hassakeh, Dier- Ez-zor and Dara’a governorates in Syria through (1) Repair and Rehabilitation of Water Systems, (2) Repair and Rehabilitation of Sewage Networks (3) Manhole Cover (4) Installation of waste bins in community areas and (5) Distribution of water tanks to households. For the sustainability of the rehabilitated water systems, SIF will actively seek solutions for operation and maintenance through the support to local committees and water authorities directly after the completion of the rehabilitations. Water tanks will supplement a comprehensive shelter programme, providing safe and dignified accommodation standards for the displaced population and returnees. Water interventions will be complemented by solid waste management services through the installation of waste bins in common public areas. Additionally, SIF will support sanitation through the repair of community-level sewage networks and covering of manholes.
The project interventions will improve access to basic WASH facilities through the identification of critical gaps and providing WASH assistance in particular in areas with a high number of IDPs and returnees and areas with anticipated significant inward displacement. Through the proposed intervention, SIF will directly contribute to the Humanitarian Response Plane 2021 strategical objective 3 (specific objective 3.3) along with focusing on the WASH sector’s objectives 1 and 2 in some of the highest severity ranking sub-districts in the targeted governorates. These targeted localities are selected based on their level of accessibility and the severity of needs as per 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), SIF field assessments and consultations with local authorities and municipalities of each targeted sub-district. All targeted localities are of the severity scale 3 and higher except for Ar rastan which has the severity scale of 2. However after a comprehensive need assessment in December 2021 by SIF Team, SIF considered to target the locality of Ar rastan as it was reported to have a large population with high needs for WASH assistance.
Overall, the project intends to reach 301,285 individuals targeting the most vulnerable women, men, girls and boys and prioritizing families with high vulnerabilities according to SIF and the SARC vulnerability criteria. The proposed intervention will be implemented in close coordination with SARC, local authorities and the WASH sector. Participatory approach will be adopted in all phases of the project cycle including design, planning, implementation and the monitoring phase. The MEAL team will set up a complaints and feedback mechanism.

4 - Needs

The 2021 Syria HNO WASH Sector reports around 36% of the population is not relying on piped water and using alternative means which are often unsafe to meet their water needs. 70% of the sewage discharged is untreated and 50% of the sewage systems are in need of repair. About 29% of garbage is inappropriately disposed leading to heightened risk of health problems and depletion of the standards of living for many communities across Syria. SIF’s assessment found that in Arbin, Nashabhiye, Maliha and Duma (Rural Damascus), Ar-Rastan (Homs), Kafr Zaita, Muhrada and Madiq Castle (Hama), As Safira and Aleppo (Aleppo)’s WASH infrastructures are severely damaged leading to raw sewage to mix and pour either into the water sources or flood in the streets, thus contaminating soil and the overall water resource management.These deteriorated conditions pose significant health risks by fostering the development of harmful waterborne diseases and forcing people to adopt negative coping mechanisms such as spending significantly more of their income on purchasing water. The assessment further highlighted the problem of littering of solid waste in various areas of Dara’a city, Arbin and Duma as a result of lack of garbage bins in common public areas. These problems are also leading to environmental pollution caused by the degradation of water systems and littering of solid waste. Open manholes in Kafr Zaita, Madiq Castle and Muhradha where harmful solid waste mixes with the sewage system are leading to further water pollution, hygiene and health problems and incidence of accidents especially in the rainy seasons. The bad smell on the streets is another problem caused by damaged sewage networks and lack of waste dins in public spaces.

5 - Implementation of activities

SIF will coordinate with the relevant local authorities such as the general water foundation, municipalities, SARC and the WASH sector to identify the localities of intervention and avoid any overlapping or duplication with other NGOs. SIF will also select beneficiaries, obtain the necessary approvals and issue official acceptance certificates for handover. SIF will rely on a contractor to rehabilitate sewage networks and WASH facilities. Contractor selection will be based on SIF Standards of Procedures (SoPs) in order to ensure transparency and accountability in the selection process. Composed of skilled and experienced engineers, SIF WASH technical team will supervise contractor teams in order to ensure the quality delivery of rehabilitated WASH systems. Contractors will also be asked to provide a period of guarantee in order to ensure the sustainability of the works. The activities will be monitored on a daily basis by SIF WASH technical team through field visits, data collection and reporting to SIF HQ and donors to report progress, highlighting areas of concerns and ensuring optimal completion of project activities. Through monthly project and budget follow-up, SIF will ensure that the standards of project implementation are followed and funded resources are being effectively used. Participatory approach, through regular structured dialogues with groups of different ages, gender and background, particularly those with specific needs on WASH issues (e.g. persons with disabilities and elderly), will ensure that any protection concerns highlighted are discussed and resolved. The MEAL team will put in place mechanism for complaints and feedback, ensuring that women, men, boys and girls are all comfortable with it.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk1: Discrimination or perception of discrimination may occur if WASH services are provided to a specific group/ individuals while others are excluded.Likelihood: Medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: (1) To ensure transparent beneficiary selections procedures based on standards vulnerability by SARC criteria to avoid discrimination or the perception of discrimination while selecting beneficiaries (2) Ensure that comprehensive service mapping has taken place that risk mitigation and communication with communities are proactive and informed. (3) ensure close coordination and consultation with local communities on the proposed location of WASH services in order to receive their inputs and understand their perspectives. Risk 2: Risk of gender based violence (GBV) while accessing the WASH infrastructures. Likelihood: MediumImpact: HighMitigation: (1) conduct safety audit to ensure safe access of beneficiary to WASH infrastructures sites. (2) ensure close coordination and consultation with local communities on the proposed location of WASH infrastructures in order to receive their inputs and understand their perspectives in respect to safety of beneficiaries. (3) Consolation with women, girls, boys and men should be held. (4) sensitizing others sectors in gender sensitive measures in WASH. Risk 3: The existence of unexploded ordnances Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: (1) conduct a thorough assessment and infrastructural technical assessment of the sites that are aimed to be rehabilitated (2) coordinate the interventions with local authorities and other actors that might be aware of security issues in the neighborhood, (3) ensure that unexploded ordnances’ clearance is conducted

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Not getting approvals from national security to enter the area. Likelihood: Low. Impact: high. Mitigation: SIF will target easy-to-reach areas and establish a constant dialogue and follow-up with the authorities. Risk 2: Risk of increased prices due to heavy inflation. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigation: (1) SIF has already accounted for the increased inflation rate in the budget, (2) in case of continuous inflation SIF will adjust the BoQs accordingly. Risk 3: The security situation deteriorates and effects the implementation of the activities. Likelihood: low. Impact: HighMitigation: (1) regular monitoring of the security situation by SIF Logistics and Security Manager (2) ensure updated contingency plan is in place at field and coordination level (3) ensure the preparedness of SIF team through regular briefings and regular updates on any change in the situation. Risk 3: Risk of Fraud and misuse of resourcesLikelihood: MediumImpact: HighMitigation: (1) Ensure that complain and feedback mechanism are at place to report any fraud. (2) Close monitoring by MEAL department (3) Random spot checks (4) Ensure procurement procedures are transparent and in line with SIF and Donor requirement (5), Insure that staff is hired in a fairly adhering to SIF procedures. (6) Ensure SIF’s Anti-Fraud policy is strictly followed to detect, prevent and address cases of fraud.
