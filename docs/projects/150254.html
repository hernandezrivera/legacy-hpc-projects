Needs

The impact of climate change in the last ten years in Opt affected the productivity of the small ruminants like sheep, in addition to increase the spread of contagious diseases. UAWC’ field search during the first half of 2017 shows that many of seasonal diseases which affected the flocks in southern and eastern parts of Hebron governorate ( Khirbet ar Rahwa, Khirbet Zanuta, Deir Saeeda,Umm Sidra,Tatrit,Anab Al Kaber, Kharoubeh,Arab Al Frejat), Bethlehem (Al Ubeidiya, Zatara, Ash Shawawra,Tuqu’a, Al Khushna) and east Jerusalem (E1, Alsawahreh ilsahrqiyeh) , now affects the same flocks all year round. In West Bank there are 650 thousand sheep and goat, and the majority of these flocks in both governorates of Bethlehem and Hebron which form 25% of the total numbers. In addition to eastern slopes of West Bank the density of the flocks increases. According to HNO 2017, Food insecurity across oPt is higher for female headed households at 32 percent. These factors are further constrained by poor access to markets and collective selling systems designed to increase income levels and hence reduce food insecurity. . In the first half of 2016, ACF updated an assessment for 150 communities in Area C, which included a risk analysis, coping mechanisms and mitigation measures. The proposed target communities were identified as top priority due to their vulnerability in terms of economic conditions, unemployment rates, income resources, demolition/ eviction orders. Women and girls are primarily responsible for care of livestock, feeding, milking and milk processing. Whereas, men are primarily responsible for marketing and flock management decisions. The project will encourage women to have a role in access to markets. The method of raising sheep in West Bank is the rangeland dependent way, which is not regulated and form 96 % of the methods of raising in general. According to UAWCS field research and after collaborating this data with MoA the sheep and small ruminants sector faces many problems, which lead to the high losses in this sector, which directly affected the numbers of sheep and their capacity of production, the most important of which are the following: • The high cost of concentrated feeds with high protein content suitable for feeding sheep, and this led sheep breeders rely on the varieties of barley and bran • The high incidence of diseases causes sheep abortion. Malta fever (Brucellosis), chlamydia, toxoplasma and Q fever are the most important endemic diseases in the sheep of the West Bank. Currently, there is a high percentage of sheep suffering from these diseases, which the morbidity rates from 10 to 15% annually. • Available breeds and poor production capacity due to internal mating and non-change of rams. • The elderly rams (more than 4 years of age ewes) of production, which weakens their ability to regular annual production and because of selling 1 -2 years of age ewes for meat. • The high mortality rate in new born, and this occurs by more than 22% in most areas of the West Bank, and there are many reasons leading to the death of new born, including: - Inadequate barns to raise and receive newborns - Lack of heating facilities in nurseries allocated for birth - Lack of sufficient experience of sheep breeders with the most important pathogens that affect suckling’s lambs - Climate change and mortality of lambs because of starvation some times • Rangelands are limited due to settlement policies, the segregation wall, lack of rain and overgrazing • Shortage of water sources for sheep • Risks of investment in livestock and lack of use of technology in livestock production • Markets, price irregularities and agricultural control

Activities

The humanitarian intervention to reduce food insecurity will be designed and harmonized with the MINISTRY OF AGRICULTURE, Food Security Sector and relevant stakeholders, based on the humanitarian needs of the population. The interventions will include activities to address identified constraints in order to achieve an improved food security status for the livestock farmers, thus ensuring their agricultural livelihoods and reduce humanitarian needs in the area. UAWC has is responsible for the baseline and end line data collections, identification of target herding communities, and to propose the technical components for agricultural inputs. And prior to any project implementation, the organization will strictly coordinate the final definition and selection of localities and beneficiaries with line ministries offices, and with any other organization working in the same area and sector of activities. A local project committee will be established in each targeted locality to help facilitate the implementation, Activity 1: Rehabilitation of grazing land: The project will rehabilitate one of the largest grazing land of 2,000 dunums east of Bethlehem. This pastoral reserve is home to more than 220 sheep farmers from the town of Tuqu’a, east of Bethlehem, and owns at least 3,500 sheep. It will need to cultivate pastoral crops, and will cultivate 100 thousand pastoral seedlings in this reserve and this reserve will be closed for at least two years, the aim is to let the seedlings to growing and enabling the production of feed for sheep during the summer. Activity 2: Distribution of fodder seeds: at least 225 tons of drought-resistant fodder will be distributed in different pastoral areas, whose population depends on the traditional feeding of sheep. The Bedouin areas include east and south of Hebron and east of Bethlehem, as well as areas of East Jerusalem. Activity 3: Rehabilitation of 15 collective water harvesting cisterns for sheep breeders in the Bedouin areas: At least 300 sheep breeders have at least 9,000 heads of sheep in the nomadic communities east of Hebron Governorate, relying on collective grazing in desert areas. in these areas there are at least 15 water harvesting cisterns which dependent on Rainwater, the average capacity of each cistern is at least 250 cubic meter, and this will be saving water for animals. Which will help farmers continue their presence in area C.Activity 4: Rehabilitation of sheep pens and nurseries: at least 100 sheep pens will be rehabilitated whose owners suffer from poor productivity in sheep because of the inability of these barns to provide the basic requirements of the weather conditions associated with raising sheep, and will choose the poorest families to benefit from this activity.

Indicators

Monitoring and evaluation: • A baseline survey will be conducted at initiation of the project, which will be updated and compared to a final evaluation. Information collected through semi structured interviews, household visits and field observations. On-going monitoring will allow UAWC to evaluate methodologies and impact of the activities, to identify challenges and best practices; information will be gathered and analyzed on monthly basis. Particular attention will be paid to beneficiary selection, methods of working with local communities, village councils and protections committees. UAWC will ensure proper mechanism of asset distribution, gender sensitivities, as well as the appropriateness of the intervention in each target area. Project activities will be monitored by a Project Management Team composed of key stakeholders responsible for monitoring and evaluation; the results communicated to humanitarian actors and project beneficiaries. Accountability and transparency will be ensured through ongoing active beneficiary feedback mechanism in UAWC. Output 1: 2000 dunums of pastoral reserve rehabilitated and 220 sheep farmers have a pastoral fodder for their animals. Output 2: 225 tons of fodders seeds distributed and planted in 18000 dunums to 1500 sheep farmers. Output 3: 9 thousands head of sheep had access to 3750 cubic meter of clean water harvested from rehabilitated 15 cisterns. Output 4: the health status and managements of sheep farms improved for 100 sheep farmers through rehabilitation of their shelter. Indicators: The expected objectively verifiable indicators and target of the project are: • 220 male and female sheep breeders will have continuous pastoral feed (at least 30% are female)• 300 male and female sheep breeders will have 3750 cubic meter of water during the summer season (at least 20% are female)• 1500 male and female sheep breeders will benefit from the drought tolerant seeds as fodder for their sheep(at least 20% are female)• 100 male and female sheep breeders will benefit from rehabilitation of their sheep shelter (at least 30% are female)
