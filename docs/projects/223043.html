Project Summary

FAO's objective is to improve the food security and resilience capacity of the Rohingya refugees and host community living in Cox's Bazar. In the Rohingya camps, the refugees hardly have any access to diversified nutritious food, and are almost entirely dependent on food aid. To enhance their access to diversified nutritious food the project will promote homestead gardening practices in the Rohingya camps. Taking the stressed environment and limited space available into consderation in the camps, climate-smart techniques such as sack gardening, vertical gardening, and trellis growing will be introduced and enhanced. In addition, nutrition awareness session will be conducted to sensitize the Rohingya community on importance of ensuring nutrition in regular diet. Homestead production will allow them to produce their own food and sell the surplus amount which will enhance their self-reliance.
Due to the vulnerable geographic location of Cox's Bazar, it is prone to natural shocks. A significant damage in agricultural production occurs every year due to unusual weather events. To cope with such natural shocks, the project will promote climate-smart agriculture practices in agricultural production. In addition, the host community households will be trained on the usage of improved technology in agricultural production, integrated pest management, post-harvest loss management, and high-value crop production techniques to maximize their production capacity and agricultural income. Apart from training, the farmers will be provided with high-yielding hybrid seeds and organic fertilizer to boost their production capacity.
FAO will build the capacity of /aggregation centers by providing business planning, financial literacy, and organization development training to the Aggregation Center Management Committees (ACMC) so that they can effectively enhance market linkages and ensure fair prices for the community farmers. Market linkage workshops and agriculture fairs will be organized to make a functional linkage between ACMCs and market actors. Special emphasis will be given on making functional linkages with the camp markets to maximize local supply to the camp markets. This will contribute to bringing a cohesive environment between Rohingya and the host communities. Local Service Providers (LSPs) and Digital Service Providers (DSPs) will be established and trained on farm machinery repair and maintenance, organic farming techniques, and adaptation of ICT-enabled technology in agricultural production, which will further support the community farmers productivity, and ensure a sustainable impact of the project interventions.
For the overall improvement of the ecosystems in Cox Bazar, FAO will support reforestation to restore degraded landscapes, mitigate landslide risks and promote nature-based solutions as roadside, and streambank plantations to prevent erosions both in Rohingya refugee and host communities. The reforestation activities will be carried out by providing technical support, training and planting materials and through cash for work intervention in the camps and among host community. FAO will work to mitigate human-wildlife conflict by forming a wildlife rescue team at the host community level and by promoting wildlife non-preferred crops in conflict-prone areas. A holistic watershed management plan will be developed for the host and camp communities to mitigate disaster risks such as flooding, landslides, and water quality, quantity. It will further contribute to minimize community conflict on water usage from natural streams and build climate resilient communities in the long run. As part of disaster risk reduction, FAO will strengthen the prototype of the Landslide Early Warning System (LEWS) already featured in Bangladesh Meteorological Department conducting seasonal landslide inventory, developing SoP of LEWS operation, providing training to the relevant Government staff to manage the system.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

As the availability of humanitarian funds are reducing due to multiple crises around the world, it is imperative to enhance the self-reliance of Rohingya refugees and host communities by increasing and diversifying their capacity to produce their sources of nutritious food. According to FAO's RIMA round-3 study conducted in 2023, food insecurity among Rohingya households increased from 50% to 58% along with 93% of households adopting consumption-based coping such as relying on less preferred food, limiting meal size, and reducing the number of meals. More than 80% of households are unable to access to the nutritious food (WFP REVA 7, 2023). The Rohingya refugees need complementary sources of food in addition to the general food assistance they receive in the camps. While there are space restrictions and congestion in the camps, there is an opportunity to increase access to nutritious food by using innovative techniques, such as sack and vertical gardening and trellising, to grow their own food. To maximize the benefits of what they get from the food rations and growing their own food, it is also important to improve their knowledge on and preparation of nutritious foods. Women-headed households, households with pregnant, lactating mothers and under-five children, and/or with disabilities will be prioritized for the interventions. Due to the presence of more than 1 million Rohingya refugees in Cox Bazar, host communities are facing multi-faceted challenges such as supply chain disruptions, price hikes, loss of agricultural lands, and competition in the labor market. In addition, seasonal climatic shocks such as cyclones, storms, flood and landslides have a significant adverse impact on agricultural production. In 2023, 57% of households faced adverse impact on agricultural production due to climatic shock which is up from 22% in 2022 (FAO RIMA 3, 2023). In relation to that, 40% of households are found suffering from food insecurity phased at phase 3+ (IPC 2024). Around 56% of households were found have adopted livelihood-based coping strategies such as borrow money to meet essential needs, engaging children in labor and reducing cost on non-food items (FAO RIMA 3, 2023). Economic vulnerability is pronounced, with 65% of households in Cox’s Bazar spending below the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB), a significant rise from 38% in 2023 (WFP REVA 7, 2023). Smallholder farmers around the Rohingya camps need support to increase their productivity and incomes. There is an opportunity to build social cohesion between the Bangladeshis and Rohingyas through markets in the refugee camps. By supporting Bangladeshi farmers to produce quality crops and building their capacity in business and financial management, they can increase their participation in and supply to e-voucher outlets and Rohingya refugee camp markets, therefore increasing their incomes. In addition, the farmers have limited access to the input and output markets which restrict their access to quality inputs as well as don't allow them to get the fair price. Aggregation center initiative will play a crucial role to mitigate these problems. To bring an inclusive impact of the project intervention among the community, 30% of the project beneficiaries will be female farmers. In addition, to promote women leadership, at least 15% of the aggregation center management committee members will be female farmers. A massive deforestation happened to accommodate the large number of Rohingya community which adversely affected the ecosystem health, biodiversity, and wildlife. To restore degraded landscapes and enhance health, biodiversity, and wildlife, FAO will support reforestation activities through cash for work activities, related training. The cash for work beneficiaries will be selected maintaining a proper gender balance.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

FAO integrates “Do No Harm” approach in designing, developing, implementing and monitoring programme interventions in Cox’s Bazar to ensure that all activities target and benefit the most vulnerable individuals, enhancing safety and dignity. In addition, FAO follows Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) guidelines, maintain hotline and internal referral pathway to report sexual exploitation and abuse and other forms of sexual violence. A dedicated hotline is also maintained to receive programmatic complaints to promote accountability to the affected population, taking differences of Rohingya refugees and host communities into consideration. Ensuring gender, age, and disabilities will also be taken into account acknowledging the fact that climate change and natural disasters will affect different societal groups differently. Throughout the project period the feedback of the different groups will be collected and addressed in the program design if required.

2.3 - Age & Disability

FAO follows GEDSI (Gender Equality, Disability, and Social Inclusion) approach in program interventions to ensure that all individuals have equal access to resources, services, and decision-making processes, that their diverse perspectives and needs are considered. The elderly and persons with disability (at least 3%) will be prioritized considering their vulnerable status in the beneficiary selection process. Washington group questions will be used to identify the person with disability at the beneficiary selection stage. The aggregation center structure will be designed/updated to make it accessible for all age groups including for the person with disability. In addition, the feedback from all age group including person with disability will be recorded and will be incorporated in the intervention design if required. Thus, FAO strives to create inclusive environments and promote equal opportunities for all individuals.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

FAO strictly enforces a policy of Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) which ensures that all information relevant to project stakeholders is shared in advance of commencing project implementation organizing stakeholder consultation workshops. At the initial stage of the project, beneficiaries will be selected in an accountable and transparent way through a beneficiary selection survey. All age groups especially women, older persons, PWD, and youth will be prioritized as beneficiaries. Throughout the project implementation period, feedback from the project beneficiaries will be recorded through Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) survey, random spot checks, and organizing Feedback sessions with the project beneficiaries. To ensure need based and inclusive response the feedback will be incorporated into the project design at any stage of the implementation if required. A dedicated hotline number will be set up so that the beneficiary can directly access with any complaints and feedback to FAO. The targeted beneficiaries will be sensitized with the hotline number and AAP messages when delivering services at the field level. If the beneficiary comes up with any complaint through the hotline number, then the responsible staff will immediately go for the field investigation to verify the complaint and report to the senior management. The investigation and reporting will be done by maintaining a high level of confidentiality. Immediate actions will be taken within shortest possible time to address the grievance from the community level.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

FAO has a well-established PSEA mechanism that follows zero tolerance for any form of sexual exploitation and abuse. In FAO Cox’s Bazar sub-office, a hotline number is maintained to receive any PSEA complaint which is 24/7 standby and operated by a PSEA focal point. For wider dissemination of the hotline number and PSEA policy followed by FAO, flyer/poster is designed and attached to the fixed points. Besides, the number is shared by organizing feedback sessions with the community, and at distribution and training events. Furthermore, before signing partnership with any NGO it is carefully checked with support from the PSEA network to determine whether the selected NGO gone through PSEA capacity assessment or not and all the partner staff are properly oriented on PSEA policy immediately after onboarding of the partner. Thus, FAO is mainstreaming PSEA in it’s intervention.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, the project addresses climate action by integrating Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), Disaster Risk Management (DRM), Anticipatory Actions, and environmental issues through climate change adaptation and mitigation. As part of DRR, FAO will strengthen the prototype of the Landslide Early Warning System (LEWS) already featured in Bangladesh Meteorological Department conducting seasonal landslide inventory, developing SoP of LEWS operation, providing training to the relevant Government staff to manage the system. FAO is planning to provide Training of Trainers (ToT) to 100 landslide volunteers in 2025 on Landslide Disaster Response including search and rescue items usage at emergency period. FAO has tasks to conduct landslide inventory 2025, update LEWS at BMD web portal, update landslide susceptibility mapping and develop community risk mapping at both host community and the Rohingya Refugee camps. In addition, FAO is planning to conduct plantation activities for slope stabilization through nature-based solution (NBS) to reduce landslide risks. FAO also has tactics for watershed management (user groups, conflict mitigation, improving water quality and quantity, maintaining ecosystem) action plans for DRR context and to ensure climate change adaptation.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project includes Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) measures designed to enhance resilience and continuity in disaster situations. The project has developed a business continuity plan to ensure essential operations remain functional during and after emergencies. This includes coordination with and support to local authorities and key stakeholders to help maintaining critical services and resource flows for both host communities and Rohingya camps in Cox’s Bazar. The Emergency Response Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) development works can start from 2025 to organize responses to landslide events. The project does not have any contingency funds to cover immediate needs and mobilize resources during landslide-related crises.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project will include youth groups age ranges from 15-24 as landslide volunteer. They will be trained to mitigate landslide risk and damage. Youth group will also be targeted for cash for work activities. In addition, potential youth farmers will be selected as Digital Service Provider (DSP) who will be responsible to provide digital extension services to the community farmers.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes. The project will promote self-reliance and resilience through the promotion of homestead gardening in the Rohingya camps. Given the limited access to diversified food group for the Rohingya community, homestead intervention will allow them to produce diversified and nutritious food by themselves. It will increase their resilience capacity to cope with food insecure conditions. In addition, homestead production will allow them to sell the surplus after consumption that will bring economic benefit, and ensure self-reliance.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

Agriculture and disaster management are the two key development priority area in Cox's Bazar. The project will contribute to both of these two areas. Under this project, the farmers of the host community will be trained on climate-smart agricultural techniques, integrated pest management, post-harvest loss management, and high-value crop production techniques which will help them to maximize their production capacity. In addition, the project will support farmers to ensure better access to both input and output markets. Wider marketing opportunities will allow them to sell farmers' produce at a fair price as well as will help them to get quality inputs. A resilient watershed management plan will be developed to mitigate the risk of flooding and landslide. In the long term, it will build the community's climate resilience and minimize conflict. The project will also strengthen the Landslide Early Warning System to reduce damage due to the landslides.
