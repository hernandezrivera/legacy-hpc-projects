Project Summary

AHF will maintain the provision of nutrition services through the stabilization center in Sarmada children’s hospital which is considered as the referral pediatric hospital at Idleb governorate, 2 OTP centers in Sarmada and Salama, 8 RRTs covering the camps in communities around Sarmada (280,000) (in Dana subdistrict) and 4 covering Salama IDPs camps (40,000), one RRT covers Kafr Bonni area (10,000), and one RRT covers Atareb area (70,000), (IDPs 296,000, Host communities 100,000 and 4,000 returnees) including 16,000 PwDs, the number of targeted children under 5 years is 86,550, 15,848 PLWs and 2,360 of other caregivers including men.
The staff of the centers and the RRTs is qualified and well-trained in many topics in the nutrition cluster.
The nutrition services include:
- Outpatients therapeutic Program Centre (OTP), 2 centers in Sarmada and Salama run by 1 pediatrician, 1 Nutritionist, 1 nurse, and 1 data Entry cover OPD services on daily, providing assessment and management of outpatient pediatrics with malnutrition including SAM, MAM patient follow up, routine assessment and treatment for MAM and SAM without complication, receive referrals from RRTs to OTP Centre.
- Stabilization Center in Sarmada children’s hospital: which is run by 1 pediatrician, 1 Nutritionist, 3 nurses (1 nurse every 8 hours, 3 shifts) and 1 data Entry, 3 cleaner cover inpatient services on daily 24/7, providing daily assessment and management of inpatient pediatrics with SAM with complication.
- RRTs: 14 RRTs (8 in Dana subdistrict, 1 in Kafr Nabi, 4 in A'zaz subdistrict, and 1 in Atareb) each team includes: 1 Nutritionist, 2CHW, 1 data Entry, and 1 nurse, cover outreach services:
Screening, referral to OTP center and follow up children 6-59, pregnant and lactating women who are malnourished, Provide emergency IYCF counseling for mothers with moderately malnourished children
Distribute micronutrients for preventing anemia and malnutrition among children and pregnant, lactating women, IYCF-E recommended feeding practices, monitor distribution and use of breast milk substitutes, Support health/nutrition education and promotion, and Nutrition Supplements distribution, These RRTs will be linked to AHF's health facilities in the target communities to receive the referred cases.
During the project implementation, M&E team will regularly collect the required data that help in making enlightened decisions. Monitoring and evaluation of the quality of the service provision through analyzing the collected data based on the Log-Frame, the project will be monitored and evaluated through three levels:
1) The operational, for which the project manager is responsible.
2) The technical, for which the technical team is responsible.
3) The objectives, for which the MEAL team is responsible.
Monitoring tools:
• Performance Indicator Tracking Tables (PITTs), issued on a monthly basis.
• Satisfaction surveys among beneficiaries and stakeholders will be analyzed and reported to stakeholders.
• Project Evaluation Report, by the end of the project term.

4 - Needs

The nutrition situation in NWS is deteriorating, CU5 & PLWs are the most susceptible to malnutrition since their nutritional requirements are relatively high, based on Syria-HNO 2022, the prevalence of malnutrition especially chronic type is a significant public health challenge in Syria. The main groups affected by malnutrition are infants, CU5 and PLWs. According to the last SMART Survey conducted between (02nd June – 13th June 2021), The overall Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate by Weight for Height (WHZ) was 2.5% (1.3 - 4.5 95% C.I.) and Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) was 0.4% (0.1 - 1.7 95% C.I.). The GAM rate by Middle Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) was 4.7% (2.7 – 8.1 95% C.I.). The prevalence of wasting among pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) was 12.4% (7.7-19.3 95% C.I.).In addition, according to the SMART survey conducted by PAC in June 2021, the prevalence of chronic malnutrition among children aged 6-59 months increased from 19 percent to 24.5 percent over the same period, the prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition GAM increased from 0.9 percent to 4.7 percent between May 2019 and June 2021 in Northwest Syria in Aleppo and Idlib governorates. Among PLW, the prevalence of acute malnutrition across northwest Syria is 12.4 percent.According to HNO 2022, the project areas had the following number of PiN: Dana subdistrict has 0.37 million, A'zaz has 0.1 M, Mareet Tamisrin has 0.26 M, Atareb 0.06 M, with 5 level at the severity scale for all project locations.

5 - Implementation of activities

Through this project, AHF will work on the recruitment, provide the salaries to the staff and support the operational costs, provide capacity building program to staff members covering many topics.Through 2 SCs, 2 OTPs, and 14 RRTs AHF will provide the following:- Screening, referral and follow-up children 6-59, pregnant and lactating women who are malnourished. - Provide emergency IYCF counseling for mothers with moderately malnourished children.- Disseminating IYCF-E messages among mothers of children under five (individual counseling and group sessions)- Registering all screened cases and reporting to the nutritionists and data entry team members. - Distribute micronutrients for preventing anemia and malnutrition among children and pregnant, lactating women Disseminate messages (IEC/BCC) on IYCF-E recommended feeding practices.- Nutrition Supplements distribution.- Disseminate messages (IEC/BCC) on IYCF-E recommended feeding practices.- Referral CU5 with dangers signs and Medical problems, referral PLWs with Medical problems. - Referral of medical cases to the nearby health facilities.- Treatment MAM in CU5 by OTP and the RRTs.- Treatment SAM uncomplicated in CU5 by the OTPs.- Treatment SAM with complications in CU5 by the stabilization centers.- Identifying the abused cases among boys and girls 6-59 months, pregnant and lactating women to be referred to child protection partners to take an action.-Monthly 4Ws reporting to the cluster (M&E mentioned in Summary)

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

AHF is an active member of the PSEA network, AHF has updated PSEA and child safeguarding policies and procedures in early 2021, including all levels from prevention, reporting, investigation, and survivor support. AHF has male & female PSEA focal points in Syria and in Turkey offices, they support the implementation of AHF PSEA strategy, and they carry out the following tasks:- Ensure that employees are aware of their obligations related to protection from sexual exploitation and abuse. - Equip employees with the knowledge and skills to prevent Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and Response to Both.- Create mechanisms for reporting protection from sexual exploitation and abuse at the community level, including establishing contact points, according to the location and settings for reporting and posting.- Engage with communities and key stakeholders in strengthening community complaints mechanisms, including determining the provision of victim assistance services in all targeted locations.- Conduct regular awareness-raising activities on protection from sexual exploitation and abuse, policies, standards, and codes of conduct related to protection from sexual exploitation.- Provide specific awareness sessions to target groups such as women, girls, boys, and men in reception centers, camps, sites for displaced people, host communities, and society.- Ensure all project personnel sign the Code of Conduct for PSEA.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

AHF will coordinate closely with the Nutrition cluster, as well as local authorities and humanitarian actors inside Syria throughout the proposed intervention, informing the cluster if and when adjustment is needed. AHF has access and qualified program team, the Equipment is available, the place is available, the field staff is available, the required supplies and medication available, the electricity is available by functioning electrical generators.Risk 1: Conflict-related threats including aerial bombardment, advancement of hostile actors, and shifting alliances, Change of control in the services area, access constraints will continue to be the most significant threat to humanitarian operations. (L)Response: A comprehensive security and safety plan will be prepared so that the staff and the patients will be secured and we will coordinate to relocate the services depending on the new needs.Risk 2: Lack of Nutrition supplements (H)Response: prepare a comprehensive plan for all requirements and keep an emergency store of available supplements to avoid suspension for the RRT activities.Risk 3: Finance: cash transfer to Syria and within Syria with the absence of a banking system. (L)Response: Using Banks for all money transfers to Syria either for operational costs, or vendors and contractors.Risk 4: the UN Resolution non-extension (M)Response: we'll seeking for new funds during the implementation period, and for items from Turkey, contingency stock will be available
