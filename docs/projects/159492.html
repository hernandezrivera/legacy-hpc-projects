Project Summary

FAO will contribute to the Nutrition Cluster’s goal of reducing the prevalence of malnutrition and preventing the occurrence of new cases in Somalia. The project specifically aims to improve nutrition through agriculture (crops, livestock and fisheries production) and related food systems. The project will contribute to the Nutrition Cluster’s Strategic Objectives 2 and 3.
In 2020, FAO will reach around 70,000 households with cash and livelihood assistance. To promote Nutrition Cluster efforts, FAO will ensure all these households are also reached with training on improved household nutrition (from infancy to old age). In targeting these farmers, agropastoralists, pastoralists and fishers, the project will encourage the production and consumption of fresh, nutrient rich foods based on livelihood (e.g. green vegetables and fruits among farmers; milk and dairy products among pastoralists; and protein rich fish among fishers). FAO will directly train two nutrition champions per village using Nutrition and Food Safety Education for Livelihoods training materials developed in collaboration with the Nutrition Cluster and the SUN movement. Empowered nutrition champions will share information and knowledge on household nutrition and food safety with other members of the community.
To complement the Nutrition Cluster’s food and nutrition education in rural communities, FAO, together with the SUN movement, will establish 30 school gardens to promote good diet, develop livelihood skills and environmental awareness among children (age 10+). This will contribute to the Federal Government’s initiative to incorporate nutrition into the school curriculum. The SUN movement will coordinate different sectors of government involved in this activity (e.g. Ministries of Agriculture and Ministries of Education). FAO will also contribute to the prevention, management and reduction of food waste and food loss through the development of context specific communication materials. The materials will target one value chain and communicate messages on how to prevent losses from production to consumption (e.g. maximizing nutrient quality in food during planting, harvesting, processing and preparation).
In Somalia, fish is not part of the typical diet even in some coastal areas let alone inland communities. The project will therefore promote the consumption of fish and fish products through at least 20 campaigns in coastal, inland and urban communities. The campaigns will raise awareness of nutritious value of fish and include fish tasting, cooking demonstrations and nutrition education. In addition, FAO will develop and promote a fish powder (targeting 2,000 households) that can be used to enrich meals and increase protein intake for adults and children (above 6 months).
FAO will conduct a Food Environment Analysis in Somalia to analyze underlying causes of malnutrition in relation to food access and availability. The Study will identify these underlying causes at household level in different agro-ecological zones which face very diverse challenges relating to food access, availability and utilization. The ultimate aim is to develop more effective nutrition and food security programmes.
Gender sensitive approaches will be used to ensure that women are included and participate fully in activities without compromising on their role as the primary caregiver in the household.

Project Activities

1. Integrating tailored nutrition awareness messages and training into FSC livelihood programmes to enhance rural communities’ knowledge, attitude and consumption practices (e.g. improving dietary diversity and encouraging consumption of fresh and nutritious foods high in protein, vitamins, minerals etc.). This includes analysis of seasonal production calendars and empowering community members as nutrition champions (one man and one women from each village). Target: at least 70,000 rural households. 2. Establish 30 small school gardens, together with the SUN movement, where children (age 10+) learn about the food path. This includes provision of gardening kits containing nutrient-dense, fast-growing varieties of fruit and vegetable seed (e.g. watermelon, carrots, leafy greens, tomatoes, onions and sweet potatoes [in the South]), micro-irrigation systems and guidance notes for teachers. 3. Develop context specific materials and campaigns for food waste and food loss prevention, reduction and management for both rural and urban areas. For example, training on storage and processing to prevent food waste. 4. Promote fish consumption through at least 20 tastings, cooking demonstrations and nutrition education campaigns – targeting coastal, inland, rural and urban communities. 5. Production and promotion of dried fish powder to enhance the protein content of food and enrich household diets (especially for children 6-59 months). 6. Conduct a Food Environment Analysis Study to look into the relationship between food availability, access, quality and consumption in different livelihood zones of Somalia.

Mitigation Measures

2a. Exclusion and marginalization: Members of marginalized community’s/ minority groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistanceFAO Somalia’s targeting process involves consultations with local elders, district authorities and partners to ensure interventions reach the most vulnerable. Before the implementation of any intervention, FAO trains partners on the targeting process to make sure that it is well understood and the most vulnerable, regardless of their position within the community, are included in consultations. FAO’s Call Center verifies inclusion within these consultations through calling participants randomly , partners also have to send GPS enabled photographs of the consultations. In areas with IDPs, both the displaced and the host communities are consulted and the most vulnerable, as defined by the community, benefit from the intervention. In addition, FAO ensures thorough sensitization and communication at the community level on upcoming projects; their purpose, activities and benefits to allow for open channels of communication with all members of the community. 2b. Exclusion and marginalization: Women, girls and boys are not included in consultations and decision-making processes.It is mandatory for community level consultations to include 30 percent women when holding consultation meetings for decision making. In addition, activities at the village level consider cultural aspects and therefore ensure the active participation of both men and women. For instance, all villages have one man and one women trained as nutrition champions. Interventions are also tailored to address specific needs, for example school gardens target children. 3b. Interference of external stakeholders in impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries: Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance.FAO has a toll free call center that is open for beneficiaries to anonymously report sexual harassment and any other exploitation cases they may encounter during project implementation. All reported cases are thoroughly investigated and action taken accordingly. The hotline number is disseminated through posters, radio messages and bulk SMS for all beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries. Field monitors are also sent out regularly to the target communities to consult with beneficiaries, track progress and evaluate the impact of interventions. FAO also uses the call center to verify that the target beneficiaries identified in the selection process meet the selection criteria. All partners and FAO staff are trained on Protection Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and adhere to a strict code of conduct on sexual harassment in all its forms. 4c. Protection mainstreaming: Forced taxation/extortion or theft of humanitarian assistanceThere is direct verification of beneficiaries to assess the quality and timeliness delivery of interventions by implementing partners. This verification process takes place throughout the intervention period. Beneficiaries are also informed of their entitlements through bulk SMS. FAO ensures that it receives the necessary permits to provide the inputs to the beneficiaries as required, e.g. micro-irrigation pipes for the school gardens if sourced from outside Somalia will have to be cleared through Somali government processes as required.
