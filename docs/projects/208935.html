Project Summary

This intervention by CARE international in Sudan is designed to deliver a comprehensive FSL, WASH, health, nutrition, and protection package to IDPs, returnees and host communities and residents living in unsafe environments in South Darfur, East Darfur, Kassala, Gedaref, Al Jazeera, Khartoum and South Kordofan. The overall objective of this project is to contribute to “Decreased morbidity, mortality, and Gender Based Violence risks through provision of improved and sustained access to Cash, food assistance, livelihood support, water supply, sanitation, hygiene promotion, health, nutrition, protection and GBV services. This objective is developed in line with the 2024 Sudan humanitarian response plan and the FSL, WASH, health, nutrition, protection, and GBV clusters priorities in addressing the growing needs for the affected population across Sudan after the 15th of April conflict through construction and rehabilitation of facilities, operation and maintenance and capacity building of communities. The total number of individuals targeted by this project is 1,327,738

CARE’s HRP project for 2024 is targeting to reach 10% of the Sectors’ targets in all CARE’s operating localities.
CARE Nutrition HRP’s project will focus on (1) Provision of preventive and protection nutrition interventions: through MYCN counselling, dissemination of IYCF/E messages, supporting Vitamin A and Deworming for under 5 yrs old. In addition, supporting the Early detection of the malnutrition among under 5 yrs old and PLW through MUAC screening and early referral for treatment (2) CARE will support the Blanket Supplementary Feeding Program in its operating areas to reach a target of 1% of children from 6-59 months and PLW to prevent the risk of developing acute malnutrition. CARE project will (3) maintaining and scaling up treatment of severe and moderate acute malnutrition services for children and PLW, supporting the operating of the OTPs and TSFPs and SCs. In addition, (4) CARE will be supporting providing evidence and monitoring, through SMART surveys and supporting the monitoring and reporting at CARE’s supported sites to monitor admissions trends and to monitoring of supplies.
CARE Health HRP project for 2024, will focus on (1) Supporting essential public health functions with focus on PHC. CARE will continue supporting the continue of essential PHC service, to prevent and control communicable diseases, provision of SRH services and supporting the provision of EPI activities. In addition, CARE will address the gaps in Secondary health care services, supporting the operation of hospitals in overstretched areas. (2) Strengthen emergency preparedness, response, and all-hazards emergency risk management: through supporting the early alerts identification, reporting and investigation in all the supported localities and health facilities, through strengthening the surveillance systems and EWARS, and addressing the gaps for supporting the (RRT) where is needed in CARE’ operating localities.
CARE ensures that H&N supported facilities are integrated with WASH essential services insides the facilities, and communities are oriented about Hygiene promotion messages.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Along with the 15th of April conflict, the long-standing conflict and displacement inSudan have made children, girls, men, and women are vulnerable to various forms ofabuse and exploitation. In their daily life, women and girls face a significant risk ofabuse. Women and girls travelling to far places for various reasons, such as to collectwater and firewood, and even to distant marketplaces, face different kinds of abuseand men are not able to move freely as they will be exposed to killing and kidnappingby the armed people. Domestic violence is also common. For CARE and its partners,understanding how men and women are affected differently by conflict, and designingthe right programs and projects that address gender issues is a priority, with the aimof preventing and responding to GBV risks. protection and safety of the targetedcommunity will be taken in to consideration throughout the project implementationperiod. Priority will be given to the protection and security of the targeted groups whowalk for more than 3-5km to collect water. Coordination with protection and WASHclusters will continue through-out the project period to ensure safety, protection andsecurity of all beneficiaries who involved in receiving CARE support. protection andsafety of the targeted community will be taken in to consideration throughout theproject implementation period. Priority will be given for setting up the water tanks ordistribution points at strategic locations so that it reduces the travel time from thewater source as per the SPHERE standards (within 500 meters). Prior to theinstallation of water tanks or rehabilitation of water systems, women, men, childrenand people with special needs will be consulted for the selection of water schemessites in order to ensure access, safety and security. The project will ensureimplementing safe programming approaches by taking in to consideration DO NOTHarm principles and approach by making sure that our interventions do not createand/or increase risks to affected people’s safety and security. CARE will endeavor tocontinue improve the protection of targeted beneficiaries by addressing issues relatedto systematic conflict-related violence. Sexual Violence will focus on raising men’sawareness about issues of systematic violence. This will focus heavily upon reducingcommunities risk from sexual violence, specially women’s. Attention will also beplaced upon raising awareness among beneficiaries of the available humanitarianservices and how to access assistance and securing access to humanitarianassistance for newly affected people

A comprehensive and multi-entry Feedback and Accountability Mechanism (FAM)portfolio, including phone lines, complaint boxes, and in-person reportingmechanisms is in place across all project sites for participants, partners, and staff toreport any cases or suspected cases. Stakeholders will provide direct feedback toCARE staff, community volunteers, or via suggestion boxes or phone calls in theproject locations. Complaints received via FAM channels will be documented in an“compliant issues log” maintained by MEAL staff. With an ongoing rolling basis,complaints will be monitored and prioritized based on protection and risk issues, withthe highest risks addressed immediately by the program team’s senior management.AS CARE has Zero tolerance policy for Sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse, theMEAL team will review complaints received through the FAM; sensitive cases will bereferred to the Country Director, such as sexual harassment, exploitation, GBV. AllCARE staff and related personnel receive training on PSHEA and are required to signthe PSHEA policy and protocols which are well socialized. In this project’s budget,PSHEA and GBV risk mitigation training is a planned activity for staff, partners,nutrition and health care service providers, and outreach workers. Central toAccountability to Affected Populations (AAP) are the responsible use of power andCARE’s FAM will be used to monitor and act in response to any identified risks offraud/corruption, exclusion of beneficiary sub-groups, or diversion of resources duringproject implementation. CARE will work to ensure that all standards of confidentialityand protection of participants are followed. Complaints are logged and followed up byCARE’s Monitoring Officers, and subsequent appropriate and necessary adjustmentsto activities are made as part of the response.
