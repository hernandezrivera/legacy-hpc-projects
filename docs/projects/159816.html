Project Summary

The Somalia Humanitarian Bulletin May 2019, has presented this year in Somalia as the third driest GU seasons since 1980’s which has exuberated the humanitarian situation to 2.2 million people who are trying to recover from the prolonged 2016-17 drought. According to an appeal by the District Commissioner Luuq on 26th September 2019, the drought situation in Gedo has caused scarcity of water since most sources have dried up and communities have been forced to access water for domestic and livestock consumption further away from settlements exposing children and women to potential abuse. Since July 2019,2500 newly IDPs have arrived from Ethiopia and neighbouring Bay and Bakool Regions and are in need of emergency water and storage containers. Similar reports were received from the Head of Projects and Humanitarian Liaison Officer that more than 30,000 families in Doloow are in dire need of WASH services with huge influx of IDPs coming to existing IDP camps of Kabasa and Qansaxley on a daily basis.
A needs assessment conducted by CSOs in Bardera in June 2019, shows more than (94%) of the assessed household reported not treating their drinking water, which increases the risk of contracting water borne diseases such as cholera. The situation is the same with learning and health institutions where only 9 were reached with a full WASH package so far out of 150 institutions targeted in 2019 from the data available for August 2019 WASH cluster Dashboard. The scenario continues to negatively affect education for pupils and in particular girls and women attending the health facilities. El-Berde district is among the areas with highest WASH vulnerability in South West State of Somalia, most vulnerable families in the villages walk over 30Km to 40Km to reach to the nearest water point which is the El-Berde town borehole. According to the assessment results provided by UNICEF partners, over 90% of the total school children in 7 schools and people that access health services in 5 MCHs in the district had inadequate access to safe water in their institutions. Most of the pupils’ lack clean water for use in the schools. Patients in the health centres and OTP were also lack access to water for drinking and hygiene. This poses great risks that can contribute to emergency AWD outbreaks and spread of other water borne diseases.
COOPI proposes activities that will address the immediate WASH needs of 34,382 vulnerable target persons with transition to more sustainable interventions while prioritizing respect for the rights, dignity and safety of the people affected by crisis. For life saving COOPI proposes to provide emergency water through vouchers for two months to 500 IDPs in Gedo and 500HH in Togdheer and provide them with water 20 liter jerricans to enhance the storage and minimise contamination during handling.1500 persons will also benefit from disinfection of 30 hand dug wells in Elbarde and Barawe in Bakool and middle Shabelle respectively.
For sustainable WASH interventions, almost filling up and collapsing 120 latrines for IDPs in Baidoa and Banadir will be desludged and minor structural repairs done while ensuring local artisans are engaged. In Elbarde, a newly drilled borehole will be equipped with solar pumping and pipeline extended to the town including two schools and a health facility, in addition 20 hand dug wells will be protected and installed with hand pumps in Luuq, Doloow, Belet-Hawa, Garbaharey and Bardheere. COOPI proposes to provide De Martino Hospital in Banadir with a sustainable and low technology solar still to desalinize salty borehole water with a production of 5000l/day. the system will also double up as rain water harvesting system. Provision of ceramic filters for HH water treatment is also a step towards durable solutions as they are reusable.
In the rural villages of Gedo, Bay and Bakool COOPI will identify villages for CLTS triggering in line with the MoH protocol in addition to Hygiene promotion for all site

Mitigation Measures

Lense 2: Exclusion and marginalization- Members of marginalized community’s/ minority groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance- Women, girls and boys are not included in consultations and decision-making processesMitigation Measures• Consultation will be done with women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, older persons and marginalised persons to collect accurate information about their specific WASH requirements and preferences for location and design of WASH facilities• Considering that most often women and children collect water, COOPI staff will talk to them directly about safety of the location of and routes to the water collection point• Community and committee representatives will be engaged to play an active role in identifying solutions and the decision-making processes that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response.• Coordination will be done with civil society specialising in working with persons with impaired mobility or disabilities to help identify such individuals and use them as a resource to improve service delivery, train staff and for the referral of cases.• COOPI WASH staff and committees will be representative of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, disability) and all staff and members should be given protection mainstreaming training.• COOPI WASH team will ensure that the hygiene kit size is appropriate for the beneficiaries to be able to carry home with no additional physical or financial burden and without being dependent on others to carry it for them• No facilities or distribution points will be placed near military installations or threats such as attacks stray bullets, crossfire or violence. Weapon bearers will not be present inside distribution points.• COOPI will Identify central locations for distribution points - beneficiaries will not traverse long distances or pass through conflict zones to access distribution points.• Distributions will take place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall.
