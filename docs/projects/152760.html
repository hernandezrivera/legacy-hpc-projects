Project Summary

This project aims to meet the immediate, medium and long-term food security needs of the target communities in Eastern Equatoria, Western Bahr el Ghazal, Unity, Jonglei and Central Equatoria states that were affected by armed conflict, insecurities, climate change and displacement. This will be achieved by:
a) Access to cereal seed kits: To ensure the target communities recover from hunger cereal seed kits and tools accompanied by agricultural extension activities, focusing on good agronomic practices including season of planting, seedbed preparation, planting and spacing, weed management, irrigation, integrated pest and production management, harvesting and post-harvest management. This intervention will be delivered using lead farmers and farmer field school approaches with demonstration plots.
b) Access to vegetable kits: In areas with access sufficient water for vegetable growing, beneficiaries will be provided with vegetable kits containing a variety of vegetable seeds followed by extension training on good agronomic practices including seed and nursery bed preparation, seed planting and spacing, irrigation, integrated pest and production management, harvesting and post-harvesting management.
C) Access to fishing kits: Intervening at the household level to meet the emergency food security needs through provision of fishing NFIs. Fishing has a potential to immediately address the food security needs of the targeted vulnerable households through provision of fishing equipment to enable the people to immediately harvest fish from the rivers and swamps in the areas.
d) Improving the capacity of farmer field schools: lead farmers and extension workers will be trained as trainers for community farmers. The lead farmers and extension workers will then facilitate discussions and demonstration on good agronomic practices for the vegetable and cereal crops producer groups.
e) Setting up irrigation system: Vegetable farmers will be provided with treadle pumps, watering cans at household levels while drip irrigation systems will be established at farmer field school gardens or demonstration plots. Drip irrigation systems together with community training on their adequate and sustainable use will enhance the copying mechanism during the dry season.
f) School feeding program: Food for education and school feeding programme will be considered in the project areas where PAH carries food security and livelihood activities with seeds distribution and vegetable and cereal crops production.
i) Conditional General food distribution: Conditional general food distribution programming/project is already being developed and submitted awaiting approval. This will target the most vulnerable food insecure population with limited access to markets and having poor road network.
j) Cash Programming is based on initial market and community assessments, considering cash vouchers or cash for work. Food for asset/cash will be conducted in the areas where there is need and local markets are able to support the project targeting establishment of community structures and facilities like schools and infrastructures like community road projects and communal farms establishment.
k) Integration: For more holistic approach, PAH will integrate interventions with sectors of WASH, nutrition, education, protection and S/NFI through combined hygiene and nutrition promotion in addition to screening and referral to nutrition partners. Education and health will be integrated through training of school hygiene clubs and school gardening and awareness at the schools, nutrition and health centers. PAH will work with nutrition and health partners like Johanniter in Wau, RMF in Boma while the PAH ERT will integrate the WASH, S/NFI and FSL as in the projects in Magwi, Boma, Koch and Jur River counties since 2017. Protection will be mainstreamed across all sectors and additional protection team will be supporting the community where PAH implements FSL and other sectors.

Needs

According to an assessment conducted by PAH in Jur River county in July 2018, the economic crisis increased food insecurity particularly for population that is highly market dependent. Furthermore, public health crisis that has led to the increase in cholera, measles and malaria cases and other seasonal and climatic shocks like floods, drought, crop and livestock diseases, pests including the fall army worm are further undermining people’s resilience by depleting household’s food stock. Acute malnutrition worsened in the Greater Baggari area in former Wau with extreme critical level of Acute Malnutrition (GAM>30%) and other counties of Western Bahr el Ghazal showing critical levels of acute malnutrition, GAM 15.0 to 29.9% (Source: PAH FSL BASELINE REPORT JUR RIVER COUNTY JULY 2018). Another assessment conducted in Boma by PAH in July 2018 mentioned several challenges that resulted in low food production, among them were flooding (74%) pests (67%), and diseases (60%, shortage of seeds and tools, drought and floods, inadequate skills among others. (Source: PAH FSL Jebel Boma Baseline Report). In Jonglei the area of Pibor County has been in IPC, Phase 4, and the rest of the region continues IPC Phase 3. Early 2019, food security situation in Jonglei is expected to deteriorate in northern parts. Based on the September IPC analysis, it is expected that 6.1M people (59% of the total population) faced Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity at the peak of the lean season (July – August 2018), of whom 47,000 were in catastrophe (IPC phase 5) and 1.7M were in emergency (IPC Phase 4). Food security has improved slightly with the green harvest in September relative to July and August and further improvements are expected in the post-harvest period between October and December 2018. However, an anticipated earlier than normal start of the lean season will result in an estimated 5.2 million (49% of the total population) people in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity between January and March 2019, with 36,000 in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). In September 2018, it is expected populations are in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) in Leer and Mayendit (Unity state), Yirol East and Yirol West (Lakes state), Canal/ Pigi (former Jonglei state), Panyikang (Upper Nile state), and Greater Baggari in Wau (Western Bahr el Ghazal state). In October - December 2018, Catastrophe (IPC phase 5) outcomes are expected in Leer and Mayendit (Unity state), Pibor (former Jonglei state), Nasir, Ulang and Panyikang (Upper Nile state) and Greater Baggari in Wau (Western Bahr el Ghazal state). In January - March 2019, Catastrophe is expected in Pibor and Canal/ Pigi (former Jonglei state) and Leer and Mayendit (Unity state), Nasir, Ulang and Panyikang (Upper Nile state). Central Equatoria with Kajo Keji has been highly under-served, as ongoing violence constantly forces populations to relocate. With more stability expected in the coming months, more can be done to address the gaps from next year on.Of greatest concern are counties where harvests are likely to be poor, access to humanitarian assistance may be limited for a section of the population, possible returns from within and outside of the country may cause further pressure on already scarce resources, insecurity, lack of freedom of movement and extreme depletion of livelihoods prevails. This calls for immediate and unhindered provision of humanitarian assistance and close monitoring. (Source: KEY IPC FINDINGS: SEPTEMBER 2018 – MARCH 2019). Furthermore, based on recent data from partners Koch County (Unity) is expected to have high food insecurity levels, however it has not been accessible over the last few months, hence the lack of clear data.

Activities

a. Support to beneficiaries through fishery activities (fishing kits - 2 x boxes of hooks, 1 x monofilament, 2 x twines), distribution and demonstration on proper use, training on preparation and preservation of fishb. Support on vegetable production for beneficiaries (distribution of vegetable kits- seeds from 20g to 100g, depends on preference). Training the beneficiaries in good agronomic practices.c. To ensure the success of vegetable production, irrigation systems and adequate training on their use will be provided. Vegetable farmers will be provided with treadle pumps and 10 to 15 liter watering cans. Drip irrigation systems together with community training on their adequate and sustainable use will enhance the copying mechanism during the dry seasond. Support to cereal crop production for beneficiaries (distribution of cereal kits – 2-5Kg depending on preference, and culturally acceptable tools). Training the beneficiaries in good agronomic practices. e. Training lead farmers/extension workers and setting up lead farmers schools, Farmer Field Schools for practical demonstration of farming practices. The lead farmers/extension workers will then facilitate discussions and demonstration on good agronomic practices for the vegetable and cereal crops producer groups. Lead farmers will be conducting training sessions to farmers on their own fields.f. Food for Education and school feeding programme will be considered in the project areas where PAH carries food security and livelihood activities with seeds distribution and vegetable and cereal crops production.g. Conditional General food distribution: Conditional general food distribution will target the most vulnerable food insecure population with limited access to markets and having poor road network. h. Cash programming will be based on initial market and community assessments, considering cash vouchers or cash for work. Cash programming strategies for various sectors are currently developed with the involvement of PAH staff in Cash Working Group. Food for Asset/cash will be conducted in the areas where there is need, local markets are able to support the project. This will target establishment of community structures and facilities like schools and infrastructures like community road projects.i. Mainstreaming cross-cutting issues across all activities with particular focus on gender mainstreaming, GBV, protection, accountability to affected population and committing to improve effectiveness of cooperation with communities. j. Integrated projects: PAH will have an integrated approach with FSL and WASH, NFI and nutrition, specifically community hygiene and nutrition promotion on food hygiene and safe water management so as to break the chain of contamination at the household level; nutrition screening and referral of SAM/MAM cases to nutrition centers. This will also be integrated with education and health through training of school hygiene clubs and school gardening and awareness at the schools, nutrition and health centers. PAH ERT will continue to integrate the WASH component as PAH WASH and FSL have already been working in integration in Magwi, Boma, Koch and Jur River counties since 2017. PAH NFI team will work together with other sectors in identifying S/NFI and Protection gaps and respond accordingly.k. Post-distribution (PDM), post-intervention (PIM) monitoring will be done across the project done by independent PAH M&E team, as long as the location are still accessible and secure. PDMs/PIMs gives clear view of whether beneficiaries have profited from the provided assistance and whether they are satisfied with the quality and safety of carried activities. These reports will provide clear lessons learnt and recommendations and will be used to improve overall quality of PAH’s projects.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Meeting with authorities and community members at early stage of project design and implementation to discuss project scope. Focus group discussions with various groups to collect feedback on the needs which is related to providing information on activities carried by PAH. Complaint/feedback system allows beneficiaries to approach PAH team member in case clarification or follow up is needed. This includes establishing help desk, supported by both men and women responsible for providing information to the community members. Interaction sessions during distributions, demonstrations and training. Using IEC materials for the promotion of key messages.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Some messages are general for all population, depends on items provided. Specific sessions for women, girls, people with disabilities, will be taking place to ensure safety and security during distributions and on the way to and from distribution site. Needs analysis will focus on ensuring dignity of the beneficiaries through provision of appropriate items and training relevant to different age groups and gender.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

PAH has internal regulations on PSEA, with all employees signed commitment. PAH is also a member of the protection cluster and understands the modalities to ensure protection is mainstreamed within our activities. Key staff employed on field level (enumerators, crowd controllers, other dealing directly with beneficiaries) needs to sign the safety code of conduct. Training is provided with clarification on beneficiary abuse, reporting abuses, confidentiality and risk mitigation.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Focus group discussions with women groups, HH visits and consultations on specific needs related to gender, age group, disability, also within various sectors. Individual interviews with community members, institutional users (Health facilities, Nutrition Centers, Schools, etc.) on needs, protection concerns, other. Community needs to be inclusive of a wide representation of people in an intervention. Equitable Representation must include: Men, Women, Elderly, Youth, Disabled, Ethnic minorities, the most Economically and/or Politically Marginalized people, Boys and Girls. In practice this is challenging with, for example, women only discussions monitored by community leaders. Ensuring that community gatekeepers are managed and allow fair representation is carefully considered by PAH implementing team. Protection issues that may arise are reviewed with protection team supporting project implementation at all levels.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

All PAH’s stakeholders can give feedback and make complaints, including direct and indirect beneficiaries, non-beneficiaries, partners, local leaders, group of persons, organizations, project staff or any person can provide feedback about the PAH program, activities, staff, implementing partners and project suppliers. Complaints are collected through paper based system on the field level, phones calls, emails, complaint boxes, during monitoring visits, established help desk, coordination/review meetings, recipient/complaint committees. Possible courses of action for resolving disputes and complaints include: Apologize and accept the complaints when genuine and consider all relevant information and arguments; Refer/forward to alternative agencies if the complaint/issue raised is not within PAH mandate and is unable to assist with services; Explain the complaint process and the steps that will be followed after complaint submission, if possible, resolve the complaint immediately or commit to a time frame for investigating the complaint further; Refer if the complaint requires additional information and further investigation/analysis by management for decision making; Referral to government body if the complaint requires government intervention.

Yes

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project measure access of beneficiaries to assistance provided through complaint mechanism, tailored focus group discussions with women groups, youth groups, people with disability, elderly and other community members. Protection specific data collection tools are used by the monitoring team to gather valid information for lessons learnt and future recommendations.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

The project measures the safety of beneficiaries by establishing a complaint mechanism, consultative problem identification and solving through dialogue. Monitoring team has protection related questionnaires, developed by protection partners, approved by protection cluster. The tools gather key feedback from beneficiaries on safety related to assistance delivery.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Dignity of beneficiaries is measured through focus group discussions with the beneficiaries in which the beneficiaries will express their satisfaction or dissatisfaction of the process. Complain desks will be established during activities of distribution, registration and verification to address complains in a dignified way. Meanwhile throughout the project period beneficiary feedback, complaints and means of addressing will be documented in a well-designed form and analysed by the independent monitoring and evaluation team.
