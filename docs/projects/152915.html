Project Summary

This project seeks to provide life-saving GBV prevention, mitigation and response in alignment with the HRP Strategic Objectives 2 & 3, Protection Cluster Strategic Objectives 1 & 2, and the priority actions identified by the GBV Sub Cluster.
The project prioritized Tier 1 locations Yambio and Nasir/Luakpiny, as well as Tier 3 locations Tonj East, Twic, Tambura, Ezo, Nagero, Ibba (prioritizing Tier 1 locations but recognizing the absence of resourced GBV response services in Tier 3 locations for 2019 and World Vision’s operational presence with capacity to respond in the emergence as a hotspot).
The project will target a total of 675,000 individuals (W: 227,500; G: 227,500(Adolescent: 150,000); M: 110,000; B: 110,000 (Adolescent: 90,000)), recognizing a specific emphasis on strengthening life-saving assistance meeting the unique and unmet needs of child survivors, specifically adolescent girls, and engaging male/female youth and adolescents in GBV prevention.
The project will include the following key interventions in all locations, based on specific assessed need, disaggregated by sex and age: GBV case management; provision of PSS; safe referrals (medial, legal, livelihood, other); provision of legal advice to survivors; GBV information management; engagement of and advocacy to police and justice actors (formal and traditional) to strengthen access to justice; monitoring of GBV risks for prevention/mitigation through safety audit & safety perception mapping; community engagement interventions to prevent and respond to GBV such as establishment of Women & Girl Safe Spaces & comprehensive programming per the WGSS South Sudan Guidelines; establishment/strengthening of community based protection/GBV structures and systems including women’s groups; community based advocacy and awareness raising, especially engaging male/female youth as change agents in adoption of positive masculinities, respect for women and girls’ equal rights, ending of social acceptance of violence (especially sexual/physical violence) and positive attitudes/behaviours that promote the end of GBV.
The project reflects the feedback of beneficiary consultations and includes accountability mechanisms, and considers the unique needs and adapts its design based on gender and age considerations/vulnerabilities (Code 4). The project approach seeks to place communities at the centre of interventions and contribute to localization through capacity building in all community-based initiatives (i.e. strengthening community protection committees in monitoring, reporting, referral). The project design prioritizes a Do No Harm approach with an explicit intention to address protection issues experienced by women, girls, boys and men, specifically GBV, recognizing that respect for human rights and increasing gender equality are enablers of the South Sudan humanitarian response strategy.

Needs

In 2018, the humanitarian crisis continued in South Sudan despite the signing of the peace agreement in August, and continues to be defined by widespread protection concerns and devastating incidents of GBV. A GWI/IRC study found that 65% of women and girls have experienced GBV at least once in their lifetime, while early/forced marriage can be as high as 85% in rural areas (71% in urban locations such as Juba). GBVIMS quarterly trend reporting find the most common form of GBV is IPV, representing 42% of all reported incidents from Jan-June 2018. A recent OCHCR report found widespread practices of sexual violence, particularly in former Western Equatoria, not only conflict-related but also due to a gross disrespect for human rights, specifically of women.Comparison GBVIMS data between 2017 and 2018 (to Q3) demonstrated an increase of 24%, though incidents are vastly under-reported. According to GBVIMS Jan-Jun 2018, women and girls represent 97% of survivors, of which 79% are adults, 16.5% adolescents and 4.5% children. The majority of reported incidents (42%) are from the community, while 38% are IDP and 28% refugee, indicating the urgent need to target services not only to IDP-concentrated locations, but also at community level. Despite this data there are significant gaps where no life-saving GBV response services exist. It is essentially to scale up availability of life-saving GBV response service points, including case management, referral to CMR, provision of PSS, and referral to access to justice, livelihood and other support services. Specific services to better link child protection and GBV services for the care of child survivors, particularly the unique vulnerabilities of adolescent girls, is required.KAP surveys from Upper Nile, Central & Western Equatoria indicate reasons that women and girls may not report or access services include unavailability of services, fear of stigma or re-victimization (such as the practice of jailing a survivor for their “protection” or forced marriage as a form of “settlement”), and low confidence in police and the legal system (GBVIMS Trends Report), requiring advocacy and capacity building with government actors and local authorities to better realize a survivor-centered approach, particularly for women and girls.Women and girls continue to lack safe spaces to share their experiences, receive information about availability services, learn new information or skills on health, nutrition, healthy relationships, literacy and entrepreneurship and income generation/alternative livelihoods. Safe spaces for women and girls are necessary both as a space to initiate and provide GBV response services to survivors, as well as to support in the prevention of GBV - first instance but more so repeat victimization. Underlying the pervasive trend of all forms of GBV is the normalization of violence (GWI/IRC) and gross gender inequality that prevents or severely limits their control of resources and participation and leadership in decision-making at all levels – household, daily life and governance. A 2018 KAP Survey in Melut found for example that 52% of women identified physical beating by an intimate partner as ‘acceptable,’ while reports from Yambio/Tambura found male youth reported feeling ‘entitled’ to rape women that anger them by turning down their advances. These attitudes and behaviours, coupled with the absence of rule of law, indicate the necessity for GBV prevention and risk mitigation. Increasing community awareness; engaging with police, traditional and statutory legal actors; leveraging faith-based and community advocacy for prevention and change of harmful attitudes/behaviours that normalize GBV; and creation of spaces for women’s leadership and working male and female youth to promote gender equality are all essential needs to reduce all forms of GBV.

Activities

Objective 1 – Provision of life-saving assistance to GBV survivorsKey Activities- GBV Case Management- Provision of PSS and legal advice to facilitate access to justice- Safe referral to medical, access to justice, livelihood and other support services- Specialized services for child survivors- GBV Information Management per GBVIMS SOPs and ISP for South Sudan The project will provide immediate life-saving assistance to the protection needs of GBV survivors (PC SO1, HRP SO 2). This will include the provision of GBV Case Management, referral to health providers for CMR services, provision of specialised psychosocial support (PSS), supporting access to justice through legal guidance and safe referrals where possible, safe referral to livelihood recovery opportunities, and other forms of support where available (i.e. material support, alternative shelter). As a Child Protection actor, WVSS will ensure the provision of specialized GBV case management/PSS and work strengthen referral pathways between CP and GBV for child survivors. OBJ 2 – Prevent, mitigate and respond to GBV through enhanced preparedness and resilienceKey Activities- Women & Girl Safe Spaces and programming (information, basic PSS, referrals, literacy & skills training incl income generating activities/alternative livelihoods)- Strengthening of community based GBV/Protection structures and systems- Conduct safety audit & safety perception mapping for prevention/mitigation of GBV risks- Community awareness raising, attitude and behaviour change with communities, faith leaders- Advocacy, engagement with police & legal/justice actors (statutory, traditional)- Engagement of male & female youth in local advocacy as change agents, promotion of gender equality- Support local women’s organizations & opportunities for women’s leadership opportunities & empowermentThe project will prevent, mitigate and respond to GBV risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience (PC SO 2, HRP SO 2&3) through the establishment and operation of Women & Girl Safe Spaces (WGSS), with programming including safe talking spaces; information on available services for women & girls, reporting and seeking support as a survivor of GBV, sexual & reproductive health, human rights and legal protections; basic and specialised group PSS; and skills training (basic literacy, numeracy, business skills, income generating/livelihood activities). WGSS function as a means for both prevention of GBV by helping to empower women & girls, and one mechanism to support survivors. As a Child Protection actor, WVSS will ensure WGSS programming includes specific and tailored opportunities to meet the needs of adolescent girls. To further prevent, mitigate and respond to GBV, the project will seek to strengthen access to justice by increasing community awareness, especially women and girls, of their legal rights and protections accorded by domestic law in South Sudan, and build capacity of local and traditional authorities in the statutory laws, policies and procedures to promote the rule of law and a survivor centred, rights based approach; where formal justice mechanisms do not exist, engagement with traditional and religious authorities will be prioritized. Community protection mechanisms will be established where needed and strengthened where existing, support community protection/women’s rights committees, and local monitoring, reporting and response mechanisms. Community awareness raising campaigns will focus on increasing awareness of available services, how, where and when (within 72 hrs) to report, and basic information on the harms, causes and consequences of GBV. Community led dialogues will be supported to focus on attitude and behaviour change, specifically engaging male and female youth, to focus on adopting attitudes/behaviours that respect women and girls safety, rights and equality, and encouraging positive masculinities.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Physical barriers to access are assessed in each location of operation. Efforts to mitigate access barriers are made, such as moving services closer to boma/household level to avoid long distance travel and providing services in the home (Ex. Case management, PSS, GBV prevention dialogues). Other barriers can include those that are security related, in which case WVSS works closely with local authorities, community leaders and relevant UN Access Negotiation teams. Implicit barriers resulting from gender inequality and social norms specifically affect women & girls from accessing services; efforts are made to address this type of barrier through community awareness raising and engagement of youth in promoting women’s and girl’s equal rights and gender equality.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project is designed to specifically address gender-based violence, which includes within its definition all forms of coercion, exploitation, deprivation and neglect that is perpetrated by one person against another’s will due to social norms of gender inequality, and caused by this as well as disrespect for human rights and gendered power imbalances. This project will address GBV directly by providing response services to GBV survivors, through prevention efforts to change attitudes and behaviours, and through mitigation seeking to reduce or avoid risks that act as contributing factors to GBV.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project monitoring measures access of beneficiaries through Women & Girl Safe Space participation registers, GBVIMS and project activity participation tracking. In addition, routine Protection monitoring (Safety Audit, Safety Perception mapping) assesses and consults community members on access and risks experienced therein. Feedback mechanisms such as comment boxes/Help Desks provide an opportunity for community members to share access issues.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Safety Audits are completed for each project site and/or service point, with recommendations used to informed the continued use of the location and any adaptions that are required to improve the safety of the site / service point (or reaching this location). Safety Audits are completed prior to establishment of the project site or service point (ex. WGSS) and repeated quarterly or ad hoc, for example in the instance of population displacement or significant event that may be perceived to change local dynamics. Safety Audits are complemented by Safety Perception mapping, which captures the safety issues or unsafe areas perceived by beneficiaries, which may be different from observation; this is completed quarterly or ad hoc as necessary and used comparatively to inform project implementation and any adaptations as necessary.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Dignity of beneficiaries is reviewed in overall project methodology, as well as specifically for each activity implemented or service provided, with an emphasis on promoting beneficiary dignity. This is achieved through use of a survivor centered approach in provision of GBV response services (i.e. case management, PSS), as well as through strategies such as Community Based Protection Committees and engagement of youth leaders, which builds capacity and then supports committees and youth leaders to have ownership of local protection solutions and systems.
